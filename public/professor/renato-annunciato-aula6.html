Chapter 3: The Code

He Has Been Covering

Something that started, is still running, and is being reviewed now

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"He has been covering the interior since January."

"The report has been arriving late for two months."

"The backlog has come down from forty accounts to twelve."

"The first order was signed in May."

Two sentences describe something still running. Two describe a result or a finished moment. What changes in the verb?

FormWhen you use itExample
have / has been + verb-ingAn activity that started in the past and is still running now. The focus is the activity itself, not the result.He has been covering the interior since January.
have / has + past participleThe result of the period, counted or finished.The backlog has come down to twelve.
since & forsince + a point in time (since January, since he joined) · for + a length of time (for two months, for six years)
The questionHow long have you been + verb-ing?How long have you been managing this team?
The negativehas not been + verb-ing — very common in a review, because it describes a habit, not one mistake.He has not been sending the report on time.
Verbs that stay simpleState verbs never take -ing: know, have, need, understand, believe, own.I have known him for three years. Never I have been knowing.
Three trapsNever He is working here since March — since takes has been working. Never How long time — just How long. And a finished moment with a date takes the past simple: She finished the report yesterday.
Practice

Choose the Form

Say it first, then click to check

"He ___has been covering the interior since January." (cover, and he still is)
"The report ___has been arriving late for two months." (arrive, and it still does)
"How long ___have you been managing this team?" (you, manage)
"I ___have known him for three years." (know — careful, it is a state verb)
"She ___finished the report yesterday." (finish — a named finished moment)
The Structure

Three Parts, Thirty Seconds

The performance conversation you already run twice a year, with the words that make it land in English.

1 · What they have been doing well, with evidence

"You have been building a strong relationship with the hospitals in Campinas, and both of them ask for you by name."

2 · The one area for improvement, named once

"The area for improvement is reporting. The weekly report has been arriving late since April."

3 · What changes before the next review, agreed out loud

"From now on the report goes out every Friday before six, and we move to a one-to-one every two weeks. Do we agree?"

Evidence → one problem → one change. Never a soft sentence to open, never a second problem to close.

The Mid-Year Form

Mid-Year Appraisal -- Sales Consultant, Interior of Sao Paulo

Consultant: Marcos Ferraz. Period: January to June. Line manager: Renato Annunciato. Marcos has been covering the interior of Sao Paulo since January, and he has been working with the largest group of clinics in the territory. Over the six months of this appraisal he has reached ninety-four percent of his visit plan, and his track record on new accounts is the strongest in the team.

Three competencies are rated in this cycle. On client relationships the rating is four out of five: he has been building a strong relationship with the two teaching hospitals in Campinas, and both have asked for him by name. On planning the rating is three, because he has been running his week without a written plan and the manager has been asking for one since March. On product knowledge the rating is four.

Two milestones have been reached. The backlog of unvisited accounts has come down from forty to twelve, and the first order from the Ribeirao Preto group was signed in May. The area for improvement is reporting. Marcos has not been sending his weekly report on time, and in April and May it arrived after the regional meeting. He has not been underperforming on results, but the team cannot plan around a report that does not arrive.

The following actions have been agreed. Marcos will send the weekly report every Friday before six, and the manager will hold a one-to-one every two weeks instead of every month. If the report is late twice more in the next cycle, the manager will escalate it to the Commercial Director. The manager has acknowledged in writing that Marcos has been carrying the heaviest workload in the territory. Retention of this consultant is a priority for the region, because turnover in the interior has been high for two years.

Dialogue

Before the Review

I
Renato, I have read the form. Before you sit down with him, tell me in your own words how the six months have gone.
R
Strong, on the whole. He has been covering the interior since January, and his track record on new accounts is the best in the team.
I
And the ratings? I see a three on planning, and fours everywhere else.
R
That is right. Two milestones have been reached: the backlog is down from forty accounts to twelve, and the first order from Ribeirao Preto was signed in May. I want to acknowledge both in the meeting.
I
Good. And is there anything you have been avoiding saying to him?
R
One thing. The area for improvement is reporting. The weekly report has been arriving after the regional meeting since April. He has not been underperforming on results, but the team cannot plan around a report that does not arrive.
I
Say it exactly like that. One sentence, and then stop. Most managers say it and then spend a minute taking it back.
R
Understood. We move to a one-to-one every two weeks, and if it is late twice more I will escalate it to the Director.
I
That is a real appraisal. And write down that you acknowledged the workload — turnover in the interior has been high for two years, and retention of this one matters.

Why Most Reviews Fail

Eleven years of performance conversations. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Where do the bad conversations fail?
At the beginning — with two soft sentences, before the real point arrives.
2. How much evidence should the first part have?
One piece, not five.
3. What are the three things a good conversation does?
Says what the person has been doing well with one piece of evidence, names one area for improvement without softening it, and ends with something that changes before the next review, agreed out loud.
4. Why do people leave, according to him?
Not because a manager was clear — because six months of work was reviewed in four minutes by somebody who had not been paying attention.

Listening 2

The Conversation She Postponed

Four months of waiting, and who paid for it. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Why did she really postpone the appraisal?
Not because she was busy — because the conversation was going to be uncomfortable, and it became more uncomfortable every week.
2. What had changed by the time they sat down?
The thing she needed to say was not news any more — it had become a habit, and the clinics were mentioning it to her instead of to him.
3. Who pays the cost of waiting?
The person, who spends four months not knowing something everybody else already knows.
4. What did she never say out loud, and what happened?
That his relationship with the hospitals was the best in the team. He told her afterwards that he had been considering another offer.

Your Review

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "He is working on the interior since March."
"He has been working on the interior since March." (since never goes with the present continuous)
2. "How long time are you managing this team?"
"How long have you been managing this team?" (just How long, with no time)
3. "I am knowing him for three years."
"I have known him for three years." (know is a state verb and never takes -ing)
4. "She has been finishing the report yesterday."
"She finished the report yesterday." (a named finished moment takes the past simple)

Chapter 6: Your Turn

From Guided to Free

Same review, less help each round

Role-Play

Run the Review

Scenario: You are running the mid-year appraisal with a consultant who has been covering the interior since January. Give the three parts: what he has been doing well with one piece of evidence, the one area for improvement, and what changes before the next review.

Keyword chips:

Role-Play

Hold the Sentence

Scenario: Same review, but the consultant pushes back twice: he has been hitting his numbers, and nobody told him the report mattered. Separate results from behaviour, and keep the sentence without softening it away.

Keywords:

You have not been underperforming on results. the team cannot plan around it I am telling you now. Do we agree?
