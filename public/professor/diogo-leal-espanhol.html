De: profissional que já fala espanhol todos os dias no trabalho — chefe chileno, clientes de seis países — mas apoiado no ouvido e no portunhol, sem gramática sistematizada e com a escrita, nas palavras dele, "al cero".

Para: profissional que escreve um e-mail corporativo sem medo, escolhe o tempo verbal certo sem pensar e apresenta a si mesmo e ao produto com precisão. Em 12 aulas.

Promessa Transformadora

O Diogo não precisa aprender a falar — ele já fala. Precisa parar de quase acertar. Em 12 aulas ele troca a fluência apoiada em adivinhação por forma consciente: artigo certo, tempo verbal certo, palavra espanhola no lugar do decalque do português — e ganha a escrita, que hoje não existe.

Forças
  • Sustenta dez minutos de conversa sem apoio, incluindo passado e planos futuros

Etapa 1.4: Consejo de Gramática -- Ser + Profesión Grammar

The rule behind what you just read.

Profession, nationality and role take NO article after SER. This is where Portuguese and Spanish split: Portuguese says "sou um analista", Spanish says "soy analista".

PortuguésEspañol
Sou um analista.Soy analista.profession alone
Ele é um gerente.Él es gerente.role alone
Meu chefe é chileno.Mi jefe es chileno.nationality

The article comes back as soon as you add a description:

"Soy un analista con diez años de experiencia."

"Camila es una gerente muy exigente."

Etapa 1.4: Consejo de Gramática -- El Género Grammar

Three endings solve almost everything.

The ending tells you the gender. You do not have to memorise word by word — you memorise four endings.

EL (masculine)LA (feminine)
-o · el contrato-a · la propuesta
-e · el informe-ción · la solución
-or · el error-dad · la calidad

The trap: Spanish gender does not copy Portuguese.

a viagem → el viaje  ·  o sinal → la señal  ·  a origem → el origen

The exception worth knowing: words of Greek origin end in -a but are masculine — el sistema, el problema, el tema.

The article drags everything with it: los clientes corporativos, las soluciones integradas.

Etapa 1.4: Consejo de Gramática -- Indefinido vs Perfecto Grammar

The time marker decides — not the verb.

Ask one question: is the period closed or still open?

Period CLOSED → indefinidoPeriod OPEN → perfecto
ayer, anteayerhoy, esta mañana
la semana pasada, el mes pasadoesta semana, este mes
hace tres días, en marzoya, todavía no, aún no
"Ayer tuve una llamada.""Hoy he tenido una llamada."

The surprise that matters for you: in Latin America the indefinido covers today as well. "Hoy hablé con el cliente" is what you hear from your Chilean boss every day, and it is correct there. In Spain — where you want to travel — "hoy he hablado" is the only option.

Seven irregulars you use every day. None of them takes a written accent:

tener → tuve · hacer → hice · decir → dije · poder → pude · ser/ir → fui · venir → vine · haber → hubo

Etapa 1.4: Consejo de Gramática -- Ser y Estar Grammar

One question decides it.

Ask: am I saying WHAT it is, or HOW it is?

SER — ¿QUÉ es?ESTAR — ¿CÓMO está?
identity, professionstate, condition
origin, nationalitylocation
characteristicsresult of a change
time and datehow someone feels
"Soy analista." "Es a las tres.""Está retrasado." "Estoy disponible."

It is NOT permanent vs. temporary. That explanation breaks half the time: "está muerto" is permanent and takes estar; "es joven" passes and takes ser.

Four adjectives change meaning:

ser seguro = trustworthy · estar seguro = certain

ser listo = smart · estar listo = ready

ser aburrido = boring · estar aburrido = bored

ser malo = bad quality · estar malo = sick

Etapa 1.4: Consejo de Gramática -- La Estructura del Correo Grammar

Five parts, always the same five.

ParteFórmula
1. AsuntoConcrete, no verb: "Informe de agosto"
2. Saludo"Estimada Camila:" — colon, never comma
3. Motivo"Me dirijo a usted en relación con..."
4. Desarrollo"Adjunto encontrará..." · "Le informo que..."
5. Cierre"Quedo a su disposición" + "Saludos cordiales,"

Usted or tú? New client, someone outside, first contact → usted. A colleague or your Chilean boss, someone you speak to daily → tú. In Latin America, usted with a client is not coldness — it is the norm.

The verb moves with the register:

usted: "Le informo que..." · "Adjunto encontrará..." · "Quedo a su disposición."

tú: "Te informo que..." · "Adjunto encontrarás..." · "Cualquier cosa me avisas."

Never mix the two in the same email. Pick one and hold it to the end — mixing is the single mistake that reads worst.

Etapa 1.4: Consejo de Gramática -- Si + Presente + Futuro Grammar

One structure, and one trap that comes straight from Portuguese.

Si + presente, + futuro. The condition goes in the present; what follows goes in the future — or in the present, which is just as common in speech.

Condición (presente)Consecuencia (futuro o presente)

Same idea, two distances.

The six forms you need. They come from the past tense you already know:

tuve → tuviera · fui → fuera · pude → pudiera · dije → dijera · hubo → hubiera · quise → quisiera

Why this matters more than grammar. In corporate Spanish the hypothetical is the polite form. It is not about probability — it is about distance:

"Confirme antes del viernes." → sounds like an order

"Si pudiera confirmar antes del viernes, nos ayudaría mucho." → sounds like a request

And it is how you disagree without accusing anyone: "Si yo fuera el responsable, ajustaría el calendario" says the calendar is wrong without saying that whoever set it made a mistake.

deber + infinitivoobligación fuerte, casi normaa quien nombras
hay que + infinitivoobligación impersonala nadie
deberíamos + infinitivorecomendacióna todos, tú incluido
deber de + infinitivosuposición, no obligación—

One "de" changes everything.

"El cliente debe revisar el contrato." → it is his obligation.

"El cliente debe de estar revisando el contrato." → I assume he is doing it.

Why "hay que" is the useful one. In a meeting with people from three countries, "tienes que" in front of everyone costs you something. "Hay que" carries the same urgency and leaves nobody exposed — it is the same diplomacy you learned in lesson 8, applied to responsibility instead of requests.

Etapa 4: Situaciones Quiz

Choose the form that fits the situation.

Meeting with three countries. Something was not reviewed and you do not know whose job it was.
A "Hay que revisar los términos antes de firmar."
B "Tienes que revisar los términos."
C "Ustedes deben revisar los términos."
The client is not answering and you are guessing why.
A "El cliente debe estar en la reunión."
B "El cliente debe de estar en la reunión."
C "El cliente tiene que estar en la reunión."
You want to suggest something without it sounding like blame.
A "No avisaste al cliente."
B "Deberíamos avisar al cliente antes del lunes."
C "Tienes que avisar al cliente."

Think, then record.
