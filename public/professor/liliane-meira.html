Jornada de Transformação

De: Artista que lê Harvard e o MIT em inglês, acompanha depoimentos na Art Basel há três anos e mesmo assim, nas palavras dela, "se perde no básico, na construção de uma frase". Hoje traduz trechos densos no Linguee e perde horas nisso. Entende tudo pelo contexto e trava na hora de falar do próprio projeto — justamente com curadores e artistas, que é onde o trabalho dela precisa existir.

Para: Artista que enuncia a própria premissa em uma frase, sustenta dois minutos sobre a pesquisa, discorda com elegância numa mesa de conferência e conduz uma reunião da agência em inglês — com a mesma exigência que já tem em português.

Promessa do Programa

"De 'patinar numa frase boba' a falar de arte, filosofia e pesquisa em inglês no mesmo nível em que ela já pensa em português — porque o inglês dela precisa estar à altura do pensamento dela."

Forças

  • Fluência receptiva avançada. Lê livros e artigos acadêmicos em inglês por iniciativa própria (MIT, Harvard, Art Basel, revista Noema) e ouve depoimentos de feira sem apoio
  • Repertório temático já formado em arte contemporânea, filosofia e arte-saúde — o conteúdo existe; falta a estrutura que o carrega
  • Metacognição rara. Diagnosticou sozinha o próprio gargalo: "às vezes eu me perco no básico... é uma frase boba e eu estou ali patinando". Aluno que sabe onde trava economiza meses
  • Exigência alta e declarada: "eu quero uma pessoa que me puxe sempre para cima". Material raso é o risco real aqui — nunca material difícil
  • Vida com uso concreto do idioma: feiras internacionais, reuniões da agência e leitura diária de textos densos

Pontos de Melhoria

  • Produção oral travada mesmo com vocabulário disponível — o bloqueio é de montagem de frase, não de palavra
  • Lacunas de base fossilizadas convivendo com estrutura de nível alto (o gap de A1 dentro do B2 que a própria consultoria nomeou)
  • Escuta em velocidade nativa: compreende pelo contexto e perde detalhe — "às vezes me perco um pouco"
  • Dependência de tradução para processar texto denso (Linguee), o que custa tempo e impede automatização
  • Zero prática oral regular: não há contexto diário de inglês falado; o uso é episódico, em viagens e reuniões

A Jornada — narrativa do programa

A Liliane é artista de arte contemporânea e a pesquisa dela é a obra. Desde 2015 investiga a interseção entre arte e saúde e busca um manifesto do próprio trabalho; segue o Dr. Arthur Kleinman (Harvard), lê os Documents of Contemporary Art (MIT) e a revista Noema — que publica cada artigo com áudio, formato que ela já usa sozinha. Vai à Art Basel, em Basel, há três anos. E tem uma segunda frente que é fácil esquecer e que puxa metade do inglês real dela: é sócia da irmã numa agência de turismo, e o nicho dela ali é organizar grupos de viagem de arte, com reuniões em inglês.

O paradoxo que organiza o programa está na fala dela: quer repertório sofisticado — "uma linguagem muito polida, muito educada, filosófica" — e ao mesmo tempo patina montando frase simples. Por isso não se baixa a densidade do tema para consertar a base: a base entra por dentro do assunto adulto, diagnosticada pelo uso e não pela nomenclatura. Ela largou uma professora particular recente porque a professora não dava conta dos textos que ela trazia — o risco de churn aqui é material raso, nunca material difícil.

A direção acadêmica fechou na consultoria que as primeiras aulas são de descoberta: a aluna fala dela, do trabalho e dos interesses, e quem conduz a aula mapeia até onde ela vai e onde estão os gaps. Daí a dosagem deste primeiro bloco — peso alto em produção oral sobre o universo dela, com o benchmark da aula 1 (produção livre de 2 minutos) sendo recomparado na aula 5. Também foi combinado: input autêntico com meaning em inglês, nunca tradução, e espaço para ela registrar a percepção dela sobre o material, que vira tema de debate na aula seguinte.

O eixo anti-repetição é estrutural: cada uma das cinco primeiras aulas tem um domínio próprio — a pesquisa como obra (1), arte-saúde e medicina narrativa (2), argumentação e ponto de vista (3), a agência de viagens de arte (4) e a virada da fotografia para a arte contemporânea (5). Cada fato biográfico dela entra uma vez, como porta de entrada, e depois dá lugar. Aula ímpar segue o modelo de fala (diálogo + role-plays); aula par, o de leitura. Viagem de 10 dias em setembro/2026 — as aulas se remanejam.

Metodologia — 5 Abordagens

1. Um domínio por aula, declarado antes de escrever

O universo dela é largo — pesquisa, arte-saúde, argumentação, curadoria e mercado, a agência de viagens, a trajetória, as feiras. Cada aula recebe um desses eixos, com vocabulário e gênero próprios, e o eixo é escolhido antes de a aula ser escrita. É o que impede que cinco aulas virem cinco variações da mesma conversa sobre arte contemporânea.

2. O fato biográfico é porta, não refrão

Kleinman, a Art Basel, a Noema, a irmã, a fotografia: cada um abre uma aula e depois sai de cena. O fato pode voltar como pano de fundo, jamais como assunto de novo. Num pacote de 96 aulas isso é a diferença entre personalização e caricatura.

3. Base por dentro do tema adulto

Ela tem repertório de B2+ e lacunas de base. A correção da base entra dentro do assunto denso — a mesma frase que enuncia a premissa é a que treina o present perfect — e nunca por meio de exercício infantilizado. Nomear a regra é secundário: a consultoria foi explícita em não ficar dando nome à gramática o tempo todo.

4. Meaning em inglês, nunca tradução

Combinado na consultoria e coerente com o B2: definição em inglês simples, zero português na tela. É também o que ataca o hábito caro que ela relatou — traduzir trechos no Linguee — substituindo tradução por paráfrase.

5. Ela fala, quem conduz mede

Neste primeiro bloco o objetivo é diagnóstico: máximo de tempo de fala dela, correção adiada para o fim, e dois números anotados na produção livre (quantos usos da estrutura-alvo, quantos acertos). O benchmark de 2 minutos da aula 1 é recomparado na aula 5.

Vocabulary

Practice-based research -- research where making the work is the method itself, not a report written about it afterwards
"Practice-based research is hard to explain to people who expect an object at the end."
A body of work -- everything an artist has made over the years, seen as one continuous project
"My body of work has been growing around a single question since 2015."
An inquiry -- a question you keep asking over years, rather than a problem you solve once
"This is an inquiry, not a project with a delivery date."
A premise -- the idea a whole project stands on, accepted at the start and tested by everything that follows
"The premise is that illness produces a kind of knowledge medicine has no room for."
To sit with a question -- to keep a question open for a long time instead of forcing an answer out of it
"I have been sitting with this question for ten years, and I am in no hurry."
A strand -- one line of thinking inside a larger project, running beside the others
"The photographs are one strand. The recorded voices are another."
To unfold -- to develop slowly, with each stage revealing the next one
"The project unfolds. I cannot plan the ending in advance."
An outcome -- what a piece of research produces, which is not always an object
"The outcome is that somebody else can enter the question now."
Field notes -- the writing a researcher keeps while observing, before anything has been made
"My field notes are not preparation. They are already part of the work."
To underpin -- to be the foundation that holds an argument or a project up
"One idea underpins everything I have made in the last ten years."
Tentative -- not final; offered as a first attempt that may still change
"That is a tentative title, and I am comfortable with that."
To frame something -- to present an idea in a way that shows how it should be read
"How you frame the work decides how a curator reads it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

An artist opens her studio to a curator who has come a long way to see it. There is nothing on the walls. What she does is practice-based research, and she has been working on the same inquiry since 2015: that illness produces a form of knowledge clinical language cannot hold. That idea underpins everything in the room, and it is the premise the whole project stands on.

The curator asks what has changed in ten years. The premise has not changed at all. The strands have. She started with photographs; for the last three years she has been recording voices and filling notebooks, and those field notes are not preparation for the work, they are the work. Nothing has been finished in the usual sense, because the project unfolds instead of ending.

Then comes the question a board always asks: what is the outcome? She has given the same answer three times this year, and she gives it again. The outcome is that somebody else can now enter the question. It is a tentative answer, and she would rather offer a tentative one than a tidy one. How she chooses to frame that answer, she knows, decides how the curator reads the whole body of work.

1. "She has been working on the same inquiry since 2015." What does the -ing form add here?
A That the work was interrupted and started again.
B That the activity started in 2015 and is still running now.
C That the work was finished in 2015.

The turning point was that sentence, and nothing more dramatic than that. She did not photograph anything for eleven months.

What made it hard was not failure. She was letting something go that still worked. For three years afterwards she second-guessed herself, partly because the art world had pigeonholed her long before she decided to change. In hindsight, she says, the first decade was formative: the discipline of printing is still in everything she makes. What looked like a leap had in fact been prepared for years, and the detour, as she calls it now, is the half of the work most people never see.

1. "By that afternoon she had been photographing the same subject for nine years." What does this form tell you?
A That she photographed it once, nine years earlier.
B That the nine years were already complete and still running when the curator asked the question.
C That she is still photographing it today.
2. "Every winter she used to print a new series." Why used to and not the past simple alone?
A Because it was a repeated habit over years that has now ended, and used to marks exactly that.
B Because it happened only once.
C Because used to is more polite than the past simple.
3. "She had already stopped believing in the answer she gave." Which happened first?
A Giving the answer, then losing the belief.
B Losing the belief, and only afterwards giving the answer she no longer believed.
C The two happened at exactly the same moment.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Perfect and Narrative Tenses

GRAMMAR

Two layers of the past: the story line, and what was already true when the story got there.

LayerFormExample
The story linepast simpleA curator asked me a question.
Already finished before ithad + past participleI had already stopped believing in them.
Still running up to ithad been + verb-ingI had been photographing it for nine years.
Old habit, now overused to + base verbI used to print a series every winter.
Repeated past actionwould + base verb, never for statesThe gallery would sell it by March.
Signpostsby the time, by then, already, before thatBy the time I left, I had been there ten years.

Never: I used to leave in 2014 · by then I had took the decision · when I was young I would be shy · when the curator asked, I already stopped believing.

Stage 2: Put the Story in Order

Order

Six lines of the story. Put them in the order that makes the turn make sense to a listener.

?"I came to this from photography, and I was largely self-taught."
?"For a decade I made a living from editions and commissions."
?"By then I had been circling the same question for nine years."
?"The turning point was one question I could not answer honestly."
?"Letting go was hard precisely because the work was not failing."
?"In hindsight those years were formative, and I say so on purpose."

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen, then record yourself. Let had already collapse the way a native speaker does.

By the time I left, I had been working on the same question for years.
The medium changed. The question did not.
I was not letting go of a failure, and that is what made it hard.
In hindsight, those years were formative rather than incidental.
I put the detour in my biography, and I do not apologize for the years.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a selection panel would want to hear.

An interviewer asks how long you had been in photography before the change. You answer:
A "When I changed, I already worked ten years with it."
B "By the time I changed, I had been working in photography for ten years."
C "I am working in photography for ten years before."
You want to describe something you did every winter for a decade. You say:
A "Every winter I used to print a new series."
B "Every winter I was using to print a new series."
C "Every winter I would be a printer of series."
A panel asks whether you regret the earlier decade. You answer:
A "It was a waste of time, but I am here now."
B "I prefer not to talk about that period."
C "In hindsight those years were formative, and the discipline of printing is still in everything I make."
You want to say you stopped something on purpose, although it was still working. You say:
A "I let it go while it was still working, and that is what made it difficult."
B "It failed, so I stopped."
C "I was pigeonholed, so I had no choice about anything."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. This one is the benchmark for the whole block.

In three minutes, tell your whole story: where you started, what had already changed when the turn arrived, what you let go of, and what the earlier years gave you that you still use. Use at least three past perfect forms, and do not apologize for a single year.
