Chapter 3: The Code

The Past Behind the Past

had done · had been doing · used to · would

Grammar Discovery

Which of These Is Further Back?

"A curator asked me what the photographs were for."

"By then I had already stopped believing in them."

"I had been photographing the same subject for nine years."

"Every winter I used to print a new series, and the gallery would sell it."

The blue verbs are the story itself. The red ones were already true when the story reached that point — and they always begin the same way.

LayerFormExample
The story linepast simpleA curator asked me a question.
Already finished before ithad + past participleI had already stopped believing in them.
Still running up to ithad been + verb-ingI had been photographing it for nine years.
Old habit, now overused to + base verbI used to print a series every winter.
Repeated past actionwould + base verb (not for states)The gallery would sell it by March.
The trapused to for habits, never for one eventNot I used to stop in 2014.

In one line: past simple carries the story, had plus participle carries what was already true when the story got there.

Dialogue

Recording, Take One

A
Everybody introduces you as a photographer who became something else. Is that how you would put it?
L
Not quite. By the time I stopped printing, I had been working with the same question for years. The medium changed. The question did not.
A
Was there a turning point, or is that something interviewers invent?
L
There was one, and it was very small. Somebody asked me what the photographs were for, and I gave an answer I had stopped believing years earlier.
A
And the work was selling at the time.
L
It was. I was making a living, and every year there was a commission. That is what made it hard: I was not letting go of a failure.
A
Do you regret the years before? Some people call that a detour.
L
In hindsight, those years were formative, and I say so on purpose. I spent three years second-guessing myself. I do not any more.
Comprehension

About Adam

1. How does Adam introduce her at the start, and why does she correct it?
As a photographer who became something else. She corrects it because the medium changed and the question did not.
2. What does Adam suspect about turning points, and what does he observe about the work at that time?
He suspects interviewers invent them. He observes that the work was selling at the time.
3. What word does Adam offer for the earlier years, and how does she answer it?
A detour. She answers that in hindsight those years were formative, and she says so on purpose.

1. The last paragraph uses one tense that the list above cannot use. Which, and what does it add?
The past perfect continuous, in had been circling. It adds the layer that was already true before she left the medium.
2. Why does the text say formative rather than incidental?
Because it refuses to treat the first decade as an accident: it shaped what she makes now, which is exactly what a selection panel wants stated.

The 2015 line. There is no explanation and no apology: the turn is stated as a fact and the reasons live in the paragraph below.
