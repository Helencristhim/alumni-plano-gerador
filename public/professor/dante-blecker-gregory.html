Personagem-Jornada

De: Menino de 8 anos que entende quase tudo e quase nada consegue responder. Absorveu inglês por filme e vídeo, então o ouvido está muito à frente da boca — e a escrita ficou para trás de vez.

Para: Menino que responde o que entende: monta a frase inteira sozinho, conta uma história de 3 a 4 frases sobre o que ama (dragão, Lego, robô, carro, São Paulo) e escreve porque já leu e ouviu o bastante para isso vir sozinho.

Promessa Transformadora

O gargalo do Dante não é compreensão — a consultora registrou que ele entendeu perguntas bem acima do nível dele e simplesmente não conseguiu responder. Por isso o andaime desta jornada fica na produção (chunk pronto, sentence frame, keyword na tela), nunca no input: o que ele ouve e lê continua no teto dele. Reading e listening são a espinha de toda aula; writing entra só como saída curta ancorada no que ele acabou de ouvir ou ler — escrita é consequência, não exercício isolado. O material começa em A2 confortável e sobe para A2+ por volta das aulas 13-16 (3 a 4 meses), conforme orientação da consultora.

Forças

  • Listening ~2 níveis acima do speaking — entende contexto e intenção
  • Interesses ricos e declarados (dragão, Lego, tecnologia, robô, futebol)
  • Exposição natural prévia ao idioma por mídia infantil
  • Quer começar — motivação alta em casa e da família

Pontos de Atenção

  • Writing ~3 níveis abaixo → nunca cobrar escrita solta; sempre depois de ler/ouvir
  • Entende e não responde → dar o chunk, esperar em silêncio até 5s, NUNCA completar por ele
  • Erros de produção A1-A2 → anotar 2 e devolver no fim, jamais corrigir no meio da fala
  • 8 anos, 60 min presencial → trocar de atividade a cada ~5 min e levantar do lugar 2x por aula

Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

Dragon Rider

Dragon words (wings, claws, tail, scales, cave, brave, breathe fire, ride) and the magic sentence: "He can fly, but he can't swim." Listen, repeat, and get ready to ride.

Stage 1.1: Dragon Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen. Say the word out loud. Do the action with your body!

Wings -- the two big parts a bird or a dragon uses to fly
"The dragon opens his wings."
Claws -- the sharp, hard nails on the feet of an animal
"His claws are very sharp."
Tail -- the long part that grows at the back of an animal
"Storm hits the rock with his tail."
Scales -- the small hard pieces that cover the skin of a fish or a dragon
"His scales are red and hot."
Cave -- a big dark hole inside a mountain or a rock
"The dragon sleeps in a cave."
Brave -- not afraid to do something difficult or scary
"Be brave. Climb on his back!"
Breathe fire -- to send hot flames out of your mouth
"Storm can breathe fire!"
Ride -- to sit on an animal or a bike and travel with it
"I can ride a dragon."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the story. Then answer the questions.

Storm lives in a dark cave in the mountain. He is a big red dragon. His scales are hot and his claws are very sharp. Every morning he opens his wings and he flies over the river. Storm can fly very fast and he can breathe fire, but he can't swim. Nina is his rider. She is small, but she is very brave. She can ride Storm with no rope!
What can Storm do?
A He can fly and breathe fire.
B He can swim and breathe fire.
Which sentence is TRUE about Nina?
A She is big and she can't ride Storm.
B She is small, but she is very brave.
Why does Storm stay away from the river?
A Because he can't fly.
B Because he can't swim.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

can / can't + verb

Use can to say what someone is able to do. Use can't to say what someone is not able to do.

+   Storm can fly.
-   Storm can't swim.
?   Can Storm fly?  --  Yes, he can. / No, he can't.
Put two ideas together with but:   He can fly, but he can't swim.

Careful: the verb after can never changes. Not can to fly. Not cans fly. Always can fly.

Stage 4: What Do You Say?

Quiz

A "No, he don't swim."
B "No, he can't. He can fly, but he can't swim."

A "Be brave! He is friendly."

A "What your dragon can do?"
B "What can your dragon do?"

Reflection

Example: "My dragon's name is Rocket. He is blue and he has big wings. He can fly very fast and he can jump, but he can't breathe fire. I am brave, so I can ride him!"

Stage 1.1: City Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen. Say the word out loud. Then find the real thing in your room!

Brick -- a small hard block used to build something
"I need one more red brick."
Wheel -- the round part of a car that turns and makes it move
"This truck has six wheels."
Roof -- the top part of a house that covers it
"The roof is not finished."
Tower -- a very tall narrow building
"There is a tall tower in the middle."
Bridge -- something you build over a river so you can cross it
"The red bridge goes over the river."
Street -- the road in a town where cars drive and people walk
"There are three streets in my city."
Shelf -- a flat board on a wall where you keep books or toys
"My city is on the shelf."
Piece -- one part of something that has many parts
"One piece is missing!"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read about Max's city. Then answer the questions.

Max builds cities. His city is on a big shelf in his bedroom. In the middle there is a grey tower. In front of the tower there is a small park. Behind the tower there are eleven houses, and every house has a different roof. There is a red bridge over the river, and under the bridge there are two boats. Max has a problem: there are only twelve wheels in the box, and he needs sixteen. So four cars in the street have no wheels.
What is in the middle of the city?
A There is a grey tower.
B There are eleven houses.
Where are the two boats?
A They are on the bridge.
B They are under the bridge.
Why do four cars have no wheels?
A Because there are only twelve wheels in the box.
B Because the cars are broken.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

there is / there are

Use there is and there are to say what exists in a place. Count first, then choose.

1 thing   There is a tower in the middle.
2+ things   There are three streets.
-   There isn't a park. / There aren't any cars.
?   Is there a bridge?  --  Yes, there is. / No, there isn't.

Where is it?

in on under next to behind in front of

Careful: it is in front of the house, never in front the house.

Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

Robot Factory

Factory words (check, carry, lift, fix, battery, machine, shift, heavy) and the secret of the letter S: "He works every night." Listen, repeat, and get ready for the night shift.

Stage 1.1: Factory Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen. Say the word out loud. Do the action with your body!

Check -- to look at something carefully to see if it is right or safe
"He checks the machines every night."
Carry -- to hold something and take it to another place
"She carries the boxes to the truck."
Lift -- to move something up, usually something heavy
"The robot lifts a car with one arm."
Fix -- to repair something that is broken
"The engineer fixes the arm at four o'clock."
Battery -- the part that gives electric power to a machine
"Nina checks the battery every morning."
Machine -- a thing with motors that does a job for people
"There are nine machines on this floor."
Shift -- the hours when a person or a machine works
"Bolt works the night shift."
Heavy -- difficult to lift because it weighs a lot
"These boxes are too heavy for me."

Stage 1.2: Match the Meaning

Practice

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read about Bolt's night. Then answer the questions.

Bolt is a robot and he works the night shift. He starts at ten o'clock. First he checks the nine machines on the first floor. Then he carries the heavy boxes to the truck. He lifts two hundred kilos with one arm. He never stops and he never sleeps. At six in the morning he goes to the charging room. Nina arrives at seven. She checks his battery and she writes the report. When something is broken, the engineer fixes it.
What does Bolt do first?
A He checks the machines.
B He carries the boxes.
Who checks the battery?
A The engineer checks it.
B Nina checks it.
Which sentence is correct?
A Bolt never stop and he never sleep.
B Bolt never stops and he never sleeps.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

present simple: the letter S

Use the present simple for things that happen every day. After he, she and it, add -s to the verb.

no S   I / you / we / they  work at night.
+ S   he / she / it  works at night.
-   He doesn't work in the morning. (the S goes to does)
?   Does he work at night?  --  Yes, he does. / No, he doesn't.

Three special ones

carry → carries fix → fixes go → goes

Careful: never I works, and never he work.
