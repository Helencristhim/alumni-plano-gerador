Lesson 5 · The Traveler

Two Weeks in Italy

Somebody asks which city you liked best. Anybody can say it was beautiful. Today you say why one place beat the other, and you sound like a person, not a brochure.

Chapter 1: First Words

Say How Much Better

2. One Structure

3. Your Trip

Between the same and the best in the world there is a whole scale, and it is the scale that makes you sound like somebody who was actually there.

One Question

Chapter 3: The Code

Better, and by How Much

The whole scale between the same and the best

Grammar Discovery

Order Them by Size of Difference

"Lucca is slightly less crowded than Florence."

"The food there is about as good as anything in the centre."

"The old town is far quieter than anywhere we had been."

"That lunch was by far the best meal of the two weeks."

All four compare the same two towns. The red words are not decoration: they are the only thing telling your listener how big the difference actually is.

JobFormExample
Short adjective-er than · the -estquieter · the quietest
Long adjectivemore / less · the mostmore crowded · the most crowded
A big differencefar · much · a lot · considerablyfar quieter · much more expensive
A small differenceslightly · a little · marginallyslightly less crowded
Roughly equalabout as ... as · just as ... asabout as good as the other one
Not remotely equalnowhere near as ... asnowhere near as expensive
The trapnever two comparatives togetherNot more cheaper, not more better

In one line: anybody can say better. The modifier is what tells your listener you were actually there.

Dialogue

Dinner in Rome

G
So, two weeks. Where have you been so far, and what was the worst part?
R
Florence, three days. The worst part was August. The centre was packed, and we spent more time in queues than in front of anything.
G
Everybody makes that mistake. Nobody tells you to leave. Did you go anywhere off the beaten track?
R
One day trip, to Lucca. Fifty minutes on the train, and it was far quieter than anywhere else we had been.
G
And? Better than Florence?
R
Not better. Nowhere near as impressive, honestly. But we walked the whole wall in an hour, we had lunch in a place with six tables, and it was by far the best meal of the trip. It was worth the detour.
G
That is the right answer. Where are you going next, and how long do you have?
R
Four days here, then a stopover in Lisbon on the way home. What would you do with four days, if you had never seen the city?
Comprehension

About Giulia

1. What does Giulia ask for first, and what is unusual about the second half of her question?
She asks where he has been so far and what the worst part was. Asking for the worst part is unusual: most people only ask what you liked.
2. What does she say about the mistake he made, and who does she blame for it?
That everybody makes it, and that nobody tells you to leave the centre. She blames the advice, not him.
3. She says that is the right answer. What exactly is she approving of?
That he did not claim the small town was better. He said it was nowhere near as impressive and still worth the detour, which is a real judgement rather than a compliment.

Listening 1 · A Travel Writer

I Stopped Making Lists

Somebody who travels for a living, on what she changed. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she say a list does to a city, and what do you end up missing?
A list turns the city into a series of tasks, and you walk past the thing you would have loved on the way to the thing somebody told you to see.
2. What does she do now instead, and what is the result?
One thing in the morning, properly, and then she wanders until she is tired. The result is that she remembers far less and far better.
3. What is her claim about the second city, and how does she rate it?
That it is almost always more interesting. The town forty minutes away is quieter, cheaper and about eighty per cent as good, which she calls a much better deal than it sounds.

Listening 2 · The Best Day

The Day We Did Not Plan

Somebody telling the story of one afternoon. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. How many days did they have in Florence, and what does he say about that number?
Three days, which he says was two days more than they needed, because in August the centre is packed.
2. What did they do on the second morning, and what was the town like?
They took a train to Lucca, fifty minutes away, which almost nobody had mentioned. It is a small walled town, you can walk the whole wall in an hour, and it was noticeably calmer.
3. What is he careful NOT to claim, and what is his actual point?
He is careful not to claim Lucca is better than Florence, and says he would go back to Florence tomorrow. His point is that the day trip was worth far more than a third day in a queue.
