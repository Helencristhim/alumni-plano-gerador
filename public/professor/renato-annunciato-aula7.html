Chapter 3: The Code

If We Approve It, We Will Start

Promising something that depends on a condition

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"If we align with medical this week, we will have buy-in before the meeting."

"If the budget is not approved, we will start with two clinics."

"We will lose the window if we do not decide today."

"I will send the rationale as soon as I have the numbers."

Every sentence is about the future. But only one half takes will. Which half, and what does the other one use?

PartShapeExample
The conditionif + present simple — never willIf medical approves the material...
The resultwill / will not + verb...we will start in October.
The orderEither half can come first. Comma only when the if half comes first: "If we align, we will have buy-in." / "We will have buy-in if we align."
Same rule, other wordswhen · as soon as · before · after · until · unless all take the present simple, never will. And unless = if not.
Softer resultsSwap will for may, might, can when you are less certainIf they push back, we may lose two weeks.
Two trapsNever If we will approve it. And never mix in would — would is for the imaginary, and a proposal on the table is real: "If we do not decide today, we will lose the window."
Practice

Choose the Form

Say it first, then click to check

"If medical ___approves the material by the fifteenth, we will start in October." (approve)
"If they push back on the date, I ___will offer a contingency." (offer)

"Unless we ___align this week, the plan will not be ready." (align)
"If the budget is cut, we ___may lose two months." (may, lose — we are not certain)
The Structure

Three Parts, One Decision

Ten minutes on an agenda is enough for exactly this and nothing else.

1 · The rationale, in one sentence

"The rationale is simple: half of the interior is not covered today."

2 · The trade-off, before anyone finds it

"The trade-off is fewer visits in the south for six months. I would rather say it now than in November."

3 · The ask, with a date

"If you approve the material by the fifteenth, we will start in October. That is what I need today."

Rationale → trade-off → the ask, with a date. A decision with no date is not a decision.

Dialogue

Ten Minutes on the Agenda

N
Renato, you have ten minutes. I have read your note, so please start with the reason, not with the background.
R
The rationale is simple. Half of the interior of Sao Paulo is not covered today, and that is where our largest accounts are.
N
That is a commercial problem. What do you actually need from medical?
R
One thing only. If your team approves the training material by the fifteenth, we will start in October.
N
And if we do not? That is our constraint: we have three submissions that month.
R
Then the contingency is to start with two clinics instead of ten. I would rather show you that now than discover it in November.
N
Good. And the trade-off? Every plan has one, and the plans that hide it come back to me later.
R
Fewer visits in the south for six months. I will tell that team myself, before it appears on anybody's slide.
N
Then you have my buy-in. Put the deadline in the decision log and I will not push back on the date again.
Comprehension

About Nadia

1. What does she ask him to start with, and why?
The reason, not the background — she has already read his note.
2. What is her constraint that month?
Her team has three submissions in the same month.
3. What does she say about plans that hide the trade-off?
They come back to her later.
4. What does she ask him to do at the end?
Put the deadline in the decision log — and then she will stop pushing back on the date.

Listening

Why Most Proposals Die

Forty of these meetings a quarter, from the other side of the table. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What kind of ideas die in these meetings, according to him?
Not the weak ideas — the ideas that arrive with no reason attached to them.
2. What happens after twelve slides of context?
The room has already stopped listening by the time you ask for a decision.
3. Why should you name the trade-off yourself?
Because if you do not, somebody else in the room will — and then it sounds like you were hiding it.
4. What does he say about a decision with no date?
It is not a decision. It is a conversation that will happen again next month, with the same people.

“People in commercial teams often think medical says no because we are slow, or because we enjoy saying no.”

Listening 2

What Medical Needs to Hear

The person who has to say yes, on what makes her say it. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Why does she say medical actually says no?
Not because they are slow or enjoy it — because they cannot see the risk you have already thought about.
2. What are the three things she needs, and how long does it take?
What you want to do, what could go wrong, and what you will do if that happens — about thirty seconds.
3. Why can she not approve a plan that sounds perfect?
Because nothing is perfect — if she cannot find the weak point, she assumes you did not look for it either.
4. What is her advice about when to ask?
Never in the week of a submission. Two weeks earlier and she reads it properly; on the Friday you get the fastest possible no.
Chapter 5: Practice

Your Proposal

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "If we will approve the budget, we will start in October."
"If we approve the budget, we will start in October." (nothing after IF takes will)
2. "If the medical team not agree, we lose the quarter."
"If the medical team does not agree, we will lose the quarter."
3. "I will send the rationale when I will have the numbers."
"I will send the rationale when I have the numbers." (when follows the same rule as if)
4. "If we do not decide today, we would lose the window."
"If we do not decide today, we will lose the window." (this is real and possible, so it takes will)
