The Whole Board

Nine lessons of pieces. Today they are one meeting, from the headline to the last question you did not see coming.

Chapter 1: First Words

Four Sentences, Four Lessons

One comparison with than. One recommendation with should. One opening with the number of points. One sentence that buys you four seconds.

Say all four, out loud, one after the other. Ten lessons ago none of them existed.

Today’s Goal

Nothing New. Everything Together.

1. The Words

a board member, a stakeholder, a fiscal year, a projection, a takeaway, a range...

2. Five Structures

comparatives, should and must not, verb + -ing or to, past continuous, going to

3. Your Answer

one board, ten minutes, four interruptions, one decision asked for

Nothing here is new. That is the point of today: the difference between knowing five things and being able to use them in the same ten minutes.

One Question

What Is Your Headline?

One sentence about your real quarter. The one sentence you want a board member to repeat in the corridor afterwards.

One sentence. Not two, and not a paragraph with a full stop in the middle.

Chapter 3: The Code

Five Structures, One Presentation

Nothing new. Everything at once.

Grammar Review

Which Lesson Is Each One From?

"Coverage is lower than the target, and the gap is the widest in the south."

"We should hold the price, and we must not buy the gap with a discount."

"We were visiting the second wave of workshops when the supplier stopped."

"I recommend giving a range, and we are going to hold the price through the year."

Every one of these came from a different lesson, and the last one carries two at once. Name the structure and say what job it is doing in the sentence.

Where in the meetingStructureExample
The numberscomparatives and superlativesCoverage is lower than the target.
The recommendationshould · must not · have toWe should hold the price. We must not discount.
What happenedpast simple, and past continuous for the backgroundWe were visiting when the supplier stopped.
Where we are nowpresent continuousThe rollout is running a week behind.
What comes nextgoing toWe are going to hold the price.
Running the roomverb + -ing or verb + toI recommend giving a range. I plan to finish early.
The interruptionthe time-buying sentences"Let me make sure I have that right."

In one line: each part of a board meeting has its own tense, and you have had all of them for a while.

The Page That Goes Out on the Friday

Board pack -- the page that goes out on the Friday before

Headline. Coverage in the workshop segment is at nineteen per cent against a target of twenty-two, and the recommendation on the table has not changed: the budget should stay where it is, and we must not buy the gap with a discount. The takeaway, if you remember nothing else from this page, is that we are three points behind and we are behind on purpose.

What happened. We launched the coverage programme in March and the first quarter on quarter movement was slower than the projection, because the first visit to a new workshop is the expensive one and we underestimated how many visits it takes. What is happening now. The rollout is running about a week behind, the bottleneck is the label supplier, and we are splitting that order from Thursday. Two regions are ahead of plan and the south is not.

What comes next, and what we need. We are going to hold the price through the fiscal year and we are not going to run the trade fair. The projection for January is a range rather than a single number, between twenty-two and twenty-five per cent, because the second wave of visits has not started yet. From this board we have to have the budget signed before the fifteenth. We would like a second field team and we do not have to have it this year. Everything on this page is open to discussion except one line, and it is the discount.

Listening 1 · A Director

Three Points and One Decision

A director opening a quarterly board. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is his headline, and why does he say he gives it first?
Coverage is at nineteen against a target of twenty-two, three points behind and behind on purpose. He gives it first because he would rather the board heard it from him.
2. What reason does he give for the slower quarter on quarter movement?
The first visit to a new workshop is the expensive one, and they underestimated the number of visits.
3. What is the one decision he asks for, and what is the deadline?
The budget, and the deadline is the fifteenth.

Listening 2 · A Board Member

What a Board Remembers

Twenty years of sitting on boards, in three observations. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is the first thing she says the memorable presentations have, and how does she test it?
A headline. She tests it by what a board member repeats in the corridor afterwards.
2. Why does she prefer a range to a single number?
Because a single number for a period that has not happened is either luck or a guess. She would rather be told between twenty-two and twenty-five than find out in February that the number was decoration.
3. What is the third thing, and what is her line about it?
Somebody who flags the bad news themselves, early, in their own words. A board can work with a problem it heard about in October, and cannot work with the same problem in February.
