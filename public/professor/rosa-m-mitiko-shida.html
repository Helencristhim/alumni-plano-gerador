A dor é UMA e ela nomeou com precisão

Perguntada sobre o que resolveria se pudesse resolver uma coisa só, a Rosa respondeu sem hesitar: “parar de… quando você começa a falar e aí você não sabe continuar, você não acha aquela palavra para continuar uma conversa e não vem”. Não é falta de base — é vocabulário ATIVO que não chega na hora. Ela entende o e-mail, entende o treinamento, lê sem dificuldade; o que falha é a palavra na boca no meio da frase.

O programa inteiro atende a isso: 10 a 12 expressões novas por aula, sempre do mundo dela (aviação comercial, carteira de clientes, reunião regional), sempre com gap-fill com banco de palavras e sempre recicladas no warm-up da aula seguinte. A palavra só vira ativa quando volta.

Calibragem: o inglês de lazer flui, o de trabalho trava

(a) A inibição é contextual, não de temperamento. Ela disse: “quando você viaja, é mais tranquilo… quando você vai a trabalho, aí você dá aquele frio na barriga de alguém perguntar alguma coisa, aí você fica: será que eu estou preparada?” Consequência prática: construa o role-play do menos exposto para o mais exposto. E-mail e apresentação breve primeiro; reunião ao vivo com várias pessoas por último. Nunca abra uma aula com a simulação de maior pressão.

(b) Ela precisa de roteiro visível. “Eu sou mais aquela estruturada… se eu não tenho aquela coisa de um encaminhamento, eu fico assim: e agora, o que eu tenho que fazer?” e “eu preciso daquela coisa: faz isso”. Anuncie a agenda no início e nomeie cada transição (now we move to…). Estrutura, para ela, é escudo emocional — não preferência cognitiva.

(c) Silêncio de até 5 segundos é permitido. Histórico de vergonha em turma na infância (“se eu falar eu vou errar… acho que fiquei traumatizada”). Não complete a frase dela antes de ela tentar; valide antes de corrigir (Good — and we can also say it this way:). ZERO português na tela do aluno (REGRA 13, B1).

Programa de 48 aulas. A geração alterna os dois formatos por número de aula: aula ímpar gira em torno de diálogo e role-play, aula par em torno de um texto autêntico com gist e true/false — atendendo ao pedido dela por leitura dirigida sem transformar o curso inteiro em leitura. Cada aula acrescenta 10 a 12 expressões novas, e nenhuma expressão volta como conteúdo novo (REGRA 22). Os blocos seguintes são detalhados ao final de cada bloco de 12.

Metodologia

Vocabulário ativo, não passivo

A dor dela é a palavra que não chega. Cada expressão aparece quatro vezes na mesma aula: reveal card, gap-fill com banco, produção guiada e survival card — e volta no warm-up da aula seguinte.

Agenda visível, sempre

Anuncie os três blocos da aula no início e nomeie cada transição. Ela declarou que trava sem encaminhamento: previsibilidade aqui é método, não formalidade.

Do menos exposto ao mais exposto

Guiado > semi-livre > livre, sempre nessa ordem. A reunião ao vivo é o último degrau da aula, nunca o primeiro — a inibição dela é específica do contexto de trabalho.

Listening em duas velocidades

O incômodo declarado é a velocidade nativa em contexto corporativo. Perguntas ANTES do play (REGRA 2.1), 0.75x na primeira escuta e 1x na segunda, quantas repetições ela quiser.

Valide antes de corrigir

Tolerância ao erro média e oscilante (“depende do dia”), com histórico de vergonha em turma. Reformule sem expor: Good — and we can also say it this way:. Silêncio de até 5 segundos é dela.

Contexto real, sempre o dela

Aviação comercial, carteira de clientes, regionalização da América do Sul, convenção e treinamento global. Nada de exemplo genérico de escritório: ela reconhece a diferença na hora.

Estrutura sugerida da aula (60 min)

  • 5 min — O fio: callback do vocabulário da aula anterior + warm-up direto ao tema, sem saudação scriptada
  • 12 min — As expressões da aula: reveal cards + pronúncia + gap-fill com banco de palavras
  • 10 min — Language focus: descoberta da estrutura dentro de frase de reunião, e-mail ou convenção
  • 15 min — Input autêntico: diálogo line-by-line (aula ímpar) ou texto com gist e true/false (aula par) + dois listenings

CCQs da Aula 1 (Concept Checking Questions)

  • Account manager — Do I sell to one person once, or do I look after a company over time? (I look after a company over time.)
  • Client portfolio — Is it one client, or the whole group I am responsible for? (The whole group.)
  • To report to — Is that person above me or below me? (Above — my direct manager.)
  • Corporate client — A family booking a holiday, or a company with a contract? (A company with a contract.)
  • To roll out — Does it all happen on one day? (No — step by step, over time.)
  • Workload — Is it how difficult the work is, or how much of it there is? (How much of it there is.)
  • To keep up with — Am I ahead of it, or at the same speed? (At the same speed as something that keeps moving.)
  • “I manage thirty corporate accounts.” — Only today, or every month? (Every month — it is my role, so present simple.)
  • “The company is rolling out a regional structure.” — Is it finished? (No — in progress, so present continuous.)
  • “I am spending more time with clients this quarter.” — Permanent or temporary? (Temporary — that is why it is continuous.)
  • “I belong to the commercial area.” — Why not I am belonging? (Because belong describes a state, not an action — state verbs stay simple.)
  • “She manages the Santiago office.” — Why the -s? (Third person singular in the present simple. It disappears in the continuous: she is managing.)

She works for an American airline, in the commercial area — which means sales and marketing together, not one or the other. Her title is account manager, and she is in charge of the corporate clients in her region: companies with a contract, not families booking a holiday.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple and Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

Two tenses carry your whole professional description. One says who you are; the other says what is moving around you this year.

FormWhat it saysExample
Present simple
work · manage · report		Your role, your routine and what is generally true: every week, usually, twice a day, never."I work for an American airline."
"I manage thirty corporate accounts."
Present continuous
am rolling out · am preparing		In progress now, temporary and still changing: at the moment, this year, this quarter, still."We are rolling out a new structure."
"At the moment I am preparing the numbers."
Negative and questionThe simple borrows do / does; the continuous already has am / is / are and needs nothing else."I do not handle private travelers."
"Are you joining the call on Thursday?"
Third person
he · she · it		In the present simple the third person adds -s. It disappears the moment you use the continuous."She manages the Santiago office."
"She is managing two teams this quarter."
The trap: state verbsbe, have, know, belong, understand, mean describe a state, not an action. They stay simple even when you mean right now."I know most of these clients."
NOT "I am knowing most of these clients."

A quick test that never fails: if you can put every week in front of the sentence and it still makes sense, use the simple. If only right now or this year fits, use the continuous.

Quiz

You are on the first regional call with colleagues from four countries. Choose the best thing to say.

A "I am working in the commercial area since many years."
B "I am Rosa, and I am in charge of the corporate accounts in this region."
C "I am the responsible of the corporate accounts."
A colleague asks what your week normally looks like. You answer:
A "I usually spend one week in the city and one week on the road."
B "I am usually spending one week in the city and one week on the road."
C "Usually I spending one week in the city."
You want to say that the new structure is not finished yet. You say:
A "We roll out the new structure at the moment."
B "We are still rolling out the new structure."
C "We are still roll out the new structure."

A "Yes, yes." (and you write nothing down)
B "Sorry, could you say that again, a little more slowly?"
C "I am not understanding nothing."

B "I think maybe, I am not sure, it depends." (and you stop there)
C "The accounts are not moving since this quarter."

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

She has worked in travel and aviation for more than twenty years, and she has been with this company since 2014. She did not plan any of it. She studied business administration, went into tourism because a friend called her about a job, and never left the industry again.

A lot has changed in those twenty years. Airlines have merged and disappeared. The corporate contract has moved from paper to a system that is renegotiated every quarter. And this year the company has announced the biggest change of all: four countries, one regional office.

Her own year has been full. She has attended two colleague training sessions in English, she has signed eleven new contracts so far, and last month she has just passed a milestone she never expected: one hundred active corporate accounts. Some things are still open. She has not met the Bogota team face to face yet, and she has not decided which client she is going to talk about at the convention. Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Present Perfect

GRAMMAR

The tense that connects what you did to who you are today. In Portuguese the same idea often takes the present. In English it never does.

FormWhat it saysExample
have / has + third form
worked · sent · met · taken		The past, connected to now. You do not say when it happened, because that is not the point."I have worked here for twelve years."
"She has taken over the Chile accounts."
for · sincefor + how long the period is. since + the point where it started. Both mean it is still true today."for twenty years" · "for two months"
"since 2014" · "since March"
just · already · yetjust = a moment ago. already = sooner than expected. yet = still missing, and it goes at the end of the sentence."They have just announced it."
"I have already sent it."
"I have not decided yet."
Negative and questionThe auxiliary is already in the sentence. For a question you move it to the front; for a negative you add not to it."I have not met them yet."
"Have you signed the contract?"
The trapPortuguese keeps the present with desde and há. English never does. And after have comes the third form, not the past: take → took → taken.NOT "I work here since 2014."
NOT "She has took over the accounts."

One question decides everything: is the period closed, or still open today? If it is still open, use have or has plus the third form.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are at the regional convention and at your desk the week before it. Choose the best thing to say.

Somebody asks how long you have been in the industry. The best answer is:
A "I am in this industry since more than twenty years."
B "I have been in this industry for more than twenty years."
C "I work in this industry since twenty years."
Your manager asks whether the proposal is with the client. It is. You say:
A "Yes, I have already sent it."
B "Yes, I have sent it yet."
C "Yes, I am sending it already."
A colleague asks whether you have met the Bogota team. You have not. You say:
A "No, I have yet not met them."
B "No, I did not meet them yet."
C "Not yet — we meet at the convention in November."
A client asks what the company has done this year. The clearest answer is:
A "We have opened a regional office and launched two new routes so far."
B "We are opening a regional office since this year."
C "We have opened a regional office yet."
You want to say that somebody else started doing a job you used to do. You say:
A "She has took over the Chile accounts."
B "She has taken over the Chile accounts."
C "She has take over the Chile accounts."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer, and nobody is scoring it.

Choose one client you have worked with for a long time. In about ninety seconds, say how long you have worked together, two things you have done for them, one difficult moment you have solved, and why you think they have stayed. Use for and since at least once each, and try to finish with one sentence using not yet.
