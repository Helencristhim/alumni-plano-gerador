Jornada de Transformação

Forças

  • Compreensão acima da produção. "Eu entendo muito mais do que eu falo": o ouvido já trabalha, e o material parte disso

Pontos de Melhoria

  • Produção espontânea. Entende, mas ainda não reage nem se expressa com a mesma naturalidade

  • Feedback do exercício. Reclamou que, no matching da plataforma antiga, não via qual resposta tinha errado. Aqui cada linha mostra certo ou errado: vale mostrar isso a ele na primeira aula

Metodologia

1. Da compreensão para a reação

Ele já entende. Cada aula treina o passo seguinte: reagir, concordar, discordar, recusar, contar, sempre com uma resposta curta antes da resposta longa.

2. Expressões, não listas

Doze palavras e expressões por aula, quase sempre as que um nativo usa sem pensar (fair enough, I see your point, take a rain check), sempre dentro de uma situação, nunca em lista solta.

3. Um ponto de B2 por aula

Descoberto em frases (discovery), nunca regra solta: respostas curtas com auxiliar, comparativos com modificadores, perguntas indiretas, gerúndio x infinitivo, discurso indireto, tempos narrativos, would rather, used to, wish, passiva de relato.

4. O que ele já consome vira aula

Filme com legenda, desenho em inglês, letra de música, ficção, podcast: os temas que ele trouxe na consultoria são conteúdo, e os listenings trazem sotaques variados (americano, britânico, australiano, indiano).

5. Prática fora da aula, com direção

Na consultoria ele pediu "algo mais dinâmico" além dos exercícios. Os Complementares trazem filme, talk e podcast ligados ao tema, cada um com uma tarefa curta. Zero português na tela (B2).

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Short Responses -- So Do I, Neither Have I

GRAMMAR

To react, English does not repeat the main verb. It borrows the auxiliary from the first sentence: do, does, did, have, can, will, am, is, are.

JobFormExample
Agree with a positiveSo + auxiliary + I“I love winter.” — “So do I.”
Agree with a negativeNeither + auxiliary + I“I haven’t been there.” — “Neither have I.”
The informal wayI do too / I don’t either / Me too / Me neither“I don’t eat meat.” — “I don’t either.”
Disagreesubject + the opposite auxiliary“I can’t skate.” — “I can!”
Show interestan echo question with the same auxiliary“I grew up in Toronto.” — “Did you?”
The trapthe auxiliary follows the tense of the first sentenceNot “I went there.” — “So do I.” Say So did I.

Never: I went there. -- So do I. · Neither I have · I don’t like it. -- Me too. · I get along my cousin

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Cauê wants a certificate for his career, so he is weighing up the options. The TOEFL is by far the most common test for universities in the United States, but for many employers the TOEIC is much more familiar.

The online test is far more affordable than the others, and it is not nearly as long. It is a worthwhile first step to check his level. But most results are valid for only two years, and then they expire.

His plan: first a mock test, then a decision. “The more I know the format, the calmer I’ll feel,” he says.

A Because it shows that the difference is big.
B Because far makes the comparison more polite in formal writing.

2. What is Cauê’s first step?
A Booking the TOEFL and the IELTS on the same day, so he can compare the results.
B A mock test.
C Asking his employer which certificate the company will pay for this year.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “The more I know the format, more calm I feel.”
B “More I know the format, the more calmer I feel about the test.”
C “The more I know the format, the calmer I feel.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Comparatives and Superlatives -- Saying How Much

GRAMMAR

At B2, it is not enough to say which is bigger. Say how much bigger: far, much, a lot for a big difference; slightly, a bit for a small one; by far the for the clear winner.

JobFormExample
A big differencefar / much / a lot + comparativefar cheaper, much more affordable
A small differenceslightly / a bit + comparativeslightly longer, a bit easier
The clear winnerby far + the + superlativeby far the most common test
A big difference, negativenot nearly as + adjective + asisn’t nearly as long as the TOEFL
Two things change togetherthe + comparative ..., the + comparative ...The more you practice, the calmer you feel.
The trapvery does not go with a comparativeNot very cheaper or more cheaper. Say much cheaper.

Never: very cheaper · more cheaper · the most easy · the more you practice, more you learn

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Indirect Questions -- Could You Tell Me Where It Is?

GRAMMAR

A direct question can sound abrupt to a stranger. Start with could you tell me, do you know or I was wondering, and the rest of the question takes the order of a normal sentence, with no do, does or did.

JobFormExample
Open politelyCould you tell me / Do you know / Can you tell meCould you tell me where ...
Wh- questionsopening + wh- word + subject + verbwhere the restroom is (not where is the restroom)
Yes / no questionsopening + if / whether + subject + verbDo you know if this bus goes downtown?
No do / does / didthe second part has no auxiliaryhow much it costs (not how much does it cost)
Extra politeI was wondering if / whether ... (no question mark)I was wondering whether you have a table.
The trapquestion order after the openingNot could you tell me where is it. Say where it is.

Never: Could you tell me where is it? · Do you know what time does it open? · Can you tell me how much does it cost? · Let’s split up the bill
