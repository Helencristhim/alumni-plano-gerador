More Than It Was Good

Warm-up

Ten Days in October. Why That Trip?

Not where you are going. Why you are going, and who you are going with.

The Target

One Answer, Three Moves

1. The answer. Short, and true.

2. The reason, or the result. This is the sentence you never say.

3. One question back, so the other person keeps talking.

Diagnostic

How Was Your Weekend?

Somebody you have just met asks you: “So, how was your weekend?”

Answer, and then keep going. Do not stop until you have said three sentences.

Chapter 3: The Code

The Sentence That Buys You Time

A reason, a result, and the word Brazilians always translate wrong

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Answers Share?

"It was good, because we finally got out of the city."

"The beach was crowded, so we went back on Tuesday."

"I am travelling with my mother, and that is why this trip is different."

"We arrived at midnight because of the layover."

The red words open the reason. The blue words open the result. And look at what comes after each one: sometimes a whole sentence, sometimes only a noun.

JobFormExample
Give the reasonbecause + subject + verbIt was good, because we got out of the city.
Give the reason in two wordsbecause of + noun onlyWe arrived late because of the layover.
Give the resultreason, so + resultThe beach was crowded, so we left.
Point at the resultand that is why + resultI speak no Spanish, and that is why I chose Florida.
Answer a direct Why?Because + full sentence“Why October?” — “Because my mother is free then.”
The order can flipBecause + reason, + resultBecause it was crowded, we left early.
The trapnever for that, never because of + sentenceNot for that we left. Say that is why we left.

In one line: because opens a reason, so opens a result, and because of is followed by a noun and nothing else.

Day One, by the Pool

G
Is this chair free? Everything down by the water is taken.
V
It is free, please. It is very crowded today.
G
It always is in October. First time in Miami?
V
Second time, but the first time with my mother. We arrived at midnight, because we had a long layover in Panama.
G
Rough start. And what have the two of you got planned?
V
Nothing, and that is why I like this trip. We want to get around by bus, hang out in the morning, and see what the locals do.
G
Then take the bus down to Coconut Grove. It is quiet, it is green, and it is worth it. Just a heads-up: the last bus back is earlier than you think.
V
Thank you. That is the kind of thing I never find on the internet, so I am writing it down. And you, are you here on holiday too?
Comprehension

About Greg

1. Why does Greg ask for the chair, and what does he say about October in Miami?
Everything down by the water is taken, and he says it is always crowded in October.
2. Which place does he suggest, how does he say to go there, and what three things does he say about it?
Coconut Grove, by bus. He says it is quiet, it is green, and it is worth it.
3. What is the heads-up he gives her at the end?
The last bus back is earlier than she thinks.

The Second Sentence

A podcast host on why conversations die. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. She says the answer “it was good” is correct and polite. So what is wrong with it?
It closes the door. The other person has nothing to hold on to, so they change the subject.
2. What does she say the fix is, and what does she say it is not?
The fix is one more sentence, and the easiest one is a reason. It is not more vocabulary.
3. After the second sentence, what does the other person have?
Two doors to walk through — the city and November.

Greg Asks Again

Ask Before You Tell

Scenario: You are standing in the breakfast queue next to somebody you have never seen. Ask two questions about their week here, before you say one word about your own trip — then connect their answer to yours in a single sentence.

Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

Sixty Seconds, No Notes

Scenario: Somebody at the airport gate asks how the ten days were. Tell them: what the highlight was and why, one thing that was not worth it and why, one thing you ended up doing without planning it, and one question for them.

No keywords, no notes, sixty seconds. Every answer needs a second sentence.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I never stop at one sentence. I always add a reason or a result.
I use because with a full sentence, and because of with a noun only.
I say that is why, and I never say for that.
I can hand a question back to a stranger instead of ending the conversation.
