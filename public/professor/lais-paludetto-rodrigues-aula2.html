Chapter 3: The Code

Done, or Done When?

One question decides it: is there a date in the sentence?

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I have sent the medical form."

"I sent the medical form on Monday."

"They have not received my passport copy yet."

"The committee confirmed my host family last week."

Two of them tell you when. Two of them do not. Which is which?

FormUseExample
Present perfect
have / has + participle		The result matters now. No date in the sentence, and no date needed.I have sent the form.
They have confirmed the family.
Past simple
sent / confirmed / went		A finished moment with a known time: yesterday, last week, in June, on Monday.I sent it on Monday.
They confirmed it last week.
The four signalsalready (sooner than expected), yet (in questions and negatives), just (a moment ago), never / ever (in your whole life) — all four pull the sentence to the present perfect.
The trapA date and the present perfect never share a sentence. "I have sent it yesterday" does not exist. Choose one: I have sent it or I sent it yesterday.

One question, two answers: is there a date? If yes, past simple. If no, present perfect.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I have sent the form yesterday."
"I sent the form yesterday."
"They have took my passport copy."
"They have taken my passport copy."
"My documents didn't arrived on time."
"My documents didn't arrive on time."

A closed date never sits next to have. After have comes the participle, not the past. And after didn't the verb goes back to its base form, because did already carries the past.

Reading

Read for the Main Idea

Welcome to the Burnaby International Programme

Dear Lais, welcome. We received your application in June and the placement committee confirmed your host family last week, so the hardest part is already behind you. This letter tells you what is still open and what happens in your first days here.

Paperwork. We have already received your school letter and your passport copy. We have not received the medical form yet, and that one is mandatory before the first day of class. Please also fill out the emergency contact sheet, which is attached to this email. Last year several students sent it in September and missed the deadline for the school insurance.

Orientation. Orientation is on the first Monday of the term, at nine, in the main hall. You will meet the international counsellor, you will get your timetable and your student card, and somebody from the buddy programme will show you the facilities. It usually finishes around one o'clock.

Getting around. Most of our students buy a transit pass in the first week, because the bus is the fastest way to school from your neighbourhood. You can sign up for it online, but you have to do it in person the first time, with your student card. Bring a jacket. It has rained every September since we opened this programme.

Quick Fire

One Situation at a Time

1 / 6

The office asks whether you have sent the medical form. You sent it on Monday. Answer them.

  • I've already sent it.
  • I sent it on Monday.
  • Did it arrive?

You are at the counter and you want the transit pass. Say what you want and what you have with you.

  • I'd like to get a transit pass, please.
  • I've brought my student card.
  • Is this the right desk?

Somebody asks if you have ever lived abroad before. Answer honestly and add one detail.

  • I've never lived abroad before.
  • I travelled to Europe with my mum last year.
  • This is my first time.

You missed a deadline by one day. Explain what happened without making a drama of it.

  • I'm sorry, I sent it yesterday.
  • I didn't see the email until Friday.
  • Is it still possible to hand it in?

The counsellor asks how long you have been studying English. Answer and give one fact about it.

  • I've studied English since I was four.
  • I studied at a language school for two years.
  • My school in Brazil is bilingual.

A form asks for something you do not understand. Ask the person at the desk for help.

  • Sorry, what does this line mean?
  • Do I have to fill this part out?
  • Can you show me an example?
