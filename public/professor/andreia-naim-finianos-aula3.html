Chapter 3: Then and Now

What the Date Does to the Verb

Two tenses, one decision

Grammar Discovery

Two Have, Two Do Not

"We signed the partnership agreement in March."

"We have signed four agreements this year."

"I went to Shanghai last year and the deal fell through."

"I have been to Shanghai twice, and I would go again tomorrow."

Two of the blue expressions name a moment that is over. Two of them do not. The red verb follows — every time, with no exceptions.

JobFormExample
A moment that is overpast simpleWe signed it in March.
A period still runninghave / has + participleWe have signed four this year.
Experience, no moment namedhave / has + participleI have been to Shanghai twice.
The moment arrivesswitch to the past simpleI went there last year.
Words that close the periodin 2024, last year, in March, ago, whenall of them force the past simple
Words that keep it openthis year, so far, ever, never, twice, yetall of them keep have
The trapnever both at oncenot I have gone to the US last year

In one line: the moment you name the moment, the verb goes to the past. A story usually needs both, one sentence after the other.

Dialogue

The Year, in Four Minutes

C
Before we start on the numbers, tell me about last year. How was it, honestly?
A
Honestly, it started badly. We signed our first partnership agreement in March, and by June it had almost fallen through.
C
That is not the answer I expected. What happened?
A
Our contact left the company. The person who replaced him backed out of half of what we had agreed. It was a real setback, and for two months I thought we had lost the year.
C
And what did you do?
A
Something very boring. We followed up every week, same day, short message, no pressure. Eventually he called. That call was the breakthrough, and it had nothing to do with the product.
C
So the year ended well after all.
A
It did. We have signed three more agreements since then, and I run every one of them the same way now. I learned that the hard way. And you, what did last year look like on your side?
Comprehension

About the Conversation

1. What does Chen ask for before the numbers, and how does he ask for it?
He asks about last year, and he asks for it honestly — he says "how was it, honestly?" before anything technical.
2. What is Chen’s reaction to the first answer?
He says that is not the answer he expected, and then he asks what happened.
3. Two sentences in this conversation use a named month or a named time. Which ones, and what tense do they use?
"We signed our first partnership agreement in March" and "by June it had almost fallen through". Both use the past, because March and June are moments that are over.

Real Document

The Page They Read Before They Meet You

OUR STORY
tax and finance software · company timeline
2014first office opens · payroll module only
2019the pivot · from small firms to large groups
2022acquisition of a compliance product
2024first partnership agreement signed abroad
To datefour agreements signed · three markets · no office overseas
The line the page does not print is the one between 2024 and today. The first agreement almost fell through, the contact left, and the person who replaced him backed out of half of it. It took eight weeks of following up before anybody called back. We have signed three more since, and we run all of them the way we learned to run that one.
1. Four lines of this timeline take one tense and one line takes another. Which line is different, and why?
The "to date" line. Every other line names a year, so it takes the past simple. "To date" means up to now, so the period is still open and it takes have plus the participle.
2. What does the paragraph below the timeline say the page does not print?
The line between 2024 and today: that the first agreement almost fell through, the contact left, and his replacement backed out of half of it.
3. Why would anybody put that paragraph in front of a new partner at all?
Because a timeline with no problem in it sounds like a brochure. The setback and what was done about it are the part that shows how the company behaves when something goes wrong.

The Shape

Four Sentences, One Story

1 · WHERE WE WERE — past simple, with the date

"We signed our first partnership agreement in March."

2 · WHAT WENT WRONG — past simple, and do not skip it

"By June it had almost fallen through. Our contact left and his replacement backed out."

3 · WHAT WE DID — past simple, and make it concrete

"We followed up every week, same day, for two months. Eventually he called."

4 · WHAT IS TRUE NOW — present perfect, and this is the point

"We have signed three more since then, and I run every one of them the same way now."

Now tell one real story from your own year in exactly these four sentences. Then tell it again in twenty seconds, and again in one minute.
