Lesson 9 · What They Said

What They Said

You come off a call. Somebody who was not on it has to decide something based on what you say next. Three sentences, three verbs, no adjectives.

Chapter 1: First Words

Tell Me About a Real Call

First, from last week: one sentence as it would appear in the minutes, and the same thing as you would say it at a dinner.

Now the new one. Think of a difficult customer call you had this month. Tell me what the customer said — not how he sounded, what he said.

Today’s Goal

Three Verbs, No Adjectives

1. The Words

to mention, to claim, to deny, to imply, to insist, to point out, on record...

2. One Structure

he said he would pay — what happens to the verb when you repeat it

3. The Line

what he said, and what you think he meant, in separate sentences

The person you are reporting to has to be able to tell which part came from the customer and which part came from you. That is not politeness. It is the difference between a useful report and a rumour.

One Question

Three Different Things

Take that same call and split it into three: something the customer stated as a fact, something he claimed but you have not checked, and something he never said but clearly meant.

Which of the three would you put in an email, and which would you only say out loud?

Chapter 3: The Code

One Step Back

What happens to the verb when you repeat it

Grammar Discovery

What Happened to Every Verb?

"I will pay after the second harvest." → He said he would pay after the second harvest.

"I am waiting for the co-operative." → He told me he was waiting for the co-operative.

"Can you extend the deadline?" → He asked if we could extend the deadline.

"Send the statement again." → He asked me to send the statement again.

On the left is what he actually said, in green. On the right is how you repeat it, in red. Something happens to every single verb, and something else happens to the question mark.

He saidYou reportExample
present simplepast simple"I pay after the harvest" → He said he paid after the harvest.
am / is / arewas / were"I am waiting" → He told me he was waiting.
willwould"I will pay" → He said he would pay.
cancould"We can wait" → He said they could wait.
a yes-or-no questionasked if ... , no inversion"Can you extend it?" → He asked if we could extend it.
a wh- questionthe wh- word, then normal order"What is the problem?" → He asked what the problem was.
an order or a requestasked / told somebody to + verb"Send it again" → He asked me to send it again.

In one line: everything takes one step back, and a reported question stops being a question.

Dialogue

Ten Minutes After the Call

E
You spoke to him this morning. Before anything else: what did he actually say?
L
He said he would pay after the second harvest, and he told me he was waiting for the co-operative to settle with him first.
E
Good. Did he say anything about the other supplier?
L
He denied having bought from anybody else this season. He also claimed the last payment had already left his bank, and I have not seen it.
E
So one of those is a fact and one is not. Keep them apart when you write the note. Did he ask for anything?
L
He asked if we could extend the deadline to May, and he asked me to send the statement again. He also asked what the interest would be.
E
And what do you think? Not what he said. What you think.
L
Separately, and this is mine, not his: he insisted the delay was the weather, three times. I think he is short of cash. He never said that. He implied it.
E
That is the most useful sentence you have said all week. Put the first part in the file and tell me the second part out loud, like you just did.
L
Understood. And he asked me to pass along that he wants to talk to you directly before April. Do you want me to set that up?
Comprehension

About Erin

1. What is the first thing Erin asks for, and what does she rule out?
She asks what he actually said, before anything else. She rules out everything that is not the customer's own words.
2. Erin notices that two pieces of information are not the same kind. What does she say about them?
That one of them is a fact and one is not, and that they have to be kept apart when the note is written.
3. At the end, Erin splits the report in two. Which part goes where, and why would she do that?
The reported words go in the file; the interpretation is told to her out loud. The file has to hold what can be attributed, and the judgement belongs to the person who made it.

Listening 1 · The Editor

Stated, Claimed, or Implied

A newsroom editor on the rule they use before printing what anybody said. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she say is the gap that almost every complaint comes from?
The gap between what somebody said and what you understood.
2. What are the three things she says you have to be able to distinguish?
Whether the person stated it as a fact, claimed it (said it, not checked), or implied it (never said it, and you decided what they meant).
3. What does she say "off the record" does and does not mean, and what happens if you get it wrong?
It does not mean secret or unusable. It means you may know it and may not attribute it. If you pass it along with their name on it, you will not get a second conversation with that person or anybody they talk to.

Listening 2 · The Credit Manager

The Mood Instead of the Words

A credit manager on the mistake he made for five years. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What was he reporting for the first five years, and why did it not work?
He reported the mood: that the customer was positive, or sounded worried. None of that survives contact with a decision.
2. What did his manager actually need? Give the three examples he lists.
That the customer said he would pay after the second harvest, that he denied having taken product from another supplier, and that he asked whether the deadline would be extended. Three sentences, three verbs, no adjectives.
3. What is the second thing he learned, and what goes wrong if he ignores it?
To keep what the customer said separate from what he thinks the customer meant. If both go into the same sentence, his manager cannot tell which part is the customer's and which is his.
Real Document

The Call Note That Goes Into the File

CUSTOMER CALL NOTE
overdue account · recorded the same day
ReportedHe said he would pay after the second harvest
ReportedHe told me he was waiting for the co-operative
Claimed, not verifiedThat the last payment had already left his bank
DeniedBuying from another supplier this season
RequestedAn extension to May, and a copy of the statement
Analyst viewNot stated by the customer. Possible cash shortage
Note on how to use this form: the last line is the only one that belongs to the analyst, and it is labelled so that nobody reading the file in six months mistakes it for something the customer said. Everything above it can be attributed. Everything below it is judgement, and judgement is welcome as long as it is signed.
1. Two lines look similar but are treated differently. Which are they, and what separates them?
"He said he would pay after the second harvest" is reported and can be attributed. "That the last payment had already left his bank" is labelled as claimed and not verified, because nobody has checked it.
2. Why is the last line labelled the way it is?
So that nobody reading the file in six months mistakes the analyst's judgement for something the customer said.
3. According to the note, what is the condition for judgement to be welcome in a file?
That it is signed, in other words clearly marked as belonging to the person who formed it.

Chapter 6: Your Turn

Report It, Three Times

Role-Play 1 of 3 — Guided

Erin Asks Again

Scenario: The finance director asks the same four questions: what he said, what he said about the other supplier, what he asked for, and what you think. Answer all four. Keep the last one clearly separate from the first three.

Keyword chips:

He said he would ... He denied / he claimed He asked if we could ... This is mine, not his
Role-Play 2 of 3 — Semi-free

Listen First, Report After

Scenario: A customer talks for a minute without stopping. He complains, contradicts himself once, and asks for two things in the middle of it. Say nothing while he talks. Then report the call in five sentences, and say which part you would not put in writing.

Keywords:

He mentioned He insisted He asked me to off the record
Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

Two Minutes, No Notes

Scenario: Take a real conversation you had with a customer this month and report it to somebody who was not there. What he said, what he claimed, what he asked for, and — clearly marked as yours — what you think is actually going on.

No keywords, no notes, two minutes.
