1 · What a review is for

Before you read

In the lesson you are going to read two reviews of the same movie. One is positive and the other is negative.

Three of the sentences below are true. Mark those three.

Na aula você vai ler duas resenhas do mesmo filme: uma é positiva e a outra é negativa. Antes disso, marque as três frases verdadeiras sobre para que serve uma resenha.
A review says what someone thinks about something.
That is the opinion, and it is the easiest part to write. It is also the part nobody can argue with.
A review has to tell the whole story.
A review that tells the whole story is a spoiler. Reviews say enough for you to decide, and no more.
A good review explains why.
The reason is the only part the reader can actually use. Without it, a review is just a number of stars.
You have to agree with a review for it to be useful.
You can read a bad review, see the reason, and decide that the reason does not bother you. That review just helped you.
A review usually ends by telling you what to do.
Watch it, skip it, wait for season two. That is the recommendation, and it is what the reader came for.
Reviews are always written by professionals.
Most of the reviews you read were written by people your age, on a phone, right after watching.
Opinion, reason, recommendation. Three different jobs, and the middle one is the one people remember.
Why this lesson starts here
For seventeen lessons you asked for things in English: a table, a ticket, directions. Nobody once asked you what you thought. This one does — about something you choose.

1 · What keeps a conversation going

Before you listen

Three of the sentences below are true. Mark those three.

Na aula você vai ouvir duas pessoas discordando por dois minutos, sem que nenhuma delas pare a conversa. Antes disso, marque as três frases verdadeiras sobre o que mantém uma conversa de pé.
Going quiet while you think ends your turn.
Silence is read as “I have finished”, and the other person starts talking. Three seconds of silence costs you the turn.
If you do not understand something, it is better to answer anyway.
Answering something else is how conversations go wrong quietly. Asking takes two seconds and fixes it.
Asking the other person a question keeps the conversation going.
It hands the turn back, and it buys you the whole time they take to answer.
You should only speak when you are sure your English is correct.
Nobody would say anything, ever. The two people you are about to hear make mistakes and neither of them stops.
Saying something like let me think keeps your turn while you decide.
It says “I am still talking” without saying anything else. It is the cheapest three seconds in English.
Disagreeing with someone usually ends the conversation.
It usually starts one. The two people in this lesson disagree the whole way through and neither of them leaves.
Three of these are about time: buying it, using it, and not losing it. That is what the lesson is about.

3 · Where the auxiliary goes

Find the little word that moved

In each question below, where is the word do, did, are or can?

She can help you with the deadline.

You are new here.
Same two words, other way round. This is the only difference between telling and asking here.

4 · The rule, in one line

It only swaps two things

You are new here.  →  Are you new here?
She can help you.  →  Can she help you?
You chose biology.  →  Did you choose biology?

In the first two, one word changed places. In the third, a new word appeared — and something else changed too. What?

Two things, and that is all The auxiliary goes in front of the subject. If there is no auxiliary in the sentence, do, does or did comes in to be one.

And then the main verb goes back to its plain form: did you choose, never did you chose. The past is already carried by did, and English never marks it twice.
4 · When nothing moves

The one case that stays still

Look at these two. They are both correct.

Who did you sit with?

Why does the first one have no do in it? Answer that first, out loud.

Then look at these six: three of them need no auxiliary at all. Mark those three.

Who wants to go first?
Who is the person doing the wanting. There is no other subject to put an auxiliary in front of.
What happened after that?
What is the thing that happened. Same case as who sits over there, and the reason there is no did.
Who did she talk to?
Here who is not doing the talking — she is. So the auxiliary comes back and goes in front of her.
What did you choose?
You is doing the choosing, so did is needed. Compare it with what happened and the difference is who does the action.
How many people came?
The people are the ones who came. The question word is the subject, so nothing moves.
How many people did you invite?
The people did not invite anybody — you did. As soon as there is another subject, the auxiliary is back.
Ask yourself one thing on each: is the question word the one doing the action? If it is, nothing moves. If somebody else is doing it, the auxiliary comes in.
