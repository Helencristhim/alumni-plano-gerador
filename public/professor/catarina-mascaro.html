Personagem-Jornada

De: Adolescente reservada que responde por FRAGMENTOS e nunca inicia. Depende de pergunta atrás de pergunta da professora para continuar na conversa; quando falta a palavra, recorre ao português ou pergunta como se diz. Não está diagnosticado se é timidez, falta de repertório ou pouca afinidade com os temas.

Para: Adolescente que sustenta três minutos sobre um tema conhecido: responde com frase completa, acrescenta uma razão, faz duas perguntas próprias e contorna a palavra que não sabe — em inglês, sem voltar ao português.

Promessa Transformadora

O gargalo da Catarina não é compreensão: é INICIATIVA. Por isso a mecânica central do ciclo não pode ser a professora perguntando — isso a mantém exatamente onde ela já está. Toda aula tem de dar a ela uma razão real para tomar a iniciativa: escolhas, rankings, Would you rather, quizzes, adivinhações, cartões de pergunta e missões em que ELA precisa perguntar. A rotina comunicativa de cada aula é sempre a mesma: escolher ou reagir → frase completa → razão ou detalhe → pergunta relacionada → mais um turno. Apoio lexical SEMPRE antes da produção; correção no fim, nunca no meio.

Forças

  • Participa mais em jogos, desafios e tarefas com objetivo concreto
  • Pede ajuda quando trava ("como se diz...?") — já tem a intenção, falta a fórmula em inglês
  • Compreensão acima da produção
  • Sem assunto proibido: campo temático amplo para explorar

Pontos de Atenção

  • Responde por fragmentos → devolver a frase inteira, nunca aceitar a palavra solta
  • Não inicia pergunta → a tela tem de EXIGIR isso (slide 18), não só convidar
  • Recorre ao português → as cinco frases do Survival Card são a alternativa, cobradas em toda aula
  • Desengaja em tema sem afinidade → anotar o que ela escolhe no warm-up e usar nas próximas
  • Muitas faltas no histórico → toda aula fecha um ciclo em si mesma

Does

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Last weekend was strange. On Friday I stayed home and watched two films. On Saturday my cousin called me at nine in the morning, so I woke up earlier than I wanted. Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Past simple — it happened, and it is over

Most verbs add -ed. A few become a new word. The form is the same for everybody — no S here.

Regular

stay → stayed
call → called
visit → visited
watch → watched

Irregular

meet → met
feel → felt
have → had
go → went

+ I met my friends on Saturday.
− I did not meet them on Sunday. (after did, the verb goes back to normal)
? Did you meet them? (not "Did you met")

When: yesterday · last weekend · last night · two days ago · on Saturday

The -ed has three sounds: watched (T) · called (D) · visited (a whole extra syllable).

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Two presents, two jobs

In general

Now / these days

She is working at the moment.
We are not going out these days.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

The future: it is about when you decided

Decided before you spoke → going to. Deciding while you speak → will.

Going to

I am going to study tonight.
She is going to call you.
We are not going to stay home.

Will

Your bag is heavy — I’ll help.
Okay, I’ll text you later.
I’ll have the same, thanks.

+ I am going to see my friends on Saturday.
− She is not going to come. (after "going to", the verb stays normal)
? Are you going to watch it? (not "Do you going to")

In speech you will hear gonna. Understand it — but always write going to.

Count the syllables, then choose

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. Then answer the questions.

This week was a disaster. I watched eleven episodes — too many, I know — and I did hardly any homework. My screen time was twenty-nine hours. Twenty-nine! That is too much for one week. I do not have enough hours for everything, and honestly I have plenty of things I should be doing instead. My sister gave me some advice: choose a few things and drop the rest. B Because eleven is a bigger number than twenty-nine.
What advice did her sister give?

B To choose a few things and drop the rest.
Why does she say "some advice" and not "some advices"?
A Because "advice" is uncountable — it has no plural.
B Because it was only one sentence.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

One test: can you count it one by one?

One episode, two episodes → countable. Time, homework, advice → uncountable.

Countable

episodes · friends · messages
How many?
a few · too many

Uncountable

time · homework · advice · money
How much?
a little · too much

Safe for both sides, when you are not sure:

a lot of · plenty of · some / any

✗ two advices · ✓ some advice / two pieces of advice
✗ hardly no time · ✓ hardly any time
too much / too many are not neutral — they mean it is a problem.

Say a lot of as one block. The "of" almost disappears.

Mid-Program Check-In

No new grammar. Eight tools for when you get stuck (What I mean is, Can I try again?, Let me think, Sort of, The thing is, Actually, I’m not sure but, How do you say...?) and all five tenses from lessons 1 to 7, mixed.

Stage 1.1: Tools for Getting Stuck

Vocabulary

These are not new words. They are what you say instead of stopping — or instead of Portuguese.

What I mean is — you say the same idea again, in a clearer way
"It was weird. What I mean is, nobody expected it."
Can I try again? — you want a second attempt at the same sentence
"That came out wrong. Can I try again?"
Let me think — you need a second before you answer
"Let me think. Okay — probably Friday."
Sort of — not exactly, but close enough
"Do you like it? Sort of. It depends on the day."
The thing is — you are about to give the real reason
"I’d love to. The thing is, I have a test on Monday."
Actually — what comes next is different from what people expect
"Actually, I liked the second one better."
I’m not sure, but — you answer even though you are not certain
"I’m not sure, but I think it starts at eight."
How do you say...? — you ask for the exact word you are missing
"How do you say it when somebody never gives up?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read it. Every tense from lessons 1 to 7 is in here. Then answer the questions.

Do you want the short version or the long one? Let me think. Okay. Normally I wake up at seven and my brother wakes up at six. Last weekend, though, I stayed home and watched nine episodes, which was too many. The thing is, I have a test on Monday and I did hardly any homework. So right now I am sitting here with three chapters to read, and these days I am sleeping about six hours. Next weekend I am going to do it differently — two episodes a day, homework first. Actually, no. Can I try again? One episode a day. That is more realistic than two.
Why "stayed" and "watched" but "am sitting"?
A The first two are finished; the third is happening now.
B Because "sit" is an irregular verb.
What does "Can I try again?" do at the end?
A It asks the reader a question.
B It repairs the plan she just said — she changes two episodes to one.
Why "too many episodes" and not "too much episodes"?
A Episodes can be counted one by one.
B Because nine is a big number.

Grammar
