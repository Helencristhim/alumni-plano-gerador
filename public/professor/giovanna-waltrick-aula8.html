Chapter 3: The Code

The Scene and the Interruption

One tense for what was going on, another for what cut through it

Discover the Rule

What Are the Two Verbs Doing in Each Sentence?

I was waiting at the gate when they called my name.
The officer was checking my passport when I realized I had the wrong form.
We were boarding when the storm started.
While I was standing in the queue, a crowd was forming behind me.
Click to reveal the rule
Each sentence has two layers. The past continuous (was / were + verb-ing) is the scene: something long, already going on. The past simple is the short event that cuts through it. That is why when usually comes before the short one (when they called my name) and while before the long one (while I was standing). Look at the last sentence: two continuous verbs, and no interruption at all — that is two things happening at the same time, which is how you paint a whole scene.

What Is Actually Happening During a Delay

What Is Actually Happening During a Delay

A delay almost never has one cause, and this is why nobody at the gate can give you a straight answer. While you are sitting there, three or four different teams are working on the same aircraft at once, and each of them is waiting for one of the others.

Here is a normal example. The plane arrived late from another city, so the cleaning team started late. While the cleaning team was still working, the crew reached the end of the hours they are legally allowed to work, and the airline had to find another crew. Meanwhile the passengers were sitting at the gate, watching a screen that still said forty minutes.

That screen is the reason people get angry, and it is not a lie. It is the best estimate at that exact minute, and the minute keeps changing. The person at the desk usually knows the same thing you know, which feels impossible when you are standing in a crowd of two hundred people who all want an answer.

One thing is worth knowing. In most countries, if the delay passes a certain number of hours, the airline has to give you food, and sometimes a hotel. Almost nobody asks. The passengers who ask are not luckier than the others. They are simply the ones who ask.
