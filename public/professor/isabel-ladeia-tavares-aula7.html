Lesson 7 · The Morning After

Something Went Wrong at the Bench

Every lab has a morning when the plates are cloudy or the labels are the wrong way round. Today is about the first three sentences you say after you find it.

Chapter 1: The Morning After

The First Thing You Said

Last week you wrote what was done, in order, with nobody in the sentence. Three steps of something you did at the lab this week, out loud.

Now the question of the day. Think of the last time something went wrong because of you. What was the very first sentence you said, and to whom?

Today’s Goal

Report It Without a Defense

1. The Words

a mix-up, a setback, to own up, hindsight…

2. Three Kinds of Past

what surely happened, what maybe did, what should have

3. The Report

tell your supervisor before anybody finds out

Nobody remembers the mistake for long. What people remember is whether you came to them first, and whether you spent the conversation defending yourself or fixing it.

One Situation

Monday, 8:15

On Friday you prepared twelve plates for somebody else’s experiment. On Monday morning every one of them is cloudy. You remember a phone call halfway through, with the lids off.

Your supervisor walks in. Tell her what happened, out loud, right now.

Chapter 3: The Code

Sure, Maybe, No Way, and If Only

Four ways of talking about a past you did not see happen

Grammar Discovery

Sure, Possible, Impossible, Regret

"I must have left the lids off during the phone call."

"The incubator might have been opened by somebody else."

"It cannot have been the power. Every other freezer was fine."

"I should have checked the labels before pooling."

All four are about the past, and none of them uses the past simple. Look at the three words in color: what do they have in common?

UseFormExample
Almost certainmust have + past participleI must have left the lids off.
Possiblemight / could have + past participleIt might have been the incubator.
Impossiblecannot have + past participleIt cannot have been the power.
The right thing that did not happenshould have + past participleI should have checked the labels.
The wrong thing that did happenshould not have + past participleI should not have answered the phone.
The trapmust have is not obligationObligation in the past is had to: I had to leave early.

In one line: modal + have + past participle — and the modal tells you how sure you are.

Dialogue

Before Anybody Finds Out

D
You wanted to see me? You look like somebody who has found something.
I
I have, and I would rather tell you now than have you find it. There has been a mix-up with yesterday’s samples.
D
Okay. What kind of mix-up?
I
Two tubes in the second rack were labeled the wrong way round. I must have swapped them when I was relabeling, so the pooled samples are not what we think they are.
D
Could it have been somebody else? Three people used that rack this week.
I
It might have been, but I do not think so. I traced it back to my own handwriting. I should have checked the labels against the sheet before pooling.
D
Right. That is a setback, but a small one. Thank you for flagging it before the measurements, not after.
I
I have already set aside new tubes. I can redo the pooling this afternoon and make up for the lost day.
Comprehension

About Daniel

1. What does he notice before she says anything?
That she looks like somebody who has found something.
2. What possibility does he offer her, and why?
That it could have been somebody else, because three people used that rack this week.
3. How does he describe the problem, and what exactly does he thank her for?
He calls it a setback, but a small one, and thanks her for flagging it before the measurements, not after.

Every plate she prepared was cloudy, contaminated. She must have left the lids off while labeling them, because a phone call interrupted her halfway through.
2. What did she do for forty minutes?
She looked for a version of the story in which it was not her: the incubator might have been opened by somebody else, the medium could have been bad.

The plates are contaminated; I think I must have left them open; I can have a new set by Thursday. Her supervisor remembered that she came to her first, before anybody else found out.

Listening 2 · A Message About the Freezer

Two in the Morning, Minus Forty

The facilities team found the freezer warm. Write down what he rules out, what he thinks happened, and what he needs. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does he rule out, and how does he know?
The power. Every other freezer on that corridor was fine, and the logs show no cut at all.
2. What does he think must have happened?
Somebody must have opened the door late yesterday, and the seal might have caught on the corner of a box.
3. What two things does he need, and what is he not doing?
A check of whether the samples on the top two shelves should have been moved to the backup freezer, and the name of whoever used it last. He is not looking for anybody to blame.
Real Document

The Deviation Report

LAB DEVIATION REPORT — No. 0417
Reported by I. Ladeia Tavares · reviewed by D. (supervisor)
What happenedtwo tubes in rack 2 labeled the wrong way round
When foundWednesday, 08:40, before the first measurement
Probable causelabels must have been swapped during relabeling
Ruled outreagent batch · same batch used in rack 1, no problem
Impactpooled samples discarded · one day lost
Corrective actionpooling repeated Wednesday afternoon
Preventionlabels checked against the sheet before every pooling
Reported before any data were collected. No measurement was affected.
1. Why was the reagent batch ruled out?
Because the same batch was used in rack 1, and there was no problem there.
2. Why does the last line matter so much?
Because the problem was found and reported before any data were collected, so no measurement was affected. That is what flagging it early bought.
3. Say the Probable cause and the Prevention fields as two sentences to your supervisor.
I must have swapped the labels when I was relabeling. From now on I will check them against the sheet before every pooling.
