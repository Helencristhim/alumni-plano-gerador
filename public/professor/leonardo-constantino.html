Metodologia — 5 Abordagens

1. Converter leitura em fala

O inglês do Leonardo está em modo leitura e vocabulário. O programa transforma isso em PRODUÇÃO oral estruturada, sempre no universo tributário-financeiro dele — nunca em frases genéricas de Business English.

2. Cenário real de reunião

Todo role-play replica a call técnica com cliente do setor financeiro — com objeção e imprevisto injetados pelo professor. É o cenário exato que ele descreveu como "terrível", treinado em terreno seguro.

3. Gramática indutiva + correção explícita

A regra nunca vem primeiro: exemplos, percepção, sistematização, produção. Depois da produção, feedback corretivo DIRETO — ele tem alta tolerância ao erro e nomeia os próprios gaps.

4. Progresso visível

Ele trocou de aula particular por não ver "para onde estava caminhando". Cada aula abre com objetivo explícito; as Aulas 8 e 15 recomparam a gravação de baseline da Aula 1.

5. Certificação integrada

FCE/TOEFL/TOEIC não é trilha paralela: leitura de texto formal, escrita executiva e listening estruturado entram dentro das aulas de conteúdo e se consolidam na Aula 12.

GRAMMAR

How to say what is always true about your practice and what you are working on right now.

FormUseExample
Present Simple
I / you / we + verb (he / she + -s)		Facts, routines and permanent situations - always true.I advise financial institutions.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect vs Past Simple

GRAMMAR

The test is simple: if the sentence says WHEN, use the past simple. If the time is still open, use the present perfect.

FormUseExample
Present Perfect
have / has + past participle		The period started in the past and still counts now. No finished date.I have advised financial institutions since 2016.
Past Simple
verb + -ed / irregular		The period is closed, and we usually say when it happened.I joined the firm in 2016.
Negativehave not / has not + past participle · did not + base verbWe have not signed the mandate. / We did not sign it last week.
QuestionHave / Has + subject + past participle · Did + subject + base verbHave you ever advised a foreign bank? / When did you join the firm?
for / sincefor + a length of time (for ten years, for six months) · since + a starting point (since 2016, since I became a partner)
already / yet / justalready = sooner than expected · yet = not up to now (negatives and questions) · just = a moment ago
ever / neverExperience in your whole life, with no date: Have you ever worked abroad? I have never worked in London.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Must, Have to, Should, Can

GRAMMAR

One question decides the modal: is this the law, my advice, or an option? And remember the project update tense: what has already happened, and what has not happened yet.

FormUseExample
must + base verbA strong obligation, often written in the law itselfThey must file a return in January.
have to / has to + base verbAn obligation from outside: a rule, a client, a deadlineEvery holding has to report the tax base.
should + base verbYour professional recommendation, not an obligationThe board should review the structure now.
can + base verbPossibility, option, or what your firm is able to doWe can mitigate the risk before the vote.
Negativemust not = prohibited · do not have to = not necessary. They are opposites, not synonyms.
QuestionDo we have to file this quarter? · Should we wait for the final text? · Can you send the memo today?
No "to", no "-s""should to consider" and "he musts file" do not exist. Only have keeps the to.
Project updateWe have already assessed the impact · They have not approved it yet · I have just sent the model · for eight months · since the first draft.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Relative Clauses

GRAMMAR

A relative clause joins two facts into one sentence. It is the difference between a list and a structure: "The client has a subsidiary. It is in the Cayman Islands. It carries an exposure." becomes "The client has a subsidiary in the Cayman Islands, which carries a significant exposure." Same information, one breath.

FormUseExample
whoPeople onlyThe partner who signed the opinion is in London.
whichThings, companies and ideas — never peopleThe provision, which was added in 2023, is unclear.
thatPeople or things, but only in defining clauses — never after a commaThe subsidiary that files in Brazil is the smaller one.
wherePlaces, countries and jurisdictionsThe jurisdiction where the fund is domiciled sets the rate.
Defining — no commasTells you which one. Remove it and the sentence loses its meaning: "The clients who hold offshore subsidiaries are the ones most affected."
Non-defining — two commasExtra information about something already identified: "Leonardo Constantino, who leads the financial institutions practice at ALMA, presented the scenario." Never that here.
Object positionWhen the pronoun is the object, you can drop it: "The regulation (that) we reviewed last month has been revised."
Two frequent mistakesNever repeat the object: "the provision that we challenged it" — wrong. And never use which for a person: "the partner who advises the fund", not "which".
