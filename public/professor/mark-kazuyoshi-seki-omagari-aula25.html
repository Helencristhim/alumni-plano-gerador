The Game, a Game, Games

Three versions of the same noun, and each one tells the listener something different.

Chapter 1: The Explanation

Explain It to Somebody Who Has Never Seen It

Pick one game, one film or one series you love. The person in front of you has never heard of it. Take one minute and make them understand what it is.

One Noun, Three Sentences

Same Word, Different Message

You do not know it yet

"I found a game you would like."

You know exactly which one

"The game takes forty hours to finish."

All of them, everywhere

"Games are expensive in Brazil."

The third one is the hardest, because it has no article at all. Portuguese puts one there and English removes it, so this is the version that disappears from your English for years without anybody telling you.

Chapter 3: A, The, or Nothing

The Smallest Words Carry the Most

One letter of difference, and the listener hears something else.

Grammar Discovery

Four Sentences, Four Listeners

You land on an island with nothing.

The island is different every time you start.

Islands are the easiest maps to design.

I read about it on the internet, like everybody else.

The noun barely changes. What changes is how much the listener already knows: nothing yet, something from one second ago, the whole category, or the only one there is.

FormUse it whenExample
a / anYou are introducing one member of a group. The listener cannot identify it yet."I found a game you would like." / "She is a journalist."
the (second mention)You already introduced it, so now the listener can identify which one."There is a crafting system. The crafting system is where people give up."
the (only one)There is only one in the conversation, or the words after it point to one."the internet" / "the news" / "the first game in the series"
no articleYou mean the whole category: a plural or an uncountable noun in general."Games are expensive." / "Music helps me concentrate."
a or an?Decide by sound, never by spelling: an hour, an honest answer, a university, a European studio.
Two trapsNever the games are expensive for a general statement — that sentence means some specific games you both know. And with school, bed, work and home, English drops the article when it means the activity: "I go to school", not "I go to the school".
Practice

Say it first, then click to check. A dash means no article at all.

"I found ___a game you would like. It came out last year."

"It takes ___an hour before the map opens up."
"I read about it on ___the internet, three years after it came out."
"I play it after ___— school, never before."
The Tool

Three Questions, in Order

Question 1

Do I mean the whole category, or one member of it?

The whole category takes no article. This is the one Portuguese hides from you.

Question 2

Is this the first time it comes up in the conversation?

A first mention takes a or an. Choose between them with your ear.

Question 3

Can the other person identify which one I mean?

If they can, it is the — whether because you just said it, or because there is only one.

Explaining something new is these three questions on repeat. You introduce it, you refer back to it, and you place it inside a category the other person already knows.

Dialogue

Explaining It to Somebody Who Has Never Played

G
So I have to write four hundred words about this and I have never played anything. Start from the beginning. What is it?
M
It is a survival game. You land on an island with nothing, and the island is different every time you start.
G
Different how? Give me something I can put in the article.
M
There is a crafting system, and the crafting system is where most people give up in the first hour. That is the learning curve everybody complains about.
G
And you kept going anyway. Why?
M
Because it is a sandbox. There are almost no cutscenes and nothing ever stops to explain the plot. The game hands you the tools and then leaves.
G
That sounds like a lot of work for something you do for fun.
M
There is a grind, I will not pretend there is not. But games are not all the same genre, and this one is closer to building than to winning. The soundtrack alone is worth the two bad hours.
G
I have never seen it in a shop, and I looked. Is it new?
M
It is four years old. It is not a mainstream franchise, it is niche, and it grew by word of mouth. That is the whole story, and it is the part you should write about.
G
Fine. If I wanted to try it this weekend, what would you tell me?
M
Skip the tutorial and let the game teach you. And give it three hours, because nobody has ever got into it in twenty minutes.
Comprehension

About Grace

1. Why is she asking him about the game at all?
She has to write four hundred words about it for the school newsletter and she has never played anything.
2. What does she say about the effort the game asks for?
That it sounds like a lot of work for something people do for fun.
3. What did she already try to do before the interview, and what happened?
She looked for the game in a shop and never saw it there, which is why she asks whether it is new.

Chapter 5: Practice

Choosing Without Thinking

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "The games are expensive in Brazil, so I wait for the sales."
"Games are expensive in Brazil." (a general statement about the whole category takes no article; with "the" it means some specific games you have both been discussing)
2. "I go to the school by bus and I play after."
"I go to school by bus." (school, bed, work and home drop the article when we mean the activity, not the building)
3. "It takes a hour before the map opens up."
"It takes an hour..." (the H is silent, so the ear hears a vowel; choose a or an by sound, never by spelling)
4. "I found the game you would like. It came out last year."
"I found a game you would like." (first mention: she cannot know which one yet, so it is a, not the)
5. "My brother assists every match of the championship."
"My brother watches every match..." (in English "assist" means to help, never to watch)
The Real Thing

The Recommendation Card

Recommendation card — for somebody who has never played
Mark · the school newsletter
five lines
no jargon
What it isIt is a survival game, and survival games are slower than people expect.
The hookYou land on an island with nothing, and the island is different every time.
The warningThere is a grind, and the grind is the reason half of my friends stopped.
The contextIt is not a mainstream franchise. It grew by word of mouth, with no advertising at all.
Your cardFive lines about something you love. Introduce it once, refer back to it once, and say one true thing about the whole category.
1. Why does the first line use "a survival game" and then "survival games"?
The first introduces one member of the category, so it takes "a". The second is a statement about the whole category, so it takes no article at all.
2. Why does the third line say "a grind" and then "the grind"?
The first mention introduces it, and by the second mention the reader can identify which grind is meant, so it switches to "the".

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I introduce something new with "a" or "an" and refer back to it with "the".
I drop the article when I mean the whole category, as in "Games are expensive".
I use "the" when there is only one, as in "the internet" and "the news".
I choose between "a" and "an" by sound, not by spelling.
I can explain something nobody in the room has seen, in about two minutes.
