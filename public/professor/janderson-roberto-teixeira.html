Janderson tem uma história de recomeços. Estudou desde criança por obrigação, fez intercâmbio no Canadá, quase dois anos de Berlitz presencial, e comprou um pacote de 100 aulas online das quais fez 2. Este programa existe para não repetir os erros conhecidos:

Metodologia

Encurtar, não ampliar

Ele trava porque tenta traduzir um período longo do português. Quando isso acontecer, corte com shorter — eight words. Duas frases curtas e uma pergunta de volta é o formato do programa inteiro.

O fio entre as aulas

Todo warm-up retoma o vocabulário da aula anterior antes de abrir o novo. É o que ele nunca teve nas escolas onde recomeçava sempre do básico.

Finanças como cenário

O banco, o cliente offshore e a reunião aparecem como CONTEXTO de fala. NUNCA como texto denso de economia — foi exatamente isso que o fez abandonar um curso de quase um ano.

Cobrança com leveza

Ele PEDIU estrutura e cobrança. Comece toda aula perguntando se ele fez o Pre-class e diga o percentual em voz alta. Uma frase, sem sermão, toda aula.

Errar em voz alta

Ele passou três horas por semana, por um ano, fingindo que entendia. Aqui vale o contrário: I don't understand, could you say that again? entra no Survival Card da aula 1 de propósito.

Aula às 20h30

Ele chega direto do banco, cansado. Energia dinâmica e variada, blocos curtos, nada de exposição longa. Se um bloco não render, siga — ele volta no Pre-class.

CCQs da Aula 1 (Concept Checking Questions)

  • To be based in — Is it where I am today, or where I usually work? (Where I usually work or live.)
  • A colleague — A friend, or somebody from work? (From work. It can become a friend, but the word means work.)
  • To take care of — Do I just help, or am I responsible? (I am responsible.)
  • At the moment — Always, or now? (Now — and it will change.)
  • To deal with — Only people, or also problems? (Both. It is the work you handle.)
  • A client — Does the client pay me, or do I pay the client? (The client pays.)
  • To cover for — Is that person here? (No — they are away and I do their work.)
  • Straight away — Today, or immediately? (Immediately.)
  • “I work at a bank” — Only today, or every week? (Every week — it is permanent, so present simple.)
  • “This week I am covering for a colleague” — Permanent or temporary? (Temporary — so present continuous.)
  • “He deal with clients” — Is this correct? (No — he, she and it take an S: he deals.)
  • “What you do here?” — What is missing? (Do. A present simple question borrows do or does.)
  • “I am not understanding” — Why is this wrong? (Understand is not an action, so it never takes -ing: I don't understand.)

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple and Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

The question is never "when?" It is always "permanent or temporary?" Answer that first and the verb chooses itself.

FormWhat it saysExample
Present simple — positivePermanent. Your job, your routine, the facts about you."I work at a bank."
"She takes care of the visitors."
Present simple — negative and questionThe sentence borrows do or does, and the main verb loses its S."I don't work on Saturdays."
"Does she deal with clients?"
Present continuous — positiveTemporary. True this week, this month, this project, and it will stop."This week I am covering for a colleague."
Present continuous — negative and questionNothing is borrowed. Am / is / are simply moves."I am not covering for anybody."
"Are you working from home?"
The words that give it awayEvery day, usually, never, on Tuesdays → simple. At the moment, this week, right now, today → continuous."I usually answer straight away."
"At the moment I am reading it."
The trapA small group of verbs about the mind never takes the -ing form: understand, know, want, need, mean, remember. They are not actions.NOT "I am not understanding"
"I don't understand."

In a first conversation you will almost always need both, one after the other: what you do, and what you happen to be doing this week. That second sentence is what makes you sound like a person and not a job title.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Past Simple

GRAMMAR

One tense covers everything that already finished. The whole difficulty is one question: is the past inside the verb, or inside did? It is never inside both.

FormWhat it saysExample
Regular verbs: verb + EDMost verbs simply take -ed, and the form is the same for every subject. No S anywhere."We booked a table."
"He walked away."
Irregular verbs: a new wordAbout a hundred common verbs change completely. The ten most common cover half of what you say every day.go → went · take → took · have → had
find → found · eat → ate · sit → sat
Negative: did not + plain verbDid carries the past, so the main verb loses it."I did not go anywhere."
NOT "I did not went"
Question: did + subject + plain verbSame idea, and English needs that did. A rising voice is not enough, as it is in Portuguese."Did you take the day off?"
NOT "You took the day off?"
The exception: to beWas and were work alone. They never borrow did."The restaurant was not open."
"Were you on your own?"
The words that give it awayYesterday, last weekend, two hours ago, in twenty nineteen, when I was twenty — all of them close the door on the present."Last weekend I took a day off."

You already recognise every one of these forms when you read them. The work now is producing them at speed, in a corridor, before you have had time to think.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Should, Ought To and Had Better

GRAMMAR

Three ways to say the same thing at three different weights. Choosing the wrong weight is what makes advice in a second language sound either invisible or aggressive.

FormWhat it saysExample
should + plain verbThe everyday one. An opinion, easy to accept and easy to refuse."You should ask."
"You should not sign it today."
ought to + plain verbSlightly more formal. Points at what is right rather than at what you think."You ought to think twice."
had better + plain verbA warning, with an unspoken consequence behind it."You had better call him before six."
"You had better not be late."
No to, no S, no doThe verb after them stays plain, and they make their own questions and negatives.NOT "should to ask" · NOT "he shoulds"
"Should I call him?"
The softenersThe same advice, with the edge taken off. Safest in a first conversation."I would wait."
"If I were you, I would wait."
Asking instead of tellingOften the strongest move is a question, not a recommendation."What are you tempted to do?"
"What would you regret more?"

One rule for a client conversation: had better almost never belongs there. It sounds like a threat in a language you do not fully control. I would recommend and if I were you do the same job and leave the decision where it belongs.
