A Jornada — narrativa do programa

Viviane é gerente de RH e cuida de cultura organizacional, performance e engajamento — foi ela quem conduziu o projeto que rendeu à empresa o selo Great Place to Work. A empresa foi comprada por um grupo italiano e as primeiras reuniões em inglês começam a aparecer. Mas o objetivo dela é mais direto e mais honesto que isso: empregabilidade. Nas palavras dela, "o inglês hoje é uma barreira" — ela vê vagas que pedem inglês avançado, cumpre todos os outros requisitos, e não se candidata.

O gargalo nº 1 é listening: ela não acompanha a fala de um americano em velocidade real, nem com legenda. A leitura ela resolve, porque é no tempo dela. Por isso o programa não começa com diagnóstico: ela já fez 52 aulas com a gente, já tem professor fixo, e a consultoria já mapeou tudo. A Aula 1 vai direto ao que ela precisa — o vocabulário de RH que ela usa todo dia em português, a estrutura de três frases para responder "what do you do?", e dois listenings, um deles com um recrutador dizendo explicitamente que não espera inglês perfeito e que pedir para repetir é marca de bom candidato. Da Aula 2 em diante o programa avança pelos tempos verbais dentro do universo de RH dela, com blocos dedicados de listening (Aulas 14, 22, 42) e um mock interview completo na Aula 47. Cada bloco fecha com uma milestone explícita — ela pediu para saber se avançou.

Metodologia — 5 Abordagens

1. Listening como treino, não como teste

O gargalo nº 1 dela. Todo listening começa a 0.75x e repete quantas vezes ela pedir, sem constrangimento. Ela vai à academia — a analogia de treino funciona: é músculo, não talento.

2. O vocabulário que ela já domina

Engajamento, turnover, onboarding, retenção — ela vive esses conceitos em português. O programa só coloca a etiqueta em inglês, o que dá vitória rápida e sensação de competência.

3. Empregabilidade como eixo

"Meu foco 100% é empregabilidade." Ela mesma descartou o inglês de viagem. Cada aula termina com algo que ela poderia usar numa entrevista ou numa reunião com a liderança italiana.

4. Exercício que obriga a estudar

Ela se declara preguiçosa para estudar sozinha e gosta do pre-class exatamente porque força a preparação. O Pre-class é levado a sério aqui — e fica salvo para ela revisar depois, como pediu.

5. Progresso visível

"Eu estou a mesma coisa ou eu avancei?" O checklist fecha cada aula, e as milestones (20, 24, 30, 35, 39, 44) recomparam com a Aula 1. Combinado com o consultor: relatório e mock test por trimestre.

A With he, she and it, the present simple always adds an -s.

2. The text says "she is responsible for culture", not "she responsible for culture". Why?
A Because "responsible" is a verb.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple and Questions

GRAMMAR

How to say what you do, and how the question comes back to you.

FormUseExample
I / you / we + verbYour job and your routine -- things that are always true.I work in HR. We measure engagement.
he / she + verb + sThird person always takes an -s.She works in HR. He leads the team.
am / is / are + responsible forWith responsible, use the verb to be. Never I work responsible.I am responsible for engagement.
Question: Do / DoesQuestions need do or does, and the verb goes back to the base form.Do you work in HR? / Does she lead the team?
Negativedo not / does not + base formI do not work with payroll. She does not lead that team.
Two trapsNever I am work in HR -- choose one verb. Never What you do? -- the question needs do.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Simple: Regular, Irregular and Questions

GRAMMAR

How to say what happened, what did not happen, and how to ask about it.

FormUseExample
regular verb + -edMost verbs. One word, and it is finished.I finished the report. We talked on Friday.
irregular verb changesA small group changes shape. Learn them one by one.run → ran · send → sent · have → had · write → wrote
did not + base formThe past is already in did. Never two pasts in one verb.I did not finish it. (never did not finished)
Did + subject + base formQuestions work the same way as the negative.Did you send the payroll? Did she talk to him?
was / wereThe verb to be is the exception: no did.It was a busy week. They were in the store.
time markersThey close the past: the action is over.last week, yesterday, on Monday, two days ago

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Telling a Story in Order

GRAMMAR

The words that turn a list of facts into a story a recruiter can follow.

FormUseExample
At the beginning / At firstWhere the story starts, and usually the problem.At first nobody believed in it.
First · Then · After thatThe order of your actions. Use after that, not after alone.First we ran a pilot. After that we trained the managers.
It took + person + timeHow long something took. No preposition after took.It took us eight months.
... laterMoving forward inside the story.Six months later we ran the survey again.
In the end / FinallyThe result of the whole story.In the end we got the certification.
CarefulTwo traps for Brazilian speakers.Never in the final. Never it took to us.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the definition. These are the words of any planning meeting.

Priority -- the thing that has to come first, before the others
"My first priority next quarter is the leadership programme."
Budget -- the money available for a plan or a team
"I am going to ask for a small budget in January."
Forecast -- a guess about what will happen, based on numbers
"My forecast is twelve new hires this quarter."
Target -- the result you want to reach, usually a number
"Our target is seventy-five per cent in the next survey."
Timeline -- the plan of when each step will happen
"Here is the timeline for the next six months."
Resource -- the people, money or time you need to do something
"I do not have the resources to do all three projects."
Scope -- everything a project will include, and everything it will not
"The scope of the pilot is four stores only."
Trade-off -- what you give up in order to get something else
"The trade-off is simple: speed or quality."
Quarter -- a period of three months in the business year
"We are going to close the titles this quarter."
Approval -- a yes from the person who decides
"I will start as soon as I get your approval."
