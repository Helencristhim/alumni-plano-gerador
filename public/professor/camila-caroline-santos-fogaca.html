Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

In a large group, HR is not one team. It is organised in three parts, and each part has a different job. The first part is the HR Business Partner. The business partner sits inside the business, next to the leaders of each business unit, and is responsible for the people agenda there: hiring, performance, organisation and cost of the team.

The second part is the centre of excellence. This team designs the rules: the policy on pay, the policy on promotion, the way performance is measured. It usually sits at headquarters, and it has a global scope. The centre of excellence writes the policy; the business partner explains it to the managers and makes it work in each country.

The third part is shared services. This team runs the operations: contracts, payroll, documents, systems. When a manager asks for a certificate or a payslip, shared services takes care of it. The business partner does not.

A global business partner has one more line on the organisation chart. She has a reporting line to the global HR director at headquarters, and often a dotted-line reporting to a regional leader. The solid line is the manager; the dotted line is the person she also keeps informed. Both are stakeholders, and so are the country managers, the finance team and the employees themselves.

This is why the same person can be responsible for three entities in three countries and still say, without any contradiction, that payroll is not her responsibility. Knowing where your scope ends is part of explaining what you do.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

Every car that leaves a showroom carries two decisions: who sells it and who pays for it. The group sells the car through its dealer network, and a captive finance company pays for it, first for the dealer and then for the driver.

For the dealer, the product is the floor plan. The cars in the showroom were produced and delivered long before any customer arrived, and somebody has to finance them while they wait. For the driver, the product is retail financing: a loan for the person who takes the car home. The two products have the same name in Portuguese conversation and very different names in an English meeting.

The rental company sits next to them. Its clients are companies with fleets, and its products are fleet rental and leasing. The idea is simple: the client uses the car and gives it back. The group takes care of the rest.

All these loans together form the loan portfolio, and the share of the cars sold that the group itself finances is the penetration rate. When the financial results are presented, these two words appear in almost every slide, and they are not interchangeable: the portfolio is the size, the rate is the share.

A last point about form. Finance is the action and the area. Financing is the activity as a thing: retail financing grew, fleet financing was stable. Financial describes another word: financial results, financial services, financial institution. Three forms, one family, and a mistake in the middle of a sentence is enough to make a clear idea sound uncertain.
