What Will Happen? -- Will for Promises, Predictions and Decisions on the Spot
A aula em que o inglês da Paula deixa de ser sobre o que ela já viveu e passa a ser sobre o que ela vai fazer. Nas aulas 14 e 15 ela contou o passado; na 13 ela aprendeu o going to dos planos já decididos. Hoje entra a outra metade do futuro: will — a palavra da promessa (I will send it today), da previsão (the meeting will probably be long) e da decisão tomada na hora de falar (don't worry, I'll check now). E é esta última que resolve o maior medo dela: quando alguém pergunta algo que ela não sabe, ela não precisa mais pedir desculpa — ela promete. I will check and I will call you back. A aula é de LEITURA: ela lê o comunicado da Sofia com o ano inteiro da equipe — o sistema novo em março, os clientes novos em junho e, em novembro, a reunião anual em Miami, a primeira viagem internacional dela pela Zurich. Seis palavras novas (promise, probably, maybe, confirm, delay, worry), o Greg prometendo buscar ela no aeroporto, e a Sofia fechando com a frase que a aula inteira constrói: “por dois anos você foi uma voz numa call; em novembro você vai estar na sala.” -- 35 slides

Chapter 2: The Words

Six Words for What Hasn't Happened

Duas delas existem só pra dizer “eu não tenho certeza” — e isso é permitido.

Vocabulary

Words 1-3

0 / 3 words revealed

You say this when you give your word. "I ___ I will send the report today."
Promise
prometer / promessa
"I promise I will send it today." (Prometo que mando hoje.) Diga PRO-mis — força na primeira sílaba e o fim é mis, nunca “pro-MAIZ”.
Almost sure, but not one hundred percent. "The meeting will ___ be long."
Probably
provavelmente
"The meeting will probably be long." (A reunião provavelmente vai ser longa.) Diga PRO-ba-bli — e americano falando rápido diz quase “PRO-bli”.
Fifty-fifty. You really don't know. "___ the flight will be late."
Maybe
talvez
"Maybe the flight will be late." (Talvez o voo atrase.) Diga MEI-bi — duas sílabas. Menos certeza que probably.

Chapter 3: The Code

One Word, Every Person

Não muda com I, she ou they. E o verbo depois dele fica pelado.

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

As palavras verdes falam de antes ou de depois? E o verbo logo depois delas — ele mudou?

I will send the report today.

The meeting will probably be long.

She will confirm the dates in September.

Don't worry — I'll check it now.

Todas falam do que ainda não aconteceu. E repare: I will, the meeting will, she will — a palavra nunca muda. O verbo depois dela também não.

UsoExemploQuando
PromessaI will send it today.você dá a sua palavra
PrevisãoIt will probably be long.você acha que vai ser assim
Decisão na horaI'll check it now.você decide enquanto fala
NegativoI won't forget.won't = will not
PerguntaWill you come? · When will you arrive?will vai pra frente
Duas coisas só, e as duas são alívio: (1) will é o MESMO para I, you, she, he, we, they — nunca wills. (2) o verbo depois dele fica pelado: will send, nunca will to send, nunca will sends. Na fala vira 'll — I'll, she'll, we'll.
Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I will to call you tomorrow."
"I will call you tomorrow."
"She will confirms the meeting."
"She will confirm the meeting."
"I don't will travel in November."
"I won't travel in November."

Os três erros vêm de colocar coisa demais perto do will. O primeiro poe um to que não existe: depois de will o verbo é pelado (will call). O segundo poe o -s da terceira pessoa — e isso acontece porque você APRENDEU a regra do -s; depois de will, ela dorme (she will confirm). O terceiro chama o don't pra ajudar, e o will não precisa de ajuda: ele nega sozinho e vira won't.

The Year That Hasn't Happened Yet

The Year Ahead — A Message to the Large Risks Team

This year will be different for our team. In March the new system will arrive, and everyone will learn it together. It will probably take two months, so don't worry: nobody will be alone with it. I will book the training days myself.

In June we will receive the new large-risk clients from the north. Paula will take the international accounts, and she will speak with Miami every week. Maybe the first calls will be difficult. That is normal, and it will not stay difficult.

In November we will all meet in Miami for the annual meeting. Three days. Paula, this will be your first trip with us, and I promise you one thing: you will not be a voice on a call. You will be in the room.

I will confirm the dates in September, and I will send the hotel and the flights in October. If there is a delay with a visa, tell me early and we will solve it together. This year will ask a lot of us. I think we will be ready.

Comprehension

About Greg

1. What will Greg do at the airport?
He will pick Paula up himself. She only has to send him the flight number — and if there is a delay, he will wait.
2. What does Greg say about the meeting?
It will probably be long, it will be all in English, and nobody will speak slowly. He tells her now so that it is not a surprise.
3. Greg makes three promises for day one. What are they?
He will sit next to her; if a question is too fast, he will write it on paper; and if she wants to say something, he will stop the room.
4. How much does Greg expect Paula to say on the first day, and why?
Maybe two sentences — that is enough for the first day. The team has read her reports for a year, so they will want to hear her, not him.

0:000:00
1. What will happen when Paula arrives in Miami?
She will land and the first thing she will feel is the heat. Then about forty people will say hello to her in one hour, and she will not remember all the names. David says that is normal.
2. What does David say about the first morning?

Listening 2

Sofia: The Sentence That Saved a Career

Sound first — no text. Leia as perguntas antes de ouvir.

0:000:00
1. Which sentence does Sofia say saved her career?
"I will check, and I will call you back." She says it is what you say when somebody asks you for something and you do not have the answer yet.
2. What did Sofia think a professional was when she started, and what does she think now?
She thought a professional was a person who knew everything immediately. Now she says a professional is a person who promises something and then does it.

She said yes — not because Paula's English will be perfect in November (it will not be, and that is fine), but because she will have three sentences ready: "I will check", "I will send it today", "I promise". Chapter 6: Your Turn

Now It's You

Da colega curiosa à call sozinha — em três passos.

Quick Fire

One Question at a Time

1 / 6

A client asks for a number you don't have right now. Answer with a promise, not an apology.

  • I don't have it now. I will check and I will call you back.
  • I'll find out today and I'll come back to you.

Greg asks what time your flight will land in Miami. You are not sure yet.

  • I'm not sure yet. I will confirm the flight next week.
  • Maybe it will land in the morning. I'll send you the number.

A colleague is worried about the new system. Say it will not be hard, and offer help on the spot.

  • Don't worry. I'll help you with it.
  • It won't be difficult. I'll show you tomorrow.

Make a prediction about the Miami meeting. Use 'probably'.

  • The meeting will probably be long.
  • It will probably finish late.

Sofia asks if you will send the report today. Promise it.

  • Yes, I will send it today. I promise.
  • I'll send it before six, I promise.

Your flight has a delay of three hours. Call Greg and tell him what you will do.

  • There is a delay, so I will arrive late. I'll call you when I land.
  • My flight has a delay of three hours. Don't worry, I'll send you a message.
Role-Play

Guided — A Colleague Heard About Miami

Scenario: A colleague at Zurich heard about the trip. At the coffee machine she asks: "So, when will you travel? Who will meet you there? Will the meeting be long?" Answer her about November. The lines are right here on the screen.

Keywords:

I will travel in November. Greg will pick me up at the airport. The meeting will probably be long. I will confirm the hotel next week.
Role-Play

Semi-free — The Client Asks What You Don't Have

Scenario: A client in Miami calls and asks you three things you do not have in front of you: the exact price of a policy, the date of the inspection, and the name of the broker. Never say only "I don't know". Every time, make a promise instead — and say when.

Keywords:

I will check... I will call you back... I will confirm... today · tomorrow · this week
Free Practice

The Year Ahead — No Screen, No Notes

Scenario: Sofia asks you two questions, one after the other. "What will happen this year, and what will happen in November?" And then: "What will you do when somebody asks you something you don't know?" No screen, no notes. Sixty to ninety seconds.

One rule, and it is the whole lesson: never apologize — promise. I will check. I will call you back. I will send it today. And when you are not sure, you are allowed to say probably and maybe.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I know my six words: promise, probably, maybe, confirm, delay, worry.
After "will" the verb stays bare — no "to", no "-s" ("she will confirm").
I can say the negative with "won't", not with "don't will".
I can make a prediction when I am not sure: "It will probably be long", "Maybe it will be late".
When I don't know the answer, I promise instead of apologizing: "I will check and I will call you back."
