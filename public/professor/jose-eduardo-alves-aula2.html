A Week Inside a Small Firm

A Week Inside a Small Firm

Marina Duarte runs a firm of seven lawyers in Curitiba. She specializes in commodity contracts: her clients buy and sell corn, soya and sugar, and they almost never meet the people on the other side of the deal. Marina does not go to court. She says a good week is a week in which nobody has to.

Her routine rarely changes. She arrives before eight and reads the overnight messages from Europe first, because Rotterdam is five hours ahead and the answer usually has to leave before lunch. She then reviews the contracts her associates prepared the day before. Rafael, the youngest associate, handles the smaller deals on his own; he sends her the difficult clauses and she reads every one of them.

The firm charges a fee for each contract and a second fee if a dispute starts. Marina explains this to every new client in the first meeting, because the client who understands the schedule of fees never argues about the invoice. She also explains what her firm does not do: it does not find buyers, it does not negotiate the price, and it does not sign anything for the client.

On Friday afternoons she stops. Nobody in the firm sends a contract for review after four o'clock on a Friday, and that rule is hers. She says the mistakes she has seen in twenty years almost all happened late on a Friday, in a hurry, on a document that somebody read once.

Chapter 4: The Code

The Letter Everybody Drops

One verb, one person, one -s

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"She specializes in commodity contracts."

"Rafael handles the smaller deals on his own."

"She does not go to court."

"Does he review the difficult clauses?"

In the first two, the red verb carries an extra letter. In the last two, that letter is gone — and the blue word carries it instead.

JobFormExample
He, she or it, positiveverb + -sShe reviews every clause.
Negativedoes not + bare verbShe does not go to court.
QuestionDoes + subject + bare verbDoes he handle the big deals?
Spelling-ch, -sh, -s, -x take -es; consonant + y becomes -ieswatch → watches · study → studies
Two irregular oneshave → has · do → doesThe firm has seven lawyers.
How oftenalways, usually, often, rarely, never — before the verbHer routine rarely changes.
The trapnever two -s in the same sentenceNot does she reviews. Say does she review.

In one line: the -s belongs to the sentence, not to the verb — and does takes it whenever does turns up.

Quick Fire

One Question at a Time

1 / 6

Say what Marina does, in one sentence, using she.

  • She specializes in commodity contracts.
  • Watch the -s: she specialize is the mistake, she specializes is the form.

Now say what she does NOT do. Two things from the text.

  • She does not go to court.
  • Her firm does not negotiate the price. Note: after does not, the verb loses the -s.

Ask a question about Rafael, using the third person.

  • Does he handle the big deals too?
  • Not: Does he handles. The -s goes to does, never to both.

The text says her routine rarely changes. Where does rarely go in the sentence?

  • Before the verb: she rarely changes her routine.
  • With to be it goes after: she is rarely late.

Say the same about your own associate, or about a partner in your firm.

  • He handles the smaller cases and sends me the difficult ones.
  • She reviews every contract before we send it.

Ask me two questions about the firm in the text, one with does and one with a question word.

  • Does she go to court?
  • Who reviews the difficult clauses?

The Cargo That Changes Owner at Sea

How a grain cargo is sold three times before it arrives. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What surprises people about the grain trade, according to the speaker?
The cargo often changes owner three or four times while the ship is still at sea.
2. What does the lawyer in this market never ask, and what does he ask instead?
He never asks what the product looks like. He asks who owns it today, what the contract says about a late ship, and which country decides a disagreement.
3. Which of those questions does he say costs money?
The last one — which country decides if the two sides disagree — when nobody answers it in advance.
