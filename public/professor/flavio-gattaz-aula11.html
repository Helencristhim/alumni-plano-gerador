Lesson 11 · The Interview Warm-Up

The First Ten Minutes

Before the hard questions there is easy conversation, and somewhere in it a sentence you do not catch. What you say next is the whole lesson.

Warm-Up

You Wrote the Plan. Now Somebody Asks About It.

Last time the page was yours and you had all the time in the world. Today the questions come at normal speed, from somebody you have never met.

In two sentences: what is the first thing an interviewer would ask you about your plan, and what would you want to ask them back?

Today’s Goal

Asking, Without Losing the Room

1. The Words

small talk, rapport, nerves, to catch, to rephrase...

2. One Structure

asking a question inside a longer, softer sentence

3. Your Turn

four minutes of warm-up, and three things you did not catch

A candidate who says “sorry, could you say that again” sounds careful. A candidate who answers the wrong question sounds lost, and the panel cannot tell the difference between not hearing and not knowing.

One Question

What Do You Actually Say?

Somebody asks you a question in English and you hear about half of it. Say the exact words you would use, out loud, right now.

Then the second one: you want to know how long the program takes. Ask it politely, in one sentence.

Chapter 3: The Code

A Question Inside a Longer Sentence

Indirect questions, and the four ways to ask somebody to say it again

Grammar Discovery

What Happened to the Word Order?

"Where is the interview?" → "Could you tell me where the interview is?"

"How long does the residency last?" → "Do you know how long the residency lasts?"

"Do you interview by panel?" → "I was wondering whether you interview by panel."

"Say that again." → "Would you mind repeating the last part?"

Everything in blue is the direct question. Everything in red is the same question inside a longer sentence, and two things have happened: the verb has gone back behind the subject, and do, does and did have disappeared.

JobFormExample
The openerCould you tell me... / Do you know... / I was wondering...Could you tell me how the interviews are structured?
Word ordersubject first, then verb — statement orderNot where is the interview. Say where the interview is.
No do, does or didthe auxiliary disappears and the main verb takes the endingNot how long does it last. Say how long it lasts.
Yes or no questionwhether / ifI was wondering whether you interview by panel.
Ask to hear it againWould you mind + -ing / Sorry, did you say...?Would you mind repeating the last part?
Check the meaningWhen you say X, do you mean...? / Do you mean X or Y?When you say scope, do you mean the size or the list of tasks?
Buy five secondsa full sentence, not a noiseThat is a fair question, and I want to answer it properly.

In one line: the opener is polite, the order inside is a statement, and do, does and did never survive the trip.

Dialogue

Minute Two, and Minute Six

N
Good to see you. I have you at nine your time, so this is the middle of your working day. Did I take you off a site?
F
You did, and I was glad to leave. It is thirty-one degrees in Sao Paulo today. Could you tell me how long we have, so that I keep my answers the right length?
N
Forty minutes, and I will keep an eye on it. Let me ease into this. Before we talk about the company, what did you do this morning?
F
I walked two hundred metres to a slab that was poured on Saturday and argued with an engineer about it for half an hour. That is a normal morning.
N
Good. So, given the trajectory, how would you say your remit has shifted since you came into the directorship?
F
I am sorry, I did not catch one word there. Would you mind rephrasing the middle of the question?
N
Of course. What is your job now that it was not five years ago?
F
That is a fair question and I want to answer it properly. Five years ago I signed things. Now I decide which things exist. Could I ask whether the panel sees the goals essay before this conversation or after it?
Comprehension

About Nadia

1. What does Nadia notice about the time of the call, and what does she ask because of it?
That nine in the morning his time is the middle of his working day, so she asks whether she took him off a site.
2. What is her first real question about him, and why does she ask that one first?
She asks what he did that morning. She says she is easing into it, so the ordinary question comes before the one about the company.
3. What does she do when he says he did not catch a word, and what does she not do?
She says "of course" and asks the same question again in nine plain words. She does not apologise at length, comment on his English, or make the rest of the conversation easier.

Listening 1 · An Admissions Director

What the First Five Minutes Are For

An admissions director on the part of the interview nobody prepares. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she say the small talk is actually measuring?
Whether the candidate can hold an ordinary conversation in English at normal speed, which is what the classroom is made of. It is not a courtesy.
2. What does she say about candidates who never ask her to repeat anything?
That half of them understood everything and half of them did not, and she cannot tell which is which, so she stops trusting the answers.
3. What is the sentence she says she is always glad to hear, and why?
"Would you mind rephrasing that?" She says it costs the candidate nothing and tells her the person is actually listening rather than performing.

Listening 2 · A Candidate

The Minute I Lost the Thread

A candidate on the moment he stopped following, and what he said next. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Where in the question did he stop following, and what did he do about it the first time?
Around the second half of a long question. The first time he guessed, answered something she had not asked, and watched her write something down.
2. What did he say the second time it happened, and how long did the whole exchange take?
He said he had not caught the last part and asked her to say it again. He says the whole thing took about six seconds.
3. What does he say he had got wrong about asking for repetition?
He had believed it would sound weak. He says the weak thing was the confident answer to the wrong question, and that nobody in the room was grading his ears.

Listening 3 · Business Radio

Half a Second of Delay

A radio report on what a video call does to a conversation. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is the delay on a normal video call, and what does the report say it does to a conversation?
Between a fifth and half a second. It is enough to make people talk over each other and to make a normal pause look like hesitation.
2. What did the researchers find about speakers using a second language on video?
They waited longer before speaking and asked for repetition less often than on the phone, and the report says both of those make them sound less sure than they are.
3. What two pieces of advice does the report end with?
Say the first three words slightly early so the line is yours, and ask for repetition in a full sentence rather than a noise, because a full sentence survives the delay.
Real Document

The Email That Starts It

INTERVIEW CONFIRMATION
Executive MBA Admissions · Round 1

Dear Flavio,

Thank you for your application. We are pleased to invite you to an admissions interview.

DateTuesday 20 October
Time8:00 a.m. Eastern Time
FormatVideo, 40 minutes
InterviewerOne member of the committee
MaterialsNone required

The conversation is informal for the first few minutes and there is nothing to prepare for that part. Please reply to this message to confirm, and let us know of anything that would help us run the call well.

Note: the last sentence is an invitation, and almost nobody takes it. Candidates who reply asking one or two clear questions about the format are remembered before the interview has even happened.
1. Three practical things are not in this email. Name them, and ask for one of them in a full indirect question.
Who the interviewer is by name, which platform the call uses, and what the time is in Sao Paulo. For example: "Could you tell me which platform we will be using?"
2. What does the note say about the last sentence of the email?
That it is an invitation almost nobody takes, and that candidates who reply with one or two clear questions about the format are remembered before the interview happens.
3. The email says the first minutes are informal and there is nothing to prepare. Is that true?
The part about preparation is true, and the part about it not counting is not said anywhere. The first minutes are still the interview.
