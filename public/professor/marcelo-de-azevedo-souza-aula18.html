The Long Way Home

The Long Way Home

The last two hundred kilometers were the hardest, and by the time I rolled into my own street, the bike and I were held together by tape and stubbornness. Three months on rough roads leave a mark. The next morning I dropped the bike off at the workshop and asked for a quote. The mechanic spun the wheels, listened to the bearings, and told me what I already knew: after that much wear and tear, a tune-up would not be enough. So I had the whole thing overhauled.

While the bike was in pieces, so was I. Everyone warns you about culture shock when you leave. Nobody warns you about reverse culture shock when you come back. The supermarket felt enormous. My own kitchen felt like somebody else's. There were four hundred emails waiting, a backlog that grew every hour, and I was swamped before I had even unpacked. So I did the small things first. I ran errands. I got the mail sorted, I had the water heater looked at, and I got my sister to drive me around for a week, until my own car was roadworthy again.

It took about a month to readjust, and what helped was not rushing it. I had my best photographs printed and put them on the wall, one for each country, and every evening I sat with them and let the trip settle. Friends kept asking when the next one was. The honest answer was: not yet. First I had to recharge my batteries, get the house back in order, and remember how to be a person who stays. The road will still be there. So, for that matter, will the bike, and this time it is in better shape than I am.

Adapted from a long-distance cycling journal, The Long Way Home https://www.adventurecycling.org/

This text is full of the CAUSATIVE. You use it when somebody else does the work for you. THING: have / get + the THING + past participle. 'I HAD my bike OVERHAULED.' 'I GOT the mail SORTED.' PERSON: have + the PERSON + base form, or get + the PERSON + to + base form. 'I HAD the mechanic LOOK at it.' 'I GOT my sister TO DRIVE me.' The object always comes BEFORE the past participle: 'I had my bike serviced', never 'I had serviced my bike'.

Chapter 4: The Code

The Causative

Have and get something done

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I had my bike serviced before the trip."

"I got the brakes checked in the next town."

"I had the mechanic look at the front wheel."

"I got my neighbor to water the plants."

In all four, somebody else does the work. Only the shape changes.

You name...FormExample
the thinghave + object + past participleI had my bike serviced.
the thing (informal)get + object + past participleI got the brakes checked.
the personhave + person + base formI had the mechanic look at it.
the person (informal)get + person + to + base formI got my neighbor to water them.
bad newshave + object + past participleI had my wallet stolen.

The object always comes before the participle: "I had my bike serviced", never "I had serviced my bike".

Language Focus

Thing or Person?

AnalyseWhich type? (blue = you asked a PERSON to do it)
AI had my bike serviced before the trip.
BI got my neighbor to water the plants.
CI need to get the brakes checked this week.
DI had the mechanic look at the front wheel.
A and C are about a THING: have / get + the thing + past participle (serviced, checked). B and D, in blue, are about a PERSON: have + person + base form (had the mechanic LOOK), or get + person + to + base form (got my neighbor TO WATER). Ask yourself first: am I naming the job, or naming the person who does it?
Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

✗ WRONG

"I had serviced my bike at the workshop."

✓ RIGHT

"I had my bike serviced at the workshop."

✗ WRONG

"I got my sister drive me to the shop."

✓ RIGHT

"I got my sister to drive me to the shop."

Put the object first, then the participle. And after get plus a person, you always need to.

Say What You Had Done

Scenario 1

Check Your Work

Model Answers

Try the gap-fill and the scenarios first. Reveal the key only to compare.

+Reveal the answer key
Word order is everything. 'I had my bike serviced' means a mechanic did it. 'I had serviced my bike' means you did it yourself, earlier. And after 'get', a person needs 'to': 'I got my sister TO DRIVE me', never 'I got my sister drive me'.
  1. Gap-fill: 1 = dropped the bike off, 2 = had the whole bike, 3 = got the mail, 4 = had the water heater, 5 = got his sister to, 6 = had his best photographs.
  2. Model (a thing): 'I had my bike completely overhauled before the trip.'
  3. Model (a person): 'I got my neighbor to water the plants while I was away.'
  4. Structure: have / get + THING + past participle. Have + PERSON + base form. Get + PERSON + to + base form.
