From an Idea to Your Living Room

From an Idea to Your Living Room

Every Saturday night, a singing show is watched by millions of people, and almost nobody thinks about how it is made. Most of the work is invisible, and a lot of it starts in another country. The format was created in the Netherlands and was sold to dozens of countries. That means that the rules, the red chairs and even the order of the rounds are decided by the people who own the format, not by the local team. The local team decides everything else, and everything else is a lot.

Pre-production starts months before the first episode. Singers are found in auditions all over the country, and only a few of them are invited back. Songs are chosen with each singer, and then every song has to be cleared: permission is requested from the people who own it. If a song is not cleared in time, it is cut, and the singer is asked to choose another one. Rehearsals are scheduled around the band, the coaches and the studio, and the schedule changes almost every day.

On the day of a live show, a call sheet is sent to the whole crew the night before. It says who is needed, where, and at what time. During the show, every minute is controlled by the run of show, a document that is checked and changed until the last second. Hours of footage are recorded by fifteen cameras, and the music is mixed live for the audience at home.

Then post-production begins. The footage is edited, the music is mixed again, and the colors are corrected. A ninety-minute episode is made from about ten hours of material, so most of what was recorded is never seen. When the episode airs, the credits roll for about thirty seconds. Notice how often this text does not say who did the work. That is not an accident. In production, the work usually matters more than the name, and English has a structure for exactly that.

Chapter 4: The Code

The Work Comes First, the Name Later

When who did it is not the point

Grammar Discovery

Who Did It? The Sentence Does Not Say.

"The format was created in the Netherlands."

"Every song is cleared before rehearsal."

"Hours of footage are recorded by fifteen cameras."

"The call sheet was sent the night before."

What do the red words and the blue words have in common in all four sentences? And which two are about every week?

JobFormExample
The formbe + past participleis cleared · are recorded · was created · were sent
Every week, a routineis / are + participleThe call sheet is sent the night before. The vocals are tuned first.
One finished momentwas / were + participleThe song was cut last season. The singers were told that night.
Negative and questionis not / was not · Is it...? / Was it...?Song 7 was not cleared. Was the duet recorded? When is it sent?
Who did it, only if it matters+ by + the person or thingrecorded by fifteen cameras · decided by the format owners
Why use itthe work matters more than the name, or nobody knows the nameThe episode was edited overnight. (Who? It does not matter.)
The trapirregular participles, and verbs with no objectNot was choose: was chosen. Not the show was happened: the show happened.

In one line: be + participle puts the work at the front of the sentence, and the person who did it at the end, or nowhere.

One Question at a Time

1 / 6

Say where your show is recorded, without saying who records it.

  • Our show is recorded in a big studio outside the city.
  • is + participle: the place matters, not the people.

Say when the call sheet is sent on your team.

  • The call sheet is sent to the crew the night before.
  • Present passive for a routine that happens every week.

Say what happened to a song that was not cleared, last season.

  • It was cut, and the singer was asked to choose another one.
  • Past passive: was + participle, for one finished event.

Ask a question about the footage, in the passive.

  • How many hours of footage are recorded for one episode?
  • Question: are + subject + participle.

  • The order of the rounds was decided by the format owners.
  • Add by only when the person really matters.

  • Two passives in one sentence: the process, step by step.

The Hard Part Is Not the Cameras

The woman who clears every song you hear on a singing show. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. When is a song cleared, according to her?
Only when the permission comes back in writing. A request is sent to the publisher first.
2. What happened to the song for the semi-final last season?
It was chosen on a Monday, and the permission was refused on Thursday, two days before the show.
3. What was done after that, and when was the new song sung?
A new song was chosen, a new arrangement was written in twenty-four hours, and the band rehearsed it on Friday morning. It was sung live on Saturday.

Almost Everything Recorded Is Never Seen

An editor on what happens to the footage after the show. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. How much footage does he start with, and how long is the episode?
About ten hours of footage for a ninety-minute episode.
2. What is done while the show is happening, and what is ready by Sunday night?
Every camera is logged by an assistant while the show is happening. The first version is cut by Sunday night.
3. Why are whole performances cut, and what happened to the duet?
Because they arrive at the wrong moment in the story. The duet was perfect, but it was too long, so it was removed in the last version.
