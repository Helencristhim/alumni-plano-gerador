Decided before this conversation → going to. Deciding while you speak → will.

going to — the plan

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the text. Then answer the questions.

In short, I run a commercial desk: eight people, roughly twelve hundred corporate clients. I have been at the bank for twenty-three years — I joined in two thousand and one, when I was twenty-six. B In short.

B As far as I know.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

No new grammar today — four shapes, one map

Every question in November lands on one of these four. Know which one, and the sentence builds itself.

The job · the career

I oversee a desk. (always true)
I joined in 2001. (finished)
I have been here for 23 years. (still true)

The quarter · November

We are restructuring. (now)
I’m going to present. (planned)
I’ll take that one. (decided now)

To open a summary: In short, … · Basically, …
To turn the argument: That said, … · On the other hand, …
To hedge, instead of inventing: As far as I know, … · It depends on … · Roughly …
To land the plane: The point is, …

These eight are the reason fluent people never seem to pause. They pause — but they pause inside a phrase.
