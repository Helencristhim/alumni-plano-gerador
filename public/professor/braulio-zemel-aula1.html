Chapter 3: The Code

Three Tenses, One Story

I worked · I read · I am still settling in

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I worked at the same company for more than thirty years."

"I retired this year."

"I read three newspapers every morning."

"I am still settling into the new routine."

Two of the coloured verbs are finished, one is a habit, and one is still changing. Which is which?

FormWhat it saysExample
Past simple
worked · retired		Finished, and over. The time is closed: for thirty years, this year, in March."I worked there for thirty years."
"I retired this year."
Present simple
read · go · help		What is generally true, and what you do again and again: every morning, twice a week, usually."I read three newspapers every morning."
"I go to the club twice a week."
Present continuous
am settling · am looking		In progress right now, temporary, still changing. Perfect for a life that has just turned over."I am still settling into the routine."
"I am looking for something to take up."
Now that + presentThe new situation, given as the reason for the new habit. Two ideas in one short sentence."Now that I have time, I read the news properly."

Your entire story fits in these three. You do not need a fourth tense to be interesting — you need the right one, fast.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I am working there for thirty years."
"I worked there for thirty years."
"I would have liked to have been able to say what I do now."
"I wanted to say what I do now."
"Every morning I am reading three newspapers."
"Every morning I read three newspapers."

A closed period in the past takes the past simple, and a habit takes the present simple — the continuous is only for what is happening or changing now. And the middle pair is the one that matters most here: knowing the long structure is not a reason to use it. The short sentence you finish beats the elegant one you abandon.

Dialogue

The Seat Next to You

C
Business or pleasure?
B
Pleasure, this time. I have a house in Bahia, and I try to spend ten days there every month.
C
Every month? That sounds like an excellent arrangement. What do you do that lets you do that?
B
Nothing, now. I retired this year, after a long and very demanding career.
C
Congratulations. Do you miss it?
B
I miss the people. I do not miss arriving home at ten at night. Now that I have time, I read the news properly.
C
What kind of news? I ask because I edit a politics podcast, so it is professional curiosity.
B
Then we are going to get on well. Current affairs, mostly. American politics, the Middle East, Brazil. Ask me anything.
Comprehension

About Claire

1. What does Claire do for a living?
She edits a politics podcast.
2. Why does she ask what kind of news he reads?
Because of her work — it is professional curiosity.
3. What does she say about spending ten days a month in Bahia?
That it sounds like an excellent arrangement.
4. What does she want to know about his career?
Whether he misses it.
