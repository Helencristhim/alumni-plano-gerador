Forças a explorar

  • Lê business em inglês diariamente, por conta própria: o input escrito não precisa ser simplificado.

Pontos de melhoria

  • Concordância de 3ª pessoa no presente: conhece a regra e a perde em produção espontânea.
  • Transferência de estrutura do português — constrói a frase em português e a traduz.

Repertório já disponível

O vocabulário de M&A e o léxico corporativo que ele já reconhece entram como recurso dentro das situações comunicativas, sem constituírem objetivo central. Retomar o que ele já domina reduz o valor percebido da aula e favorece que ele assuma a condução.

Interesses produtivos

A venda e a expansão da própria empresa; estratégia corporativa, produtividade e comportamento humano, temas de sua leitura diária; corrida e musculação. Cultura pop, música e entretenimento genérico não funcionam como contexto — ele foi explícito sobre isso.

Condições de aprendizagem

Tempo fora da aula: relata não dispor de tempo adicional na agenda. As atividades prévias precisam caber em 15 a 20 minutos e ser autossuficientes.

Atenção e dispersão: relata dificuldade de sustentar a atenção em áudios longos. Trechos curtos, com apoio visual e com a tarefa apresentada antes da escuta, favorecem o engajamento.

Condução da aula: relata que, em experiências anteriores, acabava assumindo a direção das aulas. Um percurso explícito e previsível sustenta o enquadramento e reduz esse deslocamento.

Correção: pede correção direta e imediata dos próprios erros, e reage mal à tolerância percebida. Devolutivas curtas e objetivas funcionam melhor do que explicações longas.

Aula 01 · Reading / R1 — Lesson 01 — Before you read the email

1 · The email before the meeting

Before you read

You are going to read the first email from a buyer who has not met you yet. Before you open it: this kind of message always does two jobs at once.

Mark the three statements that are true of a buyer’s first approach.

It asks for information and, at the same time, tests how you answer.
Everything it asks for is a question with a question mark.
The date in it is usually the most important line.
It is written so that a slow reply costs you nothing.
Some of what looks like a question is actually an offer.
It expects a formal document in return.
A first approach is short on purpose. What it asks for is easy to send; what it is really measuring is whether you answer, how fast, and whether you understood which of the three things mattered.
Why the block opens here
You told us the thing you would fix first is understanding what people are saying. This block starts one step earlier: with a message you already understand — and with what happens after you understand it. Reading is the strongest thing you have. Saying it back is the part that stops.

2 · Key language

Vocabulary

Six expressions, six different jobs
None of them adds information. Each one tells the other person what you are doing with what they said — naming it, checking it, ranking it, or reading what is behind it. That is why they buy you time without sounding like you need time.

3 · What each line is doing

Reading
From: Diane Whitfield · Corporate Development, Halden Group
Dear Caio,

I lead corporate development at Halden Group, and your company came up twice in our screening of data and AI services in Brazil. I would rather start with a short conversation than with a formal process.

Before we meet, three things would help us prepare. First, how your revenue splits between recurring managed services and project work. Second, how much of your delivery already runs in English for clients outside Brazil. And third — and I realize this may be premature — whether a minority investment is something you would consider at all, or whether you are only open to a full sale.

We are putting our shortlist together on Thursday, so anything you can send before then goes into it.

Best regards,
Diane Whitfield

Each line below comes from the email. What is it doing?

“Your company came up twice in our screening.”
“How your revenue splits between recurring services and project work.”
“How much of your delivery already runs in English.”
“Whether a minority investment is something you would consider.”
“We are putting our shortlist together on Thursday.”
“I would rather start with a short conversation.”
The minority-investment line is not a question
Whether a minority investment is something you would consider reads like the smallest of the requests. It is the largest: they are telling you what they might do, and asking you to react. The two data questions can be answered by anyone in your company. That one only you can answer.

4 · Read it once more, for what it asks

Reading

Mark the three statements that are true of the email.

It asks three things, and none of them is numbered.
It asks for a formal presentation of the company.
The date matters more than any single one of the three answers.
It says they are only interested in buying the company outright.
One of the three questions is about the language your team delivers in.
It asks you to reply after the shortlist is closed.
Three requests, one date, no numbering — that is the standard shape. The date decides whether the other answers are worth anything, which is why it opens the reply.
The one people miss
The second question — how much of your delivery runs in English — is not small talk about your team. For a foreign buyer it is a risk question, and it is the one you are best placed to answer honestly and turn into a plan.

Aula 02 · Listening / L1 — Lesson 02 — Before you hear any of it

1 · They are talking about you

Before you listen

In the call you are about to hear, two people from the buyer’s side are talking to each other. You are on the line, and you have not spoken yet.

Mark the three statements that are true of that situation.

The most important information is often said in passing, not announced.
If you look lost, they will slow down.
Asking for a figure again tells them you are following.
Waiting until the end is the polite way to say you missed something.
A condition can be stated without ever being called a condition.
Anything that matters will be repeated later.
Nobody in that call is monitoring whether you are following. They cannot see you, and it is not their job. The one signal that works is the one you send: a question, at the moment you lose the thread.
Why this lesson exists
You said that if you could fix one thing, it would be understanding what people are saying. This lesson is that — but the product is not comprehension. It is what you do in the second where comprehension fails.

2 · Key language

Vocabulary

Choose the better reading for each expression. You will use all six live, in class.

Sorry, can you go back to…?
Could you say that number again?
Do you mean X or Y?
Just to check I got that: you said…
Before you move on — the part about…
Sorry?
Five of these are precise, one is not
Sorry? is the one everybody uses and the weakest of the six: it hands the choice of what to repeat back to the other person. The other five name the gap, and you get back exactly the piece you lost.

3 · What each line is doing

Listening
The call — transcript
Mark: So, on the Brazil one. I looked at the numbers last night. Recurring is somewhere around forty percent, which is lower than the two we saw in Mexico, but the retention is better. Peter, do you have the churn figure?

Peter: I have six point four percent annual, but that is from their own deck, so take it as directional. The thing I keep coming back to is delivery. If we fold them in, half of that delivery team is talking to our clients directly within a year.

Mark: Right, and that is the piece I want to hear from him on. Not whether they can do it. Whether they have done it. And look, if the answer is no, that is fine. It changes the price and it changes the timeline. It does not change whether we do this.

Peter: Agreed. One more thing before he joins. We should be clear that we are talking about a minority position first. Anything else and we spend six months on a structure nobody wants.

Each line below comes from the call. What is it?

“Recurring is somewhere around forty percent.”
“Six point four percent annual, but that is from their own deck.”
“If the answer is no, it changes the price and the timeline.”
“It does not change whether we do this.”
“The thing I keep coming back to is delivery.”
“We are talking about a minority position first.”
The one that is worth the most
“It changes the price and the timeline — it does not change whether we do this” is a condition, and nobody calls it one. It tells you that the honest answer costs you money and time, and not the deal. That is the single most useful thing said in those ninety seconds.

4 · What they actually said

Listening

Mark the three statements that are true of the call.

They compare the company to two others they have already looked at.
The churn figure comes from an independent source.
They want to hear whether the team has already delivered in English, not whether it could.
A no on English delivery ends the deal.
They have decided to open with a minority position.
They plan to spend six months on the structure.
Two of the four false statements are the comfortable readings: that the figures are solid, and that a no would be fatal. Neither is what was said.
Have done, not can do
Not whether they can do it — whether they have done it. That distinction is the whole question they will put to you, and the answer they are prepared to hear is the true one.

Aula 03 · Grammar / G1 — Lesson 03 — Before you build any of it

1 · Half of their questions are about people

Before you build

A buyer asks about numbers and about people, in roughly equal measure. The people questions are the ones you answer without preparing.

Mark the three statements that are true.

Describing who decides what is as much of the conversation as the figures.
A missing verb ending changes the meaning of the sentence.
The form slips most when the sentence gets long.
Knowing the rule is enough to say it right under pressure.
Correcting yourself mid-sentence is better than stopping to fix it.
If you speak slowly enough, the ending takes care of itself.
The ending does not carry meaning — nobody misunderstands he want. It carries something else: whether the person listening spends any attention on your English instead of on your answer.
Why this lesson, and why now
You named this one yourself: she like, he have. You know the rule — so this lesson explains almost nothing and drills a lot. The target is not knowing it. It is having it survive a long sentence about something else.

2 · Who does what

Vocabulary
Both sides of the table
Your side
• Head of Delivery — runs the delivery teams and decides who goes on which account.
• CFO — owns the numbers the buyer asked for, and signs off what leaves the company.
• Head of People — knows who on the team already works in English, and who does not.
• You — decide whether this conversation happens at all.

Their side
• Mark — runs finance, and wants the recurring revenue number confirmed.
• Peter — advises them, and keeps coming back to delivery.
• Diane — leads corporate development, and writes the emails.
• Their board — meets monthly, and approves the structure.

Who would answer each of these questions?

“How much of the revenue is recurring?”
“Who would be on our account in the first year?”
“How many of the team already work in English?”
“Would you consider a minority position?”
“Can we see the last three years audited?”
“Are you selling because you want out?”
Two of the six are only yours
Everything factual can be delegated. The two that cannot are the two about intention — and those are the ones that get asked in English, live, with no preparation.

3 · Four of these are right

Noticing

Mark the sentences that are correct. Two of the six are not.

Our Head of Delivery decides who goes on each account.
Their CFO want the recurring number confirmed.
The team handles three markets from São José.
She report to me, and I report to the board.
Their board meets once a month.
Peter keeps coming back to delivery.
Both of the wrong ones are third person singular with the ending missing. Nothing about them is hard to understand — and that is exactly why they survive for years.
What you are looking for
Do not look for the rule. Look at what is different between the four that are right and the two that are not, and say it out loud before you check — in class, that is the whole of stage 3.

4 · Finish the sentence

Practice

The whole form, in one line
He, she and it take the -s. I, you, we and they do not. American English treats the team and the board as one thing, so they take it too.

5 · Same information, different subject

Practice

The information does not change. Only the subject does — and with it, the verb.

I run the delivery side. → She …
They handle three markets. → He …
She has the audited accounts. → We …
He does the pricing. → They …
We decide the structure. → The board …
You know who speaks English. → My Head of People …
Where it actually slips
Not here. It slips three clauses into a sentence about something else, when the subject changed two clauses ago and you are thinking about the answer. That is what the ninety seconds in class are for.

Aula 04 · ESP / E1 — Lesson 04 — Before you time any of it

1 · Three minutes at the top

Before you time it

You get three minutes before anyone asks you anything. Most people spend them saying what the company does.

Mark the three statements that are true of those three minutes.

Their job is to make the buyer want to ask a question.
They should cover everything the company does.
What you leave out matters as much as what you say.
Detail builds credibility, so more of it is better.
The one thing only you can say belongs in them.
If you go over, they will stop you.
Nobody stops you at three minutes; they stop listening, which is worse because you cannot see it. Three minutes that end with a question from them is the whole objective.
This lesson closes the block
Everything from the last three lessons arrives here at once: saying it back, asking for it again, and keeping the form while you think about something else. Nothing new is introduced today.

2 · What belongs in three minutes

Preparation
The meeting brief
Who is in the room
Diane Whitfield (corporate development), Mark (finance) and Peter (adviser). They have spoken to two other companies in the region.

What they already know
Roughly what you do, roughly your size, and the numbers you sent on Thursday. Nothing about how you work.

What they came to decide
Whether to open a conversation about a minority position — not whether to buy the company.

What you have
Three minutes at the top of the call, and then their questions.

Where does each of these belong?

What the company does, in one sentence.
Why this conversation is worth their time.
The revenue split, in detail, by service line.
What you would want out of a minority position.
The full list of your enterprise clients.
What you think their board will decide.
The one people always put in the wrong place
What you would want out of a minority position feels like an answer to a question they have not asked. It is not: they said in their own call that this is what they came to decide. Saying it first is what makes the three minutes theirs.

“Four today. If we go ahead, that is the number I would be hiring against.”
“That would not be a problem for us.”
“Four — and I would rather tell you that now than in due diligence.”
“It depends on what you mean by delivered.”
The three that work all start with the number. Two of the three that do not are the comfortable answers, and both of them get checked in due diligence, where being wrong costs far more than being small.
Why the number first
They told each other, in their own call, that a no changes the price and the timeline and not whether they do the deal. The number is not the risk. Discovering it later is.

5 · The question you did not expect

Practice

One of the three is the move that keeps you in the conversation.

They ask something you did not prepare for.
You are not sure which of two things they are asking about.
You genuinely do not have what they are asking for.
They asked two things and you have only answered one.
They spoke fast and you lost the end of the question.
None of these is about English
A person who runs a company in Portuguese does all five of these every day without noticing. The only thing that is new is that they have to come out in another language, at the speed of the question.

6 · Your three minutes, written down first

Writing

Complete the skeleton. Five sentences, and the whole three minutes fits in them.

Write it about your own company — this is the one activity in the block where the invented version would be worth less.

a data and AI company, and what we actually do is solve one problem for our clients.

that we already do at scale what you are buying elsewhere.

a partner, and not an exit.

everything in English today, and I would rather say that now.

one hour with your delivery people, before anybody talks about structure.

This is what you will say with nothing on the screen
Five sentences is three minutes at a normal speaking pace. If it takes you longer than that to read them out loud, the sentences are too long — and long sentences are also where the verb endings go missing.

A · Objetivo e produto

Objetivo comunicativo: Ler o primeiro e-mail de um comprador estrangeiro e devolver em voz alta, em dois minutos, o que eles perguntaram, qual é o prazo e o que ele responde — sem o texto à vista.

B · Percurso da aula

C · Antes de abrir a aula
  • Leia o e-mail antes da aula. Ele traz três perguntas e um prazo, e as três estão embutidas no texto corrido — nenhuma está numerada.
  • A terceira pergunta (minority stake) é a que muda a conversa: não é um pedido de dado, é uma proposta disfarçada de pergunta. É por ela que ele deve fechar.
  • Ele lê bem. A dificuldade desta aula não é entender o e-mail — é sair dele. Se a aula virar tradução, ela perdeu o objeto.
  • O prazo (quinta-feira) é o item que mais some quando alguém devolve de memória. Se ele esquecer, não corrija na hora: anote e devolva na etapa 8.
D · Na condução
  • Feche o e-mail na tela antes da etapa 7. Com o texto à vista ele lê em voz alta, e ler em voz alta não é a operação que esta aula mede.
  • O turno é dele nos dois minutos. Não corrija durante. Anote e devolva no fim — ele pediu correção dura, e correção dura entregue na hora certa é o que faz diferença.
  • Se ele quiser usar um caso real da empresa dele em vez do e-mail inventado, aceite: o material real dele vale mais que o simulado.
  • Ele tende a assumir a condução. Se começar a propor o que fazer em seguida, devolva ao percurso: o roteiro está na barra de etapas, e ele pediu roteiro.
  • Esta é a primeira produção oral gravada dele em inglês. Diga isso — ele pediu evidência de progresso, e esta gravação é o marco zero.
E · O que observar na produção
  • Quantas das três perguntas ele nomeia sem consultar o e-mail, e se o prazo sobrevive.
  • Se ele traduz frase a frase ou reformula. Traduzir é o padrão declarado dele: 'penso em português, aí eu crio uma frase que é totalmente em português'.
  • O que ele CORTA nos 45 segundos. Se cortar o pedido ou o prazo, a hierarquia ainda não está clara.
  • Concordância de 3ª pessoa na produção livre — é a linha de base contra a qual a aula 3 vai medir.
  • Leitura isolada da fala: é o primeiro registro de compreensão escrita deste ciclo.

A · Objetivo e produto

Objetivo comunicativo: Descrever, em noventa segundos, quem faz o que dos dois lados da mesa — o time dele e o do comprador — com a concordância de 3ª pessoa estável enquanto a atenção está no conteúdo.

Produto principal: Descrição gravada de 90 segundos dos dois times, e a mesma descrição em 45 segundos com o foco no que cada um decide.

B · Percurso da aula

C · Antes de abrir a aula
  • O erro é dele e ele já o nomeou: she like, he have. Ele sabe a regra. O que falta é automatização — e é por isso que esta aula quase não explica.
  • As seis frases da etapa 3 são da produção dele nas aulas 1 e 2. Substitua pelo que ele realmente disse: quatro corretas e duas com o erro, sem dizer quais.
  • Se ele não produziu nenhuma frase de 3ª pessoa nas duas primeiras aulas, use as da tela como estão e registre isso — a ausência também é informação.
  • O drill da etapa 5 é rápido de propósito: doze frases, resposta imediata. Não pare para explicar no meio.
D · Na condução
  • Etapa 1 sem gramática nenhuma. Ele descreve o time dele em trinta segundos e você anota. Essa é a linha de base da aula, e ele não pode saber o que você está medindo.
  • Não dê a regra na etapa 3. Ele já a conhece; ouvi-la de novo é o que o irrita. Pergunte o que muda entre as frases e espere.
  • Na etapa 5, correção imediata e seca — ele pediu isso com todas as letras. Uma palavra: he wants. Sem explicação e sem consolo.
  • Nas etapas 7 e 8 não corrija durante. Anote e devolva no fim.
  • Se ele autocorrigir sozinho no meio da fala, diga isso a ele no fecho: autocorreção em tempo real é exatamente o que se está construindo aqui, e é medível.
E · O que observar na produção
  • A taxa de erro no drill contra a taxa na produção livre. A distância entre as duas é a medida da automatização — e é o registro mais importante desta aula.
  • Se ele autocorrige sem parar a fala, ou se para a frase inteira para consertar.
  • Se o erro volta quando a frase fica longa (é o padrão esperado).
  • Se a sintaxe portuguesa aparece — anote, mas não trate hoje: é o outro erro declarado e é do bloco 2.
  • Comparar com a produção da aula 1: as mesmas frases, dois dias antes, sem nenhum trabalho de forma.

C · Antes de abrir a aula

Lesson 01

Say It
Back

You understood the email.
The meeting starts with what you do next.

1 · What landed in your inbox

Someone is building a shortlist

Who is writing

Diane Whitfield runs corporate development at a foreign group. She has never met you, she has your company on a list with others, and she closes that list on Thursday. She would rather talk than open a process.

What does she want from you, and what will she do with it?

2 · Before you read

First contact, and a date at the end

A buyer writes for the first time. What are they measuring, besides the answers?
Which is worth more in that email — the questions, or the date?
Can a question be an offer? What would that look like?
3 · Read it once

Three things, and none of them numbered

From: Diane Whitfield · Corporate Development, Halden Group

Subject: Introduction — and a few questions

Dear Caio,

I lead corporate development at Halden Group, and your company came up twice in our screening of data and AI services in Brazil. I would rather start with a short conversation than with a formal process.

Before we meet, three things would help us prepare. First, how your revenue splits between recurring managed services and project work. Second, how much of your delivery already runs in English for clients outside Brazil. And third — and I realize this may be premature — whether a minority investment is something you would consider at all, or whether you are only open to a full sale.

We are putting our shortlist together on Thursday, so anything you can send before then goes into it.

Best regards,
Diane Whitfield

What are the three things? And what happens on Thursday?

4 · What they actually asked

Asked, implied, or not there at all

Whether you would take a minority investment.
That Thursday is the date that matters.
That they are talking to other companies.
How much of the revenue is recurring.
How much of the delivery runs in English.
How much they would pay.
5 · Repeating or answering

Two of these say something back

Which of these is saying it back — and which is just repeating it?

“You said that you lead corporate development at Halden Group and that our company came up twice in your screening.”
“What you are asking is three things, and the third one is the one I can answer today.”
“I read your email. It is very interesting. Thank you for contacting us.”
“Let me make sure I have this right: you need this before Thursday, not after we meet.”
Repeating gives the sender their own sentence back. Saying it back gives them your reading of it — what you understood, in fewer words than they used.
6 · Say it back

Six openings, out loud

What they are asking is…
They want to know whether…
The deadline they gave is…
Let me make sure I have this right…
The part I would answer first is…
What they are really proposing is…

Your teacher gives you the content. You give it back, with the opening.

7 · Your two minutes

Tell your partner what just came in

Your teacher is your partner.
They have not read the email, and they decide today whether the meeting is worth taking.

What did they ask, when do they need it, and what do you answer first?

Open by naming what they asked — one sentence, not three.
Say the date out loud, before anyone asks for it.
Say which of the three you would answer first, and why it is that one.
Read the offer behind the third question.
Close with what you would send her today.
What they are asking is…  ·  They want to know whether…  ·  The deadline they gave is…
Let me make sure I have this right…  ·  The part I would answer first is…  ·  What they are really proposing is…
8 · Forty-five seconds

Again, to someone already late

Same partner. They are walking to another meeting.

Forty-five seconds. What goes first, and what goes?

What changes when the clock does
The three questions can wait. The date cannot, and neither can the one line that says what this person is actually proposing — those two are the reason your partner needs to know at all.

An email from a buyer or an investor that you will answer this week — and roughly when it arrives.

Who is on the line

Mark runs finance on the buyer's side. Peter is the adviser they brought in. They have looked at two other companies in the region already, and they are five minutes into the call before you join.

What are they deciding, before you say anything?

2 · Before you listen

One line, and a guess

“So, on the Brazil one. I looked at the numbers last night…”

Is he about to say what impressed him, or what worries him?

3 · Listen once

Ninety seconds, no transcript

What are the two of them worried about — and it is not the numbers?
What did they decide to open with?

4 · Listen again

Said, implied, or not there at all

Speed

One of the six was not in the call. Finding it is the point.

They will open with a minority position.
They are looking at other companies in the region.
They think the price would come down.
A no on English delivery would end the deal.
Recurring revenue is around forty percent.

5 · The four moves

Two of these get it back

Two of these get you the missing piece back. Which two?

“Sorry?”
“Could you say that number again? The churn one.”
“I did not understand. Can you repeat everything?”
“Just to check I got that: a no changes the price, not the deal?”
The two that work say which piece is missing. The two that do not hand the choice back to the other person — and they will repeat what they think mattered, which is rarely the part you lost.
6 · Ask for it back

Five repairs, out loud

Speed
Sorry, can you go back to…?
Could you say that number again?
Do you mean X, or Y?
Just to check I got that: you said…
Before you move on — the part about…

Your teacher stops the call four times. You say the repair, there and then.

7 · Your turn on the call

You just joined, and nobody stops for you

Your teacher is Diane. The call is already running, and she is speaking fast.

Get one thing repeated, and confirm one thing you understood. Nobody will invite you in.

Come in without waiting to be asked.
Ask for one figure again, and name which one.
Check one thing you understood, in a single sentence.
Answer the question they have been waiting to put to you.

Same call, one repair only

A quarter faster, and you get to stop them once.

Lesson 03

He Wants,
She Needs

Eight people, ninety seconds.
The hard part is not who they are.

1 · Say it once

Four people around you

Thirty seconds. No notes.

Who are the four people closest to you at work, and what does each one decide?

2 · Both sides of the table

Eight roles, one line each

Your side

Head of Delivery — runs the delivery teams and decides who goes on which account.
CFO — owns the numbers the buyer asked for, and signs off what leaves the company.
Head of People — knows who on the team already works in English, and who does not.
You — decide whether this conversation happens at all.

Their side

Mark — runs finance, and wants the recurring revenue number confirmed.
Peter — advises them, and keeps coming back to delivery.
Diane — leads corporate development, and writes the emails.
Their board — meets monthly, and approves the structure.

Which two of their questions could nobody but you answer?

3 · Four of these are right

Two of them are not

Four of these six are correct. Which two are not?

Our Head of Delivery decides who goes on each account.
Their CFO want the recurring number confirmed.
The team handles three markets from São José.
She report to me, and I report to the board.
Their board meets once a month.
Peter keeps coming back to delivery.
Both wrong ones are third person singular with the ending gone. Neither is hard to understand — which is precisely why they last.
4 · The form under it

One line, and you already know it

he · she · it → the verb takes -s
He runs delivery. She reports to me. It depends on the board.

I · you · we · they → nothing
I decide. They handle three markets. We hold the majority.

In American English, the team, the board and the company are one thing — so they take the -s too. The two irregular ones that come up constantly: has and does.

Does the team take it? Does my partners and I?

5 · Twelve, fast

Same information, different subject

Their CFO
The delivery teams
Their board
My partners and I

Your teacher gives you the new subject. You say the sentence again, immediately.

6 · Your two teams

Eight sentences, one at a time

The chart is still on screen. Use it.

One sentence per person: what does each of them decide?

He runs…  ·  She reports to…  ·  Their CFO wants…
The team handles…  ·  Their board approves…  ·  My partners and I hold…
7 · Ninety seconds

Both sides, nothing on screen

Your teacher is a partner who has never met any of these people.

Who is on each side of this table, and what does each one decide?

Start with your side, and say what each person decides — not what they are called.
Move to theirs, and say what each one wants from you.
Finish with the two questions only you can answer.
If the form slips, correct it and keep going. Do not restart the sentence.
8 · Forty-five seconds

Same eight, half the time

Now your partner only wants to know one thing about each of them.

What does each person decide? Forty-five seconds.

What the clock does to the form
Faster is where the ending goes missing — not because you forget the rule, but because there is no attention left over for it. Holding it here is the whole difference between knowing a form and having one.

8 · Feedback & close

Today, and where it goes

How confident do you feel right now?
Where you would use it
Next one
A conversation where you describe your team and theirs — and roughly when.
What to try
Say who does what on both sides, without going back to fix the verb.
One phrase

Lesson 04

Three Minutes
with the Buyer

Then their questions.
You have not seen them.

1 · The meeting

They came to decide one thing

The brief

In the room: Diane (corporate development), Mark (finance), Peter (adviser). They have spoken to two other companies in the region.

What they already know: roughly what you do, roughly your size, and the numbers you sent on Thursday. Nothing about how you work.

What they came to decide: whether to open a conversation about a minority position — not whether to buy the company.

What you have: three minutes at the top of the call, and then their questions.

What has to be inside those three minutes, and what can wait?

2 · Try it once

Three minutes, starting now

No preparation, no notes, nothing on the screen.

Tell them what your company is, and why this conversation is worth their hour.

3 · What they came for

In the three minutes, or in the answers

What the company does, in one sentence.
Why this conversation is worth their time.
The revenue split by service line.
What you would want out of a minority position.
Your full client list.
What you think their board will decide.
4 · The honest no

Three of these keep the conversation

Three of these keep the conversation. Which three?

“Four have delivered in English. Everyone else has worked with English-speaking clients, but not led.”
“All of them can work in English.”
“Four today — and that is the number I would be hiring against.”
“That would not be a problem for us.”
“Four, and I would rather tell you now than in due diligence.”
All three that work start with the number. The two that do not are the comfortable answers — and both get checked later, where being wrong costs more than being small.
5 · The four moves

For the question you did not expect

That is a fair question — the way we handle it is…
Before I answer, can I check what you mean by…?
We do not have that today. What we do have is…
Let me come back to your first point…

Your teacher gives you the situation. You answer with the move.

6 · Round one

Three minutes, then two questions

Your teacher is Diane. Mark and Peter are on the call and do not speak.

Nothing on the screen. When they ask, answer — and ask again if you need to.

Speed
7 · Feedback

Two things, and one number

What gets measured today
How long the three minutes actually took. Whether the question that came too fast got asked again, or got answered over. Whether the number came first in the honest no. Those three are the block, and they are now on record.

Which of the two questions was harder — and was it the English, or the answer?

8 · Round two

The opening only, in sixty seconds

Same buyer. She has ten minutes before her next call, and she says so.

Sixty seconds. What survives, and what goes?

What sixty seconds is for
Not a shorter pitch — a different one. Cut the description and keep the ask, and the meeting after this one exists. Keep the description and cut the ask, and it does not.
