Chapter 3: The Code

Who Believes It, Who Checked It

The impersonal passive and the causative

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

“It is expected that the market will recover in the second half.”

“The decline is believed to be temporary.”

“We had the forecast reviewed by our two largest clients.”

“In my first month I had the accounts audited by an outside firm.”

In which sentences do we know who did the work, and who asked for it?

FormWhat it doesExample
It is + expected / said / believed + that...Reports a general view without naming who holds itIt is expected that the market will recover.
Subject + is believed / thought + to be / to have...The same idea, starting with the thing you are talking aboutThe decline is believed to be temporary.
have + object + past participleYou arranged it, and somebody else did the workWe had the forecast reviewed by two clients.
In an interviewUse the first two to report the market. Use the third to show that a number was checked, and by whom. A board trusts the passive that names the check.

Reading

Read for the Main Idea

Why boards stop listening to the finance director

It is often said that boards do not understand numbers. Most of the non-executive directors interviewed for this note disagreed. They understand numbers well enough; what they do not understand is a presentation that starts with forty lines of a spreadsheet and never says which three of them matter.

The finance directors who are listened to work the other way round. They open with one sentence that a person from sales or from the creative side could repeat in the corridor: revenue is up, the margin is down, and this is why. Only then do they separate what happened once from what will still be there next quarter. A one-off cost explained in the first minute is a detail. The same cost discovered in the appendix is a credibility problem.

The third habit is about where the numbers came from. A forecast that is simply described as expected tells a board very little. A forecast that was reviewed by the two largest clients, or that the CFO had checked by an outside firm, tells the board that somebody did the work. Directors said they trusted the passive when it named the check, and distrusted it when it hid the person behind the number.

None of this requires simpler numbers. It requires a finance director who has decided, before the meeting, which sentence the board should leave with, and who can say it in plain English.

Adapted from board practice notes on finance reporting

Dialogue

As You Would to My Board

M
I came up through the creative side. Explain last year to me as you would to my board.
F
In one sentence: revenue was up eight percent year on year, and the margin was down one point.
M
Down one point. Should my board be worried?
F
Half of that point was a one-off, the office move. The other half is a headwind from the exchange rate, and that one will still be there.
M
And next year? Everybody tells me the market is coming back.
F
It is expected that the market will recover in the second half, but I would not build the plan on that.
M
So what would you build it on?
F
On the volume our two largest clients have confirmed. We had the forecast reviewed by both of them before it went to the board.
M
That I could repeat in a corridor.
F
That is the version I would give your board. I am happy to show the detail behind it if it is useful.
Comprehension

Explain last year as he would to her board, because she came up through the creative side.

3. What would he build next year’s plan on?
On the volume the two largest clients confirmed, after he had the forecast reviewed by both of them.
