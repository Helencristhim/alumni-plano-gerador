Chapter 1: The Paper on the Table

Something You Recommended Out Loud, and Then Had to Write Down

Which of the two versions took you longer — and which one did people act on?

The Real Cost

Two Ways to Put the Same Thing on the Table

A request

"We would like the regional limit to be raised, if possible, and we hope the board can look at it."

Gets a discussion. Gets an opinion. Gets postponed to the next meeting.

A proposal

"It is proposed that the regional limit be raised to thirty-five million, subject to approval of the treaty."

Gets read out and voted on, in the words you chose.

Same person, same authority, same numbers. The only difference between the two columns is a verb form — and the second one does not exist in the English most people learn by speaking.

Today's Goal

Three Missions

1. The Words

proposal, safeguard, undertaking...

2. The Code

it is proposed that the limit be raised

3. The Paper

one page to your regional leaders, in board register

Chapter 3: The Code

The Verb That Refuses to Agree

Where correct English looks like a typing error

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I recommend that the limits be revised before the renewal."

"The board requires that each region submit its schedule by Friday."

"It is essential that this not be delayed until February."

"If I were to propose a single change, it would be this one."

Three of these verbs do not agree with their subject, and none of them is a mistake. What is being marked?

TriggerWhat followsExample
Verbs of recommendation and demandrecommend · propose · request · require · insist · demand · ask · move · urge — then that + subject + bare verbThe board requires that each region submit its schedule.
Adjectives of necessityit is essential · vital · imperative · critical · advisable · necessary — then the same structureIt is imperative that the treaty be signed first.
The bare verb, in fullNo -s, no does, no tense at all: that he go, that she report, that it lapse. The verb is frozen in its dictionary form, whoever the subject is.
The negativeJust not, straight in front: that this not be delayed. Never that this does not be delayed, never that this is not delayed.
The passive — the one you will write mostBoard papers are written in the passive, so the form you need is be + past participle: that the limit be raised, that the deferral be recorded, that it be reported within five days. Never that the limit is raised.
Hypothetical wereIn formal writing, were replaces was for anything hypothetical: If I were to propose one change · If it were up to me · and inverted, with no if at all: Were the board to defer, the treaty would not wait.
Why it feels wrongYour first language has this form too, and there you can hear it: the ending of the verb changes. In English nothing is added — the -s is taken away, and the absence is the mark. It looks like a typing error, which is exactly why so few non-native drafts contain it.

The Structure

One Recommendation, Four Registers

Same content, four documents. Choosing the wrong rung costs you in both directions.

Spoken · the corridor

"Can we look at the limits before the renewal?"

Neutral e-mail · a colleague

"Could I ask you to revise the limits before the renewal?"

Formal recommendation · a directive

"I recommend that the limits be revised before the renewal."

Board paper · for decision

"It is proposed that the limits be revised prior to renewal, subject to approval of the treaty."

The top rung in a corridor sounds absurd. The bottom rung in a board paper sounds like somebody asking permission.

Reading

Board Paper 16/4

Board Paper 16/4 -- Regional Underwriting Authority, Large Industrial Risks

This paper is submitted for decision. It proposes that regional underwriting authority for large industrial risks be raised from twenty to thirty-five million, and that the increase be reviewed after four quarters rather than made permanent at this stage. The rationale is set out in section two and the safeguards in section three.

The Directorate recommends the increase on two grounds. Cases between twenty and thirty-five million currently take an average of eleven working days to clear the group referral process, and in four instances during the year the broker had placed the risk elsewhere before the referral returned. The Directorate does not argue that the referral process be abandoned. It argues that the threshold no longer matches the market it was written for.

Three safeguards are proposed, and the Directorate regards the third as a prerequisite rather than a refinement. First, that every case written under the raised authority be reported to the group within five working days. Second, that the increase be subject to approval of the reinsurance treaty currently under negotiation. Third, that the authority lapse automatically at the end of the fourth quarter unless the board ratifies it again. The Directorate has given an undertaking to that effect and asks that it be recorded in the minutes.

Two matters remain at the board's discretion. The paper stipulates a single regional limit; the board may prefer that each market be treated separately, which the Directorate would not oppose although it would slow implementation. The paper also proposes that the decision be binding from January. Were the board to prefer a later start, the Directorate requests that the deferral be recorded with a date, since the treaty negotiation will not wait.

Board paper -- Commercial Directorate, submitted for the November meeting

The Form That Looks Like a Typing Error

A director on learning the written register late. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What did she assume the first time she saw "be revised" in a draft?
That the person editing her draft had made a mistake, and she nearly corrected it back.
2. What difference does she describe between the two languages?
In Portuguese the subjunctive is audible in the ending of the verb; in English nothing is added — the -s is taken away, so it looks like a typing error.
3. Where does she say the form actually lives?
Not in speech. In papers, in minutes, in anything that will be read by people who were not in the room.
4. What changed when she started using it?
The tone of the replies changed before the content did — the same recommendation stopped being received as a request.
Chapter 5: Practice

Your Own Recommendation

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "The board requires that each region submits the schedule by Friday."
"The board requires that each region submit the schedule by Friday." (after require + that, the bare verb: no -s)
2. "It is essential that this is not delayed until February."
"It is essential that this not be delayed until February." (the negative subjunctive: not + be, never is not)
3. "I solicit that the deadline be extended by two weeks."
"I request that the deadline be extended by two weeks." ("solicitar" is not "solicit" — in American English that verb means something else entirely, and never appears in a board paper)
4. "We will inform the decision to the regional leaders on Monday."
"We will inform the regional leaders of the decision on Monday." (in English you inform a PERSON of something — the thing never comes first)

Your versionWhat is the single sentence you would want read aloud and voted on, unchanged?
1. Why do the safeguards sit inside the recommendation and not at the end?
Because a board that can see the limit of what it is approving approves faster. A safeguard at the end reads as a disclosure; in the recommendation it reads as design.
2. Why name two matters as the board's own to decide?
Because a board that is given nothing to decide will find something. Naming the two you can live with protects the sentence you cannot.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I write "it is proposed that the limit be raised", not "we would like it to be raised".
I leave the -s off the verb after recommend, require and propose, and I trust it.
I can write the negative and the passive: that this not be delayed, that it be reported.
I put the safeguard and the expiry date inside the recommendation itself.
I name what is binding, and what remains at the board's discretion.
