The Update That Says What You Decided

The Update That Says What You Decided

Every closing update that Laura Mendel receives is, in her words, a list of decisions dressed up as a list of facts. The BPO sent two entries late. The tax file arrived on day four. What she actually wants to hear is what you did about it, and that, she says, lives almost entirely in the verbs. I decided to escalate it. I managed to close on time anyway. I avoided posting anything until the numbers were confirmed. Three sentences, three decisions, and every one of them tells her something about the person speaking.

She has noticed that these verbs come in two families, and that people who mix them up sound less sure of their own decisions. Some verbs are followed by to and a base verb: decide to, agree to, plan to, refuse to, manage to. They tend to look forward, toward an action. Others take the -ing form: avoid, consider, recommend, risk, keep, finish. I recommend reviewing the entries once more is a recommendation. I recommend to review sounds, to her ear, like a translation.

The most useful verb in a difficult update, she says, is the one people least like to use: refuse. I refused to post the adjustment without support is not a confession of being difficult. It is evidence of a thorough review, and directors trust it more than any promise. The second most useful is risk, because it puts a price on a choice: if we post it today, we risk reopening the period in March.

And the verb she hears least, and wishes she heard more, is a phrasal one. I kept putting off the conversation with the BPO, and then I decided to have it on Monday. It is honest, it shows a change, and it uses both families in one breath. Most people, she says, have a sentence like that about their English as well. They just have not considered saying it out loud yet.

Chapter 4: Verbs That Choose Their Form

To, or -ing?

The first verb decides, and it does not change its mind

Grammar Discovery

Which Verbs Take To?

"I decided to escalate the issue directly to the director."

"We agreed to submit the report by the end of the day."

"I avoided sending the email before confirming the figures."

"We kept following up with the BPO until they answered."

Look at the second verb in each sentence. After the blue verbs, what comes first? After the orange ones, what ending does the second verb take? Who decides — you, or the first verb?

FamilyVerbsExample
verb + to + base verbdecide, agree, plan, expect, offer, refuse, manage, need, hope, prepareI decided to escalate it.
verb + -ingavoid, consider, finish, keep, suggest, recommend, risk, mind, enjoy, practiceI recommend reviewing the entries.
phrasal verb + -ingput off, keep on, give upI kept putting off the call.
stop: two meaningsstop to = pause in order to · stop -ing = no longer do itI stopped to check one figure. / I stopped checking it.
remember: two meaningsremember -ing = a memory · remember to = not forget a taskI remember sending it. / Remember to send it.
the trapnever mix the familiesNot decided escalating. Not recommend to review.

In one line: the first verb chooses the form of the second — learn each verb together with its partner.

The Call I Kept Putting Off

An accountant on the regional call she avoided for years.

0:000:00
1. What did she use to do instead of presenting, and why?
She prepared everything and let her manager present it, because she was afraid of getting stuck in the middle of a sentence.
2. What happened the month her manager was on vacation?
She had to present. She did not manage to say everything she had planned, and she made at least four mistakes.
3. What did people remember, and what did she decide after that?
That the numbers were finally explained by the person who built them. She decided to volunteer every quarter.
Real Document

The Closing Update

TEAMS · CLOSE UPDATE — DAY 5
keitma gomes · to: regional finance director

Status: closed on time.

What happened: the BPO sent two accrual entries two days late.

What I decided: I decided to post one of them manually, with full support attached. I refused to post the second one without the invoice, so it moves to next month. I avoided reopening the period.

What I recommend: I recommend agreeing a cut-off with the BPO for day two. If we keep accepting entries on day four, we risk missing the deadline in a heavier month.

Next step: I plan to discuss the cut-off with them on Monday.

Note: one line of problem, four lines of decisions.
1. What did Keitma decide to do with the first late entry?
To post it manually, with full support attached.
2. Why does the second entry move to next month?
Because she refused to post it without the invoice.
3. What does she say they risk if they keep accepting entries on day four?
Missing the deadline in a heavier month.
