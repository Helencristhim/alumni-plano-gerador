Chapter 3 -- The Code

Three Ways to Promise the Future

will / going to / present continuous

Discovery

Listen and Notice

All four are about the future. What makes each one different?

"That is a good point -- I will chase it up this afternoon."

"We are going to escalate it, as we agreed on Monday."

"I am meeting counsel at nine on Thursday."

"Look at the backlog -- the date is going to slip."

FormWhen the decision was takenExample
will + base verbright now, while you are speaking; also a promise or an offer"I will chase it up this afternoon."
be going to + base verbbefore you spoke: the plan or intention already existed"We are going to escalate it, as we agreed."
be going to (prediction)the evidence is on the table right now"Look at the backlog -- the date is going to slip."
present continuousalready arranged: there is a time and a person"I am meeting counsel at nine on Thursday."
will (prediction)an opinion about the future, with no evidence in the room"I think the supplier will accept."

Key idea: a follow-up email uses all three in one paragraph. What you decide on the spot is will; what the meeting already decided is going to; what is in the diary with a time is the present continuous. Never write "I will meeting" and never write "we are going to sign off it" -- the object goes between: "sign it off" or "sign off on it".

The Follow-up Note

Follow-up note -- Q3 board meeting, two weeks on

Two weeks after the board meeting, five actions are on the tracker and two of them are still outstanding. The Houston budget is closed: the board signed off on it on Thursday, and nothing further is needed from anyone on this note.

The supplier quote has not arrived. Procurement is carrying a three-week backlog after the holiday, so this is a queue problem rather than a decision problem. I am going to chase it up today, and if the quote is not with us by Friday the installation date is going to slip by a fortnight.

The contract review is the real bottleneck. Legal has not moved on it for two weeks, and everything downstream is waiting for that one signature. I have already sent one nudge and made one call. I am meeting counsel at nine on Thursday, and I have told her plainly that if the review has not moved by Friday afternoon I will escalate it to the board, so that nobody is surprised when it appears on the agenda.

Two smaller points. The Houston site visit is booked: I am meeting the local team at nine on Thursday the fourteenth. And on the insurance renewal, which has not started, we are going to loop in counsel before we reply to the broker. Finance and I will touch base on Wednesday. Please reply to this note only if a date on it is wrong.

Listening 2

Why "I Will Look Into It" Fails

Sound first -- no text on screen.

0:000:00
1. What is wrong with writing "I will look into it"?
It has no owner and no date, so nobody can tell whether it is a decision, an intention or a polite no.
2. What three things does she put in every action item?
A verb, an owner and a date -- and the right future form for each one.
3. When does she use the present continuous instead of "will"?
When the thing is already arranged, with a time and a person in the diary.
4. What does she do before she escalates something?
She nudges once, chases up once, and tells the owner the date she is going to escalate -- so nobody is surprised.
