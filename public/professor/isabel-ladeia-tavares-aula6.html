What Was Done, and In What Order

The methods section is written for somebody you will never meet, who has to run your experiment again without asking you anything. Everything strange about it comes from that.

Chapter 1: The Stranger Who Repeats It

Could Somebody Else Run It?

Last week you turned something down in one sentence, with no excuse and a real alternative. Do it once more, now, about something on your calendar this month.

Now the question of the day. Think of an experiment you ran this year. If you disappeared tomorrow, could somebody repeat it from what is written down?

Today’s Goal

Three Things a Method Has to Do

1. The Words

replicate, apparatus, to pool, to thaw…

2. No People

the thing comes first, the person goes

3. An Exact Order

what was already finished, and what came next

A method can be completely true and still be useless. If the order is missing, the reader in another building cannot run it, and that is the only thing this section exists to make possible.

Two Lines

Same Steps, Different Story

"The samples were stored at minus twenty degrees and were analyzed in March."

"The samples had been stored at minus twenty degrees before they were analyzed in March."

Almost the same words. One of them tells you something the other does not. Say what, out loud, in one sentence.

Chapter 3: The Code

The Words That Carry the Order

And the verb form that says: this one had already finished

Grammar Discovery

Which One Had Already Finished?

The samples were collected in March. Prior to analysis, they had been stored at minus twenty degrees.

Following collection, each sample was pooled with two replicates.

Once the apparatus had been calibrated, measurements were taken every six hours.

The control group was not treated at any point and was subsequently discarded.

Nobody appears in any of them, and that part you already know. What is new is in blue and in one of the red forms: two of these steps had already finished before the step next to them.

UseFormExample
A finished step (you know this)was / were + third formThe samples were collected in March.
A step that had already finished before ithad been + third formThey had been stored at minus twenty.
Before, in a paperprior to + noun, never a verbPrior to analysis, the tubes were thawed.
After, in a paperfollowing + noun, never a verbFollowing incubation, the plates were photographed.
Later in the same sequencesubsequently + the verbThe plates were subsequently discarded.
The trapbeen never disappearsNot The apparatus had calibrated.

In one line: had been marks what was already done, and prior to and following take a noun, not a verb.

Reading

Who Is It Written For?

Written for a Stranger

Every section of a scientific paper has a reader in mind, and the methods section has the strangest one of all. The introduction is written for somebody deciding whether to care. The discussion is written for somebody who wants to argue. The methods section is written for a person you will never meet, working in another building, who has to run your experiment again next year without asking you a single question. Everything odd about the way it is written comes from that one fact.

That is why the people disappear. Nobody writes I put the tubes in the incubator, because the reader does not need to know that it was you, and next year it will not be. The tubes were incubated. The samples were collected in March. The control group was not treated at any point. The thing that had something done to it moves to the front of the sentence, the person is removed, and what is left is the only part that can be repeated. It is not modesty and it is not bad style. It is the section doing its job.

The second thing that carries the whole section is order, and order is not obvious from the verbs alone. Two steps written side by side can be simultaneous, or one can depend on the other having already finished, and the reader cannot tell which unless you say so. That is the work done by prior to, following, once and subsequently, and by the form that marks something as already finished before the step you are describing: the apparatus had been calibrated before the first measurement was taken. Take those words out and the section is still true, and no longer reproducible.

Which is where most first drafts fail, and it is worth knowing before you write your own. The steps are usually all there, and the problem is that they are only listed. Somebody who was in the room can read the draft and follow it perfectly, because they already know what happened, so the writer never notices. The reader in another building cannot. If you take one habit from this section, take this one: write it, then read it as somebody who was not there.

The Paper We Could Not Repeat

Four weeks, nine hundred reais, and no result. Write down the three things that were missing. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What three things were missing from the paper?
The temperature at which the samples were stored between collection and analysis; whether the replicates were pooled before or after the first measurement; and a sentence about the control group that could be read two ways.
2. What does he say about the original authors, and why does that matter?
Nobody was hiding anything, and he is fairly sure the work itself is fine. Somebody wrote the section, read it back, and it made sense to them because they had been standing there.
3. What test does he give the group for their own methods section?
Not whether it is true. Whether somebody in another building could run it on a Tuesday without emailing you.

Listening 2 · A Voice Message

What Happened While You Were Away

A colleague looked after your samples for a week. Put the events back in the order they happened, not the order she says them. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. One thing happened before everything else she describes. What was it, and why did she do it?
The whole rack had already been relabeled, because two of the labels had come off in the freezer and she did not want to guess.
2. What happened on Monday, and why was it not done in the water bath?
The tubes were taken out of the freezer and thawed on the bench, for about forty minutes, because the water bath was broken again.
3. What is the one thing she does not know, and what does she tell you to do about it?
Whether the tubes had been thawed once before, in June. She says to ask the person who ran it then, and to write it down, because it belongs in your methods.
Real Document

A Draft With Your Name on It

2. MATERIALS AND METHODS
undergraduate research project · draft 1 · I. Ladeia Tavares

2.1 Sample collection. Sixty samples were obtained from three separate collection sites in March. Prior to transport, each sample was labeled and sealed. All samples were subsequently stored at minus twenty degrees Celsius.

2.2 Preparation. The tubes were thawed on the bench for forty minutes. Following thawing, the replicates were pooled, three into one. The procedure was carried out at room temperature throughout.

2.3 Measurement. Once the apparatus had been calibrated against a known standard, measurements were taken every six hours for four days.

2.4 Controls. A control group of twelve samples was not treated at any point. It was stored under the same conditions and measured on the same days.

Supervisor’s note: this is close. Read it once as somebody who was not in the room.
1. Two things in this draft had already finished before the step next to them. Which two?
The labeling and sealing, which happened prior to transport, and the calibration of the apparatus, which is marked with had been calibrated before the measurements were taken.
2. Section 2.4 says the control group was not treated. What else does it have to say, and does it say it?
It has to say that the control group was kept under the same conditions and measured on the same days, otherwise the comparison means nothing. It does say it, in the last sentence.
3. One order in this draft is still missing. Which step could a stranger not place, and what sentence would fix it?
Whether the samples were labeled before or after they were collected at each site is clear, but nothing says when the sixty samples were divided into replicates. A sentence such as Prior to storage, each sample was divided into three replicates would fix it.
