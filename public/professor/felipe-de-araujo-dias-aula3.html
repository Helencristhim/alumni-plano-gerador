Chapter 3: Softer, Not Weaker

The Same Question, Two Doors

How English makes a question polite without making it weak

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Could you tell me when the shipment leaves?"

"Do you know if the quote includes freight?"

"I was wondering whether you could confirm the date."

"Would you mind telling me who signs the purchase order?"

Direct questionIndirect questionWhat changes
When does it ship?Could you tell me when it ships?No does. The verb keeps the -s.
Is the quote final?Do you know if the quote is final?Add if or whether. Subject before verb.
Who signs this?Would you mind telling me who signs this?Who is already the subject, so nothing moves.
Can you deliver in March?I was wondering whether you can deliver in March.A statement, so no question mark.

The polite opener carries the question. Everything after it goes back to normal order: subject, then verb.

Grammar Practice

Open the Same Door Politely

Say the full question, then click to compare

"Could you tell me when the second batch leaves?"
2. Is the lead time still forty days? (Do you know...)
"Do you know if the lead time is still forty days?"
3. Who confirmed the purchase order? (I was wondering...)
"I was wondering who confirmed the purchase order."
4. Can you send the quote today? (Would you mind telling me...)
"Would you mind telling me whether you can send the quote today?"
Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"Could you tell me when does the shipment leave?"
"Could you tell me when the shipment leaves?"
"Do you know where is the factory?"
"Do you know where the factory is?"
"I was wondering that you can confirm the date."
"I was wondering whether you can confirm the date."

Only the first part is a question. After when, where, who, if or whether, the sentence goes back to normal order and the auxiliary disappears.

Dialogue

The Call That Moves a Date

F
Good afternoon, Priya. Thank you for taking the call. I have three questions about the spring order.
P
Of course, Felipe. Go ahead.
F
Could you tell me when the first batch leaves the factory?
P
The eighteenth of March, if the fabric arrives on time.
F
I see. And do you know if that date is already in the purchase order?
P
It is not. The order still says the fifth of March. I will correct it today.
F
Thank you. I was wondering whether the quote includes freight to Santos.
P
It does not, I am afraid. Freight is quoted separately, and the port is busy this month.
F
That is useful to know. Would you mind telling me who I should write to about it?
P
Write to me. I confirm every change in writing, so nothing is lost.
F
Perfect. One last thing. If the fabric is late, how many days do we lose?
P
Ten, normally. If it goes past ten, I call you before you have to ask.

Chapter 5: Real Talk

Ask It on the Spot

Read each situation. Ask out loud first, then tap Tips for support language.

1 / 5

A new supplier answers the phone. You need the ship date of the first batch, and you have never spoken to this person before.

  • Open with Could you tell me, not with a bare question.
  • After when, the sentence goes back to normal order: subject, then verb.

You are not sure whether the price on the quote includes freight. Ask, without suggesting the supplier hid it.

  • A yes-or-no question needs if or whether inside.
  • Do you know if the quote includes freight?

The purchase order has the wrong date. Ask who can change it and how long it takes.

  • Who is already the subject, so nothing moves after it.
  • Ask the second question in the same breath, not in a new email.

Your manager is on the call and asks you to check whether the factory can deliver two weeks earlier. Ask the supplier.

  • I was wondering whether you could... is the softest opener you have.
  • Give the reason before the question, not after.

The supplier gives you a date you cannot accept. Push back without saying no.

  • Name the problem, then ask what is possible.
  • That date does not work for us. Could you tell me what is possible?
Chapter 6: Your Turn

From Guided to Free

Three rounds, less help each time

Say It Yourself

Three Situations, Full Questions

Scenario 1

You need three pieces of information from a supplier you have never spoken to: the ship date, the price of freight and who signs the change. Ask all three, and start each one differently.

Scenario 2

A factory has just told you the lead time is fifteen days longer. Ask why, ask what is possible, and ask for it in writing. Do not accuse anybody.

Scenario 3

You have to push the delivery back by three weeks and the supplier will not like it. Say it politely, give one reason, and finish with a question.
