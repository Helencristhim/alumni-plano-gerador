Chapter 3

The Backstory

The past before the past

Grammar Discovery

Before the Main Moment

"When I got to the gate, the plane had already left."

"I was nervous because I had never flown alone before."

"It dawned on me that I had left my passport at the hotel."

"By the time we arrived, the restaurant had closed."

FormUseExample
had + past participleThe EARLIER action in a past storyThe plane had left.
past simple + past perfectMain moment + what happened before itWhen I arrived, they had gone.
had never / had alreadyExperience or completion before that momentI'd never seen snow before that trip.
hadn't + past participleSomething that did NOT happen beforeI hadn't checked the time.
⚠ CarefulUse the past PARTICIPLE: 'had gone', never 'had went'. Don't use it for every verb: the main events stay in the past simple ('I ran to the gate'). In speech, 'had' often becomes 'd: 'I'd forgotten'.

Reading

The Flight I Missed

The flight I missed in Lisbon

Two years ago, I was on my way home from a work trip to Lisbon. I had planned everything. I had checked in online, I had packed the night before, and I had left the hotel three hours early. Or so I thought.

When I got to the airport, the departure board looked strange. My flight was not there. Then it dawned on me: I had read the ticket wrong. The flight had left at 10:15 in the morning, not at night. I was stranded in a city where I knew nobody, and my phone was almost dead.

I sat down and, to be honest, I almost cried. Out of the blue, a woman next to me asked if I was OK. She had missed her connection too. Meanwhile, the airline desk was looking for two seats on the next flight to Sao Paulo. We got them in the nick of time.

Long story short, we talked for ten hours on that plane, and today she is one of my closest friends. Missing that flight was a blessing in disguise. I still check my tickets three times, though.
