Warm-up

Your English in Three Words

Not as it was in Lesson 1, but as it is today. Then open the last panel you moderated in thirty seconds, with one inversion and one word from last lesson: an inflection point, a show of hands, to set the tone, to hand over to.

The Target

Read the Statement Like a Lawyer

1. Separate what was decided from what was only signaled.

2. Check whether the reported fact is still true today, or only was.

3. Retell it to an investor without making the promise stronger or weaker.

Diagnostic

What Did the Agency Say?

A fund manager in Toronto missed yesterday’s press conference and calls you: “What did the agency say about the tariff review?”

Report it in three sentences. Invent the details if you need to, but make it clear which parts were a decision and which were only a signal.

What the Agency Actually Said

By the time yesterday’s press conference ended, three different headlines were already circulating. One said the government would cut toll road tariffs. Another said the concessions would be renegotiated. A third said nothing would change. All three came from the same forty minutes, and none of them was exactly what the agency said.

The director opened by confirming that the tariff review for the third batch of highways would begin in October, after a public consultation of forty-five days. That part was a decision. She then indicated that the review could lead to lower tariffs on some stretches of road, which is a signal, not a promise. When a journalist asked whether contracts would be renegotiated, she clarified that the review followed the formula already written in each contract, and that operators were not expected to cut investment during the consultation period.

The more important news came in answer to a question about the floods in the south. The director acknowledged that several operators had filed rebalancing claims, and said that each claim would be assessed case by case. She did not say whether the floods would be treated as force majeure, and a senior official later suggested that the agency had not yet reached a view. She also confirmed that contracts signed before 2025 are protected by a grandfathering clause, which means the new rules do not apply to them.

For investors doing due diligence, the useful lesson is not the headline. It is the verbs. Confirmed means you can build a model on it. Indicated and suggested mean you should build two. And when a statement is reported without a person — the operator is said to be preparing a claim — it means somebody wanted the information out without having to own it.

Chapter 4: The Code

How Sure Was the Speaker?

The reporting verb measures the promise; the tense says whether it is still true

Grammar Discovery

Three Verbs Step Back in Time. One Does Not.

"The minister indicated that the framework would be revised by the fourth quarter."

"The agency clarified that operators were not expected to cut investment."

"Sources suggested that the fund had already committed."

"The regulator confirmed that the auction is still scheduled for March."

In the red sentences, the verb moves one step back in time. Why is the blue sentence allowed to stay in the present? And look at the reporting verbs: which one would you build a financial model on, and which would make you build two?

JobFormExample
Report a statementreporting verb + that + one step back in timeShe said that the review would start in May.
Keep what is still trueno backshift when it is still true todayThe agency confirmed that the auction is in March.
A firm statementconfirmed, announced, statedThe ministry confirmed the date.
A signal, not a promiseindicated, suggested, signaledThe minister indicated tariffs could fall.
A correction, warning or admissionclarified, warned, acknowledgedShe acknowledged that claims had been filed.
Report without a personsubject + is said to / is expected to + verbThe operator is said to be preparing a claim.
The trapnever said me, never explained meNot She explained me the rule. Say She explained the rule to me.

In one line: The reporting verb tells the investor how firm the promise was; the tense tells them whether it is still true.

Quick Fire

One Question at a Time

1 / 6

A fund manager asks what the agency decided yesterday. Report the one decision, without making it stronger.

  • The director confirmed that the tariff review would begin in October, after a forty-five-day public consultation.
  • Confirmed for the decision; would begin, one step back in time.

The same manager asks whether tariffs will fall. Report the signal as a signal.

  • She indicated that tariffs could fall on some stretches, but that was a signal, not a promise.
  • Indicated, not confirmed. Could, not will.

An investor says he heard all concessions will be renegotiated. Correct him politely.

  • That is not what was said. The agency clarified that the review follows the formula already in each contract.
  • Clarified is the verb for correcting a misunderstanding.

Explain to a CFO from Singapore what a grandfathering clause means for her contract signed in 2023.

  • Her contract is protected by a grandfathering clause, so the new rules do not apply to it.
  • Protected by, not protected for.

Report a rumor you cannot confirm about an operator preparing a large claim.

  • The operator is said to be preparing a rebalancing claim, but nobody has confirmed it.
  • is said to + be + -ing for something happening now.

A lender asks what happens if the operator fails to invest for two years.

  • That would be a material breach, and the lenders could use their step-in right to replace the operator.
  • Material breach first, then the consequence.

It confirmed that the tariff review will go ahead in October, and clarified that it would follow the formula written into each contract.

Seven operators, and the claims will be assessed individually.

Whether the floods would be treated as force majeure. The analyst warned that the uncertainty could delay two auctions planned for the first half of next year.

Two Clauses Investors Skip

A contracts lawyer speaks at an investor roundtable. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Which two clauses does the lawyer say investors skip?
The force majeure clause and the grandfathering clause.
2. Why does he say force majeure is not a formality, and what should investors read in it?
Because in a country with floods and droughts it decides who pays when the road is under water. Investors should read the list of events and who has to prove what.

It lost a year of returns because its lawyers assumed the old rules would continue, and the grandfathering clause said otherwise.
