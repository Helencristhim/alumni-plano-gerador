Personagem-Jornada

De: Já estudou inglês várias vezes ao longo da vida e já visitou dezenas de países — a bagagem cultural é enorme. Mas interrompe ou simplifica o próprio raciocínio quando falta a palavra certa, e carrega erros gramaticais já automatizados que explicação tradicional não desfaz. Viaja com a esposa, que fala inglês bem, e acaba ficando no canto da conversa.

Para: Puxa conversa sozinho num hotel, num restaurante ou diante de um monumento — e a MANTÉM. Conta uma história de viagem até o fim, dá opinião sobre arquitetura e cidades, discorda com educação e faz a pergunta de volta. Deixa de ser o acompanhante e passa a ser interlocutor.

Promessa Transformadora

De frases interrompidas a conversas que ele sustenta até o fim. O desafio do Ricardo não é começar — é ampliar o repertório lexical a ponto de nunca mais precisar abandonar uma ideia no meio. O motor do programa é VOCABULÁRIO (collocations, phrasal verbs, expressões naturais) dentro dos temas que genuinamente o interessam. Gramática entra sempre pelo contexto, nunca isolada, e a correção é por reformulação — erro fossilizado se desfaz com input e produção, não com regra na lousa.

Metodologia

Vocabulário como motor

Objetivo nº1 declarado. Toda aula introduz collocations, phrasal verbs e expressões naturais — nunca listas soltas, sempre dentro do tema.

Gramática contextualizada

Nunca isolada. Erro fossilizado não cede a explicação — cede a input de qualidade, reformulação e repetição em contexto.

Escuta ativa

Contrapeso ao Talking Time alto: forçar confirmar, resumir e reformular ANTES de responder. Faz parte de toda aula.

Arquitetura como porta

Sempre pelo viés cultural, histórico e turístico — NUNCA técnico ou profissional. Foi pedido explícito na consultoria.

Football Minute

Alguns minutos por aula ligando o tema ao futebol. Elemento motivador identificado na consultoria — use quando a energia cair.

Comparação constante

A bagagem dele é o maior ativo. Sempre puxar: qual país te surpreendeu, que cidade lembra São Paulo, onde a comida foi melhor.

Estrutura sugerida da aula (da consultoria)

  • 15% — Aquecimento: small talk, revisão do vocabulário da aula anterior
  • 50% — Tema principal: discussão guiada, vocabulário novo, ampliação lexical
  • 10% — Football Minute: curiosidade ligando o tema ao futebol
  • 15% — Language Focus: correção dos erros observados + uma estrutura contextualizada
  • 10% — Wrap-up: revisão do vocabulário novo e desafio para a próxima aula

CCQs da Aula 1 (Concept Checking Questions)

  • "I live in Sao Paulo" — Is that today only, or always? (Always — present simple para fato permanente.)
  • "My wife speaks English" — Why speakS and not speak? (Because "my wife" is she — he/she/it takes the -s.)
  • "I have 73 years" — Is this correct? (No — age uses TO BE: "I am 73 years old.")
  • Destination — Is it where you start, or where you arrive? (Where you arrive.)
  • Heritage — Is it something new, or something old that we protect? (Something old that we protect.)
  • Landmark — If I say "meet me at the landmark", is it easy or hard to find? (Easy — everybody knows it.)
  • To wander — When you wander, do you have a plan? (No — you walk with no fixed plan.)

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

My name is Ricardo Wertheim and I live in Sao Paulo. I am an architect, so I look at buildings everywhere I go. I travel two or three times a year, and my wife comes with me. She speaks English very well, so she usually orders the food and asks for directions.

This August we go to Croatia and Slovenia. Dubrovnik has a famous old town, and the city walls are more than six hundred years old. I don't like crowded tourist restaurants, so I always ask a local person where to eat. My wife doesn't mind a long walk, and I love to wander through narrow streets with no map at all.

1. Why does the text say "my wife speaks English" and not "my wife speak"?
A Because "my wife" is she, and he / she / it takes the -s.
B Because the action is happening right now.
C Because it is a plural subject.
2. The text says "I don't like crowded tourist restaurants" but "my wife doesn't mind a long walk". Why the difference?
A Because one is a routine and the other is happening now.
B Because with he / she / it the -s moves to "does", giving "doesn't".
C Because "mind" is an irregular verb.
3. "I travel two or three times a year." What does this sentence tell us?
A That he is travelling at this exact moment.
B That he travelled in the past and stopped.
C That this is his routine, something generally true about him.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Present Simple

GRAMMAR

The tense you use to say who you are and what you do.

FormUseExample
Affirmative
I / you / we / they + verb		Facts about you and things you do regularly.I live in Sao Paulo.
Third person
he / she / it + verb + s		Same idea, one other person or thing. Watch out: this -s is the most forgotten letter in English.She speaks English.
Dubrovnik has an old town.
Negative
don't / doesn't + verb		The -s moves to does, so the main verb goes back to its plain form.I don't like tourist menus.
She doesn't mind walking.
Question
Do / Does + person + verb		Same rule again: does carries the -s.Do you travel often?
Does she live here?
Frequency wordsalways, usually, often, sometimes, never go before a normal verb (I usually travel), but after the verb to be (I am usually early).

Age is different in English: you are 73 years old, you do not have 73 years.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Past Simple

GRAMMAR

The tense that carries every travel story you will ever tell.

FormRuleExample
Regular verbs
verb + ed		One ending for every person: I, you, he, she, we, they.We arrived late.
I walked for hours.
Irregular verbs
a NEW word		No -ed at all. These seven carry most travel stories, so learn them as words, not as rules.go → went · see → saw · eat → ate · meet → met · take → took · feel → felt · find → found
Negative
didn't + plain verb		The past moves to did, so the main verb loses it. Never "didn't went".I didn't take a photo.
We didn't eat at the hotel.
Question
Did + person + plain verb		Same rule again, and did comes before the person.Did you see the harbor?
Where did you stay?
Time expressionslast year · in 2019 · a few years ago · when I was in Lisbon · on my last trip. They open or close the sentence: Last year we went to Portugal. / We went to Portugal last year.

One time expression is enough for the whole story: once you say ten years ago, every verb after it stays in the past.

B They are identical and you can always swap them.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Past Continuous

GRAMMAR

The camera behind the action: what was already happening when the story began.

FormRuleExample
Affirmative
was / were + verb -ing		Two words only: was for I, he, she, it — were for you, we, they.I was strolling along the harbor.
People were sitting outside.
Background vs eventThe -ing form is the long thing already happening; the past simple is the short thing that cuts into it.I was looking for a table when I noticed a door.
Negative
wasn't / weren't + -ing		The not attaches to was or were. The verb keeps its -ing.Nobody was taking photographs.
We weren't looking at a map.
Question
Was / Were + person + -ing		Was or were comes before the person, exactly like a question with to be.What were you doing when the music started?
Was she working that night?
when vs whilewhen goes with the short event, while with the long background. Both sentences mean the same thing: I was strolling when I heard music. / While I was strolling, I heard music.

Some verbs almost never take -ing, because they are not actions you can watch: know, want, like, need, believe. You say "I knew the square", never "I was knowing the square".

Three small verbs that change how strong you sound, without changing anything else in the sentence.

VerbWhat it doesExample
shouldadvice -- the right thing to doYou should go early.
coulda soft suggestion -- one option among othersWe could take the ferry.
mighta guess -- you are not sureIt might be crowded.
NegativeAdd not: you should not judge a city at noon · it might not be open on Monday.
QuestionThe modal moves to the front: Should I book it? · Could we take the ferry? Never "Do I should...".
The shape after themAlways the bare verb: should go, not "should to go" · she might come, not "she might comes". Modal verbs never take to and never take s.

A useful pair for opinions: "You should see it early" sounds like real advice, while "You could see it early" leaves the decision with the other person.

Stage 2: Put the Change of Mind in Order

Order

Put the story of one traveller changing his mind in the order it happened.

?That afternoon he took the bus inland, off the beaten track, and had a typical lunch that lasted three hours.
?First he read an article saying that the old town was overrated and not worth it any more.
?In the end he reconsidered his whole opinion, and told his friends that the place was fine and the hour was the problem.
?Then he went at noon in August and almost agreed, because he could hardly walk.
?The next morning he came back at seven and found the same street completely empty.

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen, then record yourself. You will see which words came through clearly.

I am not sure I agree. I think the hour matters more than the place.
That is a fair point, but I would still go early.
Have you considered that it might be the crowds and not the city?
From my perspective, it is absolutely worth it.
You should give yourself one afternoon off the beaten track.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Somebody disagrees with you at a dinner table. Choose the best thing to say.

Someone says your favourite city is overrated. You disagree, but you want to keep the conversation friendly. You say:
A "No. You are completely wrong about that."
B "I am not sure I agree. I think the hour matters more than the place."
C "Maybe. I do not have any opinion about this."
You want to give a friend real advice about the best time to visit. You say:
A "You should to go at eight in the morning."
B "You should go at eight in the morning."
C "You shoulds going at eight in the morning."
You are not sure whether the museum opens on Monday. The honest answer is:
A "It might not be open on Monday. We should check."
B "It might not to be open on Monday."
C "It not might be open on Monday."
You want to ask a local whether booking in advance is necessary. You ask:
A "Do I should book the walk in advance?"
B "I should to book the walk in advance?"
C "Should I book the walk in advance?"

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Choose one famous place you think is overrated and one nobody talks about that is absolutely worth it. Take about ninety seconds. Say what each one is, why people are wrong about it, what a visitor should do, and what might change their mind. Use should, could and might at least once each, and four words from this lesson. Do not stop to correct yourself.
