Grounding in the Approach Channel

Grounding in the Approach Channel

At 0412 on 14 March a bulk carrier of 32,000 tonnes ran aground in the outer approach channel of a South Atlantic port. The vessel had left her berth six hours earlier and was proceeding under pilotage at eight knots when the incident occurred.

According to the report published by the flag state, her draft had been recorded as 11.4 metres on departure and the channel had been surveyed at 12.1 metres. The under-keel clearance was described by the operator as adequate under normal conditions. Conditions that night were not normal: a falling tide, combined with a strong southerly set, was later identified as the principal contributing factor.

No injuries were reported among the twenty-two crew, and no pollution was observed. The hull was breached in two forward compartments, both of which were isolated within the hour. The cargo, loaded three days earlier, was declared unaffected. The vessel was refloated on the following tide with the assistance of two harbour tugs and was escorted to an anchorage, where she was inspected before any decision on repairs was taken.

The investigation found that the passage plan had not been amended after the tidal window was revised. The bridge team had been informed of the revision by radio, but the change was never entered in the log. The recommendations issued to the operator include a review of bridge resource management and of the procedure by which navigational information is passed between the pilot and the officer of the watch.

Chapter 4: The Code

The Voice of the Report

Nobody did anything. Everything was done.

Grammar Discovery

Who Did It?

"The hull was breached in two forward compartments."

"The passage plan had not been amended after the tidal window was revised."

"The change was never entered in the log."

"Navigational information is passed between the pilot and the officer of the watch."

Every one of these sentences hides the person who acted. Where would you put the ship, the officer or the pilot in each one -- and why does a report leave them out?

FormWhat it doesExample
be + past participlethe standard passive: what happened matters more than who did itThe hull was breached.
had been + past participlepast perfect passive: it had already happened before the moment describedThe plan had not been amended.
is / are + past participlepresent passive: a standing procedure, true every dayInformation is passed between the pilot and the officer.
by + agentonly when the agent is the point. Most report sentences drop itThe clearance was described by the operator as adequate.
The reading trapan option that turns the passive into an active names somebody the text never named. Find the verb before you look for a person.

A report is written in the passive because it is about the ship, not about a person. That is exactly where the trap lives: swap the passive for an active and the option quietly appoints a guilty party the text never appointed.

Quick Fire

One Question at a Time

1 / 6

The text says the ship 'ran aground'. Which would a question write instead: went adrift, became hard-fast, or was under way?

  • Aground means touching the bottom and not moving.
  • Adrift means moving with no control. Under way means moving under command.

The text says 'no injuries were reported'. Say that in the register a question would use.

  • The incident exacted no human toll.
  • No casualties were sustained.

An option says: The operator judged the clearance to be insufficient. Is that what the text says?

  • The text says the operator described it as adequate under normal conditions.
  • Insufficient is the opposite. The option keeps the topic and flips the meaning.

Give the exam-register word for the place outside a port where a ship waits at anchor.

  • Roadstead.
  • The text calls it an anchorage.

The report says: The change was never entered in the log. Who entered nothing, and why does the report not tell you?

  • The passive leaves the agent out because the report is about the ship, not about a person.
  • An option that names somebody is adding information the text does not carry.

In one sentence, say what the investigation found.

  • The passage plan had not been amended after the tidal window was revised.
  • The bridge team knew, but the change never reached the log.

Why the Plan Was Never Amended

An investigator explains the finding. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. When was the tidal window revised, and how did the bridge team learn about it?
It was revised at twenty-two hundred the night before, and the bridge team was told by radio. Two of them repeated the new time back.
2. What is the step the investigator says never happened?
Nobody entered the revision in the log and nobody amended the passage plan, so the plan on the chart table still described a tide that no longer existed.
3. Why does he say the finding is not about one careless officer?
Because it is about a procedure that allowed a change to be spoken and never written.
