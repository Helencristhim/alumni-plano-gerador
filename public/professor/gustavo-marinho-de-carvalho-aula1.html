Chapter 3: What You Do, What You Are Doing

Two Questions That Sound Like One

One answer never changes. The other changes every week.

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Do?

"We work with clients in several countries, and I handle the international side."

"This week I am putting together the proposal for your team."

"At the moment we are reviewing the timeline for the first quarter."

"I agree with you, and I think the deadline is realistic."

The red sentences are true every week of the year. The blue ones are true this week and will not be true in six months. The orange ones are not actions at all — they are what is going on inside your head.

JobFormExample
What the business always doespresent simpleWe work with clients in several countries.
What your job is, in generalpresent simpleI handle the international side.
What is on your desk this weekam / is / are + verb-ingThis week I am putting together the proposal.
A change that is happening nowam / is / are + verb-ingCosts are going up again this year.
What goes on inside your headpresent simple, never -ingI think it is a good idea. I agree.
The signal wordsevery week, usually / this week, at the momentUsually I travel in March. This year I am staying here.
The trapagree is already the verbNot I am agree. Say I agree.

In one line: the shape you pick is not grammar. It is how long the sentence is going to stay true.

Comprehension

About Claire

1. What does Claire want to do before they look at the numbers, and what does she talk about first?
She wants to catch up for a minute, and she starts with the weather, which she says is terrible as usual.
2. What does she say she does not know about the company, and why?
She does not know what the company does in general, because she only ever sees her own part of it.
3. She says the news is “quite good”. What does she ask immediately after that?
She says she might have one or two small questions about the timing, and she asks whether the deadline at the end of the month is still realistic.

The First Ninety Seconds

A short radio piece on the part of an international call that nobody rehearses. Sound first — no text.

They rehearse the presentation, the numbers and the proposal. Almost nobody rehearses the first ninety seconds.

What does your company do, and what are you working on at the moment. The first is about the business in general and never changes; the second is about this week and changes every time.

Real Document

The Email That Came Afterwards

Re: Proposal — next steps
Claire Hensley → Gustavo Marinho · Thursday, 11:52

Hello Gustavo,

Many thanks for your time this morning. It was good to put a voice to the emails at last.

Three points, so that we are on the same page:

1. You send the full proposal by the end of next week.
2. We confirm the numbers for the first quarter within five working days.
3. I introduce you to Daniel, who handles the contracts on our side.

One small thing: our finance team needs the payment terms in writing before we sign anything. Could you include them in the proposal rather than in a separate document? That would make life easier here.

Kind regards,
Claire

Hensley & Partners · Manchester · This message is a follow-up to the call of the same morning.
1. Of the three points, which one is Gustavo responsible for, and by when?
Point one: he sends the full proposal, by the end of next week.
2. What does Claire ask for, and where does she want it?
The payment terms in writing, inside the proposal itself rather than in a separate document.
3. She writes “that would make life easier here”. Is she asking or telling?
She is asking, but the request is real: the soft wording is politeness, not an option. Her finance team needs the terms before anything is signed.

The Same Call, Three Times

Role-Play 1 of 3 — Guided

Claire Calls Again

Scenario: Claire calls a week later. She spends the first two minutes on the weather and your week, and then asks the same two questions: what the company does, and what you are working on right now. Answer both, and finish by asking her something.

Keyword chips:

to catch up a supplier to handle at the moment
Role-Play 2 of 3 — Semi-free

Ask Before You Tell

Scenario: Claire introduces you to Daniel, who handles the contracts on their side and has never spoken to you before. Ask him two questions about his work before you say a single word about yourself — then connect his answer to yours in one sentence.

Keywords:

What do you handle on your side? What are you working on at the moment? a deadline a follow-up
Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

Ninety Seconds, No Notes

Scenario: A new client in England opens the call and says: “I know almost nothing about you. Tell me.” Ninety seconds: what the company does, what your part in it is, what is on your desk this week, one thing that is changing at the moment, and one question back.

No keywords, no notes, ninety seconds.
