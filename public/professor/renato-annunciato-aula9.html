Chapter 3: The Code

To Present, or Presenting?

What decides the shape of the second verb

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"We decided to open with the headline."

"I am responsible for managing two regions."

"She wants to recap at the end, not in the middle."

"I look forward to presenting the results next month."

Two take to + verb. Two take verb + ing. Look at the word immediately before each one.

What comes beforeShape of the second verbExample
Certain verbsto + verb — want, decide, need, plan, agree, hope, offer, refuse, learnWe decided to open with the headline.
Other verbsverb + ing — enjoy, avoid, finish, keep, suggest, mind, consider, practiseShe suggested keeping the Q and A for the end.
Any prepositionverb + ing, always — for, before, after, without, by, in, at, ofI am responsible for managing two regions.
The trap: to as a prepositionlook forward to · be used to · object to — here to is a preposition, so it takes -ing: I look forward to presenting, never to present.
Both, same meaningstart, begin, continue, like, love, hate — "I like presenting" and "I like to present" are both correct.
Two trapsNever for to come — a preposition never sits next to to. And never I am responsible for manage — after a preposition the verb always wears -ing.

The Structure

Three Minutes, Four Moves

The opening is the only part of a presentation you fully control. Build it once, and it works for every presentation you ever give.

1 · Take the floor

"Good morning, everyone, and thank you for the floor. I am Renato, Commercial Manager for the South and Southeast of Brazil."

2 · The outline

"I will walk you through three things today: the quarter, the interior of Sao Paulo, and what I need from you."

3 · The headline, before the detail

"The headline is that we have grown in both regions, but the interior is still the gap."

4 · Where the questions go

"I would like to keep the Q and A for the last five minutes, but please stop me if something is not clear."

Floor → outline → headline → questions. Never an apology, and never the context first.

Listening 2

Rehearse the Order, Not the Sentences

A first presentation in English, remembered honestly. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. How did he prepare the first time, and what does he say about it now?
He wrote the whole script and read it about forty times. He says rehearsing the words was the wrong preparation.
2. What saved him when he lost himself?
The outline. He had said in the first minute that he would walk them through three things, so he looked at that list and found his place.
3. What happened when somebody asked for a number he had left out?
He had a backup slide, so instead of apologising he said he had it there and showed it — and the room started treating him as somebody who knew the business.
4. What is his advice in one sentence?
Do not rehearse the sentences. Rehearse the order, and prepare for the one question you are afraid of.

The Real Thing

Your Opening Slide

Commercial review
South & Southeast, Brazil
Renato Annunciato
Commercial Manager
The headline
Growth in both regions — and the interior is still the gap

Today, in three parts

  • The quarter, and what drove it
  • The interior of Sao Paulo
  • What I need from you

Q and A: last five minutes. Detail: in the handout. Timing: twenty minutes in total.

1. Why is the headline above the outline on the slide, but said after it?
On the screen it is the first thing the eye finds. Out loud the outline comes first, so the room knows how long the headline has to last.
2. Why does the detail live in the handout instead of the slide?
So the slide stays readable and nobody reads ahead of you. The detail is still there for anyone who wants it.
