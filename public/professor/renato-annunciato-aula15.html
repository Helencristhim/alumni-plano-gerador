Chapter 3: The Code

What They Said

Reporting other people's words

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I am happy with the visit." → She said she was happy with the visit.

"I need a month." → She told us she needed a month.

"The shipment will arrive late." → He told me the shipment would arrive late.

"We have finished the training." → He said they had finished the training.

Compare the blue words with the original. What happens to the verb? And which verb has a person right after it: said or told?

They saidYou reportExample
am / is / needwas / neededShe said she was happy.
will / canwould / couldHe told me it would arrive late.
have done / didhad doneHe said they had finished.
say or tell?say + what: she said (that) … · tell + PERSON: she told me / us / the team (that) …
Time wordstoday → that day · tomorrow → the next day · next week → the following week
Two trapsNever she said me — it is she told me. And never she told that — tell needs a person: she told us that.
Practice

Report It

Say it first, then click to check

"I am happy with the visit. → She said she ___was happy with the visit." (was / is)
"We will send the samples. → He said they ___would send the samples." (would / will)
"I cannot come on Friday. → She told me she ___could not come on Friday." (could not / cannot)
"We have finished the training. → He said they ___had finished the training." (had finished / have finished)
"Doctor Costa ___told me she needed a month." (told / said)
The Structure

An Update in Four Lines

Status first. The blocker early. Every next step has a name and a date.

1 · Status

"This week, the Porto Alegre project is on track."

2 · Highlight

"Doctor Costa said she was happy with our joint visit."

3 · Blocker

"I want to flag one blocker: the distributor told me the shipment would arrive two weeks late."

4 · Next steps

"I will call the supply team today and report back by Friday."

Where we are → what they said → what stops us → what happens next.

Dialogue

The Weekly Update

M
Hi, Renato. Let us do the weekly status update. Are the Porto Alegre clinics on track?
R
Mostly, yes. The highlight: we did the joint visit on Tuesday, and Doctor Costa said she was happy to see us again.
M
That is good news. Did she say she would come back to us?
R
Not yet. She told us she needed a month to finish her stock from the competitor. So that clinic is a little behind schedule.
M
Okay. Is there a blocker I should know about?
R
Yes, I want to flag one. The distributor told me the November shipment would arrive two weeks late.
M
Two weeks? That could hurt us again. Who is the action item on?
R
It is on me. I will call the supply team today, and I will report back by Friday.
M
Perfect. And the second clinic?
R
That one is on track. The owner said they had a training day the following week, and he invited our consultant.
M
Great. Send me the next steps in writing, and keep me posted. We will touch base again next Monday.
Comprehension

About Megan

1. What does Megan want to know first?
If the Porto Alegre clinics are on track.
2. What does she want to know about Doctor Costa?
Whether the doctor said she would come back to them.
3. How does she react to the two-week delay?
She is worried: she says it could hurt them again.
4. What does she ask Renato to do at the end?
To send the next steps in writing and keep her posted; they will touch base next Monday.

In the first half, because she cannot help with a problem she hears about weeks later.

Chapter 5: Practice

Your Week

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "The distributor said me the shipment was late."
"The distributor told me the shipment was late." (tell + person; say + what)
2. "She told that she needed a month."
"She told us that she needed a month." (tell needs a person)
3. "Doctor Costa said she need a month."
"Doctor Costa said she needed a month." (one step back in time)
4. "We had a reunion with the clinic on Tuesday."
"We had a meeting with the clinic on Tuesday." (a reunion is when old friends meet again)
5. "The owner explained me the problem."
"The owner explained the problem to me." (explain something to someone)
