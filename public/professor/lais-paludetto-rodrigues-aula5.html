Why This Matters

You Already Say This Every Day

It is the advice sentence

"If I were you, I'd..." is how English gives advice without giving orders

It is the daydream sentence

"If I lived in Puglia, I'd..." — you have said this one in Portuguese a hundred times

It is the polite sentence

"It would be great if..." is how you ask for something big without pressure

The structure is not the problem. Two verbs change place, and that is the whole thing.

Chapter 3: The Code

Possible, or Only in Your Head?

Same word, different world. Two verbs move back one step.

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"If I miss the bus, I will walk."

"If I lived in Puglia, I would swim every day."

"If I were you, I would say yes to the game."

"What would you do if you had a free weekend?"

Only the first one is really going to happen. What changed in the other three?

HalfReal and possibleImaginary
The if halfpresent: if I misspast: if I missed
The result halfwill + verbwould + verb
The past is not the past"If I lived in Puglia" is not about yesterday. The past form here only means this is not real. Both halves move back one step, and nothing else changes.
If I wereWith the verb to be, English uses were for everybody in this sentence: if I were, if he were, if it were. "If I were you" is the standard way of giving advice.
would, could, mightwould = I am sure. could = it is one option. might = maybe. All three take the base verb.

One question decides everything: can this really happen? Yes, first conditional. No, second.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"If I would live in Canada, I would learn to ski."
"If I lived in Canada, I would learn to ski."
"If I am you, I would say yes."
"If I were you, I would say yes."
"I would to travel more if I had money."
"I would travel more if I had money."

Same rule as last week, one level up: would never sits in the if half. With to be, the imaginary form is were for everybody. And after would the verb goes straight in, with no to.

Detail

Order, Suggestion or Advice?

Same idea, three temperatures

Order (strong) — "___Say yes to the game."
Suggestion (medium) — "___You should say yes to the game."
Advice (soft) — "___If I were you, I'd say yes to the game."
Softest of all — "___I might say yes, if it were me."

Somebody Who Arrived Before You

N
You're the new one, right? Brazil. I'm Nico, I got here in August. How's it going?
L
Honestly? Strange this week. Everything was exciting and now it just feels awkward, and I don't know why.
N
Week six. Everybody gets it. It's culture shock, and it goes away, I promise. If I were you, I wouldn't decide anything this month.
L
What would you do differently, if you started again?
N
I'd talk more in the first month. I was so scared of sounding stupid that I said nothing until November, and by then I was the quiet one.
L
That's tempting though. It's easier to say nothing than to say something wrong.
N
Easier, yes. Worth it, no. And say yes to things. If somebody invites you to a hockey game on a Tuesday, go, even if it's cold.
L
Okay. If I get invited to anything this week, I'll go. Even hockey, and I don't understand hockey.

Real Document

The Curve Everybody Rides

Counselling Office Six Months, Four Stages
WEEK 1-3Everything is new and everything is interesting. You say yes to everything and you sleep badly.
WEEK 4-8The novelty stops. Small things get on your nerves. You miss food you did not even like at home.
WEEK 9-14It becomes normal. You stop translating in your head. You have a favourite bus stop.
WEEK 15+You belong. And then you start counting the weeks you have left, which is its own problem.
You are in week six and you want to go home. What would you do?
"If I felt like that, I'd call my sister, I wouldn't decide anything for two weeks, and I'd say yes to the next invitation."
A friend of yours is in week six next year. What advice would you give?
"If I were you, I wouldn't panic. It is a stage, not a decision."

Quick Fire

One Situation at a Time

1 / 6

A new exchange student arrives in October and says nobody talks to her. Give her one piece of real advice.

  • If I were you, I'd say yes to the next invitation.
  • It happened to me too, in week two.
  • It gets better around week six, honestly.

Somebody in class says something about Brazil that is not true. Correct it without making it a fight.

  • That's a common idea, but it's not really like that.
  • Where I live it's actually...
  • If you came to Sao Paulo, you'd be surprised.

You are invited to a hockey game on a Tuesday. It is cold, you have homework and you would rather stay home. Answer honestly, but do not close the door.

  • I'd love to, but I have an assignment due tomorrow.
  • If it were Friday, I'd say yes immediately.
  • Is there another game this month?

Your host mum asks if you are homesick. You are, a bit. Say it without making her worry.

  • A little, mostly on Sundays.
  • It's not a problem, it comes and goes.
  • If I could change one thing, I'd have a shorter time difference.

Somebody asks what you would do if you had a whole free weekend in Vancouver. Answer with a real plan.

  • If I had a free weekend, I'd go to Granville Island.
  • I'd take the bus to the sea.
  • What would you do?

A friend says something that sounds rude to you, and you are not sure whether it was meant that way. Check, instead of getting upset.

  • Sorry, did you mean that as a joke?
  • I don't want to take it the wrong way.
  • In Brazil that would sound quite direct.
