Chapter 3: The Code

Two Things, Side by Side

Três letras e uma palavra.

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

O que as palavras em laranja ganharam no fim? E o que essa palavrinha than está fazendo ali?

Option B is cheaper than Option A.

Option A is safer than Option B.

The discount is bigger in the first quote.

This week is busier than last week.

Adjetivo curto + -er, e depois than para a coisa comparada.

AdjetivoComparativoRegra de escrita
cheapcheaper thansó acrescenta -er
safesafer thanjá termina em e — só o -r
bigbigger thanuma vogal no meio — dobra a consoante
busybusier thantermina em -y — troca por -ier
A regra em uma linha: adjetivo curto (uma sílaba, ou duas terminadas em -y) ganha -er, e a coisa comparada vem depois de than. Compara sempre DUAS coisas — nunca três.
The Other Half

Long Words Take More

Palavra curta ganha -er no fim. Palavra longa ganha more na frente. Nunca os dois.

Curto → -erLongo → more
cheaper thanmore expensive than
safer thanmore complete than
bigger thanmore difficult than
busier thanmore useful than

Como saber qual é qual? Conte as sílabas. Uma sílaba (cheap, safe, big) leva -er. Três ou mais (ex-pen-sive, com-plete… e dif-fi-cult) levam more. E a regra que salva a frase: nunca os dois na mesma palavra — “more cheaper” não existe.

One Among All

The -est and The Most

Duas coisas → comparativo. Uma entre todas → superlativo — e sempre com the na frente.

AdjetivoDuas coisasUma entre todas
cheapcheaper thanthe cheapest
safesafer thanthe safest
bigbigger thanthe biggest
expensivemore expensive thanthe most expensive

This quote is the cheapest of the three.

Option A has the most complete coverage.

The Exceptions

Only Two Are Irregular

Não são vinte. São dois — e você já ouviu os quatro mil vezes.

AdjetivoDuas coisasUma entre todas
good (bom)better thanthe best
bad (ruim)worse thanthe worst

Option A is better than Option B for this client.

The claim service is the worst part of that quote.

better já é a forma comparada — então more better não existe. Mesma coisa com worse.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"Option B is more cheap than Option A."
"Option B is cheaper than Option A."
"This quote is more good than the other one."
"This quote is better than the other one."

Os dois erros vêm do português, e não de falta de estudo: aqui o “mais” vem sempre na frente (mais barato, mais bom). Em inglês, palavra curta guarda a comparação no fim (cheaper) — e good nem isso: vira outra palavra, better.

The Artifact

The Renewal Comparison

Coveragefire, flood, transportfire only
Discount (new alarm)12%5%

RECOMMENDATIONclient meeting — Thursday
1. Which option is cheaper, and how much cheaper is it?
Option B is cheaper — fifty thousand reais cheaper than Option A. It is the cheapest of the two.
2. Which option has the more complete coverage, and why does that matter in Santos?
Option A: fire, flood and transport. Option B covers fire only — and after the fire in warehouse 4, the client also moves goods on trucks every day.
3. Look at the last three lines. Which option is better for a client with an open claim?
Option A. The discount is bigger (twelve percent), the answer to a claim is faster (twenty-four hours, not five days), and it has two site visits a year. Option B has none.
4. Thursday, the client asks you: "Which one do you recommend?" Answer in one sentence.
There is no wrong answer here — say it out loud, with a comparative and a reason. For example: "Option B is cheaper, but Option A is safer, and after the fire I recommend Option A."

Comprehension

What Did Greg Hear?

1. Which quote is cheaper, and by how much?
Option B — it is fifty thousand reais cheaper than Option A.
2. Greg thinks the cheaper quote is the best choice. What are the two reasons against it?
The coverage in Option A is more complete (fire, flood and transport, not fire only), and the discount for the new alarm is bigger in Option A — twelve percent.
3. What does Greg say about the other insurer's claim service?
He says it is the worst part of that insurer: Option B takes five days to answer a claim, and Option A takes twenty-four hours.
4. Why does Greg mention warehouse 4?
Because of the fire last month. After it, fire is not the only risk in Santos — the client also moves goods on trucks, and Option B does not cover transport.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I know my six words: cheap, safe, option, quote, coverage, discount.
Short words take -er ("cheaper"), long words take more ("more expensive") — never both.
For one among all, I use the + -est or the most: "the cheapest", "the most complete".
I know the two irregulars: good → better → the best, and bad → worse → the worst.
I can recommend one option and say why — not only list the prices.
