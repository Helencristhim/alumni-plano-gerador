CCQs da Aula 1 (Concept Checking Questions)

  • “I work in pricing” — Is that today only, or always? (Always — present simple para fato permanente.)
  • “My team analyzes the market” — Why analyzeS and not analyze? (Because “my team” is an IT — he/she/it takes the -s.)
  • “I am work in pricing” — Is this correct? (No — one verb is enough: “I work in pricing.”)
  • Retail — Do you sell to a family, or to another company? (To a family, in a store.)
  • Wholesale — Do you sell one bottle, or fifty? (Fifty — large quantities, lower price per unit.)
  • Margin — Is margin the price, or what is left after the cost? (What is left after the cost.)
  • Supplier — Does a supplier buy from you, or sell to you? (Sells to you.)
  • To negotiate — If I say yes to everything, am I negotiating? (No — both sides have to move.)

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Present Simple

GRAMMAR

The tense you use to say who you are and what you do.

FormUseExample
Affirmative
I / you / we / they + verb		Facts about you and things you do regularly.I work in pricing.
Third person
he / she / it + verb + s		Same idea, one other person or thing. Watch out: this -s is the most forgotten letter in English, and "my team" and "the company" are both an it.My team analyzes the market.
The company publishes its results.
Negative
don't / doesn't + verb		The -s moves to does, so the main verb goes back to its plain form.I don't decide alone.
She doesn't approve it without data.
Question
Do / Does + person + verb		Same rule again: does carries the -s.Do you negotiate every week?
Does your team meet on Monday?
Frequency wordsalways, usually, often, sometimes, never go before a normal verb (I usually start at five), but after the verb to be (I am usually early).

One verb is enough for a routine: say I work, never I am work.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect vs Past Simple

GRAMMAR

One question decides the tense: is there a closed moment in the sentence?

FormUseExample
Present perfect
have / has + past participle		Experience and results with no closed moment. It is still true today. Signal words: for, since, already, just, never, ever, yet.I have delivered three projects.
Past simple
verb + -ed or new form		A closed moment in the past. Signal words: in 2022, last year, last quarter, two years ago.In 2022, I led the project.
for / sinceOnly with the present perfect. For takes a period; since takes a starting point.for ten years / since 2019
Negative & questionPresent perfect: Have you ever presented to a board? / I have not presented yet. Past simple: Did you present in 2022? / I did not present.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect Continuous

GRAMMAR

For work that started in the past and is still going on today.

FormUseExample
have / has been + verb-ingWork that started in the past and is still going on. The focus is the activity, not the result.I have been working on the model.
for / sinceThe same partners as the present perfect: for takes a period, since takes a starting point.for three months / since 2019
Activity or result?Continuous shows the work in progress. The simple form shows the finished result.I have been building it. / I have built three.
State verbsVerbs like know, understand, believe, need, want never take the continuous. Say "I have known this market for ten years", never "I have been knowing".

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Modal Verbs

GRAMMAR

Four small words that show exactly how sure you are.

ModalHow sureUse and example
mightLeast sureA careful hypothesis. "This might explain the drop."
couldPossibleAn option or alternative reading. "It could be an investment."
