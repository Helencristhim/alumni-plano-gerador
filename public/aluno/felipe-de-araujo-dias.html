Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

What I Actually Do -- Saying Your Job Out Loud

The eight words that describe your real job: supply chain, retail, supplier, facilities, maintenance, loss prevention, to oversee, to report to. Structure: the present simple for facts and routines, and the -s that belongs to he, she and it. Mission: answer "So, what do you do?" in three sentences instead of one.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Supply chain -- the whole path a product takes, from the factory to the store
"A delay in the supply chain empties a shelf three weeks later."
Retail -- the business of selling products directly to the people who use them
"I work in retail, in a company with more than three hundred stores."
Supplier -- a company that sells goods or services to another company
"We buy from more than four hundred suppliers."
Facilities -- the buildings and equipment a company needs in order to work
"Facilities covers the offices, the stores and the distribution centers."
Maintenance -- the work of keeping buildings and machines in good condition
"Maintenance costs money, but a broken freezer costs more."
Loss prevention -- the work of stopping products from being stolen or damaged
"Loss prevention is closer to the shop floor than to the office."
To oversee -- to be in charge of a team or a job and check that it goes well
"I oversee four areas, so I sign what I do not build myself."
To report to -- to have someone as your direct manager
"I report to the CFO, and four managers report to me."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Marcelo works for a retail group with three hundred stores. He oversees four areas, and each area has its own manager. He reports to the chief financial officer, and four managers report to him.

His week does not follow a fixed shape. Some days he visits a distribution center; other days he stays in the office and meets suppliers. He does not handle prices, and he does not decide the marketing budget. Does he enjoy it? He says the variety is the best part of the job.

1. Three verbs in the first paragraph end in -s: works, oversees, reports. Why does the last one, report, have no -s?
A Because it is a negative sentence.
B Because the subject is "four managers", which is not he, she or it.
C Because the verb "report" never takes an -s.
2. The text says "he does not handle prices", not "he does not handles". Why?
A The -s appears only once. "Does" already carries it, so the verb goes back to its base form.
B "Handle" is an irregular verb and has no -s form.
C Negative sentences in English always use the plural form.
3. Look at the question in the text: "Does he enjoy it?" What would be wrong with "He enjoys it?"
A Nothing. Both are equally correct in a meeting.
B A real question needs "do" or "does" in front. Without it, it is a statement with a question mark.
C The verb should move to the end of the sentence.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Present Simple

GRAMMAR

The tense you use for facts, jobs and routines. One verb, one -s, one place.

SubjectPositiveNegativeQuestion
I / you / we / theyI oversee four areas.I do not oversee sales.Do you oversee sales?
he / she / itShe oversees four areas.She does not oversee sales.Does she oversee sales?
Spelling of the -swork → works · report → reports · watch → watches · go → goes · have → has · do → does
The -s appears onceIf does takes the -s, the verb gives it back: "she does not oversee", never "does not oversees".
No am / is / are"I work in retail", never "I am work in retail". Am, is and are never sit next to another verb here.

Useful with this tense: usually, often, sometimes, never, every month, twice a week. They tell the listener that you are describing a shape, not a single day.

Lesson 02 -- Pre-class

A Week That Never Repeats -- Routine and Right Now

The words of a working week: warehouse, shift, deadline, to deal with, to follow up, headquarters, workload, on site. Structure: the present simple for what is always true, and the present continuous for what is only true this week. Mission: describe your real week without mixing the two.
0%
▼

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Warehouse -- a large building where products are kept before they reach the stores
"The warehouse receives four trucks a day."
Shift -- a fixed block of working hours, for example from six in the morning to two
"The night shift starts at ten."
Deadline -- the day or hour by which something has to be ready
"The deadline for the monthly report is always Friday."
To deal with -- to give attention to a problem or a person until it is resolved
"The night shift is dealing with the extra volume."
To follow up -- to check again later that something really happened
"I am following up on the supplier contract today."
Headquarters -- the main office of a company, where the senior team sits
"The results meeting is on Monday, at headquarters."
Workload -- the amount of work a person or a team has to do
"The workload is heavy, but nobody is complaining yet."
On site -- at the place where the work happens, not at the office
"See you on site on Wednesday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Every Monday the senior team meets at headquarters, and the deadline for the report is always Friday. That part never changes. I visit the warehouse twice a month, and the night shift starts at ten.

This month, though, the shape is different. Two suppliers moved their dates, so the warehouse is receiving seven trucks a day instead of four. I am spending two days on site this week, and the team is dealing with a much heavier workload. It is not permanent. Next month we go back to normal, and I am following up on the supplier contract today so that it really happens.

1. Why does the writer say "the warehouse is receiving seven trucks" and not "receives"?
A Because "receive" is an irregular verb and needs the -ing form.
B Because it is only true this month, not always. The normal number is four.
C Because the sentence is about the future.
2. In the first paragraph, why is every verb in the simple form?
A Everything there is a routine or a fact that does not change from week to week.
B Because the first paragraph of a text never uses the continuous.
C Because those actions are finished and the continuous is only for the past.
3. Which words in the text tell you which tense to use?
A The names of the places: headquarters, warehouse.
B The time words: every Monday and twice a month against this month, this week and today.
C The numbers: four trucks, seven trucks, two days.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple and Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

One tense for your life, one tense for your week. The time word decides, not the verb.

TenseUse it forExample
Present simple
I visit / she visits		Facts, routines and things that do not change: your life.I visit the warehouse twice a month.
Present continuous
I am visiting		Now, this period only, and fixed plans in the near future: your week.This week I am visiting it twice.
Time wordsSimple: always, usually, every month, twice a week, never · Continuous: now, today, this week, at the moment, tonight
Negative and questionSimple: I do not visit · Does she visit?  |  Continuous: I am not visiting · Are you visiting?
Verbs with no -ingSome verbs describe a state, not an action: know, want, need, believe, belong, understand. "I know the supplier", never "I am knowing".

A useful habit: read the time word first, choose the tense, and only then say the verb. That order stops almost every mistake in this pair.
