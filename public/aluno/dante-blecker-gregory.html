Lesson 1 — Dragon Rider

Series
Dragons: Race to the Edge — Season 1, Episode 1

Hiccup and Toothless fly to new islands and meet new dragons. Every episode is full of can and can't: what each dragon can do, and what it can't.

Tip: watch in English with English subtitles. After the episode, say one sentence: "The dragon can ___, but it can't ___."

Watch on Netflix →
Audio Story
St. George and the Dragon — Storynory

A free 9-minute audio story, read slowly by a real actor. A brave knight, a scared town and one very big dragon. The full text is on the same page, so you can listen and read at the same time.

Tip: listen once with your eyes closed. Then listen again while you read the words.

Listen on Storynory →
Video
Finale — Dragons: Race to the Edge (Netflix Family)

A short clip on YouTube, free. Big wings, sharp claws, dragons flying over the sea — exactly the words from this lesson, in a real scene.

Tip: pause the clip and name three parts of the dragon: wings, claws, tail.

Watch on YouTube →

Lesson 2 — My Lego City

Series
LEGO City Adventures — Season 1, Episode 1

Short, funny episodes set in a city full of towers, bridges and streets. The characters say there is and there are in almost every scene, because they are always describing where things are.

Tip: watch in English with English subtitles. After the episode, name three things that are in the city.

Watch on Netflix →
Audio Story
The Town Mouse and the Country Mouse — Storynory

A free audio story about two mice: one lives in the city, one lives in the country. Full of houses, streets and rooms — and the text is on the same page, so you can listen and read together.

Tip: after listening, say two things that are in the town and two things that are in the country.

Listen on Storynory →
Video
I Built a LEGO City From Scratch! — Half-Asleep Chris

A real person builds a whole LEGO city and explains every step out loud: where the tower goes, what is next to it, how many bricks are left. It is this lesson, in a real video.

Tip: pause when he puts a new building down and say where it is before he does.

Watch on YouTube →

Lesson 3 — Robot Factory

Movie
WALL·E — Disney+

A robot who does the same job every single day, alone, for hundreds of years: he lifts, he carries, he fixes, he never stops. The first forty minutes have almost no words, so you understand everything from the pictures — and then the English starts.

Tip: watch in English. After twenty minutes, say three things WALL·E does every day. Don't forget the -s.

Watch on Disney+ →
Podcast
Who decides what robots look like? — But Why

A free podcast made for curious kids. In this episode they visit a real factory with hundreds of robots, and two engineers answer children's questions: why robots are made of metal, and whether robots cry.

Tip: listen in the car. Afterwards, tell somebody one thing the factory robots do every day.

Listen on Vermont Public →
Video
Atlas Gets a Grip — Boston Dynamics

A real robot, not a cartoon. Atlas picks up a heavy bag of tools, carries it across a building site and throws it up to a worker. Every verb from this lesson happens on screen in two minutes.

Tip: watch it twice. The second time, say what the robot does out loud, while it does it: "He lifts the bag. He carries it."

Watch on YouTube →
