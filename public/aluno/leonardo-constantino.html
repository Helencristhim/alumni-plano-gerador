GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Leonardo is a partner at a boutique law firm in Brazil. He works in tax law, and almost all of his clients are financial institutions: banks, insurers, fintechs and investment funds. Every month he presents projections to those clients, and he answers hard questions about them. These days he is working on the tax reform. Right now he is building three scenarios, because every stakeholder in the room wants a number before the rules change. He knows the technical side very well. What he is improving this year is the English that carries it.

1. Why does the text say "he is working on the tax reform" and not "he works"?
A It is a current project, in progress these days (present continuous).
B It is something he does every single day of his life.
C It finished a long time ago.
2. "Every month he presents projections" uses the present simple because:
A It is happening at this exact second.
B It is a routine that repeats.
C It is temporary, just for this week.
3. The text says "he knows the technical side", never "he is knowing". Why?
A Because the sentence is about the past.
B Because "know" is an irregular verb.
C Because "know" is a state verb, and state verbs do not take -ing.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple vs Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

How to say what is always true about your practice and what you are working on right now.

FormUseExample
Present Simple
I / you / we + verb (he / she + -s)		Facts, routines and permanent situations - always true.I advise financial institutions.
Present Continuous
am / is / are + verb-ing		Right now, today, these days - temporary or unfinished.I am reviewing the reform impact.
Negativedo not / does not + verb · am not / is not / are not + -ingWe do not advise individuals. / She is not leading that project now.
QuestionDo / Does + subject + verb · Am / Is / Are + subject + -ingDo you advise banks? / Are you working on the reform?
State verbsknow, want, need, understand, believe, mean, prefer almost never take -ing: "I need two more days" (not "I am needing").
Time wordsevery day, always, usually, never → simple · right now, today, this month, these days → continuous

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Leonardo has worked in tax law for more than ten years. He started his career trajectory at a Big Four firm, where he spent four years in audit, and then he moved to a large bank. He joined his current firm as an associate, and a few years later he became a partner. Since then, he has led several advisory mandates for banks and payment companies. This year he has already carried out three compliance reviews, and he has just finished the impact projections for the next reform milestone. He has not decided the date of his doctoral application yet, but he has never stopped studying. His audit background still helps him read a balance sheet faster than most lawyers.

1. The text says "he spent four years in audit", not "he has spent". Why?
A Because the action is still happening today.
B Because that period is finished and closed in the past.
C Because "spend" has no past participle.
2. In "he has worked in tax law for more than ten years", what does "for" tell us?
A How long the period is, and it still continues today.
B The exact year when he started.
C That the period ended ten years ago.
3. Why does the text use "he has not decided the date yet"?
A Because he decided it a long time ago.
B Because "yet" always goes with the past simple.
C Because it has not happened up to now, and it is still expected.
4. "Since then, he has led several advisory mandates." What does "since" introduce?
A The length of the period, in years.
B The starting point of a period that reaches the present.
C A reason, like "because".

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect vs Past Simple

GRAMMAR

The test is simple: if the sentence says WHEN, use the past simple. If the time is still open, use the present perfect.

FormUseExample
Present Perfect
have / has + past participle		The period started in the past and still counts now. No finished date.I have advised financial institutions since 2016.
Past Simple
verb + -ed / irregular		The period is closed, and we usually say when it happened.I joined the firm in 2016.
Negativehave not / has not + past participle · did not + base verbWe have not signed the mandate. / We did not sign it last week.
QuestionHave / Has + subject + past participle · Did + subject + base verbHave you ever advised a foreign bank? / When did you join the firm?
for / sincefor + a length of time (for ten years, for six months) · since + a starting point (since 2016, since I became a partner)
already / yet / justalready = sooner than expected · yet = not up to now (negatives and questions) · just = a moment ago
ever / neverExperience in your whole life, with no date: Have you ever worked abroad? I have never worked in London.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Leonardo is briefing a foreign partner on two live mandates. "My role involves indirect tax for financial institutions, and I am currently leading the reform implementation project," he says. "We have already assessed the impact for eleven clients. In four of them the cash flow impact was positive, because the new tax base works in their favour. The committee has not published the final rate yet, so nobody can promise a number." Then he moves to the advice. "Every financial holding must file a return under the new rules from January. That is the law, not my opinion. Before that, the client should review the structure, because that is where we can mitigate risk. They do not have to decide this week, but good tax planning keeps them ahead of the curve. What I recommend is one meeting with the CFO, so that we cut through the noise and deal with the tax liability question first."

1. Why does the text use "must file a return" and not "should file a return"?
A Because "must" is a politer way of giving advice.
B Because it is an obligation written in the law, not a personal recommendation.
C Because the action happens in the past.
2. "They do not have to decide this week." What does this mean?
A Deciding this week is prohibited.
B Deciding this week is not necessary, but it is allowed.
C They have already decided.
3. Why does he say "the committee has not published the final rate yet"?
A Because the committee published it a long time ago.
B Because "yet" always goes with the past simple.
C Because it has not happened up to now, and it is still expected.
4. "That is where we can mitigate risk." What does "can" express here?
A A legal obligation for the client.
B An option, and what his firm is able to do.
C Something that happened last year.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Must, Have to, Should, Can

GRAMMAR

One question decides the modal: is this the law, my advice, or an option? And remember the project update tense: what has already happened, and what has not happened yet.

FormUseExample
must + base verbA strong obligation, often written in the law itselfThey must file a return in January.
have to / has to + base verbAn obligation from outside: a rule, a client, a deadlineEvery holding has to report the tax base.
should + base verbYour professional recommendation, not an obligationThe board should review the structure now.
can + base verbPossibility, option, or what your firm is able to doWe can mitigate the risk before the vote.
Negativemust not = prohibited · do not have to = not necessary. They are opposites, not synonyms.
QuestionDo we have to file this quarter? · Should we wait for the final text? · Can you send the memo today?
No "to", no "-s""should to consider" and "he musts file" do not exist. Only have keeps the to.
Project updateWe have already assessed the impact · They have not approved it yet · I have just sent the model · for eight months · since the first draft.
