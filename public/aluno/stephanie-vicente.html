Block 1 — the first four lessons

Four lessons built around one thing you do every week: talking about teaching, in English, with someone who is not going to switch to Portuguese.

LessonTopicWhat you will do

Lesson 01 — Before you read either one

1 · Who is each document for

Two documents describe the same course. One is a lesson plan; the other is the syllabus the course was sold with.

Mark the two statements that are true of a course syllabus and not of a lesson plan.

It is written by a teacher
People outside the classroom rely on it
It says what will happen in one session
Changing it needs someone else to agree
It can be adjusted while the course runs
It lists every task the students will do
A syllabus is a commitment: it is read by people who were not in the room, and it is not the teacher’s to change alone. A plan is an instrument for one lesson.

2 · Key language

Choose the better meaning for each expression. You will meet all six in the two documents.

to allocate something
to run beyond something
a commitment
to be allocated to
approval
to state something

3 · What the sentence is doing

Six sentences from a meeting about a course. Match each one to what the speaker is doing.

“The document allocates twenty hours to the unit.”
“So somebody changed it after it was approved.”
“Who signs this off — you or the head of studies?”
“I would leave the assessment where the syllabus has it.”
“Sorry — is that week seven or week eight?”
“That is in the plan, not in the syllabus.”
Why these six
Every one of them will happen in the lesson. The difference between the first two is the whole point of the lesson: one can be checked against the page, the other cannot.

4 · The plan, on its own

Read the lesson plan. Nothing else — you are not comparing anything yet.

Lesson plan — Unit 7, Reporting what happened
Unit 7 runs over 18 teaching hours, from week 5 to week 7. The unit closes with a written report of 180–220 words. The report is corrected by a classmate against the checklist on page 4, and then rewritten once; the teacher reads the rewritten version. The unit assessment is in week 7, in the last session. There is a listening task in session 4 built on a recorded staff meeting; it is not assessed.

Mark the two things the plan does not tell you.

How long the unit runs
Why the report is corrected by a classmate
When the assessment happens
How long the written report is
Whether anyone approved these choices
What the listening task is built on
The plan says what will happen. It never says why, and it never says who agreed to it — and that is normal for a plan.

5 · The syllabus, on its own

Now the syllabus. Again on its own.

Course syllabus — General English B1, second semester
The semester is divided into four units. Unit 7 is allocated 20 teaching hours, and no unit may run beyond its allocation without approval from the coordination. Written work produced at the end of a unit is corrected by the teacher, with written feedback returned to the student within one week. Each unit is assessed in the week after it ends. This syllabus states what the course commits to; it does not list every task a teacher may use.
A unit that needs more hours than it was given
The end-of-unit written work
A task that this document does not mention
The last line is the one to hold on to
A document that does not claim to list everything is not disagreeing with a document that lists more. You will need that distinction in class.

“These two on who corrects the writing.”

“ that nobody reread the syllabus.”

Lesson 03 — Five moments, before you tell them

1 · What an observation note is for

This is the form a school gives an observer to fill in.

Observation record — teaching practice
Section A: what happened, in the order it happened
Section B: what the observer concluded
Note: Section B is discussed with the teacher. Section A is not open to discussion.

Mark the two statements that follow from that note.

Section A has to be something both people saw
Section B is the more important one
Putting an opinion in Section A takes away the teacher’s right to reply
The observer should avoid concluding anything
Section A is written after the class
Both sections say the same thing in different words
The two sections exist because they have different statuses. What is in A is shared ground; what is in B is the observer’s, and can be argued with. Mixing them removes the argument without winning it.

2 · Key language

Choose the better meaning for each expression. All six describe classrooms.

to work in pairs
a checking question
to move on
to go quiet
to notice something
the pace of a lesson

3 · Which section would it go in

Six sentences from observation notes. Which section of the form does each one belong to?

“Three students had their hands up when he moved on.”
“The pace was too fast for this group.”
“They were still writing when he collected the papers.”
“The instruction came before they were ready for it.”
“He asked a checking question and took the first answer.”
“The group was not following by the third example.”
The test
Could the teacher who taught the class disagree with the sentence? If yes, it is Section B, whatever it looks like.

4 · What was already going on

Read the note. Do not change anything — just answer the questions under it.

The students were working in pairs on the error-correction task when the teacher gave the next instruction. Two of them were still finishing the previous one. The back table went quiet as soon as he got to the third example.

Lesson 04 — Before you sit down with him

1 · What the meeting is

This is what the school tells new teachers about observation feedback.

After an observation
The observer meets the teacher within two working days. The purpose is development, not assessment. Nothing said in the meeting goes into a performance record, and the teacher decides what to do with the feedback.

Mark the two things that follow from that.

The teacher can decline a suggestion
The observer should avoid saying anything critical
The observer has to give reasons, not instructions
The meeting has no consequences at all
The teacher has to agree by the end
The observer decides what changes
If the decision is his, then what you bring has to be arguable. That is not softness — it is what makes the meeting worth having.

2 · Key language

Choose the better meaning for each expression. All six are in the conversation.

it might be worth …-ing
to push back
that is fair
to look at something (in feedback)
to have a point
to act on something

3 · Who decides

Six sentences from feedback meetings. In each one, who is left deciding what happens next?

“You need to change the order of those two stages.”
“Have you thought about changing the order?”
“Something to think about for next time.”
“One thing you might try is giving it earlier.”
“Please make sure the instruction comes first.”
“We could look at that again after the next class.”
Politeness is not the same as room
Number 5 is the politest sentence in the list and it leaves him nothing to answer. The question is never how nice it sounds — it is who is left deciding.

4 · The class you watched

These are the notes you took. Read them once.

09:38 — third time. Checking question asked; first answer taken; three hands still up when he moved on.
Teacher is new to the school. Group is warm, engaged, and slow to finish.
Happened three times
Happened once, and you saw it
Is not in your notes at all
The checking question matters
He did ask one. If he brings that up in the meeting, he is right — and your notes already say so.

What he cannot do is deny what you both saw. Everything in the first column is about the reason — and about the reason, he knows more than you do.
