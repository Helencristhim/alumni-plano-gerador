Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

In a large group, HR is not one team. It is organised in three parts, and each part has a different job. The first part is the HR Business Partner. The business partner sits inside the business, next to the leaders of each business unit, and is responsible for the people agenda there: hiring, performance, organisation and cost of the team.

The second part is the centre of excellence. This team designs the rules: the policy on pay, the policy on promotion, the way performance is measured. It usually sits at headquarters, and it has a global scope. The centre of excellence writes the policy; the business partner explains it to the managers and makes it work in each country.

The third part is shared services. This team runs the operations: contracts, payroll, documents, systems. When a manager asks for a certificate or a payslip, shared services takes care of it. The business partner does not.

A global business partner has one more line on the organisation chart. She has a reporting line to the global HR director at headquarters, and often a dotted-line reporting to a regional leader. The solid line is the manager; the dotted line is the person she also keeps informed. Both are stakeholders, and so are the country managers, the finance team and the employees themselves.

This is why the same person can be responsible for three entities in three countries and still say, without any contradiction, that payroll is not her responsibility. Knowing where your scope ends is part of explaining what you do.

2. A manager asks for a payslip. Who takes care of it?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- responsible / responsibility, report / reporting line

GRAMMAR

The same idea has more than one form in English, and the form depends on the place in the sentence. After the verb be, you need the adjective. After have, or after my, you need the noun.

FormWhat it doesExample
be responsible for + somethingadjective after the verb beI am responsible for three entities.
my / a responsibilitynoun: the thing itselfHiring is my responsibility.
report to + person or roleverb: the actionI report to the Global HR Director.
my reporting linenoun phrase: the line in the structureMy reporting line goes to headquarters.
Watch outthe preposition. It is responsible for and report to. "Responsible of" and "report for" do not exist in English.

Quick test before you speak: after "I am", use responsible. After "It is my", use responsibility.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

Every car that leaves a showroom carries two decisions: who sells it and who pays for it. The group sells the car through its dealer network, and a captive finance company pays for it, first for the dealer and then for the driver.

For the dealer, the product is the floor plan. The cars in the showroom were produced and delivered long before any customer arrived, and somebody has to finance them while they wait. For the driver, the product is retail financing: a loan for the person who takes the car home. The two products have the same name in Portuguese conversation and very different names in an English meeting.

The rental company sits next to them. Its clients are companies with fleets, and its products are fleet rental and leasing. The idea is simple: the client uses the car and gives it back. The group takes care of the rest.

All these loans together form the loan portfolio, and the share of the cars sold that the group itself finances is the penetration rate. When the financial results are presented, these two words appear in almost every slide, and they are not interchangeable: the portfolio is the size, the rate is the share.

A last point about form. Finance is the action and the area. Financing is the activity as a thing: retail financing grew, fleet financing was stable. Financial describes another word: financial results, financial services, financial institution. Three forms, one family, and a mistake in the middle of a sentence is enough to make a clear idea sound uncertain.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- finance / financing / financial, lease / leasing / leased

GRAMMAR

The same family, three jobs in the sentence. The action, the activity and the description are different words, and English does not accept them in the wrong place.

FormWhat it doesExample
finance (verb)the action: somebody pays for somethingWe finance four out of ten cars sold.
financing (noun)the activity as a thing you can measureRetail financing grew last year.
financial (adjective)describes another nounThe financial results, a financial institution.
lease / leasing / leasedthe same three jobs in the rental businessThe client leases a car; leasing grew; the leased cars come back.
Watch outusing the -ing form as a description. "The financing results" and "the leasing cars" are the two mistakes a native ear hears immediately.

Quick test: before a noun, use financial. Alone, as the thing itself, use financing. With a subject doing it, use finance.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

Three messages arrive before nine o'clock, and they are not the same kind of message. The first one is a request: a manager in Argentina asks for an approval. The second one is an issue: shared services found a problem in the contract of a new joiner. The third one is a policy update from headquarters, and it ends with four letters that change everything: FYI, for your information.

The first question is not "what do I answer?" but "what kind of message is this?". A request needs a decision. An issue needs an action, sometimes from another team. A policy update usually needs nothing at all, except the two minutes it takes to read it.

The second question is the deadline. The manager in Argentina writes "as soon as possible", and everybody writes that. The manager in Chile writes that the person leaves on the thirtieth. Only one of those two is a real date. When you answer, you give a real date back: "I get back to you by end of day."

Every morning you go through the inbox, you decide what your priority is, and you leave some messages open. Those are pending items. Once a week you follow up on them, because a pending item that nobody looks at becomes a problem in three weeks. Some of them are on hold, and that is different: they are paused because something else has to happen first, usually a budget confirmation.

Notice the tense in the sentences above. All of this happens every day, so it lives in the present: I go through, I decide, I follow up. The past belongs to what finished: last Friday I approved the position in Chile. When you say "I needed to approve it", a manager reads a closed story, and your real message, which is about today, does not arrive.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

Every answer to a request has three parts, and they always come in the same order: the decision, the reason and the next step. What changes between a good answer and a bad one is not the decision. It is whether the other two parts are there.

Look at the yes first. "The replacement is approved, subject to approval by the CFO." The manager reads a positive decision and, in the same line, learns that one sign-off is still missing. Nobody is surprised two weeks later.

Now the not yet. "Could you provide more details about the workload and the monthly cost?" This is not a refusal; it is a clarification. The request was incomplete, and a requirement is missing. The manager has something to do, and the conversation continues.

And the no. "I'm afraid we can't approve a new position at this stage." Two expressions carry the weight. I'm afraid announces bad news politely, and it is always I am, never I have: afraid is an adjective, like sure, sorry and ready, and adjectives go with the verb be. At this stage says today, not forever.

One more family to keep straight, because it appears in every one of these messages. Approve is the action: I approve the request. Approval is the thing: the request needs my approval. Approved describes the request after the decision: the position was approved on Friday. Three forms, one decision, and one sentence is enough to show which one you control.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- approve / approval / approved, and be + afraid

GRAMMAR

One family for the decision, and one rule about adjectives that stops the most repeated mistake of your consultancy session.

FormWhat it doesExample
approve (verb)the action you takeI approve the request today.
approval (noun)the official yes as a thingThe position needs the approval of the CFO.
approved (past participle)describes the request after the decisionThe request was approved on Friday.
be + afraid / sure / sorry / readyadjectives always go with be, never with haveI'm afraid we can't. I'm sure about the date.
Watch out"I have afraid", "I have sure" and "I am approval". Afraid goes with be; approval is a thing, not an action.

Quick test: if the word describes you, use I am. If it is a thing you give or need, use approval. If it is what you do, use approve.
