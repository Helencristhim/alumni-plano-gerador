1 · Context & lead-in

The call you are about to hear

The call

Your school is deciding whether to adopt a new coursebook. Three people are on the line and you are the only one from your school.

Where it sits

Nothing is decided today. This is the call where each side says what matters to them.

Your job afterwards

Tell your coordinator who wanted what — and ask the one question nobody asked.

2 · Prediction

Before you hear it

Before you hear it: on a call like this, who usually speaks first, and why?
aThe publisher, because they called the meeting✓ Main idea
bThe person who already uses the material, because everyone wants the practical answer first✓ Main idea
cThe author, because it is their book✓ Main idea
B. On a call like this the practical answer is what everyone is waiting for, so the person who has actually used the thing speaks first. If you chose A, you were thinking about who organised the call — which is a different question from who the room wants to hear.
Two quick predictions. We will check them against the recording.
  • 1What will the publisher want to talk about?
  • 2What will the teacher who already uses it want to talk about?

5 · Micro-listening

What the three moves do

Each move, and the line that shows it
Checking buys you the right to be wrong.Tom: “So, to be clear before we go on — you are saying the material works?”
Correcting is not disagreeing.Marta: “I am saying the material works and the clock has to be planned for.”
Conceding keeps the point alive.Priya: “But I understand that it costs the teacher time.” — said while keeping the design decision.

7 · Your call

Two minutes, and one thing to check

Your side

You join the same call, for your school. You have two minutes: say where your school stands on the pacing, check one thing you did not catch, and ask the question nobody asked.

Their side

Your teacher plays Tom. He will summarise what you said — and he will get one part of it slightly wrong, on purpose.

Three things have to happen
  1. 1You say where your school stands, without turning it into a complaint.
  2. 2You check something you did not catch — before it goes in the minutes.
  3. 3When he summarises you wrongly, you correct how you were understood, not him.
Two minutes. That is what you get on a real call.
