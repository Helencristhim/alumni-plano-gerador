Chapter 3: The Code

By Thursday It Will Have Happened

The two tenses that carry a prediction

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"By Thursday the negotiators will have been in the same building for eleven days."

"This time next year we will still be talking about the same border."

"By the time the season starts, that record will have been rewritten twice."

"At this hour on Tuesday the results will be coming in from the north."

All four are about the future. In two of them, the thing is finished before a moment arrives. In the other two, it is still going on when the moment arrives. Which is which, and what changes in the verb?

FormWhat it saysExample
will have + past participleFinished BEFORE a moment in the future. The moment is always named."By Thursday they will have signed it."
will be + verb-ingIn progress AT that moment. Not finished, not started then either."At this hour on Tuesday the results will be coming in."
will have been + for + durationHow long something will have lasted by that moment. This is the analyst's favorite."By Thursday they will have been there for eleven days."
The words that trigger themby, by the time, this time next year, at this hour. When you hear one of these, one of the two forms is coming."By the time the season starts..."
The trapA simple will after by the time says the wrong thing: it puts the event AT the moment instead of before it.NOT "By Thursday they will sign it."

Both forms exist in Portuguese and you already use them. What is new is how often English puts them at the front of a sentence, and how much of a news broadcast is built out of them.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"By the time the vote comes, the coalition will collapse."
"By the time the vote comes, the coalition will have collapsed."
"By December they will be at war for three years."
"By December they will have been at war for three years."
"This time next year we are still talking about the same border."
"This time next year we will still be talking about the same border."

The trigger word decides the form. After by or by the time, the event has to be over, so it takes will have. A duration always needs will have been, never a plain will be. And this time next year is the future, so the present continuous cannot carry it. Read the correct versions aloud twice.

Dialogue

Two Hundred Reports From Now

D
Had you read about that story before you heard the broadcast, or were you coming to it cold?
B
I had read about it in Portuguese that morning. It was still too fast for me.
D
Too fast, or too much at once? Those are different problems and only one of them is about speed.
B
Too much at once, I think. I get the names and then I lose the sentence around them.
D
Then you are already doing the right thing. By the end of the year you will have listened to about two hundred of those reports, and by then the speed will have stopped being the problem.
B
And in the meantime? What should I be listening for?
D
The names and the days. They carry the skeleton, and while you are catching those you will be training the rest without noticing.
B
So I stop trying to hear the whole sentence and I start collecting the skeleton.

2. Which distinction does she make about the problem?
Between too fast and too much at once -- two different problems, and only one of them is about speed.
3. What does she predict about the end of the year?
That he will have listened to about two hundred reports, and that by then the speed will have stopped being the problem.
4. What does she tell him to listen for in the meantime?
The names and the days -- they carry the skeleton of the report.

Listening 2

Four Rules for the News

A short method clip. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What is the first rule, and how much of the difficulty does he say it removes?
Know the story before you listen -- read two paragraphs in your own language, and that removes about ninety percent of the difficulty.
2. What exactly should you listen for, and why those words?
The proper nouns -- names of people, names of places, days of the week. They are stressed, they are slow, and they are the skeleton of the report.
3. What is the fourth rule, and what does he say it proves?
Listen to the same three minutes twice, on two different days. The second time feels like a different recording, and that feeling is the only proof of progress you get.

Free Practice

Ninety Seconds on the Week Ahead

Scenario: You are the analyst closing the broadcast. Take the story you named at the start of the class. Say where it stands now, name one flashpoint and the day to watch, and make two predictions with dates.

No keywords. The broadcast is yours.

The Habit

Three Minutes, Twice

Before the next class, read two paragraphs about one story in Portuguese. Then find three minutes of the same story in English and listen twice, on two different days, writing down only the names and the days.

The second listen will feel like a different recording. That feeling is the progress.
