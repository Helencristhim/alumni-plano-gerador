Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

Who Is Ana, Really? -- Your Life, Not Your Job Title

Introducing yourself through your life: your town, your house and your dogs. Key words: countryside, commute, neighborhood, pace of life, rush hour, to grow up, to move away, peace and quiet, to be fed up with, to settle down. Structures: past simple (finished time) and present perfect (experience and time that is still open).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Countryside -- the land and small towns outside big cities
"I have lived in the countryside since 2021."
Commute -- the daily trip between your home and your work
"My commute used to take two hours each way."
Neighborhood -- the area around your home and the people who live there
"I grew up in a busy neighborhood in Sao Paulo."
Pace of life -- how fast or slow daily life feels in a place
"The pace of life here is much slower."
Rush hour -- the time of day when traffic is at its worst
"I have not sat in rush hour once since I moved."
To grow up -- to spend your childhood in a place and become an adult there
"I grew up in Sao Paulo and left at forty."
To move away -- to leave a place and go and live somewhere else
"We moved away from the city six years ago."
Peace and quiet -- calm, with no noise and nobody disturbing you
"I came here for the peace and quiet."
To be fed up with -- to be tired and annoyed with something that has lasted too long
"I was completely fed up with the traffic."
To settle down -- to start a calm, permanent life in one place
"We settled down in a town of forty thousand people."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Ana grew up in a busy neighborhood in Sao Paulo and lived there for forty years. For most of that time she did not question the pace of life: two hours of commute in the morning, two more at night, and rush hour every single day. Then one winter she got completely fed up with it. In 2021 she moved away and settled down in a town of forty thousand people. She has lived in the countryside since then, and she has never regretted it. People often ask her if she misses the city. She has not missed it once — she came for the peace and quiet, and she found it.

1. Why do we say "she has lived in the countryside since 2021" and not "she lives"?
A It is in progress these days, not finished (present continuous).
B It is something she does every single day.
C It happened once and it is completely finished.
2. "She has lived in the countryside since 2021" uses the present perfect because:
A It is happening at this exact moment.
B It is a routine that repeats every day.
C It is a temporary situation, just for this month.
3. The text says "she wants a quieter life", never "she is wanting". Why?
A Because the sentence is about the past.
B Because "want" is an irregular verb.
C Because "want" is a state verb, and state verbs do not take -ing.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple vs Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

How to say what is always true and what is happening now.

FormUseExample
Present Simple
I / you / we + verb (he / she + -s)		Facts, routines and permanent situations - always true.I live in the countryside.
Present Continuous
am / is / are + verb-ing		Right now, today, these days - temporary or unfinished.I am restoring a cabinet.
Negativedo not / does not + verb · am not / is not / are not + -ingI do not drive to work. / She is not sleeping.
QuestionDo / Does + subject + verb · Am / Is / Are + subject + -ingDo you live here? / Are you working now?
State verbslike, love, want, need, know, believe, understand almost never take -ing: "I like this town" (not "I am liking").
Time wordsevery day, always, usually, never → simple · right now, today, this week, these days → continuous

Lesson 1 — The Life I've Had

Series
The Repair Shop -- Season 1, Episode 1 (full episode)

People bring broken family treasures to a barn full of craftspeople, who bring them back to life: furniture, clocks, old toys. Every object has a story behind it. If you love restoring things, this is your world in English -- and the restorers explain exactly what they are doing while they do it.

Tip: listen for the present continuous. The restorers narrate their own work: "I am taking this apart", "I am sanding it down". Say one sentence about what you are fixing at home this week.

Watch on YouTube ↗
Podcast
Urban Exodus, E69 -- Academics leave city life for a farm in the Australian bush

Louise and David sold a comfortable city life in Sydney and moved 27 kilometers from the nearest town. They talk about what they gained, what they miss, and what nobody tells you about the silence. It is the same decision you made, told by other people -- in Australian English.

Tip: this is a different accent from the American English you hear in class. Listen once for the general story only, without trying to catch every word. That is the skill we are training.

Documentary
Inside the Mind of a Dog (Netflix)

What dogs actually think, how they read us, and why a rescued street dog bonds the way it does. Narrated clearly and slowly, with scientists explaining their work. Seventy-five minutes about the animals who wake you up every morning.

Tip: watch with English subtitles the first time, then rewatch one favorite scene with the subtitles off and see how much you still catch.
