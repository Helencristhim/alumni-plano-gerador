Kirsten Dirksen -- "They left for city. Offspring came back to restore farmstead"

A family that moved to the city two generations ago, and the children who came back to rebuild the old farmstead by hand. It is your lesson in real life: an entire way of living that people used to have, told by the people who went back for it.

Tip: write down every "used to" and every "would" you hear about the old days. You will notice that states take used to and repeated actions take both.

Lesson 11 — Getting Used to the Quiet

Video
Kirsten Dirksen -- "Left BBC's Planet Earth to start dream family homestead (full tour)"

A wildlife cameraman who filmed Planet Earth left that life to build a self-sufficient homestead with his wife on eighty acres of old quarry land in Wales. Forty minutes of exactly what this lesson is about: the trade-offs, the first hard winters, and the slow way a difficult place becomes home.

Tip: write down every "used to", "we are used to" and "we got used to" you hear. He switches between all three in the same interview, so it is the best listening test for tonight's structure.
