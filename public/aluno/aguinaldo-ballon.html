If the Tender Fails -- Talking About Risk

An investor asks what will happen if something goes wrong. Key words: a tender, a bid, to award, a tariff, exposure, a penalty, to comply with, a shortfall, a guarantee, uncertainty. Structure: first and second conditional.

▼

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A tender -- an official process where companies compete for a public contract
"The state will open a tender for the new highway."
A bid -- the offer a company makes in a tender, with its price and conditions
"Our client lost the bid by a small margin."
To award -- to give a contract officially to the winner of a tender
"The contract was awarded to a Spanish group."
A tariff -- the price users pay for a public service, such as a toll or electricity
"If the tariff goes up, users will complain."
Exposure -- how much a company can lose if something goes wrong
"The fund wants to reduce its exposure to currency risk."
A penalty -- money a company must pay when it breaks the rules of a contract
"If the work is late, the concession will pay a penalty."
To comply with -- to follow a rule, a law or a contract
"The company must comply with the new safety rules."
A shortfall -- the difference between the money you need and the money you have
"A fall in traffic would create a revenue shortfall."
A guarantee -- a promise, usually from a bank or the state, to pay if something fails
"The investors asked for a state guarantee."
Uncertainty -- a situation where you do not know what will happen
"Uncertainty about the rules is the biggest risk for investors."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- First and Second Conditional

GRAMMAR

A real possibility: if + present, will. An imaginary situation: if + past, would.

JobFormExample
A real possibility (first conditional)If + present simple, will + verbIf the regulator delays the license, we will ask for more time.
An imaginary or unlikely situation (second conditional)If + past simple, would + verbIf the rules were more stable, more funds would invest.
With be, in the second conditionalIf I / he / it were ...If I were the regulator, I would publish it.
The order can changeresult first, no commaWe will ask for more time if the regulator delays it.
Less certain resultmight or could instead of will / wouldIf traffic falls, we might need a guarantee.
The trapnever will after ifNot If the regulator will delay. Say If the regulator delays.

Never: If the regulator will delay · If the rules are more stable, investors would invest · If I was the regulator, I will publish · If traffic falls, it would have a shortfall (for a real risk)

GRAMMAR

When you report what somebody said, the verb usually moves one step back in time. Tell needs a person; say does not.

present becomes pastsaid (that) + past simple"It is slow." → She said it was slow.
