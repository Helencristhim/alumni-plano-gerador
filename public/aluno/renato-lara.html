New in the Line-Up

Stage 1.1: Beach Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Line-up — the place in the water where surfers wait for a wave
"The line-up is quiet at six in the morning."
Local — a person who lives in that place
"She is a local. She surfs here every day."
Crowded — full of people, with no space left
"The beach is crowded on Saturday."
Stoked — very happy and excited about something
"I am stoked. The swell is perfect."

"No worries, mate."
Host family — the family you live with in another country
"My host family is really relaxed."
Nervous — worried, because something is new
"I am nervous, but it is a good kind of nervous."
Swell — a group of waves that comes from far away
"The swell is small today, about one metre."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. Then answer the questions.

My name is Renato and I am fifteen. I am from Brazil and I am a surfer. Today is my first day in Australia. The beach is ten minutes from the house and the line-up is crowded, because it is Saturday. My host family are the Wilsons. They are nice, and their kids are in the water too. I am a little nervous, but I am stoked.
Why is the line-up crowded?
A Because the swell is big.
B Because it is Saturday.
How does Renato feel?
A A little nervous, and also stoked.
B Tired and bored.
Why is it "they are" and not "they is"?
A Because the sentence is a question.
B Because "they" is more than one person.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

The verb to be — and why it is never missing

Every English sentence needs a verb. To say who you are, where you are or how you feel, that verb is am, is or are.

Full form

I am
You are
He / She / It is
We / They are

Short form

I'm
You're
He's / She's / It's
We're / They're

+ The swell is big today.
− The swell is not big today. (short form: isn't)
? Is the swell big today? (the verb moves to the front — never "Do you are")
→ Yes, it is. / No, it isn't.

Lesson 02 — Pre-class

Surf School Monday

Stage 1.1: Surf School Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Wetsuit — a rubber suit that keeps you warm in cold water
"The water is cold. You need a wetsuit."
Tide — the movement of the sea up the beach and back
"High tide is at seven in the morning."
Paddle — to move your arms in the water to push the board forward
"We paddle out at six every morning."
Wipe out — to fall off your board in a wave
"I wipe out ten times in one session."
Lifeguard — the person who watches the beach and helps people in trouble
"The lifeguard sits in the tower until five."
Forecast — information about the weather and the waves for the next days
"He checks the forecast before he gets up."
Spot — one particular place where people surf
"The best spot at high tide is the north end."
Gear — all the equipment you need for a sport
"They take their gear from the van."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read about a coach's Monday. Then answer the questions.

Dave works at a surf academy. His Monday starts at five. He checks the forecast on his phone before he gets up. At six the students arrive, they take their gear from the van and they put on their wetsuits. Dave doesn't shout. He watches from the water and he corrects one thing at a time. The session finishes at eight. The students go to school, but Dave stays. He surfs alone for another hour, every single Monday.
What does Dave do before he gets up?
A He checks the forecast on his phone.
B He puts on his wetsuit.
What happens at eight?
A The students arrive.
B The session finishes and the students go to school.
Why is it "the students arrive" but "Dave arrives"?
A Because he, she and it add an -s to the verb.
B Because the second sentence is in the past.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Present simple — things that are always true

Routines, habits and facts. The verb never changes — except after he, she and it, which add an -s.

I / you / we / they

I check
I don't check
Do you check?

He / she / it

He checks
He doesn't check
Does he check?

Only one -s per sentence. If does or doesn't is there, the verb goes back to normal.
Spelling: watch → watches, finish → finishes, go → goes, study → studies.
Where the adverb goes: I always check. He never surfs on Sunday. (before the verb)
