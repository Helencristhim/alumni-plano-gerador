Stage 1.1: Ten Words for a First Conversation

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Ten words, three minutes.

Be into — to like something a lot and do it often
"I'm really into volleyball, but I don't play in a team."
Hang out — to spend free time with friends, with no plan
"After school we just hang out at somebody's house."
Get along with — to have a good relationship with somebody
"I get along with my cousins better than with my classmates."
In common — something two people both like or both have
"We have one thing in common: neither of us can cook."
Spare time — the hours when you are free, after school
"In my spare time I watch a lot of series."
At the moment — in this period of your life, not forever
"At the moment I'm learning two languages at the same time."
Most of the time — almost always, but not always
"Most of the time I walk home, but on Fridays I don't."
Actually — you say it to add a fact people do not expect
"Everyone thinks I'm from here. Actually, I moved last year."
An only child — a person with no brothers and no sisters
"I'm an only child, so the dog gets all the attention."
How about you? — the question that gives the turn back
"So that's my week. How about you?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the introduction. Then answer the questions.

Hi, I am Ruby and I live in Manchester. I am twelve. I am really into drawing, and I play the drums on Tuesdays. Most of the time I hang out with my cousin, because she lives on the same street and we get along with each other really well. At the moment I am learning Spanish at school, and honestly it is not going very well. I am also reading a book in Spanish this month, which was a bad idea. Actually, I am an only child, so my cousin is basically my sister. That is me. How about you?
Why is it "she lives on the same street" and not "she is living on the same street"?
A Because it is a permanent fact about her, not something happening this month.
B Because "live" can never take the -ing form.
Which one is only true for now, and will probably change?
A "I play the drums on Tuesdays."
B "I am reading a book in Spanish this month."
Why does she start a sentence with "Actually"?
A To add a fact the reader was not expecting.
B To say that everything before it was false.
What does the last line do?
A It closes the conversation politely.
B It gives the turn to the other person, so the conversation continues.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Present simple × present continuous

One tense is for your life. The other is for your week.

Present simple

I play volleyball.
She plays volleyball.
I don't play in a team.
Do you play every week?

Present continuous

I am learning English.
She is learning Spanish.
I am not learning French.
Are you learning it now?

Use the simple for routines, facts and the person you are: every week, on Saturdays, usually, most of the time, never.
Use the continuous for what is happening now or around now, and will change: at the moment, right now, this week, this year, today.
Do not forget the -s in the third person of the simple: she plays, he lives, it works. It sounds like a Z.
In questions the simple needs do or does: What do you do in your spare time? — never What you do...?

Stage 4: Meeting somebody new

Quiz
You play volleyball with your friends every Saturday. What do you say?
A I am playing volleyball every Saturday.
B I play volleyball every Saturday.
You started English classes this year and you are still going. Which one is correct?
A At the moment I'm learning English.
B At the moment I learn English.
You want to ask a new club partner about her free time.
A What you do in your spare time?
B What do you do in your spare time?
You have just said three things about yourself. What do you say next?
A So that's me. How about you?
B So that's me. Goodbye.
In the middle of a sentence you cannot remember a word. What is the best move?
A Stop the sentence and say the word in Portuguese.
B Say "I don't know the word. It's the thing you use to..." and describe it.

Stage 5: Sixty seconds about you

Reflection
Introduce yourself out loud for one minute, as if the club were listening. Who are you and where do you live? What are you into? What are you doing at the moment that you were not doing last year? Finish by giving the turn back with a question. Before you record, check one thing: routines go in the present simple, and things that will change go in the present continuous.
