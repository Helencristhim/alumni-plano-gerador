Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

Your Own Words -- The Conversation That Starts Everything

Saying who you are, what you study and where you have been. Key words: travel, trip, flight, airport, alone, understand, schedule, course. Structure: present simple for facts and routines.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These are the words you need every time somebody asks about your life.

To travel -- to go from one city or country to another
"I travel to the United States with my family."
Trip -- the whole journey, from leaving home to coming back
"My last trip to the United States was in January."
Flight -- the part of a trip that happens on the plane
"The flight from Sao Paulo to Miami takes eight hours."
Airport -- the place where you take a plane
"I always arrive at the airport two hours early."
Alone -- with nobody else
"This time I want to travel alone."
To understand -- to know what a person means when they speak
"I understand English well, but speaking is harder."
Schedule -- the plan of your week, hour by hour
"My schedule is full from Monday to Friday."
Course -- a programme of classes on one subject
"I study Administration and I also do a Fashion course."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Ryan is nineteen and he lives in Denver, in Colorado. He studies Business at a state university. His week is busy: he has classes in the morning, and three afternoons a week he works in a coffee shop near the campus. He is also learning Portuguese, because his grandmother is Brazilian. He understands a lot when people speak slowly, but when he tries to answer, he stops in the middle of the sentence.
Why does the text use "studies", "works" and "stops" with an -s?
A Because the actions happened in the past
B Because the subject is "he" — third person singular
C Because the actions are happening at this exact moment
What does "three afternoons a week he works in a coffee shop" tell you?
A It is a routine — something that repeats
B He is in the coffee shop right now
C He worked there only once

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple

GRAMMAR

Use the present simple for facts and routines — things that are always true, not only today.

I / you / we / theyverb with no changeI travel every year.
he / she / itverb + -sShe studies fashion.
negativedo / does not + verbHe does not work on Sundays.
questiondo / does + subject + verbDo you travel a lot?

Careful 1: after does the verb loses the -s. "Does she works?" is wrong — it is "Does she work?"

Careful 2: one pronoun per sentence. If you start with she, finish with she.

My Life, My Story -- The Trip You Already Took

Telling a finished story in English. Key words: arrive, passport, immigration officer, customs, declare, nervous, on my own, used to. Structure: past simple, affirmative, negative and question.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. All eight live in the same place: the line at passport control.

To arrive -- to get to a place at the end of a journey
"We arrived in Miami at six in the morning."
Passport -- the document that proves who you are in another country
"The officer looked at my passport for a long time."
Immigration officer -- the person who decides if you can enter the country

"I was nervous in the line, but I said nothing."
On my own -- without help from anybody else
"Next time I want to do it on my own."
Used to -- something that was true before, but is not true now
"My father used to answer for me at immigration."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Last January I travelled to Miami with my family. We arrived at six in the morning and the airport was already full. I was nervous in the line, but I said nothing. When it was our turn, the officer asked three questions and my father answered all of them. I did not say a single word. Then we went through customs, and we had nothing to declare. I used to think that was normal. Now I want to do it on my own.
Why does the text use "arrived", "asked" and "answered" with -ed?
A Because they are happening right now
B Because they are finished actions in the past
C Because the subject is he or she
"I did not say a single word." Why is it "say" and not "said"?
A Because the past is already in "did"
B Because the sentence is about the present
C Because "say" is an irregular verb

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Simple

GRAMMAR

Use the past simple for actions that are finished — they happened and they are over.

regular verbsverb + -edWe arrived at six.
irregular verbsa new word, learned one by onego → went, say → said, buy → bought
negativedid not + base verbI did not understand.
questiondid + subject + base verbDid you buy anything?

Careful 1: after did the verb goes back to normal. "I did not understood" is wrong — it is "I did not understand".

Careful 2: the past does not change with he, she or it. I went, she went, they went — the same word.

Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

Checking In, Speaking Up -- The Night the Room Was Not Ready

Checking in, asking for what you want, and telling what went wrong. Key words: reservation, front desk, check-in, check-out, floor, key card, would rather, a piece of cake. Structure: past simple with first, then, after that and finally.
0%
▼

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. Six of them are things you point at; two of them are how you ask for what you want.

Reservation -- a room you booked before you arrived
"I have a reservation under the name Waltrick."
Front desk -- the counter in the hotel where you check in
"The front desk is open twenty-four hours."
Check-in -- the moment when you arrive and get your room
"Check-in starts at three in the afternoon."
Check-out -- the moment when you leave the hotel and pay
"What time is check-out on Sunday?"
Floor -- one level of a building
"My room is on the fifth floor."
Key card -- the card that opens the door of your room
"My key card stopped working after one day."
Would rather -- used to say which of two things you prefer
"I would rather have a room on a higher floor."
A piece of cake -- something that is very easy to do
"The third time, checking in was a piece of cake."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

We arrived at the hotel after midnight. First, I gave the clerk my passport and the confirmation of my reservation. Then he told me that the room was not ready. After that, he offered us a room on the second floor, but I did not want to change — I said I would rather wait for the room we booked. Finally, thirty minutes later, he came to find us at the bar with a new key card. The second time we checked in, it was a piece of cake.
What do "first", "then", "after that" and "finally" do in this text?
A They say when the story happened
B They put the events in order for the listener
C They make the sentences more polite
"I did not want to change." Why is it "want" and not "wanted"?
A Because the past is already in "did"
B Because the sentence is about the present
C Because "want" has no past form

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Telling a Story in Order

GRAMMAR

The tense is the past simple, the same as last lesson. What is new is the words that put the events in order.

firstthe beginningFirst, we arrived at the hotel.
thenthe next thingThen he asked for my passport.
after thatthe thing after the next thingAfter that, he gave me a key card.
finallythe endFinally, we went up to the room.

Careful 1: after did and did not, the verb goes back to normal. "Did you called?" is wrong — it is "Did you call?"

Careful 2: would rather takes the verb with no to. "I would rather to wait" is wrong — it is "I would rather wait".

Lesson 04 -- Pre-class

What Are You Into? -- Your Life Right Now

Talking about your two courses and what you are in the middle of. Key words: trend, collection, fabric, style, brand, launch, semester, deadline. Structure: present continuous against the present simple.
0%
▼

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. Six of them come from your fashion course, two from the university.

Trend -- a style that a lot of people are choosing at the moment
"This trend started on TikTok and reached the shops six months later."
Collection -- a set of pieces a brand puts out together
"The new collection has forty pieces and one colour palette."
Fabric -- the material a piece of clothing is made of
"I am working with a lighter fabric this semester."
Style -- the particular way somebody dresses or designs
"Her style is simple, and that is why people copy it."
Brand -- a company with a name and an identity people recognise
"That brand is small, but everybody in my course knows it."
To launch -- to put a new product on the market for the first time
"They are launching the collection in March."
Semester -- one half of the academic year at a university
"I am in my fourth semester of Administration."
Deadline -- the last day you can hand something in
"I have three deadlines in the same week."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Giovanna studies Administration at university and she is in her fourth semester. She also takes a fashion course at night, and this year she is taking the fabric module. Right now she is working on a project about lighter fabrics, and she has a deadline in two weeks. She does not know yet which of the two areas she wants to follow, and at the moment she is not choosing. She is looking forward to the final project.
Why is it "she studies Administration" but "she is working on a project"?
A Because studying is always true and the project is happening now
B Because the project happened in the past
C Because "work" has no simple form
Why is it "she wants" and not "she is wanting"?
A Because want is a verb we do not use in the continuous
B Because the sentence is about the past
C Because want is an irregular verb

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple x Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

English has two present tenses, and they do different jobs. Portuguese often uses one for both — that is where the mistake comes from.

present simplealways true, routineI study Administration.
present continuousam / is / are + verb-ingThis semester I am taking a fashion course.
markers of the simpleusually, always, every yearBrands launch two collections a year.
markers of the continuousright now, at the moment, this semesterRight now they are working on next winter.

Careful: know, like, love, want, need do not go into the continuous. "I am wanting" is wrong — it is "I want".

And the -s never disappears: she loves it, not she love it.
