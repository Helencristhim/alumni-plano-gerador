Why Pension Money Waits

Why Pension Money Waits

Every year the same number appears in conference slides: the region needs hundreds of billions of dollars of infrastructure, and the world’s pension and sovereign wealth funds are sitting on trillions. The obvious question is why the two do not meet. The answer is rarely the projects. It is the shape of the risk, and the language investors use to describe it.

A pension fund does not chase the highest return. It has a fiduciary duty to the people who will retire on its money, so it looks for a risk-adjusted return that it can defend to its board. A greenfield port in a new market may promise fifteen percent, but if construction, currency and politics can each destroy that number, the fund will choose a brownfield road that pays nine percent from day one. That preference has a side effect: so many funds now want the same safe assets that yield compression has pushed returns down.

This is where structure matters. A sponsor who wants a fund on board rarely changes the project; it changes the capital around it. Developers now have their financial models audited before the first meeting, and many get a development bank to provide credit enhancement, so that the senior loan looks safer. Concessional lending can carry the riskiest early years, and subordinated debt can sit between the bank and the equity. When public and private money are combined on purpose in this way, the result is called blended finance.

The last piece is the one foreign investors ask about first. A toll road earns reais, but a Canadian pension fund reports in dollars. Without a currency hedge, a bad year for the exchange rate can wipe out a good year on the road. Funds are not waiting for better projects. They are waiting for somebody to take the currency and the construction risk off the table — and for the first anchor investor to go in, so that the rest can follow.

Chapter 4: The Code

Who Made It Happen?

The difference between a sentence that names the agent and one that hides it

Grammar Discovery

Three Sentences Name the Agent. One Hides It.

"The fund had its exposure reviewed by an outside adviser."

"The operator got the concession extended for ten years."

"The concession was extended for ten years."

"We had the project structured as blended finance."

The red sentences tell you who caused the action, even when somebody else did the work. The blue one does not. Why would an investor reading the blue sentence want to ask a question?

JobFormExample
You arranged a servicehave + object + past participleWe had the model audited.
You pushed to make it happenget + object + past participleThey got the tariff approved.
Name the expert who did the work... + by + agentWe had the project structured by a local bank.
Stay silent about the agentbe + past participleThe tariff was approved in May.
Something happened to youhave + object + past participleThe fund had its rating downgraded.
Ask or persuade a personhave + person + verb / get + person + to + verbWe had the bank run the numbers. We got the ministry to publish the date.
The trapnever make + object + to beNot We made the contract to be reviewed. Say We had the contract reviewed.

In one line: Have and get tell the room who made it happen; the passive lets you stay silent about it.

Quick Fire

One Question at a Time

1 / 6

A Canadian fund manager asks why a nine percent road can beat a fifteen percent port. Answer in two sentences.

  • What matters is the risk-adjusted return: the road pays from day one, and the port can lose that fifteen percent to construction and currency.
  • Frame it with What matters is, and land on risk-adjusted return.

Say that your team arranged an independent audit of the model before the roadshow.

  • We had the financial model audited by an independent firm before the roadshow.
  • have + object + past participle, never made the model to be audited.

An investor asks who got the tariff approved. Say it was the operator, after a long negotiation.

  • The operator got the tariff approved after nine months of negotiation.
  • get + object + past participle shows effort.

Explain blended finance to a CFO who has never heard the term, in one sentence and without jargon.

  • It means mixing public money that accepts more risk with private money that accepts less, so that the project becomes investable.
  • Define it without using the word finance twice.

A fund in Abu Dhabi asks what protects it if the real falls against the dollar.

  • The term sheet requires a currency hedge, so a bad year for the exchange rate does not erase the return.
  • Name the tool, then say what it prevents.

Say why the second and third funds are waiting for the pension fund to sign.

  • They are waiting for an anchor investor. Once one serious fund goes in, the others follow.
  • Anchor investor, stress on AN-chor.

A double-digit return five years ago; today it struggles to promise nine percent.
2. According to the segment, why have returns come down?
Because so many pension and sovereign wealth funds are bidding for the same operating assets that prices went up. It is yield compression, not traffic.

Offering guarantees, so that a new greenfield project looks more like an old one to a cautious fund.

0:000:00

With what can go wrong, and in what order.

Construction risk first, then currency, then the contract itself.
