GRAMMAR

negative — I / you / we / theydo not + verbWe do not develop new drugs.
negative — he / she / itdoes not + verbShe does not work with children.

One -s per sentence. "Does she publishes?" is wrong — the -s is already inside "does".

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Going to and Will

GRAMMAR

Both are the future. The difference is when you decided. Use going to for a plan you already had before you spoke. Use will (usually I'll) for a decision, an offer or a promise made at the moment of speaking.

plan you already hadam / is / are going to + verbI am going to take part in the Wednesday workshop.
negative planam / is / are not going to + verbWe are not going to stay for the closing dinner.
question about a planam / is / are + subject + going toAre you going to the dinner on Wednesday?
decision, offer or promise nowwill / I'll + verb"I do not have your paper." -- "I'll send it tonight."
fixed programme timepresent simpleThe congress starts on October 26th.

After look forward to comes a noun or an -ING verb: "I am looking forward to meeting you", never "to meet you".

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Comparatives and Superlatives

GRAMMAR

Count the syllables. Short adjectives (one syllable, or two ending in -y) take -er and the -est. Longer adjectives take more and the most. Never both at once.

short adjectiveadjective + -er thanHer explanation was clearer than mine.
short adjective, topthe + adjective + -estIt was the smallest room in the building.
long adjectivemore + adjective + thanThis design is more complex than the other one.
long adjective, topthe most + adjectiveIt was the most useful session of the week.
irregulargood / bad / farbetter, the best -- worse, the worst -- further, the furthest

"More bigger" is wrong: English chooses one of the two forms, never both. And the second half of a comparison is than, not that.
