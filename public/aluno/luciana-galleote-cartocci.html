Say That Again -- Following a Meeting in English

Nobody understands everything in a meeting. Key words: to catch, to follow, to clarify, to rephrase, to slow down, to sum up, to double-check, a key point, background noise, the gist. Structure: polite requests and indirect questions.
0%
▼

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To catch -- to hear and understand something that someone says
"Sorry, I didn’t catch the last number."
To follow -- to understand a conversation or an explanation while it continues
"It is hard to follow a call when four people speak at the same time."
To clarify -- to make something clear or easier to understand
"Could you clarify the second point, please?"
To rephrase -- to say the same idea again with different words
"Let me rephrase that: we need the proposal by Friday."
To slow down -- to speak or do something at a lower speed
"Would you mind slowing down a little?"
To sum up -- to say the most important ideas again in a few words, at the end
"To sum up, we agree on the price and the date."
To double-check -- to check something again to be sure it is correct
"Let me double-check the date: is it the fifteenth?"
A key point -- one of the most important ideas in a conversation or a text
"The key point is the delivery time."
Background noise -- sounds around you that make it difficult to hear
"There is a lot of background noise on your line."
The gist -- the general meaning of something, without the details
"I did not understand every word, but I got the gist."

GRAMMAR

Short, polite questions keep you in the meeting. Watch the verb after would you mind, and the word order inside could you tell me.

Never: Could you tell me what does it mean · Would you mind to repeat · Could you repeat again · I didn’t understood

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect for Trends

GRAMMAR

Use have / has + the third form for a change that started in the past and is still true. With a finished date (in 2023, last year), use the past simple.

JobFormExample
A change that continues until nowhave / has + third formDemand has grown a lot.
From a point in timesince + a datesince 2023, since January
For a periodfor / over the last + timefor three years, over the last two years
Before now, maybe earlier than expectedalready / so farWe have already reached our capacity.
Finished timepast simple, not present perfectIn 2023 we built two data centers.
The questionHow has / Have you...?How has the market changed?

Never: Demand has grown in 2023 · We work with hospitals since 2005 · Power consumption have increased · We have already reach our capacity
