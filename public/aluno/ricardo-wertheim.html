Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

Who Is Ricardo? -- The First Conversation

Introducing yourself in English: your name, your city, your work and your travels. Key words: destination, architecture, heritage, cuisine, journey, landmark, opinion, to wander. Structure: the present simple for facts and routines, and the -s of he, she and it.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Destination -- the place you are travelling to
"Dubrovnik is our first destination in August."
Architecture -- the art and style of designing buildings
"I work with architecture, so I look at every facade."
Heritage -- buildings and traditions from the past that a country protects
"The old town is a UNESCO World Heritage Site."
Cuisine -- the style of cooking of a country or region
"I want the local cuisine, not a tourist menu."
Journey -- the act of travelling from one place to another
"The journey from Zagreb to Ljubljana takes two hours."
Landmark -- a building or place that is easy to recognise in a city
"The cathedral is the main landmark of the old town."
Opinion -- what you think about something
"In my opinion, Ljubljana is greener than Prague."
To wander -- to walk slowly with no fixed plan
"I love to wander through narrow streets with no map."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

My name is Ricardo Wertheim and I live in Sao Paulo. I am an architect, so I look at buildings everywhere I go. I travel two or three times a year, and my wife comes with me. She speaks English very well, so she usually orders the food and asks for directions.

This August we go to Croatia and Slovenia. Dubrovnik has a famous old town, and the city walls are more than six hundred years old. I don't like crowded tourist restaurants, so I always ask a local person where to eat. My wife doesn't mind a long walk, and I love to wander through narrow streets with no map at all.

1. Why does the text say "my wife speaks English" and not "my wife speak"?
A Because "my wife" is she, and he / she / it takes the -s.
B Because the action is happening right now.
C Because it is a plural subject.
2. The text says "I don't like crowded tourist restaurants" but "my wife doesn't mind a long walk". Why the difference?
A Because one is a routine and the other is happening now.
B Because with he / she / it the -s moves to "does", giving "doesn't".
C Because "mind" is an irregular verb.
3. "I travel two or three times a year." What does this sentence tell us?
A That he is travelling at this exact moment.
B That he travelled in the past and stopped.
C That this is his routine, something generally true about him.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Present Simple

GRAMMAR

The tense you use to say who you are and what you do.

FormUseExample
Affirmative
I / you / we / they + verb		Facts about you and things you do regularly.I live in Sao Paulo.
Third person
he / she / it + verb + s		Same idea, one other person or thing. Watch out: this -s is the most forgotten letter in English.She speaks English.
Dubrovnik has an old town.
Negative
don't / doesn't + verb		The -s moves to does, so the main verb goes back to its plain form.I don't like tourist menus.
She doesn't mind walking.
Question
Do / Does + person + verb		Same rule again: does carries the -s.Do you travel often?
Does she live here?
Frequency wordsalways, usually, often, sometimes, never go before a normal verb (I usually travel), but after the verb to be (I am usually early).

Age is different in English: you are 73 years old, you do not have 73 years.

Lesson 02 -- Pre-class

The Trip That Changed Everything -- Talking About Past Travel

Telling the story of a trip you took years ago. Key words: stunning, breathtaking, medieval, cobblestone, square, harbor, lively, unforgettable. Structure: the past simple with regular and irregular verbs, and time expressions like last year, in 2019 and a few years ago.
0%
▼

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Stunning -- extremely beautiful, in a way that surprises you
"The light on the old stone was stunning."
Breathtaking -- so beautiful that you stop and stare for a moment
"We turned the corner and the view was breathtaking."
Medieval -- from the Middle Ages, between about the year 500 and 1500
"The medieval walls still go around the whole old town."
Cobblestone -- a small round stone used to build old streets
"I walked for hours on those cobblestone streets."
Square -- an open public space in a city, with buildings all around
"We had dinner in a small square behind the market."
Harbor -- a protected place by the sea where boats stay
"The fishing boats came back to the harbor at six."
Lively -- full of people, noise and energy
"The old town was quiet in the morning and lively at night."
Unforgettable -- so special that you remember it for the rest of your life
"That evening by the water was unforgettable."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Ten years ago my wife and I spent three days in Split. We arrived late at night, and the taxi left us in front of a small door in a wall. Behind that door we found a whole city.

In the morning we walked on cobblestone streets between medieval houses. At eleven we sat down near the harbor and watched the fishing boats. An old man told us where to eat, and we took his advice. That evening we ate grilled fish, and we didn't look at a map once. Did we plan any of it? No. That is exactly why it was unforgettable.

1. The text says "we walked" but "we ate". Why does only one of them end in -ed?
A Because "ate" happened before "walked".
B Because "walk" is regular and takes -ed, while "eat" is irregular and becomes a new word.
C Because "ate" is a plural form.
2. The text says "we didn't look at a map" and not "we didn't looked". Why?
A Because the action was not important.
B Because "did" already carries the past, so the other verb goes back to its plain form.
C Because "look" has no past form in English.
3. "Ten years ago my wife and I spent three days in Split." What does "ten years ago" tell you?
A That the trip is still happening now.
B That the trip will happen in ten years.
C That the trip started and finished in the past, so every verb goes into the past simple.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Past Simple

GRAMMAR

The tense that carries every travel story you will ever tell.

FormRuleExample
Regular verbs
verb + ed		One ending for every person: I, you, he, she, we, they.We arrived late.
I walked for hours.
Irregular verbs
a NEW word		No -ed at all. These seven carry most travel stories, so learn them as words, not as rules.go → went · see → saw · eat → ate · meet → met · take → took · feel → felt · find → found
Negative
didn't + plain verb		The past moves to did, so the main verb loses it. Never "didn't went".I didn't take a photo.
We didn't eat at the hotel.
Question
Did + person + plain verb		Same rule again, and did comes before the person.Did you see the harbor?
Where did you stay?
Time expressionslast year · in 2019 · a few years ago · when I was in Lisbon · on my last trip. They open or close the sentence: Last year we went to Portugal. / We went to Portugal last year.

One time expression is enough for the whole story: once you say ten years ago, every verb after it stays in the past.

Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

What Were You Doing? -- Setting the Scene in Travel Stories

Giving your travel stories a background. Key words: stroll, stumble upon, glance at, notice, crowd, fountain, terrace, atmosphere. Structure: the past continuous for what was already happening, in contrast with the past simple, plus the linkers when and while.
0%
▼

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To stroll -- to walk slowly and calmly, because you enjoy it
"We strolled along the harbor after dinner."
To stumble upon -- to find something good by accident, without looking for it
"We stumbled upon a tiny courtyard with six tables."
To glance at -- to look at something very quickly, for one second
"She glanced at the menu and put it down again."
To notice -- to see or understand something that you did not see before
"I noticed a small door that I had never seen before."
Crowd -- a large group of people together in one place
"A crowd appeared around the fountain in two minutes."
Fountain -- a stone structure in a square where water comes out
"The fountain was the only source of fresh water in the town."
Terrace -- a flat outdoor area of a bar or house, with tables
"Every terrace on the street was full at nine o'clock."
Atmosphere -- the feeling that a place gives you
"The atmosphere in that square was completely different at night."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

It was almost nine, and I was strolling along the harbor with no plan at all. The sun was setting behind the old walls, and people were sitting outside on every terrace.

I was looking for a quiet table when I heard music. I followed it, and out of nowhere a crowd appeared around the fountain. While the old women were dancing, I noticed that nobody was taking photographs. At that point I realized I was the only tourist in the square, and the atmosphere changed completely for me.

1. The text says "I was looking for a table when I heard music." Which action started first?
A The looking. It was already happening, and the music cut into it.
B The music. It started before the looking.
C Both started at exactly the same second.
2. Why does the text say "people were sitting" and not "people sat"?
A Because "sat" does not exist in English.
B Because it is the background of the scene, not one finished action.
C Because there was more than one person.
3. The text uses "while the old women were dancing" and "when I heard music". What decides which one you use?
A "While" is formal and "when" is informal.
B They are identical and you can always swap them.
C "While" introduces the long background; "when" introduces the short event.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Past Continuous

GRAMMAR

The camera behind the action: what was already happening when the story began.

FormRuleExample
Affirmative
was / were + verb -ing		Two words only: was for I, he, she, it — were for you, we, they.I was strolling along the harbor.
People were sitting outside.
Background vs eventThe -ing form is the long thing already happening; the past simple is the short thing that cuts into it.I was looking for a table when I noticed a door.
Negative
wasn't / weren't + -ing		The not attaches to was or were. The verb keeps its -ing.Nobody was taking photographs.
We weren't looking at a map.
Question
Was / Were + person + -ing		Was or were comes before the person, exactly like a question with to be.What were you doing when the music started?
Was she working that night?
when vs whilewhen goes with the short event, while with the long background. Both sentences mean the same thing: I was strolling when I heard music. / While I was strolling, I heard music.

Some verbs almost never take -ing, because they are not actions you can watch: know, want, like, need, believe. You say "I knew the square", never "I was knowing the square".
