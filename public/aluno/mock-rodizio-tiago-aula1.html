Brainstorming

Read it once, quickly

Four hundred words nobody read

Sofia coordinates production at a metal parts factory in Joinville. Every Friday afternoon, for two years, she wrote a status report for her American client. It was thorough. It had numbers, dates, the name of every supplier, and it was usually four hundred words long. She was proud of it, and she never missed one.

In March the client called her, upset. A shipment had slipped by ten days and he had found out from his own warehouse, not from her. Sofia opened her sent folder and found the report from three weeks earlier. The delay was there. It was in the fourth paragraph, in the middle of a long sentence about tooling.

So the information had been sent, and the information had not arrived. Those are two different things, and she had been measuring the wrong one.

She changed the format that same week. Not four hundred words on Friday, but sixty seconds on Monday, on the phone, always in the same order: where we are, what is stuck, what I need from you, when it closes. If nothing was stuck, she said so, and the call took twenty seconds.

Nobody ever thanked her for the long reports. Four months into the short calls, the client asked her to explain the format to his other two suppliers.

Diving Deep

Sixty seconds, five moves

AnalyseThese five lines are Sofia's Monday call. Read them again.
1We're on track for the 12th.
2We're running about four days behind.
3The blocker is the tooling supplier.
4What I need from you is a decision on the second batch.
5By Friday we'll have the sign-off.
Analyse and answer
  1. 1Sofia says all five in under a minute. What order does she put them in, and why that order?
  2. 2Two of the five are about the problem. Three are about what happens next. Which are which?
  3. 3Line 2 says about four days, not four days. Why the about?
  4. 4If you removed line 4, what would the client be left with?
+Reveal answer key
Do not hand over this order as a rule before he finds it. Ask, wait, then open.
  1. Order: where we are (1) → the gap (2) → the cause (3) → what I need from you (4) → when it closes (5). Status first, then the ask — never the other way around.
  2. Problem: 2 and 3. What happens next: 1, 4 and 5.
  3. About buys a small margin and still commits to a number. Four days exactly invites a fight about the fourth day; no number at all sounds like hiding.
  4. News with no ask — the client hears a problem and has nothing to do with it. Line 4 is what turns an update into a conversation.

Practice

Same fact, fewer words

This is how the email says it. Say it in one line, on the phone. Use the expression given.
  1. 1I would like to inform you that the production of the second batch has unfortunately been affected by a delay in the delivery of the tooling.(The blocker is) 
  2. 2Everything is proceeding in accordance with the schedule we agreed upon in our last meeting.(We're on track for) 
  3. 3We are currently approximately one week later than the date originally planned.(We're running about) 
  4. 4It would be very helpful if you could kindly let us know your decision regarding the packaging as soon as possible.(What I need from you is) 
  5. 5The documentation should be finalized and ready for your review by the end of this week.(By Friday we'll have) 
+Reveal answer key
Accept justified variations: if it fits in one breath and says the same thing, it counts.
  1. The blocker is the tooling — it arrived late.
  2. We're on track for the date we agreed.
  3. We're running about a week behind.
  4. What I need from you is a decision on the packaging.
  5. By Friday we'll have the documents ready for you.
Your Turn

Four updates, sixty seconds each

Case 1 — the good week

Nothing is stuck. Both batches left on time and the client has heard nothing from you since Monday. You still have to make the call.

Case 2 — the supplier

The tooling supplier moved the lead time from three weeks to five. Production slips by nine days. There is a second supplier, more expensive, who could do it in two.

Case 3 — your own mistake

The shipment is four days late because your team sent it to the old address. Nobody outside knows why yet. The client is going to ask.

Case 4 — waiting on him

You cannot start the second batch without the client's sign-off on the packaging. You asked twice. It has been eleven days.

Give the update
  1. 1Take Case 1. Give the whole update out loud, without stopping. Sixty seconds, even though nothing is wrong.
  2. 2Now Case 2. Where you are, what is stuck, what you need from him, when it closes — in that order.
  3. 3Case 3 is the only one where the blocker is you. What is your first sentence — and what do you NOT say first?
  4. 4In Case 4 the person who is late is the client. How do you say that without making it an accusation?
  5. 5Which of the four would you rather send as a message than say on a call? Defend it.
