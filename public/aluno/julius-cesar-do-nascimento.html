Thirty-Five Years, One Table -- I’ve Worked / I’ve Been Working

Key words: headquarters, a subsidiary, a plant, to oversee, to be in charge of, a track record, a supplier, revenue, market share, to expand, the board, to step into a role. Structure: present perfect simple x present perfect continuous.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Headquarters -- the main office of a company, where the top managers work
"The group’s headquarters is in Tokyo, but my office is in Sao Paulo."
A subsidiary -- a company that is owned and controlled by a larger company
"We are the Brazilian subsidiary of a Japanese group."
A plant -- a large factory, especially one that makes industrial products
"We have two plants in Brazil, and I visit one of them twice a week."

"Our plant has a strong track record on safety."
A supplier -- a company that provides parts or materials to another company
"We are a supplier to some of the biggest car makers in Brazil."
Revenue -- the money that a company receives from its sales
"Our revenue has grown every year since 2022."
Market share -- the part of all the sales in a market that one company has
"We have been gaining market share in heavy industry."
To expand -- to become bigger, or to start working in new places
"The group has been expanding into new countries."
The board -- the group of people who make the most important decisions in a company

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect Simple x Continuous

GRAMMAR

Use have / has + past participle for a result or a total that you can count. Use have / has been + -ing for an activity that started in the past and is still going on. State verbs (know, be, own, belong) stay in the simple form.

JobFormExample
Result, total, how manyhave / has + past participle“We have opened two plants.”
Activity still in progresshave / has been + verb-ing“I’ve been traveling a lot lately.”
Questions about a totalHow many / How much + have you + past participle?“How many countries have you visited?”
Questions about durationHow long + have you been + verb-ing?“How long have you been working there?”
Signal wordsalready, ever, never, so far x lately, recently, all week, for / since“I’ve never lived abroad, but I’ve been studying English for years.”
The trapstate verbs (know, be, own, belong) do not take -ingNot “I’ve been knowing him for years.” Say I’ve known him for years.

Never: I work here since 1990 · I’ve been knowing him... · How long are you the president? · This year we’ve been opening two plants.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive in Different Tenses

GRAMMAR

The passive is always a form of be + the past participle. Only the tense of be changes: is poured, was designed, has been replaced, is being tested, will be needed. Use it when the process or the result matters more than the person who does it.

JobFormExample
Present simpleam / is / are + past participleMolten metal is poured into the mold.
Past simplewas / were + past participleThis mold was designed in Japan.
Present perfecthas / have been + past participleCar bodies have been built this way for a century.
Present continuousam / is / are being + past participleThe process is being improved.
Future and modalswill be / can be / must be + past participleCast iron will be needed for years.
The trapbe in the right tense + the past participle, never the -ing or the base formNot “The mold has been damage.” Say The mold has been damaged.

Never: The part was casted · The alloy is testing now · It has been install · It will shipped

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Indirect Questions

GRAMMAR

Start with a polite opening: Could you tell me... / Do you know... / Can you explain... / I was wondering... Then use a normal sentence: question word (or if / whether) + subject + verb. Do not use do, does or did after the opening.

JobFormExample
Polite openingsCould you tell me... / Do you know... / Can you explain... / I was wondering...“Could you tell me when it arrives?”
Wh- question insideopening + wh- word + subject + verb“Could you tell me why the shipment is late?”
Yes / no question insideopening + if / whether + subject + verb“Do you know whether it is delayed?”
No do / does / didthe auxiliary disappears“Can you explain what you mean?”
Clarifying phrasesSorry, I didn’t catch... / Could you rephrase that? / Just to confirm...“Sorry, I didn’t catch the last part.”
The trapdo not keep the question word order after the openingNot “Could you tell me where is the plant?” Say Could you tell me where the plant is?

Never: Could you tell me where is the plant? · Do you know when does it arrive? · Can you explain what do you mean? · I was wondering could you...

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Narrative Tenses

GRAMMAR

Past simple for the main events. Past continuous (was / were + -ing) for the background. Past perfect (had + past participle) for something that happened before. Past perfect continuous (had been + -ing) for how long something had been going on.

JobFormExample
Past simplethe main events, in orderWe signed the agreement.
Past continuouswas / were + verb-ing: the background, an action in progressWhile they were explaining the numbers, I...
Past perfecthad + past participle: an action before another past momentI had never eaten anything so fresh.
Past perfect continuoushad been + verb-ing: how long, up to a past momentI had been traveling for thirty hours.
Linkerswhen, while, by the time, before, afterBy the time we left, we had stopped talking about numbers.
The trapuse the past perfect only for the earlier action, not for every past actionNot “I had arrived and I had gone to the hotel.” Say I arrived and went to the hotel.

Never: When I arrived, the meeting already started · I never had eaten... · I was traveling for thirty hours when I arrived · While we were eat...
