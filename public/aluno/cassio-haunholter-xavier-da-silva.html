Vocabulary

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The kick-off meeting for the new industrial block starts at two. Fifteen people are on the line, and the coordinator asks for a short round. The first engineer says: “I manage the piping team here in Helsinki, and at the moment we are checking the equipment list.”

B No. He manages the piping team; checking the equipment list is what he is doing at the moment.

A Because it is the structure of his team, and it does not change from week to week.
B Because it happened once, in the past.
C Because reporting is a temporary task this week.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “At the moment I work on the layout of block 2.”
B “At the moment I am working on the layout of block 2.”
C “At the moment I am work on the layout of block 2.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple vs Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

The simple form says who you are and what is always true. The -ing form says what you are in the middle of right now.

JobFormExample
Your role, always truepresent simple: I / we + verbI manage the projects department.
A routine, how oftenpresent simple + every week / usually / alwaysI report to the regional director every week.
Something temporary, happening nowam / is / are + verb-ingAt the moment I am working with the Houston office.
A project in progress, not finishedam / is / are + verb-ing + this month / right nowWe are mobilizing a structural team this month.
The two questionsWhat do you do? / What are you working on?What do you do here? — What are you working on at the moment?
The trapverbs of state stay simpleNot I am knowing that client. Say I know that client well.

Never: I am knowing that client · Every week I am joining the call · At the moment I work on block 2 · What are you doing in the company? (to ask about the job, say What do you do?)

GRAMMAR

Four verbs for four different levels of pressure. The question that separates them: who decided this, and what happens if you do not do it?

JobFormExample

An obligation that comes from outside youhave to / has to + verbWe have to send the minutes within 24 hours.
Something necessary for the work to moveneed to + verbI need to know the equipment load before Friday.
Advice, good practiceshould + verbYou should copy the piping lead in.

The trapmust not is a prohibition, not an absence of obligationYou must not send drawings outside the distribution list = it is forbidden. You do not have to send them = it is optional.

GRAMMAR

The small word that adds the detail: who for people, which for things, that for both, whose for possession.

JobFormExample

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The design review opens with two options for the main frame. Option A is reinforced concrete; option B is structural steel. Everybody in the room already knows that steel is considerably faster to erect. The interesting part is everything else.

The load from the process equipment is far higher than usual, and the client may add more in three years. At an eighteen-metre span, the two options are nearly equal on the foundation, and steel is only slightly cheaper on the frame itself.

So the team recommends concrete, and says the trade-off out loud: four weeks on site, in exchange for future capacity. On the external walls the choice is easier. Precast panels are by far the quickest option, and their durability in a humid area is significantly better than masonry.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Comparatives, Superlatives and Degree

GRAMMAR

The comparison says which one. The degree word says by how much, and that is the part an engineer is listening for.

JobFormExample
A small differenceslightly / marginally / a little + comparativeConcrete is slightly heavier on the foundations.
A large differenceconsiderably / significantly / far / much + comparativeSteel is considerably faster to erect.
The clear winnerby far + the + superlativePrecast is by far the quickest option.
Two things that are equalas + adjective + asPanels are as durable as masonry here.
A difference in the other directionnot as + adjective + as / nowhere near asMasonry is nowhere near as durable as concrete.
The trapnever more + -er, and never the most + -estNot more cheaper. Say cheaper. Not the most fastest. Say the fastest.

Never: more faster · the most quickest · not so durable than · more heavy that steel
