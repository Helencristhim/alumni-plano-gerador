Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

Who Is Joyce, in Sixty Seconds? -- Your Career in Two Tenses

How to introduce yourself to people on a global call who have never met you, and tell twenty years of work without mixing up the tenses. Key words: a track record, a turning point, to oversee, to be responsible for, to report to, hands-on, to move up, a stakeholder, a first impression, my background. Structure: the past simple for the finished parts of a career, and have plus the third form for the parts that are still going on.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A track record -- the list of results a person has already delivered in their career
"Her track record in audits is the reason the global team trusts her numbers."
A turning point -- the moment when a career or a situation changes direction
"Becoming a manager was the turning point of my career."
To oversee -- to watch and direct the work that other people do
"I oversee the monthly close for three companies."
To be responsible for -- to be the person who has to make sure that something gets done
"I am responsible for the tax team in Brazil."
To report to -- to have somebody as your manager, the person you answer to
"I report to the regional finance director in Zurich."
Hands-on -- doing the work yourself, not only telling other people to do it
"I am a hands-on manager, and I still review the big reconciliations myself."
To move up -- to get a more senior position in a company
"I moved up from analyst to manager in six years."
A stakeholder -- a person or a group that is affected by a decision and cares about it
"Before we change the process, we need to talk to every stakeholder."
A first impression -- the opinion people form about you in the first minutes
"On a global call, your first impression is your first sentence."
My background -- the education and the experience a person brings to a job
"My background is in audit, so I look at every number twice."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Priya is a finance manager in Mumbai, and last month she joined a call with a new director. This is how she introduced herself. “I started my career at an audit firm in 2008. I spent five years there, and it was hard work, but it gave me my background. In 2013 I moved up to a controller role at a bank.” Every verb in that part is in the past simple, because every one of those things is finished and has a date.

Then her story comes to the present, and the verbs change. “I have been with this company since 2019. I have led the tax team for three years, and I report to the regional director in London.” She is still with the company and still leads the team, so she uses have plus the third form, with since for the start and for for the length.

Her last sentence is the one the director remembered: “Right now I am responsible for the monthly close in four countries, and I am quite hands-on.” Finished part, open part, and today. That order made a good first impression in under a minute.

Why does Priya say "I started my career in 2008" and not "I have started my career in 2008"?
A Because 2008 was a difficult year.
B Because the start of her career is finished and has a date.
C Because she does not work in audit anymore.
Why does she say "I have been with this company since 2019"?
A Because she is still with the company today.
B Because the sentence is a question.
C Because she left the company in 2019.
What is the difference between "since 2019" and "for three years"?
A They are the same, and you can use either one.
B "Since" is only for companies.
C "Since" gives the starting point, and "for" gives the length of the time.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Simple versus Have plus the Third Form

GRAMMAR

A finished part of your career, with a date: the past form. A part that is still going on: have plus the third form.

JobFormExample
Finished, with a datepast formI joined the company in 2019.
Still true todayhave / has + third formI have worked here for six years.
The starting pointsince + a dateI have led the team since 2022.
The length of the timefor + a periodI have led the team for three years.
What you do nowpresent simpleI oversee the monthly close.
The -ed sound/t/, /d/ or /id/worked /t/ · joined /d/ · started /id/

Never: I have joined the company in 2019 · I work here since 2019 · responsible of · I worked there since three years

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

A new director has just asked you to introduce yourself. Put your answer in a clear order: today first, then the story, then the point.

?"I have been with this company since 2019, and I report to the regional director."
?"Hi, I am the finance manager for Brazil, and I am responsible for the monthly close."
?"So if you have a question about Brazil, I am the person to call."
?"I started my career in audit, at a Big Four firm."
?"Right now my main topic is the tax reform in Brazil."
?"I moved up to a management role about ten years ago."
