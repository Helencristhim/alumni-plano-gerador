The Year-End Room

In the minutes, nobody does anything: the budget is approved, the report was submitted. At the party, three hours later, the same room needs the opposite grammar.

Who Actually Decides?

Now the new one. When a credit limit is raised in your company, who actually decides — and would that name ever appear in a document?

Read for the Argument

The Person Disappears From the Sentence

Who Decided That?

The minutes of last Tuesday's board meeting run to six pages, and in all six pages almost nobody does anything. The budget is approved. The report was submitted. A new head of region has been hired. Actions happen, dates are recorded, and the person who decided disappears from the sentence. That is not laziness and it is not evasion. In a document that will be read in four countries by people who have never met, what matters is the decision and the date, not who happened to be holding the pen. English has a whole grammar for exactly that, and corporate life runs on it.

The same construction has a second, less innocent use, and it is worth being able to hear it. Mistakes were made removes the person from the sentence just as efficiently as the budget was approved does, and in that case the disappearance is the point. This is why the passive has a bad reputation with people who teach writing, and why the advice to avoid it is too simple. The question is never whether the sentence hides the actor. It is whether hiding the actor is honest in that particular sentence. A credit limit was approved on the fifteenth is a fact. A decision was taken to release the product is often somebody hoping you will not ask who took it.

And then there is the year-end party, which is the same building and the exact opposite of the same language. Legally the event is treated as an extension of the workplace, so it is organised as carefully as a meeting. Socially it is the one evening in the year when the grammar has to change back. Nobody wants to hear that the file was reviewed and the limit was raised. They want to hear that you read it on a Sunday, that you called the dealer yourself, and that you were wrong about him. On the page the actor disappears and the decision survives. At the table it is the other way round, and an analyst who cannot make that switch spends the whole evening sounding like a document.

Chapter 4: The Code

Where the Person Went

The grammar of the minutes

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"The report is submitted every quarter."

"The revised limits were approved on the fifteenth."

"All accounts are reviewed by the compliance team."

"I read the file on a Sunday and I called the dealer myself."

In three of these, nobody does anything: the red verbs all have two parts and the same shape. In one of them the person comes back, in blue. And the last sentence is a different animal entirely.

JobFormExample
Something that is done regularlyis / are + past participleThe report is submitted every quarter.
Something that was done oncewas / were + past participleThe limits were approved on the fifteenth.
When the actor matters after all... by + the person or the teamAll accounts are reviewed by the compliance team.
Why you would use itthe action matters, the actor does not, or is obviousA new head of region will be hired before March.
Still happening nowis / are being + past participleTerms are being negotiated with the two largest distributors.
The other roomat a dinner, put the person backI read the file on a Sunday and I called him myself.
The trapthe verb be is not optionalNot the report submitted every quarter. Say is submitted.

In one line: on the page the actor disappears and the decision survives. At the table it is the other way round.

extract · credit and collections items

Item 4.4Southern portfolio delegated to the regional analyst
Item 4.5Terms with two distributors are still being negotiated
Item 9Year-end event confirmed. Regional teams to be invited
Note on style: minutes are written this way on purpose. They are read months later, in other countries, by people who were not in the room, and the record has to survive without them. What the record cannot do is replace the ten minutes somebody spends asking the analyst a question, which is why item 9 exists at all.
1. Rewrite item 4.3 so that the person appears, and say when you would use each version.
For example: the board approved the revised limits without objection. The passive is used in the record; the active is used when you say out loud who decided.
2. Which item was delegated, and to whom? Which item is still open, and why?
Item 4.4 was delegated to the regional analyst. Item 4.5 is still open because terms with two distributors are still being negotiated.
3. What was proposed about the credit policy in item 4.2?
No change was proposed. The policy was reviewed and noted.
4. According to the note on style, who reads minutes, and when?
People who were not in the room, months later, in other countries.
