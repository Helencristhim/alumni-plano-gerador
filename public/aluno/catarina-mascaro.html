Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the chat. Then answer the questions.

Bia: Hi! Do you live near the school?
Sam: Yes, ten minutes on foot. What do you do after class?
Bia: I play volleyball on Mondays. How often do you play basketball?
Sam: Twice a week. My sister plays too — she is very competitive.
Bia: Ha! Where does she train?
Sam: At the club near the park. She is there every day. She is so energetic.
How often does Sam play basketball?
A Every day.
B Twice a week.
Why does the chat use "does" and not "do" in "Where does she train?"
A Because the question is about "she".
B Because the question starts with "Where".
Who asks more questions in this chat?
A Bia.
B Sam.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Questions in the present — you always need do

Use do with I / you / we / they. Use does with he / she / it.

Do

Do you play sports?
What do you watch?
How often do they train?

Does

Does she play sports?
What does he watch?
Where does Bia live?

+ I play volleyball on Mondays.
− I do not play volleyball on Fridays. (short: don't)
? Does she play volleyball? (not "Does she plays")

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. Then answer the questions.

My week is always the same. I wake up at seven and my brother wakes up at six, because he trains before school. After school I usually hang out with two friends, or I scroll on my phone until dinner. At night my sister and I chat about everything. She spends more time on her phone than I do, but she does not like it when I say that. On weekends I get bored fast, so I put on music. I am really into one band right now. Honestly, I am hooked on them.
Why does the brother wake up at six?
A Because he trains before school.
B Because school starts at six.
Why is it "wakes up" for the brother but "wake up" for the writer?
A Because the brother wakes up earlier.
B Because "he" gets an S, and "I" does not.
What happens to the writer on weekends?
A She gets bored fast, so she puts on music.
B She hangs out with two friends.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Routines — and the S that only he / she / it get

Use the present simple for things that happen again and again.

No S

I wake up at seven.
You hang out a lot.
They chat every night.

With S

He wakes up at six.
She hangs out a lot.
Bia chats every night.

+ She scrolls on her phone at night.
− She does not scroll at night. (the S moved into does)
? Does she scroll at night? (same thing — not "Does she scrolls")

When it happens: every day · in the morning · at night · on weekends · after school · twice a week

Lesson 03 — Pre-class

That Was Fun

Stage 1.1: Story Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Watch the past form next to each one.

Meet — to see a friend somewhere, or to see someone for the first time (past: met)
"I met my cousins at the park on Saturday."
Feel — to have something inside you: happy, tired, nervous (past: felt)
"I felt so tired on Sunday night."
Visit — to go and see a person or a place (past: visited)
"We visited my grandmother on Sunday."
Call — to talk to someone on the phone (past: called)
"She called me three times on Friday."
Stay home — to not go out at all (past: stayed)
"I stayed home all Saturday and I loved it."
Miss out — to lose the chance to do something fun
"I missed out on the party. Everyone went."
Have a great time — to enjoy something a lot (past: had)
"We had a great time at the concert."
A piece of cake — very easy — it is not really about cake
"The maths test? A piece of cake."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. Then answer the questions.

Last weekend was strange. On Friday I stayed home and watched two films. On Saturday my cousin called me at nine in the morning, so I woke up earlier than I wanted. We met at the mall at two and we had a great time. Then we visited my aunt, and I felt so tired that I fell asleep on her sofa. On Sunday everyone went to the beach and I missed out, because I did not wake up in time. The homework, at least, was a piece of cake.
Why did the writer wake up early on Saturday?
A Because her cousin called at nine.
B Because they went to the beach.
Why is it "did not wake up" and not "did not woke up"?
A Because "wake up" is irregular.
B Because "did" is already the past, so the verb goes back to normal.
What happened on Sunday?
A She went to the beach with everyone.
B She missed out, because she woke up too late.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Past simple — it happened, and it is over

Most verbs add -ed. A few become a new word. The form is the same for everybody — no S here.

Regular

stay → stayed
call → called
visit → visited
watch → watched

Irregular

meet → met
feel → felt
have → had
go → went

+ I met my friends on Saturday.
− I did not meet them on Sunday. (after did, the verb goes back to normal)
? Did you meet them? (not "Did you met")

When: yesterday · last weekend · last night · two days ago · on Saturday

The -ed has three sounds: watched (T) · called (D) · visited (a whole extra syllable).

Lesson 04 — Pre-class

Right Now

Eight words for the present moment (right now, at the moment, these days, still, not anymore, get better, get worse, once in a while) and the difference between "I watch" and "I’m watching".
0%
▼

Stage 1.1: Words for Now

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Right now — at this exact moment
"I am talking to my teacher right now."
At the moment — in this part of your life — not forever
"I am not doing any sport at the moment."
These days — in this period — different from before
"These days I am listening to a lot of pop."
Still — it started before and it has not stopped
"I am still watching that series."
Not anymore — it happened before, but it stopped
"I do not play volleyball anymore."
Get better — to improve, little by little
"My English is getting better."
Get worse — to become worse, little by little
"The traffic is getting worse every year."
Once in a while — not often, but it does happen sometimes
"I do not watch football, but once in a while I do."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. Then answer the questions.

I usually study in the afternoon, but this week I am studying at night, because we have three tests. Right now I am sitting in the kitchen with my books everywhere. My brother is watching something loud in the next room and he is not helping. I still have two chapters to read. These days I am sleeping about six hours, which is bad, but my marks are getting better, so I am not complaining. I do not go out much anymore — well, once in a while, on Saturdays.
When does she usually study?
A In the afternoon — but not this week.
B At night, every week.
Why is it "I am studying at night" and not "I study at night"?
A Because night is a special time.
B Because it is only this week, not in general.
What is getting better?
A Her sleep.
B Her marks.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Two presents, two jobs

In general → present simple. Now, today, this month → am / is / are + verb-ing.

In general

I study every day.
She works on Mondays.
We do not go out much.

Now / these days

I am studying right now.
She is working at the moment.
We are not going out these days.

+ I am watching a series right now.
− I am not watching anything at the moment.
? Are you watching that series? (not "Do you watching")

In real speech nobody says it long: I’m watching · she’s working · they’re not sleeping.
