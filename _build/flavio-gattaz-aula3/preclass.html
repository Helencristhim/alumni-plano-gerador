Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

A Day You Would Not Believe -- The Working Day

Walk me through a typical day is a standard interview question, and it is never really about the day. Key words: a commute, to juggle, back-to-back, to fit something in, to put out fires, a window, to wrap up, to run behind, downtime, to touch base, to be swamped, to block out time. Structure: tend to, will for habit, used to and be used to.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A commute -- the daily journey between home and work, counted as part of the day
"My commute is four minutes, which is the only luxury in my whole week."
To juggle -- to keep several tasks going at the same time without dropping any of them
"I juggle four sites, a supplier crisis and a school pick-up, most days."
Back-to-back -- describing meetings with no gap: one starts the moment the last one ends
"Tuesday is back-to-back from eight thirty until lunch."
To fit something in -- to find room in a full day for something that was not planned
"He asked for twenty minutes and I fitted him in before the site visit."
To put out fires -- to spend the day solving urgent problems instead of the work you planned
"I did not manage anything today. I put out fires from nine to six."
A window -- a free period inside a full schedule, big enough to put something in
"I have a window at four. If it does not happen then, it does not happen today."
To wrap up -- to finish something and close it properly, instead of leaving it half done
"Let us wrap up here and pick up the rest on Friday."
To run behind -- to be later than planned, with everything after it running late as well
"By ten o'clock I am running behind, and the rest of the day follows."
Downtime -- time when work stops and nothing moves forward
"Two hours of downtime on a site costs more than the whole meeting that caused it."
To touch base -- to make brief contact with somebody just to check where things stand
"I touch base with each site manager before eight. Two minutes each, no agenda."
To be swamped -- to have far more work than you can handle at this moment
"I am swamped this week. Can we do it on Monday instead?"
To block out time -- to reserve part of the day so that nothing else can be booked into it
"I block out the first hour. If I do not block it out, the day takes it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Ask a director what a normal day looks like and you get two answers. The first is the plan. He tends to be at the office by half past eight, he touches base with each site manager before nine, and the morning is back-to-back until lunch. Tend to is a soft verb on purpose: it describes what usually happens, not a rule that never breaks.

The second answer is the day itself. A delivery fails at ten to nine, he is running behind by ten, and the rest of the morning goes on putting out fires. There is a window at three that he blocked out on Monday, and it is gone by two. His site manager will call at seven every single morning — not tomorrow, not next week, but always, which is what will does when it is not talking about the future.

Then there is the part that changed. He used to be on site every day, back when he was an engineer, and he does not do that any more: used to plus a verb is a habit that stopped. Today he is used to the office, and he is used to working with the phone on the table — be used to plus a noun or an -ing form, and it means the opposite thing. Not a habit he left behind: a habit that stopped feeling strange.

1. "He tends to be at the office by half past eight." What does "tend to" add?
A That it is an obligation he cannot avoid.
B That it is what usually happens, without claiming it never breaks.
C That it will happen tomorrow.
2. "His site manager will call at seven every single morning." What is "will" doing here?
A Making a promise about tomorrow.
B Describing typical behavior, something the person always does.
C Asking for permission.
3. According to the text, what is the difference between "used to be" and "is used to"?
A The first is a habit he left behind; the second is a habit that stopped feeling strange.
B There is no difference; both describe the past.
C The first is formal and the second is informal.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Habit and Tendency

GRAMMAR

Four ways to talk about a habit, and only one of them is the present simple.

JobFormExample
A tendency, not a ruletend to + base formI tend to spend the morning on site.
Typical behaviorwill + base form (never for one event)He will call me at seven, every time.
A past habit that stoppedused to + base formI used to design buildings.
Accustomed to somethingbe used to + noun or verb-ingI am used to the noise / to working alone.
Becoming accustomedget used to + noun or verb-ingI got used to the six o'clock alarm.
A plain routineusually / always + present simpleI usually go to the site on Tuesdays.
Negative and questiondid not use to / Did you use to...?I did not use to work in the office.

Never: I am used to wake up at six · I use to go there on Tuesdays · he tends to calls me · I used to design buildings since 2011 · I am used to work with noise.

Order

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

An interviewer asks you to describe your normal morning. You answer:
A "I use to arrive at eight thirty."
B "I tend to be at the office by half past eight, and the morning is back-to-back until lunch."
C "I am used to arrive at eight thirty."
You want to say that you designed buildings in the past and stopped. You say:
A "I used to design buildings, but I do not any more."
B "I am used to design buildings before."
C "I use to design buildings in the past."
You want to say that the noise on site no longer bothers you. You say:
A "I used to the noise."
B "I am used to the noise. I stopped hearing it years ago."
C "I am used to hear the noise."
Somebody asks for twenty minutes on a day that is already full. You answer:
A "Today is impossible. I have no time in my life."
B "I am swamped today, but I have a window at four. Can I fit you in then?"
C "I am very busy. Please call another day."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

An interviewer says: walk me through a typical day, and then tell me about the last day that went completely wrong. In two minutes, give the shape of the normal day, what you protect in it, what you used to do and stopped, and what you have got used to.
