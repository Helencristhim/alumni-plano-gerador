NPR Life Kit -- "Feeling short on time? Do a time audit"

A short episode on writing down where the week actually goes, instead of where you believe it goes. The register is everyday spoken American English, spoken at normal speed, and every word from this lesson about a full calendar turns up in it.

Tip: listen once without the transcript and write down every expression they use for having too much on. Then open the transcript and check how many you caught.

Stanford GSB -- Last Lecture Series: "How to Win Without Crushing Your Soul", Graham Weaver

A lecture to executive students about the working life you end up with if you never decide what to protect in it. Weaver runs a private equity firm and teaches at Stanford, so the English is exactly the register you will be sitting in from October.

Tip: this is a full lecture in front of an American audience. Watch fifteen minutes at a time, without subtitles, and stop only when you lose the thread.

Talk
TED -- Laura Vanderkam, "How to gain control of your free time" (12 min)

She studied the diaries of very busy people for a living, and her conclusion is the same one the operations director reaches in this lesson: the week is not full, it is unprotected. Twelve minutes, slow and very clear delivery.

Tip: after the talk, block out one real window in your own week and tell the class next time whether it survived.
