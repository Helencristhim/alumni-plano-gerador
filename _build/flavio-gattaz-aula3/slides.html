Chapter 3: The Code

What You Do, What You Did, What Stopped Being Strange

Four ways to talk about a habit, and only one of them is the present simple

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"I tend to spend the morning on site."

"He will call me at seven, every single time."

"I used to design buildings. Now I run the company."

"I am used to the noise. I stopped hearing it years ago."

Three of the red parts contain the word to, and in two of them what follows is a verb, while in the third it is a noun. That difference is the whole rule, and it is the one Brazilian speakers get wrong most often.

JobFormExample
A tendency, not a ruletend to + base formI tend to spend the morning on site.
Typical behaviorwill + base form (never for one event)He will call me at seven, every time.
A past habit that stoppedused to + base formI used to design buildings.
Accustomed to somethingbe used to + noun or verb-ingI am used to the noise / to working alone.
Becoming accustomedget used to + noun or verb-ingI got used to the six o’clock alarm.
A plain routineusually / always + present simpleI usually go to the site on Tuesdays.
The trapbe used to never takes the base formNot I am used to wake up at six.

In one line: used to + verb is a habit you left behind; be used to + -ing is a habit that stopped feeling strange.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Day, at Full Speed

One conversation, three recordings, one calendar

Dialogue

Coffee Break, Day One

D
Diane. Operations, Chicago. Please tell me your morning is worse than mine, because mine started at five.
F
Six, but with two children to get to school first. My commute is four minutes, though, so I am not going to complain.
D
Four minutes. I hate you a little bit. So what does the rest of it look like?
F
I tend to be at the office by half past eight, and the morning is back-to-back until lunch. On a bad day I put out fires until three and get to the real work after everybody has gone home.
D
That was my entire life until about two years ago. What changed for you?
F
Honestly, not enough yet. I used to be on site every day, and I do miss it. I am used to the office now, but I would not say I chose it.
D
Here is the only thing that worked for me. I block out two hours on Wednesday afternoon and I defend them like a client meeting. Everybody thinks it is free time.
F
And nobody argues with a client meeting. I might steal that. What happens in those two hours — is it thinking, or is it the work you never fit in?
Comprehension

About Diane

1. How does Diane open the conversation, and what does she say about her own morning?
She gives her name, her area and her city, and says her morning started at five, asking him to tell her his was worse.
2. What does she say about the kind of day he describes?
That it was her entire life until about two years ago, and she asks him what changed for him.
3. What is the one thing that worked for her, and how does she protect it?
She blocks out two hours on Wednesday afternoon and defends them like a client meeting, because nobody argues with a client meeting.
Listening 1 · A Director

The Hour Before Anybody Arrives

A construction director describing the day he plans and the day he gets. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he do with the first hour, and why?
He blocks it out, before anybody arrives. It is the only hour that is his, and if he does not block it out the day takes it.
2. What has usually happened by ten o'clock, and what does the morning become?
Something has gone wrong on one of the sites, so he is already running behind and spends the morning putting out fires instead of the work he planned.
3. What did he use to do, and what is he used to now?
He used to be on site every day as an engineer. He is used to the office now, and he says he still misses the noise.
Listening 2 · Operations

How I Know It Is Going to Be That Kind of Day

An operations director on the first sign, and the rule she made for herself. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the first sign that the day is going to be a bad one?
Two calls before eight. One call is normal; two before eight means somebody has been awake with the problem since six.
2. What does she say about a day of back-to-back meetings?
That it is a day with no room in it, so the first thing that goes wrong pushes everything after it and she runs behind until the evening.
3. What rule did she make for herself, and how do other people read it?
She keeps one window in the afternoon empty on purpose. Everybody thinks it is free time, and it is the only reason the week does not fall over.
Listening 3 · Business Radio

Who Actually Owns Your Week?

A business radio report on what a study found inside executive calendars. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the study find about the executive week, and who had booked it?
About seventy per cent of the week was in meetings, and most of it had been booked by somebody else.
2. What surprised the researchers about the people who felt in control of their time?
They did not have fewer meetings. They had blocked out the same two hours every week for years, and they defended them.
3. What does the reporter say about the word downtime?
That it makes it sound as though nothing is happening, when it is the only part of the week where anything actually gets decided.
Real Document

A Tuesday, On Paper

TUESDAY — F. GATTAZ
as it was planned on Monday evening
07:30school run
08:30touch base with the four site managers
09:00 – 11:30back-to-back: supplier, bank, safety review
12:00lunch at home
13:30site visit — the tower
15:00 – 17:00blocked out — do not book
What actually happened: a delivery failed at 08:50, the morning ran behind by forty minutes, the site visit was cut to twenty, and the blocked-out afternoon was used to put out fires. The only thing that survived the day exactly as planned was lunch.
1. Which part of the morning has no gap in it, and what is the word for that?
Nine to half past eleven: supplier, bank and safety review, one after the other. That is back-to-back.
2. One line on this calendar was not asked for by anybody. Which one, and what happened to it?
The three to five block, marked do not book. It was used to put out fires.
3. According to the note at the bottom, what started the whole day running behind?
A delivery failed at ten to nine, and the morning ran behind by forty minutes from there.
