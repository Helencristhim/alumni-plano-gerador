What Went Wrong at the DC -- Reporting a Problem

The words of a bad night in operations: distribution center, pallet, forklift, to break down, downtime, backlog, root cause, to escalate. Structure: past continuous for the background and past simple for the event. Mission: report a real incident in ninety seconds, in the right order.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Distribution center -- the large building where goods are sorted before they go to the stores
"The distribution center loads trucks for forty-two stores every night."
Pallet -- the wooden base that goods are stacked on so a machine can move them
"Nine pallets were waiting at the door when the line stopped."
Forklift -- the small vehicle that lifts and moves heavy loads inside a building
"A forklift hit the sensor arm at ten past two."
To break down -- to stop working, usually a machine or a vehicle
"The conveyor broke down in the middle of the night shift."
Downtime -- the period when work stops because something is not working
"Four hours of downtime cost us nine trucks."
Backlog -- the work that piled up because it was not done on time
"We cleared the backlog by two in the afternoon."
Root cause -- the real reason behind a problem, not the first thing you see
"The forklift was the accident. The root cause was the missing guard."
To escalate -- to pass a problem to somebody more senior when you cannot solve it
"The supervisor escalated it to me at three in the morning."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At ten past two the night shift was loading the trucks for the north stores. Two people were working at the end of the aisle and nobody was watching the corner. While they were loading, a forklift turned too fast and hit the sensor arm on conveyor three. The line stopped immediately.

The supervisor tried to restart it three times. While she was trying, nine trucks arrived at the dock and waited. At twenty past three she escalated it. The engineer inspected the machine at five, and while he was checking the November photos he found the real cause: somebody removed a guard during the layout change and nobody put it back. I knew the answer before the report arrived, but I waited for it anyway.

1. Why is it while they were loading, a forklift turned and not while they loaded?
A Because loading is the long action that was already in progress. The forklift interrupts it.
B Because load is an irregular verb and needs the -ing form.
C Because the sentence starts with while, and while always takes -ing.
2. Why does the text say I knew the answer and not I was knowing?
A Because know is a state, not an action, so it does not take the -ing form.
B Because the sentence is in the first person.
C Because knowing something is always short.
3. What does the continuous add that the simple alone would not?
A It makes the sentence longer and more polite.
B It shows what was already happening, so the reader understands the cause and not just the order of events.
C It shows that the action never finished.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Continuous and Past Simple

GRAMMAR

Two tenses, two jobs. One paints the scene and the other moves the story. A good incident report needs both, in that order.

FormUse it forExample
was / were + -ingThe long action, already in progress when something happened.The team was loading trucks.
Past simpleThe short action that interrupts, and everything that follows it.A forklift hit the arm. The line stopped.
while + continuousMarks the background. While the team was loading, the conveyor stopped.
when + simpleMarks the moment. The supervisor was trying to restart it when I called.
Two continuousTwo long actions at the same time: While I was calling Marco, the team was clearing the aisle.
State verbsknow, want, need, understand, believe never take -ing: I knew the cause, never I was knowing.

Before you speak, ask which action was long. That one takes was doing. Everything else takes the simple past, and the order you say them in does not change that.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Something went wrong last night. Choose the best thing to say.

Your director asks what happened. The clearest first sentence is:
A A forklift hit a sensor and everything stopped.
B At two in the morning the team was loading trucks when the conveyor stopped.
C While the conveyor stopped, the team was loading trucks.
She asks what the supervisor did before calling you. You say:
A She was tried to restart the line three times.
B She tried to restart the line three times, and then she escalated it.
C She is trying to restart the line three times.
She asks when you understood the real cause. You say:
A I was knowing it at six, before the report.
B I knew it at six, before the report arrived.
C I was known it at six.
The carrier asks why nine trucks waited. The most useful answer is:
A It was not our fault. The forklift driver did it.
B While we were clearing the line, the trucks arrived. We moved them to the second dock and cleared the backlog by two.
C The trucks were waiting because they were waiting outside.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think about one real problem at work in the last six months. Record about ninety seconds. Start with the background: what was already happening, and who was doing what. Then say what went wrong, in the past simple, and how long it lasted. Then separate the accident from the root cause. Finish with one thing that changed afterwards. Use at least four words from this lesson, and at least two sentences with while. Do not stop to correct yourself.
