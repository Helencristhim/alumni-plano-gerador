Chapter 3: The Background and the Event

What Was Happening When It Happened

Past continuous for the scene, past simple for the moment

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"At two in the morning the night shift was loading trucks."

"While they were loading, the conveyor stopped."

"A forklift hit the sensor arm."

"Nobody was watching that corner."

FormUse it forExample
Past continuous: was / were + -ingThe long action. The background. What was already in progress.The team was loading trucks.
Past simpleThe short action that interrupts, and the sequence after it.The conveyor stopped. A forklift hit the arm.
while and whenwhile goes with the long action, when goes with the short one.While they were loading, the conveyor stopped.
Two long actionsBoth in the continuous, when they happened at the same time.While I was calling Marco, the team was clearing the aisle.
Verbs with no -ingknow, want, need, believe, understand describe a state, not an action. I knew the cause at six, never I was knowing.

The continuous holds the door open and the simple walks through it. A report with only the simple is a list; a report with only the continuous never gets to the point.

Grammar Practice

Background or Event?

Say the full sentence, then click to compare

1. While the team ______ (load) the trucks, the conveyor ______ (stop).
"While the team was loading the trucks, the conveyor stopped."
2. The supervisor ______ (try) to restart the line when I ______ (call) her.
"The supervisor was trying to restart the line when I called her."
3. Nobody ______ (watch) that corner at two in the morning.
"Nobody was watching that corner at two in the morning."
4. I ______ (know) the root cause before the engineer ______ (send) the report.
"I knew the root cause before the engineer sent the report."
Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"While I was call Marco, the line stopped."
"While I was calling Marco, the line stopped."
"While the conveyor stopped, we were loading."
"While we were loading, the conveyor stopped."
"I was knowing the root cause at six."
"I knew the root cause at six."

Ask yourself which action was long. That one takes the -ing, and the other one does not, no matter which one you say first.

Chapter 4: Finding the Real Cause

It Was Never the Driver

The engineer walks you through what he found

Dialogue

The Engineer and the Sensor

F
Marco, thank you for coming so early. What were you doing when the call came in?
M
I was driving to another site, so I turned around. I arrived at five and started the inspection.
F
And what did you find? Karen told me a forklift hit the sensor.
M
The forklift hit the arm, not the sensor. The sensor was working perfectly when I tested it.
F
So the machine did not break down by itself.
M
No. The arm was standing there with no guard around it. In the November photos the guard was in place.
F
I see. Could you tell me who removed it during the layout change?
M
I cannot. I only know that nobody put it back, and nobody checked. That is your root cause.
F
Four hours of downtime for a missing guard. What do you need from me?
M
A confirmation this morning. If you confirm, I install two new guards on Friday, while the line is off anyway.
F
You have it. Send me the report today and I will escalate the process part myself.
M
Good. And Felipe, the driver was not the problem. Please say that in the meeting.
Did You Catch It?

About Marco

1. What was Marco doing when the call came in?
He was driving to another site, so he turned around.
2. What exactly did the forklift hit, and what was still working?
It hit the arm. The sensor itself was working perfectly.
3. What does Marco need from Felipe, and by when?
A confirmation this morning, so he can install two guards on Friday.
