Lesson 14 -- Pre-class

Saying It Better

How to react to a place, good or bad, with force. Key words: impressive, disappointing, charming, touristy, dull, exhausting, a pleasant surprise, live up to. Structure: so and such -- the wine was so good, it was such a charming village.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Impressive -- so good or so big that you admire it
"The cathedral in Zagreb is really impressive."
Disappointing -- not as good as you hoped
"The famous restaurant was a little disappointing."
Charming -- pleasant and attractive in a small, gentle way
"Piran is a charming little town by the sea."
Touristy -- full of tourists, and made more for them than for the people who live there
"The main square is nice, but very touristy in August."
Dull -- boring, with nothing interesting happening
"The first half of the game was really dull."
Exhausting -- making you very, very tired
"Six hours on the bus was exhausting."
A pleasant surprise -- something good that you did not expect
"The small market was a pleasant surprise."
To live up to -- to be as good as people say or as you hoped
"The view lived up to the photos."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last summer Marta and her husband spent a week in Croatia. In Dubrovnik the old town was so crowded that they could not walk on the main street, and the restaurants were so expensive that they ate sandwiches on the city walls. It was a beautiful place, but for them it did not live up to the photos.

Then they took a ferry to a small island. It was such a quiet place! There were no cars, and the people were such friendly neighbors that one woman gave them figs from her garden. The island was so charming that they canceled their last night in Dubrovnik and stayed there.

1. The text says "It was such a quiet place". Why does it use such a and not so?
A Because the sentence is about the past, and so is only used to talk about the present.
B Because a noun comes after it: place.
C Because the island was small.
2. Look at "the restaurants were so expensive that they ate sandwiches". What does the part after that tell you?
A The name of the restaurant where they had dinner on the last night.
B The time when they ate the sandwiches on the city walls with the other tourists.
C The result: what they did because the restaurants were very expensive.
3. Why does the text say "such friendly neighbors", with no a?
A Because neighbors is plural.
B Because the neighbors were not really friendly.
C Because friendly is an adjective that never takes the word a in front of it, in any sentence.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- So and Such

GRAMMAR

Two small words that turn a fact into a feeling.

FormRuleExample
so + adjectiveno noun after itThe wine was so good.
such a / an + adjective + nounone thing (singular)It was such a charming village.
such + adjective + nounplural or uncountable: no asuch friendly people · such good wine
so / such ... thatthat + the resultIt was so expensive that we ate sandwiches.
so / such or very?very gives information. so and such carry your feeling: The view was so beautiful!
The trapNever so a beautiful view, never such beautiful with no noun. One question decides it: is there a noun?

A verdict is not "it was nice". It is "it was such a charming village that we stayed an extra day": the feeling, and what it made you do.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are giving your verdict on places and meals. Choose the best thing to say.

A friend asks about a small town you loved. You say:
A "It was so a charming town, really, one of the best in the region!"
B "It was such a charming town!"
C "It was such charming."
The wine at a family cellar was excellent. You say:
A "The wine was such good that we drank it all afternoon in the garden."
B "It was so good wine."
C "The wine was so good!"
A famous restaurant was worse than the reviews. You say:
A "It did not live up to the reviews."
B "I did not live up to the restaurant, because the reviews online were so good."
C "It was a pleasant surprise."
The people at a market were very kind. You say:
A "They were such a kind people, and everybody at the market smiled at us."
B "They were so kind people."
C "They were such kind people."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think of a place in the world that surprised you, for good or for bad. Take about ninety seconds. Say what you expected, what you found, and your verdict. Use so and such at least two times each, one sentence with so or such ... that, and four words from this lesson. Do not stop to correct yourself.
