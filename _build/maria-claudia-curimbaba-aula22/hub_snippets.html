Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Nudge -- a light, friendly reminder about something pending
"I will send David a nudge about the signature."
Chase up -- to contact someone repeatedly to get something that is late
"Could you chase up the quote from the supplier?"
Bottleneck -- the single point in a process that slows everything else down
"Legal review is the bottleneck, not the budget."
Slip -- to be delayed, so a date moves later than planned
"If the quote does not arrive today, the date will slip."
Backlog -- the pile of work that has not been done yet
"Procurement has a three-week backlog after the holiday."
Escalate -- to take a problem to someone with more authority
"If nothing moves by Friday, I will escalate it to the board."
Sign off -- to give the final, formal approval to something
"The board will sign off on the budget on Thursday."
Loop in -- to include someone in a conversation from now on
"I am going to loop in counsel before we reply."
Touch base -- to make brief contact with someone to stay updated
"Let us touch base on Wednesday before the call."
Outstanding -- still not done or not paid; still open
"Two action items are still outstanding from the last meeting."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Two weeks after the board meeting, two action items are still outstanding. The Houston budget is closed: the board signed off on it on Thursday. The supplier quote has not arrived, because procurement is carrying a three-week backlog after the holiday, so Maria Claudia decides there and then: "I will chase it up this afternoon." The contract review is the real bottleneck -- legal has not moved for two weeks and everything downstream is waiting. She has already sent one nudge, and the plan agreed on Monday still stands: "We are going to escalate it if it has not moved by Friday." She has told counsel the exact date, so that nothing is a surprise. Two things are already in the diary, so they take the present continuous: "I am meeting counsel at nine on Thursday", and "finance and I are touching base on Wednesday." On the insurance renewal, which has not started, they are going to loop in counsel before they reply. And looking at that backlog, she is honest with the board: "The date is going to slip."

1. Why does she say "I will chase it up this afternoon" and not "I am going to chase it up"?
A Because she decides it at that moment, while she is speaking.
B Because the decision was taken at the meeting two weeks ago.
C Because it is already in her diary with a time.
2. Why is "I am meeting counsel at nine on Thursday" in the present continuous?
A Because it is happening right now.
B Because it is already arranged: there is a person and a time.
C Because she is not sure whether it will happen.
3. What does "outstanding" mean in this text?
A Excellent, of very high quality.
B Still open, still not done.
C Cancelled by the board.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Will, Going To & Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

A follow-up email uses all three futures in the same paragraph, and the choice is not about time -- it is about when the decision was taken. WILL = you decide now, while you are speaking (also a promise, an offer, or an opinion about the future). GOING TO = the plan already existed before you spoke, or the evidence is in front of you right now. PRESENT CONTINUOUS = it is already arranged, with a person and a time.

FormUseExample
will + base verbdecided now; promise or offer"I will chase it up this afternoon."
be going to + base verbthe plan already existed"We are going to escalate it on Friday."
be going to (prediction)the evidence is on the table"Look at the backlog -- the date is going to slip."
present continuousalready arranged: person + time"I am meeting counsel at nine on Thursday."
will (opinion)a guess about the future, no evidence"I think they will accept."

Key point: after will and after going to the verb is in the base form -- "I will meeting" → "I am meeting", and "we are going to escalating" → "we are going to escalate". And sign off takes on: "the board signed off on the budget".

Stage 2: The Follow-up in Order

Order

Listen first, then put the steps of following up on a late action in the correct order.

?You chase it up and find out what the real bottleneck is behind the delay.
?You escalate to the person above, loop in whoever needs to see it, and update the tracker.
?You write the action item with a verb, an owner and a date, so nobody can read it two ways.
?You warn the owner and name the exact date on which you are going to escalate.
?You send a friendly nudge a few days later, because most things are simply sitting in a queue.
