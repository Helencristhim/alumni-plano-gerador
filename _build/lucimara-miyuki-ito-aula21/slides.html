Chapter 3: The Future in Progress

This Time Tomorrow

will be flying · will be boarding · will you be checking...?

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"This time tomorrow, I will be flying over the Atlantic."

"We will be boarding in about ten minutes."

"Will you be checking any bags today?"

"At nine a.m., I will be walking into the trade fair."

What do the orange words have in common? Is each action finished, or in progress at a moment in the future?

FormUseExample
will be + verb-ingan action in progress at a moment in the futureAt 2 a.m., I'll be flying.
won't be + verb-ingthe negativeI won't be sleeping on the plane.
Will you be + verb-ing...?a polite question about someone's plansWill you be checking any bags?
We will be + verb-inga planned event, in announcementsWe will be landing soon.

Use a time marker: this time tomorrow, at nine a.m., on Thursday afternoon. In speech: I'll be, you'll be, we'll be.

Chapter 4: The Check-in Counter

Before the Gate

Dialogue

At the Check-in Counter

D
Good evening. Where will you be flying today?
L
To Lyon, with a connection in Paris. It is a long-haul flight, so I came early.
D
Good idea. Will you be checking any bags?
L
Just this one. Is it within my baggage allowance?
D
It is two kilos over the limit. That is an excess baggage fee of sixty dollars.
L
Oh, no. I will move my shoes to my carry-on. Is it OK now?
D
Perfect. You will be boarding at gate twelve at nine forty. After security, you can wait in the lounge.
L
Great. And will I be going through passport control in Paris or in Lyon?
D
In Paris. And be quick there: your connection is only ninety minutes.
