Lesson 21 -- Pre-class

Navigating Airports and Flights

Get ready for a long-haul trip with a connection: the check-in counter, the gate, and the flight, with the future continuous ("I will be flying", "Will you be checking any bags?"). Key words: long-haul flight, security checkpoint, baggage allowance, excess baggage fee, passport control, lounge, duty-free, cabin crew, turbulence, final call.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Long-haul flight -- a very long flight, usually between continents
"Sao Paulo to Paris is a long-haul flight of about eleven hours."
Security checkpoint -- the place where officers check you and your bags before the gates
"The line at the security checkpoint was very long this morning."
Baggage allowance -- the weight or number of bags you can take for free
"My baggage allowance is one bag of twenty-three kilos."
Excess baggage fee -- the money you pay when your bag is too heavy
"The excess baggage fee is more expensive at the airport."
Passport control -- the place where an officer checks your passport when you enter a country
"You will be going through passport control in Paris."
Lounge -- a quiet, comfortable room at the airport for some passengers
"I answered my emails in the lounge before the flight."
Duty-free -- shops at the airport where you pay no local tax
"She bought perfume at the duty-free shop."
Cabin crew -- the people who take care of passengers on the plane
"The cabin crew will be serving dinner after takeoff."
Turbulence -- sudden movement of the plane because of the air
"There was some turbulence over the Atlantic."
Final call -- the last announcement before the gate closes
"This is the final call for the flight to Paris."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Lucimara is flying to a trade fair in Lyon in November. It is a long-haul flight with a connection in Paris, so she is planning every hour. She writes in her notebook: "At ten fifty p.m., the plane will be leaving Sao Paulo. At two a.m., I will be flying over the Atlantic, and I won't be sleeping much, because I want to read my notes for the fair."

She also remembers her last trip. At the check-in counter, the agent asked her: "Will you be checking any bags today?" Her bag was over her baggage allowance, and she paid an excess baggage fee. This time, she is traveling light. "At two p.m. on November 13, I will be waiting for my connection in Paris," she writes. "And on Friday morning, I will be walking into the fair."

1. What will Lucimara be doing at two a.m.?
A She will be flying over the Atlantic.
B She will be sleeping in the lounge in Paris before her connection.
C She will be checking her bag at the counter.
2. Why won't she be sleeping much on the plane?
A Because the cabin crew will be serving dinner all night long on the flight.
B Because she wants to read her notes for the fair.
C Because there will be turbulence over the Atlantic for the whole trip.
3. Which question did the agent ask?
A "Will you checking any bags today, madam?"
B "Will you be check any bags today, madam?"
C "Will you be checking any bags today?"

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Future Continuous

GRAMMAR

How to talk about an action in progress at a moment in the future.

FormUseExample
will be + verb-ingan action in progress at a moment in the futureAt 2 a.m., I will be flying over the Atlantic.
won't be + verb-ingthe negativeI won't be sleeping on the plane.
Will you be + verb-ing...?a polite question about someone's plansWill you be checking any bags today?
We will be + verb-inga planned event, in announcementsWe will be boarding in ten minutes.
RuleUse will be + the verb with -ing. Add a time marker: "this time tomorrow", "at nine a.m.", "on Friday morning". In speech, use contractions: I'll be, you'll be, we'll be. Do not forget "be": "I will flying" is wrong.
