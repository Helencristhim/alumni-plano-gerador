Lesson 21 — Pre-class

The Lobby Cake Sale

About ten minutes. Eight words from a cake sale — batch, frosting, tray, rise, leftovers, flyer, lobby, pre-order — and how to talk about what was necessary in the past: had to, didn't need to, needn't have.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud.

Batch -- a group of cakes or cookies you bake at the same time
"The first batch didn't rise, so we had to start again."
Frosting -- the sweet, soft cream on top of a cake
"I made two bowls of frosting, and we only needed one."
Tray -- a flat thing you use to carry food
"I carried the cupcakes down on a big tray."
Rise -- to get taller in the oven
"If the cake doesn't rise, it stays flat."
Leftovers -- the food that nobody ate or bought
"There were no leftovers, so I didn't need to bring any home."
Flyer -- a small paper that tells people about an event
"We had to put a flyer on every floor of the building."
Lobby -- the big room at the entrance of a building
"We set up a little table in the lobby."
Pre-order -- to ask for something before it is ready
"Twenty neighbors made a pre-order, so we knew how many to bake."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read Sophia's message to her cousin. The words in bold say what was necessary, and what was not.

The lobby cake sale — Yesterday was a crazy day! First, I had to put a flyer on every floor of the building. Twenty neighbors made a pre-order, so I didn't need to guess how many cupcakes to bake.

The first batch didn't rise, so my mom and I had to start again. Our neighbor gave us eggs, so we didn't have to go to the supermarket.

At four o'clock everything was sold, and there were no leftovers. But I needn't have made two bowls of frosting. Half of it is still in the fridge!

"We didn't have to go to the supermarket." Did they go?
A No, it wasn't necessary.
B Yes, they went twice, because they needed more eggs for the second batch.
C Yes, and it was necessary.
"I needn't have made two bowls of frosting." What happened?
A She didn't make any frosting, because her mom bought it at the supermarket.
B She made two bowls, but one was enough.
C She made one bowl.
Which sentence is correct?
A Yesterday I must put a flyer on every floor of the building.
B We didn't had to buy eggs.
C We had to start again.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Was it necessary?

  • had to + verb = it was necessary, and you did it: We had to start again.
  • didn't have to / didn't need to + verb = it was not necessary (and you probably didn't do it): I didn't need to buy any eggs.
  • needn't have + past participle = you did it, but it was not necessary: I needn't have made so much frosting.

Watch out

  • must has no past form: yesterday I had to, never "I must". And after didn't, the base form: didn't have to, never didn't had to.

Remember: your life up to now is still have: Have you ever sold anything? Yes, I have.
