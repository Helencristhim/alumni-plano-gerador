Chapter 3: The Code

I Oversee, or I Am Overseeing?

The job, and the thing you are doing right now

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I oversee the commercial operation in two regions."

"This quarter we are rolling out a new coverage plan."

"I report to the Commercial Director in Sao Paulo."

"At the moment I am covering the south myself."

Two sentences describe the job. Two describe right now. What changes in the verb?

FormWhen you use itExample
present simpleThe job. Things that are true every week, with or without you thinking about them.I oversee two regions. I report to the Director.
am / is / are + verb-ingA project or a temporary situation. It started, and it will finish.We are rolling out a new plan this quarter.
Markers that pull the simpleevery week · usually · normally · twice a month · always · in general
Markers that pull the -ingat the moment · currently · this quarter · right now · this year · these days
Verbs that stay simpleState verbs never take -ing: know, have, need, understand, believe, own.I know the market. Never I am knowing.
Two trapsNever I am working here since 2019 — since takes I have worked / I have been working. And actually does not mean atualmente: for the present moment use currently.

The Structure

Three Sentences, Forty Seconds

"Tell us about yourself" is the question you prepare once and then use for the rest of your career.

1 · The scope

"I am the Commercial Manager for the South and Southeast of Brazil, and I am based in Sao Paulo."

2 · The people

"I oversee seven direct reports in the field, and I report to the Commercial Director here."

3 · The one thing right now

"At the moment we are rolling out a new coverage plan for our largest accounts."

Scope → people → the one thing right now. Never a list of fourteen tasks.

Chapter 4: Meeting Claire

Meet Claire

Global Commercial Director · the first call with the country teams

Dialogue

The First Introduction

C
Renato, good morning. We have not met. Give me your area in a minute, and please do not read me a list.
R
Good morning, Claire. I am the Commercial Manager for the South and Southeast of Brazil, and I am based in Sao Paulo.
C
Good. And the people? I do not need the headcount of the whole country, only yours.
R
Seven direct reports, all of them in the field. I oversee the two regions and I report to the Commercial Director here in Brazil.
C
And what do you actually decide? Where does your scope stop?
R
I decide how we cover the territory and which accounts we protect. Pricing is outside my scope. We sell botulinum toxin, and our stakeholders are the doctors who prescribe it.
C
Thank you for saying that in plain words. Half this call has no idea what we sell in your market. One last thing: what are you changing this year?
R
At the moment we are rolling out a new coverage plan for the largest accounts. It is cross-functional — medical and marketing are in it with us.
C
That is the first introduction today that I will remember. Send me one page on the coverage plan and put me on the next review.
Comprehension

About Claire

1. What does she ask him not to do at the start?
Read her a list. She wants the area in about a minute.
2. Which number does she say she does not need?
The headcount of the whole country — only the people who report to him.
3. Why does she thank him?
Because he said what the market sells in plain words — half the call did not know.
4. What does she ask him for at the end?
One page on the coverage plan, and a place on the next review.
Listening

What a Global Director Actually Asks

She meets a new country manager every month. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is her first question to a new country manager?
Not how many people work for you — what do you actually decide.
2. Why does she say a job title tells her nothing?
Because in one company the same title signs off on pricing, and in another it cannot approve a lunch.
3. What three things does she want, and how long should it take?
The scope of the job, who reports to you and who you report to, and the one thing you are working on that will still matter in a year — about forty seconds.
4. What does she say about apologising for your English?
Do not do it. She is not a native speaker either, and nobody has ever remembered an accent.
Listening 2

The List That Nobody Remembered

A manager who got it wrong the first time. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What did he prepare the first time, and how long was it?
A list of fourteen items, everything he does in a week — about four minutes.
2. Why did he think it had gone well, and why was he wrong?
Nobody asked a question at the end. A colleague told him a list is the easiest thing in the world to forget.
3. What were the three sentences he used the second time?
What his area covers, who is in it, and what they are changing this year.
4. What advice does he wish somebody had given him earlier?
Say the name of your market out loud, early, in plain words — most of the room has no idea what your business sells.
Chapter 5: Practice

Your Area

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "I am reporting to the Commercial Director."
"I report to the Commercial Director." (it is the job, not a project)
2. "Actually I oversee two regions."
"Currently I oversee two regions." (actually = in fact, not now)
3. "My team have seven persons."
"My team has seven people." (team takes a singular verb here, and the plural of person is people)
4. "This quarter we roll out a new coverage plan."
"This quarter we are rolling out a new coverage plan." (it started and it will finish)
