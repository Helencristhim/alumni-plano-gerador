Lesson 13 -- Explaining Why

Conference Talk
"The mystery of chronic pain" -- Elliot Krane, TED

A pain specialist explains, for a general audience, how an injury can cause pain that continues long after the injury is gone. Eight minutes of cause and effect, told in simple words -- exactly what you practiced for your own poster.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Write down every "because", "so" and "as a result" you hear, and draw his chain in boxes, like your mechanism panel.

Video
"Why Things Hurt" -- Lorimer Moseley, TEDxAdelaide

A pain scientist with a strong Australian accent tells two stories to explain why the brain, not the body, decides when something hurts. A good model of a scientist making a complex mechanism funny and clear for a mixed audience.

Tip: listen once without subtitles -- a new accent is part of the training. Then choose one of his stories and retell it out loud in sixty seconds with "because of", "since" and "therefore".

Lesson Video
"How do pain relievers work?" -- George Zaidan, TED-Ed

Four animated minutes on what a pain reliever actually does in the body, from the injury to the brain. Your own field, explained step by step for students -- every step is one link of a cause-and-effect chain.

Tip: pause after each step and say the link out loud: "The injury triggers..., so...". Then explain the same video to an imaginary colleague who works on heart drugs.
