Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. Half of these words make something happen; the other half name what happens.

To cause -- to make something happen, often something bad
"Leprosy causes damage to the nerves of the hands and feet."
To lead to -- to be the first step that brings a later result
"Without treatment, nerve damage can lead to pain that lasts for years."
To trigger -- to start a reaction suddenly
"In some patients, cold weather can trigger a pain attack."
Factor -- one of the things that has an effect on a result
"Age is one factor, but it is not the most important one."
Outcome -- the final result of a treatment or a study
"The outcome was better in the group that started treatment early."
To be related to -- having a connection with something, but not always causing it
"Is the pain related to the level of inflammation?"
Inflammation -- the body's reaction to an injury or an infection: heat, swelling and pain
"Because of the inflammation, the nerve becomes thick and painful."
Sensory loss -- when a part of the body can no longer feel touch, heat or pain
"Sensory loss in the feet is often the first sign, before the pain."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Elif runs a pain clinic in Istanbul. "Last year we gave the same drug to two groups of patients. In the first group the pain went down, so we were happy. In the second group almost nothing changed. We looked at the files, and the answer was not the drug. The second group started treatment two years late, and because of that delay, the nerve damage was already permanent. Since the damage was older, the drug had less to protect. As a result, we changed our message to the clinics: look at the feet early. Therefore, our next study is about diagnosis, not about new drugs."
Why does Elif say "because of that delay" and not "because that delay"?
A Because the delay was very long
B Because "that delay" is a noun, and a noun after the cause word needs "because of"
C Because the sentence is in the past
In the text, "Since the damage was older" means...
A from the time the damage was older
B because the damage was older
C after the damage was older

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Links Between Cause and Effect

GRAMMAR

A cause and an effect need a small word between them. Three words go in front of the cause, and three go in front of the effect. What comes right after the word decides which one you can use.

because + full sentencecauseWe stopped the trial because the side effects were too strong.
because of + nouncause, no verb after itBecause of the inflammation, the pain levels increase.
since + full sentencecause, usually at the startSince we had limited data, we could not draw strong conclusions.
soeffect, conversationalLeprosy damages the nerve, so the patient loses sensation.
therefore / as a resulteffect, more formal, new sentence + commaThe drug reduced the pain. Therefore, we extended the study.
negative and questionthe link does not changeThe drug did not work because the damage was old. / Why did it not work?

One link per sentence is enough: never because and so together. Use so in a conversation, therefore or as a result on a poster or in a Q&A.

Quiz
At your poster, a cardiologist asks why leprosy causes pain. Which answer is best?
A "The bacteria live inside the nerve, so the body attacks it. The inflammation leads to damage."
B "Because of the bacteria live inside the nerve, so the body attacks it."
C "Leprosy is pain because nerve."
In a Q&A, somebody asks why your results were not conclusive. What do you say?
A "Because the sample was small, so the results were not clear."
B "Since the sample was small, we could not draw strong conclusions."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Ninety seconds for somebody outside your field. Explain in simple terms why leprosy causes pain: the cause, the chain that follows, and what early treatment changes. Use at least four different links -- because, because of, since, so, therefore, as a result -- and open with "That's what makes it so interesting" at least once. No script -- just speak.
