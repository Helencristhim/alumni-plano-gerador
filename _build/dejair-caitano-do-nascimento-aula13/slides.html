Chapter 3: The Code

The Links in the Chain

because, because of, since, so, therefore, as a result

Grammar Discovery

Cause or Effect?

Leprosy damages the nerve, so the patient loses sensation.
Because of the inflammation, pain levels increase.
Since we had limited data, we could not draw strong conclusions.
The treatment reduced the pain. Therefore, the team continued the trial.
Click to reveal the rule
Three words go in front of the CAUSE: because + a full sentence (because the sample was small), because of + a noun, with no verb (because of the inflammation), and since + a full sentence, usually at the start (Since we had limited data, ...). Three go in front of the EFFECT: so joins two parts of one sentence and is conversational; therefore and as a result usually start a new sentence, with a comma after them, and sound more formal — right for a poster or an answer in a Q&A. Never put because and so in the same sentence: one link is enough.

Chapter 4: In the Room

Same Drug, Different Outcome

And the reason was not in the drug

Listening 1

It Was Not the Drug

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What was different between the two groups of patients?
The second group started treatment two years after the first symptoms.
2. Why did the drug work less in the second group?
Because of the delay, the nerve damage was already permanent, so the drug had less to protect.
3. What is their next study about, and why?
About diagnosis, not new drugs — because early treatment leads to a better outcome.

Poster session, row C

But Why Does It Hurt?

N
I work on heart drugs, so please be patient with me. Why exactly does leprosy cause pain?
D
It is a fair question. The bacteria live inside the nerve, so the body attacks the nerve itself.
N
The body attacks its own nerve? Because of the bacteria?
D
Exactly. The attack causes inflammation, and the inflammation leads to damage. Since the nerve is damaged, it sends the wrong signals.
N
And that is the pain. But your poster says many patients also feel nothing. That seems like the opposite.
D
That's what makes it so interesting, because both come from the same damage. Some fibers stop working, and as a result the patient feels nothing. Others send pain all the time.
N
We see something similar with diabetes in my hospital. Is the pain related to how long the patient waited?
D
Very much. Late diagnosis is the biggest factor. Therefore, our message to clinics is simple: look at the feet early.
N
So a better outcome depends on an early look, not on a stronger drug. I did not expect that from a pharmacologist.
D
Neither did I, at the beginning. That is why I am here.

Chapter 5: Practice

Three Factors, One Answer

A hard question in a Q&A, answered in order

Listening 2

Why Did They Stop?

Read the questions, then listen.

1. Why does one visit cost the patients a full day of work?
Because many of them live far from the clinic.
2. Why did some patients think the drug was making them sick?
Nobody explained the side effects at the start, so they did not expect them.
3. What did the team change, and what was the result?
A ten-minute conversation before the first dose. The next year, only one patient in ten stopped.
