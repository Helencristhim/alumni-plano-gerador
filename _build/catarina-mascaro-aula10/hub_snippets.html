Lesson 10 — Pre-class

You Should Try This

Eight words for recommending (recommend, suggest, skip, addicted, remind, pick, give it a try, don’t judge a book by its cover) and the scale that decides how strong your advice sounds: should, could, might.

Stage 1.1: Recommending Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Recommend — you tell someone to try it, because you know it is good
"I recommend it. Seriously, watch it."
Suggest — you put an idea on the table, softly
"Can I suggest something? Start with episode two."
Skip — to jump over it and not do it at all
"Skip the first episode. It is the worst one."
Addicted — you cannot stop, even when you want to
"I am completely addicted to that game."
Remind — to make someone think of something they forgot
"Remind me to send you the link."
Pick — to choose one out of several
"You pick the film this time."
Give it a try — you ask someone to test it once, with no promises
"Just give it a try. One episode."
Don’t judge a book by its cover — it looks bad from outside, and it is not
"The poster is terrible. Don’t judge a book by its cover."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. Notice how the strength changes. Then answer the questions.

Three things. First: you should definitely watch that series — I recommend it more than anything I have seen this year. But skip the first two episodes, they are slow. Don’t judge a book by its cover: the poster is terrible and the show is not. Second: you could try the album I sent you. It is thirty minutes, and you could always just pick three songs. Third: there is a podcast about history that I am addicted to. You might like it — that one really depends on you. Just give it a try. And remind me on Friday, because I always forget to send links.
Which recommendation is she most sure about?
A The series — she says "you should definitely".
B The podcast — she is addicted to it.
Why does she use "might" for the podcast?
A Because she does not like it much herself.
B Because she is not sure the other person will like it.
What does "don’t judge a book by its cover" mean here?
A It looks bad from outside, but it is actually good.
B You should read the book before the series.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

It is a scale, not three separate rules

All three give advice. What changes is how sure you sound — and that is a social choice, not a grammar one.

Should — strong

You should watch it.
You should definitely try it.

Could — an option

You could try it.
You could always skip it.

Might — maybe

You might like it.
It might be worth a try.

✗ You should to watch it  ·  ✓ You should watch it
✗ She shoulds try it  ·  ✓ She should try it (no S, ever)
? Should I skip the first one? (not "Do I should")
Saying no politely: I don’t think you should... (softer than "you shouldn’t")

In normal advice, should is weak and almost sticks to "you". Only hit it hard when you are insisting.

Stage 3: Say it out loud

Speaking

Tap Listen, then Record. Keep "should" weak — if you hammer it, you sound like you are telling somebody off.

You should definitely try it.
You could always skip the first part.
And you? What would you recommend?

Stage 4: In the conversation

Quiz
Someone you barely know asks what to watch. Which sounds right?
A "You should watch it. It’s the best thing ever."
B "You might like it — it depends on what you usually watch."
Your friend says no three times. What is the smart move?
A Go softer: "Just give it a try. One episode."
B Say "you SHOULD watch it" louder.
Which sentence is correct?
A "She should to try the new one."
B "She should try the new one."

Stage 5: Sell it to me

Reflection
Record one minute in English. Pick ONE thing you really love — a series, a song, a game, an account — and make your teacher want it. Say what it is, why she should try it, what she could skip, and who might not like it. Use at least three words from Stage 1.1 and all three modals: should, could, might. Finish by asking what she would recommend to you.
