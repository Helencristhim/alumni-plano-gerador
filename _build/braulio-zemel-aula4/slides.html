Where You Start

Speed Is Not the Problem

Italians are easier

Not because they are slower -- because they finish their words

You read faster than you hear

The words are already in you, in the wrong channel

The gap is decoding

Not vocabulary, not grammar, not intelligence

You told your consultant that an Italian speaking English is easier to follow than an American. That is not an accident and it is not your ear failing. Americans compress, link and swallow, and a listener who was trained on written English is looking for words that are simply not there any more.

Chapter 3: The Code

I Wish I Could Hear It

The structure for what is not true, and for what did not happen

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I wish I understood American films without subtitles."

"I wish they spoke a little more slowly."

"I wish I had studied the sounds at twenty-four."

"I wish I had not stopped practising for so long."

Two of these are about now and two are about a past you cannot go back to. Which is which, and what changes in the verb?

FormWhat it saysExample
wish + past simple
I wish I understood		About NOW. It is not true today, and you would like it to be."I wish I understood films without subtitles."
wish + past perfect
I wish I had studied		About the PAST. It did not happen, and it is too late."I wish I had studied the sounds earlier."
wish + would
I wish they would		About somebody else's behaviour, and a small complaint inside it."I wish they would stop mumbling."
wish + couldAbout an ability you do not have now."I wish I could follow three people at once."
If onlyThe same structures, with more feeling in them."If only I had started ten years ago."

The verb after wish always steps one tense backwards. Present becomes past, past becomes past perfect. That step backwards is English saying: this is not real.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I wish I can understand American films."
"I wish I could understand American films."
"I wish I studied the sounds when I was twenty-four."
"I wish I had studied the sounds when I was twenty-four."
"I wish that they will speak more slowly."
"I wish they would speak more slowly."

After wish nothing stays in the present and nothing goes into the future. Can becomes could, will becomes would, and a finished past takes the past perfect — otherwise the second sentence says you did study, which is the opposite of what you mean. Read the correct versions aloud twice.
