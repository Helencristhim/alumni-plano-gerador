Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To oversee -- to be in charge of an area and to check that it works, without doing the work yourself
"He oversees five areas, and he writes none of the reports himself."
To coordinate -- to make different teams work together on the same plan, at the same time
"Two people coordinate the report with the sales offices every Wednesday."
To align -- to make sure everybody agrees on the same plan before the work starts
"On Friday he aligns the plan with the general manager."
To launch -- to put a new product or campaign on the market for the first time
"The team launches something new about four times a year."
To track -- to follow a number over time and see whether it goes up or down
"On Monday the team tracks the numbers of the week before."
Quarterly -- happening four times a year, once every three months
"The board meeting is quarterly, and the report goes out before it."
A dashboard -- one screen that shows the main numbers of the business together
"He starts the day on the dashboard, before the office fills up."
A target -- the number a team has to reach inside a period of time
"Every Monday they compare the week with the target."
A campaign -- a set of actions to promote a product during a period of time
"The campaign runs before the rainy season, when workshops buy more."
Market share -- the part of all the sales in a market that belongs to one company
"Market share in this business rarely moves quickly."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last time you said what you do. Now the sentence is about somebody else, and one letter appears. "He oversees five areas of the business." "She tracks the weekly numbers and she owns the dashboard." That s belongs to he, she and it, and to nobody else. A team counts as an it, so it takes the s too: "The team tracks the numbers every Monday."

Some verbs need a little more. After s, sh, ch, x and o the ending is -es: he launches the campaign, she watches the competition, it goes up. And the s disappears the moment does arrives, because does has already taken it: "The team does not launch a campaign every month." "Does he align the plan with the general manager?" Never does he aligns.

The second half of this lesson is how often, and English is strict about where it puts the answer. A one-word adverb goes before the verb: "He never opens his e-mail first." "They usually compare the week with the target on Monday." A longer expression goes at the end: "She coordinates the agencies every Wednesday." "We report to the board quarterly." Portuguese lets you say ela vai sempre; English does not. Market share in a slow market moves once a quarter, not once a week, and a good team aligns on that before anybody panics.

1. "The team tracks the numbers." Why does the verb carry an s if a team is many people?
A Because track is an irregular verb.
B Because a team is one group, so it works like it.
C Because the numbers are plural.
2. Why is it "does he align" and not "does he aligns"?
A Because does already carries the s, so the verb after it stays bare.
B Because align is a short verb.
C Because questions never use the s.
3. Where does a one-word frequency adverb like never or usually go?
A At the end of the sentence, like every Monday.
B Before the verb: he never opens, they usually compare.
C Right after the verb, the way Portuguese does it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Third Person and How Often

GRAMMAR

He, she and it take the s. Does takes it away. And a one-word adverb goes in front of the verb.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A visitor asks who looks after the weekly numbers. You answer:
A "Rita. She track the numbers and she own the dashboard."
B "Rita does. She tracks the weekly numbers and she owns the dashboard."
C "Is the Rita. The dashboard is of her."
You want to say your team launches something new four times a year. You say:
A "The team launches a new campaign about four times a year."
B "The team launch four times per year a new campaign."
C "The team does launches a new campaign four times a year."
You want to say you always start on the dashboard and never on your e-mail. You say:
A "I start always on the dashboard and I open never the e-mail first."
B "Always I start on the dashboard, never I open the e-mail first."
C "I always start on the dashboard, and I never open my e-mail first."
The visitor has finished answering. You want to keep the conversation going. You say:
A "Interesting. Our team is bigger."
B "And you, how often does your team meet?"
C "How often your team meets?"
