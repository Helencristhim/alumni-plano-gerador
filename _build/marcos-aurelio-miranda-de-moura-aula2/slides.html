Chapter 3: The Code

One Letter, and Where Always Sits

He, she, it — and how often

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"He usually starts the day on the dashboard."

"She tracks the numbers every Monday."

"The team does not launch a campaign every month."

"Does he present to the board every quarter?"

Two red verbs carry an extra letter and two do not — look at who each sentence is about. Then look at the blue parts: one sits in the middle of the sentence, and one sits at the end.

JobFormExample
He, she or itverb + sShe tracks the numbers.
Spelling of the s-es after s, sh, ch, x and ohe launches · she watches · it goes
A team is an itone group = one verb with sThe team tracks the numbers.
Negative and questiondoes not / Does + bare verbHe does not launch it. Does he present?
How often, one wordalways, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never — before the verbHe never opens his e-mail first.
How often, several wordsevery Monday, twice a year, once a quarter — at the endShe tracks the numbers every Monday.
The trapthe s appears once, never twiceNot does he presents. Say does he present.

In one line: he, she and it take the s, does takes it away, and a one-word frequency adverb goes in front of the verb.

Listening 1 · A Colleague

What My Director Actually Does

Somebody describing their own boss to a visitor. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the director do first every morning, and what does he never do first?
He opens the dashboard first. He never opens his e-mail before that.
2. What happens on Wednesday, and who is involved?
Two people from market intelligence coordinate the report with the sales offices.
3. How often does the director present to the board, and what does the speaker say about those weeks?
Once a quarter. The speaker says everybody in the team notices, because he asks for the numbers twice.
Listening 2 · A Podcast

Why Everybody Looks at the Same Screen

A business podcast on weekly tracking. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say a weekly number is good for, and what is it not good for?
It is good for seeing a direction early. It is not good for making the decision, because one week is too short.
2. What mistake does she say teams make with dashboards?
They add more and more numbers until nobody reads any of them. She says a good dashboard fits on one screen.
3. What does she say about market share in slow markets?
That it moves slowly and that a good team measures it once a quarter, not once a week.
