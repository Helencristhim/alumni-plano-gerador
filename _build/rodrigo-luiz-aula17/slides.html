Chapter 3: The Pattern

Short Word, Long Word

cheaper than · more reliable than · better

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

Indonesia is cheaper than Madagascar.
Indonesia is faster than Madagascar.
Madagascar is more reliable than Indonesia.
The quality from Madagascar is better.

Two words take -ER. One takes something else. Why? And what word comes after the comparison?

UseFormExample
Short adjectiveadjective + -er thancheaper than, faster than
Long adjectivemore + adjective + thanmore reliable than, more expensive than
Ends in -y-y becomes -iereasy → easier, busy → busier
Irregulargood → better, bad → worseThe quality is better this quarter.
The other wayless + adjective + thanIndonesia is less reliable.
CarefulNever "more cheaper": it is -ER or MORE, never both. And after the comparison comes THAN — never "that", never "of".

Chapter 4: Two Suppliers

A Call With Paris

two vanilla suppliers, one budget, one decision

Dialogue

Cheaper or More Reliable?

C
Rodrigo, we need to choose a vanilla supplier for next year. What do you think?
R
I compared the two. The supplier in Indonesia is cheaper than the supplier in Madagascar.
C
How much cheaper?
R
About twelve percent. But Madagascar is more reliable. Their deliveries are never late.
C
That's important. And which one is faster?
R
Indonesia is faster: twenty days, not thirty-five. But the quality from Madagascar is better.
C
I see. And our stock in Brazil? Is it better than last quarter?
R
Yes, it is higher. We have stock for three months now. Last quarter we had only six weeks.
C
Good. So my question is: for us, is cheaper better, or is reliable better?
R
For vanilla, reliable is better. Madagascar is more expensive, but a late delivery is more expensive for the client.
