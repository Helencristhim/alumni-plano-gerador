Lesson 02 -- Pre-class

Which Test Opens Which Door -- Comparing Like a B2

A short guide to the English proficiency tests. Key words: a certificate, valid, to expire, a band score, a mock test, a cutoff score, a requirement, an employer, affordable, worthwhile, to assess, to weigh up. Structure: comparatives and superlatives with modifiers.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A certificate -- an official document that shows you passed a test or a course
"The certificate shows my level in all four skills."
Valid -- officially accepted for a certain period of time
"My result is valid for two years."
To expire -- to stop being valid after a certain date
"My old certificate expired last year."
A band score -- a score given as a level on a scale, like 6.5 or 7
"She needs a band score of 7 for the program."
A mock test -- a practice test that copies the real one
"I took two mock tests before the real exam."
A cutoff score -- the minimum score you need to be accepted
"The cutoff score for the job was 785."
A requirement -- something you must have or do to be accepted
"English is a requirement for this position."
An employer -- a company or a person that pays people to work
"Some employers prefer the TOEIC."
Affordable -- not too expensive; at a price you can pay
"The online test is much more affordable."
Worthwhile -- worth the time, the money or the effort
"Preparing for a month was worthwhile."
To assess -- to judge or measure the level or the quality of something
"The test assesses listening, reading, speaking and writing."
To weigh up -- to think carefully about the good and bad points before you decide
"I’m weighing up the TOEFL and the IELTS."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Cauê wants a certificate for his career, so he is weighing up the options. The TOEFL is by far the most common test for universities in the United States, but for many employers the TOEIC is much more familiar.

The online test is far more affordable than the others, and it is not nearly as long. It is a worthwhile first step to check his level. But most results are valid for only two years, and then they expire.

His plan: first a mock test, then a decision. “The more I know the format, the calmer I’ll feel,” he says.

1. Why does the text say “far more affordable” and not just “more affordable”?
A Because far makes the comparison more polite in formal writing.
B Because it shows that the difference is big.
C Because affordable is a long adjective and long adjectives always need far before them.
2. What is Cauê’s first step?
A A mock test.
B Booking the TOEFL and the IELTS on the same day, so he can compare the results.
C Asking his employer which certificate the company will pay for this year.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “The more I know the format, more calm I feel.”
B “The more I know the format, the calmer I feel.”
C “More I know the format, the more calmer I feel about the test.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Comparatives and Superlatives -- Saying How Much

GRAMMAR

At B2, it is not enough to say which is bigger. Say how much bigger: far, much, a lot for a big difference; slightly, a bit for a small one; by far the for the clear winner.

JobFormExample
A big differencefar / much / a lot + comparativefar cheaper, much more affordable
A small differenceslightly / a bit + comparativeslightly longer, a bit easier
The clear winnerby far + the + superlativeby far the most common test
A big difference, negativenot nearly as + adjective + asisn’t nearly as long as the TOEFL
Two things change togetherthe + comparative ..., the + comparative ...The more you practice, the calmer you feel.
The trapvery does not go with a comparativeNot very cheaper or more cheaper. Say much cheaper.

Never: very cheaper · more cheaper · the most easy · the more you practice, more you learn
