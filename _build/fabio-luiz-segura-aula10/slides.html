Chapter 4: Which One, Right Now

Nine Structures, One Second to Choose

Nothing new today. The difficulty has moved from the form to the choice

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Have in Common?

"I have worked in wholesale banking for eleven years."

"Transaction volume was measured against the same corridor last year."

"This is the system that handles our cross-border settlement."

"She said that the pilot would be ready by the first quarter."

Not one of these is new. You have said all four before, in four different lessons. The difficulty has moved: it is no longer the form, it is choosing which one, in the second you need it.

When you want to say...Reach forExample
How long something has lasted, up to nowpresent perfect + for / sinceI have worked here for eleven years.
The number, without saying who produced itpassive: was / were + participleVolume was measured against the same corridor.
Exactly which system, person or placethat · who · whereThe system that handles settlement.
What somebody told you earliersaid that + tense one step backShe said that the pilot would be ready.
A real condition and its consequencefirst conditional: if + present, willIf the pilot works, we will extend it.
A softer, more diplomatic version of the samesecond conditional: if + past, wouldIf we started in March, we would finish in June.
A system you had before and do not have nowused to + base verbWe used to reconcile everything by hand.
What is happening now, against what is always truepresent continuous vs present simpleWe run the corridor. This quarter we are testing a new one.
The shape of a long answer, so nobody gets lostdiscourse markers: first, the point is, soSo the point is that the delay is not ours.

In one line: the question in a live conversation is never "which tense?" — it is "what am I trying to say?", and the form follows from that.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Three Voices on Getting Through the Week

Rehearsing, debriefing, and the advice nobody expects

Listening 1 · Where the Difficulty Happens

Read It, or Say It Out Loud

Both groups prepare. Only one of them has the difficulty on a Sunday. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the difference between the two groups, according to him?
Not preparation, because both groups prepare. It is where the difficulty happens: in your kitchen on a Sunday, or in the room in front of the person you wanted to impress.
2. Why does he say a rehearsed sentence comes out differently?
Because it is mechanical rather than confidence. Your mouth has physically done the movement before.
3. How many times does he say to say it, and which one do you use?
Say it badly three times and then once more. The fourth one is the one you will use.
Listening 2 · End of Day One

Two Good Things and One Blocker

A voice note recorded on the walk back to the hotel. Sound first — no text.

1. What changed in her booth conversations, and what was the result?
She stopped opening with who they are and started opening with what they are working on this quarter. Three out of five people asked a follow-up question.
2. What does she decide to park from tomorrow, and why?
Anything that is going to be published as a report. The panel cost her ninety minutes and she should have left after fifteen.
3. What is her blocker, and what is her priority in the morning?
She has no answer on the compliance side, so Thursday cannot close. Her priority is to find the person who owns it rather than chasing the person who has been apologising.
Listening 3 · Advice for a First-Timer

Get One Quick Win in the First Two Hours

His answer to that question changed over the years, and it is not "prepare more". Sound first — no text.

1. What did he use to say, and what does he say now?
He used to say prepare more. Now he says get one quick win in the first two hours.
2. What counts as a win, in his description?
Any win. Asking one person a question and understanding the answer, or saying your own two sentences to somebody who does not care.
3. What does he say actually stops people, and what beats it?
Not language and not knowledge, but the belief, built in the first morning, that they are not going to manage. It is beaten by evidence, not by preparation.
