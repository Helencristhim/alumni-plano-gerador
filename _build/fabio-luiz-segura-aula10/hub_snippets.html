Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A dress rehearsal -- a full practice run, done exactly as the real thing will be done
"The dress rehearsal is uncomfortable on your sofa so that Tuesday is not."
To debrief -- to go through what happened afterwards, while it is still fresh
"I debrief on the walk to the hotel, not three days later on the plane."
A takeaway -- the one thing you keep from a session or a conversation
"If I cannot say the takeaway in one line, I did not need to be in the room."
A contingency -- the plan you use when the first plan does not work
"A rehearsal turns the question you cannot answer into a contingency."
Bandwidth -- the time and attention somebody actually has available
"I do not have the bandwidth this week. Can we do it after Sibos?"
A blocker -- the thing that is stopping progress right now
"The compliance answer is my blocker. Nothing closes until it arrives."
To park something -- to leave a subject aside on purpose, to come back to it later
"Let us park the pricing and finish the technical part first."
A quick win -- a small result you can get fast, which buys time for the big one
"Get one quick win in the first two hours. Any win."
To prioritize -- to decide what comes first when you cannot do everything
"Four days and two hundred sessions: you prioritize or the week decides for you."
To circle back -- to come back to somebody about something you left open
"I will circle back on Thursday with the number from my head of delivery."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Nothing in this lesson is new, and that is the point. In nine lessons you have built nine structures, one at a time, each with a slide, a table and a drill of its own. In a real conversation none of them arrives labelled. Somebody asks a question, and you have about one second to reach for the right one.

The mistake is to search for the form. Under pressure, "which tense do I need?" is an impossible question, because the answer only makes sense once you already know what you want to say. Fluent speakers do the opposite: they decide the meaning, and the form follows automatically. Nobody thinks "present perfect" before saying I have worked here for eleven years. They think about eleven years that are still going, and the tense is simply what that meaning sounds like in English.

So the useful map is a map of situations, not of tenses. You want the number without naming the team that produced it: volume was measured against the same corridor. There are three systems and you mean one: the system that handles settlement. You are carrying what somebody told you on Tuesday: she said that the pilot would be ready. You are softening a proposal that may be too fast: if we started in March, we would finish before the audit. Same nine structures, sorted by the job they do.

There is one more reason to sort them this way. Every one of the four sentences above has a Portuguese-shaped version that fails: the duration with since instead of for, the passive with no participle, the relative with a second pronoun after it, the reported speech that never steps back a tense. These are not random errors. They are the four places where the Portuguese sentence and the English sentence disagree, and they are the four to listen for in your own voice.

Which is why the last exercise of this block is not a test. It is a dress rehearsal: the same four days you are actually going to live, said out loud in advance, so that the difficulty happens here instead of at the stand.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Choosing Under Pressure

GRAMMAR

Read the left column, not the middle one. The situation chooses the structure for you.

When you want to say...Reach forExample
What is always true, and what is happening this quarterpresent simple vs present continuousWe run the corridor. This quarter we are testing a new one.
How long something has lasted, up to nowpresent perfect + for / sinceI have worked here for eleven years.
The shape of a long answer, so nobody gets lostdiscourse markers: first, the point is, soSo the point is that the delay is not ours.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

Somebody at a stand asks how long you have been in this business. You say:
A I work in wholesale banking since eleven years.
B I have worked in wholesale banking for eleven years.
C I am working in wholesale banking eleven years ago.
A vendor invites you to a second meeting in a week you are already full. You say:
A No, it is impossible for me.
B I do not have the bandwidth this week, so let us park that one.
C Maybe, I will see, it depends.
You are reporting to your director what Claire told you on Tuesday. You say:
A She said that the pilot will be ready by the first quarter.
B She said that the pilot would be ready by the first quarter.
C She said the pilot is ready in the first quarter, she told.
A proposal may be too fast for the other side and you want to soften it. You say:
A We start in March and we finish before the audit.
B If we started in March, we would finish before the audit.
C If we will start in March, we finish before the audit.

Tim Ferriss — “Why you should define your fears instead of your goals” (TED)

The contingency word from this lesson, turned into a method. He writes down what could go wrong, then what he would do about each one — which is exactly what a dress rehearsal does to the question you cannot answer.

Tip: do the exercise for your own conference week. Three things that could go wrong, and one line each on what you would do. Say the lines out loud.

Laura Vanderkam — “How to gain control of your free time” (TED)

Bandwidth and priorities, argued from real data rather than opinion. Her central claim — that we do not build the life we want by saving time, but by deciding what the time is for — is the four-day week in this lesson, applied to the rest of the year.

Tip: her line about "I do not have time" meaning "it is not a priority" is worth trying in English on your own calendar. Rewrite three of your own sentences that way.
