Grammar Discovery

Look at the highlighted verbs

We paddled out at dawn.
I borrowed a board from a stranger.
My leash broke on the second wave.
A rip took him past the rocks.

It already happened → past simple

Most verbs just add -ed. Some verbs change completely, and those you learn one by one. Good news: the form is the same for I, you, he, we and they.

The Code

Two groups, one rule

Regular — add -ed

paddle → paddled
borrow → borrowed
rescue → rescued

Irregular — new word

break → broke
take → took
give up → gave up
− I didn't give up.   ? Did you borrow a board? (did already carries the past — the verb goes back to normal)
Sound Check

-ed has three sounds

paddled, borrowed

sounds like d — no extra syllable

washed, helped

sounds like t — no extra syllable

wanted, started

this one does add a syllable: -id

Did You Catch It?

What happened

1. What broke, and when?
His leash broke on the second wave, and the board went to the shore without him.
2. Why couldn't he swim back?
A rip took him past the rocks. He swam for ten minutes and didn't move.
3. What did he do in the end, and who helped him?
He stopped fighting the rip and swam sideways. Then a guy on a red board came out and helped him in.
Listening 1

End of shift

1. How many rescues were there, and at what time was the first one?
Three rescues. The first one was at seven in the morning.
2. What happened to the boy at eleven?
A rip took him past the flags. He didn't panic and he waved, so they reached him in two minutes.
3. Why does the lifeguard say the day ended well?
Because nobody went to hospital and everybody swam between the flags after lunch.
Listening 2

The other side of the story

1. Why did she go out on the red board?
She saw a boy without a board in the rip and paddled out to him.
2. What did the boy do right?
He didn't panic. He stopped fighting the rip and swam sideways.
3. What did she lend him afterwards?
Her old board, so he could go back out.
