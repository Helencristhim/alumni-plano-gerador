What Happened Yesterday

Eight story words (dawn, leash, wax, shore, rip, borrow, give up, rescue) and the past simple: regular verbs with -ed, irregular verbs one by one, and what happens to the verb after "did".

Stage 1.1: Story Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Dawn — the time when the sun comes up
"We paddled out at dawn."
Leash — the cord that keeps the board on your ankle
"My leash broke on the second wave."
Wax — the sticky stuff you put on the board so you do not slip
"I forgot the wax at home."
Shore — the part of the beach where the water meets the sand
"He swam back to the shore alone."
Rip — a strong current that pulls you away from the beach
"A rip took him past the rocks."
Borrow — to take something from somebody and give it back later
"I borrowed a board from a guy on the sand."
Give up — to stop trying
"I didn't give up, but I wanted to."
Rescue — to save somebody who is in danger
"The lifeguard rescued two people that morning."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the story. Then answer the questions.

We paddled out at dawn and it was perfect for ten minutes. Then my leash broke on the second wave and my board went to the shore without me. I tried to swim in, but a rip took me past the rocks. I swam for ten minutes and I didn't move. I didn't give up. I stopped fighting it and I swam sideways. A girl on a red board came out and helped me in. Later she lent me her old board, so I borrowed it and I went back out.
What was the first thing that went wrong?
A His leash broke on the second wave.
B He forgot the wax at home.
How did he get out of the rip?
A He swam straight to the beach.
B He stopped fighting it and swam sideways.
Why is it "I didn't move" and not "I didn't moved"?
A Because "didn't" already carries the past, so the verb goes back to normal.
B Because "move" is an irregular verb.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Past simple — it already happened

Most verbs add -ed. Some change into a new word. The form is the same for I, you, he, we and they.

Regular

paddle → paddled
borrow → borrowed
rescue → rescued

Irregular

break → broke
take → took
give up → gave up

+ My leash broke and I lost the board.
− I didn't give up. (not "didn't gave")
? Did you borrow a board? (not "Did you borrowed")
One past per sentence. If did has it, the verb does not.

Stage 4: Telling the story

Quiz
You want to ask a friend about yesterday. Which question is correct?
A Did you surf yesterday?
B Did you surfed yesterday?
Your leash broke. How do you tell the story?
A My leash breaked on the second wave.
B My leash broke on the second wave.
You want to say that you kept trying. Which sentence is correct?
A I didn't give up.
B I didn't gave up.
Somebody asks: "Did the lifeguard help you?" He did. What do you answer?
A Yes, he was.
B Yes, he did.

Stage 5: Your own story

Reflection
Tell the story of a day when something went wrong — in the water or anywhere else. Record 30 seconds in English, in order: where you were, what happened first, what you did, who helped, how it ended. Check one thing before you send it — after "did" or "didn't", the verb has no past on it.
