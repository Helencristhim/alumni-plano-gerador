Lesson 15 · After the Panel

Connecting, Networking and Being Remembered

The panel is over. Now there are two hundred people, a glass of wine and thirty minutes. The real business of a conference happens here, in small conversations. Today you learn to start them, keep them and end them.

Three Missions

Chapter 3: The Code

A Small Question at the End

Question tags that open a conversation

Grammar Discovery

Why the Small Question at the End?

"It was a great panel, wasn't it?"

"You work in infrastructure, don't you?"

"You haven't been to Brazil before, have you?"

"The moderator kept everybody on time, didn't she?"

Is the speaker really asking for information, or inviting the other person to agree and talk? Look at each blue tag: when the sentence is positive, is the tag positive or negative? Where do was, do, have and did come from?

JobFormExample
Positive sentence, negative tag..., isn’t it? / don’t you? / wasn’t it?It was a great panel, wasn’t it?
Negative sentence, positive tag..., is it? / have you? / do you?You haven’t been to Brazil, have you?
Repeat the auxiliary or bebe / have / will / can + pronounYou’ll be in Lisbon, won’t you?
No auxiliary? Use do, does or didpresent simple and past simpleShe kept us on time, didn’t she?
Intonationvoice down = I expect you to agree; voice up = a real questionIt’s cold today, isn’t it? (down)
The trapthe tag matches the verb and the subject; never isn’t it for everythingNot You work here, isn’t it? Say don’t you?

In one line: positive sentence, negative tag; negative sentence, positive tag; repeat the auxiliary and the pronoun.

Listening 1 · The Coach

How to Work a Room

A communication coach gives three pieces of advice for receptions. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she recommend arriving early?
Because it is easier to talk to people when the room is still empty.
2. What is her second piece of advice?
Ask questions that invite the other person to talk, and listen for common ground.
3. How should you end a conversation, according to her?
Say something positive, mention a next step and say goodbye.
Listening 2 · The Introvert

Networking for People Who Hate It

An introverted lawyer explains his simple method for conferences. Sound first — no text.

1. How many conversations does he plan for each reception?
Three good conversations.
2. How long is his elevator pitch?
Two sentences.
3. What does he do within twenty-four hours?
He sends a short message to each new contact.
Business Radio

What People Remember

A short radio report about what makes people memorable at events. Sound first — no text.

1. What did researchers ask the participants the day after the event?
To write down the people they remembered and why.
2. What did people remember most?
A personal story or a shared interest.
3. What did almost nobody remember?
Job titles.

Grace at the Reception

Introduce, Then Leave

Scenario: You are talking to Grace when your colleague Paulo arrives. Introduce Paulo to Grace, find a topic for the two of them, and then excuse yourself politely to talk to another investor.

Keywords:

Grace, this is You both if you will excuse me It was great to meet you let's keep in touch
Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

Two Minutes, No Notes

Scenario: At the reception, the forum chair asks you: So, tell me about yourself. Give your elevator pitch, ask the chair two questions with question tags, and finish with a clear next step.
