Asking for the Right Thing

Chapter 1: The Opening

The First Ten Minutes

A first call with a new customer is not about the product yet. It is four or five questions, in both directions, and the price of a bad question is the whole meeting.

Think of the last meeting you had with a customer. What was the first question you asked?

A native speaker almost never says send me the price. He says could you send me a quote — same request, and the difference is one word.

One Question

How Do You Ask for a Price?

You are on a call with a company you want to buy from. Ask them for a price, out loud, in one sentence.

Now the second one, which is harder: ask them to deliver it faster than they normally do.

Chapter 3: The Code

Three Words That Open Every Door

Can, could and would — the difference between a request and an order

Discovery

What Comes After the Red Word?

"Could you send me a quote by Friday?"

"We can offer a discount on larger orders."

"Would you be able to reduce the lead time?"

"Last year we could not accept those terms."

After the red word, the blue verb is always naked: no to, no -s, no -ing. And three of the four sentences are not about the past at all.

UseFormExample
Polite requestCould you + verb?Could you send me a quote?
Offer / what is possibleWe can + verbWe can offer a discount.
Softer requestWould you be able to + verb?Would you be able to deliver in ten days?
Past abilitycould / could not + verbLast year we could not accept those terms.
Saying no politelyI am afraid we cannot + verbI am afraid we cannot go below that price.
The trapnever to after can / could / wouldNot Can you to send. Not Would you able to.

In one line: could you = please, can we = we are able, would you be able to = please, but softer.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

One Call, Two Recordings

The conversation, the voicemail, and the quote that follows it

Dialogue

A Buyer You Have Never Met

K
Good morning, Roberto. I am Karen Doyle, the buyer for the Midwest region. Thank you for making the time.
R
Good morning, Karen. Thank you for the call. Could you tell me what you are looking for this year?
K
We are looking for a new supplier for our private label line. Two products, to start.
R
We can do that. Would you be able to send me the specification, so I can prepare a quote?
K
Of course. And what is your usual lead time from Brazil?
R
Thirty days to the port, and about eighteen days at sea after that. Could we start with a trial order? It is faster, and it is safer for both of us.
K
I like that. And what are your terms for a first order? Is a discount possible on a small quantity?
R
I am afraid we cannot give our best price on a small quantity, but we can offer better payment terms. Could I send you the quote by Friday?
K
Friday works. Send it to me and I will read it before our next call. If the numbers work, we can place the order in July.
Comprehension

About Karen

1. What is Karen’s job, and which region does she buy for?
She is a buyer, and she buys for the Midwest region.
2. What is she looking for, and how many products does she want at the beginning?
A new supplier for their private label line, and two products to start.
3. What exactly does she promise to do on Friday, and what does she not promise?
She promises to read the quote before the next call. She does not promise the order — she says they can place it in July if the numbers work.

The Assistant Calls Before the Meeting

1. What two changes does she ask you to remember?
The meeting moved from ten to eleven, same day and same link. And the quote has to arrive before the call, not during it.
2. What should go on the first page of the quote, and why?
The lead time and the payment terms, because Karen never reads the second page before a first call.

The minimum quantity. It is the first thing they ask.
Listening 2 · A Buyer’s Opinion

What He Decides in the First Ten Minutes

Nineteen years buying, and three things he watches for. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. In the first ten minutes, what is he actually deciding?
Not whether the product is good. He is deciding whether you are easy to work with.
2. What happens when you answer the question you prepared instead of the question he asked?
He writes one word in his notes, and it is not a good word.
3. Why does he want a supplier to say no to something?
Because a supplier who says yes to everything in the first call says no to everything in month three. If you say what you cannot do, he trusts the rest more.
Real Document

The Quote You Send on Friday

QUOTATION 2026-0431
Bom Sabor Foods · prepared by Roberto Saraiva
CustomerNorthline Foods · Midwest region
Productprivate label, two references
Trial order — minimum4 pallets, 2,400 units
Lead time30 days to the port, 18 days at sea

Discountnone on a trial order · 5% above 20 pallets
This quote is valid for15 days
Page 1 of 3 — the lead time and the payment terms are here, on purpose.
1. Karen wants a discount on the trial order. What does this document answer?
There is no discount on a trial order. The five percent starts above twenty pallets.
2. She reads the quote on the twentieth. Until when can she use this price?
Fifteen days from the quote, so she has to decide quickly. After that the price has to be confirmed again.
3. She orders today. In how many days, in total, do the goods arrive?
Forty-eight days: thirty days to the port and eighteen days at sea.
