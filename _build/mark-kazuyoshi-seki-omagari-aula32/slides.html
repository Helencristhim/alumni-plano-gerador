Four Ways to Give the Same Advice

Same Advice, Four Sizes

"Keep your passport safe."

Correct, and flat. Every instruction you give sounds exactly the same size.

"Make sure you keep your passport on you."

Now it is the one thing on the list that must not go wrong.

"Whatever you do, don't put it in your back pocket."

The loudest warning English has. Use it once, and people remember it.

"Keep it in your front pocket, or you will lose it."

An instruction with an "if" hidden inside it.

Anyone can give an instruction. Giving the important one more weight than the rest is what makes people follow it.

Chapter 3: The Instruction Kit

Not All Instructions Weigh the Same

The patterns English uses when an instruction really matters

Grammar Discovery

Four Instructions, Four Weights

Make sure you keep your passport on you.

Whatever you do, don't change money at the airport.

Do try the ramen at the station.

Leave before eight, or you will miss the fast train.

Put them in order, from the gentlest to the strongest. Then find the one with an "if" hidden inside it.

PatternWhat it doesExample
make sure (that) + presentthe one thing that must not go wrong"Make sure you keep it on you."
be sure to / remember to / don't forget to + verba reminder about something easy to forget"Don't forget to charge your phone."
whatever you do, don't + verbthe strongest warning, once per conversation"Whatever you do, don't change money there."
do + verbwarm insistence, never bossy"Do try the ramen."
never / always + verba rule with no exceptions"Never take a call on the train."
verb ..., and / or + resultA condition hidden inside the instruction. And means if you do: "Buy the pass, and you will save money." Or means if you don't: "Leave early, or you will miss it."

The Tool

Three Places It Goes Wrong

Trap 1 · no "will" after make sure

The instruction already points at the future, so the clause after make sure stays in the present: "Make sure you bring cash", never "make sure you will bring cash". It is the same rule you used after by the time.

Trap 2 · forget to, never forget + -ing

"Don't forget to charge your phone" is an instruction about later. With -ing, forget and remember talk about a memory of the past, so "don't forget charging your phone" is not an instruction at all.

Trap 3 · "no touch" is Portuguese in an English coat

A negative instruction in English starts with don't or with never, and never with both: "Don't touch", "Never touch". "No touch", "not touch" and "don't never touch" do not exist. And no subject: "Whatever you do, don't...", never "whatever you do, you don't".

Get the pattern right and the listener hears how much the advice matters. Get it wrong and they hear the mistake instead.

Listening

Giving Advice Its Weight

Your teacher on the patterns and the three traps. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Why does she say the simple instruction is not always enough?
Because it is flat: it gives every piece of advice exactly the same weight.
2. What can never come after "make sure"?
"Will". English uses the present: "make sure you keep it", never "make sure you will keep it".
3. In the pattern with a hidden condition, what does "or" mean, and what does "and" mean?
"Or" means "if you don't", and "and" means "if you do".
4. Which mistake does she say comes from Portuguese?
"No touch" or "not touch". In English a negative instruction starts with "don't" or "never", and never with both.

A Message From the Hostel

The owner of a hostel in Kyoto, to every guest arriving this week. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What happens if a guest arrives after ten without telling the hostel?
The door locks and nobody is there to open it.

The Real Thing

Before You Arrive

Kamo River Hostel · Kyoto
To: Mark Omagari — five things before you arrive
Check-in
until 10 p.m.
1 · ArrivalTell us your arrival time, or the door will be locked when you get here.
2 · ShoesTake your shoes off at the entrance. The slippers are for the hallway only.
3 · MoneyMake sure you have some cash on you: many small restaurants here do not take cards.

Your replyTwo questions for Kenji, and one tip you would give the next guest.
1. Line 3 says "make sure you have some cash", not "make sure you will have some cash". Why?
Because after "make sure" English uses the present: the instruction already points at the future.
2. In line 1, what does "or" mean, and what would happen if Mark ignored it?
"Or" means "if you don't". If he does not tell them his arrival time, the door will be locked when he gets there.
