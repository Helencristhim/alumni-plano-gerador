Lesson 32 -- Pre-class

Make Sure You... -- Complex Imperatives

"Take the train" is correct and flat: every piece of advice comes out the same size. This lesson gives you the patterns that give an instruction its weight -- make sure, be sure to, whatever you do don't, do, and the instruction with an "if" hidden inside it -- and you use them to give travel advice to somebody going for the first time. Key words: carry-on, double-check, keep an eye on, heads-up, rule of thumb, steer clear of, to be on the safe side, etiquette, stock up on, customs, pickpocket, plug adapter.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. These are the words people use when they give advice before a trip.

Carry-on -- the small bag you are allowed to take onto the plane with you
"Pack your carry-on as if the suitcase is going to disappear."
Double-check -- to check something a second time, to be completely sure
"Double-check the name of your hostel before you land."
Keep an eye on -- to watch something carefully so that nothing happens to it
"Keep an eye on your bag in the big stations anyway."
Heads-up -- a warning you give somebody before something happens
"One heads-up about money: small restaurants take cash only."
Rule of thumb -- a simple, practical rule that works most of the time
"As a rule of thumb, if everybody around you is quiet, be quiet too."
Steer clear of -- to stay away from something because it may cause you trouble
"Steer clear of the exchange booths at the airport."
To be on the safe side -- to do something extra, just in case, so that nothing goes wrong
"Carry more cash than you need, just to be on the safe side."
Etiquette -- the polite rules people are expected to follow in a place
"Now the part nobody warns you about, which is etiquette."
Stock up on -- to buy a lot of something so that you do not run out
"Stock up on cash before you leave a big city."
Customs -- the place at the border where officers check what you bring into a country
"Fill in the customs form on the plane, and you will be out first."
Pickpocket -- a thief who steals from people's pockets and bags in a crowd
"Pickpockets are rare in Japan, but rare is not the same as never."
Plug adapter -- a small device that lets your charger fit a socket in another country
"Put a charger and a plug adapter in your carry-on."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the travel page and answer the questions. Notice how each pattern in bold changes the WEIGHT of the instruction.

Last summer one of our writers spent three weeks in Japan and came back with a notebook full of mistakes. The first one happens before the plane has even left. Pack your carry-on as if the suitcase is going to disappear. Whatever you do, don't put your medicine in the bag that goes in the hold.

When you land, you go through customs. Fill in the form on the plane, and you will be out of the airport before the rest of the line. Make sure you keep the name of your hostel on your phone, because a taxi driver cannot read an address he has never seen in English.

Then the etiquette. Keep your voice down on the train, and never talk on the phone there at all. Do bow back when somebody bows to you; nobody expects it to be perfect. And don't leave a tip, or somebody will run after you to give it back.

Last, the money. Stock up on cash before you leave a big city, and be sure to carry more than you think you need, just to be on the safe side.

1. "Whatever you do, don't put your medicine in the bag that goes in the hold." Why does the writer use "whatever you do" here?
A Because it is the one warning on the list that must not be ignored.
B Because the writer is not sure the advice is right.
C Because the sentence is a question.
2. "Fill in the form on the plane, and you will be out before the rest of the line." What does "and" mean here?
A but
B if you do
C if you don't
3. "Don't leave a tip, or somebody will run after you to give it back." What does "or" mean here?
A if you do leave one
B and then
C because
4. Why is it "make sure you keep the name", and not "make sure you will keep the name"?
A Because "will" is only used in questions.
B Because "keep" is an irregular verb.
C Because after "make sure" English uses the present -- the instruction already points at the future.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Giving an Instruction Its Weight

GRAMMAR

A simple instruction is correct and flat. These patterns tell the listener how much the instruction matters, and one of them hides a condition inside it.

PatternWhat it doesExample
make sure (that) + presentthe one thing that must not go wrong"Make sure you keep your passport on you."
be sure to / remember to / don't forget to + verba reminder about something easy to forget"Don't forget to charge your phone."
whatever you do, don't + verbthe strongest warning, once per conversation"Whatever you do, don't change money at the airport."
do + verbwarm insistence, never bossy"Do try the ramen."
never / always + verba rule with no exceptions"Never take a call on the train."
verb ..., and / or + resultA condition hidden inside the instruction. And = if you do: "Buy the pass, and you will save money." Or = if you don't: "Leave early, or you will miss it."
Three trapsNo will after make sure ("make sure you bring", never "make sure you will bring"). Forget and remember take to in an instruction ("don't forget to buy", never "don't forget buying"). A negative instruction starts with don't or never -- never "no touch", never "don't never", and never with a subject.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the sentence that says exactly what you mean.

Which sentence is correct?
A "Make sure you will double-check the address."
B "Make sure you double-check the address."
C "Make sure double-check the address."
Which instruction is correct?
A "Don't forget buying a plug adapter."
B "Don't forget buy a plug adapter."
C "Don't forget to buy a plug adapter."
A sign on the train tells people not to make phone calls. Which one is right?
A "No talk on the phone."
B "Please don't talk on the phone."
C "Don't never talk on the phone."
Your friend will miss the last bus if she stays too long at the party. You say:
A "Leave before eleven, or you will miss the last bus."
B "Leave before eleven, and you will miss the last bus."
C "Leave before eleven, but you will miss the last bus."
Only ONE thing on your list is really dangerous. The best way to say it is:
A "Whatever you do, don't swim at that beach after dark."
B "Maybe don't swim at that beach after dark."
C "You don't swim at that beach after dark."
The exchange rate at the airport is terrible. You tell a friend to:
A stock up on it.
B steer clear of it.
C keep an eye on it.
