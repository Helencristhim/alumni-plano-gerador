Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

Signing Off -- The Next Steps After the Yes

The partner said yes: now you explain what happens next, and when. Key words: to sign off on, a draft, to review, a clause, a signature, a purchase order, to kick off, a milestone, to roll out, a point of contact. Structure: as soon as, once, when, before, until + present.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To sign off on -- to give the final approval to a document or a plan
"Both directors have to sign off on the contract."
A draft -- a first version of a document, before the final one
"Ryan will send the draft of the contract on Monday."
To review -- to read something carefully to check it and suggest changes
"Our lawyers will review the contract this week."
A clause -- one part of a contract, with one rule or condition
"The penalty clause is on page four."
A signature -- your name, written by you, to show that you agree
"The contract needs two signatures from each side."
A purchase order -- the official document that a client sends to buy something
"The purchase order arrived this morning."
To kick off -- to start a project or a meeting
"We will kick off the project in São Paulo."
A milestone -- an important point or stage in a project
"The first milestone is the main building."
To roll out -- to start using a new system in several places, step by step
"We will roll out the cameras one building at a time."
A point of contact -- the person you call when you need something in a project
"Marcos is your point of contact for the project."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

After a long negotiation, the partner says yes. Luciana calls her team. “Here is the plan,” she says. “As soon as Ryan sends the draft, our lawyers will review it. Once both sides sign off, we will receive the purchase order.”

Her team wants to start immediately, but Luciana is careful. “Before we kick off the project, we will meet the hospital team. And we will finish the first milestone by the end of July.”

She also explains one important rule: “We will not order any cameras until the contract is signed.” All these sentences are about the future, but after as soon as, once, before and until, the verb is in the present.

1. When will the lawyers review the draft?
A As soon as Ryan sends it.
B Next year, after the team finishes the first milestone in the main building.
C The text does not say.
2. Why does Luciana say “until the contract is signed” and not “until the contract will be signed”?
A Because the contract was signed last week, so the sentence is about the past.
B Because after until, we use the present to talk about the future.
C Because the sentence is a question.
3. What will happen by the end of July?
A The team will kick off the project with the hospital and sign the contract with Ryan.
B Ryan will send the draft.
C The team will finish the first milestone.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- As Soon As, Once, When, Before, Until

GRAMMAR

To connect two future actions, use a time word + the present for one action, and will for the other. Never use will after as soon as, once, when, before or until. Use by + a time for a deadline.

JobFormExample
Immediately afteras soon as + presentAs soon as it is ready, I will send it.
After something is completeonce / after + presentOnce we receive it, we will review it.
At that momentwhen + presentWe will send the PO when both sides sign off.
Earlier thanbefore + presentBefore we roll out, we will train your team.
Up to that momentuntil + presentI will send updates until the project is complete.
Not later than a datewill + verb + by + timeWe will finish it by the end of July.

Never: As soon as it will be ready · When we will receive it · Once we receive it, we start next month · Until the project will be complete
