Chapter 3: The Code

It Is Thought, It Has Been Shown

The voice of the research

Grammar Discovery

Your Voice, or the Field?

"I think early termination is rare." → "It is widely believed that early termination is rare."

"Studies prove that delays cost more." → "It has been shown that delays cost more than the original estimate."

"People think terminations start with tariffs." → "Most terminations are thought to start with a tariff dispute."

"A study found the risk is higher in ports." → "The risk has been found to be higher in ports than in highways."

Look at the blue words. Who is speaking now: you, or the field? Two sentences start with It. What comes after are thought and has been found?

MoveFormExample
What people in the field believeIt is (widely / generally) + believed / thought / said / known + that + clauseIt is widely believed that early termination is rare.
What research has shownIt has been + shown / found / argued + that + clauseIt has been shown that delays cost more.
Start with the topicsubject + is / are + thought / believed / known + to + verbMost terminations are thought to start with a tariff dispute.
A finding about the topicsubject + has / have been found + to + verbThe risk has been found to be higher in ports.
Then your own voicebut + my data suggest / in my viewIt is widely believed that it is rare, but my data suggest otherwise.
The trapmixing the two patternsNever it is believed early termination to be rare or termination is thought that it starts. Say it is believed that it is or it is believed to be.

In one line: it + passive + that + clause, or topic + passive + to + verb, and never mix the two.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Ten Minutes, Then Questions

A seminar chair, an expert and an abstract

Dialogue

Opinion or Research?

J
Thank you, Aguinaldo. Before I open the floor, the first question is mine. Is that your opinion, or is it what the research shows?
A
Both, and I try to keep them apart. It is widely believed that early termination is rare in Brazil. My data suggest it is not.
J
That is a strong claim. What is your evidence?
A
Forty-two contracts over twenty years. Early termination has been found to happen in about one in five of them. That supports my hypothesis.
J
Is that a correlation, or can you show the cause?
A
For now, only a correlation. Most terminations are thought to start with a tariff dispute, but I cannot prove that yet. That is the main limitation of the study.
J
I appreciate the honesty. Is there a consensus in the literature, or is the question still open?
A
Still open. The results so far are inconclusive. That is why I would like an authority on concession law to weigh in.
Comprehension

About Joanna

1. What is Joanna’s first question?
Whether it is his opinion or what the research shows.
2. How does Joanna describe his claim, and what does she ask for?
She calls it a strong claim and asks for his evidence.
3. What does Joanna want to know about the literature?
Whether there is a consensus, or the question is still open.
Listening 1 · The Seminar Chair

Where the Literature Ends

A seminar chair opens the session. Sound first — no text.

1. How long is each presentation, and what comes after it?
Ten minutes, followed by ten minutes of questions.
2. What does she ask speakers to keep separate?
What is believed or has been shown by other scholars, and their own view.
3. What does she say about limitations?
A limitation is not a weakness; a study that hides its limits is.
Listening 2 · The Expert

What We Know, What We Do Not

An expert gives his opinion on early termination. Sound first — no text.

1. What is widely believed, according to him?
That the state saves money when it takes a concession back.
2. Which costs were found to be higher than the savings?
The new tender, the legal claims and the months without investment.
3. Where is the evidence inconclusive?
On smaller contracts.
