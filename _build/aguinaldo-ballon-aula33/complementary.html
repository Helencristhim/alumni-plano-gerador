Complementary Activities

Optional extras to enjoy outside class. Mark each one as done after you watch or listen to it.

Talk
TED -- Naomi Oreskes, "Why we should trust scientists" (19 min)

A historian of science explains how a scientific consensus is built, and why it is not the same as one expert's opinion. Full of the language of evidence from today's lesson.

Tip: write down every time she says what is believed, known or shown, and repeat two of those sentences about your own research.

Talk
TED -- Melissa Marshall, "Talk nerdy to me" (4 min)

A short, practical talk on how researchers can present complex work so that a general audience understands it. Perfect before a seminar presentation.

Tip: after watching, say your research question in one sentence, without jargon, as she recommends.

Watch on TED ↗
Talk
TED -- Uri Alon, "Why science demands a leap into the unknown" (16 min)

A scientist on what happens when research does not go as planned, and why an inconclusive result is part of the process, not a failure.

Tip: note how he talks about a hypothesis that did not work, and describe one limitation of your own study in the same calm way.
