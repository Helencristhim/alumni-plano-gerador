Lesson 33 -- Pre-class

Presenting Research and Expert Opinion -- The Voice of the Research

How to present research out loud and keep it apart from your own opinion. Key words: a hypothesis, empirical, a consensus, inconclusive, a correlation, to replicate, a limitation, anecdotal, to weigh in, an authority. Structure: it is widely believed that..., it has been shown that..., delays are thought to..., the risk has been found to...

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A hypothesis -- an idea you propose first and then test with research
"My hypothesis is that early termination costs more than the state expects."
Empirical -- based on real data and observation, not only on theory
"We need empirical evidence, not only a good argument."
A consensus -- general agreement among the experts in a field
"There is a broad consensus that the right of way causes most delays."
Inconclusive -- not giving a clear answer or a clear result
"The results of the first study were inconclusive."
A correlation -- a connection between two things that change together
"There is a correlation between delays and renegotiations."
To replicate -- to repeat a study and get the same result
"Other researchers could not replicate the result."
A limitation -- a weak point that reduces what a study can prove
"The main limitation is the small number of port contracts."
Anecdotal -- based on personal stories, not on careful research
"That evidence is anecdotal: it comes from three cases I know."
To weigh in -- to give your opinion in a discussion, often as an expert
"Let me ask the regulator to weigh in on this point."
An authority -- an expert whose opinion is respected in a field
"She is an authority on concession law."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

In a seminar, the room listens for two voices. The first is the voice of the field: what many people believe, and what research has shown. In English, this voice usually has no "I". You say: "It is widely believed that early termination is rare," or "It has been shown that delays cost more than the original estimate."

You can also start with the topic and add to + verb: "Most terminations are thought to start with a tariff dispute," or "The risk has been found to be higher in ports." Both patterns are correct, but never mix them. It is "it is believed that it is rare" or "it is believed to be rare", never "it is believed it to be rare".

The second voice is yours. Only after the field has spoken do you add it: "but my data suggest otherwise." A good presenter makes it easy to hear where the literature ends and where he begins.

1. What does "It is widely believed that..." tell the audience?
A That this is what many people in the field think, not only you.
B That you personally believe it, and that you have the data from your own research to prove it to the room.
C That the idea has been proven.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "Most terminations are thought that they start with a tariff dispute in the first years."
B "Most terminations are thought to start with a tariff dispute."
C "It is thought most terminations to start with a tariff dispute."
3. Where does the presenter's own voice come in?
A At the very beginning, before anything else, so that the audience knows his opinion first.
B Never: a presenter should only speak for the field.
C After the field has spoken: "but my data suggest otherwise."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Research Voice

GRAMMAR

It + passive + that + clause, or topic + passive + to + verb. Never mix the two.

MoveFormExample
Beliefit is (widely) believed / thought / known + thatIt is widely believed that it is rare.
Resultit has been shown / found + thatIt has been shown that delays cost more.
Topic firstsubject + is thought / known + to + verbTerminations are thought to start with tariffs.
Findingsubject + has been found + to + verbThe risk has been found to be higher.
Your voicebut + my data suggest......but my data suggest otherwise.

Never: it is believed it to be · is thought that it starts · has been show · found be

Stage 2: Put the Presentation in Order

Order

Put Aguinaldo's short research talk in order, from the question to his own opinion.

?"My question is simple: does early termination cost the state more than it saves?"
?"It is widely believed that early termination is rare."
?"I studied forty-two concession contracts over twenty years."
?"Early termination has been found to happen in about one in five of them."
?"This is only a correlation, and that is the main limitation of the study."
?"So in my view, early termination should be a last resort."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A professor asks: "Is that your opinion, or is it the research?" You say:
A "Both. It is widely believed that it is rare, but my data suggest otherwise."
B "It is my opinion, of course, because I am the person who did all of the research for this project."
C "It is believed it to be rare."
Someone asks if your result proves the cause. It does not. You say:
A "Yes, it proves it."
B "Not yet. It is only a correlation, and that is the main limitation."
C "I prefer not to talk about the limitations of the study today, because we do not have much time left."
A board member says your evidence comes from three cases you know. You say:
A "You are right, my evidence is only anecdotal, so please forget what I said in my presentation today."
B "That is not important."
C "Fair point, but the data are empirical: forty-two contracts."
The chair asks if the experts agree. They do not. You say:
A "Everybody agrees with me, and I think that the question has been completely closed for many years now."
B "There is no consensus yet. The results so far are inconclusive."
C "It has been show that they agree."
