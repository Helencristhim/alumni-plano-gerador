Where Does It Hurt?

Warm-up

How Long Has That Been Going On?

First, your three if sentences about October. Then: something small that has been bothering you this week — a noise, a knee, a slow phone. What is it, and how long?

The Target

Three Facts, in Thirty Seconds

1. What is wrong. One word or two, not a story.

2. Where. Point at it and name it.

3. How long it has been going on. This is the sentence that decides how fast you are seen.

Chapter 3: The Code

How Long It Has Been Going On

For, since, and the tense Portuguese does not have

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"My ankle has been hurting since Tuesday."

"I have been feeling dizzy for two days."

"We have been waiting since half past four."

"How long has it been going on?"

Every red group has the same three pieces, and all four sentences are still true right now. Now look at the blue words, and at what kind of time comes after each one.

JobFormExample
Started before, and still goinghave / has + been + verb-ingMy ankle has been hurting.
A period of timefor + two days, an hour, a weekI have been feeling dizzy for two days.
A point in timesince + Tuesday, four o’clock, we landedIt has been hurting since Tuesday.
The questionHow long + has / have + subject + been + -ingHow long has it been going on?
With be, know and haveno -ing: has been, have known, have hadI have had a headache since this morning.
Saying that it hurtsmy X hurts · my X is sore · I have a pain in my XNot I am with pain. Say my back hurts.
The trapnever the simple present with for or sinceNot it hurts since Tuesday. Say it has been hurting since Tuesday.

In one line: for takes a period, since takes a starting point, and both need have been plus -ing — never the simple present.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Thirty Seconds at the Desk

One conversation, two recordings, one form

Dialogue

At the Urgent Care Desk

T
Hi there. What brings you in today?
V
I sprained my ankle on Tuesday. It is still swollen, and it has been hurting since then.
T
Can you walk on it? And have you been taking anything for the pain?
V
I can walk, but not far. I have been taking painkillers for two days, and they help for about four hours.
T
Understood. Any medicine you take regularly, or anything you are allergic to?
V
Nothing regular, and no allergies. Here is my passport and my insurance card. Do I need an appointment, or can I wait?
T
You can wait. It is about forty minutes. Your insurance covers the visit, and anything the doctor prescribes you buy at the drugstore next door.
V
That is fine, thank you. And could you tell me roughly what the visit will cost, so there is no surprise later?
Comprehension

About Tom

1. What two questions does he ask about the ankle and the pain?
Whether she can walk on it, and whether she has been taking anything for the pain.
2. What does he ask before he takes the documents?
Whether she takes any medicine regularly, and whether she is allergic to anything.
3. How long is the wait, and what does he say about who pays for what?
About forty minutes. The insurance covers the visit, and anything the doctor prescribes she buys at the drugstore next door.
Listening 1 · A Clinic Front Desk

The First Thirty Seconds

Eighty people a day, and what he actually needs from each one. Sound first — no text.

1. What three things does he need, and in what order?
What is wrong, where, and how long it has been going on.
2. What does he say visitors from abroad do with those thirty seconds instead?
They apologise, they explain that their English is not good, and they tell the story from the beginning of the holiday.
3. What does he say about your documents, and about your grammar?
Have the insurance card and passport in your hand, not in the bag. And nobody is judging your grammar — they are triaging a waiting room.
Listening 2 · Buying Medicine Abroad

At the Drugstore Counter

What travellers get wrong, in both directions. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say not to assume anything about prescriptions in another country?
Because some things you buy freely at home need a prescription there, and some things that need a prescription at home are simply on the shelf.
2. What does she say to do at the drugstore, and what does it cost?
Go to the counter at the back and ask the pharmacist directly. They are allowed to advise you, they do it all day, and it is free.
3. What are the two last pieces of advice, and why?
Photograph the box of anything you take regularly, because the brand name changes from country to country and the ingredient does not. And keep every receipt, because the receipt is the only thing the insurance will look at.
