Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

Where Does It Hurt? -- Describing a Symptom

Three facts in thirty seconds: what is wrong, where, and how long it has been going on. Key words: a drugstore, urgent care, an appointment, to sprain, swollen, dizzy, painkillers, a prescription, an insurance card, sore. Structure: present perfect continuous with for and since.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A drugstore -- a shop that sells medicine and everyday things
"There is a drugstore on the corner, open until midnight."
Urgent care -- a clinic for a problem that cannot wait, but is not an emergency
"It is not an emergency, so we went to urgent care."
An appointment -- a fixed time to see a doctor or a dentist
"Do I need an appointment, or can I just come in?"
To sprain -- to hurt a joint by twisting it
"I sprained my ankle on the steps, and it has been hurting since Tuesday."
Swollen -- bigger than normal, because of an injury
"My ankle is swollen and I cannot put my shoe on."
Dizzy -- feeling that the room is turning around you
"I have been feeling dizzy since we landed."
Painkillers -- medicine that takes the pain away
"Could you recommend something? I usually take painkillers for this."
A prescription -- the paper a doctor writes so that you can buy a medicine
"In the United States this one needs a prescription."
An insurance card -- the card that proves a company will pay for your treatment
"Here is my passport and my insurance card."
Sore -- painful when you touch it or move it
"My throat has been sore for two days, but I have no fever."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Nobody at a clinic desk wants the story of your holiday. They want three facts, in this order: what is wrong, where, and how long it has been going on. The first two are vocabulary. The third one is a tense, and it is the one Portuguese does not have.

Look at the shape: have or has, then been, then the verb with -ing. "My ankle has been hurting." "I have been taking painkillers." It means something started before now and is still going on, which is exactly what a symptom is. In Portuguese that idea sits in the present, and this is why the most common mistake in the world is "it hurts since Tuesday". In English, never the simple present with for or since.

Then the two little words. For takes a period of time: for two days, for an hour, for a week. Since takes the moment it started: since Tuesday, since four o’clock, since we landed. The question is the same shape turned around: "How long has it been going on?" A few verbs never take -ing here — be, know, have. "I have had a headache since this morning." "My throat has been sore for two days."

One last group of sentences, because they are the ones people translate word by word and get wrong. English does not say I am with pain. It says "my back hurts", or "my back is sore", or "I have a pain in my back". And English does not say it hurts me: it just says "it hurts". Say that, plus since or for, and hand over the insurance card. That is the whole thirty seconds.

1. Why does English use has been hurting instead of hurts?
A Because it started before now and is still going on.
B Because hurt is an irregular verb.
C Because it is more polite at a clinic.
2. Which one takes a period of time, and which one takes the moment it started?
A Since takes the period, for takes the moment.
B For takes the period, since takes the moment.
C Both take either one, depending on the verb.
3. What is wrong with "I am with pain in my back"?
A Nothing. It is correct but formal.
B It needs the continuous form: I am being with pain.
C English does not use that structure. It says my back hurts, or I have a pain in my back.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- For, Since, and How Long

GRAMMAR

For takes a period. Since takes a starting point. Both need have been plus -ing.

JobFormExample
Started before, and still goinghave / has + been + verb-ingMy ankle has been hurting.
A period of timefor + two days, an hour, a weekI have been taking painkillers for two days.
A point in timesince + Tuesday, four o’clock, we landedIt has been hurting since Tuesday.
The questionHow long + has / have + subject + been + -ingHow long has it been going on?
With be, know and haveno -ing: has been, have known, have hadI have had a headache since this morning.
Saying that it hurtsmy X hurts · my X is sore · I have a pain in my XNot I am with pain. Say my back hurts.
In speechit has becomes it’s · I have becomes I’veIt’s been hurting since Tuesday.

Never: it hurts since Tuesday · dizzy since two days · I am with pain · it hurts me when I walk

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The clinic desk asks what brings you in. You say:
A "Sorry, my English is not very good. We arrived on Sunday and on Tuesday we went to the beach and..."
B "I sprained my ankle on Tuesday. It is still swollen, and it has been hurting since then."
C "My ankle hurts since Tuesday and I am with pain."
You want to say how long you have been taking the medicine. You say:
A "I have been taking painkillers for two days."
B "I take painkillers since two days."
C "I am taking painkillers since two days."
Your throat is painful when you swallow, and it started yesterday. You say:
A "I am with a pain in the throat since yesterday."
B "My throat has been sore since yesterday."
C "My throat is being sore since yesterday."
You are at a drugstore with no prescription. You say:
A "Give me something for the headache, please."
B "I have had a headache since this morning. At home I take this. Is there something similar here?"
C "I want a prescription for the headache that hurts me since the morning."
