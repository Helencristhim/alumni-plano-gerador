Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Tasting menu -- a set menu of many small dishes chosen by the chef
"The tasting menu has five small courses."
Course -- one part of a meal, like the starter or the dessert
"The second course was a fish dish."
Wine pairing -- a wine chosen to go well with each dish
"Each course comes with a wine pairing."
Local delicacy -- a special food that a region is famous for
"The quenelle is a local delicacy in Lyon."
Bistro -- a small, informal restaurant with simple food
"We had dinner at a small bistro near the river."
Portion -- the amount of food served to one person
"The portions here are small, but delicious."
Savory -- salty or spicy, not sweet
"I prefer a savory breakfast to a sweet one."
Rich -- with a lot of butter, cream, or fat
"The sauce is very rich, so I only ate half."
Bland -- with very little flavor
"The soup was a little bland, so I added salt."
To pick up the check -- to pay for everyone at the table
"My client picked up the check after dinner."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

After the first day of the fair, Pierre invites Lucimara to dinner at a small bistro in old Lyon. When they arrive, every table is full. "It is Tuesday, and it is full," Lucimara thinks. "The food must be excellent." Pierre orders a bottle of wine without looking at the list. He must know this place very well.

The menu is in French, and Lucimara does not know some dishes. "This might be fish," she says, "but I am not sure." Pierre explains that it is the quenelle, a local delicacy, and that the sauce is very rich. Then the server brings a very small plate. "That can't be the main course," Lucimara laughs. "It is too small!" She is right: it is only the first course of the tasting menu.

1. Why does Lucimara think the food must be excellent?
A Because Pierre says that the chef of the bistro won a famous prize last year.
B Because the menu is in French.
C Because the bistro is full on a Tuesday.
2. What does Lucimara think when she sees the very small plate?
A She thinks it can't be the main course.
B She thinks the server forgot part of the dish and asks him to bring more food.
C She thinks it is the dessert.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "This must to be fish, but I am not completely sure about it."
B "This might be fish."
C "This mights be fish, but I am not sure."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Must, Might, Can't (Deduction)

GRAMMAR

How to make a smart guess when you are not 100 percent sure.

FormUseExample
must + verbyou are almost sure it is trueThe bistro is full. The food must be good.
might / could + verbit is possible; you are not sureThis dish might be spicy.
can't + verbyou are almost sure it is not trueThat can't be the main course.
might not + verbit is possible that it is not truePierre might not like sushi.
RuleAfter must, might, could and can't, use the base verb: no "to", no "-s". For a negative guess, use can't, not "mustn't". Use these modals when you have a reason (evidence) for your guess.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each real situation at the restaurant.

You see a dish called "quenelle" and you do not know it. You say to the server:
A "Excuse me, what is the quenelle? Is it very rich?"
B "I want the quenelle. What is it? I hope it is good, because I am very hungry."
C "The quenelle must to be good. I take it."
A small bistro is empty at eight on a Friday night. Your colleague asks what you think. You say:
A "The food must be excellent here."
B "The food mustn't be good, so we can't stay here tonight, I think."
C "Hmm. The food might not be very good."
The soup has almost no flavor. You say to the server:
A "This soup is terrible, and I want my money back, please, right now."
B "Excuse me, the soup is a little bland. Could I have some salt?"
C "The soup can't be bland."
Your French client invited you, and now he wants to pay. You say:
A "No, we split the check: you pay for your food, and I pay for mine."
B "OK."
C "Thank you so much, Pierre. Next time, it is my turn."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
