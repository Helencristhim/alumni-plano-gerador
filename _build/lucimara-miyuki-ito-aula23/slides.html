Chapter 3: Must, Might, Can't

Smart Guesses

must be · might be · can't be

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The bistro is full on a Tuesday. The food must be very good."

"I don't know this dish. It might be spicy."

"That can't be the main course. It's too small!"

"Pierre ordered without the menu. He must know this bistro well."

What do the orange words have in common? How sure is the speaker in each sentence?

FormUseExample
must + verbyou are almost sure it is TRUEThe food must be good.
might / could + verbit is possible; you are not sureIt might be spicy.
can't + verbyou are almost sure it is NOT trueThat can't be the main course.
might not + verbit is possible that it is not trueHe might not like fish.

After must, might and can't, use the base verb, with no "to". For a negative guess, say can't, not "mustn't".

Chapter 4: The Bistro

Dinner with a Client

Pierre recommends; Lucimara asks and decides

Dialogue

At the Bistro

P
So, Lucimara, this is my favorite bistro in Lyon. What do you think?
L
It is lovely. It is full on a Tuesday, so the food must be very good.
P
It is. I recommend the tasting menu. Five small courses.
L
What is this dish here? It must be a local delicacy.
P
Yes, it is the quenelle, a fish dumpling in a creamy sauce. It is quite rich.
L
Then I might ask for a small portion. And the chicken in red wine, is it spicy?
P
No, it can't be spicy. French food is almost never hot. Some people say it is a little bland.
L
Perfect for me. And tonight, please let me pick up the check.
P
No, no. In Lyon, the person who invites pays. You must be tired after the fair. Just enjoy it.
Comprehension

About Pierre

1. What does Pierre recommend?
The tasting menu, with five small courses.
2. How does Pierre describe the quenelle?
A fish dumpling in a creamy sauce. It is quite rich.
3. Why does Pierre say Lucimara can't pay?
Because in Lyon, the person who invites pays.
