Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These are the words that put a long career in the right order.

To graduate -- to finish a university course and receive your degree
"I graduated from medical school in Sao Paulo."
Intern -- a doctor in the first year of practice after medical school
"As an intern, I worked eighty hours a week."
Training -- the period when you learn to do a job in practice
"My training in trauma surgery lasted three years."
Turning point -- the moment when a situation starts to change direction
"The turning point was the day I saw the first data report."
Milestone -- an important step that marks progress
"Passing the first exam is a milestone, not the end."
Opportunity -- a situation that makes something new possible
"The project gave me the opportunity to learn to code."
To commit to -- to promise your time and your effort to something
"I committed to two years of study before I decided."
To move on from -- to leave a situation behind and start something new
"It took me a long time to move on from the operating room."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Bruno Tavares graduated as a physiotherapist in 2011 and worked in a small clinic for six years. In 2017 he moved to a large hospital, and that was the turning point: he discovered that he liked teaching more than treating. Since 2019 he has taught at a university, and he has not left the clinic completely — he still sees patients on Fridays. So far he has trained more than two hundred students, and he says he has never regretted the change.
Why does the text say "graduated" and not "has graduated"?
A Because the sentence carries a finished moment: in 2011
B Because he is not a physiotherapist anymore
C Because the action was very short
Why does it use "has taught" for the period since 2019?
A Because he stopped teaching in 2019
B Because it started in 2019 and is still true today
C Because nobody knows the exact year

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Simple vs Present Perfect

GRAMMAR

The tense follows the time expression, not the feeling. A finished moment takes the past simple. A line that reaches today takes the present perfect.

past simpleverb + -ed, or the irregular formI graduated in 2007. She left three years ago.
its triggersyesterday, in 2007, last year, ago, whenWhen did you leave the hospital?
present perfecthave / has + past participleI have worked here since then.
its triggerssince, for, so far, already, yet, ever, neverI have not taken the exam yet.

Careful with questions: when always takes the past simple. Say "When did you leave?", never "When have you left?".

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
A colleague at an international event asks how you got from the operating room to artificial intelligence. Record two minutes, in order: what you did first, what the turning point was, and what you have done since then. Dates for the finished parts, since and so far for the parts that are still true.
