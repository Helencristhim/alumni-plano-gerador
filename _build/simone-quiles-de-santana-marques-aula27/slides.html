Chapter 3 -- The Softening Code

I'm Afraid · It Seems · I Was Hoping

Same fact, softer tone

Discovery

Listen and Notice

Read each sentence again without the orange words. Which version sounds like an attack? What do the orange words do to the fact?

"I'm afraid the closing will slip to April."

"It seems that two buildings have an unpaid tax debt."

"The delay is slightly longer than we expected."

"I was hoping we could agree on a new date today."

SoftenerJobExample
I'm afraid (that) + sentenceintroduces bad news politely"I'm afraid the closing will slip to April."
it seems / it looks like + sentencemakes a fact less direct and less personal"It seems that two buildings have a tax debt."
slightly / a little / a bit + adjectivemakes the size of the problem smaller"The delay is slightly longer than we planned."
I was hoping / I was wondering if + we coulda softer request -- past form, present meaning"I was hoping we could agree on a new date."
not very + positive adjectivea softer way to say something negative"The first results are not very encouraging."

Key idea: softening changes the tone, never the fact: the date is still April. I was hoping looks like the past, but it talks about now. And do not soften twice in a row -- "I'm afraid it seems that maybe..." sounds like you are hiding something.

The Structure

Blunt, Tactful, Too Soft

The same bad news, three ways. Only one keeps the fact AND the relationship.

Blunt · the fact as an attack

"You will not get your closing in March."

True, but the other side hears a fight, not a fact.

Tactful · the fact, softened

"I'm afraid the closing will slip to April, and I wanted you to hear it from me first."

Same fact. The relationship survives.

Too soft · the fact disappears

"It seems that maybe there could be a small change in the timing, possibly."

Now nobody knows what you said. Candid means clear.

Soften the tone, never the fact.

Artefact

The Follow-up Email

Campinas -- revised closing timeline

To: Ryan Moore (New York) · From: Simone Quiles · Cc: Lucia, Madrid

Dear Ryan,

Thank you for your time on the call this morning. As promised, here is the summary in writing.

I'm afraid the property review found an unpaid tax debt on two buildings. It seems that the amount is limited, but local counsel needs about three weeks to confirm it.

As a result, the closing will move slightly, to the middle of April. I understand this is a setback for your client, and I empathize with that.

I was hoping we could agree on two points this week: the new date, and a special indemnity for the full amount.

Best regards,
Simone
