Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Setback -- a problem that delays your progress or makes a situation worse
"The tax debt is a setback, but it is not the end of the deal."
Blunt -- saying something very directly, without thinking about how the other person feels
"Do not be blunt with Ryan: give him the facts, but with care."
Tactful -- careful to say difficult things in a way that does not upset people
"A tactful email gives the bad news and keeps the relationship."
Candid -- honest and open, even when the truth is not pleasant
"I want to be candid with you: the closing will move."
Empathize -- to understand and share how another person feels
"I empathize with your client: nobody likes a new date."
Frustration -- the feeling of being annoyed because you cannot change or control something
"I understand your frustration. We wanted March too."
Defensive -- reacting as if someone is attacking you, and protecting yourself too much
"If you sound defensive, Ryan will think legal made a mistake."
Constructive -- helpful and focused on finding a solution, not on the problem
"Let us keep this call constructive and talk about the new date."
Way forward -- a plan or next step that helps everyone move on from a problem
"The way forward is a short delay and a special indemnity for the debt."
Take on board -- to listen to an idea or a criticism and really consider it
"I take your point on board, and I will discuss it with Madrid."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

The property review in Campinas found a real setback. It seems that two buildings have an unpaid tax debt from 2020, and local counsel needs three weeks to confirm the amount. I'm afraid the closing will slip to the middle of April, and Simone has to tell Ryan in New York. Lucia gives her good advice: be candid, but never blunt. A tactful lawyer gives the facts and protects the relationship. Simone knows that Ryan will feel frustration, so she plans to empathize with his client first. She will not sound defensive, because the review was the right decision. Instead, she will keep the call constructive. The new date is a little later than everyone wanted, but she has a clear way forward: a special indemnity for the debt. Her last sentence is ready: I was hoping we could agree on it this week. And if Ryan criticizes the plan, she will take his points on board.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Softening Bad News

GRAMMAR

Bad news has two parts: the fact and the tone. In English you keep the fact clear and soften the tone with small words. I'm afraid and it seems that introduce the news. Slightly, a little and a bit make the problem smaller. I was hoping we could turns a demand into a polite request.

FormUseExample
I'm afraid (that) + sentenceintroduce bad news politely"I'm afraid the closing will slip to April."
it seems / it looks like + sentencemake a fact less direct"It seems that two buildings have a tax debt."
slightly / a little / a bit + adjectivemake the problem smaller"The delay is slightly longer than we planned."
I was hoping / I was wondering if + we coulda soft request about now"I was hoping we could agree today."
not very + positive adjectivea soft negative"The first results are not very encouraging."

Key point: soften the tone, never the fact. I was hoping is past in form, but it talks about now. And never say "I'm afraid OF" + sentence: "I'm afraid the date will move."

Stage 2: Give Bad News in Order

Order

Listen first, then put the steps of a difficult conversation in the correct order.

?You give the news candidly, with a softener: I'm afraid, it seems that.
?You offer a way forward and agree on the next step in writing.
?You prepare the facts: what happened, how big it is, and how long it will take.
?You empathize with their frustration and do not get defensive.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best sentence for each situation.

Ryan asks about the date. Which answer is candid AND tactful?
A "Well, it seems that maybe, possibly, there could be a very small change in the timing of the closing."
B "I'm afraid the closing will slip to the middle of April."
C "You will not have March. Accept it."
Javier says legal destroyed his timeline. Which answer is not defensive?
A "I understand your frustration. The review found the problem before closing."
B "It is not my fault. You never read the register and you never listen to legal in any of these meetings."
C "Legal is always right."
You want New York to accept the indemnity. Which request is the softest?
A "Accept the indemnity."
B "You must accept the indemnity this week, or we will have to stop the whole deal and walk away."
C "I was hoping we could agree on the indemnity this week."

Reflection
