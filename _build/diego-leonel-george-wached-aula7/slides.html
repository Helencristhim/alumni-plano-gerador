Chapter 3: The Code

The Question Inside Another Question

Four polite words in front, and the order inside goes back to normal

Grammar Discovery

What Changes Inside?

"Where is the station?"

"Could you tell me where the station is?"

"Does this bus stop at the museum?"

"Do you know if this bus stops at the museum?"

The blue words are the wrapper. Look at what happens to the red part when a wrapper goes in front of it: something moves, and something disappears entirely.

JobFormExample

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Street, at Full Speed

One conversation, two recordings, one transit sign

Dialogue

A Corner in Chicago

D
Excuse me, could you tell me where the nearest subway station is?
N
Sure. Head north two blocks and it is on your right, right around the corner from the pharmacy.
D
Would you mind repeating that a little more slowly?
N
Of course. Two blocks north. At the second intersection, look right. There is a sign.
D
Thank you. Do you know if the train goes downtown from there?
N
The red line does. You may have to transfer at Fullerton, depending where you are going. There is a detour this week.
D
And could you tell me how much the fare is?
N
Two fifty, whatever the distance. Buy a transit card at the machine, it saves you doing this every time.
Comprehension

About the Directions

1. Exactly where is the station, in her words?
Two blocks north, on the right at the second intersection, around the corner from the pharmacy.
2. What two things does she say may complicate the journey?
He may have to transfer at Fullerton, and there is a detour this week.
3. How does he ask her to repeat, and what does he NOT say?
Would you mind repeating that a little more slowly. He does not apologise for his English.
Listening 1 · A Transit Announcement

The Voice on the Platform

A recorded announcement on an American subway platform. Sound first — no text.

1. Which line is affected, and what is happening to it?
The red line. It is running on a detour because of track work between two stations.
2. What are passengers told to do, and where?
Transfer to the brown line at Fullerton, and use the free shuttle bus from the north exit.
3. What does the announcement say about the fare?
That no additional fare is charged for the transfer or the shuttle.
Listening 2 · Three Years Later

What I Learned About Asking

Somebody who moved to the United States, on asking strangers for help. Sound first — no text.

1. What did she used to do wrong in the first months?
She apologised for her English before asking anything, which made people slow down out of pity and made the exchange longer and worse.
2. What two openers does she say changed everything?
Excuse me, could you tell me, and would you mind repeating that. She says four words at the front buy you the whole conversation.
3. What does she say about directions given in blocks?
That she kept converting blocks into metres in her head and losing the rest of the sentence, until she stopped and learned to count corners.

Teaching notice written for this lesson in the format American transit agencies use. The stations are real names on a real system, but this service change is not; do not plan a journey with it.
1. Turn the line "Transfer at Fullerton" into an indirect question you would ask a member of staff.
Could you tell me where I should transfer? Or: do you know if I have to transfer at Fullerton?
2. According to the sign, does the change cost anything extra, and how long does it add?
No additional fare, and about fifteen minutes.
3. Which two pieces of information on this sign would you check with a person, and why?
The shuttle exit and the frequency, because a sign says every eight minutes and a platform on a Sunday very often does not.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I can stop a stranger and ask for directions so that they stop and answer.
Inside an indirect question I keep the normal word order and drop do, does and did.
I never say could you tell me where is the station, or would you mind to repeat.
I ask for a repetition without apologising for my English.
I can follow directions given in blocks and compass points, and give them myself.
