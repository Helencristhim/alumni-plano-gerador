Podcast
NPR Life Kit -- "How to be a supercommunicator"

Twenty minutes on the thirty seconds this lesson trains: opening with a stranger, asking in a way that gets a real answer, and keeping the exchange alive long enough to get what you came for. American English at normal speed, with the full transcript on the same page.

Tip: write down every opening line the episode quotes. Then say each one out loud until it comes without thinking -- that is the whole skill.

Audiobook
Jane Jacobs -- "The Death and Life of Great American Cities" (audiobook preview)

The book that taught a generation how to look at an American street: the block, the corner, the sidewalk, and why some of them work and others empty out after six. It is this lesson’s vocabulary used as the vocabulary of a whole argument.

Tip: listen to the preview and note every street word you hear -- block, corner, sidewalk, downtown. Then describe your own street in the same words, out loud.

Music
Simon & Garfunkel -- "America"

Three and a half minutes of two people crossing the United States by bus, and a lyric made almost entirely of questions and reported speech: Kathy, I said as we boarded a Greyhound in Pittsburgh. The clearest sung American English there is, at a speed you can follow.

Tip: read the lyrics while you listen and find every question. Rewrite two of them as indirect questions, with a wrapper in front, and say both versions out loud.
