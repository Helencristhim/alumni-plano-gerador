Chapter 3: The Code

If This, Then That

The sentence that turns an opinion into a consequence

Discovery

Which Half Is Not the Future?

"If we wait another year, we will pay three times more."

"If the test fails, we will not launch in March."

"We will lose the account if the price stays at that level."

"If you give me two minutes, I will show you the number."

Both halves talk about the future, but only the red half looks like it. The half after if is in the present, always. And notice: the two halves can change places.

UseFormExample
A real future consequenceIf + present simple, will + naked verbIf we wait another year, we will pay more.
The halves can swapwill + verb ... if + presentWe will lose the account if the price stays there.
The commaonly when if comes firstIf the test fails, we will not launch. / We will not launch if the test fails.
Negativewill not + verb · if ... does notWe will not launch. If the buyer does not answer...
QuestionWhat will happen if...?What will happen if the price stays there?
The trapnever will after ifNot if we will wait. Not if the test will fail.

In one line: after IF, the present. The WILL lives in the other half, and only there.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Two Minutes and One Objection

The pitch, the coach, and the first sentence

Dialogue

The Pitch and the Question

D
Roberto, you have two minutes. Start with what you want us to decide.
R
I want a yes to entering the premium segment next year. Here is my claim: it is the only part of this category that is growing, and it will not stay open.
D
That is a strong claim. What backs it up?
R
Four years of data on one page. Volume flat, money up nineteen percent, and every point of that came from the premium end. That is the evidence, and anybody here can check it.
D
My objection is simple. We are late. Our competitor has been there for two years.
R
We are late, and that is exactly my argument, not an answer to it. If we wait another year, we will pay three times more to enter. I have seen that happen in this company before.
D
And if the shelf life test fails?
R
If the test fails, we will not launch in March, and I will come back to this room with a new date and no excuses. What I am asking for today is the decision, not the calendar.
D
Then give me your takeaway in one sentence, and we will vote.
Comprehension

About Diane

1. What does she ask for first, before any argument?
What he wants the room to decide. She starts from the decision, not from the reasons.
2. What is her objection, and what is her second question?
Her objection is that the company is late, because the competitor has been in the segment for two years. Her second question is what happens if the shelf life test fails.
3. How does she close the conversation?
She asks for the takeaway in one sentence, and says they will vote.
Listening 1 · The Coach

The First Sentence Does Half the Work

Somebody who prepares people for rooms like that one. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What are the three parts of the two-minute structure he teaches?
The claim, the evidence that backs it up, and the consequence of not deciding.
2. What does he say most people do with their first sentence, and what should it be instead?
Most people use it to thank everybody and explain the agenda. It should say what you want the room to decide.
3. What is his advice about the objection you are most afraid of?
Say it yourself, before somebody else does. An objection that you name loses most of its power, and one that catches you does not.
Listening 2 · Two Rooms

The One I Won and the One I Lost

The same person, the same year, two very different results. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What did she do differently in the meeting she won?
She opened with the decision she wanted, and she said the objection herself before anybody else could.
2. What went wrong in the meeting she lost, according to her?
Her content was right, but her tone was defensive. She argued with the first objection instead of answering it, and after that nobody was listening to the evidence.
3. What is the sentence she uses now when somebody objects?
That is a fair point, and here is what happens if we do nothing about it.
Real Document

The One Page You Take Into the Room

DECISION REQUESTED
Enter the premium segment next year · one page, two minutes
The claimthe only part of the category that is growing
Evidence 1volume flat, 4 years
Evidence 2money spent up 19%, same 4 years
Evidence 3all of that growth in the premium end
The objection I expectwe are two years late
If we go nowwe enter at today’s cost
If we wait one more yearwe pay three times more
Takeaway: the door is open and it is closing. That is the whole page.
1. Say the last two lines as full sentences, with if.
If we go now, we will enter at today’s cost. If we wait one more year, we will pay three times more.
2. Why is the objection written on the page, by the person making the case?
Because an objection you name yourself loses most of its power, and one that catches you does not. It also shows the room you have thought about it.
3. There are three pieces of evidence and one claim. Which one would you cut if you had ninety seconds instead of two minutes?
Any one of the first two, because the third one carries the argument on its own: all the growth is in the premium end. The claim and the last line never come out.
