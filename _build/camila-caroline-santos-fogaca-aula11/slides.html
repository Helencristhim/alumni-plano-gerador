Chapter 3: The Manager Who Needs Someone

An Analyst Leaves, the Freeze Stays

A conversation between the HRBP and a local manager, line by line

Dialogue

The Same Chair, a Different Request

D
Camila, I just heard about the freeze. One of my collections analysts resigns at the end of the month. Can I replace her?
C
As a rule, no. We don't open positions until the end of March, and our headcount stays where it is.
D
But I'm not asking for a new position. I need somebody in the same chair.
C
I understand. A backfill is different, and it can be an exception, but only for a critical role.
D
How do I know if her role is critical?
C
A role is critical when the business stops without it. What happens to collections if nobody does her work for three months?
D
The team covers part of it. But the late payments from the dealers grow every week.
C
Then send me the numbers. The committee decides on a case-by-case basis, and numbers help more than urgency.
D
And while we wait?
C
For the time being, split her work between two people and hold off on the job posting. I take your case to the committee on Thursday.

0:000:00

It starts on the first of November and ends at the end of March.
2. Why does the group freeze hiring, according to Claire?
It is part of the cost control plan for next year.
3. What is a critical role, as a rule?
A position that the regulator requires, or a position that stops a business line if it stays empty.

0:000:00

Chapter 5: The Code

How a Rule Sounds in English

The present simple for the rule, and for its exception

Grammar Discovery

What Makes These Sound Like Rules?

"We don't open new positions during the freeze."

"Every request goes to the committee on Thursday."

"We only approve a backfill when the role is critical."

"Temporary contracts wait unless the role is critical."

None of these sentences describes something happening at this moment. What do the colored words have in common, and what do the green and purple ones add?

FormWhat it doesExample
we don't + verbwhat does not happen while the rule is validWe don't open new positions.
every / each + verb with -sa rule for all the cases, one by oneEvery backfill needs approval.
only ... when / ifthe condition for the exceptionWe only approve it when the role is critical.
unlessexcept ifContracts wait unless the role is critical.
Watch outa rule is not happening now, it is true every day: we don't open, never we are not opening. And the -s: the freeze applies, the committee decides.

Sound Check

Say It Clearly

H and R · point 1 of 14

Why this one is hard for a Brazilian speaker

In Portuguese: The written H has no sound, and the R at the start of a word comes from the throat.

In English: The H is air before the vowel. The R comes from the tongue, pulled back, and the throat stays quiet.

If it slips: The R from the throat sounds like an English H, so "role" comes out close to "hole", and the missing H turns "hold" into "old". In a conversation about a freeze, those are the words that carry the decision.
