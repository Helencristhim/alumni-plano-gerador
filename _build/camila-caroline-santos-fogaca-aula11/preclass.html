Lesson 11 -- Pre-class

What a Hiring Freeze Is, and What It Changes

An announcement of a hiring freeze, with the rule and the exceptions. Key words: hiring freeze, headcount, backfill, critical role, exception, on a case-by-case basis, as a rule. Structure: the present simple for rules and exceptions -- we don't open, every request goes, we only approve when, unless.

Stage 1: Start with the Text

Reading

Start with the text. Read it once before you look at anything else: the language of this lesson comes out of it.

To all managers in South America. Subject: hiring freeze, November to March.

From the first of November until the end of March, the group does not open new positions in any of its companies. The freeze is part of the cost control plan for next year, and it applies in every country where the group works. Our headcount stays where it is today: no new positions, no new teams, and no temporary contracts without approval.

The freeze does not stop the business. When a person leaves a critical role, the manager can ask for a backfill. A backfill is not a new position: it replaces somebody who is no longer there. A role is critical when the regulator requires it, or when the business line stops without it. A backfill for a critical role is the main exception to the freeze.

Every request goes to the regional committee, which meets on Thursdays. The committee looks at each request on a case-by-case basis: there is no automatic answer, and the same role can be critical in one company and not in another. As a rule, the committee only approves a backfill when the manager shows what stops if the position stays empty.

If you have a vacancy that is not critical, please put it on hold until April. Talk to your HR Business Partner before you post anything, and hold off on interviews for the time being.

2. What is the difference between a backfill and a new position?

Stage 2: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

The words you have just met in the announcement, one by one. Listen to each expression and read it in context: this is the vocabulary of every conversation about the freeze.

hiring freeze -- a period when the company stops opening new positions
"The hiring freeze starts on the first of November."
headcount -- the number of people who work in a team or a company
"Our headcount stays the same until the end of March."
backfill -- the person who replaces an employee who leaves
"A backfill is not a new position: somebody left."
critical role -- a position that the business cannot leave empty
"The fleet controller is a critical role in the rental company."
exception -- a case that does not follow the general rule
"A backfill for a critical role is an exception."
on a case-by-case basis -- looking at each request on its own, not with one answer for all
"The committee decides on a case-by-case basis."
as a rule -- in most cases, as the normal practice
"As a rule, we don't approve temporary contracts during the freeze."

Stage 4: Grammar Tip -- The present simple for rules and exceptions

GRAMMAR

A rule is true every day, for everybody, so English says it in the present simple. The exception uses the same tense, with only, when or unless.

FormWhat it doesExample
we don't + verbsays what does not happen during the rule"We don't open new positions during the freeze."
every / each + verb with -sa rule for all the cases, one by one"Every request goes to the committee."
only ... when / ifthe condition for an exception"We only approve a backfill when the role is critical."
unlessexcept if"Temporary contracts wait unless the role is critical."
Watch outthe -s after every, each, the freeze and the committee: "the freeze applies", "every backfill needs approval". And a rule never uses the -ing form: "we don't open", not "we are not opening".

Say the rule first, in one sentence. Then say the exception. A manager who hears the rule clearly accepts the exception more easily.
