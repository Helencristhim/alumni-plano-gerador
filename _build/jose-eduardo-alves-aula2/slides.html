Chapter 4: The Code

The Letter Everybody Drops

One verb, one person, one -s

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"She specializes in commodity contracts."

"Rafael handles the smaller deals on his own."

"She does not go to court."

"Does he review the difficult clauses?"

In the first two, the red verb carries an extra letter. In the last two, that letter is gone — and the blue word carries it instead.

JobFormExample
He, she or it, positiveverb + -sShe reviews every clause.
Negativedoes not + bare verbShe does not go to court.
QuestionDoes + subject + bare verbDoes he handle the big deals?
Spelling-ch, -sh, -s, -x take -es; consonant + y becomes -ieswatch → watches · study → studies
Two irregular oneshave → has · do → doesThe firm has seven lawyers.
How oftenalways, usually, often, rarely, never — before the verbHer routine rarely changes.
The trapnever two -s in the same sentenceNot does she reviews. Say does she review.

In one line: the -s belongs to the sentence, not to the verb — and does takes it whenever does turns up.

Listening 1 · A Commodity Lawyer

What a Commodity Lawyer Does All Day

A partner describing the work of her office. Sound first — no text.

1. Who reads what, in her office?
The associate reads the first version, she reads the difficult clauses, and the client reads the one-page summary.
2. What does her colleague say a contract is?
A machine for disagreeing politely — and if you build it properly, nobody ever has to use it.
3. Why does the firm charge a second fee when a dispute starts?
Because by then the machine has failed, and the work becomes a different kind of work.
Listening 2 · Business Podcast

The Cargo That Changes Owner at Sea

How a grain cargo is sold three times before it arrives. Sound first — no text.

1. What surprises people about the grain trade, according to the speaker?
The cargo often changes owner three or four times while the ship is still at sea.
2. What does the lawyer in this market never ask, and what does he ask instead?
He never asks what the product looks like. He asks who owns it today, what the contract says about a late ship, and which country decides a disagreement.
3. Which of those questions does he say costs money?
The last one — which country decides if the two sides disagree — when nobody answers it in advance.
