Numbers That Move People

One figure, one pause, and the context that makes it matter. Key words: fiscal year, compound annual growth rate, uptake, addressable market, run rate, headwinds, tailwinds, inflection point, to scale up, to quantify. Structure: fronted adverbials and despite versus although for presenting data.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A fiscal year -- the twelve-month period a company uses for its accounts and its budget
"Our fiscal year ends in December, so the budget for new projects is decided in October."
Compound annual growth rate -- the average growth per year over several years, as if the growth were the same every year
"Over the last three years, the market has grown at a compound annual growth rate of about fourteen percent."
Uptake -- how many people or companies start using a new product or service
"Uptake was slow in the first year, but now most of our corporate clients use it."
An addressable market -- all the customers who could possibly buy a product, if everybody bought it
"The addressable market is every Brazilian company that sells abroad."
A run rate -- today's pace of business, projected over a whole year
"At this run rate, the pilot will process almost half a billion dollars this year."
Headwinds -- conditions that make growth or progress more difficult
"A strong dollar and high interest rates were the two biggest headwinds this year."
Tailwinds -- conditions that help growth or progress, often from outside the company
"Instant payments at home are a tailwind for every Brazilian bank that wants to go cross-border."
An inflection point -- the moment when a trend changes direction or starts to move much faster
"When the largest banks started their pilots, the market reached an inflection point."
To scale up -- to increase the size of an operation, a project or a team
"If the pilot works in one corridor, we will scale up to three next year."
To quantify -- to measure something and express it as a number
"Everybody says the opportunity is big. My job is to quantify it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On a slide, a number can wait. In a spoken presentation, it cannot. The listener hears it once, and if they do not know what the number is about, it is gone before they can place it. That is why good speakers give the frame first, and the number second.

The frame goes at the front of the sentence, followed by a short pause where a written text would have a comma. It can be a time period: Over the last three years, the market has grown about fourteen percent a year. It can be a reference point: As of the third quarter, the run rate is two billion dollars. Notice the tense after over the last and since: it is the present perfect, because the period reaches today.

The frame can also be a contrast, and here there are two words that do the same job with different grammar. Despite (or in spite of) takes a noun: Despite the headwinds, uptake has more than doubled. Although takes a full sentence, with a subject and a verb: Although the market is young, the addressable market is enormous.

Put the contrast first and the listener hears the problem before the good news, which is exactly what an honest speaker wants. And when the number finally arrives, it has somewhere to land.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Fronted Adverbials, Despite and Although

GRAMMAR

Frame first, comma, then the number.

FrameFormExample
Time periodOver the last / In the last + period · Since + year, + present perfectOver the last three years, the market has grown fourteen percent a year.
Reference pointAs of + date or quarter,As of the third quarter, the run rate is two billion dollars.
Contrast + nounDespite / In spite of + noun,Despite the headwinds, uptake has doubled.
Contrast + sentenceAlthough / Even though + subject + verb,Although the market is young, it is enormous.
Negativethe frame does not change; the negative is in the main verbDespite the pilot, uptake has not grown as fast as we hoped.
Questionframe first, then the questionOver the last year, how much has the run rate grown?

Never: despite the market is young · although the headwinds · in the last three years it grew · the number I want to focus is
