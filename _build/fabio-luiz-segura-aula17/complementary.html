Hans Rosling — “The best stats you've ever seen” (TED)

One of the most famous data presentations ever given. A statistician turns decades of numbers about health and income into a story the audience can follow, with a frame before every figure and a comparison after it.

Tip: pause the video after two numbers he presents. Write down what he says before each number and what he says after it.

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart (Stanford GSB) — “Simple Is a Superpower: How to Communicate Any Idea to Any Audience”

A communication coach who has worked with many executives explains why simple, direct messages get things done, and how to cut a complex idea down to what the listener actually needs. The same principle applies to a slide full of numbers.

Tip: write down one technique from the episode. Then use it to reduce your own pilot slide to one sentence and one number.

Video
Hans Rosling — “200 Countries, 200 Years, 4 Minutes” (BBC, YouTube)

Two hundred years of economic data in four minutes, told with frames like "by 1948" and "over the last fifty years" before every change. A short, perfect model of context before the number.

Tip: count how many sentences start with a time frame. Then say one sentence about Brazil in the same style.
