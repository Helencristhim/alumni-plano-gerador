Chapter 3: The Frame First

Context Before the Number

What goes at the front of the sentence, and why

Grammar Discovery

Four Frames for One Number

"Over the last three years, the market has grown about fourteen percent a year."

"As of the third quarter, the run rate is two billion dollars."

"Despite the headwinds, uptake has more than doubled."

"Although the market is young, the addressable market is enormous."

Where are the red words in each sentence, and what do they give the listener before the number? Now compare despite and although: what comes after each one?

FrameForm (at the front, then a comma)Example
Time periodOver the last / In the last + period · Since + yearOver the last three years, the market has grown...
Reference pointAs of + date or quarterAs of the third quarter, the run rate is...
Contrast + nounDespite / In spite of + noun (or verb-ing)Despite the headwinds, uptake has doubled.
Contrast + clauseAlthough / Even though + subject + verbAlthough the market is young, it is enormous.
Tense after the frameover the last / since + present perfectNot In the last three years it grew. Say it has grown.
The trapno clause after despite · no noun alone after althoughNot despite the market is young. Say although the market is young.

In one line: give the frame first, then the number — the listener knows where to put it before it arrives.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Numbers Under Pressure

A skeptical banker, an analyst and an investor

Dialogue

Is That Audited?

C
Fabio, I saw your slide on Brazil. Fourteen percent a year sounds almost too good. Is that audited?
F
Fair question. That is the compound annual growth rate over the last three years, and it comes from public data, not from our own estimates.
C
Fine. But growth from a small base is easy. How big is the addressable market, really?
F
Let me put that in context for a second. It is every company in Brazil that sells abroad, and most of them still pay the old way. So uptake is low, and that is exactly the opportunity.
C
And the headwinds? The currency, the regulation, the interest rates?
F
They are real. Despite the headwinds, though, the run rate of our pilot has doubled since January. The number I want to focus on is that one.
C
Doubled from what? Is that volume or value?
F
Value. As of last month, the pilot processes about forty million dollars a month. What that growth rate tells us is that we are close to an inflection point.
C
All right, I'm listening. If you scale up, what would you need from a partner like us?
F
One corridor and one fiscal year. If we can quantify the results together by December, the decision will be easy for both of us.
Comprehension

About Claire

1. What is Claire's first doubt about the fourteen percent?
She says it sounds almost too good, and asks if it is audited.
2. Why does Claire say the growth could be easy, and what does she ask next?
Because growth from a small base is easy. She asks how big the addressable market really is.
3. What two things does Claire ask about the pilot, and what does she ask at the end?
Doubled from what, and whether it is volume or value. At the end, she asks what they would need from a partner like her bank if they scale up.
Listening 1 · The Briefing

Three Numbers Overnight

An analyst opens a morning briefing. Sound first — no text.

1. How much did cross-border volume grow last fiscal year, and what was the main headwind?
About eleven percent, slower than the year before. The main headwind was the strong dollar.
2. Why does the growth of instant cross-border products look spectacular, and why is it still modest?
Uptake more than doubled, but from a very small base, so the absolute numbers are still modest.
3. What does he call the real inflection point, and what usually happens next?
Three of the five largest banks in the region running pilots. When the large banks scale up, the rest of the market usually follows within two years.
Listening 2 · The Investor

Compared to What?

An investor explains what she writes down in a pitch. Sound first — no text.

1. What number does she NOT write down, and why?
The addressable market, because every market looks enormous on a slide.
2. What does she write down instead?
The run rate, and how it has changed over the last six months.
3. What makes her stop listening, and what does a number need for her to understand it?
A speaker who says there are no headwinds. A number needs a comparison, for example twice the growth of the economy.
