One Question

Say It With a Number in It

Think about the other people applying to your program this year. In one sentence: what do almost all of them have that you do not?

Then the second one: what do almost none of them have that you do? Say it without the word only.

Chapter 4: The Code

When Does “Of the” Appear?

Quantifiers, and the difference one article makes

Grammar Discovery

Which Group Are You Talking About?

Most candidates prepare the wrong thing.

Most of the files in your pool come from consulting and banking.

Few candidates name a cost. A few of them name two.

Hardly any file gets an argument, and hardly any of them reach a third reader.

Everything in blue talks about the kind in general, and takes the plain noun: no the, no of. Everything in red talks about a group we have already identified — the files in your pool, them — and the group is what pulls in of the, of them, of your.

JobFormExample
The kind in generalmost / many / few / all + plain plural nounMost candidates prepare the wrong thing.
A group we have identifiedmost of / many of / few of + the, this, your, themMost of the files in your pool come from banking.
Almost none, and it is a problemfew (countable) · little (uncountable)Few candidates name a cost.
Some, and that is gooda few · a littleA few of them name two.
Almost none, in speechhardly any + noun · hardly anybody — never with no or nobodyHardly any file gets an argument.
A small countable numbera handful of + plural nounA handful of files go to a third reader.
Nearly everythingvirtually all / almost all (+ of the, only if the group is defined)Virtually all applicants clear the bar on paper.
Zero, formallynone of + the group · not one ofNone of the six criteria is about your title.

In one line: no article and no of when you mean the kind; of the only when the group is already on the table.

The Scored Rubric

READER SHEET — F. GATTAZ

Clarity of goals5 / 10 — below threshold

Communication in English6 / 10
Academic readiness7 / 10
Reader’s note: the two of us are within one point on five of the six lines. Only the goals answer is below the threshold: virtually all of it is about the company and hardly any of it about the two years in the program. It is offset by line four — none of the other files in this pool is run by somebody who signs off on a concrete pour at six in the morning. Recommended for interview, with one caveat: ask him about the goals answer first.
1. On how many of the six lines are you above the threshold, and which one is not?
Five of the six. Only clarity of goals, at five out of ten, is below the threshold of six.
2. What exactly is wrong with the goals answer, according to the note?
The proportion. Virtually all of it is about the company and hardly any of it is about the two years in the program.
3. What offsets the low score, and why does the reader say it works?
Line four, what you would bring to the class, at nine out of ten. None of the other files in the pool is run by somebody who signs off on a concrete pour at six in the morning, so it is the one thing the pool does not have.
Listening 1 · A Committee Member

Six Lines, and Not One Total

A reader on what the sheet does and what it refuses to do. Sound first — no text.

1. What happens to your file before the committee ever meets in a room?
Two readers score it separately against a sheet with six criteria and a number under each one, and most of the differences between the two of them are half a point.
2. What does she say candidates get wrong about the six lines?
That they are equal and added up. Progression and scope of responsibility carry more weight than anything else, the test score carries the least, and she weighs one line against another.
3. Which files does she say worry her, and why?
The ones with no low number anywhere. Almost all of them look the same — good grades, good employer, good English — and she cannot remember any of them by Friday.
Listening 2 · Rejected, Then Admitted

The One Paragraph That Changed

A candidate on the criterion he ignored the first time. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say did not change between the first application and the second?
Most of the file. He had a better job by then, but he does not think the job is what changed the outcome.
2. What was wrong with his first answer about what he would bring to the class?
He answered it with ambition and wrote about what he wanted to learn. Hardly any of that is useful to a committee, because every file in the pool wants to learn.
3. What did he write the second time, and why does he think it worked?
The thing none of the others had — rebuilding a factory line in a country whose language he did not speak, including the two months when it nearly failed. It told them what the class would lose if he were not in it.
