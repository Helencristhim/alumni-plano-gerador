Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart (Stanford GSB) -- "Want To Be Memorable? Choose Your Words Carefully"

Ada Aka, a marketing professor at Stanford, on her research into which words people actually remember: concrete, specific and slightly informal ones beat abstract ones every time. It is the research behind the last line of today's text -- the file that is remembered is the one a reader can repeat in a sentence. Free, with the full transcript on the page and a link to the audio.

Tip: listen first, transcript second. Then take the four sentences you built about the six criteria and replace every abstract word in them with a concrete one.

TED -- Regina Hartley, "Why the best hire might not have the perfect resume" (10 min)

A human resources executive on the two files that land on her desk: the one where everything lines up, and the one with a difficult road behind it. Her argument is exactly the second paragraph of today's text -- the well-rounded file is the easiest to score and the hardest to remember -- and she makes it with numbers instead of adjectives.

Tip: watch it once with subtitles off. Then write three sentences about your own file using "most of the", "hardly any" and "none of", and say them out loud.
