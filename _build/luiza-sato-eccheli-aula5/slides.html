Chapter 3: The Code

The Year That Did Not Happen (Yet)

Four sentences about an exchange: one imagined, one regretted, one mixed

Grammar Discovery

Imagined, or Regretted?

"If I were in London now, I would go to office hours in the first week."

"If he had budgeted, he would not have spent his stipend in a month."

"If I had chosen a lighter course load, I would be less tired now."

"If she were more organized, she would not have fallen behind last month."

All four sentences are about things that are not real. Which ones imagine the present, which ones imagine the past, and which two mix a past cause with a present result, or a present fact with a past result? Look at the blue and red forms.

JobFormExample
Imagine the present or the futureif + past, would + base verbIf I were there, I would go to office hours.
Imagine a different past (regret)if + had + participle, would have + participleIf he had budgeted, he would not have spent it all.
A past cause, a present resultif + had + participle, would + base verbIf I had chosen fewer classes, I would be less tired now.
A present fact, a past resultif + past, would have + participleIf she were organized, she would not have fallen behind.
Less certaincould / might in place of wouldIf I had asked, I might have gotten a better room.
With beIf I were, formal and correct for every personIf I were you, I would apply now.
The trapnever would in the if-clauseNot If I would have known. Say If I had known.

In one line: Move each clause back in time to show it is not real: past for the present, had + participle for the past, and never would after if.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Coffee With Somebody Who Went

One conversation, two recordings, one welcome sheet

Dialogue

What Theo Would Do Differently

T
Luiza! So you are really going to apply. Good. Ask me anything, because nobody told me the useful things.
L
Okay. If you were starting your semester in London again tomorrow, what would you do first?
T
I would sort out my housing before anything else. I waited, and I ended up in a tiny room with a roommate who never slept.
L
That sounds terrible. Did it take you long to settle in?
T
About a month. The culture shock was not the language, it was the rhythm. Dinner at six, pubs closed early, and I was homesick every Sunday.
L
And the classes? My brother said the course load was heavier than at GV.
T
He is right. If I had gone to office hours in the first week, I would not have fallen behind in two classes.
L
And money? The stipend does not look very generous.
T
It is not. If I had learned to budget earlier, I would still have some savings now. Honestly, I spent too much in the first month.
L
Then I will learn from your mistakes. When you look back on it, was it worth it?
T
Completely. Just make the most of it, and take one elective that has nothing to do with law.
Comprehension

About Theo

1. What would Theo do first if he started his semester again?
He would sort out his housing before anything else.
2. What was the culture shock for Theo, and when did he feel homesick?
The rhythm, not the language: dinner at six and pubs closing early. He felt homesick every Sunday.
3. Why did Theo fall behind, according to him?
Because he did not go to office hours in the first week.
4. What is his final advice about electives?
To take one elective that has nothing to do with law.
Listening 1 · A Podcast

What I Wish I Had Known

A student who came back from an exchange year. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say she would not change, and what would she change?
She would not change the city or the university. She would change the first three weeks.
2. What does she say would have happened if she had joined a club in the first week?
She would have met people much faster.
3. Why did she not budget, and what is her advice for students who feel homesick?
The stipend looked like a lot in her currency. Her advice is to go to one event instead of hiding in your room.
Listening 2 · A Voicemail

The Housing Office Calls

A message from a host university. Sound first — no text.

1. How are rooms on campus allocated, and what happened last year?
In order of application. Last year every room was taken by the end of May.
2. What happens if a student applies too late, and how much less does a shared room cost?
They have to find private housing, usually more expensive and further away. A shared room costs about thirty percent less.
3. What does he say about the stipend, and when are the office hours?
It will not arrive before the student, so they must pay the first month of rent themselves. Office hours are Monday to Thursday, ten to four.
