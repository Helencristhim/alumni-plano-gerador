Chapter 3: The Code

I Play, Do You?

Talking about your routine

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Have in Common?

"I play football every week."

"I don't watch the games at home."

"Do you like football?"

"Yes, I do. And you?"

Look at the orange words. What is new in the negative and in the question?

JobFormExample
RoutineI / you + verbI play football. You go to the gym.
NegativeI / you + don't + verbI don't watch the games at home.
QuestionDo + you + verb...?Do you like football?
Short answersYes, I do. / No, I don't.Do you play? Yes, I do.
How oftenevery week / on the weekendI go to the gym every day.
The trapno "Do" = not a questionNot You like football? Say Do you like football?

In one line: for your routine, I play; for the negative, I don't play; for the question, Do you play?

Chapter 4: At the Bar

At the Bar

One conversation, two recordings, one reservation

Dialogue

Do You Like Football?

E
Hi! Is this seat free? I don't know anybody here.
C
Yes, it is. I am Camillo. I am from Brazil.
E
Nice to meet you. I'm Emma, from Chicago. Do you like football, Camillo?
C
Yes, I do. I play every week with my friends.
E
Cool! I don't play, but I'm a big fan. Who is your team?
C
A team from Brazil. And on the weekend, I hang out with my friends at a bar, like this one.
E
Me too! I go to the gym in the morning and watch the match at night.
C
Do you go to the gym every day?
E
No, I don't. Three times a week. Oh, look! What a goal!
Comprehension

About Emma

1. Where is Emma from?
From Chicago.
2. Does Emma play football?
No. She is a fan, but she doesn't play.
3. When does Emma go to the gym?
In the morning, three times a week.

Listening 2 · A Football Routine

Diego Plays Too

A Brazilian man talks about football in his week. Sound first — no text.

1. When does Diego play football?
Every Tuesday night.
2. Who does he play with?
With his friends from work.
3. Why doesn't he watch the matches at home?
Because he likes the noise and the people at the bar.
