Lesson 10 -- Pre-class

The Words That Buy You Time -- Block 2 Wrap-Up

This one closes block 2 and there is no new grammar in it. A fluent pause is not a shorter pause — it is a pause with sound in it. Key words: anyway, by the way, actually, I mean, the thing is, let me think, sort of, in the end, that reminds me, go on. Structure: nothing new — the four codes of lessons 6 to 9 side by side, plus the small words that stop your turn from being taken.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Anyway -- you come back to what you were saying before
"Anyway, as I was saying, we finished on Friday."
By the way -- you add something that does not belong where you are
"By the way, did you get my message on Tuesday?"
Actually -- you correct something politely, without saying somebody was wrong
"Actually, it was in March, not in May."
I mean -- you are going to say the same thing again, better
"It was bad. I mean, it was not bad exactly. It was long."
The thing is -- you are about to name the real problem
"The thing is, I had two things on Saturday and neither of them moved."
Let me think -- you take a second out loud, instead of going silent
"Let me think. Sort of a quiet one, actually."
Sort of -- nearly, but not exactly -- and you do not want to be precise
"It was sort of a holiday, but I worked on the Monday."
In the end -- after everything that happened, this is where it stopped
"In the end I stayed home and I did not regret it once."
That reminds me -- their story brings back one of yours, and you say so
"That reminds me of something that happened to me in January."
Go on -- you tell somebody to keep talking
"Go on. What happened after that?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

There is nothing new to learn in this lesson, and that is the point of it. In four weeks you have collected four codes: two things side by side (noisier than, the worst), how much there is of something (too many, not enough), what was already going on when something happened (I was waiting when she called), and what somebody should do about it (you should go, you might not like it). Four codes, four weeks. None of them is the reason a sentence stops halfway.

A sentence stops because the word does not come, and then something worse happens: you go quiet. Two seconds of silence is all it takes for the other person to decide your turn is over. So the repair is not grammar and it is not vocabulary — it is sound. Let me think. The thing is. Sort of. None of those means very much, and that is exactly why they work: they are a hand held up that says, I am still here, do not start. Actually corrects without accusing anybody. I mean gives you a second attempt at a sentence you did not like. Anyway brings you back after you have wandered off, and by the way is how you wander off on purpose.

The last two are the ones people forget, and they are the most useful in a long conversation. Go on keeps the other person talking, which costs you nothing and buys you the time to build what you want to say next. That reminds me is how you take the turn back when you are ready, by hooking your story onto theirs. Put them together and you have the whole shape: fill the pause, repair without stopping, come back, hand it over, take it back. And in the end, the habit under all ten lessons is still the same one — you finish, and you hand the question back.

1. According to the text, why does a sentence stop halfway?
A Because the grammar is wrong.
B Because the word does not come, and then you go quiet — and two seconds of silence ends your turn.
C Because you have not learned enough tenses.
2. Why does the text say these small words work?
A Precisely because they mean almost nothing — they are sound that says I am still here.
B Because they are the most important words in English.
C Because they replace the verb in the sentence.
3. What is the difference between go on and that reminds me?
A They both mean the same thing.
B Go on corrects a mistake and that reminds me asks a question.
C Go on keeps the other person talking; that reminds me is how you take the turn back.

A "No, you are wrong. It was in March."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

If the word does not come, say one of this week’s words out loud and keep going.
