Chapter 3: In, On, At

Three Small Words

in the morning · on Monday · at nine

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The flight is at ten fifteen."

"We arrive on October second."

"The Louvre opens in the morning."

"I have a meeting at nine, on Monday, in October."

WordUse it forExample
ata clock timeat six o'clock · at noon · at night
ona day or a dateon Monday · on October fifth
ina part of the day, a month, a yearin the morning · in October · in 2026

Small time → at. One day → on. Big time → in. One exception to keep: at night.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

✗ WRONG

"I have a meeting in Monday."

✓ RIGHT

"I have a meeting on Monday."

✗ WRONG

"The flight is in six in the morning."

✓ RIGHT

"The flight is at six in the morning."

✗ WRONG

"What hours is it?"

✓ RIGHT

"What time is it?"

Portuguese uses em for all of them. English needs three different words — that is why this one takes time.

Dialogue

The Hotel Desk, 8 a.m.

A
Good morning. Can I help you with your day?
T
Good morning. We go to Disneyland Paris tomorrow. What time does it open?
A
It opens at half past nine in the morning, every day.
T
And the first train? What time is it?
A
The first train is at seven o'clock. The station is very quiet in the morning.
T
Perfect. My daughter is fifteen, and this trip is for her.
A
Then arrive early. At half past nine there are only two or three people in the line.
T
We are there on October fifth. Is the park open at night?
A
Yes. It closes at eleven o'clock at night. And the parade is at five in the afternoon.
T
Thank you. One more question: what time is breakfast here?
A
From half past six to ten. Your family can eat before the train.
