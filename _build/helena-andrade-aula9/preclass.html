Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

Precision Reading -- Hedging Language and the Words an Investigation Uses to Be Careful

The words of an official inquiry and the hedges that show if a text states or only suggests. Key words: negligence, testimony, investigation, finding, recommendation, contributing factor, root cause, corrective action, witness, sequence of events. Structure: hedging and would rather, had better, may as well (reading only).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read it inside a real sentence. These are the words the exam text will use.

Negligence -- the legal failure to take the care that a normal careful person takes
"A report rarely says that a person was negligent."
Testimony -- what a person says as evidence, true or not
"Testimony is what a person said, not what happened."
Investigation -- the official inquiry that finds out what happened and why
"The investigation started two days after the accident."
Finding -- a conclusion that the inquiry is sure about from the evidence
"Only the first sentence is a finding."
Recommendation -- the action that the inquiry asks somebody to take in the future
"The report makes one recommendation to the company."
Contributing factor -- something that helped to cause the event, but was not the main reason
"Fatigue was a contributing factor."
Root cause -- the main reason, without which the event would not happen
"The root cause was a missing procedure."
Corrective action -- the change made after the event to stop it happening again
"The company agreed a corrective action."
Witness -- a person who saw or heard the event
"The witness said that he had closed the valve."
Sequence of events -- the list of what happened, in the order it happened
"The report gives the sequence of events minute by minute."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Hedges and Advice (Reading)

GRAMMAR

A hedge like it would appear that or it seems that shows that the writer is not sure. Would rather is a preference, had better is strong advice and may as well means there is no reason not to. You only need to read them and see how sure the writer is.

FormWhat it doesExample
it would appear that / it seems thata hedge: the evidence points to it, but it is not certainIt would appear that the valve was closed. = Maybe the valve was closed.
more often than notusually, but not alwaysMore often than not, a root cause is first a contributing factor.
would rather X than Yprefer X to YInvestigators would rather say too little than too much.
had better + verbstrong advice. It is not the past of haveyou had better write both = you should write both
may as well + verbthere is no reason not to do ityou may as well test it first
The reading trapa hedge changes the claim. The text states and the text suggests are different exam answers, and the hedge shows which one is right.

You do not need to write these structures. You need to see them and say: is the writer sure, or only suggesting?
