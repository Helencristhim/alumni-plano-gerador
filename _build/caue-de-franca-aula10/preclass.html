Lesson 10 -- Pre-class

English on Your CV -- It Is Said That ...

English in the job market, from the job posting to the shortlist. Key words: a job posting, a recruiter, proficient, fluent, a soft skill, to land a job, a cover letter, to screen, a shortlist, an edge, to brush up on, a track record. Structure: the reporting passive.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A job posting -- an advertisement for a job, with the tasks and the requirements
"The job posting asks for advanced English."
A recruiter -- a person whose job is to find people for companies
"A recruiter called me about a position in Campinas."
Proficient -- very good at something because you have practiced it
"She is proficient in English and Spanish."
Fluent -- able to speak a language easily, without stopping to think
"Fluent doesn’t mean perfect."
A soft skill -- a personal ability, like communication or teamwork, not a technical one
"Communication is the soft skill companies ask for most."
To land a job -- to succeed in getting a job, often after a lot of effort
"He landed a job at a multinational last year."
A cover letter -- a short letter you send with your CV to explain why you want the job
"Write a short cover letter in English."
To screen -- to check candidates or documents to decide which ones continue
"Many CVs are screened by software."
A shortlist -- a small group of the best candidates, chosen from a longer list
"I made the shortlist for the interview."
An edge -- an advantage over other people
"Good English gives you an edge."
To brush up on -- to practice something again because you are out of practice
"I need to brush up on my business English."
A track record -- all the things someone has done before, which show how good they are
"She has a strong track record in sales."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

It is often said that English opens doors. In many companies, fluent speakers are believed to have an edge. But today many CVs are screened by software before a recruiter reads them.

When a job posting asks for advanced English, candidates are expected to work in English. Recruiters prefer honest CVs: proficient in reading, brushing up on speaking, with a track record to show it.

To make the shortlist, send a clear cover letter. In the interview, soft skills are tested too. A certificate can help you land a job, but in the end people hire people they can talk to.

1. Why does the text say “CVs are screened by software”?
A Because the action is more important than who does it, and by tells us who.
B Because software is a person, and by is used only for people in English.
C Because are screened is the past tense of screen, used in all formal texts.
2. What do recruiters prefer, according to the text?
A CVs that say fluent for every skill, because it is the word that software looks for.
B Honest CVs with proof, like a track record.
C CVs with no information about English, so they can test it in the interview.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “English is said that opens doors.”
B “English is said to open doors.”
C “It is said English to open doors in many companies today.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Reporting Passive -- It Is Said That ...

GRAMMAR

News, reports and job postings use the passive when the person who says, believes or does something is not important. Use it is said that + sentence, or a subject + is said / believed / expected + to + verb.

JobFormExample
Report what people sayIt is said / believed / expected + that + sentenceIt is often said that English opens doors.
With a person or thing as subjectsubject + is said / believed / expected + to + verbFluent speakers are believed to have an edge.
About the pastsubject + is said + to have + past participleHe is said to have worked in London.
The normal passivebe + past participleCVs are screened. The interview is watched.
Who did itby + agent, only when it mattersscreened by software
The trapwith a subject, use to, not thatNot English is said that opens doors. Say English is said to open doors.

Never: English is said that opens doors · Candidates are expected present · screened from software · brush up my English

Quiz

You want to report a common opinion. You say:
A "It is often said that English opens doors."

You want to say what the company expects from candidates. You say:
A "Candidates are expected present in English to the US team."
B "Candidates are expected to present in English."
C "Candidates expect to be presented in English by the company."

A "I’m fluent in everything, but I need to brush up on my reading."
