Chapter 4: The Code

It Is Said That ...

The passive that reports

Grammar Discovery

Who Says It?

"It is often said that English opens doors."

"Fluent speakers are believed to have an edge."

"Candidates are expected to work in English, not just to read it."

"Many CVs are screened by software before a recruiter reads them."

Look at the red words: is said, are believed, are expected, are screened. Who says, who believes, who expects? We do not know, and it does not matter: many people. Now look at the blue words: after are believed and are expected comes to + verb. And in sentence 4, by tells us who does the action.

JobFormExample
Report what people sayIt is said / believed / expected + that + sentenceIt is often said that English opens doors.
With a person or thing as subjectsubject + is said / believed / expected + to + verbFluent speakers are believed to have an edge.
About the pastsubject + is said + to have + past participleHe is said to have worked in London.
The normal passivebe + past participleCVs are screened. The interview is watched.
Who did itby + agent, only when it mattersscreened by software
The trapwith a subject, use to, not thatNot English is said that opens doors. Say English is said to open doors.

In one line: it is said that + sentence, or subject + is said to + verb: two ways to report what many people say.

Listening 1 · A Recruiter

Before Anyone Reads It

A recruiter at a large company explains what happens to a CV after you send it. Sound first — no text.

1. What happens to most CVs first?
They are screened by software, looking for the words in the job posting.
2. How long does she spend on a CV that makes it through?
About thirty seconds at first.
3. What does she do with candidates who say they are fluent?
She calls them and starts the conversation in English, without warning.
Listening 2 · A Careers Coach

Be Honest About Your English

A British careers coach gives advice on how to describe your English on a CV. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the biggest mistake he sees on CVs?
People write fluent when they are not.
2. What does he suggest instead?
Describe each skill separately and honestly, with proof like a certificate or experience.
3. Why does honesty help, according to him?
Because the interview will show the truth anyway, and recruiters trust honest candidates.
Listening 3 · A Hiring Manager

What I Really Look For

An American hiring manager talks about the people she hires for her international team. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she look at first, after the CV?
The track record: what the person has actually done.
2. Which soft skill does she value most?
Being able to say I don’t know, and ask a good question.
3. What does she say about accents?
An accent is not a problem; being clear and natural is what matters.
