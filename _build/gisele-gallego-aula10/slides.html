Chapter 3: The Code

Too Much, Not Enough

too · enough · so ... that

Grammar Discovery

Where Does the Small Word Sit?

"This jacket is too tight across the shoulders."

"The sleeves are not long enough for me."

"There is not enough room in the waist."

"It is so baggy that it looks borrowed."

One of the orange words always comes before the adjective. One of them always comes after it. Which is which?

FormPositionExample
too + adjectivebefore the adjective — always a problemIt is too tight.
adjective + enoughAFTER the adjective — the amount you needThe sleeves are long enough.
enough + nounBEFORE the noun — the quantity you needThere is enough room in the waist.
not + adjective + enoughthe negative: something is missingIt is not warm enough for February.
so + adjective + thatthe adjective, and then its resultIt is so baggy that it looks borrowed.

In one line: too goes in front, enough goes behind the adjective, and so ... that brings the consequence with it.

Listening 1 · The Store

Attention, Shoppers

A sale, three floors, and one rule about the fitting rooms. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is on the second floor, and how much is it?
Sweaters, hoodies and everything with a pattern, at half price.
2. What are the rules about the fitting rooms today?
Six items at a time; the fitting rooms on the second floor are closed for repair, so shoppers use the ones on three; and the staff cannot hold anything at the counter.
3. How long do you have to return a sale item, and what has to come back with it?
Fourteen days, not thirty, with the tag still attached and the receipt in the bag.
Listening 2 · A Story

It Did Not Even Close

A coat ordered online, in her usual size. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What was wrong with the coat when it arrived?
It did not close at all. It was her usual size, but in that brand the size runs two inches smaller than in the store she uses at home.
2. Where was the information that explained the problem?
In the size chart on the website, right under the picture, with the measurements in inches: chest, waist and sleeve.
3. What does she do now, before she buys anything?
She takes a garment she already owns that fits her properly, lays it flat on the bed, measures the chest, waist and sleeve in inches, and compares those numbers to the chart instead of the letter on the label.
Listening 3 · The Care Line

Why Did It Come Out Smaller?

A recorded answer about water, heat and color. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. According to the recording, what actually makes cotton shrink?
The dryer, not the wash: tumble drying cotton on high heat, and it is usually a full size.
2. Why does he tell you to turn the garment inside out?
It keeps the color from rubbing off inside the drum while the machine runs. He says it is the step people skip.
3. What two things make a dark color fade, and what does bleach do to a pattern?
Hot water and strong sun, which is why he says to hang it away from a window. Bleach does not take out the stain: it takes out the design.

1. The label says machine washable. Can you wash this hoodie in hot water?
No. Machine washable only means the machine is safe. The tag says cold, thirty degrees or below, and inside out with similar colors.
2. Two lines on this tag exist to stop the garment from shrinking or fading. Which ones?
Do not tumble dry, hang it to dry instead; and wash cold, inside out. Heat in the dryer shrinks the cotton and hot water makes the burgundy fade.
3. Your chest measures 36 inches and your waist 29. Which size do you take, and why?
The medium: 37 inches in the chest and 30 in the waist, so there is enough room. The small is 34 and 27, which would be too tight across the chest.
