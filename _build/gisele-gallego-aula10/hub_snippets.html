Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A hoodie -- a soft cotton top with a hood, worn over a shirt
"The twins live in a hoodie from October to April."
A fitting room -- the small closed room in a store where you put clothes on before you buy
"The fitting rooms are at the back, past the shoes."
To try on -- to put a piece of clothing on in the store, to see if it fits you
"Try it on before you pay. The size is not the same in every store."
Snug -- close to the body: tight, but still comfortable
"It is snug across the shoulders, and I can still move."
Baggy -- much wider and looser than the body, on purpose or by mistake
"The sleeves are so baggy that the coat looks borrowed."
A size up -- the next size bigger than the one you have in your hand
"The waist is too tight. Could I try a size up?"
A pattern -- the design on the cloth: stripes, dots, flowers, squares
"The color is fine, but the pattern is too loud for work."
Turquoise -- a bright blue-green, like sea water over pale sand
"He said the turquoise one, and I had no idea he was talking about a color."
Burgundy -- a dark red: the color of red wine
"The burgundy sweater is the only dark red thing I own."
A garment -- one single piece of clothing: the word labels and stores use
"Wash the garment inside out, in cold water."
To fade -- to slowly lose color, after many washes or a lot of sun
"Dark colors fade fast if you wash them in hot water."
Machine washable -- safe to wash in a washing machine, not only by hand
"It is machine washable, so I do not have to pay for dry cleaning."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

I took a burgundy hoodie into the fitting room in my usual size, and it was too tight across the shoulders. Not close to the body, which would be snug and fine. Tight. There was not enough room to raise my arms, and the sleeves were not long enough either.

So I asked for a size up, and the second one was the opposite problem: it was so wide in the body that it looked borrowed. My daughters would call that baggy and buy it anyway. I put it back. The pattern on the third one was too loud for me, and the turquoise was lovely, but it was not warm enough for February in Washington.

In the end I bought the first color, one size up, in a heavier cotton. The tag says the garment is machine washable, cold only, and that is the part people ignore. Wash a dark color in hot water twice and it will fade. The dryer is worse: the heat is so strong that the cotton comes out a full size smaller.

1. "There was not enough room to raise my arms." Why is enough in front of room here?
A Because the sentence is negative, and enough always moves in a negative sentence.
B Because room is a noun, and enough goes before a noun.
C Because enough must always come at the beginning of the phrase.
2. "The sleeves were not long enough." What does this tell you?
A The sleeves are shorter than she needs: something is missing.
B The sleeves are longer than she needs, which is a problem.
C The sleeves are exactly the right length for her.
3. "It was so wide in the body that it looked borrowed." What is the part after that?
A Another adjective describing the same garment.
B The reason why she went into the store.
C The result of the adjective: how wide it was is measured by what it did.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Too, Enough and So ... That

GRAMMAR

Three small words, and the position of each one is the whole rule.

FormPosition and meaningExample
too + adjectivebefore the adjective; more than you want, always a problemThis jacket is too tight.
adjective + enoughAFTER the adjective; the amount you needThe sleeves are long enough.
not + adjective + enoughthe negative; something is missingIt is not warm enough for February.
enough + nounBEFORE the noun; the quantity you needThere is enough room in the waist.
so + adjective + thatthe adjective, and then its resultIt is so baggy that it looks borrowed.
too + adjective + tothe problem, and what it stops you doingIt is too snug to wear under a coat.

Never: The sleeves are enough long · This hoodie is too much baggy · There is not enough of room · It is so snug to wear it under a coat.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

You are in the fitting room and the shirt does not close. The salesperson asks how it went. You say:
A "It is too much tight. Do you have more big?"
B "It is too tight across the chest. Do you have a size up?"
C "It is enough tight. I want other size, please."
The coat is warm, but the sleeves stop above your wrists. You say:
A "The sleeves are not enough long."
B "The sleeves are not long enough for me."
C "The sleeves have not enough of length."
A salesperson asks which color you want. You want the dark red one. You say:
A "The burgundy one, please. Do you have it in a medium?"
B "The turquoise one, please. The dark red."
C "The pattern one, please. The color of wine."
You want to know if the black hoodie will still be black next year. You ask:
A "Is this garment enough machine washable to not fade?"
B "Does this garment fade too much for wash it?"
