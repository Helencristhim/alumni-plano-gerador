Lesson 18 -- Midpoint Review

Series
The Crown -- Season 1, Episode 7 ("Scientia Potentia Est")

The most senior person in the room takes stock of her own capability and finds a gap nobody had told her about. What makes the episode useful is not the outcome but the method: she treats her own preparation as a work object, names the weakness in one sentence, and does something demonstrable about it. That is the conversation of this lesson, in a room where the stakes are absurd.

Tip: watch for the moment she stops describing how she feels and starts describing what she cannot do. Write that sentence down and rewrite it about yourself.

Podcast
WorkLife with Adam Grant -- the episodes on feedback and on rethinking

Look for the episodes on how senior people take criticism and on why experience makes us slower to change our minds. His argument sits underneath the whole lesson: the higher you go, the more people describe your performance to you in adjectives, and the harder it becomes to find out what has actually not moved.

Tip: while you listen, decide who in your own organisation would tell you the uncomfortable version. If the answer is nobody, that is the finding, and it is worth more than the episode.

YouTube
Hans Rosling -- The Best Stats You've Ever Seen (TED)

Twenty minutes of one man replacing what an entire audience believes with what the data actually shows. It is the clearest demonstration there is of the difference between the first two columns of your midpoint board and the third one. Notice how often he says the trend rather than the position: he is talking about trajectory the whole way through.

Tip: count the number of times he hedges. It is far lower than you expect, because he is describing what he knows — and that is exactly why the few hedges he does use land.
