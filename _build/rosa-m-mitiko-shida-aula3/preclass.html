Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

Then vs. Now -- The Status Update

Choosing between last Tuesday and so far, and what that choice does to a corporate email. Key expressions: status update, to follow up, quarter, to confirm, figures, to postpone, proposal, to sign off on, draft, feedback, to bring up, bottom line. Structure: past simple for a closed moment, present perfect for a period that is still open.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. These twelve stay here in your material -- they do not disappear after the class.

Status update -- a short report on where something stands right now
"I send a status update on the corporate accounts every Monday."
To follow up -- to contact somebody again about something you discussed before
"I will follow up with the client on Thursday."
Quarter -- one of the four three-month periods a business year is divided into
"The third quarter closed on Tuesday."
To confirm -- to say officially that something is certain or correct
"The client has not confirmed the contract yet."
Figures -- the numbers that show how a business is performing
"The third quarter figures came out yesterday."
To postpone -- to move something to a later date
"They postponed the meeting to the following week."
Proposal -- a written offer that explains what you can do and at what price
"They asked for a new proposal before the end of the month."
To sign off on -- to give final approval to something
"The director signed off on the budget last Friday."
Draft -- a first version of a document, before anybody has approved it
"Send me the draft and I will read it tonight."
Feedback -- the comments people give you about your work so you can improve it
"The feedback he gave me last year changed how I write."
To bring up -- to mention a subject in a meeting or a conversation
"Somebody will bring up the third quarter figures on Thursday."
Bottom line -- the most important point, once everything else is stripped away
"The bottom line is that we have not lost the account."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Every Monday morning she writes a status update for the corporate accounts, and it takes her longer in English than the work itself takes in Portuguese. The rule her manager gave her is simple, and she keeps it on a note beside the screen: say what closed, then say what is still open.

Last week closed well. They signed the renewal with the bank on Tuesday, the quarter ended nine per cent above target, and the director signed off on the new pricing on Friday afternoon. All of that happened on a day she can name, so all of it goes in the past.

The open part is different. The second client has not confirmed the contract yet. They asked for a new proposal on Monday, and she has already sent the draft to the pricing team. Nothing there has a closing date, so nothing there goes in the past.

Then the last line, which her manager reads first when the week is bad. The bottom line is that the account is not lost, and she will follow up on Thursday. She does not need to postpone anything or ask for help. She only needs to write the figures down before somebody brings them up on the call, and to remember the feedback she got the first time she wrote one of these: two sentences, not two paragraphs.

1. Why is "they signed the renewal on Tuesday" in the past simple?
A Because the sentence names a closed day, and Tuesday is over.
B Because the renewal is still being discussed.
C Because signing is always in the past simple.
2. Why is "the client has not confirmed the contract yet" not in the past simple?
A Because it happened a long time ago.
B Because it is still open and still expected to happen.
C Because the client is not important.
3. What rule did her manager give her for the update?
A Write two paragraphs about each account.
B Always start with the bottom line.
C Say what closed, then say what is still open.
4. The text says she has already sent the draft. What is a draft?
A The final version, after everybody approved it.
B A first version, before anybody has approved it.
C The price the client accepted.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Simple or Present Perfect?

GRAMMAR

Both talk about the past. Only one of them says when. That single difference is what your manager hears when he reads your update.

FormWhat it saysExample
Past simple
signed · sent · closed		The moment is closed, and you say it or everybody knows it: yesterday, last March, on Tuesday, in 2019, two weeks ago."We signed the renewal on Tuesday."
"They postponed it two weeks ago."
Present perfect
have sent · has not confirmed		The period is still open, or the moment does not matter: this year, this quarter, so far, already, yet, ever, never."I have already sent the draft."
"They have not confirmed it yet."
The pair that goes togetherOpen with the present perfect, then give the detail in the past simple. This is how a good status update is built."We have lost two clients this year. One left in March and one left in June."
Questions about a whole careerFor experience with no date, English asks have you ever. Did you ever sounds like you are asking about a period that is over."Have you ever attended the convention?"
NOT "Did you ever attend the convention?"
The trapA closed time expression and have can never share a sentence. If yesterday is in the sentence, have cannot be.NOT "I have sent it yesterday."
NOT "Last quarter we have launched two routes."

One test decides almost every case: does the sentence say when? If it does, use the past simple. If it does not, and the period is still running, use the present perfect.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are writing your Monday update and answering questions about it. Choose the best thing to say.

1. Your manager asks when the proposal went out. It went out on Tuesday. You say:
A I have sent it on Tuesday.
B I sent it on Tuesday morning.
C I am sending it on Tuesday.
2. A client asks how many contracts your team has closed this year. You say:
A We closed eleven contracts so far this year.
B We have closed eleven contracts so far this year.
C We have closed eleven contracts last year.
3. The contract is still waiting for the client. In your update you write:
A The client did not confirm the contract yet.
B The client has not confirmed the contract yet.
C The client has not confirmed the contract two weeks ago.
4. At the convention you want to ask a colleague about her experience. You ask:
A Have you ever worked with a client in Chile?
B Did you ever work with a client in Chile?
C Are you ever working with a client in Chile?
5. You want to close the update with the one thing that matters. You write:
A Anyway, that is more or less the situation.
B The bottom line is that we have not lost the account.
C The bottom line was that we did not lose the account yet.
