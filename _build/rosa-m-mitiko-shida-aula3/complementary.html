Tip: read the transcript and underline every verb in the first two minutes. Mark each one past simple or present perfect. You will see the two tenses doing exactly what we practiced. Send the page to your Kindle for the flight.

"How to save the world (or at least yourself) from bad meetings" -- David Grady, TED

Six and a half minutes on the meeting nobody prepared for, and the polite question that fixes it. Connection to Lesson 3: your Thursday regional call is exactly the meeting he is describing, and a clear status update is what stops it becoming one.

Tip: it is short, so watch it twice. The first time with English subtitles, the second without. Then write down the one question he suggests asking before you accept an invitation.

Grammar Video
"Present perfect and past simple" -- BBC Learning English, 6 Minute Grammar

Six minutes on the exact contrast of this lesson, with the rule stated in one line: if you say when, use the past simple. Connection to Lesson 3: this is the difference between "we signed it on Tuesday" and "we have not signed it yet".

Tip: pause after each example and say the same idea twice about your own week -- once with a day named, once without. The tense has to change. If it does not, say them again.
