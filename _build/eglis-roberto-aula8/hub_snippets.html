Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

The Soundtrack of My Life -- Three Songs, Three Moments

How to talk about the music of your life: what you have been listening to, how long, and what it is about a song that you love. Key words: the lyrics, a tune, catchy, to hum, a record, to be into, nostalgic, a cover, to bring back memories, a playlist. Structure: the present perfect continuous (I have been listening to it for years) for something that started in the past and continues now; cleft sentences (What I love is…, It was… who…) to give emphasis.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

The lyrics -- the words of a song
"I love the music, but I never understand the lyrics."
A tune -- a simple melody that you can remember
"He played an old tune on the piano."
Catchy -- easy to remember, and hard to stop singing
"The chorus is so catchy that I sing it all day."
To hum -- to sing with your mouth closed, without words
"My father always hums when he cooks."
A record -- a round black disc with music on it, for a record player
"I still have my first record from 1978."
To be into -- to like something a lot and spend time on it
"I have been into jazz since I was a teenager."
Nostalgic -- happy and a little sad when you think about the past
"This song always makes me feel nostalgic."
A cover -- a new version of a song by a different singer
"Her cover of that old song is better than the original."
To bring back memories -- to make you remember things from the past
"That song brings back memories of my first trip to Europe."
A playlist -- a list of songs that you choose to play together
"I made a playlist for the drive to the coast."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Roberto is sixty-three, and he has been playing the guitar for almost fifty years. “I started at fourteen, and I never stopped,” he says. He uses has been playing because the activity started in the past and it is still going on today.

Lately, he has been learning old songs from Cuba. “What I love about Cuban music is the rhythm,” he explains. He does not say “I love the rhythm.” He starts with What I love is, and the important part comes at the end, with more emphasis.

“It was my grandmother who gave me my first guitar,” he says. He wants to make it clear: not his father, not a friend. His grandmother. He knows the song she liked best, and he has known it since he was a boy. With know, he does not use the -ing form.

"He has been playing the guitar for almost fifty years." Does Roberto still play?
A No. He stopped playing many years ago.
B Yes. He started in the past and he still plays today.
C We do not know.
Why does Roberto say "What I love about Cuban music is the rhythm"?
A To put emphasis on the rhythm, at the end.
B Because it is the only correct way to say it in English.
C Because he is talking about the past.
Why does he say "has known" and not "has been knowing"?
A Because he knew it only in the past.
B Because know is an irregular verb.
C Because know is a state, not an activity, so it is not used with -ing here.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- I’ve Been + -ing, and What I Love Is…

GRAMMAR

An activity from the past until now: have or has been plus -ing. Emphasis: What I love is… or It was… who…

JobFormExample
From the past until nowhave / has been + verb-ingI have been listening to jazz for years.
How longfor + time, since + startShe has been singing for ten years, since 2015.
QuestionsHow long have you been + -ing?How long have you been playing the piano?
States (know, love, have)have / has + past participleI have known this song all my life.
Emphasis on a thingWhat I + verb + is…What I love is the voice.
Emphasis on a person or timeIt was… who / that…It was my brother who gave me my first record.

Never: I am listening to this band since 1980 · I listen to it for years · I have been knowing this song · What I love it is the voice · Was my brother who gave me

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

Two people talk about music at a bar. Put the conversation in a natural order.

?"And how did you discover them?"
?"What I love is the guitar. It brings back so many memories."
?"Great song. Are you into this kind of music?"
?"Really? What is it that you like about them?"
?"It was my older brother who played them for me, on an old record."
?"Very much. I have been listening to this band since the eighties."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A friend asks how long you have liked jazz. You started at twenty, and you still love it. You say:
A "I am into jazz since I was twenty, and I still listen to it every day."
B "I have been into jazz since I was twenty."
C "I listen jazz since twenty."
You want to know how long a street musician has been playing. You ask:
A "How long have you been playing here?"
B "How long are you playing here, and do you play every single day?"
C "How long you play here?"
You want to give emphasis to the voice of the singer. You say:
A "What I love it is the voice, more than the music or the lyrics."
B "What I love are the voice."
C "What I love is the voice."
Your father, not your mother, taught you a song. You make it clear:
A "It was my father who taught me this song."
B "Was my father who taught me this song when I was a little boy."
C "It was my father which taught me."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Make the playlist of your life: choose three songs. Record about one minute: for each song, say when it came into your life, how long you have been listening to it, and what it is about the song that you love.

TED Talk
Mark Ronson: “How sampling transformed music” (15 min)

A famous producer shows how one old song can live again inside a new one. It is Paul and Rachel in one talk: old music and new music, together.

Tip: after the talk, say which old song you would like to hear in a new version, and what it is about the original that you love.

TED Talk
David Byrne: “How architecture helped music evolve” (16 min)

The singer of Talking Heads explains that the place where music is played changes the music itself: a cathedral, a small club, a car. A good bridge from the last lesson to this one.

Tip: think of the place where you listen to music most: the car, the boat, home. Record one minute: what you have been listening to there.

TED Talk
Robert Gupta: “Music is medicine, music is sanity” (9 min)

A young violinist tells the story of a homeless musician who found his way back through music. Short, moving and easy to follow.

Tip: watch it twice. The second time, write down two sentences with has been or have been.
