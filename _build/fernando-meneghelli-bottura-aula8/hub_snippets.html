How Do You Evaluate Your Team?

The question that cost you a role, answered properly — and the twelve-month agenda behind it. Key words: a direct report, accountability, attrition, to delegate, a stretch target, to step up, bandwidth, to empower, to hold to account, a performance review, succession, to raise the bar. Structure: future perfect and future continuous, the tenses that turn an intention into a commitment.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. Half of a CFO interview is about numbers. The half that decides it is about these twelve words.

A direct report -- somebody who reports to you with nobody in between
"Four direct reports, and thirty-two people in the function."
Accountability -- being the one who answers for the result, whoever did the work
"You can delegate the work. You cannot delegate the accountability."
Attrition -- people leaving, and not being replaced
"We took a fifth of the base out through attrition rather than redundancy."
To delegate -- to give somebody else the work and keep the responsibility
"I delegated the close and I still owned the date."
A stretch target -- a target set above what is comfortably achievable
"Five days was the plan and three was the stretch target. We got four."
To step up -- to take more responsibility than the role formally requires
"Two of them stepped up in that quarter, and both were promoted the following year."
Bandwidth -- how much capacity a person or a team actually has
"The willingness was there. The bandwidth was not, and that is a management problem."
To empower -- to give somebody the authority to decide, not only to do
"Empowering a controller means letting them say no to a commercial director."
To hold to account -- to require somebody to answer for a result
"Holding someone to account is not punishment. It is asking for the answer in public."
A performance review -- the formal conversation about how somebody is doing
"If the performance review surprises anybody, the manager has failed, not the person."
Succession -- having somebody ready to take a role when it becomes free
"Succession is the only part of the job that is judged after you leave."
To raise the bar -- to increase what counts as good enough
"We raised the bar on the close and nobody worked a longer day for it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

I evaluate a finance team on three things. Does the number arrive on the day it was promised. Is it right the first time, because a number that has to be corrected costs more credibility than one that is late. And does the business go to that person before a decision, or after it. The first two I measure. The third I cannot, so I ask — and if a controller's name has not come up in a year, that is the performance review right there.

Hours are not on the list. If somebody needs twelve hours to do nine hours of work, the problem is bandwidth or process, and both of those are mine. I delegate the work and I keep the accountability, which is the only version of delegation that survives a bad quarter. I empower a controller when I let them say no to a commercial director and back them afterwards.

If I joined in September, this is what the year would look like. By the end of month one I will have met all four direct reports individually and sat in on one close. By the end of the quarter I will have decided which of the three systems we keep. By month nine I will have named a succession candidate for each of the four roles. And this time next year we will be closing in five days rather than nine. What I will not have finished is the system migration, and I would rather say that now than explain it in month nine.

1. What is the difference between "I will assess the team" and "by the end of month one I will have met all four direct reports"?
A The first is an intention with no deadline. The second is a commitment with a date, which somebody can hold you to.
B The second is simply a more formal way of saying the first.
C The first refers to the near future and the second to the distant future.
2. "This time next year we will be closing in five days." Is the closing finished by then, or happening then?
A Finished — the five-day close will be complete and behind them.
B Happening — it describes the routine they will be in at that point.
C Neither — it is a conditional statement about what might happen.
3. Why does the text say that hours are not on the list?
A Because needing twelve hours for nine hours of work is a bandwidth or process problem, and both belong to the manager.
B Because hours are difficult to measure accurately in a finance function.
C Because the company did not record working hours.
4. Why does the last paragraph say what will NOT be finished?
A To lower the expectations of the panel before starting.
B Because saying it on day one costs nothing, and explaining it in month nine costs credibility.
C Because the migration is somebody else's responsibility.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Intention or Commitment

GRAMMAR

Two future tenses turn a list of verbs into something a panel writes down. One says finished BEFORE a date; the other says happening AT a date.

FormWhat it saysExample
will + verbAn intention, with no deadline attachedI will assess the team.
Future perfect
will have + participle		Finished BEFORE a point in the future. Almost always needs a by.By the end of the month I will have met all four.
Future continuous
will be + -ing		In progress AT a point in the future — the new normalThis time next year we will be closing in five days.
Time markersby the end of the month, by the time the audit starts, within ninety days go with the future perfect. This time next year, by then, at that point go with the future continuous.
NegativeI will not have finished the migration by then — a sentence that buys more credibility than any promise.
QuestionWill you have decided before the budget cycle? — Will you be reporting to the board directly by then?
Why it matters in an interview"I will assess the team" commits to nothing and every candidate says it. The same idea with a date is a promise, and a panel writes promises down. Two or three future perfects for the milestones plus one future continuous for the destination is an entire twelve-month answer.

The most common mistake is the present simple after "by the end of": "by the end of the month I meet all four" is a direct import from Portuguese and it is audible immediately.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

The panel is asking about people. Choose the answer that is yours rather than everybody's.

"How do you evaluate your team?" The strongest opening is:
A "Three things. On the promised day, right the first time, and asked before the decision rather than after."
B "I look at accuracy, deadlines, commitment and attitude, and of course the overall results."
C "It depends a lot on the person and on the moment the company is going through."
The panel asks how you measure the criterion that has no report behind it. You say:
A "That one is more subjective, so I use my feeling about the person."
B "I do not measure it, I ask. I ask the commercial director who he calls when he is building a pitch."
C "I would build a KPI for it in the first six months."
"What would your first twelve months look like?" The answer a panel writes down is:
A "I will assess the team, review the processes and build relationships with the business."
B "By the end of the quarter I will have decided which system we keep, and this time next year we will be closing in five days."
C "In the first year I want to understand the company deeply before committing to anything."
Which sentence is correct?
A "By the end of the month I meet all four direct reports."
B "This time next year we will closing in five days."
C "By the time the audit starts we will have rebuilt the process."
A panel member asks whether you expect long hours from a finance team. The strongest answer is:
A "If somebody needs twelve hours to do nine hours of work, that is bandwidth or process, and both are mine."
B "In finance everybody knows the close week is heavy, and the team accepts that."
C "I expect commitment, and commitment sometimes means staying late."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. Nobody is correcting this one, and there is no score.

"If you joined us today as CFO, what would your twelve-month agenda look like — and what would you not have finished?" Speak for about two minutes, in one take and with no notes. Give at least four dated commitments in the future perfect, one state you will be in by the end of the year in the future continuous, and one thing you will honestly not have finished. Then stop. Do not restart the recording, and do not soften the last part.
