Chapter 3: The Code

Intention, or Commitment?

One tense turns a list of verbs into something a panel writes down

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I will assess the team and review the processes."

"By the end of the first month I will have met all four direct reports."

"This time next year we will be closing in five days rather than nine."

"By the end of the quarter I will have decided which system we keep."

All four are about the future. A panel would write two of them down. Which two, and why?

FormWhat it saysExample
will + verbAn intention, with no deadline attachedI will assess the team.
Future perfect
will have + participle		Finished BEFORE a point in the future. Always needs a by.By the end of the month I will have met all four.
Future continuous
will be + -ing		In progress AT a point in the future — the new normalThis time next year we will be closing in five days.
The time markersby the end of the month, by the time the audit starts, within ninety days go with the future perfect. This time next year, by then, at that point go with the future continuous.
Negative and questionI will not have finished the system review by then. Will you have decided before the budget cycle?
Why it matters in an interview"I will assess the team" is what every candidate says, and it commits to nothing. "By the end of the first month I will have met all four direct reports and sat in on one close" is the same idea with a date, and a panel writes it down. The tense is the difference between an intention and a promise.

Use the future perfect for the milestones and the future continuous for the state you are promising to arrive at. Two or three of each is a whole twelve-month answer.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"By the end of the first month I meet all four direct reports."
"By the end of the first month I will have met all four direct reports."
"By the end of the quarter I will have decide which system we keep."
"By the end of the quarter I will have decided which system we keep."
"This time next year we will closing in five days."
"This time next year we will be closing in five days."

After will have comes the past participle: met, decided, finished. And the continuous always keeps its be.

The Move

Three Criteria, and the One You Cannot Measure

1. On time

"Does the number arrive on the day it was promised?"

2. Right first time

"A number that has to be corrected costs more than a number that is late."

3. Asked before, not after

"Does the business go to that person before the decision, or after it?"

"The first two I measure from a calendar and an error log. The third I cannot measure, so I ask the commercial director who he calls when he is building a pitch."

Admitting that one criterion resists measurement, and saying how you check it anyway, is what makes this answer yours instead of everybody's.

Dialogue

Three Criteria and Twelve Months

S
Let us talk about people. How do you evaluate a finance team, and what do you actually look at?
F
Three things. Does the number arrive on the day it was promised. Is it right the first time. And does the business go to that person before a decision, or after it.
S
The third one is not in any system I know of. So how do you measure it?
F
I do not measure it, I ask. I ask the commercial director who he calls when he is building a pitch. If my controller's name has not come up in a year, that is the performance review right there.
S
And hours? Some of your peers put availability quite high on that list.
F
Never. If somebody needs twelve hours to do nine hours of work, the problem is bandwidth or process, and both of those are mine, not theirs.
S
Good. Then the other one. If you joined us in September, what would your first twelve months look like?
F
By the end of month one I will have met all four direct reports and sat in on one close. By the end of the quarter I will have decided which of the three systems we keep. And this time next year we will be closing in five days rather than nine.
Comprehension

About Sofia

1. What does Sofia point out about the third criterion?
That it is not in any system she knows of — so she asks how it is measured.
2. What does she say some of his peers do that he does not?
They put availability, meaning hours, quite high on the list.
3. What is the second question she asks, and when would he be starting?
What his first twelve months would look like, if he joined in September.
Listening

A CFO Answers the Same Question

Three criteria, and the one he refuses to use. Sound first — no text.

1. What are his three criteria, in order?
Does the number arrive on the promised day; is it right the first time; and does the business ask that person before the decision or after it.
2. Why does he say a corrected number is worse than a late one?
Because it costs more credibility than being late does.
3. What is deliberately not on his list, and what does he say about it?
Hours. He has never evaluated anybody on hours and would not join a company that did.
Listening 2

The Sentence She Writes Down

An HR director on the twelve-month question. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say the twelve-month question matters most to the panel?
Because it is the only part of the interview that is about them rather than about the candidate.
2. What does a weak answer sound like, and why does it tell her nothing?
A list of intentions — assess the team, review the processes, build relationships — because everybody says it.
3. Which sentence does she write down, and for what three reasons?
"This time next year we will be closing in five days rather than nine" — because it is a commitment, it is measurable, and it shows the person has understood what is broken.

Stated up front: I will not have finished the system migration inside twelve months, and I would rather say that now than explain it in month nine.

1. Which line on this page is the one a panel would write down, and why?
Month 12 — closing in five days rather than nine, because it is measurable and somebody could hold him to it a year later.
2. Why does the note say what will NOT be finished?
Because saying it on day one costs nothing, and explaining it in month nine costs credibility.
3. Read the month 9 line as a full sentence in the future perfect.
"By month nine I will have named a successor for every one of the four roles."
