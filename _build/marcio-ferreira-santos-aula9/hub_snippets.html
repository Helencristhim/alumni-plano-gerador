Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Alignment -- when different teams agree on the same direction and act on it
"We had agreement in the room, but no alignment in the stores."
Adoption -- how much people actually start using something new
"The model was good. Adoption was the problem."
Execution -- turning a decision into something that really happened
"Strategy is cheap. Execution is where companies lose."
Optimization -- making something work as well as possible with what you have
"The first year is optimization, not reinvention."
Pilot -- a small first test in one place before you go wide
"We ran a pilot in twenty stores before the rollout."
Rollback -- going back to the previous version because the new one failed
"Every pilot needs a rollback plan before it starts."
Handover -- passing a job, with everything you know, to the next person
"This is the handover note from my side."
To sequence -- to decide the order in which things will be done
"If everything is urgent, nothing has been sequenced."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Here is what we expect in the first ninety days. We do not expect you to deliver a number. We expect you to understand the business. So I would recommend spending the first month in the stores. After that, I suggest choosing one category and rebuilding the model there. Avoid announcing a big plan in week two: it never survives contact with the data. And do not try to fix everything at once. The people who succeed here are the ones who manage to finish one thing properly before they start the next. My predecessor stopped defending a decision she no longer believed in, and I regret not asking her about it earlier.

1. Why is it "I recommend spending" and not "I recommend to spend"?
A "Recommend" belongs to the group of verbs followed by -ing.
B Because the sentence is about the future.
C Because "spend" is an irregular verb.
2. What does "she stopped defending a decision" mean?
A She no longer defends it. She used to, and then she stopped.
B She paused her work in order to defend it.
C She never defended it at any point.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "We decided restructuring the pricing team."
B "We decided to restructure the pricing team."
C "We decided for restructure the pricing team."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Gerunds and Infinitives

GRAMMAR

The first verb decides the form of the second one. There is no logic, only a list.

GroupVerbsExample
verb + -ingavoid, consider, recommend, suggest, keep, finish, practice, risk, involve, enjoyI recommend reviewing the model.
verb + todecide, plan, expect, manage, need, want, offer, agree, fail, tendWe decided to restructure the team.
-ing as the subjectat the start of a sentenceMonitoring margins requires discipline.
Change of meaning"I stopped using intuition" means I no longer use it. "I stopped to review the data" means I paused in order to review it. The same applies to try, remember and forget.
