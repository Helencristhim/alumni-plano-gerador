Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

Did You? -- Asking About the Past

The other half of a conversation: did you...?, what did you do?, and the two short answers that save you. Take your time, and repeat as often as you like.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Three of them are about the interview, three about the head, and three about people and places.

Interview -- a meeting where one person asks and the other person answers
"The interview was only five minutes."
Question -- what you say when you want to know something
"I asked him four questions about the trip."
Answer -- what you say back when somebody asks you something
"His answer was short, but it was good."
Remember -- to keep something in your head, so you can say it again later
"I remember Ray, because he gave me the wrong helmet."
Forget -- to lose something from your head -- the past form is forgot
"I forgot every name in ten minutes."
Hear -- to get a sound with your ears -- the past form is heard
"Did you hear about the rain last week?"
Meet -- to be with somebody for the first time -- the past form is met
"I met Ray on my first day here."
Travel -- to go from one place to another, usually far away
"He traveled for eleven hours."
Last time -- the time before this one -- two words that open a question
"When was the last time you were in a tunnel?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the text out loud, then answer the questions below. Read slowly -- nobody is timing you.

The site newsletter asked me for a short interview with Greg, so on Thursday I went to the office container with five questions on my phone. "When did you arrive, and how long did you travel?" He arrived on the ninth of March: eleven hours in the air, and four more hours on the road. "Did you sleep on the plane?" "No, I didn't. I watched three movies and I read the report twice." "Who did you meet on your first day?" He forgot every name in ten minutes, but he remembers Ray, because Ray gave him the wrong helmet. "Did you hear about the rain last week?" "I heard about it in every e-mail. Did the water go into the lower area?" It did, on Wednesday. And my last one: "When was the last time you worked in a tunnel like this one?" Four years ago, in Norway. That answer was the best of the five.

1. The text says "Did you sleep on the plane?" and not "Did you slept on the plane?". Why?
A Because sleep is an irregular verb and irregular verbs never change.
B Because did already carries the past, so the other verb goes back to the base form.
C Because the question is about the future, not about the past.
2. Four questions in the text start with did. The last one starts with "When was". Why is that one different?
A Because it is the last question of the interview.
B Because it is a longer and more polite question.
C Because the verb there is be, and be uses was or were instead of did.
3. Greg answers one question with "No, I didn't". What would be wrong here?
A "No, I didn't slept." -- the verb never comes back after didn't.
B "No, I didn't." -- a short answer is never enough in English.
C "No, I didn't. I watched three movies." -- you cannot add information.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Did, Wh- Words and the Short Answers

Grammar

One small word comes to the front and the main verb goes back to its base form. The only verb that refuses did is be.

FormPatternExample
Yes / No questionDid + person + base verbDid you work on Saturday?
Wh- questionWhat / Where / When / Who / Why / How + did + person + base verbWhat did you do yesterday?
Negativeperson + did not (didn't) + base verbI did not call the company.
Short answersYes, I did. · No, I didn't. -- never "Yes, I worked" and never "No, I didn't worked".
CarefulOne past mark is enough: Did you work? -- never "Did you worked?"
The verb beRightWrong
QuestionWas it a good interview?Did it was a good interview?
PluralWere they late?Did they were late?
Wh- with beWhen was the last time?When did was the last time?
The testAsk yourself: is the main verb be? If yes, use was or were. If no, use did.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation. Wrong? Try again -- nobody is watching.

You want to know if the visitor slept on the plane. You say:
A "Did you slept on the plane?"
B "Did you sleep on the plane?"
C "You did sleep on the plane?"
Somebody asks "Did you call the concrete company?" and you did not call. You say:
A "No, I not called."
B "No, I didn't called."
C "No, I didn't. I forgot."
You want to know where your colleague went last March. You say:
A "Where did you go last March?"
B "Where you went last March?"
C "Where did you went last March?"
You want to know if the interview was short. You say:
A "Did the interview was short?"
B "Did the interview short?"
C "Was the interview short?"
