Two Reviews of the Same Show -- Grading a Verdict

Two critics on the same exhibition, and the small words that decide how strong a verdict is. Key words: ambitious, thought-provoking, striking, accomplished, restrained, uneven, derivative, heavy-handed, self-indulgent, overrated, to fall flat, lukewarm. Structure: gradable and non-gradable adjectives with intensifiers and softeners.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Ambitious -- trying to do something large and difficult
"It is the most ambitious show the museum has attempted in years."
Thought-provoking -- making you think seriously about something
"The best works are thought-provoking without being sad."
Striking -- very noticeable and impressive
"The first room is striking, even from the door."
Accomplished -- showing a high level of skill
"Not every work is equally accomplished."
Restrained -- controlled and quiet, never exaggerated
"The curtains are so restrained that you almost walk past them."
Uneven -- good in some parts and weak in others
"With forty artists and no argument, the show is deeply uneven."
Derivative -- copying the ideas of other artists, not original
"Some of the videos feel derivative, as if they were made after a better show."
Heavy-handed -- too obvious, explaining everything to the viewer
"The room of heartbeats is heavy-handed: it is simply loud."
Self-indulgent -- made for the artist's own pleasure, not for the audience
"A forty-minute video of the artist in a gown is self-indulgent."
Overrated -- praised more than it deserves
"The show as a whole is overrated."
To fall flat -- to fail to have the effect that was intended
"The central message falls flat."
Lukewarm -- not very enthusiastic, neither positive nor negative
"A lukewarm review can hurt an artist more than a bad one."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Two critics visited The Waiting Room, a large group show about hospitals and care, on the same morning. Both agreed that it was very ambitious. After that, they agreed on almost nothing.

The first critic called the opening room absolutely extraordinary: a row of hospital curtains so restrained that you almost miss it. She admitted that some video pieces were fairly predictable and one installation was rather long, but she left completely exhausted, in the best sense.

The second critic found the show deeply uneven. For him, the curtains were perfectly pleasant but no revelation, the room of heartbeats was heavy-handed, and one long video was utterly self-indulgent. Notice the small words: very and fairly go with adjectives that have degrees; absolutely and utterly go with adjectives that are already extreme.

1. Why does the first critic write absolutely extraordinary and not very extraordinary?
A Because extraordinary is already extreme, so it takes absolutely, not very.
B Because very is too informal to appear in a published review in a serious magazine.
C Because absolutely makes it sound negative.
2. "One installation was rather long." What does the critic mean?
A It was impressively long, and that was the best thing about it.
B It was the right length.
C It was longer than it should have been.
3. On which point do the two critics agree?
A That the curtains in the first room are the most striking work in the whole exhibition.
B That the show is very ambitious.
C That the room of heartbeats is moving.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- How Strong Is the Word

GRAMMAR

Adjectives come in two kinds, and each kind takes its own intensifiers. The small word in front of the adjective is the verdict.

KindUseExample
Gradable: ambitious, long, interesting, goodvery, extremely, really, a little"The show is very ambitious."
Non-gradable (extreme): extraordinary, exhausted, brilliant, awfulabsolutely, utterly, completely, totally, really"The first room is absolutely extraordinary." NOT very extraordinary
Softenersfairly, rather, a bit, not particularly (gradable only)"The videos are fairly predictable."
RatherIn a review it usually signals a problem"It is rather long." = too long
The trap: quitequite + gradable = fairly; quite + extreme = completely"quite good" (only fairly good)
"quite extraordinary" (absolutely)
ReallyThe safe word: it works with both kinds"really good" · "really extraordinary"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are talking about exhibitions with artists, travelers and gallerists. Choose the best thing to say.

A traveler in your art group asks about the Biennale. You loved the first pavilion:
A It was absolutely extraordinary.
B It was very extraordinary, and I think it was the best pavilion that I have seen in years.
C It was fairly extraordinary.
You liked a show, but it was too long:
A It was absolutely interesting, but it was absolutely long and I needed a coffee afterwards.
B It was utterly interesting and very long.
C It was very interesting, but rather long.
A gallerist asks how the press reacted. The reviews were neither good nor bad:
A The reviews were absolutely warm, and some critics even called the show quite good overall.
B The reviews were lukewarm.
C The reviews were very hot.
A friend says a show is a masterpiece. You think it copies other artists:
A To be honest, I found it rather derivative.
B To be honest, I found it very utterly original and not at all similar to anything else.
C To be honest, it was absolutely derivative good.
An artist friend's message was too obvious in the installation:
A The idea is important, but the installation is absolutely restrained, quiet and very controlled.
B The idea is important, but the installation is very fall flat.
C The idea is important, but the installation is a bit heavy-handed.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.
