Chapter 4: How Strong Is the Word

Very Good, Absolutely Brilliant

Two kinds of adjective, two kinds of intensifier

Grammar Discovery

Can You Say Very Extraordinary?

"For the most part it is absolutely extraordinary."

"A few of the video pieces are fairly predictable."

"The video is utterly self-indulgent."

"It is very ambitious, but rather long."

Every red word grades the adjective after it. Why does the critic choose a different word each time?

AdjectiveIntensifierExample
Gradable (it has degrees): ambitious, long, interesting, goodvery, extremely, really, a littleThe show is very ambitious.
Non-gradable (already extreme): extraordinary, exhausted, brilliant, awfulabsolutely, utterly, completely, totally, reallyThe first room is absolutely extraordinary. NOT very extraordinary
Softeners (gradable only)fairly, rather, a bit, not particularlyThe videos are fairly predictable; the last room is rather long.
Ratheroften negative or surprising in a reviewIt is rather long = longer than it should be.
The trap: quitequite + gradable = fairly; quite + non-gradable = completelyquite good (only fairly good) · quite extraordinary (absolutely)
Reallythe safe word: works with both kindsreally good · really extraordinary

In one line: very grades; absolutely confirms an extreme; fairly and rather hold back.

Listening 1 · The Gallerist

The Review She Fears Most

A gallerist who represents a dozen artists, on what a review really says. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the first thing she and her artists look for in a review?
The adjectives -- and the small words in front of them.
2. What does she say about quite?
It is a trap: quite good means only fairly good, but quite extraordinary means absolutely extraordinary.
3. Why does she fear a lukewarm review more than a bad one?
A bad review means the critic cared. A lukewarm one is forgotten, and nobody goes to the show.
Listening 2 · The Artist

The Word That Made Him Better

An artist remembers the first review that hurt. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the first serious review call his work, and how did he react?
Derivative. He read it on a train, missed his stop, and for a year said the critic was utterly wrong.
2. What did he admit about his early pieces?
They were heavy-handed: he explained everything, so nothing was left for the viewer.
3. What question did he ask himself when a later critic called his installation overrated?
Whether it fell flat because the idea was weak or because the room was wrong -- it was the room.

Five Sentences for a Verdict

Survival Card

Five Sentences for a Verdict

It is very ambitious, but rather uneven.
The first room is absolutely extraordinary.
It is fairly striking, but I would not call it brilliant.
I found it thought-provoking, if a bit heavy-handed.
The reviews were lukewarm, so I almost did not go.
