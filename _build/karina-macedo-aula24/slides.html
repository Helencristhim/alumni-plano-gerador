Chapter 4: The Code

I'm sorry about... · Let me... · I'll...

say sorry and offer help

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I'm sorry about the delay."

"Let me check the date with the factory."

"I'll send you a new invoice today."

"It won't happen again."

FormUseExample
I'm sorry about + nounsay sorry for a problemI'm sorry about the mistake.
I apologize for + nounsay sorry -- more formalI apologize for the delay.
Let me + verboffer to do something nowLet me check that for you.
I'll (I will) + verbpromise an actionI'll call you this afternoon.
won't (will not) + verbpromise NOT to do it againIt won't happen again.

After Let me and I'll: the verb with NO to (Let me check, I'll send). / Depois de Let me e I'll, o verbo vem sem to.

Dialogue

Robert Calls

R
Hello, Karina. It is Robert. Did you read my email?
K
Yes, I did, Robert. I'm very sorry about the delay.
R
The jet is two weeks late, and we have a trip to Chicago on June first.
K
I understand. Let me check with the factory. The new delivery date is May twenty-sixth.
R
OK, that is before our trip. And the invoice?
K
You are right, the price was a mistake. I apologize. I'll send you a new invoice today.
R
Thank you. One box of spare parts was damaged, too.
K
I'm sorry about that. We'll send new parts this week, and we'll give you a refund for the box.
R
That is a good solution, Karina. Thank you for your help.
K
You're welcome, Robert. It won't happen again. I'll call you on Monday.

2. What arrived damaged, according to Robert?

3. What does Robert think of Karina's answer?
It is a good solution.
