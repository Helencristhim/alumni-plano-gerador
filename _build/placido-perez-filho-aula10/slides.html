Chapter 4: The Code

Will Be Doing, Will Have Done

Two futures, one deadline

Grammar Discovery

Finished by Then, or in Progress Then?

"By 2030, AI will have automated most routine office tasks."

"In ten years, we will be working next to AI every day."

"By the time you finish this book, the software will have changed again."

"This time next year, I will be using AI to read market reports."

Red: something that will be finished before a moment in the future (look for by). Blue: something that will be in progress at a moment in the future. Imagine yourself standing in 2030: is it done, or is it happening?

JobFormExample
In progress at a future momentwill be + -ingThis time next year, I’ll be using AI.
Finished before a future momentwill have + past participleBy 2030, AI will have automated it.
With a deadlineby + time / by the time + presentBy December, we’ll have finished.
Polite question about plansWill you be + -ing?Will you be using the room tomorrow?
Negativewon’t have + past participleAI won’t have replaced human judgment.
The trapby + time for a finished action: future perfectNot by 2030 AI will automate it. Say will have automated.

In one line: will be + -ing = in progress at that moment; will have + past participle = finished by that moment.

Listening 1 · An AI Engineer

What Machines Will Do

An AI engineer from China explains what AI will and will not be able to do in five years. Sound first — no text.

1. What will most companies be using in five years, according to him?
AI assistants that read and summarize documents.
2. What will AI have learned to do by then?
Write quotes and standard emails in many languages.
3. What does he say AI will not have learned?
To take responsibility for a decision.
Listening 2 · An Economist

Jobs That Will Change

A British economist talks about what AI will do to jobs. Sound first — no text.

1. Does she think AI will cause mass unemployment?
No; she thinks most jobs will change, not disappear.
2. What happened with spreadsheets, in her example?
People thought accountants would disappear, but there are more accountants today.
3. What skill will be most valuable, according to her?
The ability to ask good questions and judge the answers.
Listening 3 · A Publisher

The Book and Its Summary

A German book publisher talks about AI summaries and the future of reading. Sound first — no text.

1. What worried her when AI summaries appeared?
That people would stop reading full books.
2. What has actually happened in her company?
Sales of full books went up among readers who use summaries.
3. What will a summary never give you, in her opinion?
The experience of reading: the voice of the author and the time with the book.
