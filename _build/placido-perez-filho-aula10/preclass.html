Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An algorithm -- a set of instructions that a computer follows to solve a problem
"The algorithm decides which news you see first."
To automate -- to make a machine or a program do a task that people did before
"We automated the quotes, and now they take minutes."
To replace -- to take the place of someone or something
"AI will not replace good salespeople."
Accurate -- correct in every detail
"The summary was fast, but it wasn’t accurate."
To rely on -- to need and trust someone or something
"We rely on our suppliers to deliver on time."
A breakthrough -- an important new discovery or development
"Language models were a breakthrough in AI."
The workforce -- all the people who work in a company or a country
"A third of the workforce will need new skills."
To phase out -- to stop using something slowly, in stages
"The bank is phasing out paper forms."
A skill set -- all the skills that a person has
"Your skill set is more important than your job title."
To keep up with -- to move or learn as fast as something that changes quickly
"It’s hard to keep up with new technology."
A chatbot -- a computer program that talks with people in writing or by voice
"The chatbot answers our clients at night."
Human judgment -- the ability of a person to decide well, using experience
"Some decisions will always need human judgment."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

By 2030, many companies will have automated their routine work. A chatbot will be answering clients at night, and an algorithm will prepare the morning reports. This is the result of a breakthrough in language technology.

Some tasks will have been phased out, and the workforce will need a new skill set. Machines are fast, but not always accurate, so companies cannot rely on them for everything.

Small companies will be trying to keep up with the changes. But AI will not have replaced the most important thing: human judgment.

A Because machines are slow.

3. Which sentence is correct?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Future Continuous and Future Perfect

GRAMMAR

Stand at a moment in the future. If the action is happening at that moment, use will be + -ing. If it is already finished by that moment, use will have + past participle.

JobFormExample
In progress at a future momentwill be + -ingThis time next year, I’ll be using AI.
Finished before a future momentwill have + past participleBy 2030, AI will have automated it.
With a deadlineby + time / by the time + presentBy December, we’ll have finished.
Polite question about plansWill you be + -ing?Will you be using the room tomorrow?
Negativewon’t have + past participleAI won’t have replaced human judgment.
The trapby + time for a finished action: future perfectNot by 2030 AI will automate it. Say will have automated.

Never: By 2030 AI will automate (finished) · will be used AI · will have finish · rely in
