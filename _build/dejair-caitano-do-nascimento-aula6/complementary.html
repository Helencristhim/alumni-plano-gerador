Lesson 6 -- Inside the Workshop

Listening
"Which country has the best schools?" -- 6 Minute English, BBC Learning English

Six minutes of two presenters comparing countries, systems and numbers -- which is exactly the shape of the sentences you practised today. Comparatives and superlatives in almost every line.

Tip: listen once with your eyes closed. Then listen again and write down every comparative and superlative you hear. Sort them into two columns: the ones with -er and the ones with more. You will see the syllable rule work by itself.

Listening
"Is it OK to disagree?" -- 6 Minute English, BBC Learning English

Six minutes about disagreeing without a fight. This is the hardest thing in a workshop in a foreign language, and it is the thing you did in the role-play today.

Tip: listen for the phrases they use to disagree politely, and write down two of them. Add them to the one you learned today: 'I do not agree, and this is why.'

Conference Talk
"The best stats you've ever seen" -- Hans Rosling, TED

A doctor and statistician compares countries, decades and health systems out loud for twenty minutes, and never loses the room. He is doing what a workshop asks of you: comparing, choosing, and saying why.

Tip: watch the first eight minutes only. Every time he compares two things, pause and say his comparison in your own words. Do it out loud, not in your head.
