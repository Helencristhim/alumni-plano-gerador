Furnishing from Zero -- Delivery, Measurements and Returns

An empty house, a delivery window and a return policy. Key words: to assemble, a warranty, curbside delivery, out of stock, a swatch, an outlet, a measuring tape, to fit through, a return policy, a restocking fee, to be backordered, wall-to-wall. Structure: the passive voice in the present and past simple.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To assemble -- to put the pieces of something together yourself, following instructions
"The legs are shipped in the box, so the sofa is assembled at home."
A warranty -- a written promise from the maker to repair or replace something for a number of years
"The frame is covered by a ten year warranty."
Curbside delivery -- a delivery that stops at the street: the boxes are left outside and are not carried in
"Delivery is curbside only, so nothing is carried upstairs."
Out of stock -- sold out for now, because the store has none of it left
"The rug was out of stock twice before it finally arrived."
A swatch -- a small piece of fabric or color that you take home to see it in your own light
"I took a swatch of the blue home before I ordered the sofa."
An outlet -- the place in the wall where you plug something in
"The sofa was placed in front of the only outlet in the room."
A measuring tape -- a long flexible ruler used to measure a room, a door or a piece of furniture
"Now I do not buy anything without a measuring tape in my hand."
To fit through -- to be small enough to pass through a door, a hallway or a staircase
"The sectional did not fit through the front door."
A return policy -- the rules a store has for sending something back and getting your money
"The return policy is fourteen days, and the sofa is picked up here."
A restocking fee -- money the store keeps when you return something, to put it back on the shelf
"I paid a hundred and eighty dollars in a restocking fee."
To be backordered -- ordered, but not in the warehouse yet, so it arrives weeks later
"The gray one is backordered until the end of the month."
Wall-to-wall -- covering the whole floor, from one side of the room to the other
"The bedrooms came with wall-to-wall carpet."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The house was empty in June, so everything in it was ordered in eight weeks. The blue sofa is made in North Carolina and it is delivered on Tuesdays. The gray one is backordered until the end of the month, and the rug is out of stock again.

Delivery is curbside only. That means the boxes are left at the street: they are not carried upstairs and they are not assembled. The legs are shipped inside the box, so a bookcase is assembled at home, on the floor, with an Allen key.

Not everything went well. The dining table was damaged in transit and a new top was sent two weeks later. The first sofa never fit through the front door, so it was picked up and a ten percent restocking fee was taken out of the refund. The bookcase was assembled by Glauber over a whole weekend, which is the one line in this story where the name really matters.

1. Why "the boxes are left at the street" and not "the driver leaves the boxes at the street"?
A Because the passive is a more polite way of saying exactly the same thing.
B Because the boxes are what matters, and which driver comes is unknown and unimportant.
C Because leave is a verb that has no active form.
2. "The sofa is made in North Carolina" and "the table was damaged in transit." What is the difference?
A The first one is always true, so it takes is. The second one happened once, in the past, so it takes was.
B The first one is about a thing and the second one is about a place.
C There is no difference. Both forms mean the same thing.
3. "The bookcase was assembled by Glauber." Why does the person come back into this sentence?
A Because every passive sentence needs a by phrase to be complete.
B Because the sentence is in the past and past sentences take by.
C Because here the doer is the point: the store did not do it, he did.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Be Plus the Past Participle

GRAMMAR

One machine, two tenses, and the one moment the person comes back.

UseFormExample
Presentis / are + participleThe sofa is made in North Carolina.
Pastwas / were + participleThe table was damaged in transit.
Negativeis not / was not + participleThe rug was not included in the price.
QuestionIs / Was + subject + participleIs the rug included in the price?
Why use itThe doer is unknown, obvious or unimportantIt is delivered on Tuesday. Which driver? Nobody cares.
by + personOnly when the doer is the pointThe bookcase was assembled by Glauber.

Never: The legs is shipped in the box · The table was deliver on Tuesday · The rug is not include in the price · The sofa was returned by me in April.

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

You are at the furniture store. Put your questions in a logical order.

?"I am looking for a bookcase for the living room."
?"Is this one in stock, or is it backordered?"
?"And when is it delivered?"
?"Is it brought inside, or is delivery curbside only?"
?"Does it come assembled, or is it assembled at home?"
?"And what is the return policy, if it does not fit through my door?"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

The store has sold every gray sofa and none are coming. The salesman tells you:
A "The gray one is backordered."
B "The gray one is out of stock."
C "The gray one is assembled."
Your table arrived with a broken leg. You call the store and say:
A "The table damaged in transit."
B "The table was damaged in transit."
C "The table is damage in transit."
You want to know whether the price covers the rug. You ask:
A "Is the rug included in the price?"
B "Is the rug include in the price?"

A "A restocking fee was take out of the refund."
