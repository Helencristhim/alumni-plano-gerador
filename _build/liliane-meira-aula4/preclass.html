Lesson 04 -- Pre-class

Twelve People, Four Cities -- Reading a Supplier Proposal

A supplier sends a priced proposal for an art travel group, with six conditions buried in it. Key words: an itinerary, a supplier, lead time, a deposit, to accommodate, a contingency, occupancy, to block rooms, a quote, on-site, to waive a fee, a no-show. Structure: first and second conditionals.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An itinerary -- the day-by-day plan of a trip, with times and places fixed
"Send me the itinerary before I show it to the group."
A supplier -- the local company that actually provides the hotel, the guide or the transport
"Our supplier in Venice has been the same for three years."
Lead time -- the number of weeks between booking something and actually using it
"The lead time for private access is eight weeks."
A deposit -- part of the payment made in advance to hold a booking
"The deposit is thirty percent, and it is not refundable after June."
To accommodate -- to change a plan so that it fits what somebody needs
"We can accommodate a change of date up to four weeks before arrival."
A contingency -- a spare plan, or spare money, kept for what goes wrong
"Keep one free morning as a contingency. Something always moves."
Occupancy -- how many people share a room, which decides the price per person
"Single occupancy carries a supplement of ninety euros per night."
To block rooms -- to reserve a set of rooms before the travelers are confirmed
"We have blocked ten rooms, held until the thirtieth."
A quote -- a written price for a specific service, valid for a limited time
"The quote is valid for thirty days from today."
On-site -- happening at the destination itself, not from the office
"Marta is our on-site coordinator. She is the person to call."
To waive a fee -- to decide not to charge something that is normally charged
"We would waive the change fee for a medical reason with documentation."
A no-show -- a traveler who has booked and does not appear
"Two no-shows on the first morning cost us the private slot."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The supplier in Venice has blocked ten rooms for a group of twelve. The quote is valid for thirty days. If the group confirms before the end of the month, the hotel will hold the current rate; if the confirmation arrives in July, the rate will change, because the city applies a different tariff once the Biennale calendar is published.

The lead time is the part clients underestimate. Private access needs eight weeks, and the curator-led visit needs ten. The agency can accommodate a change of date up to four weeks before arrival at no cost. Inside four weeks there is a fee, although the supplier would waive it if the reason were medical and documented. The deposit is thirty percent, and single occupancy carries a supplement, which is why twin rooms are quoted as the default.

Two recommendations. Keep one free morning as a contingency, because something in Venice always moves. And if the group wanted October instead, the supplier would build the week around the smaller galleries and it would cost roughly fifteen percent less. The on-site coordinator is with the group from the first morning, and she is the person the travelers should call. Two no-shows last season cost the group a private slot, so the briefing is not a formality.

1. "If the group confirms before the end of the month, the hotel will hold the rate." Why present simple after if, and not will?
A Because the confirmation already happened.
B Because English never puts will straight after if in this structure: the condition takes the present, the result takes will.
C Because the sentence is about the past.
2. "The supplier would waive it if the reason were medical." What does this form tell you?
A That this is a hypothetical case, not something that has happened: it is on the table, not scheduled.
B That somebody in the group is already ill.
C That the fee has already been waived.
3. "If the group wanted October instead, the supplier would build the week around the smaller galleries." Is October being booked?
A Yes, October is now the confirmed month.
B Yes, because would always refers to a decision already taken.
C No. It is offered as an alternative, and the past plus would marks it as hypothetical.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- First and Second Conditionals

GRAMMAR

The two sentences every negotiation runs on.

UseFormExample
Real and likelyif + present simple, ... will + base verbIf you confirm, we will hold the rate.
Hypothetical or distantif + past simple, ... would + base verbIf you wanted October, we would build it differently.
Softer requestif you could ... , we would ...If you could move the arrival, we would fit both.
were, not wasformal writing uses were for all personsIf the group were smaller...
Either ordercomma only when if comes firstWe will hold the rate if you confirm.
when, not willafter when the verb is present tooWe will pay when we receive the invoice.

Never: if you will confirm · if we would move the dates · if the group was smaller, the price will go up · when we will receive the invoice.

Stage 2: Put the Negotiation in Order

Order

Six lines of your reply to the supplier. Put them in an order that gets you what you need.

?"Thank you for the quote. I have three questions before I confirm."
?"What is the lead time for the curator-led visit?"
?"Two travelers want single rooms, so please confirm the occupancy supplement."
?"If we confirm this week, will you hold the current rate?"
?"And if we moved the arrival to Wednesday, would the rate stay the same?"
?"Please send all of this in writing and I will release the deposit on Friday."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the sentence you would actually send.

You want to know what happens if the group confirms this week. You write:
A "If we will confirm this week, you hold the rate?"
B "If we confirm this week, will you hold the current rate?"
C "If we confirmed this week, will you held the rate?"
You are exploring an option that is not decided. You write:
A "If we moved the arrival to Wednesday, would the rate stay the same?"
B "If we would move the arrival, the rate stays the same?"
C "If we move the arrival, would the rate stayed the same?"
A traveler asks for a single room. You explain the cost. You say:
A "The room alone is more expensive, sorry, nothing to do."
B "Single occupancy carries a supplement per night. If you shared, the price per person would be lower."
C "If you will want a single room, you will pay more money."
You want everything confirmed on paper before you pay. You write:
A "I will send the deposit when I will receive your e-mail."
B "Please send a written quote valid for thirty days, and I will release the deposit when I receive it."
C "Send anything you want, we pay after."
