Lesson 4 -- Twelve People, Four Cities

Podcast
The Week in Art -- Venice Biennale special (The Art Newspaper)

The whole Biennale walked through by critics who have just been there: which pavilions are worth the queue, what the international exhibition is actually about, and how a visitor should plan the days. This is the raw material of the trips you organize, in the English you will need to sell them.

Tip: listen with a notebook and write down five things you would put in an itinerary. Then say each one out loud as a full sentence to a client.

Talk
William Ury -- "The walk from no to yes" (TED, 18 min)

The negotiator who wrote the standard book on the subject, explaining how a third position changes a conversation between two people who both want to win. It is the same structure you practiced today: state the condition, offer the alternative, keep the relationship.

Tip: watch it once for the argument, then write two conditional sentences you could use in your next supplier call, one with will and one with would.

Reading
La Biennale di Venezia -- the current Art exhibition, official pages

The source your travelers will ask you about, in the institution's own English: dates, venues, the curatorial statement and the list of national participations. Reading the official text is how you find out what is really included before a supplier tells you.

Tip: pick one pavilion, read its page, and write three lines you could send to your group about why it is worth an hour of their morning.
