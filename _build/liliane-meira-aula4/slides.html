Chapter 4: If and Would

Will Happen, or Would Happen

The two sentences every negotiation runs on

Grammar Discovery

What Changes Between These Four?

"If you confirm before the end of the month, we will hold the current rate."

"If the confirmation arrives in July, the rate will change."

"If you wanted the same program in October, we would build it around the smaller galleries."

"If the group were smaller, the price per person would go up."

The red verb decides how real the condition is, and the blue one follows it. They always move together.

UseFormExample
Real, likely, this weekif + present simple, ... will + base verbIf you confirm, we will hold the rate.
Hypothetical or distantif + past simple, ... would + base verbIf you wanted October, we would build it differently.
Softer requestif you could ... , we would ...If you could move the arrival, we would fit both.
Were, not wasformal writing uses were for all personsIf the group were smaller...
Either ordercomma only when if comes firstWe will hold the rate if you confirm.
The trapnever will straight after ifNot if you will confirm.

In one line: present plus will for what may really happen; past plus would for what is only on the table.

Listening 1 · A Voice Message

Good News and Less Good News

The supplier returning your call about the September group. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the good news, and how much extra time does it give?
The hotel has agreed to hold the ten rooms until the thirtieth, which is three weeks more than he said last time.
2. What is the problem with the curator, and what does it cost the group?
The curator now runs the pavilion visits on Thursdays only. If the group arrives on a Friday, they lose that visit completely.
3. What does he propose, and by when does he need an answer?
Moving the arrival to a Wednesday, so that both the pavilions and the Thursday visit fit. He needs an answer before Friday, because the deposit invoice goes out on Monday.
Listening 2 · The Night Briefing

The Boat Leaves at Ten Past Eight

A group leader briefing twelve adults the night before. Sound first — no text.

1. What time should everybody be downstairs, and what happens to anybody who is late?
Eight, because the boat leaves at ten past and will not wait. Whoever is late meets the group at the pavilion and does the walk alone.
2. What must they bring, and why that and not the other thing?
Their passport, not their card, because the private access list is checked against passports at the door.
3. Who should they call if something goes wrong, and what does she say about the free afternoon?
Marta, the on-site coordinator, not the leader and not the office in Milan. The afternoon is free on purpose and they should not fill it.
