Lesson 27 -- Pre-class

Navigating Difficult Conversations

Say difficult things calmly: open softly, name the concern, and ask for a change with "I'd rather you + past form" ("I'd rather you told me earlier"). Key words: concern, bring up, speak up, boundary, address, work through, let it slide, follow through.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Concern -- A worry about something, said calmly
"I have a small concern about the deadline."
Bring up -- To start talking about a subject, often a difficult one
"There's something I wanted to bring up before the meeting."
Speak up -- To say what you think, especially when it is not easy
"I should speak up earlier instead of waiting."
Boundary -- A limit you set on what is acceptable for you
"Weekends are a boundary for me."
Address -- To deal with a problem directly, without waiting
"We need to address this before Friday."
Work through -- To solve a problem step by step, usually together
"Let's work through it together."
Let it slide -- To ignore a problem and do nothing about it
"I let it slide twice, but not a third time."
Follow through -- To do what you said you would do
"He promised the numbers, but he didn't follow through."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

For two weeks I let it slide. Our supplier had promised a new delivery date and did not follow through, and I kept telling myself it was not a big deal. On Monday I finally decided to speak up. I called and said, "There is something I wanted to bring up. I have a concern about the delivery dates." Then I said the hard part: "I'd rather you told me when something is late. I'd prefer it if we didn't find out from the client." The manager was surprised, but not angry. We worked through a new schedule together, and we agreed to address problems on the same day from now on. Weekends are still a boundary for me, but Monday mornings are now for honest calls.

1. Why did she wait two weeks?
A The supplier was on vacation.
B Her manager told her to wait.
C She kept telling herself it was not a big deal.
2. What did she ask the supplier to do?
A Tell her when something is late.
B Cancel the order.
C Call the client directly.
3. Which sentence uses a past form but talks about the future?
A "I let it slide."
B "I'd rather you told me when something is late."
C "We worked through a new schedule."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- I'd rather you + past form

GRAMMAR

To ask someone else to change what they do, English uses a past form with a present or future meaning.

StructureUseExample
I'd rather you + past formAsk someone else to change (now or next time)I'd rather you told me earlier.
I'd prefer it if you + past formThe same meaning, a little softerI'd prefer it if you didn't move the meeting.
Negative: didn't + base verbAsk someone NOT to do somethingI'd rather you didn't reply without me.
I'd rather + base verbAbout YOUR OWN actionI'd rather call than write.

The past form here does NOT mean the past: 'I'd rather you told me' is about next time. Never 'I'd rather you tell me' or 'I'd prefer if you don't'. And remember: 'follow through on something', 'address a problem' (no 'about').

Order
