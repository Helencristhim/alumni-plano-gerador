Chapter 3

I'd Rather You

A past form that talks about next time

Grammar Discovery

Past Form, Future Meaning?

"I'd rather you told me earlier next time."

"I'd prefer it if we didn't move the meeting again."

"I'd rather you didn't reply to the client without me."

"I'd rather call her than send another email."

FormUseExample
I'd rather you + past formAsk someone else to change what they do (now or next time)I'd rather you told me earlier.
I'd prefer it if you + past formThe same meaning, a little softerI'd prefer it if you didn't move the meeting.
Negative: didn't + base verbAsk someone NOT to do somethingI'd rather you didn't reply without me.
I'd rather + base verbAbout YOUR OWN action (Lesson 12)I'd rather call than write.
⚠ CarefulAfter 'I'd rather you' and 'I'd prefer it if', use the past form, but the meaning is now or next time: 'I'd rather you told me', never 'I'd rather you tell me'. With your OWN action, no new subject and no past: 'I'd rather call'. And 'address' takes no 'about': 'address the problem'.

Chapter 4

Real Voices

A hard conversation, handled well

Dialogue

The Third Reschedule

V
Daniel, do you have five minutes? There's something I wanted to bring up.
D
Sure. Is everything okay?
V
Mostly. I have a small concern about our weekly review. We've moved it three times this month.
D
You're right. I'm sorry, my calendar has been a mess since the audit started.
V
I understand. But I'd rather we kept one fixed slot. I can't let it slide again, because my team plans around it.
D
That's fair. And the numbers I promised last Friday... I didn't follow through, did I?
V
Not yet. I'd prefer it if you told me when something is late. Then we can work through it together.
D
Deal. Thursday at ten, every week. And I'll send the numbers today.
Comprehension

Did You Catch It?

1. Why has the weekly review moved so often, according to Daniel?
His calendar has been a mess since the audit started.
2. What did Daniel fail to follow through on?
The numbers he promised last Friday.
3. What does Daniel agree to at the end?
A fixed slot, Thursday at ten every week, and he will send the numbers today.
Listening

The Sandwich

Listen first. No text. Sound only.

1. What would the speaker do before she learned the structure?
She would let small things slide until they became big things, and then she would explode at the worst moment.
2. What are the three parts of the sandwich?
A soft opening, the honest part in one or two sentences, and a way forward.
3. What mistake do most people make?
They make the opening and the close so long that the real message disappears.
Listening

My First Boundary

A different voice, a different message. Listen carefully.

1. What was the speaker's first boundary?
He would not answer messages after eight in the evening, unless it was an emergency.
2. How did his manager react?
She simply said "fine, thanks for telling me". There was no long discussion.
3. What is his forty-eight-hour rule?
If he still cares about a problem after two days, he addresses it. If not, he really lets it go.

Spot the Error

0 / 4 errors found

"I'd rather you tell me earlier next time."
"I'd rather you told me earlier next time." (after "I'd rather you", use the past form)
"I'd prefer if you don't move the meeting."
"I'd prefer it if you didn't move the meeting." ("prefer it if" + past form)
"He didn't follow through his promise."
"He didn't follow through on his promise." (follow through ON something)
"We need to address about this problem."
"We need to address this problem." (no "about" after "address")
