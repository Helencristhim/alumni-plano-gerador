Lesson 16 -- Pre-class

The Global Presentation -- Full Rehearsal

Prepare the full rehearsal of your presentation to the global director: twelve minutes, three hard questions, one clear request. Key words: rehearsal, to run over, pace, to rush, to pause, filler word, eye contact, body language, nerves, cue card, closing line, delivery. Structures: the code of lessons 1 to 15 in one presentation (present simple for your role, past simple for results, because of, the first conditional for the plan, reported speech for the update).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the definition. Four are about practice and time, four about your voice and your body, four about the moments that matter.

Rehearsal -- a practice of a presentation or performance before the real event
"We have a full rehearsal on Thursday."
To run over -- to take more time than you planned
"Last year two managers ran over by ten minutes."
Pace -- the speed at which you speak or do something
"Slow down your pace when you give a number."
To rush -- to do something too quickly
"I rushed the results, and nobody understood them."
To pause -- to stop speaking for a short moment
"Pause after every important number."
Filler word -- a word like um or so that fills a silence but adds nothing
"My most common filler word is so."
Eye contact -- looking directly at the people you are speaking to
"On a video call, eye contact means looking at the camera."
Body language -- the message your face, hands and posture send
"Open body language makes you look confident."
Nerves -- the worried feeling you have before something important
"My nerves are worst in the first minute."
Cue card -- a small card with the key words you want to say
"I keep my opening on a cue card."
Closing line -- the last sentence of a presentation, the one people remember
"My closing line is one clear request."
Delivery -- the way you speak and move when you present
"Your content is strong. Now work on your delivery."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read about Renato's rehearsal and answer the questions. Notice the words in bold: each one comes from a different lesson.

On Thursday, Renato did a full rehearsal of his presentation with Claire Whitfield, the global commercial director. He started with a hook: "Last year, my team grew market share by three percentage points." Then he said who he is: "I lead the commercial team for the South and Southeast."

In the middle, he explained the drop: units fell because of a stockout in August. He presented the plan with a condition: "If we get six weeks of stock, we will bounce back." And he gave an update: one doctor told us she would come back in December.

He finished in eleven minutes, so he did not run over. Claire told him that the content was strong, but that he rushed the numbers. Her advice was simple: slow down your pace, pause after every number, and look at the camera.

1. Why does Renato say "I lead the team" and not "I am leading the team"?
A Because the present continuous is too informal for a presentation to a global director and her whole team.
B Because lead is an irregular verb.
C Because his role is permanent, so he uses the present simple.
2. Which sentence is correct for his results?
A "Last year, my team has grown market share by three percentage points in the South."
B "Last year, my team grew market share by three points."
C "Last year, my team grows market share."
3. Which sentence about the plan is correct?
A "If we get six weeks of stock, we will bounce back."
B "If we will get six weeks of stock, we will bounce back."
C "If we got six weeks of stock, we bounce back in the fourth quarter, as the plan says."
4. What did Claire tell Renato after the rehearsal?
A She told him that his presentation was too short and that he needed to add more slides and more details.
B She told him to read his slides.
C She told him to slow down his pace when he gives a number.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Code of Your Presentation

GRAMMAR

There is no new grammar in this lesson. Every part of the presentation uses a structure you already learned. This table is your map.

PartStructureExample
Who you arepresent simpleI lead the team. I do not manage the North.
Experiencepresent perfect + for / sinceI have worked at Ipsen for eight years.
Resultspast simple (finished time)Last year, my team grew by three points.
The dropbecause + sentence · because of + nounUnits fell because of a stockout.
The planfirst conditionalIf we get the stock, we will bounce back.
The updatereported speechShe told us she would come back.
Two trapsNever last year we have grown — finished time takes the past simple. And never if we will get — after if, use the present.

Film
The King's Speech (O Discurso do Rei, 2010)

The true story of King George VI, who had to speak to millions of people on the radio and was terrified of it. The film is about rehearsal: breathing, pace, pauses, and a coach who will not let him give up. The final speech is one of the best scenes about nerves ever filmed.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. In the final speech, notice where the King pauses, and how slow his pace is. Then say the first line of your own presentation at the same pace.

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart -- High-Stakes Communication: How to Manage Anxiety Speaking in Front of Others

A free episode from Stanford Graduate School of Business. Matt Abrahams explains why nerves before a big presentation are normal, and gives simple techniques to manage them: breathing, a short routine before you start, and thinking of the presentation as a conversation.

Tip: listen before your real presentation. Choose one technique and use it in the minute before you start.
