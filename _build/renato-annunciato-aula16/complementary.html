Lesson 16 -- The Global Presentation

Film
The King's Speech (O Discurso do Rei, 2010)

The true story of King George VI, who had to speak to millions of people on the radio and was terrified of it. The film is about rehearsal: breathing, pace, pauses, and a coach who will not let him give up. The final speech is one of the best scenes about nerves ever filmed.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. In the final speech, notice where the King pauses, and how slow his pace is. Then say the first line of your own presentation at the same pace.

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart -- High-Stakes Communication: How to Manage Anxiety Speaking in Front of Others

A free episode from Stanford Graduate School of Business. Matt Abrahams explains why nerves before a big presentation are normal, and gives simple techniques to manage them: breathing, a short routine before you start, and thinking of the presentation as a conversation.

Tip: listen before your real presentation. Choose one technique and use it in the minute before you start.

YouTube
Chris Anderson -- TED's Secret to Great Public Speaking (TED)

The head of TED explains what the best talks have in common: one central idea, built step by step, in language the audience already understands. It is a short talk, and a good final check for your own presentation.

Tip: watch it once with subtitles. Then answer one question out loud: what is the ONE idea of your presentation, in one sentence?
