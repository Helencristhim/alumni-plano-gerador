Stage 1.1: Words for After November

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say each word out loud with your own numbers. Eight words, three minutes.

Rusty — not as good as before, because you stopped practising
"My English was rusty, not gone."
Brush up on — to practise something again to get it back
"I brushed up on it before the trip."
Out of your comfort zone — doing something that is not easy or familiar
"Every lunch was out of my comfort zone."
Make the most of — to get everything you can from an opportunity
"I want to make the most of those two weeks."
Worth it — good enough to justify the effort
"It was uncomfortable, and it was worth it."
At your own pace — at the speed that works for you
"I am going to keep going at my own pace."
From now on — starting today, and continuing
"From now on, thirty minutes a day."
Keep it up — to continue doing something good
"Ten weeks is nothing if I do not keep it up."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the text. Then answer the questions.

Ten weeks ago my English was not gone, it was rusty — I had not used it at work for twenty years. I brushed up on it because I had a reason and a date. Almost every class took me out of my comfort zone, especially the ones where I had to ask questions instead of answering them, and it was worth it. Now the difficult part starts. I go back to a job where nobody speaks English, so if I do nothing, in three months I am back where I started. So, from now on: one recurring meeting in English, one article a week read out loud in the car, and one message a month to somebody from the programme. Three things, not ten. At my own pace, but every week. I want to make the most of what I built here, and the only way to do that is to keep it up when nobody is watching.
What does he say about his English ten weeks ago?
A It was rusty, not gone.
B He had never studied it.
Why does he say the difficult part starts now?
A Because he goes back to a job where nobody speaks English.
B Because the classes were easy.
How many things did he commit to, and how often?
A Three things, every week.
B Ten things, every day.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

No new grammar. Everything you have, on one page.

Nine lessons, nine shapes. Each one answers a question somebody will actually ask you.

Who you are

I oversee a desk. (1)
I have been here for 23 years. (2)
We are restructuring. (3)
I’m going to present. (4)

What you do with it

As far as I know… (5)
It is slower than ours. (6)
I was wondering why… (7)
Initially, we were… (8)
You should talk to her. (9)

× "getting rust" — the adjective is rusty
× "worth to do it" — after worth the verb takes -ing: worth doing
× "From now, thirty minutes" — the phrase is from now on

The one thing that decides the next six months is not on this page: whether you keep it up when nobody is watching.

Stage 4: In the room

Quiz
Somebody asks if the programme was worth it. Which answer says something?
A "Yes, very good, very interesting."
B "It was uncomfortable for four days, and then it was worth it. I stopped preparing sentences in my head."
Which plan for the six months after actually survives December?
A "I am going to study English every day and read a lot and watch films and practise speaking."
B "One meeting in English, one article a week out loud, one message a month."
Which sentence is correct?
A "It was worth to do it."
B "It was worth doing it."

Stage 5: The last recording

Reflection
Record three minutes in English — the longest of the programme, and the last. Do the whole thing in one go: introduce yourself, say how long you have been at the bank, what your team is doing this quarter, what you are going to do in November, and one question you would ask in a session. Then finish with the three things you are going to do on your own after November. When you are done, listen to your very first recording from lesson 1, and then this one, back to back. That difference is the ten weeks.
