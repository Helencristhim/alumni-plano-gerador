Before class 1: The questions you will need

Vocabulary

Eight lines you will use on any call where someone asks you for something. Tap Listen once for each. Do not memorise them — just let your ear meet them before class.

Before I confirm, can I check one thing? — buys you time without saying no
Use it the second someone asks for something you are not sure about.
Can I ask what's driving this? — asks for the reason behind the request
The single most useful question in this lesson.
What happens if we don't? — finds out how serious the request really is
The answer tells you whether it is a wish or a problem.
Just to be clear — checks one detail before you move on
"Just to be clear, the length stays the same?"
Do you mean X or Y? — turns a vague request into a choice
"Do you mean lighter, or physically smaller?"
So what you need is — says the requirement back in your own words
If you get this one wrong, they correct you — which is the point.
Would it help if — tests another way without promising it
"Would it help if we shipped fewer per box?"
I'll confirm by Thursday — closes with a date instead of a promise
A date is what turns "I'll check" into something real.

Before class 2: Until they are automatic

Speaking

This is the part that matters. In class you will say these to a customer who is not making it easy, and under pressure you only reach for what your mouth already knows. Record each one until it comes out without thinking.

Before I confirm, can I check one thing?
Can I ask what's driving this?
Just to be clear, the length stays the same?
So what you need is a lighter box, not a thinner part.
I'll confirm by Thursday.

Before class 3: Bring two real ones

Reflection

In class the conversation will be about your factory, not a textbook one. Come with the raw material.

Think of a request from a customer where what they asked for was NOT what they needed. Record 60 seconds: what they asked for, what it turned out to be, and how you found out. If none comes to mind, describe the request you get most often — and say what you think is really behind it.
Now the uncomfortable one. Think of a time you said yes too fast — you agreed, started the work, and only then understood what was really being asked. Record 40 seconds: what did agreeing cost, and which question would have saved you?
