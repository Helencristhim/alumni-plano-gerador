Chapter 4: The Code

The Little Question at the End

Question tags

Grammar Discovery

Where Does the Tag Come From?

"You're new here, aren't you?"

"It's been a long day, hasn't it?"

"You didn't get much sleep, did you?"

"She can cover the clinic, can't she?"

Look at the red tags and the blue one. Which verb does each tag repeat? When is the tag negative, and when is it positive?

JobFormExample
Positive sentencenegative tagYou're new here, aren't you?
Negative sentencepositive tagYou didn't sleep, did you?
The tag repeats the auxiliarybe / have / can / will / do / didIt's been busy, hasn't it? She'll come, won't she?
No auxiliary in the sentencedo / does / didYou work in dermatology, don't you?
Special casesI am → aren't I · Let's → shall weI'm late, aren't I? Let's start, shall we?
Intonationfalling = small talk, rising = real questionNice day, isn't it? ↘ · You're on call, aren't you? ↗

In one line: the tag repeats the auxiliary of the sentence with the opposite sign — and with a falling voice, it is not a question, it is an invitation to talk.

Listening 1 · An Attending

Three Things I Watch For

An attending on what makes international residents sound American. Sound first — no text.

1. According to her, which international residents struggle the most?
The ones who go straight to the point, not the ones with weak medicine.
2. Why does she say "Long night, wasn't it?" before a case discussion?
Nobody expects a real answer. It is a way of saying: I see you, and we are on the same team.
3. What does she mean when she says "Let's touch base tomorrow"?
That she is closing the conversation for now.
Listening 2 · A Resident

The Month I Sounded Rude

An international resident on the reputation he did not know he had. Sound first — no text.

1. What reputation did he have in his first month?
People thought he was rude, although he was only trying to be efficient.
2. Who told him, and where?
A nurse, over coffee. She was kind about it.
3. What two things did he change?
He started adding could you and would you mind, and he started asking the nurses about their weekends.
