To touch base -- to talk briefly to check how things are going

To hang out -- to spend free time with somebody

To grab -- to get something quickly, informally

Blunt -- so direct that it can sound rude
"Give me the chart sounds blunt in English."
To soften -- to make what you say less direct or less strong
"You can soften a request with could you."
A cue -- a signal that tells you what to do or say next
"When she stands up, that is your cue to finish."
Awkward -- uncomfortable or embarrassing
"There was an awkward silence after his joke."

"We chatted about football while we waited."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

In American hospitals, a lot of small talk ends with a question tag: It’s been a long morning, hasn’t it? You’re new here, aren’t you? The tag is not a real question. With a falling voice, it is an invitation to talk, and the other person usually answers with more than yes or no.

The tag repeats the auxiliary of the sentence, with the opposite sign. A positive sentence takes a negative tag: She can cover the clinic, can’t she? A negative sentence takes a positive tag: You didn’t sleep much, did you? When there is no auxiliary, the tag uses do, does or did: You work in dermatology, don’t you?

Brazilian speakers often use isn’t it? for everything, the way Portuguese uses one short word at the end of any sentence. In English the tag changes with the verb. Two special cases: I’m late, aren’t I? and Let’s start, shall we? Tags also soften a request: You couldn’t check room four for me, could you? sounds much less blunt than Check room four.

1. "It's been a long morning, ___?" Which tag completes the sentence?
A isn't it, because the subject is it and the morning is singular

2. What does a question tag with a falling voice usually mean?

3. Which sentence is correct?
A You didn't sleep much last night after the long shift, didn't you?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Question Tags

GRAMMAR

The tag repeats the auxiliary of the sentence with the opposite sign. A falling voice means small talk; a rising voice means a real question.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You need the chart from a nurse you do not know. You say:
A Give me the chart now, because I need to see the labs before the attending arrives.
B Chart, please.
C Could you grab the chart for me?
In the elevator, a colleague looks tired after a night on call. You say:
A Long night, wasn't it?
B You look very tired today. Did you sleep badly, or is it the work here?
C Why are you so tired?
Your attending says "Let's touch base later." What does she mean?
A She wants to meet you at the base of the building after work today, at five o'clock.
B The conversation is over for now, and you will talk again later.
C She is angry with you.
At lunch with new colleagues, the silence feels awkward. You say:
A How much do residents earn in this hospital, more or less, after taxes?
B Nothing, and you wait.
C So, do you have any plans for the weekend?
