Chapter 3: The Code

Two Events, One Sentence

No conjunction, no repeated subject — and it sounds like a native

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Having cleared immigration, he went looking for the shuttle."

"Arriving at midnight, you see almost nothing of a city."

"Given the delay, the connection was never going to work."

"With the border closed, onward travel was impossible."

Each of these could be two sentences joined by and, because or after. What has been removed — and what has to be true for that to work?

FormWhat it doesExample
Having + past participleThe first action is FINISHED before the second begins. Replaces after he had cleared — and the subject of both halves must be the same."Having cleared immigration, he looked for the shuttle."
-ing (present participle)The two things happen together, or the first causes the second. Replaces as, while or because."Arriving at midnight, you see almost nothing."
"Not knowing the city, I took a taxi."
Given · Faced with
Left · Seen from		A past participle opening, with a passive sense. Given is the most useful of all of them and behaves almost like a preposition."Given the delay, the connection failed."
"Faced with a closed desk, we waited."
With + noun + participleAdds a circumstance with its OWN subject — which is how you escape the same-subject rule when you need to."With the border closed, travel was impossible."
The rule that mattersThe participle belongs to the subject of the main clause. Break that and you get the dangling participle, which is grammatically wrong and quietly funny.NOT "Having cleared immigration, the shuttle was waiting."
(the shuttle cleared immigration?)

This is the structure that makes written English compact and spoken English fluent. It is also the one that separates a careful C1 from a B2: not because it is difficult to form, but because remembering that both halves need the same subject takes attention you do not have at the end of a long sentence.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"Having cleared immigration, the shuttle was waiting outside."
"Having cleared immigration, he found the shuttle waiting outside."
"Arrived at midnight, I saw almost nothing of the city."
"Arriving at midnight, I saw almost nothing of the city."
"Given to the delay, the connection was never going to work."
"Given the delay, the connection was never going to work."

The first is the dangling participle: the shuttle did not clear immigration. Always ask who is doing the participle, and make sure it is the subject of the main clause. The second confuses the two forms — arriving for an action you perform, arrived only in a passive sense. And given takes no preposition: never given to.

Comprehension

About Yasmin

1. What does Yasmin say her system is mostly about?
Not being angry.
2. What does she do when the queue will not move?
She goes and finds the one person who actually has information.
3. What kind of question does she say works?
A specific one somebody can answer — not when the flight will go, but whether anybody can rebook her tonight.
4. What did she book in the end, and why?
A hotel for two nights and a flight for the third, because she did not know whether the border would reopen.
Listening 1

The Announcement at Two in the Morning

A gate announcement nobody wants to hear. Sound first — no text.

1. Why is the flight not departing?
The border has closed and the crew have run out of legal hours.
2. What are passengers with onward connections told to do?
Go to the transfer desk in the arrivals hall, not to the gate.
3. What is offered, and what is not?
Hotel accommodation is offered; rebooking cannot be done at the gate.
Listening 2

What the Ground Staff Wish You Knew

An airline duty manager, talking frankly. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say is the single most useful thing a passenger can do?
Ask a specific question that somebody is able to answer.
2. Why does she say shouting fails?
Because the staff at the desk have no authority to change anything.
3. What does she say about passengers who are calm?
That they are remembered, and that being remembered at three in the morning is worth a great deal.
