Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A percentage point -- the unit for the difference between two percentages
"The rate went from 4% to 6%: up two percentage points."
Roughly -- about; not exactly
"The project will cost roughly two billion reais."
A majority -- more than half of a group
"A majority of the users approved the new service."
A fraction -- a small part of something
"Only a fraction of the budget went to maintenance."
A survey -- a set of questions that you ask many people to collect information
"The survey was answered by two thousand users."
A sample -- a small group that represents a larger group in a study
"A sample of fifty users is too small for a conclusion."
A trend -- a general direction in which something is changing
"The trend is clear: traffic grows every year."
To double -- to become two times bigger
"Traffic at the port has doubled in ten years."
To account for -- to be a particular part of a total
"Ports account for most of the state's exports."
Evidence -- facts or information that show that something is true
"There is strong evidence that the service improved."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Before a public hearing, Aguinaldo prepares the numbers for a port concession. The port handles roughly a third of the state's exports, and traffic has doubled in ten years.

Just over two thousand users answered the survey. A majority said the service has improved, but only a few said the tariff is fair. There is strong evidence on quality, and very little on price.

He checks one detail twice: the tariff went from 10% to 12%. That is two percentage points, not two percent. Notice: a few with countable nouns, little with uncountable nouns like evidence.

1. How many users answered the survey?
A A little more than two thousand.
B Exactly two thousand, because "just over" means exactly the same number in English.
C Almost one thousand, because "just over" means a little less than the number.
2. Why does the text say "very little evidence" and not "very few evidence"?
A Because "few" is only for negative sentences, and this sentence is negative.
B Because evidence is uncountable.
C Because "little" is more formal than "few", and a hearing is a formal situation.
3. The tariff went from 10% to 12%. Which sentence is correct?
A "The tariff rose by two percent, from ten to twelve percent of the cargo value."
B "It rose by two percentage points."
C "The tariff rose by twelve percentage points, from ten to twelve percent of cargo value."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Quantifiers and Approximate Numbers

GRAMMAR

Countable or uncountable, all or part, exact or approximate: the small word gives the number its weight.

JobFormExample
Countable nouns (investors, projects)many / a few / few / a number ofA few concessions were cancelled.
Uncountable nouns (money, evidence, data)much / a little / little / a large amount ofVery little evidence supports it.
A part of a specific groupmost of the / half of the / a third of the + nounMost of the users were happy.
Approximate numbersroughly / around / just over / just under / almostJust over two thousand users answered.
Percent or percentage pointsfrom 4% to 6% = two percentage points (a 50% increase)The rate rose by two percentage points.
The trapfew = almost none (negative); a few = some (positive)Few funds came (bad). A few funds came (good).

Never: very few evidence · Most of investors · Few investors came, so it was a success · from 10% to 12% = two percent
