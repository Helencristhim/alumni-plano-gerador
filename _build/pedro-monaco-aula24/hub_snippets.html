Lesson 24 -- Pre-class

Who Checked the Contract?

Legal teams under pressure delegate more and more: to AI tools, to outside firms, to paralegals. What can you delegate, and what stays with the lawyer who signs? Key expressions: a redline, a first pass, a turnaround time, a backlog, a bottleneck, a playbook, a hallucination, a rubber stamp, a retainer, legal privilege, to delegate, to streamline, to double-check, to fall through the cracks. Structure: the causative -- have something done, get something done, have somebody do something, get somebody to do something.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. All fourteen belong to the same world: how legal work moves, where it gets stuck, and what can go wrong when a machine reads it.

A redline -- a version of a contract that shows every change made to the previous draft
"The other side sent back a redline with forty changes."
A first pass -- a quick first review to find the obvious problems
"The tool does a first pass, and then a lawyer reads the whole thing."
A turnaround time -- the time it takes to finish a task and send it back
"The business wants a turnaround time of two days for every NDA."
A backlog -- a pile of work that is waiting to be done
"We have a backlog of sixty contracts waiting for review."
A bottleneck -- the one point that slows down the whole process
"The legal team became the bottleneck for every sale."
A playbook -- a document with a team's standard position on each clause or situation
"The playbook says which clauses we can accept and which we must reject."
A hallucination -- false information that an AI tool presents as if it were true
"The brief cited a case that did not exist: it was a hallucination."
A rubber stamp -- an approval given automatically, without any real checking
"If the lawyer only signs, the review becomes a rubber stamp."
A retainer -- a fee paid in advance so that a law firm is available when you need it
"We keep a firm in New York on a retainer for the US deals."
Legal privilege -- the right to keep communications with your lawyer confidential
"Pasting the memo into a public chatbot can put legal privilege at risk."
To delegate -- to give part of your work to somebody else
"You can delegate the task, but not the responsibility."
To streamline -- to make a process simpler and faster
"The new template helped us streamline the approval process."
To double-check -- to check something again to be sure that it is correct
"Always double-check every citation before you file the brief."
To fall through the cracks -- to be forgotten or missed because nobody was responsible for it
"Without a tracker, renewal dates fall through the cracks."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Pedro's team has a new process for contracts from the US office. Before, every NDA went straight to a lawyer, and the queue grew every week. Now they have every NDA reviewed by an AI tool first. The tool does not sign anything; it only marks the unusual clauses. Then Pedro has a lawyer read the redline before it goes back to the other side.

Last week, a partner in Chicago sent a contract with a strange clause on data. Pedro did not guess. He got the partner to explain where the data would be stored, and he got the answer confirmed in writing.

Notice the pattern. With a thing, the verb after the object is a past participle: have every NDA reviewed. With a person, have takes the base form (have a lawyer read) and get takes to + base form (get the partner to explain).

His manager liked the process, but she gave him one warning: when we have something done by a tool, we still own the result. Pedro wrote it on a sticky note and put it on his screen.

1. What does Pedro's team do with every NDA now?
A They have it reviewed by an AI tool first.
B They send every NDA straight to a lawyer, as they did before, and the lawyer reads it from the first page to the last.
C They get the other side to review it.
2. In Pedro has a lawyer read the redline, which form comes after a lawyer?
A To + base form: to read, the same form that comes after get and a person.
B The past participle.
C The base form: read.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A He got the partner explain where the data would be stored, and he got it in writing.
B He got the partner to explain where the data would be stored.
C He got explained the partner the data.
4. What is the manager's warning?
A That the team is still responsible for work done by a tool.
B That AI tools should never be used for NDAs, because a lawyer has to read every contract from the first page to the last.
C That Pedro should write fewer notes.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Causative

GRAMMAR

Use the causative when somebody else does the work but you made it happen. The sentence keeps you at the center: you arranged it, so you are still responsible for it.

PatternWhen to use itExample
have + object + past participleSomebody else does the work; you arranged it.We had the contract reviewed.
get + object + past participleThe same idea, more informal. Often some effort.I got the redline sent before lunch.
have + person + base formAn instruction to somebody on your team.I had the paralegal check the dates.
get + person + to + base formYou persuade somebody.We got the client to sign.
have + object + past participle (bad event)Something bad happened to you.The firm had its systems hacked.
TensesChange have or get. The participle never changes.We are going to have the playbook updated.

Watch the word order: the object comes BEFORE the past participle. We had the contract reviewed, never we had reviewed the contract, which means that you reviewed it yourself.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You work in the legal team of a Brazilian company with a new office in the US. Choose the answer a professional would actually give.

1. A colleague wants to paste a client's memo into a public chatbot to get a summary. You say:
A Sure, go ahead. Public chatbots are confidential by default, so nothing you paste there can ever leave the conversation or reach anybody else.
B Only if the memo is short.
C Please use our approved tool instead. A public chatbot could put legal privilege at risk.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A I had the associate to double-check the citations.
B I had the associate double-check the citations.
C I had double-checked the associate the citations, as the judge had asked us to do.
3. The tool's report is clean, and the director wants to sign today. You say:
A Let's sign it now and have somebody read it next month, when the backlog is smaller and the team has more time for it.
B Fine, a clean report means there are no problems.
C I'd like to have a lawyer read the definitions first.
4. You want the vendor to explain where your documents will be stored. You write:
A Could you get your team explain where our documents will be stored and who will be able to see them?
B Could you get your team to explain where our documents will be stored?
C Could you have explained your team our documents?
5. A sales manager says that legal is always the bottleneck. You answer:
A We can streamline the routine work: we'll have every NDA screened by the tool and checked by one lawyer within a day.
B That's true, and nothing can be done about it.
C We'll stop reviewing NDAs.
