Chapter 3: The Code

Somebody Else Did It, You Arranged It

Have it done, get it done, have somebody do it, get somebody to do it

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"We had the NDA reviewed by an AI tool overnight."

"I got the contract translated before the meeting."

"The general counsel had a junior associate check every citation."

"We finally got the vendor to sign the data protection addendum."

In each sentence, who actually did the work? And look at the orange words: when do you see a past participle, when a base form, and when to + base form?

PatternWhat it meansExample
have + object + past participleSomebody else does the work. You arranged it."We had the contract reviewed."
get + object + past participleThe same idea, a little more informal. Often there was some effort."I got the redline sent before lunch."
have + person + base formYou give an instruction to somebody who works with you."I had the paralegal check the dates."
get + person + to + base formYou persuade somebody who did not have to do it."We got the client to sign."
have + object + past participle (a bad event)Something bad happened to you, and you did not arrange it."The firm had its systems hacked."
Change the tense?Change have or get. The participle stays the same."We are having / will have / have had it reviewed."

The causative tells the listener that you did not do the work yourself, but you made it happen. In a contract dispute, that difference matters: the person who arranged the work is still responsible for it.

Meaning

Same Fact, Different Focus

The plain fact first, then the causative version

"An AI tool reviewed the NDA."

"We had the NDA reviewed by an AI tool."

The focus moves to who arranged the review.

"A translator translated the contract for me."

"I got the contract translated."

Shorter, and nobody needs to know who the translator was.

"I asked the paralegal to check the dates, and she did."

"I had the paralegal check the dates."

An instruction to somebody on your team.

"We asked the vendor many times, and finally it signed."

"We finally got the vendor to sign."

It took some persuasion.

Clearing the Backlog

A legal operations manager explains how her team works now. Simulated audio, recorded for this lesson. Sound first — no text.

1. What problem did the legal team have when she joined?
A backlog of almost two hundred contracts, and the sales team called them the bottleneck.
2. What were the three changes?
A playbook, a tool that does the first pass on every NDA, and a lawyer who reads every redline.
3. What did she get the vendor to do last month?
To show exactly where the company's documents are stored.
Listening 2

Page Fourteen

A general counsel remembers the contract he almost signed. Simulated audio, recorded for this lesson. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the AI tool's report say about the supply agreement?
It was clean: no red flags and nothing outside the playbook.
2. Who found the problem, and what was it?
A junior associate in her second week. The partner had changed the definition of losses, and the liability cap almost disappeared.
3. What did the general counsel change after that day?
He had the checklist updated, so that a person now reads every definition in full.
