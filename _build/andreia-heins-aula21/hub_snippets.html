Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Pitch -- a short talk to convince people to support an idea
"My pitch is only sixty seconds long."
Audience -- the group of people who watch and listen to a talk
"The audience at the meeting was mostly surgeons."
Hook -- a short story, fact or question at the start that catches attention
"A surprising number is a good hook."
Outline -- a short list of the main parts of a talk, in order
"I give the outline right after the hook."
Signpost -- a phrase that tells the audience where the talk is going next
"Moving on to the results is a clear signpost."
Emphasize -- to show that something is very important, often by saying it strongly
"I emphasize the key number and then I pause."
Engage -- to keep people interested and involved in what you say
"A short question at the start can engage the room."
Rehearse -- to practice a talk out loud before you give it for real
"I rehearse in the car on the way to the hospital."
Pace -- how fast or how slowly you speak
"A calm pace helps the audience follow you."
Conclude -- to end a talk with a final message
"I conclude with one clear request."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Next week, Andreia has sixty seconds to present the robotic surgery training program at a regional meeting. Her pitch must be short and clear, so she plans every step.

She opens with a hook, a true story: a surgery that stopped for forty minutes. Then she gives a quick outline: "Today I'd like to talk about the problem, the solution and the results." Between the parts she uses signposts: "Let me start with the problem," and "Moving on to the results..." After let me and I'd like to, she uses the base verb. After a preposition, like by or moving on to, she uses -ing or a noun.

She will emphasize one number, watch her pace, and conclude with a request: "Let me finish by asking for your support." Tonight she will rehearse it three times.

1. How does Andreia open her pitch?
A With her name and her job title.
B With the final request.
C With a true story about a surgery that stopped.
2. Which form comes after "Let me"?
A to + verb: "Let me to start."
B The base verb: "Let me start."
C -ing: "Let me starting."
3. Which form comes after "finish by"?
A "finish by asking"
B "finish by ask"
C "finish by to ask"

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Signposting Verb Patterns

GRAMMAR

After let me, I'd like to and I'm going to: base verb. After a preposition: -ing or a noun.

SignpostThenExample
Let me ...base verbLet me start with a story.
I'd like to ... / I'm going to ...base verbI'd like to talk about training.
start by ... / finish by ...-ingI'll start by telling you a story.
Moving on to ...noun or -ingMoving on to the results ...
To sum up, ...full sentenceTo sum up, training saves time.

Never: Let me to explain · I'd like to talking · I'll finish by ask · In resume, ...
