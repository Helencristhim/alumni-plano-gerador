Sixty Seconds to Convince

A regional meeting, a full room, and one minute to present the robotic surgery training program. Today you learn to structure a pitch and guide your audience from the first word to the last.

Warm-up

From the Meeting to the Stage

The Target

Three Moves of a Good Pitch

1. Open with something people remember: a story or a number.

2. Guide the audience step by step, so nobody gets lost.

3. Close with one clear message and one clear request.

Chapter 3: The Code

What Comes After the Signpost?

Base verb or -ing?

Grammar Discovery

Base Verb or -ing?

"Today I'd like to talk about our training program."

"Let me start with a quick story."

"Moving on to the results, we trained forty nurses."

"I'll finish by asking for your support."

What form comes after the orange words? And after the blue ones?

SignpostThenExample
Let me ...base verbLet me start with a story.
I'd like to ... / I'm going to ...base verbI'd like to talk about training.
start by ... / finish by ...-ingI'll finish by asking for support.
Moving on to ... / Turning to ...noun or -ingMoving on to the results ...
To sum up, ...a full sentenceTo sum up, better training means safer surgery.

After let me, I'd like to and I'm going to: base verb. After a preposition (by, to in moving on to): -ing or a noun.

The Rehearsal

A
Hello, my name is Andreia, and I am a nurse at Einstein. I work with robotic surgery and...
L
Let me stop you there. Everybody starts like that. You need a hook. What happened last year?
A
A surgery stopped for forty minutes. Nobody knew how to fix the console.
L
That's your hook. Start with that story. Then give them a quick outline.
A
Today I'd like to talk about the problem, the solution and the results. Moving on to the solution...
L
Much better. Your signposts are clear. Just slow down your pace and pause after the big number.
A
Got it. And I'll conclude by asking for their support for two more hospitals.
