Lesson 25 -- The Business Review

Film
Margin Call (2011) -- the late-night boardroom scene

An investment bank discovers a problem in the middle of the night, and a young analyst has to explain it to the head of the company, a man who has never seen his work. The CEO says it clearly: explain it to me as simply as you can. The scene is a masterclass in the meeting you practised today -- a stranger with power, very little time, and one number that decides everything. Connection to Lesson 25: listen to how every person and every number is introduced with who, which and that.

Tip: watch the boardroom scene with English subtitles. Write down 5 sentences with who, which, that or whose. Then write your own headline for that meeting in one sentence.

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart -- "How Leaders Sound Smart Without Saying Too Much" (with Adam Bryant)

Stanford lecturer Matt Abrahams talks to Adam Bryant, who has interviewed hundreds of CEOs, about the skill that decides a business review: taking something complex and making it simple without making it wrong. His advice is three or four points, never more, and one clear story behind them. Connection to Lesson 25: this is exactly the rule that Karen gave you -- three numbers, not ten.

Tip: listen once for the ideas. Listen again and write down 3 pieces of advice, each one in a sentence with who, which or that. Then say your own three numbers out loud in under thirty seconds.

"How to Use Relative Clauses in English (WHO, WHICH, THAT, WHOSE, WHERE, WHEN)"

A clear, step-by-step lesson on today's structure: when to use who, which, that, whose and where, and when the commas change the meaning. It also covers the mistakes that Brazilian speakers make most often, like "a man that his" instead of "a man whose". Connection to Lesson 25: the grammar behind every sentence you said to Richard, explained slowly.

Tip: copy the table into your notebook. Then write 5 sentences about your own accounts: one with who, one with which, one with that, one with whose and one with where.
