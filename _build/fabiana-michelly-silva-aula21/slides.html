Where Does the Fact Come From?

Report it without owning it

Grammar Discovery

Who Is Saying This?

"It is understood that nobody was hurt."

"The plant is thought to be closed for three days."

"The fire is reported to have started in the packaging area."

"The assembly line appears to have been untouched."

Is the speaker completely sure of each fact? Who is the real source? What do the highlighted words have in common?

StructureFormExample
It + passive + thatIt is understood / thought / believed / reported + that + full clauseIt is understood that nobody was hurt.
Subject + passive + to (now)is thought / said / expected / believed + to + base verbThe plant is thought to be closed for three days.
Subject + passive + to have (before)is reported / believed + to have + past participleThe fire is reported to have started in packaging.
seem / appearappears / seems + to (have) ... · It would appear that + clauseThe line appears to have been untouched. It would appear that we have room.
The trapnever mix the two shapesNot It is thought the plant to be closed. Either It is thought that the plant is closed or The plant is thought to be closed.

In one line: the fact comes from someone else, and the grammar says so — to be for now, to have been / to have + past participle for before.

Chapter 4: The Bridge Call

A Fire in Penang

One conversation, two recordings, one incident update

Dialogue

Five Minutes Before the Committee

M
The committee wants a date. When will Penang be back online?
F
I'd rather not speculate. It is understood that the assembly line is intact, but the inspection is preliminary.
M
They'll say that sounds noncommittal. Can you at least give them a range?
F
I can say the plant is thought to be closed for three days. I won't turn that into a promise.
M
And the damaged pallets? Someone in sales heard it was twenty.
F
That number is unconfirmed. Two pallets are reported to have been damaged, and I'll have photos by four.
M
Fine. If they push you for a date, will you hold the line?
F
I will. I'll level with them: eleven days of buffer, a second supplier in qualification, and an update every twenty-four hours.
Comprehension

About Marcus

1. What does Marcus say the committee wants?
A date: they want to know when Penang will be back online.
2. What does Marcus think the committee will say about her first answer, and what does he ask for?
That it sounds noncommittal. He asks her to give them at least a range.
3. What rumor does Marcus bring about the pallets?
Someone in sales heard that twenty pallets were damaged.
Listening 1 · The Holding Statement

What We Know, What We Don’t

Fabiana opens the bridge call with leadership. Sound first — no text.

1. What happened, and where does the fire seem to have started?
A fire broke out overnight at the contract manufacturer's plant in Penang. Nobody was hurt. It is reported to have started in the packaging area, and the main assembly line appears to have been untouched.
2. What is still unconfirmed, and why won't she give numbers?
The reports that two of their pallets were damaged. She would rather not speculate on the numbers until they have photos.
3. What can she commit to, and what can't she?
She can commit to the buffer: eleven days of hardware shipments to clients. She does not have a firm recovery date and will not invent one. The next update is at four.
Listening 2 · Marcus Calls Back

Careful About the Facts, Firm About the Actions

A voicemail after the executive committee. Sound first — no text.

1. How did the committee react to the holding statement, and what was noticed?
It landed well and nobody felt misled. Her composure on the bridge call was noticed.
2. What does the committee want, and what is Marcus's advice?
A firm recovery date by tomorrow morning. His advice: hold the line, buy time if she needs to, and do not walk back what she said about the buffer.
3. What are the three talking points, and what is his final rule?
Eleven days of buffer, a second supplier being qualified, and an update every twenty-four hours. The rule: be careful about the facts and firm about the actions.
Real Document

Incident Update No. 1

INCIDENT UPDATE No. 1 · PENANG CONTRACT MANUFACTURING SITE
From: Fabiana Michelly Silva, Supply Chain · Status: Preliminary
WHAT WE UNDERSTAND
It is understood that a fire broke out overnight. Nobody was hurt. The fire is reported to have started in the packaging area. The assembly line appears to have been untouched.
STILL UNCONFIRMED
Damage to two pallets of keyboards. The site is thought to be closed for three days during the inspection.
WHAT WE COMMIT TO
Eleven days of buffer stock. A second supplier is in qualification. Updates every 24 hours.
NEXT UPDATE
Today, 4:00 p.m. (with photos from the site)
1. Which section uses no distancing language at all, and why?
WHAT WE COMMIT TO. Those are her own actions and numbers, so she states them directly.
2. How long is the site thought to be closed, and why?
Three days, during the inspection. It is under STILL UNCONFIRMED, so it is not a promise.
3. What will the next update include that this one does not?
Photos from the site, at 4:00 p.m. today.
