Phrasal Verbs in the Studio

One recording day in Studio B, a desk that breaks down and a vocal put off until Thursday: how phrasal verbs work as a system. Key words: to set something up, to turn something down, to break down, to play something back, to sort something out, to come up with something, to go over something, to put something off, to fade something out, to carry on. Structure: separable phrasal verbs (the pronoun goes in the middle), inseparable ones, and phrasal verbs with no object.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each phrasal verb and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To set something up -- to prepare equipment so it is ready to use
"She set up the microphones before the band arrived."
To turn something down -- to make a sound quieter
"The drums are too loud. Can you turn them down?"
To break down -- to stop working suddenly, usually a machine
"The old mixing desk broke down in the third take."
To play something back -- to play a recording again so you can listen to it
"Let’s play it back in the control room."
To sort something out -- to solve a problem
"Twenty minutes later, the engineer sorted it out."
To come up with something -- to think of an idea or a plan
"The guitarist came up with a new intro."
To go over something -- to check something carefully, step by step
"Let’s go over the lyrics before we record."
To put something off -- to move something to a later time
"They put the vocal off until Thursday."
To fade something out -- to make a sound slowly quieter until it stops
"We fade the song out after the last chorus."
To carry on -- to continue doing something
"The show is live, so we carry on."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

It is Tuesday, and Pedro is producing a song in Studio B. A studio day is full of phrasal verbs: a verb plus a small word like up, down, back, out or off. Look at the words in bold, and look at where the object goes.

At eight, the assistant sets up the microphones. She can also say she sets the microphones up: with a noun, both orders are fine. The band is too loud in the headphones, so Pedro asks the engineer to turn the band down, and he turns it down. With a pronoun like it or them, the pronoun always goes in the middle. Never turn down it. After the take, they play it back in the control room.

Some phrasal verbs never open. Before the vocal, Pedro and the singer go over the lyrics, and they go over them again after lunch. Never go them over. Three-word verbs stay together too: the guitarist comes up with a new intro. And some phrasal verbs have no object at all: in the afternoon, the old desk breaks down, the engineer sorts it out, and everybody carries on.

At the end of the day, the singer is tired, so they put the vocal off until Thursday. Their last decision is the ending: they are going to fade the song out over twenty seconds.

1. Why does the text say “he turns it down” and not “he turns down it”?
A Because with a pronoun like it, a separable phrasal verb always takes the pronoun in the middle.
B Because turn down is a verb that never opens, so the object must always come after the whole verb, like go over.
C Because the band was too loud.
2. Which phrasal verb in the text has no object?
A To put something off, because the singer was tired at the end of a very long day in the studio.
B To go over something.
C To break down.
3. Which sentence is correct, according to the text?
A Before the vocal, Pedro and the singer go the lyrics over, and then they go them over again after lunch.
B Let’s go over the lyrics.
C Can you play back it?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Phrasal Verbs as a System

GRAMMAR

One question: where does the object go?

TypeWhere the object goesExample
Separable + nounafter the small word or in the middleset up the mics · set the mics up
Separable + pronounalways in the middleturn it down · put it off
Inseparablealways after the whole verbgo over the lyrics · go over them
Three wordsnever separatecome up with an idea
No objectnothing after the verbThe desk broke down.
Negativedon’t / didn’t + the whole verbWe didn’t put it off.
QuestionDid you / Can you + the whole verb?Can you play it back?

Remember: the small word often gives a clue to the meaning: up = ready, down = less, back = again, off = later, on = continue. And a pronoun never goes at the end of a separable verb.

Quiz
