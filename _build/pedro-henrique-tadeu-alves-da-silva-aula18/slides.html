Chapter 4: The Code

Where Does the Object Go?

Three kinds of phrasal verb. One question.

Grammar Discovery

Where Does the Object Go?

1. "She set up the microphones ... and set the headphones up for the band."

2. "He turned it down a little."

3. "He went over every connection, one by one."

4. "The old mixing desk broke down."

5. "The guitarist came up with a new intro."

Look at the red words. Where is the object? Can it move? Is there an object at all?

TypeWhere the object goesExample
Separablea noun goes after the small word OR in the middleset up the mics · set the mics up
Separable + pronounit / them / her ALWAYS in the middleturn it down · play them back · put it off
Inseparablethe object always comes after the whole verbgo over the lyrics · go over them
Three wordsnever separate; the object comes at the endcome up with an idea · come up with it
No objectnothing after the verbThe desk broke down. We carried on.
The small wordit often gives a clue to the meaningup = ready · down = less · back = again · off = later · on = continue
The trapnever a pronoun at the end of a separable verbNot turn down it, not go it over. Say turn it down and go over it.

In one line: ask where the object goes. A noun can travel. A pronoun sits in the middle. Some verbs never open, and some have no object at all.

Listening 1 · Live on Saturday

Nobody at Home Ever Knows

A sound engineer on a live TV music show. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the team set up at noon?
The stage: twelve microphones, two drum kits and a piano.
2. What did she do when the singer said her voice was too loud?
She turned it down in the singer’s ear monitor.
3. What broke down one hour before the show, and what did they do?
A wireless microphone. They went over the plan again and gave the singer a spare one.
Listening 2 · A System for Ideas

One Minute Every Morning

A songwriter on how he works. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he record every morning?
One minute of anything: a melody, a beat or a sentence.
2. What does he do on Fridays?
He goes over everything he recorded that week and plays each idea back once.
3. What was his biggest problem, and what is his rule now?
Putting things off. Now, if an idea is good, he works on it the same day, even for ten minutes.
