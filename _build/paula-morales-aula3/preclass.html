Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

My Routine, My World -- Daily Life in English

In lesson 2 you learned to say what you are (I am a commercial professional). In this lesson you learn to say what you do: six action verbs (work, start, finish, travel, meet, talk) and the present simple in the first person — I work / I don't work / Do you work? With them you describe a whole day at Zurich, from “I start at nine” to “I don't work on weekends”. This Pre-class is only a first contact: come to class with the verbs in your ear, not on the tip of your tongue.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions. The words in bold are the action verbs — the grammar of this lesson.

Wednesday, ten o'clock. The Zurich coffee machine. David is there.

"I always start my day with a coffee," he says. "I start at eight and I finish at five." Paula answers: "I start at nine. I finish at six."

David asks: "I travel every Tuesday — I visit brokers in Rio. Do you travel too?" Paula says: "Yes, I do. I travel to visit brokers." — "Every day?" — "No, I don't. Not every day — two days a week."

"And the other days?" Paula answers: "I work in the office. I meet clients, and I talk to brokers." (Notice: not one verb changed. With I, the verb stays bare — and the one that does the negating and the asking is do.)

1. Where is the verb “to be” (am) in Paula's sentences about her routine?
A Before the verb: "I am work in the office."
B Nowhere — to talk about an action, the verb goes alone: "I work in the office."
C At the end: "I work in the office am."
2. What time does David start and finish?
A He starts at nine and finishes at six — the same as Paula.
B He starts at eight and finishes at five — one hour before Paula.
C The text does not say.
3. To make a sentence NEGATIVE, what does Paula use?
A don't before the verb: "I don't travel every day."
B no before the verb: "I no travel."
C not after the verb: "I travel not."
4. To ASK about a routine, what comes at the front?
A Nothing — you only raise your voice at the end: "You travel?"
B The word Do: "Do you travel?"
C The word Are: "Are you travel?"

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple na 1ª pessoa (I work / I don't work / Do you...?)

GRAMMAR

The good news of this lesson: with I, the English verb never changes. There is only one form to learn, and you drop it straight into the sentence.

FormInglêsWhen you use it
AffirmativeI work in commercial insurance.To say what you do.
AffirmativeI start at nine and I finish at six.To give the hours of your day.
NegativeI don't work on weekends. (= do not)To say what you do not do.
QuestionDo you travel every week?Você viaja toda semana?
Short answer (yes)Yes, I do.Sim, viajo.
Short answer (no)No, I don't.The short way to say no.

Careful (the two mistakes you will want to make): (1) "I am work in insurance" — you have just learned am in lesson 2 and it will want to go everywhere. But every sentence has one verb only: either am (what you ARE), or work (what you DO) — never both; (2) "I no work" — the negative in English is don't, and it comes before the verb: "I don't work".

The routine words: usually goes before the verb — "I usually travel on Tuesdays". But every day and on weekends go at the end — "I talk to brokers every day".

Why this matters: “What do you do?” is the question that opens every work conversation — and the answer to it is your routine. With these six verbs you can answer, and with don't you can even disagree. At Zurich, that is the whole coffee break.

B "I start at nine o'clock. And I finish at six."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record your answer. Speak for 60 seconds, with no script. Do not redo it more than twice. Then compare it with your lesson 2 recording: it is the same person, one week later.

Describe your working day in English: what time you start, what you do, who you meet, when you travel — and, the most important part, ONE sentence with "I don't..." saying something you do NOT do (for example: "I don't work on weekends"). It can be your routine today or the one you imagine at Zurich. It does not have to be perfect. If you get stuck, use the rescue kit from lesson 1 and keep going.
