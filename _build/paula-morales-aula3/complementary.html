Lesson 3 -- My Routine, My World: Daily Life in English

Series
The Office (US) — season 1, episode 3 (“Health Care”) · HBO Max, Netflix or Prime Video

This episode is here for a reason you will recognize in the first scene: the whole office has to choose an insurance plan. It is your subject, all day long, in English — only from the other side of the counter, the side of the client who complains. You spent twenty years listening to that conversation in Portuguese; listen to how it sounds in English. And look at the background: it is a normal working day, from morning coffee to the end of the shift — exactly what you learned to describe in this lesson. Same honest warning as lesson 2: it is a comedy of embarrassment and the boss is a disaster on purpose. You do not have to find it funny — listen for the vocabulary.

Tip: watch it with English subtitles. Do ONE thing only: every time someone says a sentence starting with “I” + verb (“I work...”, “I start...”, “I don't...”), hit pause and write it down. In 20 minutes you will have a list of real people describing their routine — the way you will describe yours at the Zurich coffee break. Write down the words from the lesson too: work, start, meet, talk.

Podcast
VOA Everyday Grammar — “Bad Habits, Adjectives and the Simple Present” (~4 min, free, with transcript)

Four minutes, American English, and the subject is exactly the grammar of this lesson: the present simple for things that repeat. The sentence that opens the episode is “I wake up at 6am every day” — the same mould as your “I start at nine every day”. They use the song Bad Habits, by Ed Sheeran, to show why English uses this tense for habits and routines. VOA is an American public broadcaster: clear delivery, a pace slowed down on purpose for learners, and the full free transcript on the same page — no sign-up, no login, no catch.

Tip: listen the first time without reading anything, just to let your ear catch the rhythm (you understand more than you think — listening is your strength). The second time, open the transcript and follow along. Then stop and say YOUR three sentences out loud in the same mould: “I wake up at ___ every day. I start at ___. I don't ___ on weekends.” Four minutes give you more than half an hour of grammar tables.

YouTube
English Grammar — Present Simple tense · Alex, from engVid (~3 min)

If you watch only one of the three, make it this one: three minutes, and it is your lesson 3 explained by another teacher. Before you press play, look at the thumbnail: everything is written on the whiteboard by hand, with the title ROUTINES — Present Simple and the list “I wake up at 6.30 a.m. / I eat breakfast at 7 a.m. / I take the bus to work. / I drive my car on weekends.” — the same structure as your “I don't work on weekends”. He shows that the verb with I always comes bare, straight from the dictionary, and never changes. It is today's rule, on a whiteboard, in three minutes.

Tip: watch it once all the way through. The second time, pause after each sentence on the board and say YOUR version out loud — swap his example for yours: “I start at nine. I take the car to work. I meet clients. I don't work on weekends.” The video is old and the picture is simple, but the explanation is the most direct one there is for this point — and the channel is free, with no sign-up to watch.
