Review: Travel and Business -- The Visit, Start to Finish

A review of lessons 18 to 23 through one business visit: a buyer from Hamburg spends a week in Santos. Key words: itinerary, layover, venue, on short notice, hiccup, run smoothly, go the extra mile, first-hand, debrief, lessons learned. Grammar review: question tags, the present perfect continuous and reported speech.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Itinerary -- a detailed plan of a trip, with times and places for each day
"I sent the buyer the itinerary for his three days in Santos."
Layover -- a stop between two flights, when you wait at an airport
"He had a five-hour layover in Lisbon."
Venue -- the place where a meeting, a dinner or an event happens
"The venue for the dinner is a restaurant near the port."
On short notice -- with very little time to prepare
"We changed the meeting on short notice because of his flight."
Hiccup -- a small problem that causes a short delay
"There was one hiccup: the restaurant lost our reservation."
Run smoothly -- to happen with no problems
"Thanks to the team, the whole visit ran smoothly."
Go the extra mile -- to do more than people expect from you
"Our driver went the extra mile and waited two hours at the airport."
First-hand -- seen or done by yourself, not heard from other people
"The buyer wanted to see our traceability system first-hand."
Debrief -- a meeting after an event, to talk about what happened
"We had a short debrief with the team on Friday."
Lessons learned -- useful things you understand after an experience, to do better next time
"The main lessons learned are in the report."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last week, Walyson hosted a buyer from Hamburg. In the car, the buyer asked: "It's always this hot in Santos, isn't it?" That is a question tag: a short question at the end of a sentence to keep the conversation going. A positive sentence takes a negative tag, and a negative sentence takes a positive tag.

Since the visit, the buyer has been sending questions every day, and the team has been working on a checklist. These actions started in the past and are still going, so we use the present perfect continuous: have / has + been + verb-ing.

On the last day, the buyer told Walyson that his customers wanted more information. This is reported speech: we tell someone what another person said. Use said that, or told + a person + that, and move the verb one tense back: want becomes wanted.

1. Which question tag is correct? "The flight was long, ___?"
A was it, because the sentence is about the past and the tag needs a past verb
B wasn't it
C isn't
2. Why does the text say "the team has been working on a checklist"?
A Because the checklist is finished and the team gave it to the director on Friday afternoon.
B Because it is a plan for the future.
C Because the work started in the past and is still going.
3. The buyer said: "I love the fish." Which sentence reports it correctly?
A He told me that he loved the fish.
B He said me that he loved the fish.
C He told that he love the fish at the restaurant near the beach.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Three Tools for a Visit

GRAMMAR

A review of lessons 21, 22 and 23: three tools you use with every visitor.

ToolStructureExample
Question tag (affirmative sentence)positive sentence + negative tagThe hotel is near the port, isn’t it?
Question tag (negative sentence)negative sentence + positive tagYou haven’t been to Santos before, have you?
Present perfect continuoushave / has + been + -ing (+ for / since)We have been preparing the visit since Monday.
Present perfect continuous (question)How long + have / has + subject + been + -ing?How long have you been waiting?
Reported speechsaid (that) / told + person (that) + one tense backShe told me that the venue was perfect.

Remember: told always needs a person (told me, told us). Never say "he said me".

Stage 2: Put the Visit in Order

Order

You are telling your director about the visit. Put the sentences in order: the arrival, the change of plan, the problem, the farm, the debrief and what is happening now.

?"Because of his jet lag, we changed the itinerary on short notice."
?"On Wednesday, he saw our traceability system first-hand."
?"On Friday, we had a debrief and wrote down the lessons learned."
?"Since then, we have been working on a checklist for future visits."
?"On Tuesday, there was a hiccup: the restaurant lost our reservation, so we found another venue."
?"Mr. Becker arrived on Monday after a long layover in Lisbon."
