Documentary
The Social Dilemma

Ninety minutes of people who built something arguing about what it did, and almost every sentence in it is a modal doing the second job: this must be, this cannot be, this might be. It is also a masterclass in how experts mark the difference between what they know and what they suspect.

Tip: watch twenty minutes with English subtitles and write down five sentences with must, cannot or might. Then mark which are rules and which are conclusions.

Talk
Julia Dhar — “How to disagree productively and find common ground” (TED)

Twelve minutes from a world debate champion on exactly what you practised today: holding a position, conceding a point, and staying in the conversation. Her three rules are usable the same week.

Tip: watch once for the argument. The second time, write down every phrase she uses to concede something, and add them to your survival card.

Podcast
BBC Learning English — “How to use modals of deduction and speculation”

Six minutes on the harder half of today lesson: must, might and cannot when they are not about rules at all. Spoken, native speed, free.

Tip: after each example, say the same idea with a different level of certainty and notice what changes.
