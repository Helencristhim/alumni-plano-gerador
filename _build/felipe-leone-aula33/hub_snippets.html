Lesson 33 -- Pre-class

Food and Culture in Another Country -- Much, Many, a Lot Of

Ten to twenty minutes before class. How to describe food and culture on a trip with there is / are, much, many and a lot of. Key words: a dish, a custom, local food, a street market, to be surprised, a habit.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A dish -- a type of food that is prepared in a particular way
"The best dish of the trip was a simple fish soup."
A custom -- a thing that people in a place usually do
"In Japan, it is a custom to say a few words before you eat."
Local food -- the food that people from that place usually eat
"I always try the local food when I travel for work."
A street market -- a place in the street where people sell food and other things
"There are a lot of street markets in Mexico City."
To be surprised -- to feel something because you did not expect it
"I was surprised by how early people have dinner there."
A habit -- a thing that you do often, almost without thinking
"Having a long lunch is a habit in many countries."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Paulo travels to Bogota every year for client meetings. “There are a lot of small restaurants near our office, and there is a lot of fresh fruit everywhere. But there aren't many places open late.”

On his last trip, there wasn't much time between meetings. “There were many clients to see, so we ate at the office. There isn't much time for long lunches on these trips.”

"There aren't many places open late." What does it mean?
A No places are open during the day.
B Only a small number of places are open late.
C All the places are open late.
Why does Paulo say "there wasn't much time" and not "there weren't many time"?
A Because time is uncountable, so it goes with much.
B Because he is talking about the future.
C Because time was short on Monday.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Much, Many or a Lot Of?

GRAMMAR

Many with things you can count. Much with things you cannot count. A lot of with both.

JobFormExample
Countable, negative or questionmany + plural nounThere aren't many menus in English.
Uncountable, negative or questionmuch + nounThere isn't much time.
Countable or uncountable, positivea lot of + nounThere are a lot of restaurants. There is a lot of traffic.
Singular or uncountablethere isThere is a market near the hotel.

Never: There isn't many options · There are much people · There have a lot of restaurants

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

Listen first, then put the lines in order. Two colleagues talk about a work trip. Put their conversation in a natural order.

Step 1: listen to the whole conversation. Step 2: put the lines in the order you heard.
?"Not really. There wasn't much time between meetings."
?"How was the food in Lima?"
?"Next time, there are a few places I can recommend."
?"Did you have time to try them?"
?"Amazing. There are a lot of small restaurants near the hotel."

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen, then record yourself. Link the words: a lot of sounds like one word.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A colleague asks about dinner options near the hotel. There are only two. You say:
A "There aren't much options near the hotel."
B "There isn't many options near the hotel."
C "There aren't many options near the hotel."
You want to say the meeting is soon. You say:
A "There isn't much time before the meeting."
B "There aren't many time before the meeting."
C "There haven't much time before the meeting."
You describe a busy city. You say:
A "There is much people in the streets."
B "There are a lot of people in the streets."
C "There have a lot of people in the streets."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

A colleague who is going on a work trip asks you: where did the food or the culture surprise you? Answer in about one minute: what there was, what there was not, and one thing you learned.
