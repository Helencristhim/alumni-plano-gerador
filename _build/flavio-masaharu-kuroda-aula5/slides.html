Chapter 3: The Code

When a Question Needs Do

One rule, and it depends on who is doing the action

Grammar Discovery

Two Pairs, One Difference

"Who handles the invoices today?"

"Who do you send them to?"

"What happens when a supplier changes the format?"

"What do you do when a supplier changes the format?"

In each pair, ask yourself one thing: who does the action? In two of them the answer is the blue word itself. In the other two it is the person you are talking to.

JobFormExample
The question word DOES the actionno do — the verb takes -sWho handles the invoices?
Somebody else does the actiondo / does + bare verbWho do you send them to?
What, as the subjectno doWhat happens when it fails?
What, as the objectdo / does + bare verbWhat do you do when it fails?
How long, how many, how oftenthe subject comes after, so do is neededHow long does the closing take?
How many + subjectno do — the group does the actionHow many people work on it?
With the verb benever doWho is responsible for it?
The preposition stays at the endnot at the frontWho do you send them to?

In one line: if the question word is the one doing the action, drop the do.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Discovery, at Full Speed

One call, two recordings, one page of notes

Dialogue

Discovery Call with Ingrid

I
Before you show me anything: how much do you already know about how we work?
F
Almost nothing, and that is exactly why I asked for this call. Who handles the invoices today?
I
Four people in finance. Two of them do nothing else, and both of them have been here longer than I have.
F
And what happens when a supplier changes the format?
I
Somebody notices three days later, and then we fix it by hand. It is a workaround, and it has been the same workaround for six years.
F
How long does the month-end closing take you?
I
Nine working days. It should be four. That is the number my board asks me about, and it is the reason I took this call.
F
Then one more before I say anything about us. What do those nine days cost you, apart from the time?
Comprehension

About Ingrid

1. What is the first thing Ingrid asks, and what does the question tell you about her?
She asks how much he already knows about how they work, before he shows her anything. She is testing whether he prepared, and she does not want a presentation.
2. What are the two numbers she gives, and which one does she say matters to her?
Nine working days for the closing, and four days as it should be. She says nine is the number her board asks her about, and the reason she took the call.
3. Two of the questions in this call are built without do, and two are built with it. Why?
In who handles the invoices and what happens when a supplier changes the format, the question word itself is doing the action, so there is no do. In how long does the closing take and what do those nine days cost, somebody else does the action, so do or does is needed.
Listening 1 · No Demo

Why She Never Demos on Call One

A salesperson on the call she refuses to fill with slides. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say happens the moment she opens a deck?
The customer stops describing their problem and starts reacting to her product, and she says she never gets the original description back.
2. What does she say she is really listening for?
A number and a workaround. A number tells her the size of the problem, and a workaround tells her the problem has been there long enough for somebody to build a habit around it.
3. What does she say about salespeople who fill the silence?
That the silence after a question is where the useful answer lives, and that filling it is the most expensive habit in the job.
Listening 2 · From the Buyer

Nobody Has Ever Asked Me That

An operations director on the vendors he remembers. Sound first — no text.

1. What question does he say almost no vendor asks him?
What happens today when the process fails. He says everybody asks what he wants to buy, and almost nobody asks what actually goes wrong.
2. What does he say about vendors who arrive with a solution already prepared?
That they are selling to a company they invented on the way to the meeting, and that he can hear it in the first three minutes.
3. What does he say happens inside his company when a problem is never escalated?
It becomes normal. He says nobody escalates it because escalating it means admitting it has been there for years, so it stays and everybody works around it.

Six lines, and not one of them is about the product. Five of them were filled in by asking a question and then saying nothing. The sixth is empty, and it is the most expensive line on the page: a problem with a number of days has a size, but a problem with a cost has a budget.
1. Which question produced the line about the four people in finance, and why is it built without do?
Who handles the invoices today? The word who is the one doing the handling, so the verb takes -s and there is no do.
2. The note says five lines were filled in by asking a question and then saying nothing. What is the second half doing?
The silence. The answer people give after a pause is longer and more honest than the one they give when the other person keeps talking.
3. Why does the note call the empty line the most expensive one?
Because a problem measured in days has a size, but a problem measured in money has a budget. Without the cost, there is nothing for a decision to be made against.
