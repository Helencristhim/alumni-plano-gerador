Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read it inside a real sentence. These are the words the exam text will use.

Incident report -- the official written report about something that went wrong on board
"A near miss rarely appears in an incident report."
Near miss -- an event that could cause harm but did not
"The near-miss data told a different story."
Master -- the officer in command of a merchant ship
"The master was ashore on Tuesday afternoon."
Flag state -- the country where a ship is registered
"A near miss is not reported to the flag state."
Manning -- the number of crew on a ship and their qualifications
"Near misses happen most often on ships with the minimum manning."
Watchkeeping -- the job of staying on duty to keep the ship safe for a period of time
"The officers have certificates, but the watchkeeping is weak."
STCW -- the international rules for the training and the certificates of seafarers
"Under STCW, every officer must renew the certificate regularly."
ISM Code -- the international rules for safe management on board a ship
"The ISM Code says that every company must prepare for emergencies."
Muster station -- the place where each person goes when the alarm sounds
"The muster station list was out of date."
Lifeboat drill -- the practice exercise for leaving the ship in an emergency
"The crew do not practise the lifeboat drill."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

Seldom is a lifeboat drill longer than fifteen minutes. Never had the new cadet seen a real emergency.

A Lifeboat drills are never longer than fifteen minutes.
B Lifeboat drills are usually short, but some are longer.
C The writer does not know how long the drills are.

B They never went.
C After the alarm.
3. Which sentence has the normal word order?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reading Formal Emphasis (Inversion)

GRAMMAR

Formal texts sometimes put a word like rarely or not only first. Then the verb comes before the subject. The facts do not change. You only need to read it and keep the meaning.

FormWhat it doesExample
rarely / seldom / never + is, does, hadthe verb comes before the subject; the meaning does not changeRarely is a casualty caused by one failure. = A casualty is rarely caused by one failure.
not only ... but alsoonly the first half changes the word orderNot only does it make the crew tired, but it also removes...
not until ... didthe second half changes the word orderNot until a computer won did people accept it.
The reading trapRarely does not mean never. In the exam, a wrong option often changes rarely into never.

You do not need to write these sentences. You need to read them and keep the meaning: rarely = not often, but sometimes.
