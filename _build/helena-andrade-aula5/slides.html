Chapter 4: The Code

The Shape of Formal Emphasis

Rarely is, hardly had, not only does, only when does

Grammar Discovery

The Verb Came First

"Rarely is a maritime casualty caused by a single failure."

"Hardly had the industry finished congratulating itself when the data began to tell another story."

"Not only does under-manning increase fatigue, but it also removes the second person."

"Only when the drill is treated as an operation does the paperwork begin to describe the ship."

Rewrite the first one as ordinary word order and read both out loud. The facts are identical -- so what did the writer gain, and what did the reader have to do extra?

FormWhat it doesExample
negative adverb firstrarely, seldom, never, little, at no time: the auxiliary jumps in front of the subjectRarely is a casualty caused by one failure.
hardly / scarcely / no sooner ... whenone thing had only just happened when the next didHardly had they finished when the data changed.
not only ... but alsothe inversion happens in the FIRST half only; the second half is ordinaryNot only does it increase fatigue, but it also removes...
only when / only after / not untilthe inversion lands on the MAIN clause, never inside the only-clauseOnly when the drill is real does the paperwork describe the ship.
The reading trapinversion is EMPHASIS, not negation. Rarely is a casualty caused by a single failure does not mean never. Turning rarely into never, or hardly into not at all, is the standard wrong option -- and it is wrong by degree, not by topic.

Inversion tells you the writer is leaning on something. It never tells you the thing is absolute. Every question built on an inverted sentence tests whether you read the degree or only the direction.

Chapter 6: Debrief

The Debrief

Two recordings, then the reason behind every option you did not choose

Listening 1

How the Paper Is Built

An examiner explains where the marks are actually lost. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What are the three places candidates lose marks?
Degree, agency and register. Only one of the three is really about vocabulary.
2. What example does she give for degree?
The text says rarely and the option says never. Because the topic is identical, the eye accepts it.
3. Why does she say a candidate rejects the right answer on register?
Because the correct option is written in different words from the text, so it does not look like the text.
Listening 2

The Drill That Nobody Ran

A safety officer on the difference between a record and an operation. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What does he say about the drill records he has signed?
He has signed hundreds of them and watched perhaps a third of the drills they describe.
2. What is wrong with a drill that takes eleven minutes?
It ends with everyone back at work and it has taught nobody anything. It is a record rather than an operation.
3. Why does he say near misses are the only honest record?
Because nobody is ever punished for an accident that did not occur, which is exactly why people will tell you about it.
