Lesson 5 -- Speaking with Authority

Film
Worth (2021) -- Netflix

A lawyer has to explain a compensation formula to a room full of stakeholders, and the first meeting goes badly for a reason that has nothing to do with the law: he opens with the technical detail instead of with the room. Watch the meeting scenes as a case study in the four movements you practised today, and in what happens when the opening is skipped.

Tip: watch with English subtitles and write down the first sentence he says in each meeting. Then rewrite one of them as an agenda: how many points, and how long.

Podcast
Life Kit (NPR) -- "How To Give Good Feedback"

Twenty minutes on how to deliver a difficult message so that the other person can actually use it. The English is clear American English at normal speed, and the structure they describe is the same one you used today: say what you are about to cover, keep the fact separate from the person, and close with what happens next.

Tip: there is a full transcript on the page. Listen once with the transcript closed, then open it and mark every sentence where the speaker describes a fact without naming who did it.

YouTube
Stanford Graduate School of Business -- "Think Fast, Talk Smart: Communication Techniques"

Matt Abrahams on speaking with authority when you did not plan the sentence: how to answer the question you were not expecting, how to buy yourself two seconds without apologising, and why a pause is heard as confidence and not as a gap. This is the third movement of today's session, taught by somebody who trains executives for a living.

Tip: it is a long talk -- watch it in two parts. After the section on handling questions, practise one answer out loud that starts with "Let me finish this one and I will come back to that."
