Lesson 05 -- Pre-class

Speaking with Authority -- Delivering a Complete Contract Feedback Session

A feedback session has four movements: open with the agenda, walk through the findings in order of priority, handle the interruption, and close with a recap. Key words: agenda, priority, stakeholder, timeline, blocker, recap, takeaway, alignment, outstanding, minutes, walkthrough, rundown. Structure: the passive voice (be + past participle) for impersonal professional distance -- how to point at a problem without pointing at a person, and where the passive must never be used.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the definition. These are not contract words -- they are the words of the meeting itself. The goal is to say them out loud.

Agenda -- the list of points a meeting is going to cover
"Three points on the agenda today, about fifteen minutes."
Priority -- the thing that has to be dealt with first
"The indemnity clause is the first priority."
Stakeholder -- a person or team affected by the decision
"The finance team is a stakeholder here too."
Timeline -- the dates and the order in which things happen
"The timeline gives us until the end of the quarter."
Blocker -- something that stops the work from moving forward
"The missing signature is the only blocker."
Recap -- a short repetition of the main points at the end
"Let me give you a quick recap before we close."
Takeaway -- the one thing people should remember afterwards
"The takeaway is that we cannot sign as it stands."
Alignment -- everyone understanding and accepting the same plan
"We have alignment on points one and two."
Outstanding -- still open, not resolved yet
"One question is still outstanding."
Minutes -- the written record of what a meeting decided
"I will send the minutes this afternoon."
Walkthrough -- going through a document point by point with someone
"I will do a walkthrough of clause nine with you."
Rundown -- a quick summary of the situation
"Here is a quick rundown of where we are."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Ana runs the feedback session herself now. She opens with the agenda: three points, about fifteen minutes. Then she does a walkthrough of the first point, because it is the priority. The clause was drafted before the merger, so it still names the old entity, and it should be amended before signature. Nobody is named, so nobody has to defend anybody: it is understood that the wording is simply old. David interrupts with a question about the payment schedule, a blocker for another stakeholder, and she parks it until the end. Then comes the recap. The takeaway is that the contract cannot be signed as it stands. The timeline has alignment. One point stays outstanding until finance answers, and the minutes will be sent the same day. Nothing has been agreed on the payment schedule yet, and the rundown says so.

1. Why does Ana say "the clause was drafted before the merger" instead of naming the team that wrote it?
A Because the point is the fact, not the person -- naming the drafter turns a technical remark into an accusation.
B Because she does not know who wrote the clause.
C Because the passive is more polite in every sentence.
2. "The minutes will be sent the same day." How is this form built?
A will + the infinitive of the main verb.
B will be + the past participle: only the verb be moves with the tense.
C will + the past simple of the main verb.
3. If "the payment schedule will be confirmed" were written in the action items, what would be missing?
A Nothing -- an action item is always written in the passive.
B The name of the document it belongs to.
C An owner and a date -- an action item with nobody in it cannot be executed.
4. Why is the payment schedule "outstanding" and not "agreed"?
A Because David refused it during the call.
B Because finance has not answered yet, so the point is still open.
C Because it was not on the agenda.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive, and Where It Must Not Go

GRAMMAR

One structure that lets you point at a problem without pointing at a person -- and one place where it is dangerous.

FormUseExample
is / are + past participleA present fact. The document, not a person, is the subject.The old entity is named in clause nine.
was / were + past participleSomething that happened before, when who did it is not the point. This is the one that keeps a call technical.The clause was drafted before the merger.
will be / has been + past participleThe same idea in the future and in the present perfect. Only be moves; the participle never changes.The minutes will be sent today. Nothing has been agreed yet.
It is understood that... / It was agreed that...The standard professional opening when the source is the room and not one named person.It is understood that both sides sign this week.
... + by somebodyAdd the person only when the person is the point.The deadline was moved by finance, not by us.
Three trapsNever drop the participle ("was draft") or the verb be ("this point raised on the last call") -- both are half a passive, and half a passive is not a sentence. And never write an action item in the passive: "the schedule will be confirmed" has no owner and no date, so nobody does it. Findings in the passive, actions with a name.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

The call has just started and you have three findings. Your first sentence is:
A "So, about clause nine, I think there is a problem with the indemnity, and also the timeline..."
B "Three points today, about fifteen minutes: the indemnity clause, the timeline, and one question for finance."
C "Sorry for my English, I will try to be quick."
The clause with the problem was written by the manager's own team. You say:
A "The clause was drafted before the merger, so it still names the old entity."
B "Your team drafted this clause wrong and now it names the old entity."
C "Somebody made a mistake here, but I do not want to say who."
You are on point one and the manager interrupts with a question about point three. You reply:
A "Yes, of course, let me check the payment schedule now."
B "Let me finish this one and I will come back to that. It is point three on the agenda."
C "Sorry, sorry, one second, sorry."
You are writing the action items in the minutes. Which line works?
A "The payment schedule will be confirmed."
B "It is understood that the payment schedule is fine."
C "Finance confirms the payment schedule by Friday."
You have two minutes left and you want to close the session. You say:
A "OK, I think that is everything. Sorry for the long call."
B "So, any questions?"
C "Quick recap: the takeaway is that we cannot sign as it stands, the timeline has alignment, and one point stays outstanding until finance answers on Friday."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Run a complete feedback session out loud, in four movements. Open with the agenda and the time. Walk through two findings in order of priority, using the passive for the problem so that nobody is accused. Then handle one interruption ("and the payment schedule?") without losing your place. Close with the recap: the takeaway, what has alignment, and what stays outstanding -- with an owner and a date. Take your time.
