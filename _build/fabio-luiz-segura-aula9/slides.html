Chapter 3: You Mentioned That

Repeating Somebody's Words Back to Them

Accurately, which is the whole proof that you were listening

Grammar Discovery

What Happened to the Verb?

“Our platform supports the new format.” → "You mentioned that your platform supported the new format."

“The pilot will be ready by March.” → "She said that the pilot would be ready by March."

“Compliance is our main concern.” → "He told me that compliance was their main concern."

“We can have it ready in six weeks.” → "They said that they could have it ready in six weeks."

Every red verb moved one step back in time, even though nothing in the world changed. And look at the blue words: one of them takes a person straight after it, and the other one never does.

They saidYou reportExample

Verb + a persontell somebody thatHe told me that compliance was the concern.
Verb with no person after itsay that · mention thatShe said that · You mentioned that — never she said me.
The traptell needs the person; say refuses itNot he told that, not she said me. Say he told me that / she said that.
Why it matters herethe shift shows you are quoting, not claimingYou are reporting what they said, not stating it as fact yourself.

In one line: the verb steps back one tense, and that step is what separates "this is true" from "this is what you told me".

Listening 1 · Why They Fail

Nothing in It Could Only Have Been Written to You

Somebody who reads forty follow-up emails after every event. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say is wrong with most follow-up emails?
They are about the sender, and nothing in them could only have been written to one specific person.
2. What three things does the email that works do?
It quotes something the other person said in their own words, it names one thing the sender will do and when, and then it ends.
3. What does he say about the length?
Three lines, and he says he has never once thought a follow-up email was too short.
Listening 2 · The Voice Note

Before You Get to the Lift

A habit people find strange until they try it. Sound first — no text.

1. When does she record the voice note, and what does she use in it?
On the way out of the meeting, before she gets to the lift, and she uses the other person's own words.
2. What is the obvious benefit, and what is the less obvious one?
The obvious one is that she does not forget. The less obvious one is that reporting somebody's words back accurately makes the next conversation easy.
3. What does she say happens if she opens with what you told her last time?
They are already inside the subject, and nobody has to spend ten minutes rebuilding where they were.
Listening 3 · The Note Itself

Three Things, In Her Words

A real voice note recorded after a call, at full speed. Sound first — no text.

1. What two timelines did the other side give for the integration?
Twelve weeks in the good case, and she admitted that sixteen was more realistic for a bank with that number of systems.
2. What did she offer, and why does the speaker call it the most useful thing all month?
An introduction to the person who ran the same project with a bank in Colombia.
3. What was promised by Tuesday, and what is the speaker going to do about it?
The compliance answer, which the head of delivery owned. It is now Thursday, so the speaker will chase it in the morning and escalate if there is no reply.
Real Document

The Email You Actually Send

Correspondent chain — our two-corridor numbers
From: Fabio Luiz Segura · To: Claire Devlin · Cc: Marina

Claire,

You mentioned that most of the delay sat in the correspondent chain rather than in the platform. We measured exactly that across two corridors over ninety days, and the numbers are attached.

You also said that you had done this with a bank in Colombia. I would like the introduction you offered.

Next steps: you send the compliance answer by Tuesday and the introduction today. I send the corridor figures on Friday and loop in Marina, who owns compliance on our side.

Fabio

Illustrative email, written for this lesson. Notice what is not in it.
1. Which two sentences prove he was listening, and how do you know?
The two that begin "you mentioned that" and "you also said that" -- both report her own words back, with the verb one step back in time.
2. How many action items are there, and does each one have an owner and a date?
Four: her compliance answer by Tuesday, her introduction today, his figures on Friday, and looping in Marina. Every one has an owner, and three have a date.
3. What is deliberately absent from this email?
Any version of "it was a pleasure to meet you", any company overview attached for its own sake, and any request that is not already something she offered.
