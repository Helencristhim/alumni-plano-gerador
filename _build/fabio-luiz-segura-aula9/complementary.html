Talk
Aaron Painter — “Listen to This! How listening can transform work” (TEDx)

The business case for the habit this lesson is built on. He argues that listening is what earns you the second meeting, and he is specific about what listening actually looks like when somebody is doing it well.

Tip: he gives concrete signals of a good listener. Pick the one you do least and use it in your next call — then write the follow-up and see whether it is easier to draft.

Talk
TED-Ed — “4 things all great listeners know”

Five minutes, animated, and slower than the talks — which makes it a good place to practise catching a whole argument without losing the thread. Two of the four things are exactly what a follow-up email has to prove.

Tip: watch it without subtitles first. Write down the four things from memory, then watch again and check how many you had.

Video
Sibos TV — “How can banks and tech companies collaborate to benefit customers?”

Watch this one again with a new job: write down five things the speakers say, in reported speech, as if you were preparing to quote them back in an email. It is the drill from this lesson, done on real people.

Tip: get the tense shift right in all five. Then read your five sentences out loud and check that each one still sounds like something a person actually said.
