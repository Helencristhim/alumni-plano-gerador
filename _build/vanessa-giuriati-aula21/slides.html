Chapter 3

How Sure Are You?

Strong, soft, and partly-agree

Grammar Discovery

Read the Weight

"I'm convinced that remote work is here to stay."

"I'd say it works for some teams, but not for all."

"I'm not so sure about that, to be honest."

"That's a fair point, but I still prefer the office."

FormUseExample
I'm convinced (that) + sentenceStrong: you have no doubtI'm convinced that this is the right call.
I'd say / I think + sentenceNeutral: easy to soften or change laterI'd say it depends on the client.
I'm not so sure / I'm doubtful aboutPolite disagreementI'm not so sure about that.
That's a fair point, but...Agree with one part, then add your viewThat's a fair point, but the numbers say something else.
⚠ Careful'Agree' is a verb, so say 'I agree', never 'I am agree'. After 'I'm convinced' use that plus a full sentence, not 'of that'. And with it/he/she the verb takes the -s: it depends.

Chapter 4

Real Voices

Two people who disagree — and stay friends

Dialogue

The Open Office Debate

D
Vanessa, everyone says open offices make teams more creative. What do you think?
V
Honestly, I think that's overrated. I'm convinced that people focus better with some quiet.
D
That's a fair point. But isn't the noise part of the energy?
V
Maybe for brainstorming. It depends on the task, I would say.
D
I'm a bit doubtful about the quiet part, to be honest.
V
I admit it's personal. But the assumption that noise equals energy is not always true.
D
So you feel strongly about this.
V
I do. Give me one quiet morning and I'll show you the difference.
Comprehension

Did You Catch It?

1. What does Daniel say at the beginning?
He says everyone claims open offices make teams more creative, and he asks Vanessa what she thinks.
2. How does Daniel react to her first opinion?
He calls it a fair point, but he asks whether the noise is part of the energy.
3. What is Daniel doubtful about?
He is doubtful about the quiet part — he is not sure that silence is what helps people focus.
Listening

An Opinion Is Information

Listen first. No text. Sound only.

1. What did a manager tell her about opinions?
That an opinion is information, not an attack.
2. How does she start when she disagrees?
She starts with the part she accepts — "That is a fair point" — and then adds her own view.
3. What does she say is the hardest part?
Admitting when she is wrong. But the moment she admits it, the conversation moves forward instead of going in circles.
Listening

Opinion or Assumption?

A different voice, a different message. Listen carefully.

1. What is the difference between an opinion and an assumption?
An opinion is what you think after looking at something; an assumption is what you believe before looking at anything.
2. What test does the speaker suggest?
Ask yourself what would make you change your mind. If you can answer, it is an opinion; if nothing would, it is an assumption.
3. What does he say about the strongest speakers?
They say "I am convinced" and mean it, but they also say they could be wrong about one part — that is precision, not weakness.
