Chapter 1: First Words

From the Question to the Meaning

Last time you learned to ask when you do not catch something. Today we go one step further: you caught every word, but did you catch what the speaker meant?

Think of a meeting where somebody said yes, but you felt the answer was no. What made you feel that?

Chapter 3: The Code

Not a Fact, an Impression

Seem, appear and sound like

Grammar Discovery

A Fact, or an Impression?

"It sounds like they are not happy with the price."

"The investor seems worried about the tariff."

"She seems to agree, but her tone says no."

"The regulator appears to have changed its mind."

Compare: They are not happy with the price. and It sounds like they are not happy with the price. Which one is a fact? Which one is your interpretation of what you heard? And look at what comes after each blue word: a sentence, an adjective or to + a verb?

JobFormExample
Interpret what you hearIt sounds like + subject + verbIt sounds like they are not happy.
An impression with an adjectivesound / seem + adjectiveThat sounds risky. He seems worried.
An impression with a verbseem / appear + to + verbShe seems to agree.
An impression about the pastseem / appear + to have + past participleThe regulator appears to have changed its mind.
Start with the impressionIt seems (that) + subject + verbIt seems that the deal is not ready.
The traplike goes before a sentence or a noun, never before an adjectiveNot It sounds like risky. Say It sounds risky.

In one line: sound like + sentence, sound or seem + adjective, seem or appear + to + verb.

Grammar Practice

Make It an Impression

Say it first, then click to check

"It ___sounds like they want a lower price." (I heard it, and I think they want a lower price.)
"The investor ___seems worried about the tariff." (He looks and sounds worried.)
"She ___seems to agree, but her tone says no." (I think she agrees.)
"That ___sounds risky." (I think it is risky. No like here.)
"The regulator ___appears to have changed its mind." (Formal: I think it changed its mind.)
Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Meaning, at Real Speed

One conversation, three recordings, one page

Dialogue

After the Call With London

R
So, what did you think of the call with Mr. Hughes? He was very polite.
A
Too polite, maybe. He said our proposal was interesting. It sounds like he did not like it.
R
I agree. When a British banker says interesting, you need to read between the lines.
A
And he said the timeline was a bit ambitious. That seems to be an understatement.
R
Exactly. He was implying that a closing in March is impossible.
A
What about the guarantee? He did not say no, but his tone changed.
R
I noticed that too. He seems worried about the guarantee, not about the price.
A
So we change the guarantee first. And next time, I will ask him directly what he means.
Comprehension

About Ruth

1. According to Ruth, what do you need to do when a British banker says interesting?
Read between the lines.
2. What does Ruth say Mr. Hughes was implying about the closing in March?
That it is impossible.
3. What does Ruth think Mr. Hughes is worried about?
The guarantee, not the price.

Listening 2 · The Linguist

Why Native Speakers Sound So Fast

A linguist explains connected speech in a short interview. Sound first — no text.

1. What does going to become in connected speech?
Gonna.
2. Why do people with good grammar still not understand native speakers, according to him?
Because native speakers join words together. This is connected speech.
3. What is his advice?
Do not listen for every word. Listen for the stressed words.
