Lesson 14 -- The Extent to Which

Podcast
"The Scientific Process" — Hidden Brain, NPR

What happens when researchers try to reproduce published findings, and why so many do not survive. Connection to Lesson 14: it is today's vocabulary applied to real papers — premises, assumptions, generalising from small samples. Transcript beside the audio.

Tip: fifteen minutes is enough. Note every time somebody distinguishes between what a study SHOWED and what it was taken to show. That distinction is the whole lesson.

Talk
"Why You Think You're Right — Even If You're Wrong" — Julia Galef, TED

Eleven minutes on the difference between defending a position and trying to see accurately, told through a nineteenth-century military scandal. Connection to Lesson 14: it is the mental habit behind today's grammar, argued rather than asserted. English transcript on the page.

Tip: watch once. Then ask yourself her question about an opinion you hold strongly — what would change your mind? If nothing would, that is worth sitting with.

"Relative Clauses in C1" — Advanced Grammar

Defining and non-defining clauses at C1, including the quantifier structures (none of which, many of whom) that are today's central difficulty. Connection to Lesson 14: the grammar kept outside the class, as you asked.

Tip: afterwards, take any paragraph of a newspaper and rewrite it joining two of its sentences with a relative clause. Notice what the join adds — and what it commits you to.
