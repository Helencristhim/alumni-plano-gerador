Chapter 3: The Code

The Clauses That Carry Analysis

How English attaches a whole idea to a noun

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The extent to which the sample was representative is never discussed."

"He proposes a method by which the two can be compared."

"There were four studies, none of which used the same definition."

"She interviewed sixty people, many of whom had already left the country."

Each of these could be two sentences. What does joining them do to the argument — and why does academic English prefer it?

FormWhat it doesExample
the extent to which
the degree to which
the way in which		Turns a question of DEGREE into a noun phrase you can then discuss. The single most useful structure in analytical English, and almost impossible to paraphrase."The extent to which the sample was representative is never discussed."
preposition + which
by which · in which
under which · from which		The preposition moves in front of which. Formal, precise, and the version that examiners and editors expect — the alternative is to strand it at the end."a method by which the two can be compared"
(informal: a method which they can be compared by)
quantifier + of which
of whom		Attaches a whole second statement to a list. of whom for people, of which for things — and this is where Portuguese speakers reach for them."four studies, none of which used…"
"sixty people, many of whom had left"
wherebyMeans by which and is used almost exclusively about systems, rules and arrangements. One word, high register, and unmistakably precise."an arrangement whereby each side reviews the other"
in which case
at which point
for which reason		Attaches a consequence to what has just been said. Very common in careful speech, and it lets a sentence keep going where a full stop would break the argument."They may refuse, in which case the whole timetable changes."

These structures exist because analysis needs to attach a second idea to a noun without starting a new sentence. Splitting them into short sentences is always possible and always loses the connection — and the connection is what the analysis consists of.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"The extent which the sample was representative is never discussed."
"The extent to which the sample was representative is never discussed."
"There were four studies, none of them used the same definition."
"There were four studies, none of which used the same definition."
"She interviewed sixty people, many of them had already left."
"She interviewed sixty people, many of whom had already left."

The second and third are the same error and it is extremely common: after a comma, English needs a RELATIVE pronoun (of which, of whom) to join the two halves. Using them leaves two independent sentences glued with a comma, which English does not allow. And the extent always takes to which — never which alone.

The Philosopher and the Footnote

I teach one habit and everything else follows from it. Before you agree or disagree with anything, find the sentence the whole thing rests on. It is almost never the conclusion.
H
And in this piece it is in a footnote, which is either careless or very deliberate.
E

H

E

H

E
I would say: paragraph six is a leap, and you are entitled to make it, and you have to say that you are making it. That is the whole complaint.
H
Which is a smaller complaint than it sounds and a more serious one than it looks.
Comprehension

About Elif

1. What single habit does Elif teach?
Finding the sentence the whole argument rests on, before agreeing or disagreeing.
2. Why does she call the footnote careless rather than deliberate?
Because the author knows the field well enough to have stopped noticing that the definition is contested.
3. What does she say about the two studies built for another question?
That it does not make them irrelevant — it makes them evidence for a question nobody is asking.
4. What would she say to the author?
That paragraph six is a leap, that he is entitled to make it, and that he has to say he is making it.
Listening 1

The Sentence It Rests On

A seminar, ten minutes in. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she tell students to do first?
Find the sentence everything rests on, which is almost never the conclusion.
2. What does she say about strong openings?
That a watertight first section earns the trust that the weak section later spends.
3. What is her test for a leap?
Asking whether a mechanism is proposed — and whether the author says a leap is being made.
Listening 2

In Defence of the Author

A colleague answering back. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he accept about the criticism?
That paragraph six does not follow, and that the author should have said so.
2. What is his objection to the review?
That demanding a mechanism for every inference would make it impossible to write anything.
3. What does he say about the four studies?
That no two studies in any field use identical definitions, and that saying so is not a finding.
