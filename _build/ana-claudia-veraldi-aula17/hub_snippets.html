Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Etiquette -- the rules of polite behavior in a place or a group
"In Japan, there is a lot of etiquette around giving gifts."
Mandatory -- required by a rule, so you have no choice
"Wearing a seat belt is mandatory in Brazil."
Optional -- not required, so you can choose
"Dessert is optional, but nobody says no to it."
To be expected to -- to be supposed to do something because everybody thinks it is normal
"At a Norwegian dinner, guests are expected to arrive on time."
A faux pas -- a small social mistake that makes you feel embarrassed
"I walked into her living room with my shoes on. What a faux pas!"
To frown upon something -- to think that something is not acceptable
"In many countries, talking loudly on the train is frowned upon."
Well-meaning -- trying to help, even if the result is not perfect
"My aunt is well-meaning, but her advice is sometimes too much."
Unwritten rules -- rules that everybody follows but nobody writes down
"Every small town has its unwritten rules."
To speak your mind -- to say exactly what you think
"She always speaks her mind, even when it is not easy to hear."
A rule of thumb -- a simple, practical rule that is not exact but usually works
"As a rule of thumb, arrive five minutes early for dinner in Norway."
When in Rome -- when you are in another place, follow the customs of that place
"Dinner at six? Fine. When in Rome, do as the Romans do."
To take something the wrong way -- to feel offended by something that was not meant badly
"Please do not take this the wrong way, but your coat is too thin for Oslo."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

When Camila, a Brazilian, visited her friend Ingrid in Bergen, she learned the unwritten rules fast. In a Norwegian home you have to take your shoes off at the door, and you don’t have to bring an expensive present — a small one is better.

Ingrid also gave her some soft advice: it might be an idea to bring a warmer coat. Camila thought it was optional. It was not. It was minus twelve. Ingrid laughed: Next time, you had better listen when a Norwegian says maybe.

In India, Arjun’s aunt gives advice the opposite way: you must eat more, you should call your mother. It sounds like an order, but it is love, and nobody takes it the wrong way. When in Rome, listen to how people give advice before you decide what it means.

1. "You don’t have to bring an expensive present." What does it mean?
A It is forbidden to bring any present to a Norwegian home.
B You can bring one, but it is not necessary.
C You must bring a present.
2. Why was Camila cold in Bergen?
A She thought Ingrid’s soft advice was optional.
B Ingrid told her not to bring a coat because the house was warm.
C The house had no heating.
3. How does Arjun’s family understand his aunt’s direct advice?
A As an order that he has to obey or she gets angry.
B As rude.
C As love.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Rules, Choices and Advice

GRAMMAR

Necessary, not necessary, forbidden, a good idea, a warning — five different jobs.

FormWhat it doesExample
have to / mustNecessaryYou have to take your shoes off.
don’t have toNot necessary — you chooseYou don’t have to bring a gift.
mustn’tForbiddenYou mustn’t smoke inside.
should / ought toAdviceYou should try the brown cheese.
had betterStrong advice + a warningYou’d better bring warm socks.
had toThe past of must and have toLast winter I had to buy boots.

Never: You should to go · you had better to go · yesterday I must leave · you don’t have to smoke here (when you mean it is forbidden). After must, should and had better: the base verb, no to. For the past of must, use had to.

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

Lars helps Ana get ready for a trip to Oslo. Put the conversation in order.

?"You don't have to bring anything big. But you mustn't be late. Dinner at six means six."
?"You might want to bring wool socks. Actually, you'd better bring two pairs."
?"So, Ana, you are really coming to Oslo in January?"
?"Dinner at six? Okay. When in Rome, I will be there at five to six."
?"Do I have to bring a gift for your parents?"
?"I am! Tell me the truth. What should I pack?"
