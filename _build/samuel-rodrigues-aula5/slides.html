The Habit

Interrupted, and Still Finishing

1. Do not restart. Do not apologise. The sentence is still yours.

2. Take it back in six words. "If I may just finish this point."

3. Finish, then hand it over. One breath, one sentence, then "and now to your point".

The Case

Aurora, From the Other Side

Eleven percent down, one undisclosed claim, and London on the horizon.

Listening 1

What the Board Wants

0:000:00
1. Does Karl think the MAC clause lets his board walk away?
No. Revenue is down eleven percent, but it is not durationally significant and there is a carve-out for market-wide conditions.
2. What concerns him more than the MAC?
The disclosure obligation — whether the seller knew about the supplier claim in March and said nothing.
3. What does the governing law clause mean for the dispute, and how does his board feel?
It points to arbitration in London, which nobody on his board wants.

Comprehension

What Did Diane Do?

1. How does she read the eleven percent?
Her board reads it as a walk-away.
2. How many times does she interrupt, and what happens each time?
Twice — and both times he takes the floor back and finishes the sentence, without restarting or apologising.
3. What does she ask for before agreeing to anything?
The document — the data room index entry dated the twelfth of March.
Listening 2

Decide Your One Sentence

0:000:00
1. What two decisions does she tell him to make BEFORE the call?
What his one sentence is, and how he will get back to it after Karl cuts in.
2. Where should the emphasis go when a point has to land?
At the front of the sentence — "not only did the seller...", "never have I seen...".

Do not restart, and do not apologise.
