Lesson 02 -- Pre-class

The Welcome Pack -- Reading What the School Sends You

The orientation letter, the paperwork and the deadlines nobody repeats twice. Key words: orientation, mandatory, deadline, paperwork, transit pass, to sign up for, to fill out, counsellor, facilities, term. Structure: present perfect for what is done, past simple for what happened when.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. Do not translate — the example sentence is the meaning.

Orientation -- the first meeting where new students learn how everything works
"Orientation is on Monday at nine in the main hall."
Mandatory -- something you must do, it is not a choice
"The medical form is mandatory before the first day."
Deadline -- the day and time by which something must be finished
"The deadline is the Friday before classes start."
Paperwork -- all the official documents you have to fill in and send
"Start the paperwork early. It always takes longer than you think."
Transit pass -- the card you use to take buses and trains
"Get your transit pass in the first week, not the second."
To sign up for -- to put your name on a list in order to join something
"I signed up for the school volleyball team on day two."
To fill out -- to write the information that a form asks for
"Fill out the emergency contact sheet and send it back."
Counsellor -- the person at school who helps students with problems and choices
"Talk to the counsellor if your timetable does not work."
Facilities -- the buildings and equipment that a place offers you
"The school facilities include a gym, a theatre and two labs."
Term -- one of the periods the school year is divided into
"The first term runs from September to the end of January."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The email arrived in June and I read it four times. The school has already received my school letter and my passport copy, so those two lines are green. The medical form has not arrived yet, and that one is mandatory. My father signed it last Friday and sent it on Monday, so it is somewhere between two countries right now.

There is more paperwork than I expected. I have filled out the emergency contact sheet, but I have not signed up for the buddy programme, because at first I did not understand what a buddy actually was. Last year several students missed the deadline for the school insurance, so the letter says it twice, in bold.

Orientation is on the first Monday of the term, at nine, in the main hall. Somebody will show me the facilities and I will meet the international counsellor. In the first week I also have to get a transit pass, because the bus is the fastest way to school. I have never done any of this before, and somehow that is the exciting part.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Done, or Done When?

GRAMMAR

One question decides it. Is there a date in the sentence?

FormUseExample
Present perfect
have / has + participle		The result matters now. No date in the sentence, and none needed.I have sent the form.
They have confirmed the family.
Past simple
sent / signed / went		A finished moment with a known time: yesterday, last week, in June, on Monday.I sent it on Monday.
They confirmed it last week.
NegativePresent perfect uses have not / has not. Past simple uses did not plus the base verb.It hasn't arrived yet.
It didn't arrive on time.
QuestionHave / Has for the perfect, Did for the simple.Have you sent it?
Did you send it on Friday?
The four signalsalready, yet, just, never / ever all pull the sentence to the present perfect.
The trapA closed date and the present perfect never share a sentence. "I have sent it yesterday" does not exist. Pick one.

When you are not sure, ask yourself the only question that matters: is there a date? If yes, past simple. If no, present perfect.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are dealing with the school office. Choose the best thing to say.

The office asks whether you have sent the medical form. You sent it on Monday. You say:
A "I have sent it on Monday."
B "I sent it on Monday. Hasn't it arrived?"
C "Yes, is sent."
You want to say that something is still missing, right now. You say:
A "The photo didn't arrive yet."
B "The photo hasn't arrived yet."
C "The photo has not arriving yet."
Somebody asks if you have ever lived in another country. You have not. You say:
A "No, I've never lived abroad before."
B "No, I never live in other country."
C "No, I have not lived abroad yesterday."
A line on the form uses a word you do not know. The best thing to do is:
A Leave it blank and hope nobody notices.
B Write anything, because forms are never read.
C Ask: "Sorry, what does this line on the form mean?"
