The Sound Map

Five Moves That Carry the Meaning

Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I led a team of forty engineers in three countries."

"We finished the migration in March."

"That is not what the data says."

"Could you walk me through the numbers?"

The highlighted words are longer, louder and higher. What kind of words are they?

PieceWhat it isExample
Strong: content wordsNouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs, numbers, question words and negativesI led a team of forty engineers.
Weak: function wordsa, the, of, to, in, for, and, is, are, was, can, and pronouns -- shorter, quieter, lowerI led a team of forty engineers.
Thought groupThree to seven words, one idea, a small pause at the end -- and never insideI led a team of forty engineers / in three countries
Focus wordThe last content word of the group carries the biggest emphasis -- unless you are correcting somethingWe finished the migration in March.
MelodyStatements and wh-questions fall at the end: "What did you change?" Yes-no questions rise: "Could you walk me through the numbers?"

The negative is always strong: "That is not what the data says." And a consonant meeting a vowel joins: "an old system" sounds like "a-nold system".

Focus

One Sentence, Four Meanings

Same eight words. The emphasis moves, and so does the message. Say each one, then tap to check

"I never said the migration failed." ___Someone else said it. It was not me.
"I never said the migration failed." ___Not once, at any point. A stronger denial.
"I never said the migration failed." ___Something else failed, but not the migration.
"I never said the migration failed." ___It was late, or it was paused -- but it did not fail.

In a difficult meeting this is the difference between defending yourself and starting an argument.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I. LED. A. TEAM. OF. FORTY. ENGINEERS." -- every word the same length and weight
"I LED a TEAM of forty ENGINEERS." -- strong beats, and the small words run fast
"We finished the migration last week?" -- a fact said with a rising ending
"We finished the migration last week." -- the voice falls, because it is a fact
"I led / a team of / forty / engineers in / three countries." -- pauses inside the ideas
"I led a team of forty engineers / in three countries." -- one pause, between two ideas

Portuguese gives almost every syllable the same time. English does not: it runs on strong beats, and the small words between them get shorter. Group the words, land the last one, and let the ending fall.

More Practice

Where Does the Emphasis Go?

Say the full sentence out loud, then tap to check

"We finished the migration in ___MARCH." (answering: when did you finish it?)
"___WE finished the migration in March." (answering: which team finished it?)
"That is ___NOT what the data says." (the negative is always strong)
"I led a team of forty ___ENGINEERS / in three countries." (the last content word of the group)
"It was forty engineers, ___NOT SIXTY." (correcting a number someone got wrong)
Dialogue

Say It Again, But Slower

M
Every word had the same weight, so I heard none of them. Break it into three groups and put a pause at the end of each one.

Better. Now the emphasis. One word in each group carries the meaning, and it is almost always the last one.
L

In English the rhythm does half the work. And one more thing: your voice went up at the end of that sentence, so a fact sounded like a question.

M
That is the version they will remember. Nothing in the sentence changed except the music.

About the Conversation

1. What does Mark say he heard the first time she said the line?
None of the words -- every word had the same weight.
2. Which word in a group does Mark say carries the meaning?
Almost always the last one.
3. What problem does Mark hear at the end of one of her sentences?
Her voice went up, so a fact sounded like a question.
Listening 1

What the Listener Is Actually Doing

A speech coach explains why flat English is tiring. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she say an American listener uses the strong beats for?
To find the meaning -- the strong words tell the listener where the information is.
2. Why does she say flat English is tiring, even when it is correct?
Because the listener has to do all the work of deciding which words matter, and after a minute of that the room stops listening.
3. What does she tell her clients to do instead of saying um?
Close the mouth and pause -- silence costs nothing, a filler costs credibility.
Listening 2

Two Candidates, One Difference

An interviewer records a note after a long day of panels. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What does he say about the first candidate's grammar?
It was close to perfect -- the problem was never the English.
2. What did the second candidate do before answering the hardest question?
She stopped and stayed silent for about two seconds, and then answered.
3. What does he ask the recruiter to tell candidates?
To slow down and use the silence -- nobody is ever rejected for pausing.

Self-Assessment

What I Learned

I make content words strong and let function words run short and quiet.
I break a long answer into thought groups and pause between them, never inside them.
I put the emphasis on the focus word, and I move it when I need to correct something.
I let statements fall at the end, so a fact never sounds like a question.
I know the words: emphasis, pace, pause, delivery, filler, monotone, pitch, mumble.
