Chapter 3: The Code

Decided Before, or Decided Right Now

Two futures, and the difference is WHEN you chose

Grammar Discovery

Which Two Were Decided Yesterday?

"I am going to save some money this year."

"She booked the tickets last night, so she is going to travel in January."

"I am not sure yet. I will decide when I see the prices."

"Send me a photo when you get there." — "I will."

The first two were already decided before anybody opened their mouth — and both carry going to. The last two were decided inside the sentence, while the person was speaking — and both carry will. Look at what follows each one: after going to and after will, the verb is bare.

JobFormExample
A plan you already decidedam / is / are going to + bare verbI am going to save some money this year.
A decision made while speakingwill + bare verbI will decide when I see the prices.
A promise or an offerwill + bare verb"Send me a photo." — "I will." · "I will help you with that."
Negativeam not going to + verb · will not + verbI am not going to stay home again. I will not forget.
QuestionAre you going to + verb · Will you + verbAre you going to take the dog? · Will you call me?
Question word in frontWhat / Where / When + are you going to + verbWhat are you going to do this year?
When it happensnext week, next year, soon, this weekend, in the futureI am going to book it soon.
In speechI will becomes I’ll · will not becomes won’tI’ll decide later. I won’t forget.
The two trapsnever drop the to after going · never add a to after willNot I am going travel → going to travel. Not I will to save → I will save.

In one line: if you decided before you opened your mouth, going to. If you are deciding right now, will.

Listening 1 · Two Years of Saving

She Booked It in Twenty Minutes

A woman explaining how she paid for a trip. Sound first — no text.

1. How long did she save, and what did she NOT do?
Two years. She did not stop buying coffee and she did not cancel anything.
2. What exactly did she do with the money?
She opened a second account with no card, and every time somebody paid her she moved a small amount into it before she looked at the balance.
3. What does she recommend about the moment of booking?
Book it fast, before you have time to be sensible. She says she booked in twenty minutes, before she could think about it.
Listening 2 · Eleven Years

A Dream Is a Sentence. A Plan Has a Date.

A man on the eleven years he spent saying the same thing. Sound first — no text.

1. What difference does he draw between a dream and a plan?
A dream is a sentence. A plan has a date in it.
2. How long did he say he was going to learn to sail, and what finally changed it?
Eleven years. Somebody asked him when, he had no answer, and it embarrassed him enough to book a course for the following month.
3. What is his advice at the end, and why?
Keep the dream, but put a date on it this week and tell somebody — because a promise you make out loud is much harder to move.
Listening 3 · Two Boring Things

Both of Them Will Save You a Bad Morning

A woman on the two things travellers forget every year. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the first thing she says to check today, and what is the rule?
The passport. A lot of countries will not let you in if it expires within six months, so one that is valid until March is not valid for a trip in January.
2. What has to match exactly when you book a ticket?
The name on the ticket has to match the passport exactly — every letter, including the middle name you never use.
3. What do some airlines do in the first twenty-four hours, and what happens after that?
They change the name for free in the first twenty-four hours. After that they charge more than the ticket cost.
