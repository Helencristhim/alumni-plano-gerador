Plans and Dreams -- What You Decided, and What You Decide While Speaking

A dream is a sentence. A plan has a date in it — and English uses a different form for each. Key words: a plan, a dream, to book, to save money, to retire, to promise, to decide, soon, a ticket, a passport. Structure: going to for something you already decided, will for a decision you make in the middle of the sentence, and the two traps that catch every Brazilian.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

English has two ways of talking about what has not happened yet, and the difference is not when it happens. It is when you decided. If the decision is older than the sentence — if you were already thinking about it this morning — you use going to: "I am going to save some money this year." "She booked the tickets last night, so she is going to travel in January." The plan exists before anybody opens their mouth.

If the decision happens inside the sentence, while you are speaking, you use will: "I am not sure yet. I will decide when I see the prices." It is also what you use for a promise or an offer, which is why the shortest answer in English is two words: "Send me a photo." — "I will." Nobody planned that. It happened right there.

Both forms leave the verb bare, and both have a trap. After going, the little word to is not optional: never I am going travel. After will, there is no to at all: never I will to save. Negatives and questions behave exactly as you would expect — "I am not going to stay home again", "Are you going to take the dog?", "What are you going to do this year?" Add when it happens (next week, soon, this weekend, when I retire), and hand the question back. A dream stays a dream until you say it out loud with a date attached.

1. According to the text, what decides between going to and will?
A How far away the event is.
B When you decided — before the sentence, or inside it.
C Whether the sentence is positive or negative.
2. Why is "Send me a photo." — "I will." an example of will and not going to?
A Because nobody planned it. The promise happens right there, while speaking.
B Because photos are always in the will form.
C Because the answer is short.
3. What are the two traps the text names?
A Using going to for the past, and using will for a habit.
B Forgetting the subject, and forgetting the date.
C Dropping the to after going, and adding a to after will.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Going To, Will, and the Two Traps

GRAMMAR

If you decided before you opened your mouth, going to. If you are deciding right now, will.

JobFormExample
A plan you already decidedam / is / are going to + bare verbI am going to save some money this year.
A decision made while speakingwill + bare verbI will decide when I see the prices.
A promise or an offerwill + bare verb"Send me a photo." — "I will." · "I will help you."
Negativeam not going to + verb · will not + verbI am not going to stay home again.
QuestionAre you going to + verb · Will you + verbAre you going to take the dog?
Question word in frontWhat / Where / When + are you going to + verbWhat are you going to do this year?
When it happensnext week, next year, soon, this weekend, in the futureI am going to book it soon.
In speechI will becomes I’ll · will not becomes won’tI’ll decide later. I won’t forget.
The two trapsnever drop the to after going · never add a to after willNot I am going travel. Not I will to save.
