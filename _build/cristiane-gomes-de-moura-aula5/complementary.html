Film
The Way -- Martin Sheen on the Camino de Santiago

A man goes to Spain for one reason and decides, in the middle of a sentence, to walk eight hundred kilometres instead. That is this whole lesson in one scene: a plan he had, and a decision he made on the spot. It is also a quiet film about why people walk, which is closer to a spiritual question than to a travel one — the corner of cinema you said you like.

Tip: English subtitles on. Every time somebody says what they are going to do, pause and ask yourself: did they decide that before this scene, or during it?

BBC Learning English -- "Will vs Be going to" (English In a Minute)

Sixty seconds, and it is the whole rule. One speaker, one example of each, and the difference stated once, clearly. This is the card to watch on the morning of a day when you know you will have to talk about your plans.

Tip: it is one minute, so watch it five times this week — Monday to Friday, once a day. Repetition of something this short costs you nothing and it fixes the choice for good.

Talk
Tim Urban -- "Inside the mind of a master procrastinator" (TED)

Fourteen very funny minutes on why the plan you made never turned into a date on a calendar. It is the same argument as today’s second recording, told by somebody with drawings, and the last two minutes are not funny at all — they are about the things that have no deadline, which are exactly the ones that never happen.

Tip: he speaks fast, so put the English subtitles on and let the drawings carry you. You do not need every word. Watch to the end, because the last two minutes are the point.
