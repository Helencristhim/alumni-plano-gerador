Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

On the Other Hand -- Conceding a Point and Still Winning

The strongest version of your argument states the objection first, better than the other side could, and then answers it. Key words: to acknowledge, admittedly, a counterargument, to qualify a claim, grounds, arguably, to undermine, compelling, notwithstanding, a blind spot, to hold water, on balance. Structure: concessive and contrastive clauses.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To acknowledge -- to admit that a point is true, before you go on to answer it
"The honest place to begin is by acknowledging what they have got right."
Admittedly -- used to introduce something you accept, even though it weakens your own case
"Admittedly, saying so satisfies nobody."
A counterargument -- the strongest objection that can be made to your own position
"The counterargument is not that the technology is weak."
To qualify a claim -- to add a limit to a statement so that it is not too strong
"Every prediction should be qualified, however confidently it is delivered."
Grounds -- the reasons on which an argument or a decision rests
"He rejected it on the grounds that the sample was too narrow."
Arguably -- used to say a claim can be defended, without fully asserting it
"The machines are faster and, arguably, more consistent."
To undermine -- to weaken something gradually, often without attacking it directly
"The method section undermines the headline, quietly and completely."
Compelling -- strong enough to make somebody change their mind
"The tools are compelling and the claims made for them are not."
Notwithstanding -- despite -- used before the thing that does not change the conclusion
"Notwithstanding the results, the tests measure the easy tasks."
A blind spot -- the thing somebody cannot see, because of where they are standing
"The blind spot is shared: nobody is arguing about responsibility."
To hold water -- (of an argument) to survive examination
"The claim does not hold water once you read the method section."
On balance -- after weighing both sides against each other
"On balance, the sensible position is neither of the two on offer."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Every profession that sells judgement is being told that judgement is about to be automated. The honest place to begin is by acknowledging what the optimists have got right: on tasks where the output can be checked against a known answer, the machines are faster and, arguably, more consistent. Although the results are real, the tasks measured were the easiest ones to measure.

The counterargument is not that the technology is weak. Despite the numbers, the conclusion drawn from them is far broader than the evidence supports. Notwithstanding the published results, almost none of the tests examine the part of the work that consumes the most hours: deciding which question is actually being asked. Whereas a document review has a right answer, that first hour with a client does not, and nothing yet measures it. That, quietly, undermines the headline.

On balance, the sensible position is neither of the two on offer. The tools are compelling. The claims made for them, however, do not hold water once you read the method section. Admittedly, saying so satisfies nobody. The blind spot is shared, though: everybody argues about whether the work can be done faster, and nobody argues about who is responsible when it is done wrong. That second question is the one that will decide how any of this is used, and until it is answered on solid grounds, every prediction should be qualified, however confidently it is delivered.

1. "Although the results are real, the tasks measured were the easiest ones." Why although and not despite?
A Because although is more formal than despite.
B Because what follows is a full sentence with a subject and a verb. Despite would need a noun.
C Because the sentence is about the past.
2. "The tools are compelling. The claims made for them, however, do not hold water." Why is there a full stop before however?
A Because however does not join two clauses the way but does. It turns between two separate sentences.
B Because the sentence would be too long otherwise.
C Because however can only appear at the start of a paragraph.
3. Why does the writer say admittedly, saying so satisfies nobody?
A To apologize for having no opinion at all.
B To concede the weakness of his own position out loud, before a reader can use it against him.
C To signal that he has changed his mind halfway through the essay.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Concessive and Contrastive Clauses

GRAMMAR

Hand them the fact, keep the conclusion. What changes is only what comes after the connector.

JobFormExample
Concede a factalthough / even though + subject + verbAlthough the results are real, ...
Concede a thingdespite / in spite of / notwithstanding + nounDespite the numbers, ...
Contrast two caseswhereas / while + subject + verbWhereas that has an answer, this does not.
Turn between sentenceshowever · nevertheless · that said... . The claims, however, do not.
Concede a whole clausedespite the fact that + subject + verbDespite the fact that the results are real
However + adjectiveno matter howHowever confidently it is delivered
One connector per turnnever although and but togetherAlthough X, Y. (not: Although X, but Y)

Never: despite of the numbers · despite the results are real · although X, but Y · the tools are compelling however the claims are not · in spite the delay

Stage 2: Put the Argument in Order

Order

Build the argument the way the essayist does: the objection first. Put it in order.

?"Let me start with what the other side has got right."
?"On those tasks the results are real and, arguably, better than ours."
?"Despite that, the conclusion drawn from them is far too broad."
?"Whereas those tasks have a right answer, the hardest part of the work does not."
?"Admittedly, that is a limit on my own claim as much as on theirs."
?"On balance, the useful question is the one neither side is asking."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to concede a full statement before you answer it. You say:
A "Despite the results are real, the tasks were the easiest ones."
B "Although the results are real, the tasks were the easiest ones."
C "Despite of the results, the tasks were the easiest ones."
You want to turn between two separate sentences. You say:
A "The tools are compelling however the claims are not."
B "The tools are compelling. The claims, however, do not hold water."
C "Although the tools are compelling, but the claims are not."
Somebody makes a point that genuinely weakens your position. You say:
A "Admittedly, that weakens my own case, and I would still argue it."
B "That is irrelevant."
C "You have not understood my argument."
You want to say the published claim does not survive examination. You say:
A "The claim is a blind spot."
B "The claim does not hold water once you read the method section."
C "The claim is arguably compelling on balance."
