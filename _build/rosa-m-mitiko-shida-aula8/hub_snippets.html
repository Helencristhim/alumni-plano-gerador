Things Used to Be Different -- Twenty Years of This Industry

Used to and would, for the way the job worked before everything moved online. Key expressions: to phase out, legacy system, paper trail, turnaround, fleet, ground staff, overhaul, back then, to take for granted, old-school, to rely on, to die out. Structure: used to for states and habits that are over, would for actions that repeated -- and why would never works with be, have or know.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. These twelve stay here in your material -- they do not disappear after the class.

To phase out -- to stop using something gradually, over a period of time
"They phased out the paper contract over about three years."
Legacy system -- an old computer system that a company still uses
"Two of the four countries are still on the legacy system."
Paper trail -- the written record that shows what happened and who decided it
"Everybody used to know where the paper trail was."
Turnaround -- the time an aircraft spends on the ground between two flights
"The turnaround used to be an hour, and now it is forty minutes."
Fleet -- all the aircraft that an airline owns or operates
"When I started, the fleet used to be half the size it is now."
Ground staff -- the people who work at the airport, not on board the aircraft
"The ground staff would call us directly when a group was delayed."
Overhaul -- a complete change or repair of a system
"The reporting system needs an overhaul, not another workaround."
Back then -- at that time in the past
"Back then, a contract used to take three weeks to arrive."
To take for granted -- to stop noticing how valuable something is
"We used to take that weekly visit for granted."
Old-school -- done in the traditional way, before the new methods
"He is old-school, and his clients have never left him."
To rely on -- to depend on something or somebody to work properly
"We used to rely on the ground staff for what the system did not have."
To die out -- to slowly disappear completely
"That habit died out quietly, and no system replaced it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

There is a photograph on the wall of the São Paulo office, taken about twenty years ago. It shows shelves full of folders, one for every corporate client. Back then, contracts used to arrive on paper: a courier would bring them twice a week and somebody would file them by hand.

The job used to be physical in a way that is difficult to explain now. If a client called about a fare, the account manager would walk to the shelf, open the folder and read the answer out loud. It was slow. It was also completely transparent: everybody knew where the paper trail was.

Other things were different too. The fleet used to be about half the size it is today, the turnaround used to be an hour instead of forty minutes, and account managers used to rely on the ground staff for information that no system had. The company phased out the paper contract over about three years, and two countries are still on the legacy system that replaced it, which is why the reporting needs an overhaul and not another workaround.

Nobody is nostalgic about the paper, and nobody wants the courier back. What the old-school veterans miss is a habit the shelf forced on them: they would open the whole folder twice a week, problem or no problem, and see the client as a whole. That habit died out quietly and nothing replaced it. Most people used to take it for granted, which is exactly why nobody noticed it going.

1. Why does the text say "contracts used to arrive on paper" instead of "contracts arrived on paper"?
A Because used to signals that it was the normal state and it is over.
B Because it happened only once.
C Because it is still true today.
2. "The fleet used to be half the size it is now." Could you say "the fleet would be half the size"?
A Yes, they mean the same thing.
B No, because be is a state, and would only works for repeated actions.
C No, because the fleet is not a person.
3. What does the text say the veterans actually miss?
A The paper contracts.
B The courier who came twice a week.
C The habit of looking at the whole client twice a week.
4. The text says the company "phased out" the paper contract. What does that mean?
A It stopped on one day, by decision.
B It was stopped gradually, over a period of time.
C It was never really stopped.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used To and Would

GRAMMAR

Two ways of saying that something was normal in the past and is not any more. They overlap most of the time -- and there is exactly one situation where only one of them works.

FormWhat it saysExample
used to + base verbIt was the normal state of things and it is not any more. Works for habits and for states."Contracts used to arrive on paper."
"The fleet used to be smaller."
would + base verbA repeated action in the past. Warmer and more narrative — this is the storytelling one."A courier would bring them twice a week."
The one rule that mattersWould does not work for states. be, have, know, live, own, believe take only used to."I used to have thirty accounts."
NOT "I would have thirty accounts."
Negative and questionThe d disappears after did: did not use to, did you use to."I did not use to travel every week."
"Did you use to work in tourism?"
How to open a storySet the scene with used to, then switch to would for the actions that repeated. That is how a native narrates twenty years."Contracts used to arrive on paper. A courier would bring them, and we would file them by hand."

One test decides it: is this an action that repeated, or a state that was true? If it is a state, only used to works.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

A younger colleague is asking about the old days, and a long-standing client says the service has changed. Choose the best thing to say.

1. He asks how contracts arrived before the portal. You say:
A Contracts use to arrive on paper.
B Contracts used to arrive on paper, twice a week.
C Contracts would be on paper, twice a week.
2. You want to describe something that happened again and again. You say:
A A courier would bring them every Tuesday and Friday.
B A courier used to bringing them every Tuesday and Friday.
C A courier brings them every Tuesday and Friday.
3. You want to say the fleet was smaller. You say:
A The fleet would be half the size it is now.
B The fleet used to be half the size it is now.
C The fleet use to be half the size it is now.
4. You want to ask about his previous career. You ask:
A Did you used to work in tourism?
B Did you use to work in tourism before this?
C Used you to work in tourism?
5. A client says the service is not what it used to be. The best first answer is:
A That is not true, our service is better than ever.
B You are right, everything is worse now.
C You are right about one thing: we used to visit every week. Today we answer much faster, and I would like to bring the visit back for you.
