Lesson 8 -- Things Used to Be Different

Podcast & Reading
"How to change careers: 6 actionable tips" -- Life Kit, NPR

Eighteen minutes about looking back at what you have already done and deciding what to carry forward. Connection to Lesson 8: the whole episode is people describing what their working life used to be, which is the structure you practiced today. Full transcript on the page.

Tip: read the transcript and underline every used to and every narrative would you can find. Then tell one of the stories out loud in your own words, in three sentences. Send the page to your Kindle for the flight.

Talk
"A powerful way to unleash your natural creativity" -- Tim Harford, TED

Fifteen minutes from an economist on how the slow, inconvenient way of working sometimes produced better results than the fast one. Connection to Lesson 8: it is the argument the column makes about the folder on the shelf, told with different examples.

Tip: watch it once with English subtitles. Then say one sentence about a slow habit at your own company that you would defend, and one about a slow habit you are glad disappeared.

Grammar Video
"Used To & Would" -- BBC Learning English, 6 Minute Grammar

Six minutes on exactly the pair of this lesson, including the rule that decides between them. Connection to Lesson 8: would tells a story of repeated actions, and used to is the only one that also works for states.

Tip: pause after each example and say the same idea about your own first job. When the sentence contains be, have or know, notice that only one of the two forms fits. That is the whole rule.
