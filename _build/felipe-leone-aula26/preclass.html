Lesson 26 -- Pre-class

Client Relationships -- Already, Yet, Still, Never

Ten to twenty minutes before class. How to give a client a status update with the present perfect: already, yet, still and never. Key words: a deal, to close, a proposal, a deadline, to follow up, a status update.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A deal -- an agreement to buy or sell something
"The deal with the bank is worth two million dollars."
To close a deal -- to finish the agreement and make it official
"We closed the deal on Friday."
A proposal -- a document that offers a plan and a price
"The client is reading our proposal."
A deadline -- the last day to finish something
"The deadline for the report is Monday."
To follow up -- to contact a person again about the same subject
"I followed up with the client by email."
A status update -- a short report on what is done and what is not
"Jennifer asked for a status update."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Paul works with clients in Canada. Every Friday he sends a status update. This week he wrote: “We have already sent the proposal. The client hasn’t answered yet.”

He also wrote: “We still haven’t received the documents from their legal team. We have never had a delay like this with them, so I will follow up on Monday.”

"The client hasn’t answered yet." What does it mean?
A The client will never answer.
B The client has not answered, but Paul expects an answer.
C The client answered last week.
Where do we put still in a negative sentence?
A Before haven’t: We still haven’t received them.
B After the verb: We haven’t received still them.
C At the end: We haven’t received them still.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Already, Yet, Still, Never

GRAMMAR

Four small words with the present perfect, and where they go.

JobFormExample
Done, maybe before you expectedhave already + third formWe have already sent it.
Not done, but expectedhaven’t + third form + yetThey haven’t paid yet.
Not done, and it is latestill haven’t + third formWe still haven’t received it.
Not at any timehave never + third formWe have never lost a client.

Never: We have sent yet · They haven’t still paid · We never have had this problem
