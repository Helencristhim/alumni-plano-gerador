Chapter 3: The Code

Putting the Important Part Where They Will Hear It

Cleft sentences for emphasis

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"What we need is a mandate to screen every supplier."

"It was the near miss in March that changed my mind."

"All I am asking for is a six-month pilot."

"The reason we are here is the audit."

Each sentence could be said in a shorter, simpler way. Say the simple version of each one. Then compare: which part of the sentence did the orange words push to the end, and why would a speaker want it there?

FormWhat it doesExample
What + clause + be + FOCUSSplits one sentence in two to put the new, important part at the END, where the listener waits for it."We need a mandate." → "What we need is a mandate."
It + be + FOCUS + that / whoPoints at ONE element and excludes the others: this, not something else."It was the near miss that changed my mind." (not the audit)
All + clause + beAll means the only thing. It makes a request sound small and reasonable."All I am asking for is six months."
The reason / thing / person + clause + beThe same idea as the what-cleft, with a more specific noun."The thing that worries me is the timing."
What + did + was + verbFor an ACTION in focus, use do: the verb after was is the base form."What we did was review every contract."

Portuguese stresses the important word with the voice. English can do that too, but in a presentation it usually builds the stress into the sentence itself.

Emphasis

Plain Version, Cleft Version

The same fact, with and without the focus

"We need a mandate to screen every supplier."

Plain. Correct, but nothing tells the board this is the sentence to remember.

"What we need is a mandate to screen every supplier."

Cleft. The pause after is makes everybody wait for the request.

"The supplier problem in March changed my mind."

Plain. A fact among other facts.

"It was the supplier problem in March that changed my mind."

Cleft. One cause, and every other possible cause is excluded.

Use a cleft for the two or three sentences you want the board to repeat after you leave the room. Use it for everything, and nothing stands out.

Chapter 4: The Meeting

Five Minutes With the Chair

Laura Benson, chair of the board, and a request for a mandate

Dialogue

What We Are Asking For

L
Thank you, Pedro. You have five minutes, and I should warn you: this board does not like surprises.
P
Then I will get to the point. What we are asking for is a mandate to screen every supplier before we sign, not after.
L
We already have a compliance team. Why do we need another system?
P
It was the near miss in March that made the case. A supplier we had used for two years turned out to be owned by a sanctioned company.
L
That sounds expensive. What is the return on investment?
P
The tool costs less than one month of outside counsel, and it will help us rein in that bill too. All I am asking for is a six-month pilot.
L
Let me put you on the spot, then. In six months, will you stand by that number in front of this board?
P
I will. The thing I want to drive home is simple: the risk is already here. The only question is whether we find it first.
Comprehension

About Laura

1. What does Laura warn Pedro about at the start?
That the board does not like surprises.
2. What does she say the company already has?
A compliance team.
3. What does she want to know about the cost?
What the return on investment is.
4. What does she ask Pedro to commit to?
To stand by the number in front of the board in six months.
