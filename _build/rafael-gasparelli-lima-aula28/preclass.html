Lesson 28 -- Pre-class

The Data and the Close

Get ready to present data and close a presentation. Key words: graph, trend, peak, steady, sharp, slight, to drop, key takeaway. Structure: linking words for result and contrast (so, as a result, however, although).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

8 words and expressions for this lesson. Tap Listen to hear each one.

Graph -- a picture with lines or bars that shows numbers
"This graph shows our exports from January to June."
Trend -- the general direction in which something is changing
"The trend for next year is positive."
Peak -- the highest point or number
"Exports reached a peak in June."
Steady -- changing slowly and regularly, with no big jumps
"We expect steady growth next year."
Sharp -- big and very fast (for a change)
"There was a sharp drop in March."
Slight -- very small (for a change)
"There was a slight increase in April."
To drop -- to go down, to become lower
"Exports dropped because of the rain."
Key takeaway -- the most important idea that people remember after a talk
"The key takeaway is simple: the cold chain works."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the end of Rafael's presentation, then answer the questions.

Now, let's look at this graph. It shows our fruit exports to Harbor Fresh from January to June. In March there was a sharp drop. It rained a lot, so the trucks couldn't reach the farms. As a result, exports dropped to twenty-five containers.

However, the new containers arrived in April, and exports went up again. Although March was difficult, exports reached a peak in June. The trend for next year is positive: we expect steady growth. Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- So, As a Result, However, Although

GRAMMAR

Use so and as a result for a result. Use however and although for a contrast (a surprise).

WordJobExample
soresult, in the middle of a sentenceSales went up, so we hired people.
As a result,result, at the start of a new sentenceIt rained. As a result, exports dropped.
However,contrast, at the start of a new sentenceMarch was bad. However, April was good.
Although + sentencecontrast inside one sentenceAlthough it rained, we recovered.

Never: although ... but · because the rain (use because of + noun) · as result (use as a result)
