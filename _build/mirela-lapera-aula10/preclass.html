Lesson 10 -- Pre-class

Amsterdam in Autumn -- Who, That, Where

A short guide to Amsterdam in the autumn, and how to describe places and people in one sentence. Key words: a canal, a bike lane, a houseboat, a gallery, cozy, a flea market, the foliage, to wander, a local, off the beaten track. Structure: relative clauses.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A canal -- a long, narrow channel of water, made by people, for boats
"Our hotel was next to a quiet canal."
A bike lane -- a part of the street only for bicycles
"Be careful: don’t walk in the bike lane."
A houseboat -- a boat that people live in, like a house
"We stayed on a houseboat for two nights."
A gallery -- a room or building where people can see art
"The gallery has famous Dutch paintings."
Cozy -- warm, comfortable and relaxing
"We found a cozy café with candles on the tables."
A flea market -- an outdoor market where people sell old and used things
"I bought an old map at the flea market."
The foliage -- the leaves of the trees, especially when they change color
"The autumn foliage along the canals is beautiful."
To wander -- to walk slowly without a plan, just to explore
"We wandered around the old streets all afternoon."
A local -- a person who lives in the place you are visiting
"Ask a local for the best place to eat."
Off the beaten track -- far from the places where most tourists go
"We found a market that was off the beaten track."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Mirela visited Amsterdam on the same winter trip that took her to Edinburgh. It was very cold, and she wants to go back in a season when the city is calmer: the spring or the autumn.

In the autumn, the foliage along the canals turns yellow and red. It is a good time to wander through the streets where people ride their bikes, to see the houseboats that people really live in, and to find a small gallery that most tourists never see.

A local who knows the city well would probably say: find a cozy café, visit a flea market on Saturday, and take the ferry to the north, an area that is off the beaten track. And never walk in a bike lane!

1. In “a local who knows the city well”, why does the text use who?
A Because a local is a person.
B Because the city is very old, and we always use who for old places like Amsterdam.
C Because it is a question.
2. Why does Mirela want to go back to Amsterdam?
A Because she wants to live on a houseboat for the rest of her life, far from the city.
B Because her first trip was in the winter, and she wants to see the city in a calmer season.
C Because she never visited it before.
3. What is off the beaten track, according to the text?
A The big museums in the center, which everybody visits in the summer.
B The bike lanes.
C The north of the city, which you reach by ferry.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Relative Clauses -- Who, That, Where

GRAMMAR

Use who for people, that or which for things, where for places and when for times. When the pronoun is the object of the second idea, you can leave it out: the café (that) we found.

JobFormExample
A personwho / thata local who told us about a market
A thingwhich / thathouseboats that people live in
A placewherea city where everybody rides a bike
A timewhenthe season when the city is calm
Leaving it outno pronoun when it is the objectthe café (that) we found
The trapno what, and no extra it / thereNot the city where I lived there. Say the city where I lived.

Never: a museum who · a woman which · the city where I lived there · the café what we found

Quiz
