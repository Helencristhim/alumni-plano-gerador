Chapter 4: The Code

The Museum That...

Who, which, that, where

Grammar Discovery

Two Ideas, One Sentence

"Amsterdam is a city where almost everybody rides a bike."

"You will see houseboats that people really live in."

"We met a local who told us about a hidden market."

"The café we found had candles on every table."

Each sentence joins two ideas. Blue: where, for a place. Red: that, for a thing. Green: who, for a person. And sentence 4 has a secret: the word that is missing. The café [that] we found. When the pronoun is the object, you can leave it out.

JobFormExample
A personwho / thata local who told us about a market
A thingwhich / thathouseboats that people live in
A placewherea city where everybody rides a bike
A timewhenthe season when the city is calm
Leaving it outno pronoun when it is the objectthe café (that) we found
The trapno what, and no extra it / thereNot the city where I lived there. Say the city where I lived.

In one line: who for people, that or which for things, where for places.

Listening 1 · A Local

My City, My Rules

A man who was born in Amsterdam gives advice to visitors. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the first thing tourists do wrong, according to him?
They walk in the bike lanes.
2. Which part of the city does he recommend?
The north, which you reach with the free ferry.
3. Why does he like the autumn?
The city is quiet, and the light on the canals is beautiful.
Listening 2 · A Visitor

Second Time, Better Time

A British woman compares her two trips to Amsterdam. Sound first — no text.

1. When did she first go to Amsterdam, and what was the problem?
In the summer; it was crowded and the lines for the museums were very long.
2. When did she go back, and what was different?
In the autumn; the streets were calm and she could walk everywhere.
3. What was the highlight of her second trip?
A small gallery that she found by accident, run by a woman who painted canals.
Listening 3 · A Cyclist

Seeing the City by Bike

An American man explains why he rented a bike in Amsterdam, and what he learned. Sound first — no text.

1. Why was he nervous on the first day?
There were bikes everywhere, and everybody was very fast.
2. What did a local teach him?
To use his hand to show where he was turning.
3. What did he see that he would not see on foot?
Villages and windmills outside the city.
