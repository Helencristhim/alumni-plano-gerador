Since, For and Ago

How long have you been doing what you do? It is the second question anybody asks you, and English answers it with three small words that are not interchangeable.

The difference

One tense, and the person in front of you hears it immediately

A client is deciding whether to trust you with money. Whether you have done this for eleven years or you did it for two years and stopped is not a small detail: it is the whole answer. English separates the two with the tense, before the numbers even arrive.

Chapter 3: The Code

For, Since and Ago

Three small words, and only one of them belongs to the past

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I have worked here for eleven years."

"I have worked here since twenty fifteen."

"I joined the bank eleven years ago."

"How long have you been in this job?"

Three of these sentences describe the same eleven years. Two of them use the same tense and one does not. Which word forces the change?

FormWhat it saysExample
for + a period of timeHow long it has lasted. Three years, ten minutes, ages."I have worked here for eleven years."
since + a starting pointWhen it began. A year, a date, an event."I have worked here since twenty fifteen."
"since my daughter was born"
ago + past simpleHow far back the START was, counted from today. Ago always takes the past simple."I joined the bank eleven years ago."
NOT "I have joined eleven years ago"
How long have you...?The question that asks for the whole period, and it always uses this tense."How long have you been in this job?"
Still true, or finishedThe tense carries the answer before the words do."I have worked there for two years." (I am still there)
"I worked there for two years." (I left)

For and since belong to the present perfect and describe something that is still true. Ago belongs to the past simple and points to a finished moment. Mixing them is the single fastest way to confuse a client.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I work here since 2015."
"I have worked here since 2015."
"I have joined the bank eleven years ago."
"I joined the bank eleven years ago."
"I have worked here since eleven years."
"I have worked here for eleven years."

Portuguese uses the present for something that started in the past and continues; English uses the present perfect. Ago counts back from today and therefore lives with the past simple, never with have. And the choice between for and since is mechanical: a period takes for, a starting point takes since. Read the correct versions aloud twice.

Chapter 4: The New Client

The First Ten Minutes

A client you have never met, deciding whether to trust you

Dialogue

Tell Me About Your Background

L
Before we start on the numbers, tell me a little about your background.
J
Of course. I joined the bank eleven years ago and I have worked with investments ever since.
L
And before that? How long have you been in this industry altogether?
J
Almost twenty-five years. I finished my degree in the nineties and I have never left the sector.
L
What do you specialise in?
J
Clients who invest outside Brazil. I have taken on about forty families in eleven years.
L
That is a small number. Deliberately small, I assume.
J
Deliberately small. How long have you been looking for somebody here?
Comprehension

About Laura

1. What does Laura want to know before they talk about numbers?
A little about his background.
2. What is her second question about?
How long he has been in the industry altogether.
3. How does she react to the number of families?
She says it is a small number, and assumes it is deliberate.
4. What does Laura ask about first, the numbers or the person?
The person -- she asks about his background first.
Listening

Before We Begin

A colleague introducing somebody at a meeting. Sound first -- no text.

1. How long has he been with the bank, and when did he join?
Eleven years, and he joined in twenty fifteen.
2. What does he specialise in?
Clients who hold money outside the country.
3. Why does the speaker say the number of families is small?
Because he has kept it small deliberately, so that each family gets real attention.
Listening 2

The Turning Point

A short personal story from a podcast. Sound first -- no text.

1. How long had she been in the same job before the meeting she describes?
Six years.
2. What happened in the meeting?
She could not answer a question in English and somebody else answered it for her.
3. What has she done ever since?
She has spoken in every meeting, even when the sentence is short and imperfect.

The Habit

Your Background, Out Loud

Before the next class, say your background out loud once a day in three sentences: how long you have been where you are, what you did before, and what you specialise in. Sixty seconds, to nobody.

This is the answer you will give more often than any other in English. It should not be improvised every time.
