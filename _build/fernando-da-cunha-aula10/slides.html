Tell a story where one thing had already happened before another.

The structure of the day is what a story needs when it is not told in a straight line: by the time we arrived, she had already finished, and we had been driving for three hours when the rain started. One says what was complete before the moment, the other says what was still going on.

One Question

What Had Already Happened Before You Got There?

Two sentences, out loud, right now.

Take any story from the last year outside work. Say what was happening, and then say the thing that had already happened before it and explains it. English marks that earlier layer with a different tense, and without it a story arrives flat.

Chapter 4: The Code

The Layer Underneath the Story

What had already happened, and what had been going on

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"By the time we got to the pool, she had already swum her race."

"We had been driving for three hours when the rain started."

"I had not played tennis for eleven years when I took it up again."

"She had been asking to go back all winter, so we went in July."

Every sentence has two moments, and one of them is earlier than the other. Look at what the earlier one is doing: in the red pair it is finished, and in the blue pair it was still going on.

JobFormExample
Finished before another past momenthad + past participleShe had already swum her race.
Still going on up to that momenthad been + verb-ingWe had been driving for three hours.
How long, up to a past momenthad been + -ing + for / sinceShe had been asking all winter.
Negative and neverhad not / had never + participleI had never seen her swim a whole race.
Common time wordsby the time, already, never, when, beforeBy the time we arrived, it had finished.
State verbsbe, have, know: no -ing even for durationWe had had the car for two days.
The trapthe plain past is not enough when order mattersNot when we arrived she already swam. Say she had already swum.

In one line: had done is finished before the moment; had been doing was still running into it.

The Page You Write on the Way Home

NextTwo long trips, three getaways
By the time we reached the harbour the ferry had already started boarding, and we had been driving since six, so nobody argued about the queue. The open-water swim was the entry everybody remembers: she had been asking to try it since the spring, and she had never swum anywhere without a wall before. Ten days was long enough that the week we had left behind stopped being the thing we talked about, which had not happened on any of the shorter getaways.
1. Which sentence in the note describes something that was still going on up to a past moment?
"we had been driving since six" — the past perfect continuous, still running into the moment they reached the harbour.
2. Which sentence describes something finished before a past moment, and how do you know?
"the ferry had already started boarding" and "she had never swum anywhere without a wall before" — had plus a past participle, complete before the moment in the story.
3. Why does the log record "Why then" as its own line?
Because the timing was the decision: the off-season is the only window that belongs to the family rather than to the plant.
Listening 1 · The Parent

The Race I Nearly Missed

A director telling a story from outside work, in the order she lived it. Sound first — no text.

1. How long had her daughter been swimming, and why had she never seen a race?
About a year and a half. The races are on Saturday mornings and Saturday mornings were the plant.
2. What happened on the Friday night, and what was the result on the Saturday?
A client called about a delivery that had gone wrong, she spent three hours on the phone, and she arrived at the pool forty minutes late, after the race.
3. What does she say she had believed until that morning, and what does she say now?
That these things averaged out. She says they do not average out, and that each one is the only one of itself.
Listening 2 · The Researcher

Why the Habit Survives and the Plan Does Not

A researcher on recovery, routines and what actually holds. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does he say "how to build a habit" is the wrong question?
Because most of the people asking already have several — they have been getting up at the same time for a decade. What they want to know is how to stop a good habit being taken.
2. Which habits survive, and which die?
Habits attached to a fixed point in the day that somebody else does not control survive. Habits that live in the space left over die.
3. What had almost everybody who kept a routine done, and what had they not done?
They had moved it earlier rather than protecting it where it was. They had not become more disciplined.
