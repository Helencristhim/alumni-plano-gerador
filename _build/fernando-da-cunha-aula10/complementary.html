Talk
TED -- Andrew Stanton, "The clues to a great story" (19 min)

A Pixar writer on why some stories hold an audience and others do not, and almost all of it is about order: what the listener knows, and when. It is the argument behind the whole grammar of this lesson.

Tip: nineteen minutes. Watch it once, then retell one story from your own life applying his first rule.

Podcast
NPR Life Kit -- "How to better manage your time"

Practical, spoken at a normal pace, and squarely on the subject of this lesson: which parts of a week get protected and which quietly disappear. Closer to your Tuesday than any business podcast on this list.

Tip: listen once without stopping. Then say out loud which hour in your week is the one nobody else can move.

Video
"Storytelling in Business -- English Phrases To Make Your Story Unforgettable" -- Shanthi Cumaraswamy Streat

Short and practical: the exact phrases English uses to move a listener through a story, including the ones that mark what had already happened. Made for people at your level who tell stories well in their own language.

Tip: watch with a pen. Take three phrases and use them tonight, out loud, on a story you have told a hundred times in Portuguese.
