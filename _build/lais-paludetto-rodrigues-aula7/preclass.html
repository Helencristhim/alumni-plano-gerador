Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

What the Teacher Said -- Passing on Messages Without Losing Them

Relaying instructions, fixing misunderstandings and reporting what people said. Key words: to remind, to mention, to warn, to clarify, misunderstanding, to pass on, to double-check, to apologize, on purpose, to sort out. Structure: reported speech -- everything steps back one tense.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. Do not translate — the example sentence is the meaning.

To remind -- to make somebody remember something they already knew
"She asked me to remind Owen about his source list."
To mention -- to say something briefly, without going into detail
"She mentioned the room was smaller, but she did not explain why."
To warn -- to tell somebody about a danger or a problem before it happens
"He warned me that the north stairs come out by the gym."
To clarify -- to make something easier to understand
"Could you clarify whether the slides are still due on Tuesday?"
Misunderstanding -- a situation where two people understood the same thing differently
"It was a misunderstanding, not an argument."
To pass on -- to give a message to the person it is really for
"Could you pass this on to the rest of your group?"
To double-check -- to check something a second time, to be sure
"I'd rather double-check now than turn up on the wrong day."
To apologize -- to say sorry for something you did
"I apologized for forgetting, and that was the end of it."
On purpose -- deliberately, not by accident
"He did not do it on purpose, he just misheard me."
To sort out -- to solve a problem or put something back in order
"We sorted it out in two messages, once somebody asked properly."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

She caught me between classes and said four things in about forty seconds. She told me the presentation had been moved to Thursday, because the hall was being used. She said the slides were still due on Tuesday evening, and that part had not changed. She asked me to remind Owen about his source list. And she warned us that the new room was smaller.

By the time it reached the group chat, it was unrecognisable. Somebody said the presentation was cancelled. Somebody else mentioned that the slides had been cancelled too. Dylan asked whether it was on Thursday or due on Thursday, which are extremely different things, and he was right to double-check.

Nobody did any of that on purpose. It was a misunderstanding, and misunderstandings are what happen when four people repeat one sentence. I wrote the four points down while she was still speaking, so I passed them on word for word, and the whole thing was sorted out in two messages. Then I apologized to Owen for the source list, because I had almost forgotten to tell him.

Why is it "She told me" and not "She said me"?
A Because told always takes a person after it and said never does.
B Because said is only for questions.
C Because they mean different things.
"He asked whether it was on Thursday" shows that in a reported question:
A You keep the question word order.
B The word order goes back to normal.
C You always add did.
According to the text, why did the message change so much?
A Somebody changed it deliberately.
B Because four people repeated one sentence, and nobody wrote it down.
C Because the teacher explained it badly.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- One Step Back

GRAMMAR

When you repeat what somebody said, every verb takes one step back in time. That is the whole movement.

FormUseExample
present → pastis → was · have → had · can → could · will → wouldShe said the room was smaller.
yes/no questionasked + if or whether, and the word order goes back to normalShe asked if I had finished.
wh- questionasked + question word, also with normal word orderHe asked where I was from.
order or requesttold / asked + person + to + verbShe told us to send the slides.
said or toldtold always needs a person right after it; said never does.She told me that...
She said that...
time words move tootoday → that day · tomorrow → the next day · yesterday → the day before

Everything steps back one tense, and in questions the word order goes back to normal. Those two moves cover almost every sentence you will need.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are the person who has to relay the message. Choose the best thing to say.

The teacher told you the presentation is on Thursday. You report it:
A "She said me it is on Thursday."
B "She told me it was on Thursday."
C "She told that is Thursday."
Which sentence is correct?
A "He asked me where was the room."
B "He asked me where the room was."
C "He asked me where is the room."
Your host mum said "Be home by nine." You report it to a friend:
A "She told me to be home by nine."
B "She told me be home by nine."
C "She said me to be home by nine."
Somebody in the group chat has spread a wrong version. The best first message is:
A "You're wrong."
B "I think there's been a misunderstanding. What she actually said was..."
C Nothing, and let them find out on Thursday.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
