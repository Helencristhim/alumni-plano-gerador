Chapter 3: The Code

One Step Back

Everything the other person said moves back one tense

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The presentation is on Thursday." → She said it was on Thursday.

"I will send the slides." → He said he would send the slides.

"Have you finished?" → She asked if I had finished.

"Send me the slides." → She told us to send her the slides.

Compare the two halves of each line. What happens to the verb, every single time?

They saidYou reportExample
present → pastis → was · have → had · can → could · will → wouldShe said the room was smaller.
yes/no questionasked + if or whether, and the word order goes back to normalShe asked if I had finished. Never if had I.
wh- questionasked + question word, also with normal word orderHe asked where I was from.
order or requesttold / asked + person + to + verbShe told us to send the slides.
He asked me to wait.
said or toldtold always needs a person after it: she told me. said never does: she said that... — never she said me.

One movement: everything steps back one tense. And in questions, the word order goes back to normal.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"She said me the presentation was moved."
"She told me the presentation was moved."
"He asked me where was the room."
"He asked me where the room was."
"She told us send the slides on Tuesday."
"She told us to send the slides on Tuesday."

Told always takes a person, said never does. In a reported question the word order goes back to normal. And an order becomes told somebody to plus the verb.

Chapter 4: Broken Telephone

Four People, Four Versions

And you are the only one who wrote it down

Dialogue

Sorting Out the Group Chat

D
Okay, I'm confused. Somebody in the chat said the presentation was cancelled. Is that real?
L
No, that's a misunderstanding. She told me it was moved to Thursday, not cancelled.
D
Thursday. Right. And the slides? Someone said we didn't have to send them any more.
L
She said that part hadn't changed. The slides are still due on Tuesday evening. I wrote it down, so I'm sure.
D
Good, because I'd rather double-check now than turn up on the wrong day. Anything else?
L
Two things. She asked me to remind Owen about his source list, and she warned us that the new room is smaller.
D
Smaller how? Because our whole ending is a video on a big screen.
L
That's exactly what she meant. I'll put all four points in the chat, word for word, and we can sort out the ending tomorrow.
Comprehension

About Dylan

1. What did he think had happened to the presentation?
He thought it had been cancelled, because somebody said so in the chat.
2. Why does he say he wants to check?
Because he would rather double-check than turn up on the wrong day.
3. Why is the smaller room a problem for his group?
Their whole ending is a video on a big screen.
Listening

Caught Between Classes

The teacher stops you in the corridor with four things. Sound first — no text.

1. What has been moved, and why?
The presentation, to Thursday, because the hall is being used for the assembly.
2. What has NOT changed?
The slides still have to be sent on Tuesday evening.
3. What are the two things she asks about Owen and the room?
Remind Owen about his source list, and warn the group that the room is smaller, so a big screen will not work.
Listening 2

The Same Message, Three People Later

Dylan has heard four versions and believes none of them. Sound first — no text.

1. What two different versions has he heard?
That the day is Thursday, and that the slides were cancelled.
2. What is the exact question he cannot answer?
Whether the presentation is on Thursday, or whether everything has to be sent by Thursday.
3. What does he ask her to do?
Tell him exactly what the teacher said, word for word if possible.
