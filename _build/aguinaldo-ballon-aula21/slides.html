Chapter 3: The Code

What Matters Is the Focus

Cleft sentences and fronting

Grammar Discovery

Where Does the Focus Go?

"What investors need is a stable rule."

"It is the regulator that decides the tariff, not the government."

"The reason why we are here is the port."

"The currency risk, we can manage. The political risk, we cannot."

Compare sentence 1 with "Investors need a stable rule." Which version makes you wait for the important words? In sentence 2, what does It is... that point to? In sentence 4, what comes first: the subject or the object, and why?

JobFormExample
Point to the key ideaWhat + subject + verb + is ...What investors need is a stable rule.
Point to a person or a thingIt is / It was + X + that / who ...It is the regulator that decides.
Name the reason or the problemThe reason why ... is · The thing that ... isThe reason why we are here is the port.
Only one thingAll + subject + verb + is ...All we need is one more signature.
Contrast two thingsfronting: object + subject + verbThe currency risk, we can manage.
The trapnever drop the it, never add an extra itNot Is the regulator that decides · not What we need it is.

In one line: to point the listener to your key idea, start with What... is or It is... that, and never drop the it.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Pitch, at Real Speed

One conversation, three recordings, one investment memo

Dialogue

The Call Before the Committee

H
Aguinaldo, I'll be honest with you. The investment committee is skeptical about Brazil.
A
I understand. What worries them most, the currency or the rules?
H
It's the rules that worry them. The currency risk, they can manage.
A
Then let me make a case for this port. What makes it different is the regulator.
H
That's a strong selling point, but you'll need to back it up with numbers.
A
Of course. It's the framework that gives this project its credibility: three ports, and no change in the rules in ten years.
H
That's compelling. What I need now is buy-in from the two partners in London.
A
Then let's win them over together. All I need is twenty minutes with them on Thursday.
Comprehension

About Hannah

1. How does the committee feel about Brazil, according to Hannah?
They are skeptical.
2. What worries the committee, according to Hannah: the currency or the rules?
The rules. The currency risk, they can manage.
3. What does Hannah need now?
Buy-in from the two partners in London.

Listening 2 · The Fund Manager

What Our Committee Wants to Hear

A manager at an infrastructure fund explains how his investment committee decides. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the first thing the committee wants to know?
What can go wrong, and what the team will do about it.
2. What happens when a team hides a risk?
The committee loses trust in everything else.
3. What is the real deal-breaker, according to him?
A team that does not know its own numbers.
Business Radio

A Port Attracts Bids

A short business news report about a port concession in the south of Brazil. Sound first — no text.

1. How many groups sent bids for the port concession?
Five groups.
2. What was the main selling point of the project, according to analysts?
The stability of the rules.
3. When will the winner be announced?
In December.
Real Document

One Page, One Case

INVESTMENT MEMO — SOUTH PORT CONCESSION
Prepared for the investment committee · summary of the case
The opportunityA 25-year port concession · roughly 40% of the grain exports of the region
Main selling pointStable rules: no change in the contract or the tariff formula in ten years
Key risk 1Currency: revenues in reais · partly hedged by export contracts in dollars
Key risk 2Political: a new government in 2027 · the regulator is independent by law
Possible deal-breakerA change in the tariff formula · protected by a clause in the contract
Local counselAguinaldo Ballon · infrastructure and regulatory lawyer
Decision neededBuy-in from the two London partners before Thursday
The committee reads the memo in two minutes. What it reads first is the selling point; what it reads last is the decision needed.
1. What is the main selling point of the port, according to the memo?
Stable rules: no change in the contract or the tariff formula in ten years.
2. What could be a deal-breaker, and how is it protected?
A change in the tariff formula; it is protected by a clause in the contract.
3. What decision is needed before Thursday?
Buy-in from the two London partners.
