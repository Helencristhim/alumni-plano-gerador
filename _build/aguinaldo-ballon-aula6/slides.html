The structure of the day is the one academic English uses to define ideas: a regulator is an agency which controls a sector, and the author, who teaches in Rome, studied thirty contracts. The first sentence gives essential information. The second adds extra information between commas.

Chapter 4: The Code

Who, Which, That and Where

Essential information, and extra information

Grammar Discovery

Who, Which, Where, and the Commas

"A regulator is an agency which controls a sector."

"A supervisor is the professor who guides your research."

"The author, who teaches in Rome, studied thirty contracts."

"Chile, where the case study was done, has a stable framework."

The red words connect a second piece of information to a noun. In the first two sentences, the information is essential. In the last two, it is extra, and it has commas. Try deleting the part between the commas.

JobFormExample
A personwho (or that, in essential information)the professor who guides your research
A thing or an ideawhich (or that, in essential information)an agency which controls a sector
A placewherethe country where the study was done
Possessionwhosethe author whose thesis won the prize
Essential information (defining)no commas; that is possibleA clause that limits liability is common.
Extra information (non-defining)commas; never thatThe author, who teaches in Rome, studied thirty contracts.
The trapnever what, and never repeat the pronounNot the contract what I read it. Say the contract which I read.

In one line: who for people, which for things, where for places; commas and no that when the information is extra.

Listening 1 · The Professor

Welcome to the Seminar

A professor opens a doctoral seminar in regulatory law for international students. Sound first — no text.

1. Why is English the language of the seminar?
Because the students come from different countries and English is the language they all share.
2. How long is each student's presentation?
Fifteen minutes.
3. Who participates most, according to the professor?
Not the students with perfect English, but the ones who come prepared, with a question.
Listening 2 · The Student

My Year Abroad

A Brazilian lawyer describes his research year at a university in Europe. Sound first — no text.

1. Where did he do the international part of his doctorate?
At a university in Madrid.
2. What was difficult for him in the first month?
He understood the texts, but not the fast discussions.
3. What tip did his supervisor give him?
To read the abstract of each article twice before every class and write one question.
