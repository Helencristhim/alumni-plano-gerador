The Doctorate, in English -- Defining Ideas

A doctoral seminar in regulatory law, in English. Key words: a doctorate, a thesis, a supervisor, a scholar, a case study, a literature review, a clause, a precedent, liability, an abstract. Structure: defining and non-defining relative clauses.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A doctorate -- the highest university degree, based on original research
"I want to do a doctorate in regulatory law."
A thesis -- a long piece of original research written for a university degree
"His thesis is about highway concessions."
A supervisor -- the professor who guides a student's research
"My supervisor asked me to read ten articles."
A scholar -- a person who studies a subject deeply, usually at a university
"She is a scholar who writes about infrastructure law."
A case study -- a detailed study of one real example
"The article uses a case study of a port in Chile."
A literature review -- a summary of what other researchers have already written on a topic
"The first chapter is a literature review."
A clause -- a section of a contract or a law
"The contract has a clause about force majeure."
A precedent -- an earlier decision that is used as an example for later cases
"The ruling created an important precedent."
Liability -- legal responsibility, especially for paying for damage or loss
"The concessionaire has liability for accidents on the road."
An abstract -- a short summary at the beginning of an academic article
"I always read the abstract before the whole article."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Aguinaldo wants to do a doctorate in regulatory law. His supervisor, who teaches in Brazil and in Madrid, asked him to read an article which studies liability in failed concessions.

The article starts with an abstract, which is only one paragraph long. It uses a case study of Chile, where the courts follow a clear precedent. The author is a scholar whose work is well known in Europe.

Notice the two kinds of sentences. "An article which studies liability" gives essential information, with no commas. "His supervisor, who teaches in Madrid," adds extra information, between commas.

1. Which word is used for a person in the text?
A which
B who
C where
2. In "Chile, where the courts follow a clear precedent", why is where used?
A Because Chile is a place.
B Because Chile is a person.
C Because the sentence is a question.
3. Which sentence gives extra information, between commas?
A "He read an article which studies liability."
B "His supervisor, who teaches in Madrid, sent the article."
C "The author is a scholar whose work is famous."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Relative Clauses: Who, Which, Where, Whose

GRAMMAR

Connect extra or essential information to a noun. Commas, and never that, when the information is extra.

JobFormExample
A personwho (or that, in essential information)the professor who guides your research
A thing or an ideawhich (or that, in essential information)an agency which controls a sector
A placewherethe country where the study was done
Possessionwhosethe author whose thesis won the prize
Essential information (defining)no commas; that is possibleA clause that limits liability is common.
Extra information (non-defining)commas; never thatThe author, who teaches in Rome, studied thirty contracts.
The trapnever what, and never repeat the pronounNot the contract what I read it. Say the contract which I read.

Never: a contract what lets a company run a service · the professor which guides my research · The author, that teaches in Rome, ... · the article which I read it

Stage 2: Put the Research Presentation in Order

Order

It is your turn in the doctoral seminar. Put your short presentation in a logical order.

?"Thank you. My name is Aguinaldo, and I am a lawyer from Brazil."
?"My research is about liability in concessions which end early."
?"The main problem is that the state, the company and the lenders disagree about who pays."
?"I will use a case study of Chile, where the framework is older."
?"My question is whether stable regulation matters more than detailed contracts."
?"I would be happy to hear your questions and suggestions."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A colleague asks what a concession is. You say:
A "It is a contract what lets a company run a public service."
B "It is a contract which lets a company run a public service."
C "It is a contract who lets a company run a public service."
You want to add extra information about the author. You say:
A "The author, who teaches in Rome, studied thirty contracts."
B "The author, that teaches in Rome, studied thirty contracts."
C "The author who teaches in Rome, studied thirty contracts."
You want to talk about a place. You say:
A "Chile, which the case study was done, is interesting."
B "Chile, where the case study was done, is interesting."
C "Chile, who the case study was done, is interesting."
You are looking for a professor. The best sentence is:
A "I am looking for a supervisor whose work is about regulation."
B "I am looking for a supervisor which his work is about regulation."
C "I am looking for a supervisor that his work is about regulation."
