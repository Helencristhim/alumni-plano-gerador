Lesson 15 -- Pre-class

After the Talk -- Keeping the Contact Alive

The week after the congress: the follow-up message. Key words: to reach out, to stay in touch, to get back to, availability, to forward, to attach, reminder, proposal. Structure: first conditional -- if + present, will / can + verb.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. Half of these words are about people, and half are about files and dates.

To reach out -- to contact somebody first, especially after some time
"I am sorry it took me a week to reach out after the congress."
To stay in touch -- to keep talking or writing to somebody over time
"Let's stay in touch: here is my card with my email."
To get back to -- to answer somebody later, after you check something
"I don't know the dates yet, but I'll get back to you tomorrow."
Availability -- the days and hours when you are free for a meeting
"Could you send me your availability for next week?"
To forward -- to send an email or a file you received to another person
"If you like the article, you can forward it to your team."
To attach -- to put a file together with an email before you send it
"Don't forget to attach the file before you send the email."
Reminder -- a short message that helps somebody remember something
"If she doesn't answer in ten days, send a friendly reminder."
Proposal -- a written plan that asks other people to accept or support an idea
"We are writing a proposal for a joint study with a team in Kazan."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Svetlana works in a pain lab in Kazan. One week after the congress she writes to Dejair. "Dear Dejair, I am sorry it took me a week to reach out. If you send me your new paper, I will forward it to my team. We are writing a proposal for a joint study, and we need a partner in Brazil. If you are interested, we can have a short video call in November. If the first week does not work for you, I will find another date. And if I do not hear from you, I will send you a friendly reminder. I really hope we can stay in touch."
Svetlana says "If you send me your new paper", not "If you will send me". Why?
A Because the paper is not finished yet, and Svetlana does not want to wait for a new version of it
B Because after if we use the present simple, even for the future
C Because it is a question
In "If you are interested, we can have a short video call", what does "can" show?
A An offer or a possibility
B Something that happened in the past, before the congress
C An obligation: Dejair must call her

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- If + Present, Will or Can + Verb

GRAMMAR

The first conditional talks about a real situation in the future that is likely to happen. It has two halves: the condition (if + present simple) and the result or promise (will / can + base verb).

if + present, will + verba promise or a resultIf you send me your email, I'll forward you the paper.
if + present, can + verban offer or a possibilityIf you're interested, we can set up a video call.
negativedon't / doesn't after ifIf I don't hear from you, I'll send a reminder.
questionwill in the result halfWhat will you do if nobody answers?
zero conditionalalways true: present + presentIf I don't understand a question, I ask the speaker to repeat it.

Never will after if: "if you send", not "if you will send". The if half can come first (with a comma) or second (no comma).

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Sixty seconds, as a voice message. It is the day after the congress. Leave a message for a researcher you met: say where you met and what you talked about, make one concrete offer with an if-sentence ("If you send me..., I will..."), give your availability for a call, and close with "I hope we can stay in touch." No script -- just speak.
