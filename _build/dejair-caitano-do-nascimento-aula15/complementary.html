Lesson 15 -- After the Talk

Conference Talk
"Are you a giver or a taker?" -- Adam Grant, TED

An organizational psychologist explains why the people who help others first -- who send the paper, forward the contact, make the offer -- are often the most successful in the long run. It is the idea behind every good follow-up message.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Write down two sentences you can turn into an offer for your own contacts: "If you need..., I can...".

Podcast
"Why do we choose to text instead of talk?" -- 6 Minute English, BBC Learning English

Two presenters discuss why many people prefer a message to a phone call, and what we lose and gain with each. Six minutes, clear British accents, with a transcript and a vocabulary list on the page.

Tip: listen once without the transcript. Then decide: for your contacts after Bangkok, will you send a message or ask for a call? Say why, with an if-sentence.

Video
"What makes a good life? Lessons from the longest study on happiness" -- Robert Waldinger, TED

The director of a 75-year study at Harvard explains its main finding: good relationships keep us happier and healthier. A researcher presenting a long study to a general audience -- and an argument for staying in touch.

Tip: listen for how he presents the study -- the question, the method, the finding. Then say the main finding in one sentence with "if": "If you..., you will...".
