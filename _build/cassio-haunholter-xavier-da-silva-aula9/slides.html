Lesson 9 · The Skill Nobody Teaches

I Did Not Catch That

You said it in the consultation: sometimes you simply do not understand the person on the other side. This lesson is about the ten seconds after that happens, and about the sentence that makes sure you both mean the same thing.

Chapter 1: First Words

Finnish, Swedish, Texan

Half your calls are with people whose first language is not English either, and the other half are with Americans who speak at full speed. Nobody in that room is going to slow down for you unless you ask, and asking well is a skill with a grammar of its own.

Which accent costs you the most on a call, and what do you do right now when you lose a sentence?

Today’s Goal

Three Missions

1

Ten words for what goes wrong on a call, and what to say about it.

2

Ask for a repeat in a way that does not stop the meeting.

3

Repeat back what somebody said, correctly, so that a decision is safe.

The target in one line: sorry? gets you the same sentence at the same speed; so you said the valves would arrive in June, is that right? gets you a yes or a correction, and protects the decision either way. The second one needs reported speech.

One Question

What Do You Do in the Ten Seconds?

Two sentences, out loud, right now.

Think of the last time you lost a sentence on a call. Say what you actually did. Then say what you wish you had said. Do not worry about the grammar yet.

Chapter 3: The Code

So You Said That...

Repeating back, correctly

Grammar Discovery

How Do You Say It Back?

"He said the valves would arrive in June." (he said: they will arrive in June)

"She told me the drawings had been issued on Friday." (she said: they were issued on Friday)

"He asked whether we could hold the date." (he asked: can you hold the date?)

"She asked when the review was." (she asked: when is the review?)

On the left is what you say back. On the right, in red, is what the person actually said. Compare the two verbs in each pair, and then look at the word order in the last two.

JobFormExample
The tense steps back onewill → would, is → was, have → hadHe said the valves would arrive in June.
Past becomes past perfectwas issued → had been issuedShe told me the drawings had been issued.
Said takes no person, told takes onesay something / tell somebody somethingHe said that... / He told me that...
A yes-or-no question reportedask + whether / ifHe asked whether we could hold the date.
A wh- question reportedask + question word + statement orderShe asked when the review was.
The trapno inversion and no do in a reported questionNot he asked when is the review. Say he asked when the review was.

In one line: step the tense back one, drop the inversion, and use whether for a yes-or-no.

Listening 1 · The Interpreter

Why Repeating Back Is Not Rude

A conference interpreter explains the habit she wishes engineers had. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say people are afraid of when they repeat something back?
That it makes them look slow or as if they were not listening.
2. How long does she say a check-back takes?
About four seconds.
3. What does she say happens when nobody checks back?
Two people leave the call with different numbers, and neither of them knows it.
Listening 2 · The Finnish Engineer

We Are Not Offended

A Finnish engineer on what it is like to be the accent somebody else struggles with. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say happens when somebody pretends to understand her?
She finds out later, usually when something has been built or ordered wrongly.
2. What does she say she does when she does not understand a Brazilian or an Indian colleague?
She asks, and says she asks three or four times a week.
3. What does she ask people to do instead of saying pardon?
Say which part they missed, because repeating the whole sentence at the same speed helps nobody.
Listening 3 · The Facilitator

Fifteen People and One Microphone

A meeting facilitator on the mechanics of a large international call. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say is the real problem with a fifteen-person call?
That the people with the most to say are usually the quietest, and the fastest speakers fill the space.
2. What does he do at the start of a call to fix it?
He names who will speak on each topic, so that people are not competing for the gap.
3. What does he say about jargon on international calls?
That it is the fastest way to lose half the room, and the speaker never notices because nobody says anything.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I step the tense back one when I report what somebody said.
I use whether or if for a yes-or-no question, and no inversion for a wh- question.
I say which part I missed, instead of only saying sorry.
I close a call with a recap that anybody can correct.
I know the words: to break up, to talk over someone, to rephrase, to paraphrase, to spell out, a recap, to chime in, to be on mute, jargon.
