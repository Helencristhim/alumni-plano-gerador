Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

I Did Not Catch That -- Accents, Clarification and Checking Back

The ten seconds after you lose a sentence on a call. Key words: to break up, to talk over someone, to rephrase, to paraphrase, to spell out, to bear with someone, a recap, to chime in, to be on mute, jargon. Structure: reported speech for confirming what was said.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To break up -- when a voice on a call becomes broken and hard to hear
"Sorry, you are breaking up. Could you repeat the last part?"
To talk over someone -- to start speaking while another person is still speaking
"On a call with fifteen people, somebody talks over you every minute."
To rephrase -- to say the same idea again, in different words
"Let me rephrase that: the order has not been placed yet."
To paraphrase -- to say back, in your own words, what somebody else said
"If I can paraphrase, you want the drawings before the order."
To spell out -- to say a word letter by letter
"Could you spell out the supplier’s name for me?"
To bear with someone -- to ask somebody to be patient for a moment
"Bear with me, I am opening the drawing now."
A recap -- a short repetition of the main points, usually at the end
"Let me do a quick recap before we close."
To chime in -- to add a short comment to a conversation already happening
"Feel free to chime in if the piping side has a view."
To be on mute -- to have your microphone turned off so nobody hears you
"Juha, I think you are on mute."
Jargon -- specialist words that people outside the field do not understand
"Too much jargon on that call, and half the room stopped following."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The line breaks up while Juha is giving the lead time. Cassio does not say pardon. He says which part he missed: “I got the fourteen weeks, but not what came after.” Juha repeats only that part, and slows down on the numbers.

Then Cassio checks. “So you said the valves would arrive in the second half of June, is that right?” Juha confirms. Later Kelly asks when the equipment base drawings would be issued, and Cassio tells her he will confirm by Thursday.

Before closing, Cassio does a quick recap and invites the others to chime in if anything is wrong. Nobody corrects him, which is the point: everything important was said twice, once by the person who knew it and once by the person who wrote it down.

1. Why does Cassio say which part he missed, instead of just saying sorry?
A Because it is more polite in English.
B Because otherwise the whole sentence is repeated at the same speed and he loses it again.
C Because the line was not really breaking up.
2. Why does the text say “he said the valves would arrive” and not “will arrive”?
A Because the valves are not coming any more.
B Because it reports what somebody said, so the tense steps back one.
C Because the arrival is hypothetical.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “She asked when is the design review.”
B “She asked when the design review was.”
C “She asked when did the design review be.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

Step the tense back one, drop the inversion in questions, and use whether for a yes-or-no. Say takes no person; tell takes one.

JobFormExample
The tense steps back onewill → would, is → was, have → hadHe said the valves would arrive in June.
Past becomes past perfectwas issued → had been issuedShe told me the drawings had been issued.
Said takes no person, told takes onesay something / tell somebody somethingHe said that... / He told me that...
A yes-or-no question reportedask + whether / ifHe asked whether we could hold the date.
A wh- question reportedask + question word + statement orderShe asked when the review was.
The trapno inversion and no do in a reported questionNot he asked when is the review. Say he asked when the review was.

Never: He said the valves will arrive · She asked when is the review · He asked that we could hold the date · She said me that...

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

Juha said: the valves will arrive in June. You report it as:
A "He said the valves will arrive in June."
B "He said the valves would arrive in June."
C "He said that the valves arrive in June."
Kelly asked: when is the review? You report it as:
A "She asked when is the review."
B "She asked when the review was."
C "She asked when did the review be."
You missed only the end of a sentence. You say:
A "Sorry, could you repeat everything?"
B "I got the number, but not what came after it."
C "Sorry, I do not understand your accent."
You want to confirm a number before the call ends. You say:
A "So you said the order would be placed in May, is that right?"
B "So you said the order will be placed in May, is it?"
C "So you said me the order is placed in May?"
