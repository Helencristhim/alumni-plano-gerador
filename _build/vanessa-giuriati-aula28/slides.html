Chapter 3

First Impressions

Saying what you see, not who someone is

Grammar Discovery

Fact, or Impression?

"She comes across as very confident in meetings."

"He strikes me as someone who listens more than he talks."

"At first, she seemed a bit cold."

"My new manager seems like a straightforward person."

FormUseExample
come across as + adjective / nounHow a person looks to other peopleShe comes across as very confident.
strike me as + adjective / nounMY personal impressionHe strikes me as a good listener.
seem + adjectiveA softer impressionAt first, she seemed a bit cold.
seem like + nounThe same, with a nounHe seems like a straightforward person.
⚠ CarefulAlways 'come across as' and 'strike me as' (never 'strike to me'). After 'seem', use an adjective ('seems friendly') or 'like' + a noun ('seems like a friendly person'), never 'seems like friendly'. These verbs describe an IMPRESSION, so they leave space to change your mind.

Comprehension

Did You Catch It?

1. How does the new director come across at first, according to Daniel?
Very serious, and hard to read.
2. What does she do when she doesn't like an idea?
She says so directly, but politely. She is straightforward.
3. Why does Daniel think she is perceptive?
She noticed that two people didn't agree, and she asked them both.
Listening

Impressions Are Not Facts

Listen first. No text. Sound only.

1. What is the problem with the way we describe first impressions?
We describe our impression as if it were a fact.
2. Why does the speaker prefer "comes across as" and "strikes me as"?
They leave a door open: they say what she sees, not who the person really is.
3. What happens when she says "he seems arrogant" instead of "he is arrogant"?
She keeps looking, and very often she finds someone different.
Listening

My Brother, the Opposite

A different voice, a different message. Listen carefully.

1. Where is the brother usually at parties, and why?
In the kitchen, washing glasses, because it gives him something to do.
2. What shows that he is driven?
He started a furniture business at forty with no experience, and today it employs twelve people.
3. What is the speaker's advice at the end?
To know what his brother is like, watch what he does, not what he says.
