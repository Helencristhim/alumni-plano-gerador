Better, Worse, Almost the Same

Today’s Goal

One Choice, Three Moves

1. The Words

a front-runner, a toss-up, polished, raw, hit-or-miss...

2. How Much

a little better, much better, almost as good, by far the best

3. Your Decision

choose between real versions and defend the choice

In a real decision, “Take three is better” is not enough. People want to know how much better.

One Question

Who Was Better, Really?

Think of two singers from a season of The Voice or another show you worked on. Who was better, and how much better?

Then the harder one: did the better singer win?

Chapter 3: The Code

Not Just Better: How Much Better

One word before the comparison changes everything

Grammar Discovery

How Much Better?

Take three is a little warmer than take two.

The chorus is much stronger in take three.

Take one is nowhere near as polished as the others.

Your bridge in take three is by far the best version.

The red words compare. What do the blue words add? Is the difference big or small in each one?

Size of the differenceFormExample
Smallslightly / a little / a bit + comparativeTake three is slightly warmer than take two.
Bigmuch / a lot / far + comparativeThe chorus is much stronger. The vocal is far clearer.
The samejust as + adjective + asThe verse is just as good as in take two.
Almost the samealmost as / not quite as + adjective + asIt is not quite as clean as take two.
Not even closenowhere near as + adjective + asTake one is nowhere near as polished as the others.
The topby far / easily + the + superlative · one of the + superlative + pluralby far the best bridge · one of the strongest voices
The trapnever very or more before a comparativeNot very better, not more better, not more strong: much better, stronger.

In one line: the comparison says which one wins; the word before it says by how much.

Every producer does it. And in the last chorus, I'd love you to take it up a notch. The band is a lot louder than you there.

She thought it was just as good as the second verse in take two.

Listening 1 · The Best Voice, the Best Night

Two Finalists, One Winner

A vocal coach remembers the final of a singing competition. Sound first — no text.

1. How was Marina's voice different from Leo's?
Marina had by far the biggest voice, and her high notes were much stronger than anybody else's. Leo's voice was slightly smaller, and his technique was nowhere near as polished.
2. Why was Leo the safer singer, in the coach's opinion?
He was more consistent. He was a little better every week and never had a bad night, while Marina was hit-or-miss.
3. What happened in the final, and what is the situation today?
They were neck and neck until the last song. Leo sang a much simpler, quiet ballad and won. Marina was the runner-up, but today she has a bigger career than he does.
Listening 2 · The Volume Knob

Louder Is Not Better

A mixing engineer and the cheapest trick in the studio. Sound first — no text.

1. What do people say when they hear the same mix twice and the second time is a little louder?
Almost everybody says the second one is better: fuller, warmer and more exciting. It is only louder.
2. What did he do with the two mixes from the label, and what did he discover?
He turned mix B down by two decibels, so both were exactly as loud as each other. Then mix A was clearly stronger: the vocal was far clearer and the drums had a lot more space.
3. What is his rule for young engineers?
Before you say better or worse, make them the same volume. Otherwise you are comparing the volume knob, not the music.
