Chapter 4: The Turn That Keeps Going

Time, Without a Date

One form, three jobs

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"We have expanded into three markets so far."

"I have never worked overseas full time."

"We have just landed our first client in Asia."

"The company has built up a steady base since 2014."

The red verb always has two parts, and the first one is always the same. The blue words carry the time — and not one of them is a date.

JobFormExample
A period that is still openhave / has + past participleWe have expanded into three markets so far.
Life experience, no datehave / has (never) + participleI have never worked overseas.
It happened, and it matters nowhave / has just + participleWe have just landed the account.
Not yet, up to nowhave / has not + participleWe have not solved that yet.
How long, counting from a startfor + a length · since + a starting pointfor nine years · since 2014
The words that take haveever, never, just, already, yet, so far, this yearall of them open the period instead of closing it
The trapnever the simple present, never a closed datenot I work here since 2015 · not I have landed it in March

In one line: if the clock is still running, use have. The moment you name the year, the sentence goes to the past.

The Same Answer, Three Sizes

Seven Words, Fifteen, Thirty

7 words — true, and the conversation stops

"We sell tax software to large companies."

15 words — one present perfect, and it breathes

"We sell tax software to large companies, and we have expanded into three markets so far."

30 words — a fact, a period, a reason, a question back

"We sell tax software to large companies. We have expanded into three markets so far, mostly through word of mouth, because the track record does not cross the border. Where are you selling now?"

Now do the same three sizes about your own company, out loud, without writing anything down.

Listening 1 · A Sales Leader

Arriving With Nothing

A sales leader on what actually makes international expansion hard. Sound first — no text.

1. What do people expect him to say is the hardest part, and what does he say instead?
They expect him to say the language or the taxes. He says the hardest part is that you arrive with nothing.
2. What does he say his company has at home that does not exist in a new country?
A track record built up over twelve years, and business that comes through word of mouth, so they have never had to introduce themselves.
3. What does he say happens in year three, and why does it matter?
Somebody he helped two years earlier mentions the company in a room he is not in. He says that is the moment it starts.
Listening 2 · A Career

I Have Almost Never Applied

A director describing nine years in one job, at a dinner table. Sound first — no text.

1. How long has she been in the job, and what has she almost never done?
Nine years, and she has almost never applied for anything.
2. What does she say the honest version is, rather than luck?
That she has said yes to a lot of things that were not her job.
3. What was the turning point, and how did it feel at the time?
Saying yes on the phone to flying out and presenting to a client overseas, something she had never done. At the time it did not feel like a turning point; it felt like a bad week.
