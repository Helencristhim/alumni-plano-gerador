Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

What Did They Actually Say? -- Reporting Without Misquoting

Repeating what somebody said to you, in English, without changing it. Key words: a recap, to relay, to paraphrase, a takeaway, to misquote, verbatim, a caveat, to clarify, a stakeholder, to debrief, to water something down, to put words in somebody's mouth. Structure: reported speech and backshift.

10 minutes · 5 quick steps. Listen, tap, and say it out loud.

Stage 1: Words · 2 min

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A recap -- a short summary of what was said, given afterwards to people who need it
"I will send a recap tonight so nobody has to remember it."
To relay -- to pass a message from one person to another without changing it
"I relayed exactly what he said, including the part I did not like."
To paraphrase -- to say what somebody said, in your own words, keeping the meaning
"Let me paraphrase and you tell me if I have got it wrong."
A takeaway -- the one thing people should remember from a meeting
"The takeaway was that the audit report comes before any commitment."
To misquote -- to report somebody's words wrongly, changing what they meant
"I would rather ask twice than misquote him to my own board."
Verbatim -- using exactly the words that were said, with nothing changed
"I wrote his sentence down verbatim while he was still saying it."
A caveat -- a warning that limits how far a yes can be taken
"He said yes, with one caveat, and the caveat is the whole sentence."
To clarify -- to make something clearer when it could be understood in two ways
"Can I clarify one thing before I take this back to my team?"
A stakeholder -- anybody whose decision or opinion affects the project
"The person who was not in the room is usually the stakeholder that matters."
To debrief -- to go through what happened right after a meeting, with your own team
"Give me ten minutes to debrief before anybody writes anything down."
To water something down -- to repeat a message in a weaker form than it was said
"He did not say maybe. Do not water it down when you repeat it."
To put words in somebody's mouth -- to say that somebody said something they did not say
"That is close, and I would put it slightly differently."

Stage 3: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech · 2 min

GRAMMAR

Read the rule, then answer three quick questions.

They saidYou reportExample
presentpastare → were interested
present perfect / pastpast perfecthave reviewed → had reviewed
will / can / maywould / could / mightwill confirm → would confirm
a yes-or-no questionasked if / whether, normal orderasked whether we could send it
a what / when questionkeep the word, drop the inversionasked what the price was
say vs telltell somebody · say somethinghe told me — never he said me
time and place move tootomorrow → the next dayhere → there · this → that

Never: He said me that... · She asked what is the price · He said they will confirm (about a meeting that is over) · They approved it (when what they said was: we are comfortable with it)

1. "He said they were interested." The client actually said "we are interested." Why is it were?
A Because when you report what somebody said, every verb steps back one: are becomes were.
B Because they are no longer interested.
C Because they is plural.
2. "He asked whether we could send the migration plan first." What happened to the original question?
A Nothing changed except the tense.
B It stopped being a question: whether was added, the word order became normal, and can became could.
C The question mark was simply removed.
3. Somebody summarises a meeting wrongly and nobody corrects it. Why does that matter?
A Because the person is usually being dishonest.
B Because correcting people shows authority in a meeting.
C Because the wrong version becomes the record, and everybody downstream works from it.

Stage 4: Use It · 3 min

Practice

Tap the words that complete each sentence, then choose what a real speaker would say.

"He said they ."
"She asked the numbers."
"He said yes, with one , and the caveat is the whole sentence."
The partner said "we are interested". You report it to your team. You say:
A "He said they were interested."
B "He said me they are interested."
C "He told they were interested."
He asked "what is the price?". You report the question. You say:
A "He asked what is the price."
B "He asked what the price was."
C "He asked me what was the price?"
The client said "we are comfortable with the timeline". Your colleague says "so they approved it". You say:
A "Yes, exactly."
B "No, you are wrong, that is not what happened at all."
C "That is close, and I would put it slightly differently. They said they were comfortable with it."

Stage 5: Say It · 2 min

Speaking

Listen and record two sentences, then speak freely for thirty seconds.

He said they were interested, but that they could not commit yet.
Let me read that back to you before I take it to my team.
Debrief your team in thirty seconds after a call with a partner: report what they said and what they asked, keep the caveat, and do not water it down or put words in their mouth. Finish by asking one stakeholder to clarify something.
