Chapter 3: The Code

Two Small Words at the End

The English version of a word you say fifty times a day

Grammar Discovery

Where Does the Tag Come From?

You have been to Salvador, haven't you?

The wine is from the south, isn't it?

You didn't drive tonight, did you?

You live near the park, don't you?

Compare each verb with its tag. Two things happen every time — and in the last sentence, the verb in the tag is not in the sentence at all.

JobFormExample
Same auxiliaryhave → haven't · is → isn't · can → can'tYou have met Paulo, haven't you?
Opposite signpositive → negative tag · negative → positive tagYou don't eat meat, do you?
No auxiliarydo / does / didShe lives in Rome, doesn't she?
Two special onesI am → aren't I · Let's → shall weLet's sit down, shall we?
Voice goes downyou expect agreementLovely evening, isn't it? ↘
Voice goes upyou really do not knowThe dinner is at eight, isn't it? ↗
The listener's tagauxiliary + subject, to show interest“I grew up in Salvador.” “Did you?”

In one line: copy the auxiliary, flip the sign, and let the tune decide whether you are asking or inviting.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Evening, at Full Speed

One guest, two recordings, one seating plan

Dialogue

The Last Guest Arrives

R
Claire, come in. You found the building all right, didn't you? The doorman takes his job very seriously.
C
He does, doesn't he? He asked for my passport. I'm the last one to arrive, aren't I?
R
Not at all, two people are still stuck in traffic. Let me get you a drink. You prefer red, don't you?
C
I do. You remembered. This is the Brazilian wine you told me about in London, isn't it?
R
It is, from the south. It is a bit of an acquired taste, so be honest. And please help yourself to anything on the table.
C
Honestly, it is lovely. Who is the man by the window? He looks like he is about to talk shop.
R
That is Paulo, and he is. I have seated him next to an architect, so she can steer the conversation somewhere else. You are next to me, and I want to hear about Chicago.
C
Only if you tell me about Salvador. You grew up there, didn't you? I have never been north of Rio.
Comprehension

About Claire

1. What happened to Claire at the door, and what is she worried about when she comes in?
The doorman asked for her passport. She thinks she is the last guest to arrive.
2. Where had Claire first heard about the wine, and what does she think of it?
In London, from Rodrigo. She says, honestly, that it is lovely.
3. What does Claire want to hear about, and why?
About Salvador, because she has never been north of Rio. She offers a trade: Chicago for Salvador.
Listening 1 · The Host

The First Twenty Minutes Are Mine

A host describing how she runs a dinner for eight. Sound first — no text.

1. How does she introduce each guest, and what does that achieve?
With one fact that invites a question. The guest has something to say, and the person next to them has something to ask.
2. When does the lull usually come?
Just after the main course arrives.
3. What kind of question does she ask the guest who has been quiet, and why?
A small one with a tag at the end, something the guest can agree with. It sounds like nothing, but it is an open door.
Listening 2 · The Tune

Questions Like a Lawyer

Somebody who moved to London and found out why people thought he was cold. Sound first — no text.

1. What did people think of him in his first year, and who explained why?
They found him slightly cold. A colleague explained it.
2. What does he say a tag does to a question?
It turns a demand into an invitation.
3. What is the difference between the tag that goes down and the tag that goes up?
Down, you are not really asking, you are inviting agreement. Up, you genuinely do not know the answer.

Who Sits Next to Whom

Saturday · 8:00 p.m. · eight guests · host’s notes
HEAD OF THE TABLE
Rodrigo. Claire on his right: first time in Brazil, knows nobody else. Opening: Chicago.
LEFT SIDE
Paulo, litigator, will talk shop. Next to Beatriz, architect, restoring a house in Salvador. Then Tom, Claire’s colleague, who runs marathons.
RIGHT SIDE
Marina, just back from six months in Japan, very quiet at first. Then Ricardo and Julia, old friends, both happy to talk to anyone.
NOTE TO SELF
Keep Paulo and Tom apart: both litigators. Ask Marina about Japan before the main course. Short toast after dessert.
Menu: moqueca, rice, farofa · one vegetarian (Tom) · Cachaça for the brave, after dessert.
1. Why does Claire sit on the host's right?
Because it is her first time in Brazil and she knows nobody else at the table.
2. Why is Beatriz between Paulo and Tom?
Paulo and Tom are both litigators and would talk shop all evening. Beatriz can steer the conversation somewhere else.
3. Which guest will need to be drawn out, and what is the plan?
Marina, who is quiet at first. The host will ask her about Japan before the main course.
