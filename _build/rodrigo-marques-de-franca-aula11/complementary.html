Priya Parker on what a host is actually for: not the food, but the life of the group. Short, practical, and full of the exact situations from this lesson, from the guest who knows nobody to the table that goes quiet.

Tip: the page carries the full transcript. Listen once without reading and note every question tag you hear. Then check the text and count how many you missed.

Talk
Priya Parker -- "3 steps to turn everyday get-togethers into transformative gatherings" (TED)

The same author, on stage: why most dinners are forgettable, and the three decisions a host makes before anybody rings the bell. Useful for anyone who hosts clients or colleagues from abroad.

Tip: pause after each of the three steps and say, in one sentence, how you would apply it to the next dinner you host. Use at least one question tag each time.

Series
Salt Fat Acid Heat (Netflix) -- Episode 4, "Heat"

A cook who treats every meal as an excuse to gather people. The episode ends with a dinner at her home in California, and the conversation around the table is as natural and fast as the one you practiced in class.

Tip: watch the final dinner scene twice. The second time, listen only for how the guests react to each other: Really? Is it? Did you? Those short replies are what keep the table alive.
