Chapter 4: The Code

If This Happens, I Will Do That

Two halves, two different verb forms

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"If the harvest is late, the payments will move with it."

"If I do not catch the question, I will ask him to rephrase it."

"We will miss the target if the rain does not stop."

"If I do not follow up, customers do not pay."

The blue half never carries will, even when it is clearly about the future. The red half does. And the last sentence has no red at all: look at what is green and ask yourself why.

JobFormExample
A real possibility in the futureif + present simple, ... will + base verbIf the harvest is late, the payments will move.
The order can be swappedno comma when the if-half comes secondWe will miss the target if the rain does not stop.
Something that is always trueif + present simple, ... present simpleIf I do not follow up, customers do not pay.
Softer than willmay / might / can, in the second halfIf the price falls, we may have to extend the terms.
An instructionif + present simple, ... imperativeIf he calls again, put him through to me.
When, not ifuse when if it is certain, if if it is only possibleWhen the harvest comes · if the harvest is late
The trapnever will inside the if-halfNot if the client will not pay. Say if the client does not pay.

In one line: the if-half stays in the present, however far in the future it is.

Which Half Takes Will?

Say it first, then click to check

Listening 1 · The Agenda Changed

If the Flight Lands on Time

Megan calls Luiz two days before the visit. Sound first — no text.

1. If the flight lands on time, what time does the visit start?
At nine.
2. If it does not land on time, where do they go, and at what time?
Straight to the credit block, at ten fifteen.
3. What should Luiz do if Tom asks for a number he does not have?
Say so, and follow up the same day. Megan says Tom has told her that twice.
4. If lunch runs long, what will Luiz lose, and what does Megan tell him to print?
He will lose the site tour, not the credit block. She tells him to print nothing.
Listening 2 · The Visiting Director

Stop Me and I Will Say It Again

Tom Baker leaves Luiz a message before Thursday. Sound first — no text.

1. If Luiz does not understand a question, what does Tom ask him to do, and what will Tom do then?
Stop him. Tom will say it again in shorter words.
2. What does Tom say is not a problem, and what is?
Stopping him is not a problem. Guessing is.
3. If they run out of time, which block will Tom stay for, and which will he drop?
He will stay for the credit block and drop the targets block.
4. What will they do if the weather closes the airport?
They will do the whole thing on a call, at the same hour.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

When I miss a question, I ask for it again instead of answering anyway.
I can repeat back what I understood, so that both of us are on the same page.
I never put will inside the if-half of a sentence.
I can answer a hard question with a number and a date instead of an adjective.
I can say that I will confirm something and follow up, without it sounding like an excuse.
