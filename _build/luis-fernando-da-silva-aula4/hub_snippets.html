If They Ask You That

The question you did not catch, and the answer that carries a number and a date. Key words: to clarify, to rephrase, to confirm, headquarters, quarterly, revenue, a target, to follow up, to break down, an update, to be on the same page, to bear with somebody. Structure: first conditional, against the zero conditional for what is always true.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To clarify -- to make something clearer by explaining the part that was not understood
"Could you clarify what you mean by exposure? I want to be sure we are talking about the same thing."
To rephrase -- to say the same thing again using different words
"Sorry, could you rephrase that? The line is not very good."
To confirm -- to check that something is true or agreed, before you commit to it
"Let me confirm the figure and come back to you this afternoon."
Headquarters -- the main office of a company, where the senior decisions are made
"The request has to be approved at headquarters, not here."
Quarterly -- happening once every three months
"The board sees a quarterly report, so April is the first time they see the season."
Revenue -- all the money a business takes in before any cost is subtracted
"Revenue is not the interesting number in this sector. When it arrives is."
A target -- the number a team has agreed to reach by a certain date
"If the harvest is late, we will miss the target by a month, not by a number."
To follow up -- to contact somebody again about something you agreed earlier
"I will follow up in writing this afternoon, so that nothing depends on memory."
To break down -- to separate a number or a situation into its smaller parts
"Let me break that down: eleven accounts are late by a month, four by three months."
An update -- short news about how something has changed since the last time you spoke
"I will send you a short update after the harvest, whether the news is good or not."
To be on the same page -- to understand a situation in the same way as the other person
"Let me repeat that back, just so that we are on the same page."
To bear with somebody -- to ask somebody politely to wait a few seconds for you
"Bear with me for a second while I check the exact figure."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A visitor from headquarters arrives once a quarterly cycle and asks small questions on purpose. If the harvest is late, the payments will move with it. Look at that sentence carefully, because it contains the whole rule. The first half describes a condition that is possible but has not happened. The second half says what follows. And here is the part that catches every Portuguese speaker: the first half stays in the present, even though it is obviously about the future. Never if the harvest will be late.

The same shape carries the plan for the meeting itself. If I do not catch the question, I will ask him to rephrase it. If I am still not sure, I will repeat back what I understood, so that we are on the same page. If a number matters, I will confirm it and follow up in writing. The two halves can also change places, and then the comma disappears: I will send an update if the figure changes.

There is one more version, and it is not about the future at all. If I do not follow up, customers do not pay. Nothing there is a possibility: it is simply how the world works, every season, and so both halves stay in the present. That is the difference to listen for. A possibility takes will in the second half; a permanent truth does not. And when something is certain rather than possible, English changes the word itself: not if the harvest comes, but when the harvest comes. Get those three apart and a hard question stops being dangerous: you can break down the revenue, name the target, and say exactly what you will do if it slips.

1. Why is it "if the harvest is late" and not "if the harvest will be late"?
A Because the harvest is not important enough for the future tense.
B Because the if-half stays in the present, however far in the future the situation is.
C Because "late" is an adjective and adjectives cannot take will.
2. "If I do not follow up, customers do not pay." Why is there no "will" in this one?
A Because it is not a possibility. It describes what always happens, so both halves stay in the present.
B Because the sentence is negative.
C Because "customers" is plural.
3. According to the text, when should you use "when" instead of "if"?
A When the sentence is about work rather than about life.
B When you want to sound more formal.
C When the thing is certain to happen, not merely possible.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- First Conditional

GRAMMAR

The if-half stays in the present, however far in the future it is.

JobFormExample
A real possibility in the futureif + present simple, ... will + base verbIf the harvest is late, the payments will move.
The order can be swappedno comma when the if-half comes secondI will send an update if the figure changes.
Something that is always trueif + present simple, ... present simpleIf I do not follow up, customers do not pay.
Softer than willmay / might / can, in the second halfIf the price falls, we may have to extend the terms.
An instructionif + present simple, ... imperativeIf he calls again, put him through to me.
When, not ifuse when for what is certain, if for what is only possibleWhen the harvest comes · if the harvest is late
The trapnever will inside the if-halfNot if the client will not pay. Say if the client does not pay.

Never: if the client will not pay · if the harvest is late, the payments move with it in April · if the harvest comes in June, we will invoice then · can you repeat again more slowly?

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A visitor asks a question and you only catch half of it. You say:
A "Yes, exactly. That is our situation."
B "Sorry, could you say that again in a different way?"
C "Repeat again please, more slowly."
You want to say what happens if a big customer does not pay in April. You say:
A "If he does not pay in April, we will hold the next delivery."
B "If he will not pay in April, we hold the next delivery."
C "Case he not pay in April, we will hold the next delivery."
The visitor asks whether the region is under control. The answer that earns trust is:
A "Yes, everything is under control, no problem."
B "It is complicated, but we are working very hard on it."
C "Four accounts are late, all four are dealers, and I will know more after the harvest."
You do not have the exact figure in front of you. You say:
A "I think it is around forty, more or less, I am not sure."
B "Bear with me. I will confirm the figure and follow up this afternoon."
C "Sorry, my English is not good enough for this question."
