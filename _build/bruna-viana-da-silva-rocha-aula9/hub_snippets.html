She Said, He Asked -- Passing Along What You Were Told

Half of what you say in English is something somebody else said first. Key words: an announcement, a heads-up, to confirm, to clarify, a misunderstanding, to pass something on, a delay, a gate change, to double-check, apparently. Structure: reported speech — the verb that steps back one, and the difference between say and tell that gives everybody away.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An announcement -- something said out loud to everybody in the room at once
"The announcement was made twice and half the gate still missed it."
A heads-up -- an informal warning, given early, so that nothing surprises you
"She gave me a heads-up that the desk closes at nine."
To confirm -- to say officially that something is true or that it will happen
"Nobody confirmed anything, and that is why we sat there for an hour."
To clarify -- to say it again in a way that removes the doubt
"Let me clarify, because I think two of us heard two different things."
A misunderstanding -- the moment two people were sure they agreed, and did not
"It was not a lie. It was a misunderstanding, and it cost us the connection."
To pass something on -- to give a message to the next person
"Can you pass that on to the others, before somebody books the wrong day?"
A delay -- the amount of time something is late by
"A two-hour delay is annoying. A two-hour delay nobody announces is worse."
A gate change -- when your flight is moved to a different gate after you sat down
"There was a gate change, and it was only on the screen, never on the speaker."
To double-check -- to check a second time, because once was not enough
"I double-checked the time with two different people and got two answers."
Apparently -- the word you put in front of news you got from somebody else
"Apparently the crew is coming in from Denver. That is what I was told."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Half of what you will ever say in English is something somebody else said first. "The room is not ready" comes out of her mouth; five minutes later it comes out of yours as "she said the room was not ready." Two things changed, and you have to do both.

First, the verb steps back one. is becomes was, has becomes had, will becomes would, can becomes could. "The gate has changed" → "she said the gate had changed". "I will send it later" → "she said she would send it later." Nothing about the meaning moves; only the verb does.

Second, and this is the one that gives Brazilians away: say and tell are not interchangeable. Tell needs a person right after it. Say refuses one. "She told me the gate had changed." "She said the gate had changed." Never she said me, and never she told that with nobody in between.

Questions lose their question shape entirely. "Where are you from?" becomes "he asked me where I was from" — no inversion, no question mark, and the subject goes back in front of the verb. A yes-or-no question borrows if: "Have you been here before?" → "she asked me if I had been here before." And an order becomes an infinitive: "Check in early" → "they told me to check in early"; "Do not be late" → "she asked me not to be late."

1. "The gate has changed." How does that come out when you pass it on?
A She said the gate has changed.
B She said the gate had changed.
C She said the gate would have changed.
2. What is the difference between say and tell?
A Tell needs a person right after it; say refuses one.
B Say is for questions and tell is for statements.
C Tell is more formal than say.
3. "Where are you from?" reported. Which one is English?
A He asked me where was I from.
B He asked me where am I from.
C He asked me where I was from.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Said, Told, Asked

GRAMMAR

The verb steps back one. Tell needs a person and say refuses one. And a question stops looking like a question.

JobFormExample
A statementsay (that) + the sentenceShe said the room was not ready.
The same, to a persontell + somebody + (that)She told me the room was not ready.
The step backis → was · has → had · will → would · can → could“The gate has changed” → the gate had changed
A question with a question wordask + somebody + where / what / when + subject + verbHe asked me where I was from.
A yes or no questionask + somebody + ifShe asked me if I had been there before.
An order or a requesttell / ask + somebody + to + bare verbThey told me to check in early.
The negative ordertell / ask + somebody + not to + bare verbShe asked me not to be late.
Other reporting verbsexplain · mention · warn · addHe warned us that the desk closed at nine.

Never: She said me the room was not ready · She told that the gate had changed · He asked me where was I from · She asked me if had I been there · They told me for check in early
