What Somebody Else Just Told You

The announcement, the friend who missed it, the officer who asked you something. Two things happen to a sentence when it passes through you, and today you do both without thinking.

Chapter 1: First Words

What Did I Just Tell You?

One Sentence, Two Moves

1. The Words

2. Two Moves

the verb steps back one — and tell needs a person, while say refuses one

3. Your Relay

the gate, the desk, and the friend who had headphones on

You can already understand an announcement. What costs you the connection is the ten seconds after, when somebody asks what did they say? and you have to answer fast.

One Question

“What Did They Say?”

The last time somebody told you something important in English — an officer, an agent, a receptionist — what did they actually say? Tell me now, the way you would tell a friend.

Then the harder one: what did they ask you, and what did you answer?

Chapter 3: The Code

Two Things Happened On the Way Here

One to the verb, one to the person

Grammar Discovery

Find Both Changes

an hour ago she said: “The room is not ready.”

She said the room was not ready.

an hour ago she said: “The gate has changed.”

She told me the gate had changed.

an hour ago he said: “Where are you from?”

He asked me where I was from.

an hour ago she said: “Do not be late.”

She asked me not to be late.

The red verbs stepped back one form. The blue ones are the reporting verb — and two of them have a person right after them, while one refuses to take one at all.

JobFormExample
A statementsay (that) + the sentenceShe said the room was not ready.
The same, to a persontell + somebody + (that)She told me the room was not ready.
The step backis → was · has → had · will → would · can → could“The gate has changed” → the gate had changed
A question with a question wordask + somebody + where / what / when + subject + verbHe asked me where I was from.
A yes or no questionask + somebody + ifShe asked me if I had been there before.
An order or a requesttell / ask + somebody + to + bare verbThey told me to check in early.
The negative ordertell / ask + somebody + not to + bare verbShe asked me not to be late.
Other reporting verbsexplain · mention · warn · addHe warned us that the desk closed at nine.

In one line: the verb steps back one, tell takes a person and say does not, and a question stops looking like a question.

Comprehension

About Nick

1. Why did Nick miss the announcement?
He had headphones on.
2. What three questions does Nick ask to catch up?
Whether they just said something, how long they have, and whether anybody asked why — and then whether he missed anything else.
3. What does Nick never do, that most people would?
He never asks her to repeat the whole announcement. He asks for one piece at a time.
Listening 1 · The Telephone Game

“Ask Who Told Them.”

What a message loses as it goes through four people. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say survives, and what dies, when a message goes through four people?
The words survive. What dies is the part that tells you what to do with it — and people drop who said it first, every single time.
2. What example does she give of a message that changed without anybody lying?
Somebody said the meeting had been moved, and by the fourth person it had become that the meeting was canceled.
3. What is the one question she says to ask before you walk away?
Ask who told them.
Listening 2 · The Last One in the Chain

“What I Was Told Is Already Old.”

A gate agent on the question you should be asking instead. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does he say he is the wrong person to be angry at?
A gate change is decided somewhere else, by somebody looking at forty-one aircraft. He is told between four and nine minutes before the passengers, and sometimes he finds out from the board like everybody else.
2. Which question does he say not to ask him, and why?
Whether the gate is going to change — because he will only tell you what he was told, and what he was told is already old.
3. What should you ask instead, and why is that the useful one?
How far the new gate would be if it did change. In that airport the answer is sometimes four minutes and sometimes twenty-six.
Real Document

Four Messages, One Lock Screen

4:07
Thursday · 4 new messages
AIRLINE · 3:41 PM
“Your gate has changed to C19. Boarding begins at 4:15.”
HOTEL · 3:52 PM
“We are holding your room until midnight. Will you arrive after that?”
NICK · 4:01 PM
“Do not check a bag today. Two people at B4 just got told theirs would not make it.”
AIRLINE · 4:06 PM
“Correction: boarding for flight 1142 will begin at 4:40. We apologize for the confusion.”
Two of these four cannot both be acted on. Nobody here is lying, and nobody here is going to call you to sort it out.
1. Which two messages contradict each other, and how?
The two from the airline. The first said boarding would begin at 4:15 and the second said it would begin at 4:40.
2. What did the hotel ask, and what does it need from you?
It asked whether you would arrive after midnight — it is holding the room until then and it needs an answer.
3. What did Nick warn you about, and where did his information come from?
He told you not to check a bag, because two people at B4 had been told theirs would not make it. He did not see it himself — he heard it there.

Role-Play 1 of 3 — Guided

Tell Him What He Missed

Scenario: Nick had headphones on and missed the whole announcement. Give it back to him piece by piece: the gate, the time, the reason, and the thing she asked the people with connections to do. Do not quote anybody.

Who Told You That?

Scenario: You have just landed after a trip where everything was said twice and nothing was confirmed. Tell me the whole story — what the airline said, what the hotel asked, what a stranger warned you about, and which one of them turned out to be right.
