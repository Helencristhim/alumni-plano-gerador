Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

When the Schedule Slips -- Delay, Cause and Recovery

A date stops being true and the chain behind it matters more than the number of days. Key words: a delay, to slip, a bottleneck, a contingency, a mitigation, a root cause, a knock-on effect, to expedite, the critical path, a lead time. Structure: first and second conditional.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A delay -- a period of time when something happens later than planned
"The delay on the soil report is now three weeks."
To slip -- to move later than the planned date, usually gradually
"The design freeze has slipped from March to April."
A bottleneck -- the one point where work builds up and everything else has to wait
"The bottleneck is the single reviewer for all civil drawings."
A contingency -- extra time or money kept in reserve for problems
"We built two weeks of contingency into the steel package."
A mitigation -- an action taken to reduce the effect of a problem
"The mitigation is to release the drawings in two parts."
A root cause -- the real reason behind a problem, not the visible symptom
"The root cause was a survey that nobody had ordered."
A knock-on effect -- a consequence that hits something else further down the line
"The knock-on effect is on the equipment delivery, not on design."
To expedite -- to make something happen faster than the normal process
"We can expedite the order, but it costs about nine per cent."
The critical path -- the chain of tasks that decides the end date of the project
"If it is not on the critical path, the two weeks cost us nothing."
A lead time -- the time between ordering something and receiving it
"The lead time on that valve is fourteen weeks."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The foundation design has slipped by eleven days. The root cause was a site survey ordered after the kick-off instead of before it, and the bottleneck after that was a single reviewer for all the civil drawings.

The task is on the critical path, so the knock-on effect reaches the steel order, which has a fourteen-week lead time. If nothing changes, the order will move from May to June.

The mitigation is to release the drawings in two parts. If the team does that, the original order date will hold, and the contingency in the package covers the rest. The planner adds one sentence for the next project: if the survey were always ordered before the kick-off, this would not happen again.

1. Why does the delay reach the steel order?
A Because the steel team works slowly.
B Because the foundation design is on the critical path and the steel order has a long lead time.
C Because the contingency was already used.
2. What does “if the survey were always ordered before the kick-off” tell you?
A It is what happens on every project today.
B It is not what happens today, so the sentence is hypothetical.
C It will definitely happen next month.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “If the report will arrive next week, we will hold the date.”
B “If the report arrives next week, we will hold the date.”
C “If the report would arrive next week, we will hold the date.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- First and Second Conditional

GRAMMAR

Present plus will for what can still happen. Past plus would for the version you do not have. Never will or would after if.

JobFormExample
A real possibilityif + present simple, ... will + verbIf the report arrives next week, we will hold the date.
A real possibility, with a modalif + present simple, ... can / may / might + verbIf we expedite the valves, we might recover a week.
Something unlikely or impossible nowif + past simple, ... would + verbIf we had two more weeks, I would resequence the steel.
The verb be in the second conditionalif + were, for every personIf the reviewer were not the bottleneck...
Order does not matterthe if half can come first or secondWe will hold the date if the report arrives.
The trapnever will or would inside the if halfNot if it will arrive, not if I would have. Say if it arrives, if I had.

Never: if it will arrive · if I would have two weeks · if we would expedite · if the reviewer was not the bottleneck, this will not be a problem

Stage 2: Put the Delay Notification in Order

Order

You are telling a project director about a delay. Put the notification in a logical order.

?"The foundation design has slipped by eleven days."
?"It is on the critical path, so I want to be clear about what it hits."
?"The knock-on effect is on the steel order, which has a fourteen-week lead time."
?"The root cause was a survey that was ordered after the kick-off."
?"If we release the drawings in two parts, we will hold the original order date."
?"And for the next project: the survey gets ordered before the kick-off."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The report may still arrive next week. You say:
A "If the report will arrive next week, we hold the date."
B "If the report arrives next week, we will hold the date."
C "If the report would arrive next week, we hold the date."
You do not have two more weeks, but you want to say what you would do. You say:
A "If I would have two more weeks, I would resequence the steel."
B "If I had two more weeks, I would resequence the steel."
C "If I have two more weeks, I resequenced the steel."
A director asks what happens if nothing changes. You say:
A "If nothing changes, the order will move to June."
B "If nothing will change, the order moves to June."
C "If nothing changed, the order will move to June."
You want to say the delay does not affect the end date. You say:
A "It is not on the critical path, so the end date does not move."
B "It is not on the bottleneck, so the end date does not move."
C "It is not on the lead time, so the end date does not move."
