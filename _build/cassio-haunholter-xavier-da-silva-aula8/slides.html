Lesson 8 · The Conversation Nobody Wants

When the Schedule Slips

Every project has the week when a date stops being true. What separates a bad delay from a disaster is not the number of days. It is how early and how precisely somebody describes what happens next.

Chapter 1: First Words

Eleven Days, and Then What?

Last week you collected what the client really needed. This week something has moved, and the question on the call is not whose fault it is. It is what happens to everything downstream, and what you would do if you could change one decision.

Think of a project that slipped. What was the first thing that moved, and what moved because of it?

One Question

Real, or Hypothetical?

Two sentences, out loud, right now.

Take a delay you are living with. Say one thing that will happen if the current plan holds. Then say one thing you would do if the constraint disappeared tomorrow. Notice how differently the two feel.

Chapter 4: The Code

If It Happens, If It Happened

Two conditionals for risk and for regret

Grammar Discovery

Which of These Can Still Happen?

"If the soil report arrives next week, we will still hold the date."

"If we expedite the valves, the order will cost nine per cent more."

"If we had two more weeks, I would resequence the steel."

"If the reviewer were not the bottleneck, this would not be a problem."

Two of these describe something that can still happen this month. Two describe a world you do not have. Look at the verb right after if: is it the present, or the past?

JobFormExample
A real possibilityif + present simple, ... will + verbIf the report arrives next week, we will hold the date.
A real possibility, with a modalif + present simple, ... can / may / might + verbIf we expedite the valves, we might recover a week.
Something unlikely or impossible nowif + past simple, ... would + verbIf we had two more weeks, I would resequence the steel.
The verb be in the second conditionalif + were, for every personIf the reviewer were not the bottleneck...
Order does not matterthe if half can come first or secondWe will hold the date if the report arrives.
The trapnever will or would inside the if halfNot if it will arrive, not if I would have. Say if it arrives, if I had.

In one line: present plus will for what can happen; past plus would for what you cannot have.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

The Notification, and Two Voices

One document, two recordings

Real Document

Four Lines and Nothing Else

DELAY NOTIFICATION
Industrial Block 2 · foundation design · issued to client and project director
What movedFoundation design — 11 days, from May 2 to May 13
On the critical path?Yes — feeds the steel connection order
Knock-on effectSteel order (14-week lead time) would move from May to June
Root causeSite survey ordered after kick-off instead of before it
MitigationFoundation drawings released in two parts — order date held
Contingency used9 of the 14 days held in the package
Rule for next timeSurvey ordered before kick-off on every industrial project
This notification is a chain, not an apology. Each line answers one question, and the last line is the one most notifications leave out: what would have prevented it, written as a rule somebody can apply next time.
1. Why does the notification say whether the task is on the critical path?
Because that is what decides whether the eleven days cost the project anything at all.
2. Turn the Mitigation line into a first conditional.
If we release the foundation drawings in two parts, we will hold the original order date.
3. Turn the Root cause line into a second conditional.
If the survey had been ordered before the kick-off, we would not be having this conversation.
Listening 1 · The Planner

The Delay That Did Not Matter

A planner explains why two delays on the same project had completely different weight. Sound first — no text.

1. How long did the architectural package slip, and why did it not matter?
Three weeks, but it had five weeks of float and fed nothing urgent.
2. Why did four days matter more than three weeks?
Because the four days were on the critical path, feeding an order with a fourteen-week lead time.
3. What single question does he ask before any discussion of a delay?
Whether the task is on the critical path, yes or no.
Listening 2 · The Project Director

The Call Where You Have No Solution Yet

A project director on telling a client about a delay before you have fixed it. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say people wait before calling the client?
Because they want to arrive with a solution already prepared.
2. What three things does she say on the early call?
What has moved and by how much, what it hits, and that the recovery plan will come on a named day.
3. Which of the client questions does she say to prepare for?
What would you do if it were your money.
