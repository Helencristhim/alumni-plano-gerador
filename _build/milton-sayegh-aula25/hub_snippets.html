Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Tariff -- a tax that a country puts on products that come from other countries
"The new tariff makes our rings more expensive in the US."
Exchange rate -- the value of one currency when you change it for another
"A good exchange rate can make a whole year better."
Inflation -- the general rise in prices over time
"Inflation is slowing, but prices are still high."
Recession -- a period when the economy gets smaller and many businesses sell less
"In the 2015 recession, many jewelers closed their stores."
Demand -- how much of a product people want to buy
"Demand for luxury is still growing in the US."
Commodity -- a raw material, like gold or coffee, that is bought and sold in large amounts
"Gold is the commodity that decides our costs."
Customs -- the office at a border or airport that checks goods and collects taxes
"The shipment is still at customs in Miami."
Purchasing power -- how much people can buy with the money they have
"Rich buyers did not lose purchasing power this year."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Every October, Milton prepares his biggest shipment of the year to the United States. This year the picture is complicated. Although demand for fine jewelry is strong, a new tariff will make his rings fifteen percent more expensive for American clients. His costs are also higher because of the record price of gold.

Rebecca, the economist at his bank, has one piece of good news. Despite the tariff, the strong dollar means more reais for every sale. Whereas many middle-class families lost purchasing power, his buyers did not. Her advice is to ship early, because customs in Miami is slow in November due to the volume.

1. "Although demand is strong, the new tariff will make his rings more expensive." Is demand a problem for Milton?
A Yes, demand is the main problem, because American clients are buying fewer rings this year.
B No. Demand is good; the tariff is the problem.
C We don't know.
2. Why are Milton's costs higher?
A Because of the record price of gold.
B Because of the new rules at customs in Miami, which make every shipment much slower and more expensive.
C Because of the strong dollar.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A Despite of the tariff, the strong dollar still helps him with every sale to the United States.
B Despite the tariff is high, the dollar helps him.
C Despite the tariff, the strong dollar helps him.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Complex Sentences: Contrast and Cause

GRAMMAR

Look at what comes after the linking word: a full sentence (subject + verb) or only a noun?

FormRuleExample
although / even though+ subject + verb · two opposite ideasAlthough demand is strong, the tariff will cut our margin.
despite+ noun (or verb + -ing)Despite the slowdown, luxury is still growing.
whereas+ subject + verb · compare two thingsWhereas the US is growing, Europe is flat.
because+ subject + verb · a reasonWe sold more because the real was weak.
because of / due to+ noun · a reasonThe shipment is late due to customs.
The trapno but after although · no of after despiteAlthough sales are good, ... (not Although..., but...)

Compare with however and therefore: they connect two sentences. Although and despite live inside one sentence.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are talking about the economy with clients and partners. Choose the best thing to say.

A client asks why your prices went up. You say:
A "Our prices went up because of the new tariff and the price of gold."
B "Our prices went up because the new tariff and also because the price of gold is very high this year."
C "Prices up due to tariff is."
Sales are good, but the margin is smaller. You say:
A "Although sales are good, but the margin is smaller than last year in all our markets."
B "Although sales are good, our margin is smaller."
C "Despite sales are good, margin smaller."
You want to compare two markets. You say:
A "The US market is growing, despite the European market, which is flat this year for most jewelers."
B "Because of the US, Europe is flat."
C "Whereas the US market is growing, the European market is flat."
Your partner is worried about a recession. You want to calm him down. You say:
A "Despite of the slowdown, the luxury market is still growing in the United States and in Europe."
B "Despite the slowdown, luxury is still growing."
C "Although the slowdown, luxury grows."
