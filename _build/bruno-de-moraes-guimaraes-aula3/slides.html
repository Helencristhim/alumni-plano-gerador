Chapter 3: The Code

One Letter, One Word

The -s of he and she · don't and doesn't

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I wake up at five thirty."

"I don't practice on Sunday."

"She wakes up at six thirty."

"She doesn't practice on Sunday."

Two sentences are about I. Two are about she. What changes?

WhoYesNo
I · you · we · theyI wake up early.I don't wake up early.
he · she · itShe wakes up early.She doesn't wake up early.
The trapAfter doesn't, the verb loses the -s: "She doesn't wake up", never "doesn't wakes". The -s is already inside doesn't.
Key ideaOne letter for he and she. One little word to say no. That is all the present simple asks of you.

With this you can tell anybody, in any country, what your day looks like.

Dialogue

What Time Do You Wake Up?

A
Bruno, what time do you wake up in Brazil?
B
I wake up at five thirty. School starts at seven.
A
Five thirty? Wow. I wake up at six thirty and I am always tired.
B
Do you have breakfast before school?
A
Yes, with my mother. My brother doesn't. He only drinks water.
B
And after school? Do you practice every day?
A
Three days a week. I don't practice on the weekend. And you?
B
I don't practice with a team now. I play with my friends and I study English.
Comprehension

About Ashley

4. Does she practice on the weekend?
No, she doesn't.
