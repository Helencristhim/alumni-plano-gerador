Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A concert -- a show where musicians play live for the public
"The concert started at nine and finished at midnight."
A stadium -- a very big place for sports and big shows
"Seventy thousand people were in the stadium."
A world tour -- a series of concerts in many countries
"Their world tour has more than one hundred shows."
The crowd -- all the people together at a show or a game
"The crowd sang every song."
A band -- a group of musicians who play together
"U2 is an Irish band from Dublin."
The lyrics -- the words of a song
"I know all the lyrics of that song."
To sing along -- to sing at the same time as the singer
"Everybody sang along with the last song."
The opening act -- the artist who plays before the main show
"The opening act was a new singer from London."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Have you ever been to a stadium concert? On Coldplay’s world tour, everybody in the crowd gets a light. Many fans have seen the band more than once.

Bram is a sound engineer. He has worked on four world tours and he has been to more than forty countries. He has heard seventy thousand people sing along, and he has never forgotten it.

Lucy has seen Coldplay five times. She knows all the lyrics. She saw them for the first time in 2012. But she has never seen Dua Lipa or her opening act.

1. What does everybody in the crowd get on Coldplay’s tour?
A A free ticket for the next concert of the world tour in the same city.
B A T-shirt.
C A small light.
2. Which artist has Lucy never seen?
A Dua Lipa.
B U2, the Irish band, in a stadium in Dublin or in London.
C Coldplay.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “Lucy has saw Coldplay five times.”
B “Lucy has seen Coldplay five times.”
C “Lucy have seen Coldplay five times in three countries.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect for Experiences -- Ever and Never

GRAMMAR

Use have / has + past participle for experiences in your life when the date is not important. Ask with Have you ever...? Answer with I have / I have never. With a date, use the past simple.

JobFormExample
Experience (no date)have / has + past participleI have seen U2 live.
he / she / ithas + past participleShe has heard that song.
QuestionHave you ever + past participle?Have you ever been to a stadium concert?
Negativehave never / haven’t + past participleI have never seen Dua Lipa.
Past participlessee → seen · be → been · hear → heard · go → gone / beenI have been to London.
With a dateuse the past simpleI saw them in 2017.

Never: I have saw · I has been · I haven’t never seen · I have seen them in 2023
