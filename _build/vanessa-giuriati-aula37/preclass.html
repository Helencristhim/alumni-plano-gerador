Step by Step

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Set up -- To prepare a device, an account or a space so it is ready to use
"It only takes ten minutes to set up the new app."
Log in -- To enter a system or an account with your user name and password
"Once you've logged in, tap Expenses."
Tap -- To touch a screen or a button quickly and lightly
"Tap the blue button at the bottom of the screen."
Hold down -- To press a button and not let go for a few seconds
"Hold down the power button for five seconds."
Walk through -- To show or explain something to someone slowly, step by step
"Can you walk me through the new system?"
Get the hang of -- To learn how to do something well after you practice it for a while
"It's confusing at first, but you'll get the hang of it."
Glitch -- A small problem that stops something from working correctly for a short time
"There's a glitch in the app, so try again later."
Troubleshoot -- To look for the cause of a problem and fix it
"If it doesn't work, let's troubleshoot it together."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Time clauses for instructions

GRAMMAR

Use once / as soon as / until / after + you've + past participle when one step must be finished before the next. Then give the next step with the imperative.

StructureUseExample
once / as soon as + you've + past participleThe first step must be completeOnce you've logged in, tap Expenses.
until + you've + past participleUp to that momentDon't close it until you've seen the message.
after / when + you've + past participleThe order of two stepsAfter you've saved it, send it.
Make sure you + verbAn important stepMake sure you check the total.

Don't use will after once or until: 'Once you've finished', not 'Once you will finish'. No 'to' after make sure you: 'Make sure you save it'.
