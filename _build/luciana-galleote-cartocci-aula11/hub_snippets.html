After Shenzhen -- Explaining a Decision to a Supplier

Back from China: you tell a supplier what you enjoyed, what you are considering and what you decided to do. Key words: to consider, to avoid, to postpone, to afford, to be worth, a pilot, a supplier, a sample, lead time, to look forward to. Structure: verb + -ing or verb + to.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To consider -- to think carefully about something before you decide
"We are considering a second supplier for the cameras."
To avoid -- to stay away from something, so it does not happen
"We always avoid signing a contract on the first day."
To postpone -- to move an event or a decision to a later time
"The board postponed the decision until next month."
To afford -- to have enough money or time to do something
"The hospital cannot afford a long delay."
To be worth -- to be good or useful enough to justify the cost or the effort
"The factory tour was worth the long flight."
A pilot -- a small test of a new system, before you use it everywhere
"We will start with a pilot in one hospital."
A supplier -- a company that sells products or materials to another company
"Wei’s company could be our new camera supplier."
A sample -- one product that a company sends you, so you can test it
"They will send twenty samples next week."
Lead time -- the time between the order and the delivery
"The lead time for these cameras is six weeks."
To look forward to -- to feel happy about something that is going to happen
"I look forward to the next meeting."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The delegation is back from China. Luciana enjoyed visiting the factory in Shenzhen, and her team liked the new cameras. Now Wei, the sales manager, wants a big order for four hospitals.

Luciana is careful. “We are considering ordering from you,” she tells Wei, “but we want to avoid buying a thousand cameras before we test them. So we decided to start with a pilot in one hospital.”

There is one more problem: time. “Our client can’t afford to wait six months,” she says. “Please don’t postpone sending the samples. I look forward to hearing from you.” Some verbs take -ing after them, and others take to + verb.

1. Why does Luciana want to start with a pilot?
A Because she wants to avoid buying a thousand cameras before testing them.
B Because Wei asked for a small order, and the factory cannot produce a thousand cameras this year.
C The text does not say.
2. Why does the text say “decided to start” and not “decided starting”?
A Because the decision is about the past, and the start was also in the past, last month in Shenzhen.
B Because decide takes to + verb after it.
C Because start is a noun here.
3. What can the client not afford?
A To pay for twenty samples and the trip of one engineer from Shenzhen to São Paulo.
B To visit the factory.
C To wait six months.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Verb + -ing or Verb + to

GRAMMAR

When two verbs come together, the first verb chooses the form of the second one: -ing or to + verb. After a preposition (to in look forward to, in, about), always use -ing.

JobFormExample
Likes and dislikesenjoy, like, mind + -ingWe enjoyed visiting the factory.
Thinking about a decisionconsider, suggest, recommend + -ingWe are considering ordering samples.
Stopping or delayingavoid, postpone, finish, stop + -ingWe want to avoid signing too early.
A decision or a plandecide, plan, agree, want, need + toWe decided to start with a pilot.
Money or timecan / can’t afford + toWe can’t afford to wait.
After a prepositionlook forward to, worth + -ingI look forward to working with you.

Never: We are considering to order · We decided starting · I look forward to work with you · We can’t afford losing time
