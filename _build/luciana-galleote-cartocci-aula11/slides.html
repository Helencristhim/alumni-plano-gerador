Chapter 3: The Code

Doing or To Do?

The second verb has two forms

Grammar Discovery

Two Verbs Together

"We enjoyed visiting your factory."

"We are considering ordering twenty samples."

"We decided to start with a pilot."

"Our client can’t afford to wait six months."

Each sentence has a red verb and a blue verb. Two blue verbs end in -ing, and two start with to. What decides the form of the blue verb?

JobFormExample
Likes and dislikesenjoy, like, mind + -ingWe enjoyed visiting the factory.
Thinking about a decisionconsider, suggest, recommend + -ingWe are considering ordering samples.
Stopping or delayingavoid, postpone, finish, stop + -ingWe want to avoid signing too early.
A decision or a plandecide, plan, agree, want, need, hope + toWe decided to start with a pilot.
Money or timecan / can’t afford + toWe can’t afford to wait.
After a prepositionlook forward to, interested in, worth + -ingI look forward to working with you.
The trapto in look forward to is a prepositionNot I look forward to work. Say I look forward to working.

In one line: the first verb chooses the form of the second one; after a preposition, always -ing.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Decision, at Real Speed

One call, three recordings, one proposal

Dialogue

The Call to Shenzhen

W
Luciana, thank you for calling. Did you enjoy visiting our factory in Shenzhen?
L
Very much, Wei. My team really enjoyed seeing the new cameras in your showroom.
W
I am happy to hear that. So, what is the next step? Are you ready to place an order?
L
Not yet. We are considering a pilot in one hospital first. We want to avoid buying a thousand cameras before we test them.
W
That makes sense. We can send twenty samples next week. What lead time do you need for the pilot?
L
About two months. Our client can’t afford to wait longer, so please don’t postpone sending the samples.
W
No problem. And if the pilot goes well, are you planning to order cameras for all four hospitals?
L
That is the plan. I think you can be a great supplier for us. It is worth trying, and I look forward to working together.
Comprehension

About Wei

1. What did Wei want to know first?
If Luciana enjoyed visiting the factory in Shenzhen.
2. What can Wei’s company send, and when?
Twenty samples, next week.
3. What does Wei ask about the future order?
If Teleinfo is planning to order cameras for all four hospitals after a good pilot.
Listening 1 · Tom, the Consultant

Choosing a New Supplier

Tom helps hospital groups choose new suppliers. He gives advice for the weeks after a trade show. Sound first — no text.

1. What do many buyers want to do after a trade show?
They want to order immediately.
2. What should you avoid doing in the first month?
Signing a big contract.
3. Why is a pilot worth paying for, according to Tom?
Because a bad supplier costs much more.
Listening 2 · Anjali

A Message After the Trip

Anjali, who organized the delegation to China, leaves a voice message for Luciana. Sound first — no text.

1. What did several hosts ask Anjali to tell Luciana?
That they enjoyed meeting her team.
2. What does Anjali need Luciana to do this week?
Spend ten minutes answering a short survey about the trip.
3. What are they planning to organize in April?
Another trip, to a trade show in Shanghai.
Listening 3 · Giulia, the Lawyer

Two Changes Before You Sign

Giulia, the contract lawyer, has read the pilot agreement from Wei’s company. Sound first — no text.

1. How many points does Giulia suggest changing?
Two.
2. Why does she recommend removing the sentence about buying the cameras for all four hospitals?
So Teleinfo can keep the option of choosing another supplier if the test fails.
3. What is missing about the samples?
A delivery date, in writing.
