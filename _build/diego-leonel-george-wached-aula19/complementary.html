Talk
Ben Goldacre -- "What doctors don't know about the drugs they prescribe" (TED)

A British doctor explains why many trials are never published, and why that changes what we read in journals. It is full of the words of this lesson: trials, participants, placebo and results.

Tip: every time he describes a group of patients, check if he uses a participle clause, like patients given the drug.

Animation
Emma Bryce -- "The power of the placebo effect" (TED-Ed)

A short animated lesson about why patients receiving a placebo can feel better, and why trials compare a drug with a placebo. Five minutes of clear, simple English.

Tip: pause after each example and explain the two groups in one sentence: patients receiving the drug, and patients receiving a placebo.

Talk
Naomi Oreskes -- "Why we should trust scientists" (TED)

A historian of science explains how scientific evidence is checked by other experts, and when a result is robust enough to trust. It is the thinking behind every journal club.

Tip: write down the words she uses for strong and weak evidence, and use two of them in your next journal club.
