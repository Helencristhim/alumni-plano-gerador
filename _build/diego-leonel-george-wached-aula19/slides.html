Chapter 3: The Code

Patients Treated, Patients Receiving

Participle clauses: how journals make a sentence shorter

Grammar Discovery

What Is Missing?

"Patients treated with the cream had fewer flares."

"A study published in 2025 compared two doses."

"Patients receiving a placebo did not improve."

"Participants using the gel twice a day improved more."

Look at the red words: which two words are missing before them? Now the blue ones: who does the action, the patient or somebody else? And where is the main verb of each sentence?

JobFormExample
Passive meaningnoun + past participle (= who were / which was + participle)patients treated with the cream · a study published in 2025
Active meaningnoun + -ing (= who receive / who used)patients receiving a placebo · participants using the gel
The main verbcomes after the whole clausePatients treated with the cream had fewer flares.
Opening the sentenceput back who were / which waspatients who were treated with the cream
At the start of the sentenceCompared with... / Treated early, ...Compared with a placebo, the cream reduced flares.

In one line: journals remove who were or which was; a past participle after a noun is passive, an -ing word after a noun is active, and the main verb comes later.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Journal Club at Full Speed

One discussion, two recordings, one abstract

Dialogue

The Journal Club

P
Okay, Diego, your article. Read us the key sentence from the abstract.
D
Patients treated with the new cream for twelve weeks had fewer flares than patients receiving a placebo.
P
Good. Now open it for us. Who are the two groups?
D
The patients who were treated with the cream, and the patients who were receiving a placebo. They were randomized at the start.
P
And is the efficacy real, or is the study underpowered?
D
It has three hundred participants enrolled in four centers, so the result looks robust.
P
Any caveat?
D
Yes. Notably, forty participants withdrew, and the authors don’t account for them in the main analysis.
P
Good catch. So, would you prescribe it on Monday?
D
Not yet. I’d wait for a second trial conducted by an independent group.
Comprehension

If Diego would prescribe the cream on Monday.

Real Document

The Abstract

A Topical Cream for Moderate Atopic Dermatitis: A Randomized Trial
peer-reviewed abstract · presented at journal club by Dr. Diego Leonel George Wached

Methods: Adults aged eighteen to sixty-five were randomized to the new cream or to a placebo cream, applied twice a day for twelve weeks.

Results: Patients randomized to the new cream had forty percent fewer flares than patients receiving the placebo. Forty of the three hundred participants withdrew, most of them because of a burning feeling.

Conclusion: The cream showed good efficacy. A longer trial including children is needed.

Abstract written for this lesson. The study and the numbers are fictional.
1. Who were the participants, and for how long were they treated?
Adults aged eighteen to sixty-five, treated for twelve weeks.
2. What was the main result?
Patients who were randomized to the new cream had forty percent fewer flares than patients who received the placebo.
3. What is one caveat?
Forty participants withdrew, and children were not included.
