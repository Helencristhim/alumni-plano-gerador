Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A placebo -- a pill or cream with no active drug, used to compare in a study
"Half of the patients received a placebo."
A participant -- a person who takes part in a study
"The trial had three hundred participants."
To randomize -- to put people into groups by chance, not by choice
"The patients were randomized into two groups."
Efficacy -- how well a treatment works in a study
"The trial showed the efficacy of the new cream."
Topical -- put directly on the skin, not taken by mouth
"We started a topical steroid for the eczema."
Peer-reviewed -- checked by other experts before it was published
"Choose a peer-reviewed article for the journal club."
Robust -- strong and reliable; the result stays the same when you test it again
"With three hundred participants, the result looks robust."
Underpowered -- with too few participants to show a real difference
"With only twenty patients, the study is underpowered."
To withdraw -- to leave a study before the end
"Forty participants withdrew because of side effects."
To account for -- to include something when you calculate or explain a result
"The analysis does not account for the patients who withdrew."
Notably -- in a way that is important to notice
"Notably, the effect was larger in older patients."
A caveat -- a warning that a result has limits and you must be careful with it
"The result is good, with one caveat: the follow-up was short."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Medical journals have a word limit, so authors make their sentences shorter. One way is to remove who were or which was from a relative clause. Patients who were treated with the cream becomes patients treated with the cream. A study which was published in 2025 becomes a study published in 2025. A past participle after a noun has a passive meaning: something happened to the patients.

When the patients do the action, the journal uses an -ing word. Patients who received a placebo becomes patients receiving a placebo. Participants who used the gel becomes participants using the gel. This is the active meaning.

The difficult part is finding the main verb, because it comes after the whole clause: Patients treated with the cream for twelve weeks had fewer flares than patients receiving a placebo. To understand the sentence, open it again: the patients who were treated with the cream had fewer flares than the patients who received a placebo.

1. In "Patients treated with the cream had fewer flares", what is the main verb?
A treated, because it is the first verb after the word patients in the sentence
B had
C with
2. What is the open version of "a study published in 2025"?
A a study which was published in 2025
B a study which published many other articles in the year 2025
C a study publishing in 2025
3. Why do we say "patients receiving a placebo" and not "patients received a placebo"?
A Because a placebo is not a real drug, so the verb must always end in -ing in every journal
B Because receiving is the past tense.
C Because the patients do the action, so the meaning is active.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Participle Clauses

GRAMMAR

Journals remove who were or which was. A past participle after a noun is passive; an -ing word after a noun is active. The main verb comes later.

JobFormExample
Passive meaningnoun + past participlepatients treated with the cream
Active meaningnoun + -ingpatients receiving a placebo
Open itwho were / which was + participlea study which was published in 2025
Open it (active)who + verbparticipants who used the gel

Stage 2: Put the Presentation in Order

Order

You are presenting a study at the journal club. Put the sentences in a clear order: the study, the groups, the result, a detail, the caveat, and your opinion.

?"This study, published last year, looked at a new topical cream."
?"Patients enrolled in the trial were randomized into two groups."
?"Patients receiving the cream improved more than patients receiving a placebo."
?"Notably, the effect was larger in patients treated for more than eight weeks."
?"However, many participants withdrew before the end, so the study may be underpowered."
?"In my opinion, the result is interesting, but we need a larger trial."
