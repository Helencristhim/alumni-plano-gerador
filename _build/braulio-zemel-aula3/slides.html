Chapter 3: The Code

Should Have, Must Have, Might Have

Four ways to judge something that already happened

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The government should have acted six months earlier."

"They must have known what the numbers were."

"It might have been a mistake rather than a decision."

"The whole thing could have been avoided."

All four talk about the past, and all four add something the past simple cannot say. What does each one add: criticism, certainty, doubt, or a lost possibility?

FormWhat it saysExample
should have + participle
should have acted		Criticism, or regret. The right thing was not done, and it is too late now."They should have acted earlier."
"I should not have stayed quiet."
must have + participle
must have known		You are almost certain, because the evidence leaves no other reading."They must have known the numbers."
might / may / could have + participleIt is possible, and you are not claiming more than that."It might have been a mistake."
could have + participle
could have been avoided		It was possible and it did not happen. A door that was open and nobody used."The whole thing could have been avoided."
can’t have + participleThe opposite of must have: you are almost certain it did NOT happen."They can’t have read the report before voting."

Say the fact in the past simple, then judge it with a modal perfect. The vote was on Thursday is information. They should have waited is you, in the conversation.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"They should had acted earlier."
"They should have acted earlier."
"It must be a mistake, because nobody explained it at the time."
"It must have been a mistake, because nobody explained it at the time."
"The whole situation could be avoided if somebody had read the report."
"The whole situation could have been avoided if somebody had read the report."

After a modal the verb never changes form: it is always have, never had and never has. And a modal without have talks about NOW, not about the past — it must be a mistake is about this moment; it must have been a mistake is about a moment that is closed. Read the correct versions aloud twice, fast.

Chapter 4: The Discussion Group

The First Tuesday

The club's news group meets once a month, in English

Dialogue

The News Group at the Club

D
Everybody has weighed in except you. What did you make of the whole thing?
B
I think they should have waited for the second report. The vote was on Thursday and the report came out on Monday.
D
Let me play devil's advocate. If they had waited, would anything have changed?
B
Probably not the result. But the turnout might have been higher, and that matters.
D
That is a fair point. Where did you read about the turnout? Most of the coverage skipped it.
B
In three different outlets, and only one of them made anything of it.
D
So you compare. Most people take the first version and stop there.
B
I take the first twenty-four hours of any story with a grain of salt. Thirty years of reading the news does that to you.
Comprehension

About Diane

1. What does Diane do before Braulio speaks?
She points out that everybody has weighed in except him.
2. What position does she take on purpose, and why?
She plays devil's advocate, to test whether waiting would have changed anything.
3. What does she say about most of the coverage?
That it skipped the turnout.
4. What does she say most people do with a story?
They take the first version and stop there.
Listening

How to Read a Front Page

Ninety seconds from a media podcast. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the speaker say you should ask yourself when a big story breaks?
What the outlet chose to put first, not whether it is telling the truth.
2. How can two newspapers give completely different worlds from the same facts?
By deciding which fact goes at the top and which one goes in the eleventh paragraph.
3. What does she suggest doing on the same morning?
Comparing three front pages and reading the order, not only the words.
Listening 2

Tonight's Bulletin

A short radio news bulletin. Sound first — no text.

1. How long has the ceasefire been holding, and when will both sides meet again?
It is holding for a fourth day, and they will meet again on Friday.
2. What did the Senate vote to do, and how many senators crossed party lines?
To extend the sanctions for another twelve months, with nine senators crossing party lines.
3. What was the turnout in the regional elections?
Sixty-one percent, the highest since 2018.

The Habit

Three Front Pages, One Morning

Tomorrow morning, open three outlets in English on the same story and read only the order: what each one put first, and what each one buried. Then say out loud, in one sentence, what you think somebody should have done.
