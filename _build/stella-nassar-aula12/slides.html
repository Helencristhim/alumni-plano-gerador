Chapter 5: The Last Voices

The Final Conversation

A harder mock interview, an admissions officer and a museum director

Dialogue

Mr. Hale, Harder Questions

H
Last time we talked about a museum guard. Today I want to be a little harder on you. What is something you are not good at?
S
That is a fair question. For a long time I read quickly and confidently and missed what the writer was really implying.
H
And how do you know that about yourself?
S
My Cambridge reading score was lower than everything else. That said, the number mattered less than what it showed me about how I read.
H
Tell me about a time you disagreed with someone whose opinion you respected.
S
In a seminar, someone argued that AI images are art because choosing a prompt is creative. The argument was not unreasonable, but I thought it ignored whose work was inside the images.
H
Did you change his mind?
S
No. What changed was the question we were both asking, which was better than winning.
H
I have interviewed students for nine years, and that is not an answer I hear often. Do you have a question for me?
S
I do. What is something about your college that you only understood years after you graduated?
Comprehension

About Mr. Hale

1. How does Mr. Hale say this interview will be different from the last one?
He says he wants to be a little harder on her.
2. What follow-up question does he ask after Stella names a weakness?
He asks how she knows that about herself.
3. What does Mr. Hale say about Stella's answer on disagreement?
That in nine years of interviewing it is not an answer he hears often.
Listening 1

What We Hope You Remember

An admissions officer closes an information session. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. What does the officer say applicants worry about most, and what does she say matters more?
They worry about being impressive; what matters more is being specific and honest about how they think.
2. What does she advise applicants to do in the weeks before deadlines?
To stop adding activities and start reading their essays aloud to someone who knows them well.
3. What final piece of advice does she give?
To remember that the application is not a verdict on who they are, only a conversation about who they are becoming.
Listening 2

The Museum Rehang

A museum director explains a new arrangement of the collection. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. How was the collection arranged before the rehang?
Chronologically and mostly by nation, with European painting at the center and other regions in side galleries.
2. What principle guides the new arrangement?
Conversations: works from different places and periods that respond to similar questions are shown side by side.
3. What does the director concede about the new approach?
That it is not without its critics, and that some visitors miss the old, simpler story.
