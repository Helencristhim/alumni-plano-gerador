Chapter 4: The Style Code

The Word That Belongs

Collocation and near-synonyms: precision at the level of the phrase

Discover the Style

Why These Words and Not Others?

"First-year students conduct archival research in the college library."

"The department fosters close collaboration between students and curators."

"Years of drawing have helped me cultivate an eye for small details."

"What began as curiosity became a genuine fascination, not yet a devotion."

Replace each colored verb or adjective with a more general one. What does the sentence lose?

FormWhat it doesExample
verb + noun collocationthe verb that academic English expects with that nounconduct research, pursue a question, foster collaboration
adjective + noun collocationthe adjective that sounds native, not translatedclose collaboration, distinctive approach, rigorous training
near-synonym by intensitychoose the degree the evidence can supportinterest → fascination → preoccupation → devotion
mirroring institutional languageuse the terms a department uses to describe itself, preciselyobject-based teaching, seminar-based first year
The C2 trapcopying the brochure. Mirroring a college's language shows research; pasting its slogans shows nothing. Every borrowed term must be tied to something you have done or want to do.

Chapter 5: Voices from Campus

The Match, Out Loud

A counselor, a student guide and a professor

Dialogue

Mr. Brooks and the Brochure Problem

D
Your Why Us draft says the college has a world-class faculty and a vibrant community. Where did you read that?
S
On their website, more or less. It is how they describe themselves.
D
Exactly. So when you send it back to them, what have you told them that they did not already know?
S
Nothing, I suppose. It is their sentence with my name on it.
D
Try this instead. Find one course, one person and one thing you could only do there, and check that each one is real.
S
There is a course on the chemistry of pigments that is taught jointly by an art historian and a chemist.
D
Now that is interdisciplinary in a way you can prove. What does it have to do with you?
S
Since the Vermeer lesson I have wanted to know why that blue was so expensive, and what it is actually made of.
D
When I was a counselor in my first year, I let a student send a supplement that named the wrong professor. Nobody has forgotten it, least of all me.
S
Then every name in mine gets checked twice.
Comprehension

About Mr. Brooks

1. What does Mr. Brooks point out about the phrases world-class faculty and vibrant community?
That they come from the college's own website, so repeating them tells the college nothing new.
2. What three things does he ask Stella to find, and what must she do with each one?
One course, one person and one thing she could only do there, and she must check that each one is real.
3. What mistake from his first year as a counselor does he describe?
He let a student send a supplement that named the wrong professor.
Listening 1

The Tour Guide

A student leads a campus tour for visiting families. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. What does the guide say most visitors ask about, and what does she wish they asked instead?
Most ask about dorms and food; she wishes they asked what a normal Tuesday in class feels like.
2. How does she describe her first-year seminar?
Twelve students around one table, with an object from the campus collection in the middle of it every week.
3. What advice does she give about the Why Us supplement?
Write about something you saw or did on the visit that you could not have read on the website.
Listening 2

The Professor

An art history professor speaks to prospective students. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. What does the professor say he does not care about when he meets prospective students?
Whether they already know the names of famous artists or can list movements and dates.
2. What quality does he say he looks for instead?
Students who ask a question about an object and are willing to stay with it long enough to find out they were wrong.
3. What does he say about using the word passion?
Everyone uses it, so it tells him nothing; he would rather hear about one object that kept the student awake.
