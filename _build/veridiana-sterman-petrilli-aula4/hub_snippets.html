Lesson 04 -- Pre-class

What Happened Last Night -- Telling the Story

A hotel with no rooms, a line of people at midnight, and one question that worked better than ten minutes of shouting. Key words: a mix-up, to overbook, a shift, on duty, a line, to sort out, eventually, it turns out, to make a fuss, a voucher. Structure: past simple and past continuous with when, while and as soon as.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A mix-up -- a mistake in which two things get confused
"There was a mix-up with the names on the two bookings."
To overbook -- to sell more rooms or seats than the place actually has
"Hotels overbook on purpose, because some guests never arrive."
A shift -- the hours one person works before another takes over
"The night shift starts at eleven and ends at seven."
On duty -- working at that moment, and responsible for what happens
"Who was on duty when we arrived?"
A line -- a row of people waiting, one behind another
"There was a line of nine people at the desk."
To sort out -- to fix a problem, step by step, until it is finished
"The night manager sorted it out in four minutes."
Eventually -- after a long time, in the end
"Eventually he got exactly the same room, an hour later."
It turns out -- in the end we discovered that
"It turns out that four other families got the same offer."
To make a fuss -- to complain loudly, so that everybody notices
"He made a fuss for ten minutes and he got nothing."
A voucher -- a piece of paper you use instead of money, for one thing only
"She gave us a voucher for the taxi, in both directions."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Reporting a problem is one sentence. Telling the story of it is a different skill, and English does it with two past tenses working together. The first one paints the scene: "It was raining." "Four other people were standing at the desk." Nothing here is a single event — it is what was going on around you, and the shape is always was or were plus the verb with -ing.

The second one is the event: "We arrived." "She gave us a voucher." One moment, finished, and the shape is the simple past. Put the two together and you have a story: "It was raining when we arrived." The long thing had already started; the short thing cut into it. That is what when does.

While is different: it holds two long things side by side, and both halves take -ing. "While he was making a fuss, the woman on duty was typing and saying nothing." Never while he shouted. And as soon as is different again: it puts two short events one straight after the other, so both take the simple past. "As soon as she saw the confirmation, she sorted it out."

Two words end a story instead of letting it stop. Eventually means after a long wait — and it is not the Portuguese word that looks like it. "Eventually he got the same room." And it turns out introduces what you discovered at the end: "It turned out the hotel had overbooked on purpose." Scene, interruption, end. Every story you will ever tell has those three pieces.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Simple and Past Continuous

GRAMMAR

The background takes was plus -ing. The event takes the simple past.

JobFormExample
The scene, going on around youwas / were + verb-ingIt was raining.
The one thing that happenedsimple pastWe arrived.
Putting the two togetherlong action + when + short actionIt was raining when we arrived.
Two long actions at oncewhile + was/were -ing, in both halvesWhile he was shouting, she was typing.
One right after the otheras soon as + simple past, twiceAs soon as she saw it, she sorted it out.
Ending the storyeventually / in the end / it turned outEventually he got the same room.
In speechwas not becomes wasn’t · were not becomes weren’tNobody wasn’t moving is wrong: say nobody was moving.

Never: while he shouted · it rained when we arrived · as soon as she was seeing it · four people standing at the desk

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to set the scene of the arrival. You say:
A "It rained when we arrived at the hotel."
B "It was raining when we arrived at the hotel."
C "It is raining when we arrived at the hotel."
Two things were happening at the same time. You say:
A "While he shouted, she typed and said nothing."
B "While he was shouting, she was typing and saying nothing."
C "While he was shouting, she typed and was saying nothing."
One thing happened the second the other one finished. You say:
A "As soon as she saw the confirmation, she sorted it out."
B "As soon as she was seeing the confirmation, she was sorting it out."
C "As soon as she has seen the confirmation, she sorted it out."
You want to end the story. You say:
A "And that is all."
B "Eventually they sorted it out, and it turned out that four other families got the same offer."
C "Eventually it was turning out fine."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
