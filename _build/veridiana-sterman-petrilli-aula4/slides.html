Something That Went Wrong, and Got Sorted Out

Three Parts of Any Story

1. The scene. What was going on around you.

Chapter 4: The Code

The Long One and the Short One

Two past tenses, and three words that put them together

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"It was raining when we arrived."

"Four other people were standing at the desk when we walked in."

"While he was shouting, she was typing and saying nothing."

"As soon as she saw the confirmation, she stopped typing."

The red verbs all have the same shape and they all lasted a while. The blue verbs happened in a second. Now look at the last sentence: both of its verbs are blue. Why?

JobFormExample
The scene, going on around youwas / were + verb-ingIt was raining.
The one thing that happenedsimple pastWe arrived.
Putting the two togetherlong action + when + short actionIt was raining when we arrived.
Two long actions at oncewhile + was/were -ing, in both halvesWhile he was shouting, she was typing.
One right after the otheras soon as + simple past, twiceAs soon as she saw it, she stopped.
Ending the storyeventually / in the end / it turned outEventually he got the same room.
The trapwhile almost never takes the simple pastNot while he shouted. Say while he was shouting.

In one line: the background takes was + -ing, the event takes the simple past, and when, while and as soon as tell the listener which is which.

Listening 1 · The Night Manager

Why Hotels Do It on Purpose

A hotel night manager on overbooking. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say a hotel sells more rooms than it has?
Because on a normal night four or five guests never turn up, and an empty room earns nothing. Nine nights out of ten nobody notices.
2. What two things was she taught to do on her first shift?
Tell the guest the truth in the first sentence, before they ask, and say what she is going to do, not what she cannot do.
3. What has she learned about guests who make a big noise?
They do not get more than the calm ones. They only get it later, because while they are shouting, nobody is typing.
Listening 2 · A Traveller

Nine People and One Good Question

A traveller telling a story from Lisbon. Sound first — no text.

1. What was the scene when they got to the hotel?
It was eleven at night, it was raining, there was a line of about nine people at the desk, and nobody was moving.
2. What did the man in front of them do, and what did he get?
He shouted at the receptionist for ten minutes about a booking from March, and he got nothing.
3. What three things did his wife do, and what happened after forty seconds?
She put the reservation on the counter, she smiled, and she asked what they could do for them that night. The receptionist gave them a better hotel two streets away, a taxi and breakfast.

Survival Card

Five Phrases to Tell a Story

It was raining when we arrived at the hotel.

While we were waiting, the hotel told us it was overbooked.
As soon as I asked what they could do for us, everything changed.
Eventually they sorted it out, and it turned out fine.
Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I set the scene with was and were plus -ing, before I say what happened.
I use when for the interruption and while for two things at the same time.
I use as soon as with two simple pasts, never with an -ing.
I can finish a story with eventually or it turned out, instead of stopping.
I know the words: a mix-up, to overbook, a shift, on duty, a line, to sort out, eventually, it turns out, to make a fuss, a voucher.
