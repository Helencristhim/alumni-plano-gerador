Lesson 11 -- Pre-class

Say It Like You Mean It -- The Person Who, the Place Where

Describing people, places and things when you do not know the name. Key words: neighborhood, landmark, boutique, souvenir, designer, tailor, mentor, roommate. Structure: who for people, that for things, where for places.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. Four are places and things, four are people.

Neighborhood -- an area of a city where people live
"My neighborhood is the part of the city that never sleeps."
Landmark -- a famous building or place that helps you know where you are
"The old cinema is the landmark where everybody meets."
Boutique -- a small shop that sells fashionable clothes
"It is a boutique that only sells local designers."
Souvenir -- something you buy or keep to remember a place
"This key ring is the souvenir that my roommate gave me."
Designer -- a person who creates clothes or other products
"She is a designer who works with old fabric."
Tailor -- a person who makes or changes clothes to fit you
"He is the tailor who fixed my jacket in one day."
Mentor -- an experienced person who helps and advises you
"I need a mentor who knows the fashion business."
Roommate -- a person who shares a room or an apartment with you
"My roommate is the person who showed me the city."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

My friend Ryan arrives in January, so I wrote him a short guide. He should stay in a neighborhood where he can walk everywhere. If he gets lost, there is an old cinema that is a library now. It is the landmark where everybody meets. His jacket has a broken zipper, so I told him about a tailor who can fix anything in one day. And if he wants a souvenir, there is a small boutique that only sells local designers. It is the place where I spend too much money.
Why is it "a tailor who can fix anything" and not "a tailor that it can fix anything"?
A Because who takes the place of he, so you do not repeat it
B Because a tailor is a place, and places always need who in English
C Because the sentence is in the past
Why does the text say "the landmark where everybody meets"?
A Because people meet in that place, so where joins the two parts
B Because landmark is a person
C Because where always comes after the word the, in any sentence that describes something

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Who, That and Where

GRAMMAR

Who, that and where join two sentences into one. The word you choose depends on what you are describing.

whoa personHe is the tailor who fixed my jacket.
thata thing (also people, in conversation)It is a boutique that sells local designers.
wherea place where something happensThat is the cafe where students study.
two into onehe / it / there disappearI bought it in that shop. → That is the shop where I bought it.

Careful 1: never repeat the pronoun. Not the jacket that I bought it; it is the jacket that I bought.

Careful 2: if the place DOES the action, use that. A market that sells fabric, not a market where sells fabric.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
In a conversation you forget the word "tailor". What do you say?
A "It is the person who makes your clothes fit you."
B "It is the person which makes your clothes fit, the one I told you about yesterday."
C "It is the place who fixes clothes."
Which sentence is correct?
A That is the jacket that I bought it last year in the boutique near the station.
B That is the jacket that I bought last year.
C That is the jacket where I bought last year.
A visitor asks where to meet you. Which answer helps most?
A "Meet me at the old cinema, the place where everybody meets."
B "Meet me there, in that place, you know, the one that it is near the other place."
C "Meet me at the cinema who is a library."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
A friend from another country is visiting you next month. Record two minutes for them: three places and two people in your city they should know, without saying any names. Use who, that and where at least twice each. Do not write a script first — just speak.
