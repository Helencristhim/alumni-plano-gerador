Chapter 3: The Code

Who, That, Where

Person, thing or place: that is the whole decision

Discover the Rule

What Decides the Orange Word?

He is the tailor who fixed my jacket in one day.
It is a boutique that only sells local designers.
That is the cafe where students study after nine.
She is the roommate who showed me the market that sells fabric.
Click to reveal the rule
The orange word joins two sentences into one, and it depends on what you are describing. Person → who (the tailor who fixed my jacket). Thing → that (a boutique that sells local designers; that also works for people in conversation). Place → where, when something happens IN that place (the cafe where students study). The orange word takes the place of he, it or there, so you never repeat them: the shop that I bought it there is wrong; it is the shop where I bought it.

Chapter 4: Showing Somebody Around

Three Places and One Person

A neighborhood walk, and a visitor who arrives in January

Listening 1

The Neighborhood Walk

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What kind of clothes does the boutique on the corner sell?
Clothes from local designers only, and nothing costs more than a dinner for two.
2. What is the old cinema now, and why do people meet there?
It is a library now, and people meet there because nobody can miss it. It is the only real landmark.
3. Who is Mr. Costa?
He is the tailor who fixed half of the jackets in the city. He has worked in the same room for forty years.

G
