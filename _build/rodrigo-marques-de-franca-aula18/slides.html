Chapter 4: The Code

Read It from the Right

Compound nouns and the words in front of the noun

Grammar Discovery

Where Is the Noun?

"a $2.4 billion all-cash takeover"

"the São Paulo-based packaging group"

"ending a three-month bidding war"

"Veltmann will pay a $95 million break fee."

The red words all describe the last noun. Say each one the long way, with of, that or which. What happened to the plurals and the prepositions?

PatternLong versionExample
noun + nounthe first noun works like an adjectivea packaging group = a group that makes packaging
number + noun + nounsingular and with a hyphen before the nouna three-month war = a war that lasted three months
place / noun + -based, -owned, -backeda whole relative clause in one worda family-owned company = a company that a family owns
noun + noun vs. 'snoun + noun = a type; 's = belongs to a specific onea company board (a type) / Brasforte's board (theirs)
The ordernumber · adjective · noun modifier · head nouna $2.4 billion · all-cash · takeover · bid
How to read itfrom the right: the last noun is the thinga takeover bid is a bid; a bid deadline is a deadline
The trapsno plural and no of before the head nounNOT a three-months war · NOT the group of packaging

In one line: the last noun is the thing; everything in front of it describes it, in the singular.

Listening 1 · The Headline Writer

Seven Words for a Whole Story

A headline writer at a business newspaper, on how she turns a paragraph into a headline. Sound first — no text.

1. What is her job mostly about, according to her?
Deleting words: fitting a long sentence from a reporter into about seven words.
2. What happens to "a company that is based in Brazil"?
It becomes "Brazil-based".
3. What is her trick for reading a headline?
Read the noun phrase from the right: the last noun is the thing, and everything in front of it describes it.
Listening 2 · The Deal Lawyer

The Easiest Day of the Deal

A mergers and acquisitions lawyer, on what happens after the headline. Sound first — no text.

1. Why is the day of the headline the easiest day, according to him?
Because everybody is happy: the board has approved, the shareholders get a premium and the press release talks about synergies.
2. What can the antitrust authorities do?
Take up to a year to decide, and ask the buyer to sell part of the business.
3. What is his first question when a client shows him a headline?
Not the price: how long until closing, and what has to happen before then.
