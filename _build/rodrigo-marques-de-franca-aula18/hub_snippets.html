The Deal in the News -- Mergers and Acquisitions in the Press

How to read a takeover story and unpack the long noun phrases of a business headline. Key words: a takeover, a bid, a stake, a premium, to fend off, to sweeten an offer, antitrust, regulatory approval, a target, a suitor, to clinch, synergies. Structure: compound nouns and pre-modification -- a three-month bidding war / a São Paulo-based packaging group.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A takeover -- the act of buying control of another company
"The takeover was announced on Monday morning."
A bid -- an offer to buy a company at a particular price
"The board rejected the first bid as too low."
A stake -- a share of the ownership of a company
"The fund already owns a nine percent stake in the company."
A premium -- the extra amount paid above the market price
"The buyer is paying a thirty percent premium."
To fend off -- to defend yourself successfully against an attack or an unwanted offer
"The family tried to fend off the bid for almost a year."
To sweeten an offer -- to make an offer more attractive, usually by adding money
"They had to sweeten the offer twice before the family agreed."
Antitrust -- connected with the laws that stop one company from controlling a market
"The antitrust review could take six months."
Regulatory approval -- official permission from the authorities for a deal to go ahead
"The deal still needs regulatory approval in Europe."
A target -- the company that another company wants to buy
"The target's shares rose fifteen percent on the news."
A suitor -- a company that wants to buy another company
"A second suitor appeared a week after the first bid."
To clinch -- to finally succeed in getting an agreement or a deal
"A last-minute call clinched the deal."
Synergies -- the savings and benefits expected from combining two companies
"The buyer expects synergies of a hundred million dollars a year."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Monday morning, Rodrigo opened the business section and read the headline twice: Brazilian Packaging Group Agrees Dutch Takeover. The target was Brasforte, a São Paulo-based packaging group, and the buyer was Veltmann, its Dutch rival. The price was a $2.4 billion all-cash deal, a 32 percent premium to the share price before the first bid.

It had not been easy. The family-owned holding company that controls Brasforte had tried to fend off two earlier offers, and a second suitor, a US private equity-backed fund, already held a 9 percent stake. Veltmann had to sweeten its offer twice to clinch the deal and end the three-month bidding war.

Rodrigo knew the story was not finished. The deal still needed regulatory approval, and the six-month antitrust review would decide whether the promised synergies were ever real.

1. "a three-month bidding war" -- what is the head noun, the thing itself?
A month, because the number in front of it tells you how long everything took
B bidding
C war
2. Why does the text say "three-month" and not "three-months"?
A Because the war lasted exactly one month and the newspaper made a small mistake in the number.
B Before the noun, it works like an adjective, so it stays singular.
C Because it is a headline.
3. Why did Rodrigo think the story was not finished?
A Because the deal still needed regulatory approval.
B Because the family was still trying to fend off the bid and had refused to sign anything.
C Because the price was too low.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Compound Nouns and Pre-modification

GRAMMAR

English puts the description in front of the noun. Read from the right: the last noun is the thing.

PatternLong versionExample
noun + nouna group that makes packaginga packaging group
number + noun (singular, hyphen)a war that lasted three monthsa three-month war
-based / -owned / -backeda company that a family ownsa family-owned company
noun + noun vs. 'sa type vs. belongs to a specific onea company board / Brasforte's board
Money in front of the nouna deal worth $2.4 billiona $2.4 billion deal (say: a two-point-four-billion-dollar deal)
Pluralonly the last noun changestwo distribution centers

Never: a three-months war · two distributions centers · a group of packaging based in São Paulo

Stage 2: Put the Takeover in Order

Order

From the first bid to the closing. Put the story in that order.

?"Veltmann sweetened its offer twice."
?"Now the deal is waiting for regulatory approval before closing."
?"The board rejected it as too low and tried to fend off the bid."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A client asks you what kind of company Brasforte is. You say:
A "It's a group of packaging, based in São Paulo, that sells to the food industry."
B "It's a São Paulo-based packaging group."
C "It's a packagings group."
The board has approved a deal, but the competition authorities have not decided yet. You say:
A "It's closed, because the board has approved it and the shareholders are happy with the price."
B "It's a target."
C "It still needs regulatory approval."
A buyer added three dollars a share after the first offer was rejected. A journalist would write:
A "The buyer sweetened its offer."
B "The buyer fended off its offer by three dollars a share after the rejection on Friday."
C "The buyer clinched a stake."
You want to describe a review that will take six months. You say:
A "a six-months review of the antitrust questions in the deal"
B "a six-month review"
C "a review six months"

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.
