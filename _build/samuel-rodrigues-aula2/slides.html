Chapter 4: The Code

What Would Have Happened

The past you cannot change, and the sentence that analyses it.

Discover

What Do These Have in Common?

"If the contract had included a force majeure provision, Verde would have had a remedy."

"If the board had disclosed the full liability, the shareholders might have approved the deal."

"If nobody had removed the clause, the deal would not have fallen through."

The condition (past, unreal)The result (past, unreal)
If + had + past participle
If the provision had been there...		would / might / could have + past participle
...we would have had a remedy.

Neither part happened. That is the whole point: you analyse a closed past without blaming anyone in the room.

Practice

Complete the Analysis

"If we ___had kept the original wording, the risk would have been covered."
"If the licence had not been suspended, the contract ___would not have been terminated."
"The client ___might have avoided the settlement if we had asked for a cap on liability."
"If they ___had disclosed the pending investigation, we would have priced the deal differently."
Register

The Same Idea, in a Memo

Spoken

"If the provision had been included, the notice would not have been valid."

Written · formal

"Had the provision been included, the notice would not have been valid."

Drop the if, move had to the front. Nothing else changes — and the sentence goes straight into a memo.
