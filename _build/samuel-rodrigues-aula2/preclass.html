Lesson 02 -- Pre-class

The Clause That Was Never There -- Taking Control of the Room

A ten-year supply contract collapses because one provision was removed to save time. Before the lesson: the eight words that decide who pays when a deal goes wrong — provision, breach, liability, remedy, waiver, termination, disclosure, indemnification — and the structure you need in front of a board: if we had..., we would have....

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Eight words of risk. Listen to each one and read the example out loud.

Provision -- a term written into a contract to cover a situation
"The contract had no force majeure provision."
Breach -- breaking a rule or a duty in a contract
"They treated the missed deliveries as a breach."
Liability -- legal responsibility for a loss or a cost
"We always ask for a cap on liability."
Remedy -- what the law gives you when the other side fails
"With no provision, the client had no remedy."
Waiver -- giving up a right on purpose
"The client signed a waiver of its counterclaim."
Termination -- ending a contract before its natural end
"The other side served a termination notice."
Disclosure -- telling the other side something they need to know
"Full disclosure of the liability would have changed the vote."
Indemnification -- a promise to cover the other side's loss
"The scope of the indemnification is still open."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the internal note. The verbs in bold analyse a past that cannot be changed.

The force majeure provision was removed in the final round of comments, to simplify the text. Eighteen months later the regulator suspended the licence, the client could not take delivery, and the other side called it a breach.

If the provision had stayed in the contract, the client would have had a remedy. If the board had known the full liability, it might have asked for a cap. And if nobody had removed that paragraph, the termination notice would not have been valid.

None of that happened. What we do now is different: every supply contract gets a force majeure provision, a clear termination clause, and a cap on liability.

1. Did the provision stay in the contract?
A Yes — that is why there was a remedy.
B No — and that is why there was no remedy.
C The note does not say.
2. What does "the board might have asked for a cap" tell you?
A The board asked for a cap.
B The board did not ask, and it is only a possible past.
C The board will ask for a cap next time.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Third conditional — the past you cannot change

Two halves, both unreal, both in the past:

  • If + had + past participle  →  would have + past participle
  • "If the provision had been there, we would have had a remedy."
  • Softer: might have / could have — "the shareholders might have approved it."

The mistake to avoid

Never if we would have. The if half takes had, always.

Memo register: drop the "if"

"Had the provision been included, the notice would not have been valid." Same meaning, no if, had in front. This is how it goes into writing.

Stage 2: Put the Note in Order

Order

This is how a note to the board is built. Put the four moves in the right order.

?"The force majeure provision was removed in the final round of comments."
?"When the regulator suspended the licence, the company had no remedy."
?"Had the provision been included, the termination notice would not have been valid."
?"From now on, every supply contract requires a cap on liability."

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

These are long sentences. Say them slowly, in two halves — the condition, then the result.

If the provision had been included, we would have had a remedy.
Had the provision been included, the notice would not have been valid.
With the benefit of hindsight, the clause should have stayed in.
From now on we will require a cap on liability.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
The board asks what went wrong. Which answer analyses without blaming?
A "The person who removed the clause made a mistake."
B "If the provision had been included, we would have had a remedy."
C "I am very sorry. It was a serious failure."
Which sentence is correct?
A "If we would have kept the clause, the deal would have survived."
B "If we had kept the clause, the deal would have survived."
C "If we had kept the clause, the deal would survive."
You are writing the memo, not speaking. Which version fits?
A "Had the provision been included, the notice would not have been valid."
B "Had been the provision included, the notice would not have been valid."
C "If the provision would be included, the notice was not valid."
The counterparty says your client caused the loss. What is the professional reply?
A "That is not true. You caused it."
B "I see it differently. The suspension was an act of a public authority."
C "Maybe. I will check and come back."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Think of one deal in your own practice that went wrong because of something that was not in the contract. In about ninety seconds: say what happened, then analyse it with if we had..., we would have..., and finish with what you require from now on. Pause one second before you start.
