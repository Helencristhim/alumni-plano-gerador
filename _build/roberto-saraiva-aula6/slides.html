Chapter 3: The Code

Two Things at the Same Time

One was already running. The other cut through it

Discovery

Which Action Started First?

"While we were preparing the launch, the competitor announced the same product."

"The campaign was running when the shelves went empty."

"We were testing two packaging designs, and nobody looked at the price."

"When the first ad went live, awareness was already growing."

The red part was already running — it is the background. The blue part happened at one moment and cut through it. And notice which word introduces each half.

UseFormExample
The background, in progresswas / were + verb-ingWe were preparing the launch.
The event that cuts inpast simpleThe competitor announced the same product.
while+ the long actionWhile we were preparing, they announced.
when+ the short actionThe campaign was running when the shelves went empty.
Negative and questionwas not / were not + -ing · Were you...?We were not watching the price. Were you testing both?
The trapwe + were, never wasNot we was preparing. Not while we prepared.

In one line: the long thing takes -ing, the sudden thing takes the past simple, while goes with the long one and when with the short one.

Listening 1 · The Brand Manager

What We Were Doing in March

The person who ran the campaign, telling the room what really happened. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What was the team doing in March, and what happened in the middle of it?
They were testing two packaging designs in two cities. In the middle of it, the competitor announced almost the same product.
2. What decision did they take because of that, and was it the right one?
They moved the launch forward by six weeks. She says it was the right decision and the wrong preparation: they gained the date and lost the packaging test.
3. What is the one number she is proud of, and the one she is not?
Awareness doubled in eight weeks. But sales in the first month were forty percent under the plan.
Listening 2 · The Retail Buyer

Why Launches Die on the Shelf

The person who decides what gets space, and what leaves. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does he say he gives a new product, and for how long?
Shelf space, and twelve weeks. If it does not sell in twelve weeks, the space goes to somebody else.
2. What was happening in his store during the campaign, and what was the problem?
People were coming in and asking for the product by name, and the shelf was empty. The advertising arrived before the stock did.
3. What is his advice about the roll-out?
Roll it out slowly: two states first. It is better to be small and full than national and empty.
Real Document

The Campaign One-Pager

CAMPAIGN BRIEF — SPRING LAUNCH
Internal · marketing and sales
Launch datemoved from May 15 to April 1
Reason for the changecompetitor announcement, March 9
Target audiencefamilies, weekly shop, two children
Packagingdesign B · test not finished
Roll-out plannational, all regions, week 1
Stock ready on launch day2 states out of 9
Awareness after 8 weeksdoubled · sales 40% under plan
Note added in June: the roll-out plan and the stock line were never read in the same meeting.
1. Two lines on this page contradict each other. Which two, and what was the result?
The roll-out plan says national in week one, and the stock line says two states out of nine. The advertising reached nine states and the product reached two.
2. Awareness doubled and sales were forty percent under plan. How is that possible?
Awareness means people know the brand. It does not mean they can buy it. They went to the store, the shelf was empty, and they bought something else.
3. Read the note added in June. What does it say about the company, not about the campaign?
That marketing and logistics were working on the same launch without reading each other’s numbers.
