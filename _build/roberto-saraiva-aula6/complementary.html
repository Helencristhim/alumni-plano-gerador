Lesson 06 -- The Launch That Worked

Talk
Seth Godin -- "How to get your ideas to spread" (TED2003)

Seventeen minutes on why a good product with no story stays on the shelf, told with supermarket examples that could have come from your own category. Fast but very clear American English, and the whole vocabulary of this lesson is in it.

Tip: turn the English subtitles on. Then take one product of yours and describe, in three sentences, who it is remarkable for.

Watch on TED ↗
Podcast
NPR How I Built This -- "Canva: Melanie Perkins"

A launch story told exactly the way this lesson teaches: what they were doing, what happened in the middle of it, and what they changed because of it. Forty minutes, natural speed, with the transcript on the page.

Tip: good for the car. Every time you hear a sentence with two layers — something in progress and something sudden — say it back out loud.

Listen on NPR ↗
Series
Mad Men -- Season 1, Episode 1: "Smoke Gets in Your Eyes"

Forty-five minutes inside a room where a campaign is being built for a client who is about to lose everything. Slow, deliberate American English, and one of the clearest scenes ever filmed about what a brand actually is.

Tip: one episode is enough. Watch the final pitch scene twice: once with subtitles and once without.
