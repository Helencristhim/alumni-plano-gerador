Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

The Launch That Worked -- A Campaign, Told From the Inside

Two products left the factory in the same month and only one is still on the shelf. Key words: a launch, a campaign, a brand, a target audience, awareness, shelf space, to roll out, packaging. Structure: past continuous with past simple -- the background and the cut.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A launch -- the moment a new product goes on sale for the first time
"The launch was planned for March and moved to May."
A campaign -- all the advertising and actions planned together for one purpose
"The campaign was running for three weeks before anybody noticed."
A brand -- the name and identity a company sells under, and what people feel about it
"The product was fine. The brand meant nothing to anybody."
A target audience -- the exact group of people a campaign is made for
"The target audience was families, and the ad spoke to chefs."
Awareness -- how many people know that your brand exists at all
"Awareness doubled, and sales did not move."
Shelf space -- the room your product gets in a store, which somebody else wants too
"We won the shelf space and then we could not fill it."
To roll out -- to bring something to more places, step by step, not all at once
"We rolled it out in two states, and only then in the whole country."
Packaging -- the box, bag or label the product is sold in
"We were testing two packaging designs in two cities."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

In March the team was preparing a launch for the fifteenth of May. The target audience was families, the campaign was ready, and they were testing two packaging designs in two cities. While they were testing, the competitor announced almost the same product.

They moved the date forward by six weeks. The packaging test was still running when they chose design B, so nobody ever saw the result. The campaign then ran in nine states, and the stock was arriving in two.

People were walking into stores and asking for the product by name, and the shelf was empty. Awareness doubled in eight weeks. Sales in the first month came in forty percent under plan. The premium line, meanwhile, rolled out in two states only, and while everybody was watching the big brand, it quietly won shelf space in a third state.

1. "While they were testing, the competitor announced." Which action started first?
A The testing. It was already in progress when the announcement cut into it.
B The announcement. It always comes first with while.
C They started at exactly the same moment.
2. "The campaign ran in nine states, and the stock was arriving in two." Why is one form -ing and the other not?
A Because stock is a plural word and campaign is singular.
B Because the stock was still in progress, arriving step by step, while the campaign is treated as one finished fact.
C Because the stock line is a question.
3. "Awareness doubled and sales came in under plan." Why are both in the past simple?
A Because they happened at the same time.
B Because they are two finished results, not two actions in progress. Nothing is being interrupted here.
C Because numbers always take the past simple.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Continuous With Past Simple

GRAMMAR

Two layers in one sentence: what was already running, and what cut through it. It is how every business story is told.

UseFormExample
The background, in progresswas / were + verb-ingWe were preparing the launch.
The event that cuts inpast simpleThe competitor announced the same product.
while+ the long actionWhile we were preparing, they announced.
when+ the short actionThe campaign was running when the shelves went empty.
Negative and questionwas not / were not + -ing · Were you...?Nobody was watching the price. Were you testing both?
Two finished resultspast simple in both halvesAwareness doubled and sales came in under plan.

Never: We was preparing · While we prepared the launch · When the shelves were going empty (for a sudden event) · Nobody watching the price · They was testing.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

You want to say that the announcement interrupted your preparation. You say:
A "While we prepared the launch, the competitor announced."
B "While we were preparing the launch, the competitor announced."
C "While we was preparing the launch, the competitor announced."
The shelves emptied suddenly, in the middle of the campaign. You say:
A "The campaign was running when the shelves went empty."
B "The campaign ran when the shelves were going empty."
C "The campaign was running when the shelves were empty going."
A colleague asks whether both packaging designs were being tested together. You answer:
A "Yes, we was testing both."
B "Yes, we were testing both, in two different cities."
C "Yes, we testing both in two cities."
You are explaining why a big campaign did not produce sales. The clearest answer is:
A "The target audience did not like the brand."
B "The campaign was running in nine states while the stock was arriving in two, so the shelf was empty."
C "Awareness is not important for sales."
