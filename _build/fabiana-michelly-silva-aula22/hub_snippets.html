Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Win over -- to persuade someone to support you, often after they disagreed
"She won over the head of Sales in a ten-minute conversation."
Sway -- to influence someone's opinion or decision
"One clear number swayed Finance more than ten slides."
Pain point -- the problem that really hurts a person or a team
"For Sales, the pain point is the delay, not the unit price."
Pre-wire -- to discuss an idea privately with key people before the official meeting
"I pre-wired the proposal with Daniel, so there were no surprises."
Champion -- to publicly support and promote an idea
"Daniel agreed to champion the pilot in front of the vice president."
Ally -- a person on your side who helps you reach a goal
"You need one ally in every team you want to influence."
Silo -- a team that works separately and does not share information with others
"Sales and Supply Chain worked in a silo until Penang."
Turf -- an area of work that someone feels belongs only to them
"Pricing is Finance's turf, so ask before you touch it."
Sounding board -- a person you test your ideas on before you share them widely
"Marcus is my sounding board before any big proposal."
Quick win -- a small success you can get fast, which builds trust for more
"Start with keyboards: it is a quick win, not a revolution."
Meet someone halfway -- to accept part of what the other side wants, so you both agree
"Let's meet halfway: keyboards first, and we review in ninety days."
Frame -- to present an issue from a particular angle
"Frame it as a risk for Sales, not as a cost for Finance."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Two weeks after Penang, Fabiana wants a second supplier for keyboards. The problem: nobody in Sales or Finance reports to her. She cannot give an order, so she asks questions that already contain the answer. Wouldn't it make sense to stop depending on one plant? Isn't the real pain point the delay, not the unit price? Penang cost us eleven days, didn't it?

Look at the grammar. A negative question like wouldn't it or isn't does not ask for information: it invites a yes. A question tag does the same at the end of a statement. The tag copies the auxiliary and the tense, and it flips positive and negative: it cost us eleven days, didn't it? / we can't afford another delay, can we? With a falling tone, the tag says: I know you agree.

The questions are only half of it. Before the meeting, she pre-wires the idea with Daniel from Sales Operations and uses Marcus as a sounding board. She frames the proposal as a risk for Sales, not a cost for Finance, and she respects Finance's turf on pricing. Daniel is not easy to win over, so she agrees to meet him halfway: one product line as a quick win. In the end, he becomes her ally and agrees to champion the pilot.

1. Why does Fabiana use questions instead of statements?
A Because she is not sure of the facts.
B Because nobody reports to her, so she invites a yes instead of giving an order.
C Because questions are more formal than statements.
2. Why is it "Penang cost us eleven days, didn't it?" and not "isn't it?"
A Because the verb is in the past, so the tag uses the auxiliary did.
B Because isn't it is only for questions about people.
C Because the sentence is negative.
3. What did Fabiana do before the official meeting?
A She sent an order to Sales and Finance.
B She waited to surprise everyone with the idea.
C She pre-wired the idea with Daniel and tested it with Marcus.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Negative Questions and Question Tags

GRAMMAR

Ask a question that already contains the answer, and let the other person say yes.

JobFormExample
Suggest softlyWouldn't it make sense to...? · Shouldn't we...?Wouldn't it make sense to start small?
Point to what we all knowIsn't it true that...? · Haven't we all seen...?Isn't the real risk the delay?
Tag: positive statementpositive + negative tag (same auxiliary, same tense)Penang cost us eleven days, didn't it?
Tag: negative statementnegative + positive tagWe can't afford another delay, can we?
Intonationfalling tone = you expect agreement · rising tone = you really want to knowIt's the same risk, isn't it? (falling)
Answeringanswer the fact, not the grammarIsn't it expensive? Yes, it is. / No, it isn't.

Never: We lost two days, isn't it? · Wouldn't it makes sense · We can't afford it, can't we? · Shouldn't we to review it?

Stage 2: Put the Pitch in Order

Order

Fabiana opens a cross-functional meeting with Sales and Finance. Put her pitch in order.

?"I don't manage anyone in this room, so I'm here to ask a question."
?"Penang cost us eleven days, didn't it?"
?"Wouldn't it make sense to stop depending on a single plant?"
?"The proposal is a second supplier for keyboards only, for ninety days."
?"The unit price is higher, but isn't the real pain point the delay?"
?"We can review it in ninety days, can't we? If it doesn't work, we walk away."
