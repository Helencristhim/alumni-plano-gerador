Chapter 4: The Cross-Functional Meeting

A Second Supplier for Keyboards

One conversation, two recordings, one pre-read

Dialogue

The Meeting Before the Meeting

D
Dual sourcing doubles our paperwork. Why should Sales care?
F
Because you felt Penang first, didn't you? Your clients waited eleven days for their keyboards.
D
Fair. But a second supplier costs more per unit.
F
It does. But wouldn't it be cheaper than another delay? Let's look at the pain point, not the unit price.
D
You're trying to win me over before the meeting, aren't you?
F
I am. I'd rather pre-wire this with you than surprise you in front of the vice president. Isn't that better for both of us?
D
It is. If you start with one product line as a quick win, I'll champion it.
F
Then let's meet halfway: keyboards first, and we review the numbers in ninety days.
Comprehension

About Daniel

1. What is Daniel's first objection?
Dual sourcing doubles their paperwork, and he asks why Sales should care.
2. What cost concern does Daniel raise?
A second supplier costs more per unit.
3. What condition does Daniel set before he will support the plan?
She has to start with one product line as a quick win. Then he will champion it.
Listening 1 · The Pitch

I’m Here to Ask a Question

Fabiana opens the cross-functional meeting. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say she is not there to tell anyone what to do?
Because she does not manage anyone in the room. She is there to ask a question.
2. What exactly is the proposal?
A second supplier in Monterrey for one product line, keyboards only, for ninety days.
3. How does she answer the cost objection?
The unit price is four percent higher, but the real pain point is the delay: one lost week costs more than a full year of that difference.
Listening 2 · Daniel Calls Back

Nobody Felt Pushed

A voicemail after the meeting. Sound first — no text.

1. Who did Fabiana win over, and what worked, according to Daniel?
She won Daniel over and probably swayed Finance too. Asking questions instead of making demands worked: nobody felt pushed.
2. What does Finance want to add to the ninety-day review?
The freight cost, not just the unit price.
3. What did Daniel tell the vice president, and what is his advice?
That Fabiana owns the pilot and that he is her ally on the sales side. His advice: pre-wire the kickoff with the Monterrey account manager before announcing anything.
Real Document

The Pre-Read

PRE-READ · DUAL SOURCING PILOT: KEYBOARDS
From: Fabiana Michelly Silva, Supply Chain · To: Sales Operations, Finance
THE PAIN POINT
Penang: 11 days without keyboards for client orders. Sales took the complaints.
THE ASK
A second supplier (Monterrey), keyboards only, 90 days. No other product lines.
THE COST
Unit price +4%. One lost week costs more than a full year of that difference.
THE EXIT
Review at day 90. If the numbers do not work, we walk away.
1. Which section frames the proposal as a problem for Sales?
THE PAIN POINT: eleven days without keyboards, and Sales took the complaints.
2. How does the pre-read keep the pilot small?
Keyboards only, for 90 days, and no other product lines.
3. Why is THE EXIT section persuasive?
It shows the risk is limited: if the numbers do not work after 90 days, they walk away.
