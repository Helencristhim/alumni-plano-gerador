Lesson 42 -- Pre-class

My English and My Career -- Everything, Mixed

Ten to twenty minutes before class. A final mix of the course: the pasts, the right form of the word, there is and there was, already and yet, and your plans. Key words: progress, a goal, to achieve, to improve, a skill, feedback.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Progress -- the movement toward a better level
"I have made good progress this year."
A goal -- a thing that you want to achieve
"My goal is to lead meetings in English."
To achieve -- to reach a goal after hard work
"She achieved her target in May."
To improve -- to become better
"My listening has improved a lot."
A skill -- an ability that you can practice
"Presenting is a skill I want to develop."
Feedback -- the comments that help a person to improve
"My manager gave me useful feedback."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

When Marcos started his English course, he had difficulty speaking in meetings. He translated every sentence in his head. Since then, he has practiced every week, and his English has improved a lot.

Now he speaks more confidently and more directly. There are still some mistakes, but there is much less translation. Over the next year, he is going to present the results to the US team.

What was difficult for Marcos at the start?
A He was afraid of numbers.
B He did not want to study English after work, only on weekends.
C He had difficulty speaking in meetings.
"His English has improved a lot." What does it mean?
A It started to improve and it is better now.
B It improved only last week.
C It is going to improve next year, when he starts a new course.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You want to say how you speak now. You say:
A "I speak more confidently."

A "I present to the US team next year."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Your director asks: how has your English changed, and what are your plans for next year? Answer in about one minute: one thing about the start, one thing that has improved, and one plan.

Carol Dweck: “The power of believing that you can improve” (10 min)

A psychologist on the power of the word yet: the difference between I can’t and I can’t yet.

Tip: say three sentences about your English with yet and already.

TED Talk
Matt Cutts: “Try something new for 30 days” (3 min)

A short, simple talk on how small habits, repeated for 30 days, change what you are able to do.

Tip: choose one habit for your English over the next 30 days and tell your teacher what you are going to do.
