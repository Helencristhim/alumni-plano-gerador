Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A tournament -- a competition with many players or teams and several rounds
"We play in a club tournament every spring."
Doubles -- a tennis match with two players on each side
"My wife and I play doubles on Saturdays."
A serve -- the shot that starts each point in tennis
"His serve was fast, but not very accurate."
A rally -- a long series of shots before a point ends
"The last rally had more than thirty shots."
A comeback -- a win after you were losing badly
"They were down a set, but they made a great comeback."
A scholarship -- money that a school or university gives a student to pay for studies
"He was offered a scholarship to study in the United States."
To turn down -- to say no to an offer or an invitation
"He turned down the scholarship and took a job instead."
A crossroads -- a moment when you must choose between different directions in life
"At twenty, I was at a crossroads."
A turning point -- a moment when a situation starts to change in an important way
"That job offer was the turning point of my career."
In hindsight -- thinking about a past event with what you know now
"In hindsight, it was the right decision."
A regret -- a feeling of being sorry about something you did or did not do
"My only regret is that I stopped playing for ten years."
To take a risk -- to do something that might have a bad result, hoping for a good one
"If I hadn’t taken that risk, I would still be in the same job."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At nineteen, Paulo was the best junior player in his state. A university in Florida offered him a scholarship, but his father was sick, and a factory near home offered him a job. He was at a crossroads, and he turned down the scholarship.

Forty years later, he often thinks about it. “If I had accepted the scholarship, I would have played college tournaments in America. But if I hadn’t taken the job, I wouldn’t have met my wife, and I wouldn’t be the director of the company now.”

Today he plays doubles with his wife every Saturday. Last year they lost a semifinal after a great comeback. “In hindsight, the factory was my turning point,” he says. “My only regret is my serve.”

1. Did Paulo accept the scholarship?
A No. He turned it down and took a job at a factory near home.
B Yes. He went to Florida, played college tournaments and came back to Brazil after four years.
C He doesn’t remember.
2. Why does Paulo say “I wouldn’t be the director now” and not “I wouldn’t have been”?
A Because the result is in the present: he is the director now.
B Because the third conditional is always used with the word director and with other job titles.
C Because he is not the director.
3. What is his only regret?
A His serve.
B Turning down the scholarship in Florida and losing the chance to become a professional player in America.
C His wife.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Third and Mixed Conditionals

GRAMMAR

Use if + had + past participle, would have + past participle for a past that did not happen and its past result. Use if + had + past participle, would + verb when the imagined result is in the present. Might have and could have make the result less certain.

TypeFormExample
Third conditionalif + had + past participle, would have + past participleIf he had accepted, he would have played in America.
Less certain resultmight have / could have + past participleHe might have stopped playing forever.
Mixed conditionalif + had + past participle, would + verb (now)If he had gone, he wouldn’t be the president now.
Negativehadn’t + past participle / wouldn’t haveIf he hadn’t taken the job, he wouldn’t have met her.
Contractions in speechI’d have / I would’ve / If I’d knownIf I’d known, I’d have called you.
The trapnever would in the if partNot “If I would have known...” Say If I had known...

Never: If I would have known... · He would played... · She would have win... · If I had studied law, I would have been a lawyer now...
