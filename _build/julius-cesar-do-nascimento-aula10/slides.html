Chapter 4: The Code

If I Had... I Would Have...

The past that did not happen

Grammar Discovery

Did It Happen?

"If he had accepted the scholarship, he would have played in America."

"He wouldn’t have met his wife if he hadn’t taken that job."

"If he had gone to America, he wouldn’t be the president now."

"If his coach hadn’t called, he might have stopped playing forever."

None of these things happened. The speaker is imagining a different past. In sentences 1, 2 and 4, the imagined result is also in the past. In sentence 3, the imagined result is in the present: that is a mixed conditional.

TypeFormExample
Third conditionalif + had + past participle, would have + past participleIf he had accepted, he would have played in America.
Less certain resultmight have / could have + past participleHe might have stopped playing forever.
Mixed conditionalif + had + past participle, would + verb (now)If he had gone, he wouldn’t be the president now.
Negativehadn’t + past participle / wouldn’t haveIf he hadn’t taken the job, he wouldn’t have met her.
Contractions in speechI’d have / I would’ve / If I’d knownIf I’d known, I’d have called you.
The trapnever would in the if partNot “If I would have known...” Say If I had known...

In one line: past that did not happen = if + had done, would have done; result now = if + had done, would do.

Grammar Practice

Imagine the Past

Say it first, then click to check

"If he had accepted the scholarship, he ___would have moved to Florida." (move, result in the past)
"If she ___had trained harder, she would have won the tournament." (train, the condition)
"If I had studied law, I ___would be a lawyer now." (be, result now)
"We ___wouldn't have lost the match if my serve had been better." (not lose, result in the past)
"If he hadn’t taken that risk, he ___might have stayed in the same job." (stay, less certain)

Listening 1 · A British Tennis Coach

The Injury That Changed Everything

A British coach remembers the injury that ended her career as a player when she was twenty-two. Sound first — no text.

1. What happened when she was twenty-two?
She hurt her shoulder, and her career as a player ended.
2. What does she think would have happened without the injury?
She would have played for a few more years, but she probably wouldn’t have become a top player.
3. What is she doing now?
She coaches young players, and she loves it.
Listening 2 · A French Business Owner

The Risk I Almost Didn’t Take

A French business owner talks about the day he left a safe job to start his own company. Sound first — no text.

1. How old was he when he left his job?
Forty-five.
2. What did his family think?
His wife supported him, but his parents thought he was crazy.
3. What would he be doing now if he hadn’t taken the risk?
He would probably still be working in the same bank.
Listening 3 · An Indian Engineer

A Job Across the World

An Indian engineer explains how one email from Japan changed the direction of her life. Sound first — no text.

1. Where did the email come from?
From a Japanese company that wanted engineers for a new plant.
2. What did she almost do with the email?
She almost deleted it.
3. What does she say about regrets?
She has no regrets: if she hadn’t gone, she would never have learned Japanese or met her husband.
