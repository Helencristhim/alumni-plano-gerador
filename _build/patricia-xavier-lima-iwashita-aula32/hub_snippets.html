Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Performance review -- a meeting or document about how well someone worked during the year
"Karen wrote my performance review in December."
Self-assessment -- your own written opinion about your work and progress
"Before the review, I wrote my self-assessment."
Step up -- to take more responsibility, especially when it is needed
"This was the year when she stepped up."
Breakthrough -- an important moment of progress after a difficult time
"My breakthrough was leading meetings in English."
Setback -- a problem that stops your progress for some time
"Losing Harbor Health was a real setback."
Going forward -- from now on; in the future
"Going forward, I will present in every business review."
Comfort zone -- the situations that are easy and familiar for you
"Speaking at a conference is outside my comfort zone."
Fluency -- the ability to speak easily and naturally, without stopping all the time
"Her fluency is strong in long meetings."
Keep up -- to continue doing something at the same level
"I want to keep up my English every week."
Well-deserved -- earned with your work and effort
"Your promotion is well-deserved."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

In December, Karen writes Patrícia's performance review, and Patrícia writes her self-assessment. It was the year when she stepped up. In January she managed mid-size accounts; since August she has managed twelve premium accounts. Her biggest breakthrough was in meetings: she now leads them in English, and her fluency is strong. There was one setback: if the team had talked to Harbor Health's finance team earlier, they would have kept the account. Although the year was not easy, it ended with a promotion, and everybody agrees that it is well-deserved. Going forward, Patrícia wants to keep up her English and step out of her comfort zone: by next December, she will have presented at a global conference.

1. "Since August she has managed twelve premium accounts." Does she still manage them?
A Yes, she still manages them.
B No, it finished in August.
C We do not know.
2. "If the team had talked to finance earlier, they would have kept the account." Did they talk to finance early?
A Yes.
B No.
C Only in September.
3. What was Patrícia's biggest breakthrough?
A Leading meetings in English.
B Getting a discount for a customer.
C Moving to another country.
4. Which sentence is correct?
A "By next December, I will have present at a conference."
B "By next December, I will have presented at a conference."
C "By next December, I have presented at a conference."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Your Story in English: the Structures of the Program

GRAMMAR

A professional story needs a before, a now and a next. These are the structures that you learned in this program to tell it.

StructureUseExample
Past simpleA finished moment in the past.A year ago, I spoke only when someone asked me.
Present perfectFrom the past until now.Since then, I have run reviews in English.
Third conditionalA past that did not happen.If we had talked to finance, we would have kept them.
Future perfectFinished by a future date.By December, I will have presented at a conference.
Contrast linkersTwo opposite ideas.Although it was not easy, it was worth it.
AvoidNever since + past simple: not "since August I managed" → "since August I have managed". Never would in the if part: not "if we would have talked" → "if we had talked". And never "despite of": not "despite of the outage" → "despite the outage".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Your manager asks what has changed this year. You say:
A "Since January, I have led many meetings in English."

Someone asks about a mistake from this year. The best answer is:
A "I never make mistakes."

Someone asks about your plans. You say:
A "By next December, I will have presented at a conference."
