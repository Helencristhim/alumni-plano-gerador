Chapter 4: If You Move

The Sentence That Gives Without Giving Away

The shape a negotiator uses more than any other

Grammar Discovery

Which One Is Not an Offer?

"If you move on the threshold, we will move on the timetable."

"If closing slips past June, the whole timetable will change."

"If revenue passes the threshold, the earnout pays in full."

"We will not sign anything unless the clause protects the team."

Look at the red half of every sentence: not one of them contains will, even though all four are about the future. The green half is where will lives. And the orange one is not an offer at all — it is a rule that fires by itself.

JobFormExample
An offer, a trade, a condition you proposeIf + present simple, will + bare verbIf you move on price, we will move on timing.
A risk you are flaggingsame shape, and the result is the consequenceIf closing slips, the timetable will change.
An automatic rule, not a negotiationIf + present, + presentIf revenue passes the threshold, the earnout pays.
A line you will not crossunless = if notWe will not sign unless the clause protects the team.
Softer, more formalwould instead of will in the resultIf you moved on the threshold, we would consider it.
The trapwill never follows ifNot if you will accept. Say if you accept.
The permanent targetafter if, he/she/it still takes the -sNot if the buyer accept. Say if the buyer accepts.

In one line: the condition stays in the present. Only the result gets a future.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Conditions, Spoken Out Loud

Two recordings and one term sheet

Listening 1 · A Deal Lawyer

How to Say No Without Saying No

A deal lawyer on the three sentences that keep a negotiation alive. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say happens when a founder simply says no?
The other side stops proposing, and once they stop proposing, the founder has lost the only information he was getting.
2. What is the first sentence she recommends, and what does it do?
If you move on that, we can move on this — it refuses and offers at the same time, so nothing closes.
3. What does she say about the word unless?
That it is the politest hard line in English, because it names the condition instead of naming the refusal.
Listening 2 · A Fund Partner

What an Earnout Really Costs

A European fund partner on the condition founders accept too quickly. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say founders accept an earnout too quickly?
Because it raises the headline number, and the headline number is the one they will repeat to everybody afterwards.
2. What does she say an earnout really is?
Three more years of working for somebody else, on numbers somebody else chose.
3. What is her advice about the threshold?
To negotiate the threshold harder than the price, because if the threshold is wrong the price is theoretical.

1. Which part of this document is legally enforceable?
Only the confidentiality section. Everything else describes what both sides currently believe.
2. What has to happen for the earnout to pay in full?
Revenue has to grow fourteen percent a year for the three years from closing.
3. How is the retention structured, and why does that matter?
As a pool for the team rather than payments tied to named individuals, so no single person learns exactly what they are worth.
