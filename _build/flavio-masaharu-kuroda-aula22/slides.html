Chapter 4: The Code

Two Things, One Sentence

How a comparison talks

Grammar Discovery

What Joins the Two Sides?

"Invent charges per seat, whereas Clearbook charges per invoice."

"Clearbook looks cheaper today. However, its cost grows with volume."

"While Invent includes support in Italian, Clearbook does not mention it."

"Unlike Clearbook, Invent does not offer a free trial."

What do the purple words do? And where is the comma in each sentence?

LinkerWhere it goesExample
whereasbetween two clauses, comma before itWe charge per seat, whereas they charge per invoice.
whileat the start, or between two clauses (contrast, not time)While their trial is free, ours is a paid pilot.
howeverstarts a new sentence, comma after itTheir price is lower. However, it grows with volume.
on the other handa new sentence, often in the middle, between commasClearbook, on the other hand, has one plan.
unlike + nounbefore a noun or a name, never before a clauseUnlike Clearbook, we include the connector.
butsimple and informal, between two clausesIt's cheaper today, but it grows.
Neverhowever in the middle with no full stopNot: their price is lower however it grows

In one line: whereas and while join two clauses; however starts a new sentence; unlike takes a noun.

Chapter 6: Your Turn

Two Recordings, Then Giulia’s Question

Listening 1 · The Buyer

Two Pages, Same Basis

A procurement manager on how she compares software prices. Sound first — no text.

1. Why doesn't she start with the price on the page?
Because pricing pages are written to make every product look cheap, and the two numbers do not mean the same thing.
2. What scenario does she build?
Their number of users, their volume, and three years instead of one.
3. Which supplier usually wins?
The one that answers clearly, without changing the subject, and not the cheapest page.
Listening 2 · The Sales Director

Cheaper Is Not the Question

A sales director on the day a customer showed him a cheaper competitor. Sound first — no text.

1. What was his first reaction, and what did he do instead?
He wanted to offer a discount, but he asked for one hour to build a comparison on the same basis.
2. What happened to the price difference by the third year?
The competitor was twenty percent more expensive.
3. What did he send the customer?
A one-page table, with one difference per line and one sentence under each line.
