Chapter 4: The Code

Four Futures, and How Decided Each One Sounds

will, going to, present continuous, future perfect

Grammar Discovery

Which of These Is Already Decided?

"The market will be harder in 2028 than it is now."

"I am going to professionalize the company either way."

"I am applying in October."

"By 2029 I will have handed the site work to somebody else."

All four are about the future and no two of them mean the same thing. Put them in order, from the one where least has been decided to the one where most has, and notice what the blue time expression is doing in the two sentences that have one.

JobFormExample
A predictionwill + base formThe market will be harder in 2028.
A decision already takenbe going to + base formI am going to professionalize the company.
An arrangement with a datepresent continuous + timeI am applying in October.
Finished by a future pointby + date, will have + participleBy 2029 I will have handed it over.
Scheduled by somebodybe due to + base formInterviews are due to start in October.
A timetablepresent simple, for fixed schedulesThe residency starts on the fourth.
The trapwill is the weakest of the fourNot I will apply in October, if it is already arranged.

In one line: will predicts, going to has decided, the present continuous has a date, and the future perfect looks back from one.

Chapter 5: Real Documents

One Page, Two Voices

The three-year road map, an essay reader and somebody two years out

Real Document

The Three-Year Road Map

ROAD MAP — F. GATTAZ
submitted with the statement of intent
October 2026admissions interviews — due to start
January 2027enrolling, if admitted
Year 1writing the pricing process down — deliverable
Year 2bringing in a commercial director
Capstoneexposure to a single client — measured, then reduced
By 2029site work handed over
Every line on this map has a date and a thing, and the two are not the same. The interviews are due to start in October, which is the school's decision. Year one has a deliverable that exists whether or not the application succeeds, which is the line the reader believes most. And by 2029 the site work will have been handed over, which is not a promise about the program: it is a promise about the company.
1. Which line on this map is not the candidate's decision, and how can you tell?
The October interviews: they are due to start, which is the school's schedule and not his.
2. According to the note, which line does the reader believe most, and why?
The year one deliverable, because it exists whether or not the application succeeds.
3. What does the last line promise, and what does it not promise?
It promises something about the company: that the site work will have been handed over by 2029. It does not promise anything about the program itself.
Listening 1 · An Essay Reader

How I Know in Two Paragraphs

Somebody who reads goals essays for a living, on what gives a candidate away. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say she can tell in two paragraphs, and what gives it away?
Whether the candidate has a plan or a wish. The verbs give it away: an essay written entirely in will is a wish with a date on it.
2. What is the test she applies to the first goal in every essay?
Whether it would still happen if the candidate were not admitted. If nothing on the page survives that question, the program is being used as the plan.
3. What does she say about the long-term goal at the end of the essay?
That nobody expects it to come true, and that what is being read is whether the candidate can build a road map that connects it to something they are doing on Monday.
Listening 2 · Two Years Out

What I Wrote and What Happened

Somebody comparing his own goals essay with the two years that followed it. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say about the long-term goal he wrote down?
That it did not happen, that it was never going to, and that nobody has ever asked him about it since.
2. Which part of the essay turned out to matter, and why?
The first-year deliverable, writing the pricing process down. It was incremental and dull, and it is the thing that changed the company.
3. What does he say he would write differently now?
He would put fewer wills in it. He says he wrote about a future he was predicting rather than about the two or three things he had already decided.
