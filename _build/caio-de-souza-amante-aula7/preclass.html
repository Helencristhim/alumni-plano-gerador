Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

From Founder to Seller -- The Language of What Changed

A buyer is not buying what you still do. He is buying what you stopped doing, and when. Key words: to bootstrap, a hire, to step back, a successor, burnout, to delegate, a blind spot, to outgrow. Structure: used to and would for past habits and states.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To bootstrap -- to build a company using only its own revenue, with no outside money
"We bootstrapped the whole thing, so there was never anybody else to do the work."
A hire -- a person you bring into the company, seen as a decision
"The commercial director was the hire I did not want to make."
To step back -- to stop being involved in running something day to day
"Who runs this company on the Monday after you step back?"
A successor -- the person who takes your job when you leave it
"There is no single successor. Three people each own a piece of it."
Burnout -- the exhaustion that comes from working too hard for too long
"I was two months from burnout and I did not know the word yet."
To delegate -- to give a task or a decision to somebody else to handle
"I did not use to delegate anything on the technical side."
A blind spot -- a weakness in your own judgement that you cannot see yourself
"I have the same blind spot. I still read every proposal."
To outgrow -- to become too big for something that used to fit
"The company outgrew the way I used to run it, and that is the good news."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A buyer looking at a founder-led company asks one question that decides everything else: does this business still need the person who built it? He will not ask it in those words. He will ask what you used to do, and he will listen for what you no longer do. The list of things you handed over is the only evidence he accepts, and it needs a form: I used to sign every contract myself, and I stopped in 2019.

There are two forms for a life you have left behind, and they are not interchangeable. Used to plus the bare verb covers everything: a habit you had and stopped, and also a situation that is simply no longer true. I used to write every proposal. The company used to be six people in one room. Would plus the bare verb covers only the first of those two. It works for an action you repeated, usually with a time marker attached: every Friday I would sit with the engineers until the build passed. It does not work for a state. The company would be six people is not English.

One event, on one date, takes neither. It takes the plain past simple: in 2019 we hired our first commercial director. And the negative and the question drop the final letter, which is the detail almost everybody gets wrong in writing: I did not use to delegate, not did not used to.

The expensive confusion is with a completely different structure that looks almost identical. I am used to working alone means it feels normal to me now. I used to work alone means it does not happen any more. One is about the present and takes the -ing form; the other is about the past and takes the bare verb. Saying the wrong one in a diligence meeting tells the buyer the opposite of what you meant. And the permanent target does not disappear either: after used to and after would, the verb carries no ending at all. Not he used to signs. He used to sign.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used To and Would for Past Habits and States

GRAMMAR

Used to works for everything you left behind. Would only works for things you did.

JobFormExample
A habit you had and stoppedused to + bare verbI used to sign every contract myself.
A situation that is no longer trueused to onlyThe company used to be six people. Not would be.
A repeated action you rememberwould + bare verb, with a time markerEvery Friday I would sit with the engineers.
One event, on one dateplain past simpleIn 2019 we hired our first commercial director.
Negative and questiondid not use to / did you use toNot did not used to. Say I did not use to delegate.
The trapbe used to + -ing is another structureI am used to working alone = normal now. I used to work alone = not any more.
The permanent targetafter used to and would, no endingNot he used to signs. Say he used to sign.

Never: I did not used to · the company would be six people · he used to signs · I am used to sign every contract
