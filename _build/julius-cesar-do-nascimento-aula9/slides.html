Chapter 3: The Code

She Said That... He Asked Whether...

Their words, your report

Grammar Discovery

What Changed?

"Anna said that they couldn’t accept the new lead time."

"She asked whether we could ship from Joinville."

"Ravi asked why the price was higher."

"The French team suggested waiting a month."

When we report what someone said, the verb usually moves one step into the past (can → could, is → was, will → would). Reported questions lose the question order and the question mark. And the reporting verb itself tells the listener a lot: said, told, asked, suggested, warned, promised.

PatternFormExample
Statementssaid (that) / told someone (that) + one step back in the pastShe told us that they couldn’t accept it.
Yes / no questionsasked if / whether + statement orderHe asked whether we could ship from Joinville.
Wh- questionsasked + wh- word + statement orderRavi asked why the price was higher.
Suggestionssuggested + -ing / suggested that we (should) + verbThey suggested waiting a month.
Other verbspromised to, agreed to, warned that, explained thatAnna promised to send the data on Friday.
The trapsay has no person after it; tell always has oneNot “He said me that...” Say He told me that... or He said that...

In one line: one step back in the past; questions in statement order; said that, told me that, asked whether, suggested -ing.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Call With Tokyo

A report, three voices and the minutes

Dialogue

David Wants the Full Picture

D
Morning, Julius. I read your email, but can you brief me before I talk to the board? What did the customers say?
J
Of course, David. Let me summarize. The German team said that they couldn’t accept the new lead time. They pushed back quite hard.
D
Did they suggest anything?
J
Yes. Anna asked whether we could ship part of the volume from Joinville. And the Indian team raised a concern about quality. Ravi asked why the last batch had been late.
D
And was there a consensus at the end?
J
Not completely. The French team suggested waiting a month, so we postponed the final decision. But everybody agreed to share their forecasts by Friday.
D
Good. What do you need from us here in Tokyo?
J
Two things. The board needs to sign off on the Joinville plan, and I’ll send you the minutes with all the action items today.
Comprehension

About David

1. What does David want before he talks to the board?
A briefing from Julius about what the customers said.
2. What does David ask about the end of the meeting?
Whether there was a consensus.
3. What does David ask Julius at the end?
What he needs from headquarters in Tokyo.

Listening 3 · An American Executive Coach

The Verb Tells the Story

An American coach explains why reporting verbs matter more than people think. Sound first — no text.

1. Which verb does she say people use too much?
Said.
2. What is the difference between suggested and insisted?
Suggested is an idea; insisted means the person will not change their mind.
3. What does she advise when you are not sure what someone meant?
To ask them before you report it.
