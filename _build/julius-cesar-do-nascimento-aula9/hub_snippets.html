Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

The minutes -- the written record of what was said and decided in a meeting
"I will send the minutes to everyone this afternoon."
To brief -- to give someone the information they need before they act
"I briefed the CEO before the call with Tokyo."
To summarize -- to give only the main points of something, in a few words
"Let me summarize the three main decisions."
A takeaway -- the main idea or lesson that you remember from a meeting
"The key takeaway is that the customer wants faster delivery."
A stakeholder -- a person or group that is affected by a decision or a project
"We need to inform all the stakeholders before Friday."
To raise a concern -- to say that you are worried about something
"The German team raised a concern about the new lead time."
To push back -- to resist an idea or a request and say that you do not accept it
"The customer pushed back on the price."
A consensus -- an agreement among all or most of the people in a group
"After two hours, we reached a consensus."
To postpone -- to move an event or a decision to a later time
"They postponed the decision until next month."
To escalate -- to send a problem to a higher level of management
"If they don’t answer, we will escalate it to headquarters."
An action item -- a task that someone must do after a meeting
"There are four action items, and two of them are mine."
To sign off on -- to give official approval to a plan or a document
"Tokyo has to sign off on the new budget."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Thursday, Julius had a long meeting with five teams. The next morning, he briefed headquarters in Tokyo. “The German team said that they couldn’t accept the new lead time,” he explained. “They pushed back very hard.”

He also said that the Indian team had raised a concern about quality. “Ravi asked why the last batch had been late. And Anna asked whether we could ship from Joinville.”

There was no consensus. The French team suggested waiting a month, so they postponed the decision. Julius promised to send the minutes with all the action items the same day.

1. What did the German team say?
A That they wanted to postpone the meeting until the French team had sent all the forecasts for next year.
B That the quality was bad.
C That they couldn’t accept the new lead time.
2. Why does the text say “asked whether we could ship” and not “asked could we ship?”
A Because a reported question uses whether or if and the normal statement order, without a question mark.
B Because whether is the formal word for weather, and it is used in all reports to headquarters.
C Because Anna was angry.
3. What did Julius promise to do?
A To call each team again on Friday and ask every person to repeat what they had said in the meeting.
B To send the minutes with all the action items the same day.
C To escalate the problem.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

When you report what someone said, move the verb one step into the past (can → could, is → was, will → would). Questions use if, whether or a wh- word, with statement order. Choose the reporting verb with care: said, told, asked, suggested, promised, warned.

PatternFormExample
Statementssaid (that) / told someone (that) + one step back in the pastShe told us that they couldn’t accept it.
Yes / no questionsasked if / whether + statement orderHe asked whether we could ship from Joinville.
Wh- questionsasked + wh- word + statement orderRavi asked why the price was higher.
Suggestionssuggested + -ing / suggested that we (should) + verbThey suggested waiting a month.
Other verbspromised to, agreed to, warned that, explained thatAnna promised to send the data on Friday.
The trapsay has no person after it; tell always has oneNot “He said me that...” Say He told me that... or He said that...

Never: He said me that... · She asked whether could we... · He asked why was the price... · They suggested to wait...
