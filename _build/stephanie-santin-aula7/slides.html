Chapter 3: The Code

One Word Changes the Whole Instruction

Advice, obligation, prohibition, possibility

Discover the Rule

What Do These Have in Common?

You should see a specialist before you decide.
Patients must not eat before the procedure.
She must be in pain. She has not moved since she arrived.
You might want to get a second opinion.
Click to reveal the rule
After a modal the verb never changes: no -s, no to, no -ing. She must be, never she must to be. Each modal carries a different force: should = advice; must / have to = obligation; must not = prohibition; might / may / could = possibility. And must does two jobs: obligation (Patients must not eat) and logical conclusion (She must be in pain — I am almost sure). Must usually comes from inside the room; have to from a rule outside it.

Comprehension

About the Patient

1. What is the patient confused about?
Whether he can take his blood pressure pill the next morning.
2. What did his wife and his daughter say?
His wife said he had to stop everything, and his daughter asked whether he could drive himself home.
3. What does he want to know about pain during the night?
Whether he should wait until the morning.
