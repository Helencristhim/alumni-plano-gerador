Chapter 3: The Code

The Question You Cannot Rehearse

would — the verb for something that has not happened yet

Grammar Discovery

Which of These Actually Happened?

"What would you do if the team disagreed with you?"

"I would start by asking what they can see that I cannot."

"That wouldn’t be a problem for me."

"I took ownership of the migration and I stayed until it worked."

Three of them never happened: they are imagined, and they all run on the same little word. Only the blue one is a fact from your life.

UseFormExample
An imagined actionwould + base verbI would start by asking why.
The questionWhat would + you + base verb + if + past simpleWhat would you do if the deadline moved?
Spoken, shortI’d / you’d / we’d / they’dI’d talk to each person first.
Negativewould not / wouldn’t + base verbI wouldn’t wait until the last week.
Softer, more carefulwould say / would like / would preferI would say it took about two weeks.
The two sidesif + past simple, would + base verbIf they disagreed, I would ask why.

In one line: would + the naked verb, and the if side goes to the past.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

One Hour With the Hiring Manager

And three voices you would hear before and after it

Dialogue

The Interview With Paul

P
Thanks for making the time, Gisele. Let us start simple — walk me through your background.
G
I spent eighteen years at a bank in Brazil. I was responsible for the internal systems, and for the last six years I took ownership of the migration project.
P
That is a real track record. Tell me about a time when you made something simpler for the people who actually used it.
G
We streamlined the approval process. It went from six days to two, and the stakeholders were branch managers, not engineers, so I had to explain it in their words.
P
Good. Now, I see a gap after the bank. What have the last few months been?
G
A career break. My husband was transferred to Washington, I worked out my notice period, and we moved with our daughters. The break was a decision, not an accident.
P
Fair enough. One hypothetical, then the last five minutes are yours: what would you do if the team disagreed with you about a deadline?
G
I would ask what they can see that I cannot. If the date still had to move, I would say it early — I wouldn’t wait until the last week. And my question: what does onboarding look like here?
Comprehension

About Paul

1. What are the first two things Paul asks for, before any hypothetical question?
He asks her to walk him through her background, and then to tell him about a time when she made something simpler for the people who used it.
2. What does Paul call the eighteen years at the bank?
A real track record.
3. Paul sees a gap after the bank. How does he react to the answer, and what does he do next?
He says fair enough, and moves straight on to one hypothetical question. Then he gives her the last five minutes to ask him things.
Listening 1 · The Recruiter

How This Actually Works

Three steps, three weeks, and the mistake half the candidates make. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What are the three steps, and which one does she say matters?
A twenty-minute call with her, then an hour with the hiring manager, Paul, and last a short chat with two people from the team. The hour with the hiring manager is the one that matters.
2. What does she look at first, and what does she not care about?
She looks at what you took ownership of and whether you can explain it in plain words. She does not look at the school.
3. What is the biggest mistake she sees, and what does she ask at the end of every call?
Candidates who never ask anything. At the end she always says: do you have any questions for me.
Listening 2 · Voicemail

A Message You Cannot Replay in Real Life

A recruiter, a job, two things by email and a deadline. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Which job is he calling about, and what is the good news?
The systems analyst role in Washington, the hybrid one, applied to on Tuesday. The good news is that the hiring manager wants to talk.
2. What are the two things he needs by email, and why one page?
A one-page resume and a line or two about the notice period, or about when she could actually start. One page because, in his words, nobody reads three.
3. When will he call again, and what is the deadline for the email?
He will call tomorrow morning, around ten her time. The email has to arrive by Thursday at five, because the team closes the list on Friday.
Listening 3 · An Opinion

Stop Apologizing for the Gap

Why starting over in another country is the hardest project on any resume. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she say does more damage: the gap itself, or something else?
The apology. She says she hears I am sorry, I was out for a year in every second interview, and the apology does more damage than the year ever did.
2. What does she say people actually want to know?
Whether you are still the person who takes ownership of things. Eighteen months at home does not change that.
3. Why does she call moving to another country a project, and what does she call those abilities?
New systems, a new language, a whole family to move, everything late and nothing where you left it. She calls those transferable skills, the ones that never show up on a resume.
Real Document

The Job Posting

JOB POSTING — HALSTON GROUP
Systems Analyst · Washington, DC (hybrid)
Posted
6 days ago
WHAT YOU’LL DO
Take ownership of two internal systems used by 400 people
Streamline the approval process, end to end
Be responsible for the monthly release notes the branch teams actually read
WHAT WE’RE LOOKING FOR
Five years or more with internal, back-office systems
A track record of finishing what you start
Someone who can explain a technical decision to a non-technical stakeholder
NICE TO HAVE
A second language
Experience in banking or insurance
Transferable skills from a different industry — tell us about them
START DATE / NOTICE PERIOD
Start as soon as your notice period allows. Career breaks are not a problem — just tell us when you can start. Onboarding: two weeks, mostly remote.
1. Somebody on a career break has no notice period. What does this posting ask that person to do?
Just say when you can start. The posting says career breaks are not a problem.
2. Which line is about explaining things to people who are not technical, and what does it call those people?
Someone who can explain a technical decision to a non-technical stakeholder.
3. You never worked in insurance. Does that stop you? Which section is that line in?
No. It is under Nice to have, not under What we are looking for — and the same section asks for transferable skills from a different industry.
