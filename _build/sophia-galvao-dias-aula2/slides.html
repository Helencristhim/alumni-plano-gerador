Grammar Discovery

Why does the little word move?

I always get up at six.
I am always at school by seven thirty.
She hardly ever watches television.
She is hardly ever late.
I play basketball twice a week.

The verb decides where it goes

One-word adverbs of frequency (always, usually, often, sometimes, hardly ever, never) come before the main verb — but after the verb to be. Expressions with a number (twice a week, every day, once in a while) go at the end of the sentence, or right at the start.

The Code

One rule, and a scale

Where it goes

I always get up early.
I am always tired.
She never watches it.
She is never late.
I swim twice a week.

The scale

always — 100%
usually — almost every time
often — a lot
sometimes — now and then
hardly ever — almost never
never — 0%

Careful: never is already negative. You say I never watch it, never I don't never watch it.

Sound Check

Three words that shrink

I usually ride my bike to school.

"usually" is three syllables in real speech, not four

I hardly ever take the bike out.

"hardly ever" runs together into one block of sound

She never watches it on television.

"watches" gains a whole extra syllable — watch-iz
