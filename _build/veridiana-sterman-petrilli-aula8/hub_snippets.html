Two Hotels, One Choice -- Comparing Out Loud

Two hotels, nine nights, forty dollars a night of difference — and the number everybody looks at is the wrong one. Key words: a rate, a neighborhood, a review, within walking distance, a trade-off, convenient, noisy, value for money, a shuttle, to book up. Structure: comparatives and superlatives.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A rate -- the price of one night, before anything else is added
"The rate is a hundred and eighty, and breakfast is not included."
A neighborhood -- one part of a city, with its own character
"Three streets inland is a completely different neighborhood."
A review -- what a previous guest wrote about a place
"Read the reviews in the middle, not the best and not the worst."
Within walking distance -- close enough to reach on foot, without transport
"Everything is within walking distance, so we never took a taxi."
A trade-off -- when you gain one thing and lose another at the same time
"Quieter but further away: that is the trade-off."
Convenient -- easy to reach and easy to use, so it saves you trouble
"The ocean hotel is more convenient, and that is all it is."
Noisy -- full of sound that stops you resting
"It is noisy until one in the morning, every night of the week."
Value for money -- what you get for the price you paid, taken together
"It is not the cheapest, but it is the best value for money."
A shuttle -- a free bus that runs between two fixed places
"There is a free shuttle every thirty minutes until eleven."
To book up -- to fill with bookings until nothing is left
"That week books up fast, so decide before the end of the month."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Anybody can put two things side by side. The skill is saying which one wins, and why, in one sentence. English does that with two shapes, and which shape you use depends on one thing only: how long the adjective is.

Short adjectives take -er: cheap becomes cheaper, quiet becomes quieter, big becomes bigger. Long adjectives take more in front: more convenient, more expensive, more comfortable. And the two never happen at the same time. More cheaper is the most common mistake a Portuguese speaker makes here, because in Portuguese the little word is always there. In English it disappears the moment the adjective is short.

The other half of the sentence is than, and it is not optional: "the inland hotel is cheaper than the one on the ocean". When you compare against everything, not against one other thing, you get the superlative, and it always takes the: the cheapest room, the noisiest street, the most expensive hotel on the block.

Two more things. To say two things are equal, English puts the adjective naked between two as: the rooms are as clean as each other. To say one is less, it just adds not: not as close as. And a handful of adjectives ignore all of this: good becomes better and the best, bad becomes worse and the worst, far becomes further. One is convenient, the other is quieter and better value for money: that is the trade-off, and naming it is what turns a list into a decision.

1. What decides whether an adjective takes -er or more?
A Whether the thing is a place or an object.
B How long the adjective is.
C Whether the sentence is positive or negative.
2. Why is "more cheaper" wrong?
A Because -er and more never happen at the same time.
B Because cheap is an irregular adjective.
C Because it needs the in front of it.
3. How does English say that two things are equal?
A With more plus the adjective plus than.
B With the adjective between two as: as clean as.
C With the plus the adjective plus -est.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Comparatives and Superlatives

GRAMMAR

Short words take -er and -est. Long words take more and the most. Never both.

JobFormExample
Short adjective, comparing twoadjective + -er + thancheaper than, quieter than, bigger than
Long adjective, comparing twomore + adjective + thanmore convenient than, more expensive than
Short adjective, out of allthe + adjective + -estthe cheapest, the noisiest
Long adjective, out of allthe most + adjectivethe most expensive hotel on the street
Equal, and lessas + adjective + as · not as + adjective + asThe rooms are as clean as each other.
The irregular onesgood → better → the best · bad → worse → the worst · far → furtherThe location is better, the sleep is worse.
Spellingbig → bigger · noisy → noisier · nice → nicerDouble the last letter, or turn the y into i.

Never: more cheap · more bigger · most expensive hotel (without the) · quieter the other one (without than)

Stage 2: Put the Decision in Order

Order

Somebody asks which hotel to book. Put your answer in a logical order.

?"They are very close in price, but not as close as they look."
?"The inland hotel is cheaper and quieter than the one on the ocean."
?"The other one is more convenient, but that is the only thing."
?"It is not as close to the beach, but there is a free shuttle."
?"So the trade-off is really about the walk after eleven at night."
?"I would take the quieter one, because it is the best value for money."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to compare the two hotels on price. You say:
A "The inland hotel is more cheap than the other one."
B "The inland hotel is cheaper than the one on the ocean."
C "The inland hotel is cheaper the one on the ocean."
Now you compare them on convenience. You say:
A "The ocean hotel is more convenient than the inland one."
B "The ocean hotel is convenienter than the inland one."
C "The ocean hotel is the more convenient of the inland one."
Out of every hotel on the street, one is the most expensive. You say:
A "It is most expensive hotel on the street."
B "It is the most expensive hotel on the street."
C "It is the more expensive hotel of the street."
You have listed the differences. Now you finish. You say:
A "So, both are good. It depends."
B "I would take the quieter one, because it is the best value for money."
C "The quieter is more better for the money."
