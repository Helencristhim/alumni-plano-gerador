Lesson 10 -- Pre-class

Reporting What Was Said -- Other People as Evidence

"Somebody said it is hard" and "my coach, who has sent four players abroad, told me it was hard for the first month" carry the same claim. Only one of them survives a follow-up question. Key words: to quote somebody, to paraphrase, a source, to claim, to admit, to insist, to point out, to take somebody's word for it, firsthand, to put words in somebody's mouth. Structure: reported speech.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To quote somebody -- to repeat somebody's exact words, without changing any of them
"If you quote her, use her words. If you change one, you are doing something else."
To paraphrase -- to say somebody's idea in your own words, without changing what it means
"Most of the time you paraphrase, and that is fine, as long as you say whose idea it was."
A source -- the person or place a piece of information originally came from
"An argument with no source is an opinion wearing a suit."
To claim -- to say that something is true, without having proved it yet
"He claimed the course was easy, and nobody in the room had taken it."
To admit -- to say something true that does not help your own side
"She admitted that the first term had been worse than she expected, which is why I believed the rest."
To insist -- to say the same thing again, because the other person has not accepted it
"He insisted that the certificate could wait, and he was wrong about that."
To point out -- to draw attention to a fact the other person has missed
"She pointed out that the deposit was due before the scholarship letter arrived."
To take somebody's word for it -- to accept what somebody said without checking it yourself
"I took his word for it, and that is the only part of the year I would do differently."
Firsthand -- from your own experience, not from somebody who told you about it
"I know that firsthand, which is a sentence very few people in the room can say."
To put words in somebody's mouth -- to say that somebody said something they never actually said
"He never said the course was easy. Do not put words in his mouth in an argument you can win honestly."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Somebody says something to you today, and tomorrow you carry it into an argument. Two things happen on the way. The first is a choice of verb. Say never takes a person after it and tell always does: she said that the campus was huge, or she told me that the campus was huge, but never she said me. And beyond those two there is a whole set of verbs that carry meaning of their own: she admitted that, he claimed that, he insisted that, she pointed out that. Choosing between them is not decoration; it tells the listener what kind of thing was said.

The second thing is that the verb takes one step backwards in time. The first month is the hardest becomes he told me the first month was the hardest. I will call you becomes she said she would call me. It was worse than I expected becomes she admitted it had been worse than she expected. When the thing is still true today, keeping the present is also fine: he said the campus is huge, and it still is.

Questions do something extra, and it is the part almost everybody gets wrong. A reported question is not a question any more, so it loses its inversion. Where do you live? becomes he asked me where I lived, never where did I live. With no question word, use if or whether: she asked if I had the certificate. And one thing that is not grammar at all: when you carry somebody's words, do not improve them. Changing a quote so that it fits your argument better is the fastest way to lose an argument you could have won.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

The verb steps back, the question loses its inversion, and the reporting verb says what kind of thing was said.

JobFormExample
Say and tellsay (that) · tell + somebody + (that)She told me that the campus was huge.
One step back in timeis → was · will → would · can → could · did → had doneShe said she would call me in March.
Reporting verbs that carry meaningadmit · claim · insist · point out · explain + thatShe admitted that it had been worse than she expected.
Reported questions with a question wordasked + (somebody) + where / why / when + normal orderHe asked me where I lived.
Reported yes-or-no questionsasked + (somebody) + if / whether + normal orderShe asked if I had the certificate.
When you keep the presentwhen it is still true now, the step back is optionalHe said the campus is huge, and it still is.
Attribution that countsname + role + reporting verbMy coach, who has sent four players abroad, told me that...

Never: he said me that · she told that · he asked me where did I live · my coach said that he will call me.

Stage 2: Put the Answer in Order

Order

Somebody has asked whether studying abroad is worth it. Put your answer in order, from the sources to the one thing only you can say.

?"My coach, who has sent four players abroad, told me that the football would be the easy part."
?"A student in her second year admitted that the first term had been worse than she expected."
?"She said she would still go again, and that is why I believed the first half."
?"An adviser pointed out that the paperwork had to be finished before March."
?"A friend insisted that I would be fluent in three months, and he had never left the country."
?"And the part I know firsthand is that six weeks is how long settling in takes."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You are reporting what your coach told you. You say:
A "He said me that the football would be the easy part."
B "He told me that the football would be the easy part."
C "He told that the football will be the easy part."
Somebody asked you a question yesterday and you are reporting it now. You say:
A "He asked me where did I live."
B "He asked me where I lived."
C "He asked me where do I live."
A student said something that did not help her own argument. You report it. You say:
A "She admitted that the first term had been worse than she expected."
B "She claimed that the first term had been worse than she expected."
C "She said me the first term was worse."
You want your claim to survive a follow-up question. You say:
A "People say the first month is the hardest."
B "Everybody knows the first month is the hardest."
C "My coach, who has sent four players abroad, told me the first month would be the hardest."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

The last open question of this block: is studying abroad worth it for somebody who could stay at home and get the same degree? Take a position, use three people you have actually spoken to as evidence, name each of them and say why they would know, add the one thing you know firsthand, and answer the strongest objection before it is made. Two minutes, and no sentence beginning with people say.
