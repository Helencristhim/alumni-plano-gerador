Chapter 4: The Code

What Happens to a Sentence When You Carry It

One step back, and one word order

Grammar Discovery

What Moved, and What Turned Around

"The first month is the hardest." → He told me that the first month was the hardest.

"I will call you in March." → She said that she would call me in March.

"It was worse than I expected." → She admitted that it had been worse than she expected.

"Where do you live?" → He asked me where I lived.

Every blue verb took one step backwards in time. And in the last line something else happened, which has nothing to do with tense: look at the order of the words after where.

JobFormExample
Say and tellsay (that) · tell + somebody + (that)He told me that... · She said that...
One step back in timeis → was · will → would · can → could · did → had doneShe said she would call me in March.
Reporting verbs that carry meaningadmit · claim · insist · point out · explain + thatShe admitted that it had been worse than she expected.
Reported questions with a question wordasked + (somebody) + where / why / how + normal orderHe asked me where I lived.
Reported yes-or-no questionsasked + (somebody) + if / whether + normal orderShe asked if I had the certificate.
When you keep the presentwhen it is still true now, the step back is optionalHe said the campus is huge, and it still is.
The three trapssay never takes a person · tell always does · a reported question is not invertedNot he said me: say he told me. Not where did I live: say where I lived.

In one line: the verb steps back, the question loses its inversion, and the reporting verb is where you say what kind of thing was said.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Three People, Three Answers

One page of quotes, two recordings

Real Document

What Three People Actually Said

EXCHANGE PROGRAMME — EXIT INTERVIEWS
three quotes, recorded with names and roles
The coach · eleven seasons, four players sent abroad
"Nobody fails because of the football. They fail because they arrive in September and wait until March to speak to anybody."
The student · second year, came back and went again
"The first term was worse than I expected, and I would still do it again. Both of those are true."
The adviser · fifteen years in admissions
"You do not need the certificate to start the application. You do need it before the first of March, and every year somebody tells me nobody told them."
Recorded with permissionnames and roles included on purpose
Note from the programme: we publish the role next to every quote. A sentence with a role attached can be checked, argued with and disagreed with. The same sentence with nobody attached to it is just a rumour that happens to be well written.
1. The coach and the adviser both describe the same failure. What is it, and how does each of them describe it?
Waiting too long to speak to somebody. The coach says students arrive in September and wait until March; the adviser says every year somebody tells him that nobody told them about the deadline.
2. The student says two things that seem to contradict each other. Why is that quote stronger than one without the contradiction?
Because she admits something that does not help her side. A person who says the term was worse than expected and would still go again is easier to believe than one who only says it was good.
3. According to the note, why does the programme publish the role next to every quote?
Because a sentence with a role attached can be checked, argued with and disagreed with. Without anybody attached, it is a rumour that happens to be well written.
Listening 1 · The Debate Coach

The Weakest Three Words in Any Argument

A debate coach on attribution. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say is wrong with the phrase people say?
That it cannot be checked or disagreed with, so the other side can simply say that other people say the opposite, and the argument goes nowhere.
2. What does she say a name plus a role does to the same sentence?
It makes the sentence something the other person has to answer, and she says the role matters more than the name, because it explains why that person would know.
3. What warning does she give about reporting somebody else’s words?
Not to put words in their mouth. She says an argument you win by improving somebody’s quote is one you lose the moment anybody checks it.
Listening 2 · Three Pieces of Advice

Which One Turned Out to Be Wrong

Somebody reporting what he was told before he left. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the three people tell him, and which of the three was wrong?
His coach told him the football would be the easy part, a friend told him he would be fluent in three months, and an adviser told him to sort the paperwork early. The friend was wrong.
2. Why does he say he believed the wrong one?
Because it was the one he wanted to hear, and because his friend had never been abroad, so there was nothing behind the claim except confidence.
3. What does he say he does now before he takes advice?
He asks the person how they know. He says it is one question, it takes four seconds, and it separates the people who did the thing from the people who have opinions about it.
