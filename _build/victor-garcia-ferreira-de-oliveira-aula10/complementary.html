Talk
TED — Michael Shermer, "Why people believe weird things" (13 min)

A sceptic on how a claim gets accepted without a source, and how easy it is to do in good faith. It is the other side of this lesson: not how to attribute, but what happens when nobody does.

Tip: watch it and then take one thing you believe about studying abroad. Ask yourself who told you, and whether they had a reason to know.

Podcast
BBC 6 Minute English — "How are influencers affecting politics?"

Six minutes on what happens when a claim travels without a source attached to it, which is the exact failure this lesson is built to prevent. Free, with the full transcript on the same page.

Tip: listen once, then find every reporting verb in the transcript. Notice how often the presenters say who said something, and what changes when they do not.

Video
TED on YouTube — Amy Cuddy, "Your body language may shape who you are" (21 min)

A talk built almost entirely out of reported research and reported conversations, which makes it a live demonstration of everything in this lesson. Notice how she names a source before every claim that matters.

Tip: count how many times she reports what somebody else said or found. Then say one of her claims out loud in your own words, with the attribution intact.
