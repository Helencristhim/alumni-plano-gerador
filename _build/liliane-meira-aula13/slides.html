Chapter 3: The Code

The Important Word Goes Last

Information order and emphasis

Grammar Discovery

Where Is the Important Word?

"What I make are sound works from hospital wards."

"It was a nurse who started it."

"What matters to me is not the illness but the language around it."

"The reason I record at night is that nobody else does."

The red words carry no new information. Their job is to push the important part to the end, or to point a finger at it.

RuleFormExample
What-cleftWhat + subject + verb + is/are + FOCUSWhat I make are sound works.
It-cleftIt is/was + FOCUS + who/that + restIt was a nurse who started it.
Reason and thingThe reason / The thing / All + clause + is (that)The reason I record at night is that nobody else does.
End focusold information first, new information lastMy work is about care. → What my work is about is care.
Contrastnot X but Y at the endWhat matters is not the image but the voice.
The trapno second subject after the clausenot What I do it is..., not It was a nurse which...

In one line: start with what the listener already knows, and let the new word arrive last.

Grammar Practice

Move It to the End

Say it first, then click to check

"I record voices in hospitals. → ___What I record are voices in hospitals." (what-cleft)
"A nurse asked the first question. → It was a nurse ___who asked the first question." (it-cleft)
"I care about the language. → ___What I care about is the language." (what-cleft)
"In 2015 I started. → ___It was in 2015 that I started." (it-cleft, time)
"Nobody records the night, so I do. → ___The reason I record the night is that nobody else does." (reason)
Chapter 4: Real Conversations

So, What Do You Do?

One conversation, two recordings, one speaker card

Dialogue

Thirty Seconds at the Fair

M
Liliane, isn’t it? I saw your name on the list for the Conversations program. So, what do you do?
L
What I make are sound works from hospital wards. I record how patients and caregivers talk about pain.
M
That is quite a pitch. How did you end up in hospitals?
L
It was a nurse who started it. She asked me why nobody ever records the night shift, and I had no answer.
M
And what do you want people to take away from the work?
L
What matters to me is not the illness but the language around it. That is the one idea I want people to keep.
M
I am putting together a show about care for next spring, in Rotterdam. Do you have something short I could read?
L
I do. I will send you a concise statement tonight, and a tagline for the catalog, if you need one.

Listening 1 · A Gallerist

A Hundred Introductions a Day

Somebody who runs a booth at art fairs, on the introductions she remembers. Sound first — no text.

1. Where do most introductions start, according to her, and what happens?
With education, the first exhibition or the city. By the time they reach the work, she has stopped listening.
2. What did the woman she remembers say first?
What I make are sound pieces from hospital wards. It was the whole first sentence, and she asked three questions.
3. Who usually gets the second conversation?
The person who stops talking first.
Listening 2 · A Speaking Coach

The Word That Arrives Last

Somebody who coaches researchers and artists, on the order of information. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say the problem almost never is, and what is it instead?
It is almost never vocabulary. It is the order of the information.
2. Why is a list of topics weaker than what holds all my work together is care?
A list gives the topics but not the point. In the second, the important word arrives last, and it lands.
3. What does a sentence that begins with it was tell the listener?
Of all the people, notice this one: it points at the important part.
