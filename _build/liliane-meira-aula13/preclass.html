Lesson 13 -- Pre-class

Introducing Yourself in Thirty Seconds -- The Timed Pitch

A thirty-second introduction at an art fair, and the grammar that puts the important word last. Key words: a pitch, to hook, concise, to ramble, a takeaway, a tagline, to come across as, to tailor, understated, memorable, self-promotion, to get to the point. Structure: cleft sentences and information order (what I do is, it was... who).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A pitch -- a short spoken presentation designed to make someone interested in you or your work
"Her pitch lasted thirty seconds, and the curator asked for her card."
To hook -- to catch someone’s attention in the very first seconds
"Your first sentence has to hook the listener, not explain your CV."
Concise -- short and clear, with no unnecessary words
"A concise introduction leaves room for questions."
To ramble -- to talk for too long without a clear point
"When I am nervous, I ramble about my education."
A takeaway -- the one idea that people remember after they leave
"The takeaway of my work is that pain has a language."
A tagline -- a short, memorable phrase that sums up your work
"The catalog needs a tagline of no more than eight words."
To come across as -- to give a particular impression to other people
"I want to come across as confident, not arrogant."
To tailor -- to adapt something for a particular person or audience
"I tailor my pitch for a curator and for a doctor in different ways."
Understated -- quiet and modest, never exaggerated
"Her introduction was understated, and that made it memorable."
Memorable -- easy to remember because it is special or clear
"One memorable image is worth more than five dates."
Self-promotion -- talking about your own achievements to get attention or work
"Many artists hate self-promotion, but a pitch is not bragging."
To get to the point -- to say the most important thing without delay
"Get to the point in the first sentence, and the rest will follow."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At a fair, a curator asked Liliane what she does. She did not ramble about her education. What she said was: what I make are sound works from hospital wards. It was a concise pitch, and it hooked him.

When he asked how it began, she answered: it was a nurse who started it. The sentence points at one person, and the important word, nurse, is the one he remembered.

Her takeaway came last: what matters to me is not the illness but the language around it. She came across as calm and understated, and the curator asked for something to read.

1. "What I make are sound works from hospital wards." Where is the new information?
A At the start, in what I make, because that is the subject of the sentence.
B At the end: sound works from hospital wards.
C Nowhere, because the sentence only repeats the question.
2. "It was a nurse who started it." Why does she use it was... who?
A Because the sentence is about the past, and it was is the past of it is.
B To point at one person: a nurse, and not somebody else.
C Because English sentences always need it at the start.
3. Which sentence puts the takeaway last?
A The language around the illness is what matters to me, more than anything.
B What matters to me is the language.
C Language, that is what matters to me in my work.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Cleft Sentences and Information Order

GRAMMAR

How to put the important word where English expects it: at the end.

RuleFormExample
What-cleftWhat + subject + verb + is/are + FOCUSWhat I make are sound works.
It-cleftIt is/was + FOCUS + who/that + restIt was a nurse who started it.
ReasonThe reason + clause + is thatThe reason I record at night is that nobody else does.
End focusknown information first, new information lastWhat holds my work together is care.
Contrastnot X but YWhat matters is not the illness but the language.
People and thingswho for people, that for thingsIt was a nurse who... / It was a book that...

Never: What I do it is · It was a nurse which · What I want that people remember · Is the voice what matters.

Order

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the sentence you would actually say.

A curator asks what you do. You say:
A "I studied in Brazil, and then I had many shows, and I work with many things."
B "What I make are sound works from hospital wards."
C "What I make it is sound works from hospital wards."
You want to point at the person who started your project. You say:
A "It was a nurse who started it."
B "It was a nurse which started it, many years ago, in a hospital."
C "Was a nurse who started it, in the night shift of a hospital."
You want the last word to be the important one. You say:
A "The language is what matters to me, and also many other things in my work."
B "Is the language what matters to me in all of my work."
C "What matters to me is the language."
A doctor asks why you record at night. You say:
A "The reason I record at night is that nobody else does."
B "The reason because I record at night it is that nobody else does it."
C "Because the night, I record it, nobody else."
