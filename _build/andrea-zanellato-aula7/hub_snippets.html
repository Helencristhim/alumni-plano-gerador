Benefits People Want -- Comparing Offers

The health plan, the bonus, the hours: what people want most, and how to compare two offers. Key words: a health plan, a meal allowance, a bonus, a raise, paid time off, flexible hours, remote work, a perk. Structure: comparatives and superlatives.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A health plan -- a benefit that pays for doctors and hospitals
"Our health plan covers the employee and the family."
A meal allowance -- money the company gives you for food at work
"The meal allowance is on a card, every month."
A bonus -- extra money, usually once a year, for good results
"We pay a bonus in March if the company reaches its goals."
A raise -- more money in your salary, every month
"She asked for a raise after two years."
Paid time off -- days you do not work, but the company still pays you
"Employees here have thirty days of paid time off."
Flexible hours -- you can choose when you start and finish work
"With flexible hours, I can take my son to school."
Remote work -- working from home or from any place, not in the office
"Remote work is the benefit our candidates ask about most."
A perk -- a small extra thing a company offers, like free coffee or a gym
"Free lunch on Fridays is a nice perk."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Liam is an HR manager in Melbourne. His company asked: which benefit is the most important for you? The most popular answer was flexible hours. Remote work was second, and the health plan was third.

The bonus was only fifth. People said: time with my family is more important than a little more money. Two more days of paid time off were cheaper than a bigger bonus.

Anjali had two offers. The bank paid a higher salary, but the smaller company had a better health plan, flexible hours and nice perks. She chose the smaller company.

1. What was the most popular benefit in Liam’s survey?
A The bonus, because people want more money at the end of the year.
B Flexible hours.
C The health plan.
2. Why did Anjali choose the smaller company?
A Because the salary was higher than the salary at the big bank in the city.
B Because her father told her to.
C Because it had a better health plan and flexible hours.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “Flexible hours are better than a bonus.”
B “Flexible hours are more better than a bonus.”
C “Flexible hours are the more good benefit than the bonus for everybody.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Comparatives and Superlatives

GRAMMAR

To compare two things, use -er than (short words) or more ... than (long words). For the top of a group, use the -est or the most. Good, better, the best is irregular.

JobFormExample
Short adjectivesadjective + er + thanbig → bigger than · cheap → cheaper than
Long adjectivesmore + adjective + thanmore flexible than · more popular than
The top of the groupthe + adjective + est / the most + adjectivethe biggest · the most important
Irregulargood → better → the best · bad → worse → the worstThis offer is better.
Ending in -yy → iereasy → easier · busy → busier
The trapnever more + erNot more better. Say better.

Never: more better · the importantest · biger · busyer
