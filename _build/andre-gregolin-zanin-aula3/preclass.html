Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

A Day in the Hardest Year -- Already, Just and Yet

Sixteen hours a day, six days a week, and an English that has three little words for saying exactly where inside them you are. Key words: exhausting, consistency, to prioritize, a trade-off, ambition, resilience, to cope with, to keep up with, to burn the candle at both ends, in the long run. Structure: present perfect with already, just and yet inside a routine.

To cope with -- to deal with something difficult well enough to keep functioning
"I have learned to cope with the pressure better than I did in March."
To keep up with -- to move at the same speed as something that does not wait for you
"The hardest part is keeping up with the reading, not understanding it."
To burn the candle at both ends -- to use your energy early and late until there is none left in the middle
"I have been burning the candle at both ends since March, and I feel it."
In the long run -- looking at what will be true after a long time, not this week
"In the long run, the routine matters more than any single day in it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A routine and a day are not the same thing, and English uses two different shapes for them. The routine is what happens every day, and it lives in the present simple: I get up at twenty to six, I eat breakfast on the bus, I never study after half past nine. Notice that the continuous does not belong here. I am waking up at six every day is one of the most common Portuguese shadows in English, because in Portuguese the continuous can carry a habit. In English it cannot: a habit is simple, always.

Today is a different question. Today is a place you are standing in, somewhere between the beginning and the end, and three small words do all the work. Already says something is finished, and finished sooner than the listener expects: by seven I have already had breakfast. Just says it finished seconds ago: I have just finished a four-hour block. Yet says it has not happened and is still expected, and it only appears in negatives and questions: I have not started the biology unit yet.

The position of these three words is where the mistake actually happens, and it is worth more attention than the tense itself. Already and just go inside, between have and the verb. Yet goes at the very end. Not I have finished already the unit. Not Yet I have not started. One more pair worth fixing today: for takes a length of time and since takes a starting point. I have not had a day off for three weeks, and I have not had a day off since March. Both are correct. Since three weeks is not.

1. Why is “I am waking up at six every day” wrong in English?
A Because wake is an irregular verb.
B Because a habit always takes the present simple in English, even though the continuous can carry one in Portuguese.
C Because six in the morning is too early to be a habit.
2. Where do already and just go, and where does yet go?
A Already and just go at the end, and yet goes inside.
B All three go at the end of the sentence.
C Already and just go between have and the verb. Yet goes at the very end.
3. Which pair does the text say is correct?
A for three weeks and since March
B since three weeks and for March
C for March and since three weeks

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Already, Just and Yet Inside a Routine

GRAMMAR

The routine is the map. These three words are the pin that says where you are on it.

JobFormExample
The routine itselfpresent simple + always, usually, neverI get up at twenty to six.
Done, and earlier than expectedalready, between have and the verbBy seven I have already had breakfast.
Finished seconds agojust, between have and the verbI have just finished a four-hour block.
Not done, and still expectedyet, at the very end, in negatives and questionsI have not started the unit yet.
Later than expected, still not donestill, before the negativeI still have not had a real day off.
Marking the point in the dayby + a time · by the time + a clauseBy the time I get home, the day is gone.
The trapalready and just go INSIDE, yet goes at the ENDNot I have finished already. Say I have already finished.

Never: I have finished already the unit · Yet I have not started · I have not had a day off since three weeks · I am waking up at six every day
