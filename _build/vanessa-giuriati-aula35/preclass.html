Lesson 35 -- Pre-class

Will You Be Free?

Talk about your week and find a time with busy people: the future continuous for what you will be doing (I'll be traveling on Monday) and for polite questions about plans (Will you be joining us?). Key words: pencil in, tentative, slot, free up, double-book, bring forward, run late, touch base.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Pencil in -- To put a plan in your calendar for now, knowing it may change
"Let's pencil in Thursday at ten."
Tentative -- Planned, but not confirmed yet
"The date is tentative. I'll confirm on Monday."
Slot -- A period of time in a calendar for one activity
"I have a free slot at two o'clock."
Free up -- To make time or space available by moving or stopping something
"I can free up the morning for you."
Double-book -- To arrange two meetings or appointments for the same time
"Sorry, I double-booked. I have another call at two."
Bring forward -- To change a meeting or an event to an earlier date or time
"Can we bring the call forward to Wednesday?"
Run late -- To be later than planned, because something took more time
"I'm running late. I'll be there in ten minutes."
Touch base -- To talk or write to someone briefly, to check or update a plan
"Let's touch base on Tuesday to confirm."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Next week is going to be crazy. On Monday and Tuesday, I'll be visiting our office in Curitiba, so I won't be answering calls in the morning. My manager wanted a meeting on Thursday, but on Thursday I'll be running a training all day. So I asked, "Will you be working from the office on Wednesday?" She said yes, and we penciled in Wednesday at ten. It's still tentative, because she might bring it forward. Then I noticed I had double-booked: at ten I also had a call with a supplier. I freed up the morning, moved the call to an afternoon slot, and sent a message to everyone. We'll touch base on Tuesday to confirm. And if I'm running late on Wednesday, I'll send a message from the taxi.

1. Why won't the writer answer calls on Monday and Tuesday mornings?
A She will be running a training for the whole team at the main office.
B She'll be visiting the Curitiba office.
C She will be on vacation.
2. Why is the Wednesday meeting still tentative?
A Because her manager might bring it forward.
B Because the supplier has not answered her message about the new afternoon slot yet.
C Because she will be in Curitiba.
3. What did she do when she noticed she had double-booked?
A She canceled the meeting.
B She asked her manager to move the meeting to Thursday, after the training day.
C She moved the supplier call to an afternoon slot.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The future continuous

GRAMMAR

Use will be + -ing for an action that will be in progress at a moment in the future, and to ask about someone's plans politely.

StructureUseExample
will be + -ingIn progress at a future momentAt ten tomorrow, I'll be flying to Recife.
won't be + -ingWhat you will not be doingI won't be working on Friday.
Will you be + -ing...?A polite question about plansWill you be joining us for lunch?
this time next week / at nine tomorrowCommon time expressionsThis time next week, we'll be presenting.

Don't forget 'be': 'I'll be traveling', not 'I'll traveling'. Verbs of state (know, like, need) don't usually take -ing: 'I'll know tomorrow'.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

You want to know, politely, if your boss is coming to dinner. Best choice:
A "Will you be come to the team dinner with us on Friday night?"
B "Will you be joining us for dinner?"
C "You come to dinner?"
A colleague wants to call you at nine tomorrow, but you'll be on a plane. Best choice:
A "Sorry, at nine I'll be flying."
B "Sorry, at nine o'clock tomorrow morning I will flying to Recife for work."
C "Sorry, at nine I fly."
The client wants the meeting earlier. Best choice:
A "Can we bring forward it to Monday, so the client is happy?"
B "Can we postpone it?"
C "Can we bring it forward to Monday?"
The date of an event is planned, but not confirmed. Best choice:
A "The date is still tentative."
B "The date is still a double-book in the calendar of the company."
C "The date is running late."
