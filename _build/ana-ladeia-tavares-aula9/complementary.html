TED -- Ruth Chang, "How to make hard choices"

A philosopher on why the genuinely hard choices are the ones where no option is better, and why that is good news rather than bad. Fifteen minutes, and it changes what you think you are doing when you weigh two options that will not resolve.

Tip: watch it once, then take the client's three options from today's advice note and say out loud, for each one, what kind of person choosing it would make him. That is her test, not a pros-and-cons list.

Video
LSE -- "The Power of Regret, with Daniel Pink"

A public lecture at the London School of Economics on what tens of thousands of people say they actually regret, and on why the biggest category is not a thing they did. It is the research behind today's third recording, given at full length and at real speed.

Tip: it is long, so watch the first twenty minutes only. Write down the four categories he names and turn each one into a sentence with regret, choosing the form deliberately each time.

Podcast
NPR Life Kit -- "A decision coach's approach to making hard decisions"

Practical rather than philosophical: exercises for deciding faster, for knowing when to bring somebody else in, and for handling the doubts that arrive afterwards. Useful for the decision you are putting off, which is why you were asked about it at the start of the lesson. Transcript on the page.

Tip: pick one exercise and use it on a real decision this week. Then record two minutes explaining what you decided, what the downside is, and what you refused to let anybody talk you out of.
