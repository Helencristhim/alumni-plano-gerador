Plans and Promises -- Airports, Trips and What You Decide on the Spot

The two futures of English: the plan you already made, and the decision you take while you are speaking. Key expressions: to check in, hand luggage, a boarding pass, a delay, to take off, a connection, a round trip, in advance, to pick somebody up, a schedule. Structure: going to versus will.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. These ten stay here in your material -- they do not disappear after the class.

To check in -- to register at the airport or the hotel when you arrive
"I always check in online the night before."
Hand luggage -- the small bag you take with you into the plane
"I only travel with hand luggage, so I never wait at the belt."
A boarding pass -- the document that lets you get on the plane
"Keep your boarding pass. They ask for it twice."
A delay -- extra waiting time when something starts later than planned
"There is a two-hour delay and nobody has explained why."
To take off -- to leave the ground at the start of a flight
"We are going to take off about forty minutes late."
A connection -- the second flight you take to finish a journey
"If we land at nine I am going to miss my connection."
A round trip -- a journey to a place and back again
"It is a round trip, so I am back on Thursday night."
In advance -- before the time when something happens
"I booked the whole thing three months in advance."
To pick up -- to collect somebody in a car and take them somewhere
"Do not worry, I will pick you up at the airport."
A schedule -- the plan that says when each thing happens
"The schedule is tight, but it works if nothing goes wrong."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Janderson is going to fly to Miami on Tuesday. He booked the whole thing three months in advance, because it is much cheaper, and it is a round trip: he is back on Thursday night. He always checks in online the night before and he travels with hand luggage only, so he never waits at the belt.

The schedule is tight. He lands at half past ten, he has three meetings the same afternoon, and there is a connection in the middle of the journey that gives him fifty minutes. On paper it works. On paper everything works.

At the desk the woman tells him the flight is going to take off ninety minutes late. There is a delay on the incoming aircraft and nobody can do anything about it. He looks at the board and does the arithmetic. He is going to miss the connection; that is not an opinion, it is a queue and a clock.

So he decides, right there, in front of her. I will call the office now, he says, and I will ask them to move the first meeting. She says I will put you on the later flight and print a new boarding pass. And a colleague in Miami has already written to say: do not worry, I will pick you up at the airport, whatever time you land.

1. "He is going to fly on Tuesday." Why not will?
A Because the decision was made months ago, when he bought the ticket.
B Because Tuesday is close.
C Because flying always takes going to.
2. "He is going to miss the connection." What kind of future is this?
A An opinion with no proof.
B A prediction based on evidence he can see: the queue and the clock.
C A promise to somebody else.
3. "I will call the office now." Why will?
A Because it is in the far future.
B Because the decision is being taken at that moment, in the conversation.
C Because calling is a routine.
4. What is the difference between the two futures, according to the text?
A How far away the action is.
B When the decision was taken -- before the conversation, or during it.
C Whether the sentence is positive or negative.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Going To and Will

GRAMMAR

Both are futures and neither is more polite. The question is never when will it happen. It is when did you decide.

FormWhat it saysExample
am / is / are going to + verbThe decision already existed before this conversation. Plans, tickets, arrangements."I am going to fly on Tuesday."
"She is going to check in tonight."
will + verbThe decision is being taken now: an offer, a promise, an instant reaction."I will call him now."
"I will pick you up."
going to for evidenceYou can see the proof in front of you."Look at that queue. We are going to miss it."
will for opinionYou think or believe it, with no proof. Often after I think."I think it will be fine."
Negative and questionGoing to moves the verb to be. Will becomes will not or won't."Are you going to check in online?"
"I won't be late."
The trapThere is always a form of to be in front of going to.NOT "I go to travel"
"I am going to travel."

One practical rule for a meeting: everything already in your calendar takes going to, and everything you promise during the meeting takes will. That single distinction makes you sound organised instead of vague.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are at an airport desk and everything is in English. Choose the best thing to say.

Your ticket is bought and the trip is on Tuesday. You say:
A I will fly on Tuesday.
B I am going to fly on Tuesday.
C I go to fly on Tuesday.
A colleague says he has no way to get from the airport. You offer:
A I am going to pick you up.
B I will pick you up.
C I pick you up.
The queue is enormous and boarding closes in ten minutes. You say:
A We will miss the flight.
B We are going to miss the flight.
C We miss the flight.
You want to ask about the departure time:
A The flight is on time?
B Is the flight on time, or is there a delay?
C Has the flight the time correct?
You want a seat on the next flight. You say:
A I request the provision of an alternative transportation solution.
B Could you rebook me on the later flight?
C You can put me in other flight?
