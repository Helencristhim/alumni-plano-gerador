Plans and Promises

Everything you have already decided, and everything you decide on the spot. English uses two different futures for those, and mixing them is what makes you sound unsure.

Chapter 1: The Plan

The Next Trip You Already Booked

One trip, one meeting or one weekend that is already in your calendar. Where, when, and what is the first thing you are going to do when you arrive?

Where You Start

Decided, or Deciding Now

Already decided

The ticket is bought, the room is booked, the date is fixed

Decided right now

The other person just told you something and you react

The difference

Two words, and everybody hears which one you used

In Portuguese the same form covers both. In English they are separate, and the separation carries meaning: one says this was already arranged, the other says I am deciding as I speak. Get it right and you sound organised; get it wrong and you sound like you are improvising your own diary.

Chapter 3: The Code

Going To, and Will

One future for the plan, one future for the decision you make as you speak

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I am going to fly to Miami on Tuesday."

"That is a problem. I will call the client now."

"Look at that queue. We are going to miss the connection."

"Do not worry, I will pick you up."

Two of these were decided days ago. Two of them are being decided at this exact second. Which are which, and what changes in the verb?

FormWhat it saysExample
am / is / are going to + verbThe decision already exists. The ticket is bought, the date is in the diary."I am going to fly to Miami on Tuesday."
will + verbThe decision is happening now, while you speak. Offers, promises and instant reactions."I will call the client now."
"I will pick you up."
going to for evidenceYou can see it coming. There is proof in front of you."Look at that queue. We are going to miss it."
will for opinionYou think, you believe, you guess. There is no proof."I think it will be fine."
The short formsIn speech both shrink, and the short form is the normal one.going to → gonna (spoken)
I will → I'll · will not → won't

The question is not when. It is when did you decide. Before this conversation, use going to. During this conversation, use will.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I will fly to Miami on Tuesday, the ticket is bought."
"I am going to fly to Miami on Tuesday, the ticket is bought."
"Do not worry, I am going to pick you up. I decide now."
"Do not worry, I will pick you up."
"I go to travel next week."
"I am going to travel next week."

A decision taken before the conversation takes going to, even if the flight is months away. A decision taken during the conversation — an offer, a promise, a reaction — takes will. And English never says I go to travel: the pattern is be going to, with the verb to be in front. Read the correct versions aloud twice.

Chapter 4: Gate Twenty-Two

Gate Twenty-Two

Forty minutes before boarding, and the board has just changed

Dialogue

At the Desk

S
Good afternoon. Can I see your passport and your boarding pass?
J
Here you are. I am going to fly to Miami and then I have a connection to New York.
S
I have to tell you that this flight is delayed. It is going to take off about ninety minutes late.
J
Then I am going to miss my connection. What are my options?
S
I will put you on the later flight. It leaves at eight and you arrive at eleven.
J
That works. Is my hand luggage still fine, or do I have to check it in?
S
It is fine. Keep it with you. I will print the new boarding pass now.
J
Thank you. Will somebody announce the new gate, or should I keep watching the board?
Comprehension

About Sarah

1. What does Sarah ask for first?
His passport and his boarding pass.
2. How late is the flight going to take off?
About ninety minutes late.
3. What does she offer to do about the connection?
She will put him on the later flight, at eight.
4. What does she say about his hand luggage?
It is fine and he can keep it with him.
Listening

The Board Changes

An airport announcement. Sound first -- no text.

1. What is the new departure time, and how late is that?
Twenty past seven, which is ninety minutes late.
2. What should passengers with a connection do?
Go to the transfer desk in front of gate twenty-two, where staff will rebook them.
3. What is the airline going to give to passengers who are waiting?
A voucher for food and drink at any of the restaurants after security.
Listening 2

The Voice on the Line

A voice message about a trip. Sound first -- no text.

1. Which day is he going to arrive, and at what time?
Wednesday, at about half past ten in the morning.
2. What does he offer to do if there is a problem with the car?
He will take a taxi and send the receipt.
3. Why does he say the schedule is tight?
Because he has three meetings on Wednesday and he flies back on Thursday night.

The Habit

Tomorrow, Out Loud

Before the next class, say your next day out loud once a day: three things you are going to do because they are already in your diary, and one thing you will do if something goes wrong.

Your diary is already full of English practice. It costs sixty seconds to read it out loud.
