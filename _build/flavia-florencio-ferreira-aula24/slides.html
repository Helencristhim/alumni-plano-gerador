Chapter 4: The Origin Code

Where It Comes From, What Makes It Special

It comes from... · What makes it unique is... · Similar to... / Unlike...

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Parmigiano-Reggiano comes from a small region in the north of Italy."

"Queijo Canastra is made in the mountains of Minas Gerais."

"What makes it unique is its terroir."

"Similar to Parmigiano, Queijo Canastra has a geographic indication."

Look at the orange words. Which ones tell us the origin? Which one tells us what is special? Which one compares two products?

FunctionPhraseExample
OriginIt comes from + placeIt comes from Minas Gerais.
How and whereIt is (traditionally) made in / with...It is traditionally made in small farms.
What is specialWhat makes it unique is + nounWhat makes it unique is its terroir.
SameSimilar to + product, ... / Just as Italy has..., Brazil has...Just as Italy has Parmigiano, Brazil has Queijo Canastra.
DifferentUnlike + product, ...Unlike Parmigiano, it is aged for a short time.

Use from for origin, never "of": "It comes from Minas." After similar to and unlike, use a noun, then a comma: "Unlike Parmigiano, ..."

Chapter 5: At the Food Fair

A Booth in Turin

Two producers, two traditions

Dialogue

At the Brazilian Booth

E
Good afternoon. This cheese looks interesting. What is it?
F
It is Queijo Canastra. It comes from the mountains of Minas Gerais, and it is part of our heritage.
E
It has a wonderful flavor. How long do you age it?
F
Usually for a few weeks. Unlike Parmigiano, it is ready quickly. What makes it unique is the terroir.
E
In my family, we use the same recipe as my grandfather. It is a long tradition.
F
It is the same in Minas. Just as Italy has Parmigiano, Brazil has Queijo Canastra, and both have a geographic indication.
E
That is important. It protects the authenticity of the product.
F
Exactly. If you visit Brazil, I will take you to a farm in the Canastra region.
