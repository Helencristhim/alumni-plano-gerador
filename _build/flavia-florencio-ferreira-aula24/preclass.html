Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Heritage -- the traditions and culture that a community passes to its children
"This cheese is part of our heritage."
Terroir -- the land, climate, and soil that give a product its special taste
"The terroir of the Cerrado gives the coffee a sweet taste."
Authenticity -- the quality of being real and original, not a copy
"The label proves the authenticity of the product."
Flavor -- the taste of a food or a drink
"Parmigiano has a strong, rich flavor."
Biodiversity -- the many different plants and animals that live in one place
"The biodiversity of Brazil is huge."
Tradition -- a way of doing something that people repeat for many years
"Making cheese by hand is a family tradition."
Recipe -- the instructions to make a food, with the ingredients and the steps
"The recipe is more than two hundred years old."
Ingredient -- one of the foods that you use to make a dish
"This cheese has only three ingredients."
Unique -- the only one of its kind; different from all the others
"Every region has a unique product."
To age -- to keep a food for a long time so that its taste gets better
"They age the cheese for at least one year."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

At a food fair in Turin, Flavia presents Brazilian products to Italian visitors. She starts with coffee: "This coffee comes from the Cerrado, in Minas Gerais. What makes it unique is the terroir: the sun, the soil, and the altitude." Then she shows a cheese: "Queijo Canastra is made in the mountains, by hand, with an old family recipe." An Italian visitor asks: "Is it like Parmigiano?" Flavia answers: "Similar to Parmigiano, it has a geographic indication. Unlike Parmigiano, it is aged for a short time, so its flavor is softer." At the end, she says: "Just as Italy has its famous cheeses, Brazil has its own heritage."

1. Where does the coffee come from?
A From the south of Italy, near the coast and the old port of Naples.
B From the Cerrado, in Minas Gerais.
C From the mountains of the Canastra.
2. Which sentence is a comparison that shows a DIFFERENCE?
A "Unlike Parmigiano, it is aged for a short time."
B "Similar to Parmigiano, it has a geographic indication that protects its name."
C "What makes it unique is the terroir."
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "This coffee comes of the Cerrado, a big region in the center of Brazil."
B "What make it unique is the terroir."
C "This coffee comes from the Cerrado."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Origin and Comparison

GRAMMAR

How to say where a product comes from, what makes it special, and how it compares.

FunctionPhraseExample
OriginIt comes from + placeIt comes from Minas Gerais.
How and whereIt is (traditionally) made in / with...It is made by hand on small farms.
What is specialWhat makes it unique is + nounWhat makes it unique is its terroir.
SameSimilar to + product, ... / Just as..., ...Similar to Parmigiano, it has a GI.
DifferentUnlike + product, ...Unlike Parmigiano, it is aged for a short time.
RuleUse from for origin, never "of": "It comes from Minas." Use is made, not "is make". After similar to and unlike, put a noun and then a comma. "What makes it unique" has an "s": the subject is "what".
