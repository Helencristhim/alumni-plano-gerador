Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. You read all eight of these verbs every week. Today they leave the page.

To administer -- to give a medication or a treatment to a patient
"The antibiotic is administered before the procedure."
To conduct -- to organize and carry out a study or a test
"The study was conducted at three hospitals."
To analyze -- to examine data carefully in order to understand it
"The results are analyzed by an independent team."
To review -- to read something again and check whether it is correct
"Every chart is reviewed before discharge."
To update -- to change something so that it has the newest information
"The protocol has been updated twice this year."
To submit -- to send a document to somebody who will assess it
"The report must be submitted by Friday."
To discharge -- to officially let a patient leave the hospital
"She was discharged two days after surgery."
Protocol -- the official set of steps that has to be followed
"The protocol is reviewed every year."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Every year the emergency protocol of a large hospital is reviewed by a committee of eight people. First the incident reports of the previous year are collected and are analyzed. Then two external specialists are invited to read the draft. Last year one step was added: after every operation, the chart must be checked by a doctor who was not in the room. The change was suggested by a nurse, not by a director, and it has been copied by two other hospitals since then. Nobody remembers her name. In a protocol, the person disappears and the step remains.
Why is the text full of sentences like "are collected" and "was added"?
A Because the writer does not know who did those things
B Because in a protocol the step matters more than the person who does it
C Because all the events are in the future

A A director of the hospital
B A nurse

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive Voice

GRAMMAR

The passive is be + past participle. Use it when the action or the result matters more than the person who did it — which is most of medical writing.

presentam / is / are + participleThe patient is examined before surgery.
pastwas / were + participleThe data was analyzed last quarter.
present perfecthas / have been + participleThe protocol has been updated.
after a modalmodal + be + participleThe report must be submitted by Friday.

Careful: the tense lives in be, never in the participle. Has been updated, not has updated. And if you want to name who did it, use by: analyzed by an independent team.
