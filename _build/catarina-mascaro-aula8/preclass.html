Stage 1.1: Tools for Getting Stuck

Vocabulary

These are not new words. They are what you say instead of stopping — or instead of Portuguese.

What I mean is — you say the same idea again, in a clearer way
"It was weird. What I mean is, nobody expected it."
Can I try again? — you want a second attempt at the same sentence
"That came out wrong. Can I try again?"
Let me think — you need a second before you answer
"Let me think. Okay — probably Friday."
Sort of — not exactly, but close enough
"Do you like it? Sort of. It depends on the day."
The thing is — you are about to give the real reason
"I’d love to. The thing is, I have a test on Monday."
Actually — what comes next is different from what people expect
"Actually, I liked the second one better."
I’m not sure, but — you answer even though you are not certain
"I’m not sure, but I think it starts at eight."
How do you say...? — you ask for the exact word you are missing
"How do you say it when somebody never gives up?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read it. Every tense from lessons 1 to 7 is in here. Then answer the questions.

Do you want the short version or the long one? Let me think. Okay. Normally I wake up at seven and my brother wakes up at six. Last weekend, though, I stayed home and watched nine episodes, which was too many. The thing is, I have a test on Monday and I did hardly any homework. So right now I am sitting here with three chapters to read, and these days I am sleeping about six hours. Next weekend I am going to do it differently — two episodes a day, homework first. Actually, no. Can I try again? One episode a day. That is more realistic than two.
Why "stayed" and "watched" but "am sitting"?
A The first two are finished; the third is happening now.
B Because "sit" is an irregular verb.
What does "Can I try again?" do at the end?
A It asks the reader a question.
B It repairs the plan she just said — she changes two episodes to one.
Why "too many episodes" and not "too much episodes"?
A Episodes can be counted one by one.
B Because nine is a big number.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Everything from lessons 1 to 7, on one page

Nothing new here. This is your map — come back to it whenever you are not sure.

Ask anything
What do you...? Does she...?
Every week
I wake up / she wakes up — the S is only for he/she/it
It happened
I stayed / we met — after did, the verb goes back to normal
Right now
I’m sitting — question is Are you...?, never "Do you ...ing"
Not yet
decided before → going to · deciding now → I’ll
Take a side
short → -er than · long → more ... than · one of all → the
Put a number
can you count it? → many · if not → much

And when the word does not come: How do you say it when...? — never silence, never Portuguese.

Stage 3: Say it out loud

Speaking

Tap Listen, then Record. These need to come out fast, without thinking — that is the whole point of them.

I stayed home, because I had a test. What about you?

Stage 4: In the conversation

Quiz
You do not know the English word for something. What is the best move?
A Say the Portuguese word and hope they understand.
B Describe it: "How do you say it when the floor moves under you?"
Which answer keeps the conversation going?
A "I stayed home. It was good."
B "I stayed home, because I had a test. What about you?"
You started the sentence badly. What do you say?
A "Can I try again?"
B Stop talking and wait.

Stage 5: Three minutes

Reflection
Record THREE MINUTES in English — not thirty seconds this time. Talk about your life right now: your week, something that happened, something you are into these days, something coming up. Do not write a script. If you get stuck, use one of the eight tools out loud and keep going. Getting stuck is fine. Stopping is not.
