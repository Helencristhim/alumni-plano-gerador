Lesson 16 -- What Do You Think?

Conference Talk
"Why we should trust scientists" -- Naomi Oreskes, TED

A historian of science explains why science is never 100% certain -- and why that is not a reason to ignore it. A clear, careful speaker who talks about uncertainty without sounding weak.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Write down every "may", "might" and "probably" you hear, and one sentence where she disagrees politely.

Podcast
"How do climate scientists make predictions?" -- 6 Minute English, BBC Learning English

Two presenters talk about how scientists predict the future when nothing is certain, and the words they use to say how sure they are. Six minutes, with a transcript and a vocabulary list on the page.

Tip: listen once without the transcript. Then choose one prediction from your field and say it three ways: very sure, probably, and might.

Video
"Battling bad science" -- Ben Goldacre, TED

A doctor with a fast British accent shows how small studies and strong headlines make weak evidence look like a cure. Funny, quick, and very close to the story in today's reading.

Tip: this accent is fast -- use 0.75x the first time. Then retell one of his examples in sixty seconds, with a strong claim and a careful version.
