What Do You Think? -- Strong Enough, Not Too Strong

Giving your opinion on a result with the right level of certainty. Key words: claim, assumption, likely, unlikely, uncertain, to indicate, preliminary, tendency. Structure: may, might, could + verb, probably, it seems that.
0%
▼

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These words show how sure you are about a result.

Claim -- something you say is true, often before everybody agrees
"That is a strong claim for a study with only thirty patients."
Assumption -- something you believe is true, but you did not check
"Our assumption was that every patient took the drug every day."
Likely -- probably true, or probably going to happen
"It is likely that the effect is smaller in older patients."
Unlikely -- probably not true, or probably not going to happen
"It is unlikely that one drug will work for every patient."
Uncertain -- not sure; nobody knows the answer yet
"The long-term effect of the drug is still uncertain."
To indicate -- to show a sign that something is probably true
"The first results indicate that the drug is safe."
Preliminary -- early and not final, so it needs more research
"This is a preliminary finding, so we need a bigger sample."
Tendency -- something that happens often, but not every time
"There is a tendency for younger patients to respond faster."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Dr. Anika Rao studies pain after surgery in Mumbai. In a Q&A, a young doctor asks her: "Does your new treatment work?" She answers carefully. "Our data suggest that it may reduce pain in the first week. But this is a preliminary finding: we had only forty patients. It seems that younger patients respond faster, and the effect is probably smaller in older patients. It could be a question of dosage, but we are not sure yet. So I would not make a strong claim today. I'm not 100% sure, but I think we are on the right path."
Dr. Rao says "it may reduce pain", not "it reduces pain". Why?
A Because the result is preliminary, so she is not sure yet
B Because the study was in the past and she is talking about something that already finished a long time ago
C Because "may" is more formal than "will"
Which sentence from the text shows the strongest level of certainty?
A "It could be a question of dosage."
B "The effect is probably smaller in older patients."
C "It may reduce pain in the first week, but only in some of the forty patients."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- How Sure Are You?

GRAMMAR

Hedging means showing how sure you are. In science, it is not weakness: it is honesty about the data. Here is the scale, from careful to more sure.

may / might / could + base verbpossible, not sureThe drug may reduce pain. It could be the dose.
probably / likelymore sure, about 80%It probably works. It is likely that...
it seems that / our data suggest thatyou show the evidenceIt seems that younger patients respond faster.
negativeunlikely / may not / probably notIt may not work for everybody. It is unlikely.
your opiniona soft openingI'm not 100% sure, but I think it works.

After may, might and could: base verb, no -s, no to. One hedge per sentence is enough.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
In a Q&A, somebody asks if your drug works. Your study had thirty patients. What is the best answer?
A "Yes, it works. It is the best drug for nerve pain, and every clinic in the world needs to use it now."
B "I don't know."
C "Our data suggest that it may reduce pain, but this is a preliminary finding."
A colleague says your result is only placebo. What is the most polite answer?
A "I see your point. It might be part of it, but the effect lasted eight weeks."
B "You are wrong."
C "Maybe it might possibly be placebo, perhaps, or maybe it could probably be something else, I am not sure."
Which sentence is correct?
A "The drug might reduces pain in older patients, but we need to check it again."
B "The drug might reduce pain."
C "The drug might to reduce pain."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Sixty seconds. Choose one result from your own research. Explain what the data show, what is still uncertain, and how sure you are. Use at least four careful tools: may, might, could, probably, it seems that, our data suggest. Finish with "I'm not 100% sure, but I think...". No script -- just speak.
