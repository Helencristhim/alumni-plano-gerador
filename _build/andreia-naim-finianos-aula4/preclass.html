Lesson 04 -- Pre-class

The Pitch That Opens Doors -- What Happens If They Say Yes

How a buyer actually decides, and the sentence that turns a feature into a promise. Key words: a proof of concept, to automate, an audit trail, data migration, onboarding, deployment, exposure, to plug into, interoperable, a fiscal year, to cut through the noise, the bottom line. Structure: first conditional, with the zero conditional for what is always true.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A proof of concept -- a small first version a client runs to test whether it really works
"We offered a proof of concept on one company before they committed to all of them."
To automate -- to make a machine do a job that a person used to do by hand
"If you automate the filing, your team will save two weeks a quarter."
An audit trail -- the record that shows who did what, and when, so an auditor can check it
"Their auditor rejected the old audit trail, and that is why they called us."
Data migration -- moving all the information from the old system into the new one
"The contract is straightforward. The data migration is where the risk is."
Onboarding -- the first weeks in which new users are trained and set up
"Tell them how long onboarding takes for four hundred users, before they ask."
Deployment -- the work of putting the system in place so that people can start using it
"If deployment slips by a month, their whole fiscal year moves."
Exposure -- how much risk you are open to if something goes wrong
"Reducing their exposure is worth more to them than another feature."
To plug into -- to connect to a system a company already has, without replacing it
"It plugs into whatever they are already running, which is half the objection gone."
Interoperable -- able to work together with another system, exchanging information both ways
"They had four systems and none of them were interoperable."
A fiscal year -- the twelve months a company uses for its accounts, which may not start in January
"If the migration starts in October, we will be live before their fiscal year ends."
To cut through the noise -- to be heard when everybody else is saying something similar
"The pitches that cut through the noise are rarely the most impressive ones."
The bottom line -- the one number, or the one fact, that decides the answer
"The bottom line for them is the penalty, not the licence."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The note goes round before every meeting and it always says the same three things. If you open with features, you will lose the room, because the people who decide were not in the demo. Open with what changes for them instead. If you automate the filing, their team will save about two weeks a quarter — that is a promise somebody can repeat to a boss, and a feature is not.

Then deal with the risk before anybody asks. Explain the data migration. Show the audit trail. Say out loud how long onboarding takes for four hundred users and how many people deployment needs from their side. If companies do not know what a project will cost them in hours, they assume the worst, and the worst is what they put in the budget. The pitches that cut through the noise are almost never the most impressive ones; they are the ones that reduce exposure.

Finally, be conservative with numbers. If you say eight weeks and it takes twelve, you will not lose four weeks — you will lose the reference, because buyers talk to each other and a bad proof of concept is discussed at three other companies within a month. If the system plugs into what they already have, say so early: half the objection disappears. And if they ask something you cannot answer, say that too. The bottom line is that being checkable is worth more than being impressive.

1. "If you open with features, you will lose the room." Why is there no will after if?
A Because the sentence is about the past.
B Because the half with if always uses the plain present, even when it is about the future.
C Because will is only used in questions.
2. "If companies do not know what a project will cost, they assume the worst." Why is there no will in the second half either?
A Because it is not a promise about one client. It is something that is always true, so both halves stay in the present.
B Because the writer forgot it.
C Because assume is a verb that cannot take will.
3. "If you say eight weeks and it takes twelve, you will lose the reference." What is the argument?
A That four weeks of delay is a very large amount of time.
B That clients never check what a supplier tells them.
C That the real cost is not the delay but the client who will no longer recommend you, because buyers talk to each other.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- First Conditional

GRAMMAR

Two halves, and the future goes in one of them only. That is what makes it a promise instead of a hope.

JobFormExample
A real, probable promiseIf + present, ... will + verbIf you automate it, your team will save two weeks.
Something always trueIf + present, ... presentIf companies do not keep a trail, they cannot prove it.
The half with ifNEVER takes willnot if you will automate
Order does not mattercomma only when if comes firstYour team will save two weeks if you automate it.
Softer than willmay / can / should in the result halfIf it slips, we can move the go-live date.
When, not ifuse when if you are sure it happensWhen the migration starts, we will send two people.

Never: If you will automate it, your team will save time · If the migration will start in October · If we close this, we would be live in January · If companies will not keep a trail, they will cannot prove it

Order

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to promise a concrete result if the client automates the filing. You say:
A "If you will automate the filing, your team will save two weeks."
B "If you automate the filing, your team will save about two weeks a quarter."
C "If you would automate the filing, your team would save two weeks."
You want to state something that is true for every company, not a promise to this one. You say:
A "If companies do not keep an audit trail, they cannot prove anything afterwards."
B "If companies will not keep an audit trail, they will cannot prove it."
C "If companies would not keep an audit trail, they would not prove it."
You honestly think the deployment could take twelve weeks, not eight. You say:
A "Eight weeks. We will find a way."
B "Twelve weeks. If we say eight, it has to be eight, and I would rather finish early than explain a delay."
C "It depends. I cannot say anything about time."
The buyer already has a system that works. The move that opens the door is:
A Explain why their current supplier is bad.
B "You may not have to replace it. If you keep it, this plugs into it — and what went wrong for you last year?"
C Send a longer feature list.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

You have two minutes with the three people who were not in the demo. Do not describe the product. Tell them what will be different in their next quarter if they say yes, what the risk is, what you would do first to make that risk small, and one thing you are not promising. Use if at least four times, and put a real number in at least two of your promises.
