Lesson 02 -- Pre-class

The Book in Fifteen Minutes -- Ikigai

A short summary of a book about purpose and long life. Key words: a purpose, longevity, to figure out, to give up, to carry on, to take up, to look forward to, to slow down, a sense of community, flow, resilience, to keep someone going. Structure: separable and inseparable phrasal verbs.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A purpose -- the reason why you do something, or the reason your life is important to you
"For many people, work gives them a purpose."
Longevity -- a long life
"The island is famous for the longevity of its people."
To figure out -- to understand something or find the answer after thinking about it
"It took him years to figure out what he really wanted."
To give up -- to stop doing something, often because it is difficult
"She never gave up her morning walk."
To carry on -- to continue doing something
"Many people there carry on working after eighty."
To take up -- to start a new hobby or activity
"He took up painting when he was seventy."
To look forward to -- to feel happy about something that is going to happen
"I look forward to my walk in the park every afternoon."
To slow down -- to do things less quickly; to live at a calmer pace
"The book says we should slow down and enjoy the day."
A sense of community -- the feeling that you belong to a group of people who help each other
"A strong sense of community helps people live longer."
Flow -- a state when you are so focused on an activity that you forget the time
"When I read, I get into flow and forget the clock."
Resilience -- the ability to recover after a difficult time
"Resilience is not about never falling. It is about getting up."
To keep someone going -- to give someone the energy or the reason to continue
"What keeps you going on a difficult day?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Two authors travelled to Okinawa to figure out the secret of its longevity. Many people there have a clear purpose, and it keeps them going. They carry on working into their nineties, but they slow down.

One woman took up painting at seventy. Her family thought she would give it up after a month, but she never did. Today she paints every morning and gets into flow.

Her neighbors meet every week. This sense of community gives them resilience, and they look forward to seeing each other.

1. Why does the text say “give it up” and not “give up it”?
A Because it is the past tense.
B Because give up never takes an object.
C Because with a pronoun, it goes in the middle of a separable phrasal verb.
2. What do the people in Okinawa do when they get older?
A They stop working completely.
B They carry on working, but they slow down.
C They move to the city.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “They look forward to seeing each other.”
B “They look forward to see each other.”
C “They look forward seeing to each other.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Phrasal Verbs -- Where the Object Goes

GRAMMAR

Some phrasal verbs open in the middle; with a pronoun, they must. Others never open, and three-part verbs take -ing after them.

JobFormExample
Separable, with a nounverb + particle + noun, or verb + noun + particletake up painting / take painting up
Separable, with a pronounthe pronoun always goes in the middlefigure it out · gave it up
Inseparablethe object goes after the whole verbcarry on with it
Three-part verbsnever separate; a verb after them takes -ingI look forward to reading it.
No objectthe phrasal verb stands aloneDon’t give up. Slow down.
The trapno pronoun at the end of a separable verbNot figure out it. Say figure it out.

Never: figure out it · give up it · look forward to see · look forward the book to

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A friend asks if you solved a difficult problem. You say:
A "Yes, I figured out it."
B "Yes, I figured it out."
C "Yes, I out figured it."
You are happy about a trip next month. You say:
A "I look forward to travel."
B "I look forward traveling to."
C "I look forward to traveling."
Someone asks about your old hobby, golf. You stopped last year. You say:
A "I gave it up last year."
B "I gave up it last year."
C "I up gave it last year."
You want to say that you continue your work. You say:
A "I carry my work on with."
B "I carry on with my work."
C "I carry with on my work."
