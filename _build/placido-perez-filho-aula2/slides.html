Chapter 4: The Code

Where Does It Go?

One small word, two kinds of phrasal verbs

Grammar Discovery

Where Does the Small Word Go?

"It was a hard question, but he finally figured it out."

"She took up painting at seventy, and she never gave it up."

"I look forward to the next chapter."

"They carry on with their work into their nineties."

Look at the red words: they are the objects. In sentences 1 and 2, the pronoun it sits in the middle of the phrasal verb. In sentences 3 and 4, the object comes after the whole verb, and nothing can go in the middle.

JobFormExample
Separable, with a nounverb + particle + noun, or verb + noun + particletake up painting / take painting up
Separable, with a pronounthe pronoun always goes in the middlefigure it out · gave it up
Inseparablethe object goes after the whole verbcarry on with it
Three-part verbsnever separate; a verb after them takes -ingI look forward to reading it.
No objectthe phrasal verb stands aloneDon’t give up. Slow down.
The trapno pronoun at the end of a separable verbNot figure out it. Say figure it out.

In one line: with it / them, separable phrasal verbs open in the middle; three-part verbs never open, and the next verb takes -ing.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Three Voices, One Idea

A summary card and three recordings

Real Document

The Book on One Card

BOOK SUMMARY CARD
Ikigai · Héctor García and Francesc Miralles · 15-minute read
Big ideaEverybody needs a reason to get up in the morning
WhereOkinawa, Japan: one of the places with the most centenarians
Habit 1Carry on working, at a slower pace
Habit 2Take up new activities, at any age
Habit 3Find flow in daily work
Habit 4Build a strong sense of community
For the readerWhat keeps you going? What would you take up?
Summary cards like this one turn a book into five lines. The phrasal verbs carry most of the meaning: carry on, take up, keep going.
1. Rewrite Habit 1 with a pronoun.
For example: they love their work, so they carry on with it.
2. Which line is a question for you, the reader?
The last one: what keeps you going? What would you take up?
3. Sum up the card in one sentence.
A purpose, an active routine, flow and a community help people live long and well.
Listening 1 · A Book Podcast

The Book in Two Minutes

A British podcast host summarizes the book for her listeners. Sound first — no text.

1. Why did she almost not read the book?
Because she thought it was just another self-help book.
2. Which idea from the book does she use every day?
Flow: she tries to find one activity a day where she forgets the time.
3. What does she say about retirement?
The people in the book do not really retire; they slow down but carry on.
Listening 2 · A Doctor on Longevity

What the Research Says

A German doctor who studies longevity talks about the habits of very old people. Sound first — no text.

1. According to her, what matters more than genes?
Daily habits.
2. Which two habits does she mention first?
Moving every day and staying in contact with other people.
3. What is her advice for people who stop working?
To take up something that gives them a purpose, not just rest.
Listening 3 · A New Hobby at Seventy

He Took It Up at Seventy

An Italian man in his seventies talks about the hobby he started after he sold his business. Sound first — no text.

1. What did he do for forty years?
He ran a small furniture business.
2. What hobby did he take up, and why?
Woodcarving, because he missed working with his hands.
3. What keeps him going now?
The young people he teaches on Saturdays.
