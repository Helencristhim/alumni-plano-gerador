Chapter 3: The Code

The Polite Repair

could you tell me what ... / do you know if ...

Grammar Discovery

Where Did the Verb Go?

Where is the protocol? → Could you tell me where the protocol is?
What do you mean by gender? → Could you tell me what you mean by gender?
How did you measure pain? → I'd like to know how you measured pain.
Did you use pictures? → Could you tell me if you used pictures?
Click to reveal the rule
After Could you tell me / Do you know / I'd like to know, the question becomes part of a longer sentence, so the word order is like a normal sentence: question word + subject + verb (where the protocol is, not where is the protocol). do / does / did disappear, and the main verb takes the tense: how you measured. For a yes/no question, use if: if you used pictures. These questions sound softer and more polite — perfect for a Q&A, when you need the speaker to help you.

Chapter 5: Practice

Questions From the Floor

Two audience questions at real speed

Listening 1

A Question From Ankara

Read the questions, then listen.

1. Where does he work, and with which patients?
In a pain clinic in Ankara, with patients who have nerve damage after diabetes.
2. What is his question about?
The sample: how many patients were women, and if gender could be a factor in the results.
3. What is his clinic not sure about?
If women report more pain because of biology, or because men do not talk about it.
Listening 2

Two Questions in One

Read the questions, then listen.

1. How many questions does she ask?
Two.
2. What is her practical question?
Where she can find the protocol: is it published, or only on the poster?
3. Why is she worried about the pain scale?
Many of her patients cannot read or do not understand numbers well, so she wants to know if they used pictures or faces.
