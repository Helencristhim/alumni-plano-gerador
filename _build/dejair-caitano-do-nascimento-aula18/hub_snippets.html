Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. Four words describe how people speak; four describe what a good listener does.

Pace -- how fast or slow somebody speaks
"His pace was so fast that I missed the second part of the question."
Rhythm -- the pattern of strong and weak sounds in speech
"English has a rhythm of strong and weak words, like music."
Word stress -- the part of a word that we say stronger and longer
"In pharmacology, the word stress is on COL."
Intonation -- how your voice goes up and down in a sentence
"Her intonation went up at the end, so I knew it was a question."
Key word -- one of the most important words in a sentence
"Listen for the key words: the question word and the topic."
To predict -- to say what will probably happen before it happens
"Before your talk, predict the five questions people will ask."
To infer -- to understand something from the clues, without hearing it directly
"I missed one word, but I could infer it from the context."
To keep up with -- to go at the same speed as somebody or something
"When she speaks fast, it is hard to keep up with her."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Kemal is a young researcher from Izmir. At his first international congress, he did not understand the first question in his Q&A, and he froze. Now he has a plan. When he misses a word, he says: "Sorry, could you tell me what you mean by that?" When a question has two parts, he checks: "So you'd like to know if we used a control group, and how we measured pain. Is that right?" And after the session, he asks: "Do you know where the next talk is?" Nobody thinks he is weak. They think he listens carefully.
Kemal says "could you tell me what you mean", not "what do you mean". Why?
A Because the question is about something in the past that the speaker said a few minutes before
B Because "mean" is an irregular verb
C Because after "could you tell me", the word order is like a normal sentence and "do" disappears
Why does Kemal say "So you'd like to know if... Is that right?"
A To check that he understood a question with two parts
B To show the audience that the question was not very good and that he does not want to answer it
C To end the Q&A

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Could You Tell Me What You Mean?

GRAMMAR

An indirect question is a question inside a polite opening: Could you tell me..., Do you know..., I'd like to know... After the opening, the word order is like a normal sentence.

with bequestion word + subject + beCould you tell me where the protocol is?
with do / does / diddo disappears; the verb takes the tenseCould you tell me what you mean? / I'd like to know how you measured it.
yes / no questionuse ifDo you know if they used pictures?
negativein the openingI'm not sure what you mean by that.
to checkso you'd like to know if...So you'd like to know if we looked at gender. Is that right?

"Could you tell me where the protocol is?" -- never "where is the protocol". Indirect questions sound softer: perfect for a Q&A.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
In your Q&A, you miss one important word in a question. What do you say?
A Nothing. You smile and answer a different question that you prepared before the session started
B "Could you tell me what you mean by...?"
C "Repeat."
A question has two parts, and you are not sure you understood both. What is the best answer?
A "I only answer one question, so please choose the question that is more important for you."
B "Next question, please."
C "So you'd like to know if... and how... Is that right?"
Which question is correct?
A "Could you tell me where is the room for the poster session this afternoon?"
B "Could you tell me where the room is?"
C "Could you tell me where does the room?"

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Sixty seconds. Imagine the last question in your Q&A in Bangkok: it is long, fast, and has two parts. Say what you do: one repair sentence (Could you tell me..., or So you'd like to know if...), then your answer to both parts. No script -- just speak.
