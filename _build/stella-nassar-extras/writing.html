Writing Studio

Every lesson in your program ends with a piece of writing. This tab holds all twelve briefs in one place. You write in your own Google Doc, your teacher comments there, and you use the checklist under each brief to review your draft before you share it. Your checkmarks are saved here automatically.

How the Writing Studio works
  1. Create one Google Doc called Stella Nassar -- Writing Studio and share it with your teacher with permission to comment.
  2. Give each piece a heading with the lesson number and the date, so your whole portfolio lives in a single document.
  3. Before sharing a draft, tick every item in the checklist under that brief. If you cannot tick an item, revise first.
  4. Reply to every comment from your teacher: accept it, change it, or explain why you disagree.
  5. Keep a section called Lexicon at the end of the document for words and sentences you want to reuse.
The four questions your teacher reads for
Precision
Is every word the exact one, or only a nearby one?
Structure
Can a reader follow the argument without rereading?
Voice
Is there at least one sentence only you could have written?
Accuracy
Is every fact true and every structure correct?
LESSON 01 · THE EYE OF THE BEHOLDER
Short answer · 250 words

Describe a work of art you have spent real time with. Do not tell the reader why it is famous. Show what you saw.

LESSON 02 · WHY ART HISTORY?
Personal statement opening · 400 words

Expand the fifteen-minute opening you wrote in class. Add no new content: only precision.

LESSON 03 · LIGHT AND SHADOW
Formal analysis · 300 words

Write a formal analysis of Caravaggio, The Calling of Saint Matthew. Argue only from what is visible on the canvas.

LESSON 04 · THE PERSONAL ESSAY I
Revised opening + five first sentences · 250 words + 5 sentences

Revise your opening: a hook of no more than two sentences and a scene with three concrete sensory details. Then write five alternative first sentences, one of each type from the workshop handout.

LESSON 05 · WHO OWNS ART?
Argument · 350 words

Should the Parthenon Sculptures be reunited in Athens? Take a clear position, concede the two strongest counterarguments and answer them.

LESSON 06 · THE WHY US SUPPLEMENT
Research notes + supplement · 2 notes + 250 words

Complete a research note for two real colleges (course, person, something only possible there, your connection). Then write a Why Us supplement for one of them.

LESSON 07 · FROM IMPRESSIONISM TO MODERNISM
The fifth critic · 300 words

Write as a fifth critic. Agree with one of the four critics from the lesson and disagree with another, naming them precisely.

LESSON 08 · ADMISSIONS INTERVIEW I
Interview questions · 3 questions + notes

Write the three questions you would ask an interviewer, one for each college you researched. Add two lines of notes on your recorded answers from the homework.

LESSON 09 · BRAZIL ON THE WORLD STAGE
Perspective supplement · 250 words

Tell a reader about a perspective you bring from your background. Show one thing it lets you see that others might miss.

LESSON 10 · THE PERSONAL ESSAY II
Final personal statement · 650 words or fewer

Bring your personal statement to 650 words or fewer. Cut words first, sentences second, ideas last.

LESSON 11 · ART IN THE AGE OF ALGORITHMS
Banksy paragraph · 300 words

Was the half-shredded Banksy art, spectacle or a market event? Take a position and concede one counterargument.

LESSON 12 · MOCK INTERVIEW AND PORTFOLIO REVIEW
Portfolio check · all pieces

Make sure every piece in your portfolio is in its final version in your document, and write one paragraph about the two moves you use best and the two you still need to practice.
