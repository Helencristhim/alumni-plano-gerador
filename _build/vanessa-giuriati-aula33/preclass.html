Lesson 33 -- Pre-class

Checking What You Heard

Stop pretending you understood: check what you heard by repeating it back with reported speech (Did you say it was...? / You said you would...), and ask for help when you get lost. Key words: catch, mishear, follow, get the gist, lose track, spell out, on the same page, mix up.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Catch -- To hear or understand what someone says
"Sorry, I didn't catch your name."
Mishear -- To hear a word or a number incorrectly
"I misheard the time. I thought he said nine."
Follow -- To understand an explanation or a story while someone is speaking
"Sorry, I'm not sure I follow you."
Get the gist -- To understand the main idea, but not every detail
"I didn't get every word, but I got the gist."
Lose track -- To stop following a conversation, a list or a story
"Wait, I lost track. Which meeting are we talking about?"
Spell out -- To explain something in a very clear and simple way
"Could you spell out what you need from me?"
On the same page -- When two or more people understand a plan or a situation in the same way
"Let's check we're on the same page about the dates."
Mix up -- To confuse two things, like two names or two dates
"I always mix up Tuesday and Thursday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Last week I had a video call with a supplier from Chicago. The connection was bad, and I didn't catch half of what he said. In the past, I would just smile and say "OK". This time, I tried something new. When he mentioned the delivery, I said, "Sorry, did you say it was on the 15th?" He laughed and said, "No, the fifth!" I had misheard the number completely. Later, I lost track of the prices, so I asked him to spell out the costs in an email. He said he would send it that afternoon, and he did. I didn't follow every joke, but I got the gist. Before we hung up, I said, "Just to check we're on the same page: you said you would send the samples on Monday?" He said yes. No mix-ups this time.

1. What did the writer usually do in the past when she didn't understand?
A She smiled and said "OK".
B She asked the person to write everything in an email after the call.
C She ended the call.
2. What had she misheard?
A The price of the samples.
B The delivery date: it was the fifth, not the 15th.
C The name of the supplier and the city where his company is.
3. How did she check the plan at the end of the call?
A She asked him to repeat everything slowly, from the very beginning.
B She said, "You said you would send the samples on Monday?"
C She sent him a message.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported speech for checking

GRAMMAR

To check what you heard, repeat it back. The verb usually moves one step back in time.

StructureUseExample
is / are → was / wereCheck a fact"It's at three." → Did you say it was at three?
will → wouldCheck a promise or a plan"I'll call." → You said you would call.
can → couldCheck ability"I can come." → You said you could come.
Did you say (that) + statementThe checking questionDid you say she was leaving?

After 'Did you say', keep statement order: 'Did you say it was...?'. Don't say 'You said me': use 'You said...' or 'You told me...'.
