Chapter 3

You Said...

Repeat it back to check it

Grammar Discovery

The Verb Moves Back

"The meeting is at three." — "Sorry, did you say it was at three?"

"I'll send it tomorrow." — "You said you would send it tomorrow, right?"

"I can't come on Friday." — "Wait, I thought you said you could come."

"We're moving the deadline." — "Did you say you were moving it?"

FormUseExample
is / are → was / wereCheck a present fact"It's at three." → Did you say it was at three?
will → wouldCheck a promise or a plan"I'll call." → You said you would call.
can → couldCheck ability or possibility"I can't." → I thought you said you couldn't.
Did you say (that)...? / You said (that)...The checking question or statementDid you say (that) she was leaving?
⚠ CarefulAfter 'Did you say', use statement order: 'Did you say it was at three?', never 'Did you say was it at three?'. 'Say' has no person after it: 'You said that...' or 'You told me that...', never 'You said me'. If the fact is still true now, the present is also fine: 'You said the office is closed on Mondays.'

Comprehension

Did You Catch It?

1. When is the client visit, according to Daniel?
On Thursday at ten.
2. What did Daniel say Paula would do?
She would bring the contracts.
3. Why did Daniel get the lunch information wrong before?
He mixed up the two visits. The lunch plan was from last week.
Listening

Why Smart People Ask

Listen first. No text. Sound only.

1. What do many people think about asking someone to repeat?
That it makes them look weak.
2. What is the simple technique?
Repeat the important part back, but change it a little.
3. Why does it work so well?
The other person hears their own words and can correct you in one second.
Listening

Fifteen or Fifty?

A different voice, a different story. Listen carefully.

1. Why didn't he ask the client to repeat the number?
He was too shy to ask.
2. What had the client really asked for?
Fifty boxes, not fifteen.
3. What does he always do now?
He repeats numbers back: fifteen, one five, or fifty, five zero?

I can check a fact with "Did you say it was...?"
I can repeat a promise or a plan with "You said you would..."
I can move the verb back one step: is to was, will to would, can to could.
