Pumping station -- a building with pumps that move water or sewage to a higher place

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Technical documents have a voice of their own. A scope rarely says the utility operates the plant or our team measures the flow rate. It says "the plant is operated by the utility" and "the flow rate is measured every hour". This is the passive: is or are plus the past participle of the verb. It puts the action first, because in engineering what is done matters more than who does it. When the person or company is important, it comes after by: "the design is approved by the utility". When it is not important, it simply disappears: "two pumping stations are built on the east side". The two pieces are both necessary. Without is, the sentence breaks ("the plant operated by"), and without the participle, too ("the plant is operate").

1. Why does a scope say "the flow rate is measured every hour"?
A Because the action matters more than who measures it.
B Because measure is an irregular verb.
C Because the sentence is about the past.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "The results is sent every month."
B "The results are sent every month."
C "The results are send every month."
3. When do we use by in a passive sentence?
A Always.
B Never.
C Only when it is important to say who does the action.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The passive in the present

GRAMMAR

One question decides everything: is the action more important than the person who does it? Then use is / are + past participle.

FormRuleExample
Affirmativeis / are + past participleThe flow rate is measured every hour.
With the agent... + by + who does itThe plant is operated by the utility.
Negativeisn't / aren't + past participleMaintenance isn't included.
QuestionIs / Are + subject + past participleIs the design approved first?

Many participles are irregular, and they are the ones you need most at work: do > done, send > sent, build > built, choose > chosen, write > written.
