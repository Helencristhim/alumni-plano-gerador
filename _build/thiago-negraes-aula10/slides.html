Chapter 4: How Much, How Many

Counting What You Cannot Count

The noun decides the word

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"There are a lot of leftovers in the fridge."

"We do not have any bread."

"How much food should I bring?"

"How many people are coming?"

WordGoes withExample
somepositive sentences, both kindsWe have some bread.
anynegatives and questionsWe do not have any bread.
much / how muchthings you cannot countHow much food?
many / how manythings you can countHow many people?

When you are not sure, a lot of works with both kinds in a positive sentence: a lot of food, a lot of people. And some comes back in one case, the polite offer: "Would you like some coffee?"

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

✗ WRONG

"How many food should I bring?"

✓ RIGHT

"How much food should I bring?"

✗ WRONG

"We do not have some bread."

✓ RIGHT

"We do not have any bread."

Portuguese uses one word for both quantities and keeps the same word in the negative. English splits both: the noun chooses between much and many, and the sentence chooses between some and any.

What Should I Bring?

T

L
A potluck. Good question, most people never ask.
T
Then how many people are coming, and how much food should I bring?
L
About fifteen. One large portion is enough, nobody finishes anything anyway.
T
Is there anything you do not have yet?
L
We do not have any salad, and we already have too much dessert.
T
A salad, then. Homemade, or is it fine from the grocery store?
L
Nobody says it out loud, but homemade always wins.
T
Understood. And what happens to what is left?
L
Everybody takes the leftovers home. Bring a box.
