Chapter 3: The Code

Rule, Advice or Free Choice?

Three levels. That is the whole decision.

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"You have to leave the keys downstairs."

"You must not have parties."

"You do not have to clean before you leave."

"You should bring your own water."

One of these is a rule you cannot break. One is forbidden. One is completely optional. One is only advice. Which is which?

FormMeaningExample
have toAn obligation that comes from outside: a rule, a law, somebody else.I have to be there at six.
mustAn obligation you feel yourself, or a written rule in a notice.I must stop working at midnight.
must notForbidden. Do not do it.You must not change the logo.
do not have toOptional. You can if you want, nobody is asking.You don't have to bring food.
should / shouldn'tAdvice. The softest of them all, and the one you will use most.You should message me first.
can / can'tPermission and ability: "You can use everything in the kitchen." / "I can't follow a conversation in a bar."

All of them are followed by the base verb, with no -s and no to: he must go, she should send.

The Trap

Forbidden, or Optional?

FORBIDDEN — DO NOT DO IT

"You must not change the logo colour."

"You must not take the keys with you."

OPTIONAL — YOUR CHOICE

"You don't have to use their font."

"You don't have to clean before you leave."

These two are not the same thing. One closes the door. The other opens it.

This is the one mistake in this lesson that changes the meaning, not just the tone. Everything else is only register.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"You must to leave the keys downstairs."
"You must leave the keys downstairs."
"She shoulds send the layouts on Thursday."
"She should send the layouts on Thursday."
"You must send me the file today." (to a client)
"Could you send me the file today?" (to a client)

After a modal the verb never takes to and never takes -s. And must pointed at another person sounds like an order in English, so with a client you ask instead: Could you...?

H
Don't bother, we do that. And if something breaks, you should message me instead of fixing it yourself.

Deal. I promise I won't break anything on purpose.
Comprehension

About Hugo

1. What does Hugo say guests have to do with the keys?
Leave them in the metal box downstairs when they go.
2. What does he say about the rubbish?
It is not obligatory every day, but she should do it on Tuesday nights.
3. What does he warn her about, and why?
She must not have parties, because the neighbours are very strict about noise.
Listening

A Warning About Sunday

Six in the morning and a bus that does not wait. Sound first — no text.

1. What time do they have to leave, and why does it matter?
Six in the morning, because the bus does not wait.
2. What is optional, and what should she bring anyway?
She does not have to bring food, but she should bring her own water.
3. What must she not do, and what happened to the cousin?
She must not wear new shoes. The cousin did it on purpose and could not walk for three days.
Listening 2

Two Things You Should Know

Daniel has just read the new guidelines. Sound first — no text.

1. What is forbidden, and who checks it?
Changing the logo colour. The main stakeholder checks it personally.
2. What does everybody assume is obligatory, and is it?
Using the client's font in social posts. They do not have to.
3. What is his advice about the layouts?
That they should send them on Thursday instead of Friday, so the client asks for small changes instead of big ones.
