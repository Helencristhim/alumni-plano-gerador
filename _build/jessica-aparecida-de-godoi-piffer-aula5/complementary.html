Talk
Barry Schwartz — “The paradox of choice” (TED)

Twenty minutes on why more options make people less happy with what they choose — which is the mechanism behind every trade-off in this lesson. He argues by hypothetical: almost every example is a second conditional said out loud.

Tip: write down five of his if…would sentences exactly as he says them, then rewrite each about a choice of your own.

Podcast
Criminal — “The Mountain” (episode 365)

A story built entirely around a decision somebody could have taken and did not. The interviewer spends the whole episode asking versions of “what would you have done”, which is this lesson’s structure at native speed for thirty minutes.

Tip: stop at the halfway point and answer out loud, in three sentences, what you would have done at that moment. Then finish it.

Video
Brené Brown — “The power of vulnerability” (TED)

Twenty minutes from a researcher on behaviour, which is the reading you already do for pleasure. She is also one of the clearest speakers on TED for a non-native listener: long sentences, unhurried, and every argument built out of what she would and would not do.

Tip: she softens almost every claim. Count how many times she uses probably, I would say or maybe in the first ten minutes.
