Lesson 04 -- Pre-class

Saying What You Should Have Done -- Judging a Decision, Not a Person

Somebody saw the problem, wrote one line about it, and moved on. Eight months later a panel is deciding what that line was worth. Key words: due diligence, negligence, a safeguard, an oversight, to flag, accountable, to escalate, a conflict of interest, reckless, a lapse, a red flag, to sign off on. Structure: modal perfects.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Due diligence -- the checking a careful professional does before signing or agreeing to anything
"Nobody did the due diligence on the second clause, and it took four minutes to do."
Negligence -- failing to take the care that any reasonable person in that job would have taken
"The difference between an oversight and negligence is what a reasonable person would have done."
A safeguard -- a rule or a step put in place to stop a risk that somebody already knew about
"The second signature is a safeguard. It exists precisely so that one tired person cannot decide alone."
An oversight -- something important that was missed by accident, not by decision
"They are calling it an oversight, and for the first eight months that is exactly what it was."
To flag -- to point out a problem early, so that somebody else can act on it
"She flagged it once, in one line, in an email to four people."
Accountable -- expected to explain and answer for a decision you made, in front of other people
"Somebody has to be accountable for the signature, and it is not the person who flagged it."
To escalate -- to take a problem to somebody more senior, because it is bigger than you
"She should have escalated it. One line in an email is not escalating anything."
A conflict of interest -- a situation where your duty to one side clashes with your own interest or with another duty
"It is a conflict of interest whether or not anybody acts on it. That is why it has to be declared."
Reckless -- knowing the risk perfectly well and going ahead anyway
"Nobody here was reckless. That is the whole difficulty: everyone acted reasonably and it still failed."
A lapse -- a short failure in attention or standards, in somebody who is usually careful
"Twenty years without a complaint and one lapse on a Friday afternoon."
A red flag -- an early sign that something is wrong, before anybody can prove it
"Two clauses saying opposite things is a red flag in any contract, in any language."
To sign off on -- to give the final approval to something, and to own it afterwards
"Whoever signs off on a document owns it, whatever anybody else noticed first."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A trainee reads a draft agreement and sees that two clauses contradict each other. That is a red flag in any contract, in any language. She writes one line about it at the bottom of a longer email, to four people. Nobody answers. Two days later the supervising partner signs off on the draft, and eight months after that a review panel is asked to decide what that one line was worth.

Everything the panel says about it takes the same shape: a modal, then have, then a past participle. Only the modal changes, and the modal is the whole meaning. She should have escalated it criticises what was not done. They must have seen the email is a confident conclusion, because it went to four people. Somebody might have caught it is only a possibility. And she cannot have read it is the confident negative — not must not have, which in English does not do that job.

There is one more, and it is the kindest sentence in the room: she need not have worried about looking foolish. It means she did worry, and that the worry was unnecessary. The panel still has to choose between three words — an oversight, a lapse and negligence — and the test is what a reasonable person in the same job would have done. One warning for a Portuguese speaker: never write should of, however clearly you hear it in a film, and never drop the have.

1. "She should have escalated it." What does the sentence tell you about what really happened?
A She escalated it, and it was the right decision.
B She did not escalate it, and the speaker thinks she was wrong not to.
C She may or may not have escalated it; the sentence does not say.
2. Why does the text say "she cannot have read it" instead of "she must not have read it"?
A Because in English the negative of a confident conclusion is "cannot have", not "must not have".
B Because "must" cannot be used in the negative at all.
C Because "read" is an irregular verb.
3. "She need not have worried about looking foolish." What happened?
A She did not worry, and there was no reason to.
B She did worry, and the worry turned out to be unnecessary.
C She was told not to worry and ignored the advice.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Modal Perfects

GRAMMAR

The shape never changes. Choose the modal, and you have chosen how hard you are hitting.

JobFormExample
Criticism of what was not doneshould have + participleShe should have escalated it.
Criticism of what was doneshould not have + participleThey should not have signed off on it.
A confident conclusion about the pastmust have + participleThey must have seen the email.
A confident NEGATIVE conclusioncannot have / could not have + participleShe cannot have read it.
A possibility, not a conclusionmight have / may have / could have + participleSomebody might have caught it.
Something done that was unnecessaryneed not have + participleShe need not have worried.
Spoken formshould’ve, must’ve, might’ve — written in full, alwaysNever should of, however clearly you hear it.

Never: she should of escalated it · they must not have seen it · they should have not signed off · he needn't to have worried · she should escalated it.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The panel asks what the trainee should have done. You answer:
A "She should of escalated it to somebody."
B "She should have escalated it to one named person, out loud."
C "She should escalated it to one named person."
The email went to four people and none of them replied. You are certain they saw it. You say:
A "They must have seen it. It went to four people."
B "They must see it. It went to four people."
C "They had to see it. It went to four people."
One person was already on leave when the email arrived. You want to rule her out. You say:
A "She must not have read it."
B "She cannot have read it. It arrived after she left."
C "She could not read it, because she was not there."
You want to say that the trainee's fear of looking foolish was unnecessary. You say:
A "She did not need to be afraid, and she was not."
B "She needn't to have worried about looking foolish."
C "She needn't have worried about looking foolish."
