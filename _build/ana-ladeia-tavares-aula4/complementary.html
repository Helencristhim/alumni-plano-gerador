TED -- Kathryn Schulz, "On being wrong"

Seventeen minutes on the strangest fact about error: being wrong feels exactly like being right, until the moment you find out. It is the argument under today's whole lesson, and it explains why four people can read a warning and none of them stop.

Tip: watch it once without subtitles. Then write four sentences about the trainee's email using four different modals, and say which of the four is the hardest to say out loud about yourself.

Video
Leader Logic -- "The Swiss Cheese Model of Accident Causation"

A short explanation of the model that safety investigators use everywhere: no single mistake causes a disaster, and every failure has to pass through a line of safeguards that all happened to have a hole in the same place. It is the answer the partner gives in today's first recording, drawn as a diagram.

Tip: after watching, name the three safeguards that failed in the case file and say, for each one, what somebody should have done.

Hidden Brain -- "You 2.0: How to Open Your Mind"

Shankar Vedantam and the psychologist Adam Grant on the mental habit that stops smart people from checking what they already believe, and on why the confident are the last to look. Calm American English with a transcript on the page.
