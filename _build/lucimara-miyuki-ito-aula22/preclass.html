Lesson 22 -- Pre-class

Staying Confident at the Front Desk

Hotel requests and problems on a business trip in Lyon, with the causative ("I'd like to have my suit pressed", "Could I get my shirts cleaned?"). Key words: laundry service, dry cleaning, to press, toiletries, thermostat, maintenance, noise complaint, do-not-disturb sign, luggage storage, incidentals.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Laundry service -- the hotel washes your clothes for you
"The laundry service returns your clothes the next day."
Dry cleaning -- cleaning clothes with chemicals, not water
"This jacket needs dry cleaning, not washing."
To press -- to make clothes flat and smooth with an iron
"Could you press my suit before the meeting?"
Toiletries -- small things for washing, like soap and shampoo
"The toiletries in the bathroom smell of lavender."
Thermostat -- the device that controls the temperature of the room
"The thermostat is broken: the room is too cold."
Maintenance -- the team that repairs things in a building
"I called maintenance about the air conditioning."
Noise complaint -- a message to say that a place is too loud
"I made a noise complaint about the room next door."
Do-not-disturb sign -- a sign on your door that says: please do not come in
"I put the do-not-disturb sign on the door and slept."
Luggage storage -- a safe place where the hotel keeps your bags
"You can leave your bags in luggage storage after check-out."
Incidentals -- small extra costs on your bill, like the minibar or the phone
"The company pays for the room, but I pay the incidentals."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

On her first night in Lyon, Lucimara opens her suitcase. Her suit for the trade fair is wrinkled. She does not press it herself: she calls the front desk and says, "I'd like to have my suit pressed before seven a.m., please." The receptionist answers, "Of course. We'll have it pressed and sent to your room."

The room is also very cold, because the thermostat is not working. Ten minutes later, the hotel has it checked by maintenance. On the last day, her train is at six, so she gets her bags stored at luggage storage. At the fair, a colleague says, "Your suit looks perfect!" Lucimara smiles: "I had it pressed at the hotel."

1. Who presses Lucimara's suit?
A Lucimara presses it herself in the room before she goes to the fair.
B The hotel presses it for her.
C A colleague from the fair presses it the next morning.
2. Why is the room cold?
A Because the thermostat is not working.
B Because the window is open and maintenance cannot close it until the morning.
C Because the do-not-disturb sign is on the door all night.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "I had pressed my suit at the hotel by the staff."
B "I had my suit pressed at the hotel."
C "I had my suit press at the hotel."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Have Something Done

GRAMMAR

How to ask someone to do a service for you.

FormMeaningExample
have + object + past participlesomeone else does the work for youI'd like to have my suit pressed.
get + object + past participlethe same, a little more informalCould I get my shirts cleaned?
had + object + past participlethe pastI had my bags stored at the front desk.
will have + object + past participlea promise of a serviceWe'll have the thermostat checked.
RuleOrder: have / get + the thing + past participle. Compare: "I pressed my suit" (I did it) and "I had my suit pressed" (the hotel did it for me).

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each real situation at the hotel.

Your suit is wrinkled, and the fair is tomorrow. You call the front desk:
A "I'd like to have my suit pressed, please."
B "I would like to press my suit by the hotel, please, before the fair tomorrow."
C "Press my suit. The fair is tomorrow."
It is 1 a.m., and the room next door is very loud. You say:
A "The next room is very loud and I cannot sleep, so I will go there and talk to them now."
B "I'm sorry to call so late. The room next door is very loud. Could you have someone speak to them?"
C "Noise! Noise! Do something now!"
Check-out is at noon. Your train is at six. You ask:
A "Could I have my bags stored until five?"
B "Could I store my bags myself in the lobby until the train leaves at six o'clock?"
C "Can I stay in the room until six without paying anything extra?"
There is a minibar charge on your bill, but you did not use it. You say:
A "I did not use the minibar. Could you take it off my bill?"
B "The minibar is very expensive in this hotel, and the prices are not clear on the menu."
C "I had the minibar used, so please check it."
