Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

When you talk about how you feel about an activity, the second verb changes shape. "I enjoy watching the race." "I like cooking at the weekend." "I do not mind working late once a week." "I cannot stand sitting in traffic." All of them take -ing, and none of them takes to. The same happens after a preposition: "I unwind by cooking", "she is good at running". The other family is the verbs of intention, and those work the opposite way: "I want to take up cycling", "I need to book the table". In Portuguese there is a small word between the two verbs in both cases, and that is exactly why the to keeps appearing where English does not want it.

1. Why is it "I enjoy watching" and not "I enjoy to watch"?
A Because watching is a noun.
B Because enjoy is a verb of feeling about an activity, and those take -ing.
C Because the sentence is in the present.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "I like play football on Sundays."
B "I like playing football on Sundays."
C "I like to playing football on Sundays."
3. Which verb takes "to" and not "-ing"?
A mind
B cannot stand

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The verb plus -ing

GRAMMAR

Feeling about an activity takes -ing. Intention takes to.

First verbSecond verbExample
enjoy, like, love, hateverb + -ingI enjoy watching the race.
mind, cannot standverb + -ingI do not mind working late.
after a prepositionverb + -ingI unwind by cooking.
want, need, would liketo + base verbI want to take up cycling.

Spelling of the -ing form: a silent e disappears (take → taking), and a short vowel plus one consonant doubles it (run → running, sit → sitting). The negative goes on the first verb, never on the second: "I do not mind working", never "I mind not working".
