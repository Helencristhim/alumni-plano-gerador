Chapter 4: Verb plus -ing

What Happens to the Second Verb

One ending decides everything

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I enjoy watching the race on Sunday morning."

"I do not mind working late once a week."

"She likes running before work."

"I cannot stand sitting in traffic."

First verbSecond verbExample
enjoy, like, love, hateverb + -ingI enjoy watching the race.
mind, cannot standverb + -ingI do not mind working late.
after a prepositionverb + -ingI unwind by cooking.
want, need, would liketo + base verbI want to take up cycling.

The verb of feeling about an activity takes -ing. The verb of intention takes to. That single line decides almost every sentence you will say today.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

✗ WRONG

"I enjoy to watch the race."

✓ RIGHT

"I enjoy watching the race."

✗ WRONG

"I like play football on Sundays."

✓ RIGHT

"I like playing football on Sundays."

Portuguese puts a preposition between the two verbs, and the hand copies it into English as to. In English the second verb simply changes shape: watching, playing, running.

Dialogue

Monday Morning

R
Morning, Thiago. Good weekend?
T
Busy. Saturday disappeared into errands, and then I did nothing.
R
That counts as downtime. What do you enjoy doing when you actually stop?
T
I enjoy watching the race, and I like cooking when there is time.
R
Do you work out as well, or is that only in January?
T
Twice a week. I do not mind getting up at six, but I cannot stand running in the heat.
R
Then come with us on Saturday. We are taking up running again, at seven.
T
Seven on a day off. That is early, but I do not mind trying once.
R
Good. It beats binge-watching another series until two in the morning.
T
My screen time on Sunday was higher than on Monday. I am not proud of that.
R
Nobody is. See you at seven, and we can unwind at breakfast afterwards.
