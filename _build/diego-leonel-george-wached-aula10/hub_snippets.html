Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A lease -- the written contract that lets you live in a place for a fixed time
"The lease runs for twelve months from the first of July."
A deposit -- money you pay at the start and get back if nothing is damaged
"The deposit is one month, refundable when you move out."
Utilities -- electricity, gas, water and internet, paid separately from the rent
"Utilities are not included, so budget another hundred a month."
A landlord -- the person who owns the place you rent
"Ask the landlord who to call when something breaks."
A due date -- the day by which a payment has to arrive
"The due date is the fifth. After that there is a late fee."
A credit score -- a number that says how reliably you have paid in the past
"I have no credit score yet, because I have just arrived."
A statement -- the monthly list of everything that went in and out of an account
"Check the statement. There is a fee I did not expect."
Furnished -- with the furniture already in it
"The apartment is furnished, which saves the first month."
To set up -- to start an account or a service so that it exists
"I need to set up a bank account before the first payday."
An application fee -- money charged once, when you apply for something
"There is a fifty-dollar application fee, and it is not refundable."
To move in -- to start living in a new place
"I am moving in on the twenty-eighth, two days before I start."
To be up and running -- working properly and ready to use
"The internet should be up and running by Thursday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Passing the exam and arriving are two different projects, and the second one is almost entirely about things that have not happened yet. Which is why the week you land is, grammatically, a week of futures -- and English has three, with different jobs.

The present continuous is for what is already in the diary. I am moving in on the twenty-eighth. There is a date, a lease and a landlord who is expecting you. Nothing about that is a prediction.

Going to is for what you have decided but not yet arranged. I am going to set up a bank account this week. The decision is made; the appointment is not. It also covers what you can see coming: look at that queue -- this is going to take an hour.

And will, which the school taught as the default future and is not: it is for the decision you take while you speak -- no internet? Fine, I will call them now -- and for a guess: it will probably be up and running by Thursday. The rest is vocabulary that costs money if you get it wrong: the deposit comes back, the application fee does not, utilities are yours to set up, the due date is not the first, and you will have no credit score at all.

1. The move-in date is in the lease. Which form does the text say to use?
A I will move in on the twenty-eighth.
B I am moving in on the twenty-eighth.
C I move in on the twenty-eighth.
2. You have decided to open a bank account but have not booked anything. You say:
A I am going to set up a bank account this week.
B I will set up a bank account this week.
C I am setting up a bank account this week.
3. Why does the text say will is not the default future?
A Because it is informal and should be avoided in writing.
B Because it is only used in conditional sentences.
C Because it belongs to a decision taken while speaking, or to a guess -- not to what is already arranged or already decided.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Future Forms -- Will, Going To and the Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

-ing for what is in the diary, going to for what you have decided, will for what you decide as you speak or only guess.

JobFormExample
Arranged, with a datepresent continuousI am moving in on the twenty-eighth.
Decided, not yet arrangedgoing to + base verbI am going to set up an account this week.
Decided while speakingwill + base verbNo internet? I will call them now.
A prediction or a guesswill (+ probably)It will probably be ready by Thursday.
Evidence in front of yougoing toLook at that queue — this is going to take an hour.
A timetable that is not yourspresent simpleThe bank opens at nine.
The trapwill is not the default futureNot I will move in on the 28th when it is already booked.

Never: I will move in on the twenty-eighth (when it is in the lease) · I am going to call them now (deciding as you speak) · the internet is probably being ready by Thursday · the bank will open at nine every morning · I will to set up an account
