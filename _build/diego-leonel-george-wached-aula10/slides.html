Chapter 4: The Code

Arranged, Decided, or Decided Right Now

Three futures, and the difference is how settled the plan is

Grammar Discovery

Put These in Order, from Most Settled

"I am moving in on the twenty-eighth."

"I am going to open a bank account this week."

"There is no internet? Fine, I will call them now."

"The internet will probably be up and running by Thursday."

All four are about the future and all four are correct. One has a date in a calendar. One is a decision already taken but not arranged. One is being decided as the person speaks. One is a guess. Which is which?

JobFormExample
Arranged, with a datepresent continuousI am moving in on the twenty-eighth.
Decided, not yet arrangedgoing to + base verbI am going to open an account this week.
Decided while speakingwill + base verbNo internet? I will call them now.
A prediction or a guesswill (+ probably)It will probably be ready by Thursday.
Evidence in front of yougoing toLook at that queue — this is going to take an hour.
A timetable, not yourspresent simpleThe bank opens at nine.
The trapwill is not the default futureNot I will move in on the 28th when it is already booked.

In one line: -ing for what is in the diary, going to for what you have decided, will for what you are deciding now or only guessing.

Listening 1 · A Leasing Agent

What They Will Ask You For

A leasing agent explaining the process to somebody who has just arrived. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say is the usual problem for somebody who has just arrived, and what are the two ways round it?
No credit score. Either a guarantor, or paying two or three months up front.
2. What three payments does she list, and which one does not come back?
The application fee, the deposit and the first month. The application fee is the one that is not refundable.
3. What does she say about utilities?
That they are not included, that they have to be set up in his own name before he moves in, and that the internet is the one that takes longest.
Listening 2 · A Doctor Who Arrived

My First Month, Honestly

A physician who moved to the United States, on the month before the job started. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say he had prepared for, and what he had not?
He had prepared for the exams and the hospital. He had not prepared for the paperwork -- the account, the lease, the phone, all of which needed each other.
2. What is the circular problem he describes?
The bank wanted an address, the landlord wanted a bank account, and the phone company wanted both.
3. What does he say he would tell somebody arriving next month?
To do the bank account first, on the first weekday, and to write down the name of every person he speaks to.
