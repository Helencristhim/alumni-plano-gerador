Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

Cut It in Half -- Headline First, Then Stop

How to summarize something long for somebody with no time: the headline first, one reason, what you need, and a voice that goes down at the end so they know you have finished. Key words: a headline, the gist, a key takeaway, concise, to leave out, to get to the point, to ramble, an overview, to wrap up, to sum up. Structure: short sentences with a full stop, and the words that tell the listener where you are.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A headline -- the main point of a message, said first and in one sentence
"The headline is that we closed the month on time."
The gist -- the general meaning of something, without the details
"I did not read the whole report, but I got the gist."
A key takeaway -- the one idea that people should remember after a meeting
"The key takeaway from today is that March is the risky month."
Concise -- short and clear, with no words that are not needed
"Her update was concise: three sentences and a number."
To leave out -- to decide not to include something
"I left out the history and kept the impact."
To get to the point -- to say the important thing without a long introduction
"Please get to the point. We have five minutes."
To ramble -- to talk for a long time without a clear direction
"When I am nervous, I ramble and forget the main point."
An overview -- a short description of the whole situation, without the details
"Can you give us a quick overview of the Brazil numbers?"
To wrap up -- to finish a meeting or a presentation
"Let me wrap up with one number."
To sum up -- to say the most important points again, very briefly, at the end
"To sum up, we are on time and on budget."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Tom manages the finance team of a retail company in Chicago. Every Friday he sends one update to his vice president, and for two years it was a long email. “This week we had some problems with the new system and two people were sick and the bank was late and finally we closed on Thursday night.” His boss told him the truth: “I read your first line and I stop.”

So Tom changed two things. First, he put the headline at the top: “We closed the month on time.” Full stop. Then one reason: “It was hard, because the new system was slow.” Full stop. Then what he needed: “What I need is one more week of support from IT.” Three short sentences instead of one long one with four ands.

The second change was in meetings. He started to use small words that tell people where he is: first, the main point is, to sum up. And he stopped rambling at the end. Now he says the key takeaway, lets his voice go down, and waits. His vice president answers faster, because she knows he has finished.

B It was in the wrong language.

What did Tom put first in his new update?

B The list of problems.
C The name of the new system.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Short Sentences and Signposts

GRAMMAR

One idea, one sentence, one full stop. Small words tell the listener where you are.

JobFormExample
The main point, firstThe headline is… / In short, …The headline is that we closed on time.
A reason, one sentenceIt was…, because…It was hard, because the system was slow.
A requestWhat I need is…What I need is one more week.
OrderFirst, … / Then, …First, the numbers. Then, the risk.
The endTo sum up, … / Let me wrap up.To sum up, we are on time.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The CFO gives you thirty seconds. You start with:
A "So, this month, as you know, the system changed in January, and then…"
B "The headline is that we closed on time."
C "There were many things happening this month."
You have three ideas in one long sentence. The better version is:
A "We closed on time. It was hard. The bank was late."
B "And we closed, and so, and then it was hard."
C "We closed and the bank was late and the team was tired and it was hard."
You want to end the meeting clearly. You say:
A "And also one more thing, and another one."
B "So yeah, that is more or less everything, I think, maybe."
C "To sum up, we are on time and the risk is in March."
A colleague rambles for four minutes. You say politely:
A "Please continue for ten more minutes."
B "Sorry, what is the headline?"
C "Stop talking."
