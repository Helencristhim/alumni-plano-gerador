Podcast
NPR Shots -- "For better treatment, doctors and patients share the decisions"

Exactly this lesson, reported from an American hospital: how physicians lay out risk so that a patient can weigh it, and what changes in the consultation when both options are given the same weight. Audio and full transcript on the same page.

Tip: listen once and write down every sentence a doctor uses to present an option. Rewrite two of them in the second conditional, as if nothing had been decided yet.

Audiobook
Paul Kalanithi -- "When Breath Becomes Air" (audiobook preview)

A neurosurgeon who became the patient, writing about the conversations he had conducted for years and then heard from the other chair. It is the register of this lesson at its most demanding: options, honesty, and a decision that belongs to somebody else.

Tip: listen to the preview and note every sentence where he describes a possibility rather than a fact -- might, could, would. That is the grammar of an open choice.

Music
Bob Dylan -- "The Times They Are A-Changin’"

Three minutes of conditional English sung slowly enough to write down: if your time to you is worth savin’, then you better start swimmin’. Almost every verse is an if-clause followed by a consequence, which is the structure you have now met twice, in lesson 8 and again today.

Tip: read the lyrics and mark every if. For each one, decide whether it is a real condition or a hypothetical, and say what the second half would look like with would instead of will.
