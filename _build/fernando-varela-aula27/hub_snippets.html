Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Show around -- to walk with a visitor and show them a place
"Let me show you around the building."
Furniture -- the tables, beds, chairs and sofas in a home
"Most of the furniture came from donations."
Space -- an empty area that you can use
"There is not much space in the kitchen."
Supplies -- things that people need every day, like food, soap and medicine
"We buy supplies for the apartments every month."
Facilities -- the rooms, buildings and equipment for a special use
"The building has good facilities: a laundry room and a small gym."
Feedback -- comments about how good something is, and how to make it better
"The families gave us a lot of good feedback."
Impressed -- you think something is very good, and you admire it
"Sarah was really impressed with the volunteers."
Worth it -- good enough for the money, time or work that you give
"It was a lot of work, but it was worth it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Sarah is in Sao Paulo, and Fernando shows her around the pilot. How many families stayed in the apartments? Eight families, and a few of them stayed for more than a month. How much money did the network spend on furniture? Not much: a lot of the furniture came from donations.

The kitchen is small, and there is only a little space. But the families gave a lot of good feedback, and there were not many problems. Sarah says: "I'm really impressed. I think it was worth it."

1. "How many families stayed in the apartments?" Why many and not much?
A Because many is the word for big numbers, and much is the word for small numbers, like one or two families.
B Because we can count families: one family, two families.
C Because many is for money.
2. "There is only a little space." What does this mean?
A There is a small amount of space.
B There is a lot of space.
C There are a few spaces in the kitchen, and each one is big enough for a table, four chairs and a sofa.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A A lot of the furnitures came from donations, because they had many furnitures to give.
B Many furniture came from donations.
C A lot of the furniture came from donations.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- How Much, How Many

GRAMMAR

Many and a few count. Much and a little measure. A lot of does both.

WithRuleExample
Things you can countHow many? · many · a few · not manyHow many families stayed? A few volunteers come on Fridays.
Things you can't countHow much? · much · a little · not muchHow much money did it cost? There is a little space.
Botha lot ofa lot of families · a lot of furniture
No pluralmoney, space, time, furniture, feedbacka lot of furniture (not furnitures)

Ask yourself: can I count it? Families, volunteers and apartments: yes, so use many or a few. Money, space and furniture: no, so use much or a little.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
