Chapter 3: The Code

How Much or How Many?

how much · how many · a lot of · a few · a little

Grammar Discovery

What Do the Orange Words Count?

"How many families stayed in the apartments?"

"How much money did the pilot cost?"

"We have a few volunteers on weekends."

"There is only a little space in the kitchen."

Look at the orange words. Can you count families? Can you count money? When do we say many, and when do we say much?

WithUseExample
Things you can count
families, volunteers, apartments		How many? · many · a few · not manyHow many families stayed? A few volunteers come on Fridays.
Things you can't count
money, space, time, furniture, feedback		How much? · much · a little · not muchHow much money did it cost? There is a little space.
Botha lot ofa lot of families · a lot of furniture
The trapFurniture, feedback, money and space have NO plural: a lot of furniture — never furnitures; How much money? — never How many money?

Many and a few count. Much and a little measure. A lot of does both.

Chapter 4: The Tour

Welcome to the Pilot

Sarah visits the apartments with Fernando

Dialogue

The Tour

S
So this is the building! Can you show me around?
F
Of course. We have three apartments here. Come and see the first one.
S
It's lovely. How many families stayed here in the first two months?
F
Eight families. A few of them stayed for more than a month.
S
And how much furniture did you buy?
F
Not much. A lot of the furniture came from donations.
S
I'm really impressed. But the kitchen is small. There isn't much space.
F
That's true. There's only a little space, but the families say it's enough.
S
And the feedback from the families? Was it good?
F
Very good. We got a lot of feedback, and almost all of it was positive. I think it was worth it.
