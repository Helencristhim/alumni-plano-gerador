Where You Start

A Life That Is Over

You already tell this story

In Portuguese, with one verb form. English keeps two, and picks by meaning

One works for everything

Used to: actions and states. I used to work. I used to be

The other has a limit

Would: only repeated actions. "I would be a surgeon" is not a sentence

Used to says it is over. Would says it happened again and again. Only one of them takes a state.

Chapter 3: The Code

Two Forms for One Past

And the line that separates a habit from a state

Discover the Rule

What Do These Have in Common?

I used to be a trauma surgeon.
I used to work the night shift twice a week.
When I was on-call, I would sleep in the hospital.
There used to be a clear line between medicine and technology.
Click to reveal the rule
Used to says one thing: this was true before, and it is not true now. It works for actions and for states: I used to work, I used to be, There used to be. Would also describes repeated past actions — When I was on-call, I would sleep in the hospital — but it does not work for states: I would be a surgeon is not a sentence. And after did, used to loses the -d: Did you use to work nights?, I did not use to think about data.

Chapter 4: The Night Shift

Sixty Hours a Week, and a Phone on the Pillow

Two people describing a hospital that does not exist anymore

Listening 1

What She Does Not Miss

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What did she use to do when she was on-call?
She would sleep in a small room next to the emergency department, with her phone on the pillow.
2. What does she miss, and what does she not miss?
She does not miss the hours or the routine. She misses the moment when a patient wakes up and asks what happened.
3. Why would she not go back?
Because the job she loved does not exist anymore — hospitals changed and she changed too.

Chapter 5: Practice

Six Questions About Before

The ones people ask when they find out you were a surgeon

Listening 2

One Computer for the Whole Floor

Read the questions, then listen.

1. How many computers used to be on the floor, and how was information recorded?
One computer for the whole floor, and everything was written by hand.
2. What would happen if a doctor wanted an old chart?
Somebody would go down to the basement to look for it, and it could take two hours.
3. Does he think it was better?
No. It was slower, and slower is not always safer — but there used to be a conversation between them that has not been rebuilt.
