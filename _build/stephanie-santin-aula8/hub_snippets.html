The Doctor You Used To Be

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These are the words of the routine you left behind, and people will ask you about it for the rest of your career.

Routine -- the set of things you do regularly, in the same order
"My routine changed completely when I left surgery."
Shift -- a fixed period of work, usually part of a day
"I used to work the night shift twice a week."
To supervise -- to be responsible for the work of other people
"I used to supervise a team of four residents."
Resident -- a doctor who is doing supervised training in a hospital
"The residents call me when they are not sure."
On-call -- available to come in at any moment if there is an emergency
"I was on-call every third weekend."
Demanding -- needing a lot of time, energy or attention
"It was a demanding job, and I loved it."
To reflect on -- to think carefully about something that happened
"It took me two years to reflect on that decision."
Operating room -- the room in a hospital where surgery is done
"I have not been in an operating room since then."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Clara Vidal used to be a pediatric nurse in a public hospital in Salvador. She used to work twelve-hour shifts, and on the long nights she would walk through the ward every hour, even when nothing was wrong. She did not use to take holidays, because there was never anybody to replace her. Three years ago she moved to a small private clinic. The routine is calmer now. There used to be forty children on her floor; today she sees twelve a day and she knows all their names. She says she does not miss the shifts, but she still wakes up at four in the morning. That, she says, is the part that did not change.
Why does the text say "used to be a pediatric nurse"?
A Because she is still a pediatric nurse today
B Because it was true before and it is not true now
C Because she was a nurse for a very short time
Why is "would walk" possible in "on the long nights she would walk through the ward"?
A Because it is a repeated past action, not a state
B Because it describes how she felt
C Because it is a plan for the future

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used To and Would

GRAMMAR

Both talk about a past that is over. Used to works for actions and for states; would only for repeated actions.

past stateused to + verb (would is not possible)I used to be a surgeon. (not I would be a surgeon)
repeated past actionused to or wouldI used to check / I would check every chart.
negativedid not use to (no -d)I did not use to think about data.
questiondid + subject + use to (no -d)Did you use to work night shifts?

Careful with two things that look alike: I used to work nights (a past habit, over) and I am used to working nights (I am comfortable with it, and it may still be true today).

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
You are describing a job you had for ten years and do not have anymore. Which sentence is correct?
A "I would be a trauma surgeon."
B "I used to be a trauma surgeon."
C "I use to be a trauma surgeon."
You want to ask a colleague about a past habit. What do you ask?
A "Did you used to work night shifts?"
B "Did you use to work night shifts?"
C "Did you use to worked night shifts?"
You still work night shifts and you are comfortable with them. What do you say?
A "I used to work night shifts."
B "I am used to working night shifts."
