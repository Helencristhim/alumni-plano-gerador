Lesson 29 -- Pre-class

The Full Picture -- A Crisis Day

A typhoon closes the port of Kaohsiung and twelve thousand panels for Monterrey are stuck. One day, three moments: the status update, the call with the supplier, and the presentation to leadership. Key words: triage, ripple effect, worst-case scenario, single point of failure, war room, all hands on deck, status update, workaround, round-the-clock, get ahead of, paper trail, debrief. Structures: the crisis toolkit -- recommend that + base verb, should have + past participle, have something done, and mixed conditionals.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Triage -- to decide which problems are most urgent and deal with them first
"Triage the open orders before you call anyone."
Ripple effect -- a series of effects that spread out from one event
"The port closure has a ripple effect on three customers."
Worst-case scenario -- the worst result that could realistically happen
"In the worst-case scenario, the port stays closed for ten days."
Single point of failure -- one part of a system that stops everything when it fails
"One port should never be a single point of failure."
War room -- a room or team where people manage a crisis together
"Marcus opens the war room at seven."
All hands on deck -- a situation where everyone has to help
"It is all hands on deck until the panels ship."
Status update -- a short report on where things stand right now
"Send a status update to every stakeholder by nine."
Workaround -- a temporary way to avoid a problem when the normal way is blocked
"Shipping from Taichung is a workaround, not a fix."
Round-the-clock -- all day and all night, without stopping
"The Monterrey team is working round-the-clock."
Get ahead of -- to act early, before a problem becomes bigger
"Call Greg now and get ahead of the bad news."
Paper trail -- written records that show what was decided and when
"Keep a paper trail of every extra cost."
Debrief -- a meeting after an event to discuss what happened and what to learn
"We will hold a debrief when the crisis is over."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Thursday, 6:40 a.m. A typhoon has closed the port of Kaohsiung, and twelve thousand display panels for the Monterrey line are stuck there. Fabiana opens a war room with Marcus and starts with triage: Monterrey needs the panels by Monday, and everything else can wait a week. In the worst-case scenario, the port stays closed for ten days.

Marcus is angry with the supplier. "They should have moved part of the order last week," he says. Fabiana agrees, but blame can wait. She is having the panels moved to the port of Taichung as a workaround, and in her nine o'clock status update she writes: "We recommend that the Chicago order be shipped first."

At four, she presents the full picture to the leadership team. "If we hadn't added Monterrey last year, we would be out of stock today," she says. One port was a single point of failure; now the team wants a second port too. The CFO asks for a paper trail of every cost, and Priya plans a debrief for next week.

1. "We are having the panels moved to Taichung." Who moves the panels?
A Someone else does it for the company.
B Fabiana moves them herself, early in the morning, with Marcus.
C Marcus moves them.
2. "If we hadn't added Monterrey, we would be out of stock today." Is the company out of stock now?
A Yes, the company is out of stock today and cannot ship anything.
B No. They added Monterrey, so they have stock.
C It will be out of stock next year.
3. What does Fabiana do first in the war room?
A She blames the supplier for ignoring the storm warnings last week.
B She calls the CFO.
C She triages the orders.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Crisis Toolkit

GRAMMAR

No new grammar: four structures you already know, each one for a different moment of a crisis.

MomentStructureExample
The email: recommendrecommend / suggest / ask that + base verb (no to, no -s)We recommend that the order be shipped first.
The call: what went wrongshould have / could have + past participleThey should have moved the order last week.
The workaround: someone else does ithave + object + past participleWe are having the panels moved.
The presentation: past decision, present resultIf + past perfect, would + base verbIf we hadn't added Monterrey, we would be out of stock.
Questionchecking a past decisionShould we have moved the order last week?
Rememberbase verb after recommend that; participle after should havethat he call · should have called

Never: recommend that the order to be shipped · should have move · we are having moved the panels · if we didn't add Monterrey last year, we would be
