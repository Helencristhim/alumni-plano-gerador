Chapter 3: The Crisis Toolkit

Four Moments, Four Tools

The email, the call, the workaround, the presentation

Grammar Discovery

Four Moments, Four Tools

"We recommend that the Chicago order be shipped first."

"Formosa should have moved part of the order last week."

"We are having the panels moved to Taichung."

"If we hadn't added Monterrey, we would be out of stock today."

Each purple structure does a different job. Which one makes a formal recommendation? Which one criticizes something in the past? Which one says that someone else does the work? Which one connects a past decision to today?

The momentThe toolExample
The email: recommend formallyrecommend / suggest / ask that + base verbWe recommend that the order be shipped first.
The call: say what went wrongshould have / could have + past participleThey should have moved the order last week.
The workaround: someone else does ithave + object + past participleWe are having the panels moved.
The presentation: past decision, present resultIf + past perfect, would + base verbIf we hadn't added Monterrey, we would be out of stock.
The trapunder pressure, the forms get mixedNot that the order to be shipped, not if we didn't add Monterrey last year.

In one line: the crisis is new; the tools are the ones you already have.

Listening 1 · The Supplier

A Call from Tainan

Kevin Lin, from the panel supplier in Taiwan, leaves a voice message. Sound first — no text.

1. Why is the port of Kaohsiung closed, and for how long?
Because of the typhoon. It will stay closed for at least four days.
2. How many panels are waiting, and why can't they fly from Taipei?
Twelve thousand units are sitting in the port. The airlines are fully booked until Monday.
3. What does Kevin say his company should have done, and when will he send a written update?
They should have moved part of the order last week, when the first warnings came out. He will send a written update by noon, Fabiana's time.
Listening 2 · Leadership

What Priya Needs at Four

Priya Nair, VP of Operations, leaves a voice message. Sound first — no text.

1. What did Priya like about the status update?
It was short, clear, and honest about what they don't know yet.
2. What three things does the leadership team want at four?
The ripple effect on customers, the worst-case scenario with a real number, and Fabiana's recommendation.
3. What has the CFO asked for, and how long will Fabiana have?
That every cost be documented, so she must keep a paper trail. She will have fifteen minutes.
Real Document

The 9 a.m. Status Update

STATUS UPDATE #1 · KAOHSIUNG PORT CLOSURE
From: Fabiana Michelly Silva · To: Supply Chain Leadership, Sales, Monterrey Plant · 9:00 a.m.
What happenedA typhoon closed the port of Kaohsiung last night, for at least four days. 12,000 display panels are stuck there.
ImpactMonterrey line at risk from Monday. Chicago order: up to 2 days late.
What we are doingWe are having the panels moved to the port of Taichung. We recommend that the Chicago order be shipped first.
Next update1:00 p.m. today, after the call with the supplier.
1. Which line is at risk, and from when?
The Monterrey line, from Monday.
2. What is Fabiana doing about the panels, and which order does she want shipped first?
She is having the panels moved to the port of Taichung. She recommends that the Chicago order be shipped first.
3. When is the next update, and why then?
At 1:00 p.m., after the call with the supplier.
