Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

The Gallery -- Describe It So I Can See It

How to describe a painting, a church or a famous place: what it is, when it was made, who it was made by and how many people visit it. Key words: a masterpiece, a landmark, a fresco, a sculpture, breathtaking, to restore, a guided tour, the ceiling, crowded, a replica. Structure: the passive voice in the present (is visited) and in the past (was built, were painted), with by for the person who did it.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A masterpiece -- a work of art that is one of the best ever made
"The Last Supper is a masterpiece."
A landmark -- a famous building or place that helps you know where you are
"The tower is the most famous landmark in the city."
A fresco -- a painting made on a wall or a ceiling while the plaster is still wet
"The frescoes in that church are very old."
A sculpture -- a work of art made of stone, metal or wood
"The sculpture in the square was made by a local artist."
Breathtaking -- so beautiful that it surprises you
"The view from the top was breathtaking."
To restore -- to repair something old so it looks like new again
"The church was restored after the fire."
A guided tour -- a visit where an expert shows you the place and explains it
"We took a guided tour of the old town."
The ceiling -- the top part of a room, above your head
"The ceiling is covered with paintings."
Crowded -- full of people
"The museum is always crowded on Sundays."
A replica -- an exact copy of a famous object
"The statue in the square is a replica. The original is in a museum."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Marco is a guide in Florence. Every morning he says the same thing to his group: “This cathedral was built over more than one hundred years. The dome was designed by Brunelleschi, and the frescoes inside were painted by many different artists.” The tourists do not know these people, so the important thing is the building, not the builder.

“Today the cathedral is visited by thousands of people every day,” he says. “Photos are allowed, but please, no flash.” This is the present: it happens every day, now.

Some visitors ask: “When was it restored?” Marco smiles. “Many times. The last time, it was cleaned by a team of experts for five years.” To make the passive, he uses be plus the past participle, and by for the people who did it.

"The dome was designed by Brunelleschi." Who did the work?
A The tourists in Marco’s group.
B Brunelleschi.
C Marco, the guide.
"The cathedral is visited by thousands of people every day." When does this happen?
A Only in the past, a long time ago.
B Only on the day Marco gives his tour.
C Now, as a normal thing, every day.
Why does Marco say "It was built" and not "It built"?
A Because the building did not do the action. You need was plus the past participle.
B Because build is a regular verb.
C Because it happened a very long time ago.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive Voice

GRAMMAR

When the thing is more important than the person: be plus the past participle. Use by for the person who did it.

JobFormExample
Facts now, every dayis / are + past participleThe museum is visited by millions every year.
Finished pastwas / were + past participleThe chapel was painted in the 1500s.
Who did it... + by + personIt was designed by a French sculptor.
QuestionsWas / Is + thing + past participle?When was it built?
Negativewas not / is not + past participlePhotos are not allowed inside.
Irregular verbsbuild → built, make → madeThe tiles were made by hand.

Never: It built in 1500 · It was build in 1500 · It was painted from Michelangelo · It is built in 1931

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

Two friends talk about a trip to Rome. Put the conversation in a natural order.

?"Two hours? Was it worth it?"
?"Absolutely. It was breathtaking. I would go again tomorrow."
?"The Sistine Chapel. The ceiling was painted by Michelangelo."
?"So, what was the best thing you saw in Rome?"
?"Very. We waited two hours, and photos were not allowed inside."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A tourist asks about an old church in your city. You say:
A "It built in 1890."
B "It was build in 1890 from Italian workers who came here."
C "It was built in 1890 by Italian workers."
You want to know who made a sculpture. You ask the guide:
A "Who it made, and in which year did the artist finish it?"
B "Who was it made by?"
C "Who did make by?"
A friend wants to visit the museum on Sunday. You warn him:
A "It is very crowded on Sundays."
B "It is crowding very much on Sundays, so be careful."
C "It crowded on Sundays."
The painting in the hotel is not the original. You say:
A "It is a replica. The original keeps in Paris."
B "It is a replica. The original is kept in a museum in Paris."
C "It is a replica. The original is keep in a museum in Paris, I think."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Think of a place or a work of art that you will never forget: a church on the Camino, a painting in Italy, a city in Peru. Record about one minute: what it is, when it was built or made, who it was made by, and how you felt when you saw it.

TED Talk
Maurizio Seracini: “The secret lives of paintings” (14 min)

An Italian engineer uses science to look under famous paintings in Florence and finds what was hidden there by the artists. Full of the passive: what was painted, what was covered, what was found.

Tip: every time you hear was or were plus a verb, pause and say the sentence again.
