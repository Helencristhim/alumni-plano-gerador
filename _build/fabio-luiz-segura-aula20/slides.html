Chapter 4: Getting Used to It

Used To, Be Used To, Get Used To

Three structures for then, now, and on the way

Grammar Discovery

Then, Now, and On the Way

"I used to rush past every number."

"Now I'm used to presenting data in English."

"I'm getting used to starting conversations with strangers."

"I wasn't used to small talk at the bar, but I got used to it."

Which sentence is about an old habit that stopped? In the other three, what comes after used to: a base verb, or something else?

MeaningFormExample
Old habit that stopped (review)used to + base verbI used to rush past every number.
It is normal and familiar nowbe used to + verb-ing / nounI'm used to presenting data.
The process of it becoming normalget used to + verb-ing / nounI'm getting used to starting conversations.
Negativenot be used to + -ing / nounI wasn't used to small talk.
QuestionAre you used to ...? · Did you get used to ...?Are you used to speaking on panels?
The trapafter be / get used to, never the base verbNot I'm used to present. Say I'm used to presenting.

In one line: used to + verb is the past; be used to + -ing is now; get used to + -ing is the road between them.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Two Voices on Progress

A coach, and a banker who has been there

Listening 1 · The Coach

The Plateau

A language coach explains why progress seems to stop. Sound first — no text.

1. When do most of her executive clients feel they have stopped improving?
After three or four months, when the easy progress is over.
2. Why does she say the feeling is often wrong?
Because the progress is still happening, but it is in things you cannot see yourself, like speed, confidence and the words that come without thinking.
3. What are her three tips?
Record yourself once a month and compare; do one thing outside your comfort zone every week; and set one stretch goal at a time.
Listening 2 · The Banker

I Used To Hide

A banker from Mexico City remembers his first conferences in English. Sound first — no text.

1. What did he use to do at his first conferences?
He used to hide at the back of the room, check his phone at the coffee breaks, and leave before the dinner.
2. What is he used to now?
Starting conversations with strangers and presenting numbers on a panel.
3. What is he still getting used to, and what is his advice?
Jokes in English and very fast accents. His advice: do not wait to feel ready, because you get used to it by doing it.
