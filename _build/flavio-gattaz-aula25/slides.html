Chapter 3: The Code

Getting Things Done

Have, get, and the verb that comes after the person

Grammar Discovery

Who Does the Work?

"I’ll have the crew clean the slab before nine."

"Could you get the electrician to check the panel today?"

"We need to get the drawings printed by noon."

"Make sure the scaffolding is inspected before anyone goes up."

In none of these sentences does the speaker do the work. Who does it? Look at the verb after have the crew and after get the electrician: are they the same? In the third sentence, do we know who prints the drawings?

ToolFormExample
have + person + verbthe verb with no toI’ll have the crew clean the slab.
get + person + to + verbthe verb with toGet the electrician to check the panel.
have / get + thing + past participlethe person is not importantWe need to get the drawings printed.
I need you to / I’d like you todirect and polite delegationI’d like you to walk the third floor with me.
make sure (that)you delegate the result and keep the responsibilityMake sure the scaffolding is inspected.

In one line: have somebody do it, get somebody to do it, get it done — and always say by when.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Monday Walkthrough

One conversation, two recordings, one plan for the week

Dialogue

Seven Thirty, Third Floor

J
Good morning. Before we start the walkthrough, can I give you a heads-up? The inspector moved his visit to Thursday.
F
Thanks for the heads-up. Then let us prioritize the scaffolding. Make sure it is inspected by Wednesday afternoon.
J
OK. And the slab on the third floor? Paulo's crew is waiting for instructions.
F
I'll have the crew clean it before nine, and I'd like you to double-check the levels after that.
J
Got it. Who is going to call the electrician? The panel still has not been checked.
F
I will hand that off to Paulo. He can get the electrician to check it today.
J
So, to be on the same page: scaffolding by Wednesday, slab before nine, panel today.
F
Exactly. Keep me posted, and call me if anything changes on Thursday.
Comprehension

About Jessica

1. What heads-up does Jessica give at the start?
The inspector moved his visit to Thursday.
2. What does she say about the electrical panel?
It still has not been checked, and she asks who is going to call the electrician.
3. How does she check that they are on the same page?
She repeats the plan: scaffolding by Wednesday, slab before nine, panel today.
Listening 1 · A Site Manager

Four Parts of an Instruction

A site manager on how she gives instructions to her crews. Sound first — no text.

1. According to her, what are the four parts of a good instruction?
Who does it, what exactly, by when, and how you will check it.
2. What happens when an instruction has no deadline?
It becomes a suggestion, and it is done when somebody has time.
3. Why does she ask people to repeat the instruction back?
Because it costs ten seconds, and a misunderstanding on site can cost a week.
Listening 2 · A Foreman

The Best Boss I Ever Had

An American foreman on the director who taught him to delegate. Sound first — no text.

1. What did his first boss do every Monday?
He walked the site with the foremen and gave each of them three priorities for the week.
2. What did the boss never do, according to him?
He never did the foremen’s work for them, even when it was faster.
3. What did the boss ask at the end of every instruction?
What do you need from me to get this done?
