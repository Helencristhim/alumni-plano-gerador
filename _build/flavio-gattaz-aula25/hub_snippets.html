Monday Morning on Site -- Who, What, By When

Block 3 begins on site: a Monday walkthrough and a week of tasks to delegate. A clear instruction says who does it, what exactly and by when. Key words: a walkthrough, a crew, a foreman, to delegate, to prioritize, a punch list, to hand something off, to double-check, a heads-up, to keep somebody posted, to be on the same page, to get the ball rolling. Structure: causative have and get -- have somebody do something, get somebody to do something, get something done -- and make sure, I need you to, I'd like you to.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A walkthrough -- a visit around a place to check the work, step by step
"Monday starts with a walkthrough of the third floor."
A crew -- a group of people who work together on one job
"The concrete crew starts at seven."
A foreman -- the person who leads a crew and organizes its work on site
"Ask the foreman how many people he needs tomorrow."
To delegate -- to give part of your work or responsibility to another person
"A director who cannot delegate becomes the bottleneck."
To prioritize -- to decide which tasks are most important and do them first
"We cannot do everything today, so let us prioritize."
A punch list -- a list of small jobs that must be finished or fixed before a project is complete
"The punch list for the lobby still has twenty items."
To hand something off -- to give a task to another person so that he or she continues it
"I will hand off the supplier calls to Jessica this week."
To double-check -- to check something again, to be completely sure
"Double-check the measurements before the crew cuts the steel."
A heads-up -- a short warning about something that will happen
"Thanks for the heads-up about the inspector."
To keep somebody posted -- to give somebody regular news about a situation
"Keep me posted on the crane delivery."
To be on the same page -- to agree and understand a situation in the same way
"Before the crew starts, let us make sure we are on the same page."
To get the ball rolling -- to start an activity or a process
"Let me get the ball rolling with the safety briefing."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On a construction site, a director almost never does the work himself. His English has to say who does it. The most common tool is have + person + verb, with no to: I'll have the crew clean the slab before nine. With get, the verb takes to: Could you get the electrician to check the panel today? Brazilian speakers often say "have the crew to clean", which sounds wrong to a native speaker.

Sometimes the person does not matter, only the result. Then you use get + thing + past participle: We need to get the drawings printed by noon. Nobody cares who prints them. And when you want to delegate the result but keep the responsibility, make sure is the safest choice: Make sure the scaffolding is inspected before anyone goes up.

The grammar is only half of a good instruction. The other half is the deadline. An instruction with no "by when" is a suggestion, and on site a suggestion is done when somebody has time. Close every instruction the same way: Keep me posted.

1. According to the text, which form takes the verb with no "to"?
A Get + person + verb, as in "get the electrician check the panel today".
B Make sure + person + verb.
C Have + person + verb.
2. When do you use "get + thing + past participle"?
A When you want to do the work yourself, later, after everybody has gone home.
B When the person who does the work is not important.
C When the work is finished.
3. What does the text say about an instruction with no deadline?
A It is a suggestion, and it is done when somebody has time.
B It is clearer, because the crew can choose the best moment to do the job.
C It is more polite.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Have, Get and Make Sure

GRAMMAR

Have somebody do it. Get somebody to do it. Get it done. And always say by when.

ToolFormExample
have + person + verbno toI'll have the crew clean the slab.
get + person + to + verbwith toGet the electrician to check the panel.
get + thing + past participlethe person is not importantGet the drawings printed by noon.
I need you to / I'd like you todirect, polite delegationI'd like you to double-check the levels.
make sure (that)delegate the result, keep the responsibilityMake sure the scaffolding is inspected.

Never: have the crew to clean · get the electrician check · get printed the drawings · make sure the scaffolding inspected
