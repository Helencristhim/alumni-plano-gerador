Chapter 3: The Code

Grammar Discovery

What the Paper Is Really Testing

"The authority was informed of the change in April."

"She said that the ruling had not been granted."

"The desk recommends narrowing down the options first."

"If the group had filed on time, it would not have lost the benefit."

"The memo which was sent on Monday has been withdrawn."

"The writer implies that the deadline will move, but he never says it."

Six real exam items. Five of the structures are already yours. So what is being measured?

TaskWhat it measuresWhat to do
Skimmingthe main ideaThirty seconds. First and last sentence of each paragraph. Ignore every number.
Scanningone specific detailDo not read. Hunt the name, the date or the figure the question gives you.
Inferencewhat is implied, not statedAsk one question: if this sentence is true, what else must be true?
The paraphrase trapvocabulary, not memoryThe right option almost never repeats the words of the passage. Matching words are bait.
Key word transformationthe structures you already haveKeep the meaning, change the form: active to passive, direct to reported, verb to gerund.
The written answerFive moves, always the same: your view → the evidence → the other side → the limit of the other side → the conclusion. Nobody counts your ideas. The examiner asks whether sentence two follows sentence one.
The rule under all of itRead the question before the text. Everything above is worth nothing if you meet the question after four minutes of careful reading.

The exam does not ask for English you do not have. It asks for it faster, and only where the question points.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"The option uses the same words as the passage, so it must be the right one."
"The option paraphrases the passage, so it is probably the right one."
"The question asks for one reason. I gave three, because all three are true."
"The question asks for one reason. I gave the one that is relevant, in one sentence."
"The writer used the word difficult, so the writer is against the reform."
"The writer calls it difficult and still recommends it, so the tone is cautious, not hostile."

All three come from the same habit: reading for words instead of reading for the question.

Same Meaning, Other Form

Say the full sentence first, then click to check

"The authority granted the ruling in April." (WAS) ___"The ruling was granted by the authority in April."
"We will not reopen the decision," she said. (SAID) ___"She said that they would not reopen the decision."
"We advise the board to narrow down the options." (RECOMMEND) ___"We recommend narrowing down the options."
"The group did not file on time, so it lost the benefit." (HAD) ___"If the group had filed on time, it would not have lost the benefit."
"Nobody has answered the request yet." (BEEN) ___"The request has not been answered yet."
"It is possible that the structure will be challenged." (MIGHT) ___"The structure might be challenged."
The Written Answer

Five Moves, Always the Same

The same five openings work for every prompt you will be given

1 · YOUR VIEW

"In my view, the rules are too complex for a foreign investor to navigate alone."

2 · THE EVIDENCE

"This is supported by the fact that three separate authorities can review the same payment."

3 · THE OTHER SIDE

"One could argue that professional advice already solves the problem."

4 · ITS LIMIT

"However, it should be noted that the cost of that advice falls on the smallest investors."

5 · THE CONCLUSION

"In conclusion, the evidence suggests that simplification would open the market rather than close it."

Listening

The Client Reads Your Two Pages

A voicemail from the compliance director. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she say about the shape of his answer?
She could follow it from the first line to the last, so it is coherent, and she tells him to keep doing whatever he changed in January.
2. Why does she want the third paragraph cut or moved?
It is accurate but it does not address the question she asked, and the board would read it as the firm defending something.
3. She says "a number is never vague". What does that imply about the word significant?
That repeating it four times tells the reader nothing. She is asking for a figure, not for a stronger adjective.
Listening 2

Eleven Years of Marking Papers

What separates a comfortable pass from failing by two marks. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What do the candidates who fail by two marks do wrong?
They read the passage first, carefully, the way you read a contract, and meet the question last. That costs them four minutes out of seventy-five.
2. Why is hunting for matching words dangerous?
The correct option paraphrases the passage instead of repeating it, so matching words lead the candidate to the wrong answer on purpose.
3. What does he say the examiner looks for in the written part?
Not the number of ideas: whether your second sentence follows your first. One clear idea, defended, beats three ideas mentioned.
