Documentary
Trust No One: The Hunt for the Crypto King

Investors, journalists and investigators reconstruct the collapse of a Canadian crypto exchange and the two hundred million dollars that went missing with its founder. Connection to Lesson 12: almost nobody in the film says what they mean directly, so you spend ninety minutes doing exactly what a reading paper asks — working out what is implied from what is said.

Tip: watch with English subtitles and stop three times. Each time, write one sentence starting with "The speaker implies that..." and one starting with "The tone suggests that...". Then summarize the whole film in three sentences, without using its own words.

Podcast
Talking Tax — "How Transfer Pricing Can Help Fix a Student Athlete Pay Problem"

Twelve minutes on whether payments to student athletes are real market transactions or disguised salary, argued with the same tools you use on an intercompany fee. Connection to Lesson 12: this is the exact length and speed of a listening part, in your own field, with an argument you can follow and a conclusion nobody states out loud.

Tip: treat it as an exam part. Before you press play, write two questions you expect the episode to answer. Listen once at full speed, answer them, then listen again and note one thing the speakers imply but never say.

Video
English with Cambridge — "B2 First Julia and Isabela"

The official Cambridge recording of a full B2 First speaking test, with two real candidates and two examiners. Connection to Lesson 12: this is the certificate you are deciding on, seen from the inside — how the tasks are worded, how long each part lasts, and how much a candidate is expected to produce before the examiner moves on.

Tip: pause after every examiner question and answer it yourself, out loud, before the candidates do. Time yourself: a good part-two answer runs one minute, and stopping at fifteen seconds is the most common way to lose marks.
