Chapter 4: The Careful Sentence

Bad News, Written Well

Not vague, and not guilty

Grammar Discovery

Same Fact, Careful Words

"The BPO posted the accrual twice." → "It appears that the accrual was posted twice."

"Account 4100 is wrong." → "There may be a difference in account 4100."

"We have a problem with one invoice." → "I would like to bring to your attention one pending invoice."

"We made a mistake." → "Upon review, it was identified that one entry was duplicated."

Is the fact different on the right? What do the orange words change? And what comes after It appears that, and after There may be?

ShapeWhat it doesExample
It appears that + sentenceyou found something and you are still checking itIt appears that the accrual was posted twice.
It seems that + sentencethe same idea, a little less formalIt seems that the file was not uploaded.
There may be + nouna possible problem, not yet confirmedThere may be a difference in account 4100.
I would like to bring to your attention + nounyou are raising an issue politelyI would like to bring to your attention one pending item.
Upon review, it was identified that...the most formal form, passive, for the auditorUpon review, it was identified that one invoice is missing.
the next step (always)a careful sentence is followed by what you will do...we are checking it and will confirm by Thursday.

In one line: say the fact, say how sure you are, and say what you will do next.

Listening 1 · An External Auditor

The Ones I Answer First

An external auditor on two hundred client emails. Sound first — no text.

1. What do the emails she answers first tell her in the first line?
Why they are writing.
2. Which emails does she leave for later?
The long ones, with three paragraphs of thank you and hope you are well before the information.
3. Why does the careful sentence not scare her?
Because it tells her the client found the problem first.
Listening 2 · A Colleague in Mexico

The Words Already in My Mouth

A senior accountant on what changed when he stopped translating.

1. What did he do with his emails for two years?
He wrote them in Spanish and asked the AI to translate them.
2. What happened when the director called him?
He could not answer the two questions in English, because he did not know the words.
3. What does he do now, step by step?
He writes the draft himself in English, reads it out loud once, then asks the AI to check it and looks at every change.
Real Document

The Email to the Auditor

RE: MARCH ADJUSTMENT — SUPPORT
From: Keitma Gomes · To: Rachel Stone, External Audit

Opening: Hi Rachel, further to your question on Tuesday regarding the March adjustment.

Core message: The adjustment corrects an accrual that was posted twice in February.

Detail: Please find the two entries and the reconciliation attached. It appears that one supplier invoice is still missing; we are checking it with the BPO.

Close: I will send the invoice by Thursday. Could you please confirm receipt of the attachments?

Best regards,
Keitma

Note: four parts, seventy words, one careful sentence.
1. What does the March adjustment correct?
An accrual that was posted twice in February.
2. What is still missing, and who is Keitma checking it with?
One supplier invoice; she is checking it with the BPO.
3. What two things does the close say?
That she will send the invoice by Thursday, and a request to confirm receipt of the attachments.
