Lesson 14 -- Pre-class

The Email You Write Before the AI

A professional email you draft yourself, in four parts, with a careful sentence for bad news. Key words: attach, further to, regarding, confirm receipt, accordingly, inform, draft, proofread. Structure: hedging -- it appears that, there may be, I would like to bring to your attention.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To attach -- to add a file to an email so that it travels with the message
"Please find the reconciliation attached."
Further to -- a formal way to open an email that continues an earlier call, email or meeting
"Further to our call this morning, here are the updated figures."
Regarding -- a formal word meaning about, used to name the topic of an email
"Regarding your question about the March adjustment, the support is attached."
To confirm receipt -- to tell somebody that their message or document has arrived
"Could you please confirm receipt by Friday?"
Accordingly -- in the way that the situation requires; as a result of what was just said
"The deadline moved to Thursday, so please plan accordingly."
To inform -- to tell somebody a fact officially
"I would like to inform you that the close is on track."
A draft -- a first version of a text, written before it is checked and sent
"I wrote the draft myself before I opened any tool."
To proofread -- to read a text carefully to find and correct mistakes before you send it
"I always proofread the email once before I send it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Two emails to an external auditor about the same problem. The first: The BPO posted the accrual twice. Account 4100 is wrong. True, but it sounds like an accusation, and the auditor now has two worries instead of one. The second: It appears that the accrual was posted twice. There may be a difference in account 4100, and we are checking it. I will confirm by Thursday. Same facts, and now the auditor knows that you found the problem and that you are dealing with it.

This is hedging: careful words that say how sure you are. After It appears that and It seems that comes a full sentence, with a subject and a verb. After There may be comes a noun: there may be a difference. To raise an issue politely, use I would like to bring to your attention + a noun. And for the most formal email, the passive: Upon review, it was identified that one entry was duplicated.

Hedging is not hiding. A careful sentence is always followed by the next step: what you are checking, and when you will come back. Without the next step, a careful sentence is only vague. With it, it is the most professional sentence in the email.

2. What comes after There may be?
A A noun, as in there may be a difference.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Careful Sentence

GRAMMAR

Say the fact, say how sure you are, and say what you will do next.

ShapeWhat it doesExample
It appears that + sentenceyou found something and are still checking itIt appears that the accrual was posted twice.
It seems that + sentencethe same idea, a little less formalIt seems that the file was not uploaded.
There may be + nouna possible problem, not yet confirmedThere may be a difference in account 4100.
I would like to bring to your attention + nounraising an issue politelyI would like to bring to your attention one pending item.
Upon review, it was identified that...the most formal form, passiveUpon review, it was identified that one entry was duplicated.
+ the next stepwhat you will do, and when...and we will confirm by Thursday.

Never: it appears that a difference in account 4100 · I would like to inform that... · please find attach the file

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real professional would write.

You are answering an auditor about something you discussed in a call. The best opening is:
A I hope this email finds you well and that you are having a wonderful week so far.
B Further to our call this morning, here are the figures you asked for.
C Hello, it is me again about the thing.
You found a possible duplicate, but you are still checking. You write:
A The BPO made a big mistake again and posted the accrual twice, as they always do at the end of the quarter.
B It appears that the accrual was posted twice. We are checking it and will confirm by Thursday.
C Maybe something is wrong, maybe not.
You want the auditor to tell you that the files arrived. You write:
A Could you please confirm receipt of the attachments?
B Could you please confirm the receipt of the attach files, so that I know that everything arrived correctly on your side?
C Did you receive or not?
The deadline moved from Wednesday to Friday. In the email to the BPO you write:
A The deadline moved to Friday, so it is possible that you can maybe change your plan a little if you want to.
B The deadline moved to Friday. Please plan accordingly.
C Friday now.
