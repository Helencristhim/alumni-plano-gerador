Stage 1.1: A Big Day

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes. "Butterflies" here is a feeling, not an insect.

Jersey — the colored shirt a surfer wears in a heat
"I'm in the yellow jersey."
Horn — a loud sound that tells you a heat starts or ends
"Listen for the horn."
Pressure — a heavy feeling when people expect a lot from you
"There's a lot of pressure today."
Butterflies — a nervous feeling in your stomach before something big
"I always get butterflies before a heat."
Score — the number of points you get for a wave
"My best score was a seven and a half."
Reserve — an extra player who is ready if somebody in the team can't play
"The coach picks one reserve."
Selected — chosen to be in a team or a group
"Four surfers will be selected."
Proud — happy because you or somebody you love did something well
"My mum is really proud of me."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read Renato's message to the club group after the heats. Then answer the questions.

What a day! It was freezing at 7 am, wasn't it? I had butterflies in heat two, and there was a lot of pressure with the coach watching. On the sand I met Mia, from Hillarys. She's really nice, and she was in the red jersey. When the horn went, the waves were flat for ten minutes. Then a set came, and my best wave got a 7.5 score. Only the best two count, don't they? Harper was amazing, wasn't she? The coach picks four surfers and one reserve, and we find out on Monday who is selected. We all surfed well, didn't we? I'm proud of all of you.
"It was freezing at 7 am, wasn't it?" Why "wasn't it" and not "isn't it"?
A The sentence uses "was", so the tag copies it: wasn't it.
B Because "isn't" is only for questions about people.
"We all surfed well, didn't we?" Why "didn't"?
A "Surfed" is past simple with no helper verb, so the tag uses did.
B Because the sentence is negative.
Which sentence is correct?
A Only the best two count, don't they?
B Only the best two count, no?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Question tags: copy the verb, flip it

A positive sentence gets a negative tag. A negative sentence gets a positive tag. Use the same verb — or do / does / did — and a pronoun.

The verb decides

It's cold, isn't it?
You can surf, can't you?
They count, don't they?
You surfed well, didn't you?
She isn't here, is she?

Never

You're in heat two, no?
Mia is nice, isn't Mia?
You surfed well, weren't you?

Positive: The waves are small, aren't they?  ·  Negative: You don't surf here, do you?  ·  Short answer: Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
Your voice: down at the end = you're sure, you just want to talk. Up = you're not sure, you're checking.
Why use it? It's the easiest way to start talking to a stranger: say something true, add a tag.

Stage 5: Small talk on the sand

Reflection
It's 7 am on trials day. A surfer from another school is next to you, and you're both nervous. Record 30 seconds: start the conversation, and ask three things with question tags — the weather, the heat, the rules. Check one thing before you send it: copy the verb and flip it (it's → isn't it), and never "no?" at the end.
