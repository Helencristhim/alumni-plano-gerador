Planning for Maybe

Sarah and Fernando plan a housing pilot for the families of patients, and the board has not decided yet. Key words: uncertain, risk, likely, unlikely, depend on, pilot, assume, worst case. Structure: might, may, might not and may not -- The board might approve a small pilot. Some families might not come.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Uncertain -- not sure: you do not know what will happen
"The board is still uncertain about the budget."
Risk -- the chance that something bad will happen
"The biggest risk is the cost of the apartments."
Likely -- probably true, or probably going to happen
"It is likely that the board will say yes."
Unlikely -- probably not true, or probably not going to happen
"A delay of six months is unlikely."
Depend on -- to change because of another thing
"The start date depends on the treatment dates."
Pilot -- a small first test of a new project
"We want to start with a pilot of three apartments."
Assume -- to think something is true, without being sure
"We assume that most families will say yes."
Worst case -- the worst thing that can happen in a situation
"In the worst case, we start next year."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Fernando sent the proposal for the family housing pilot to the board. He is sure about one thing: the families need help. But many things are uncertain. The board might approve all ten apartments, or it may approve only three. Rent in Sao Paulo might go up next year.

Ana talked to the families. Most of them want to come, but some might not leave their city for a month. One mother may not be able to stay, because she has a job in Belem. Fernando's plan B is simple: those families might do a video follow-up from home.

1. "The board might approve all ten apartments." Is Fernando sure?
A Yes, he is sure, because the board always approves his proposals and the budget is already in the bank.
B No. He is sure that the answer is no.
C No. It is possible, but he is not sure.
2. "Some families might not leave their city." What does this mean?
A Maybe some families will not leave their city.
B Some families are not allowed to leave their city, because the hospital says it is a rule for the pilot.
C All the families left their city.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "Rent might to go up next year, so the pilot may cost more than the board assumes in the budget."
B "Rent might go up next year."
C "Rent might goes up next year."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Might and May

GRAMMAR

Will is a promise. Might is a maybe. A good leader says which one is which.

FormRuleExample
Possiblemight / may + verb (the same for all people)The board might approve a pilot.
Possibly notmight not / may not + verbSome families might not come.
Sure vs maybewill = you are sure · might = maybeWe will send it. They might say yes.
QuestionWe usually ask: Do you think + subject + will...?Do you think the board will say yes?
The trapAfter might and may, use the base form: no to, no -sit might cost (not it might costs)

Might and may mean almost the same: something is possible, but not sure. In conversation, might is more common. For a negative, put not after might or may: might not, may not.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Plans that are not sure yet. Choose the best thing to say.

Your director asks: Will the board approve the pilot? You are not sure. You say:
A "Yes, the board will approve it in March, because they always like our ideas and the budget is very small."
B "They might approves it in March."
C "They might approve it in March. It depends on the budget."
Sarah asks if all the families will come. You think some won't. You say:
A "Some families might not want to come."
B "Some families don't might want to come, because they have jobs and children in their cities."
C "Some families might to not come."
You are sure you will send the budget today. You say:
A "I might send you the budget today."
B "I will send you the budget today."
C "I may send the budget today, or maybe tomorrow, or maybe next week, it depends on many things."
A board member asks about the worst case. You say:
A "In the worst case, the pilot might costs more, and the families might to stay at home in their cities."
B "The worst case is likely."
C "In the worst case, we start small."

Reflection
