Talk
TED -- Hans Rosling, "The best stats you've ever seen" (20 min)

A doctor and statistician narrating numbers out loud, live, for twenty minutes, and making every comparison in the vocabulary you worked on today. If you want to hear what fluent data description sounds like in English, this is the recording people still send each other twenty years later.

Tip: write down five of his comparisons exactly as he says them, then rewrite each one with a number from your own company.

Video
The B1M -- "Why Japan Builds Like Nowhere Else"

A construction documentary channel comparing one country's building industry with everybody else's, which means the whole script is comparatives and figures. It is your own field, in English, at the speed a native narrator actually uses.

Tip: watch it once for pleasure and once with a pen. Every time he compares two numbers, note whether he used "as ... as" or "than".

Podcast
The Indicator from Planet Money -- "C is for C-shaped economy"

Nine minutes of two American journalists arguing about what a set of figures actually shows. Short, fast and full of the exact structures from this lesson, which is why it is a better listening exercise than something twice the length.

Tip: it is short enough to listen to three times. First for the argument, then for the numbers, then to catch the words they use to make a gap sound big or small.
