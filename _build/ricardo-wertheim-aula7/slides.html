Chapter 3: How Much, How Many

Can You Count It?

much · many · a few · a little

Grammar Discovery

Fingers or No Fingers

"There are not many tomatoes left."

"There is not much seafood left."

"We bought a few tomatoes and some cheese."

"She let me taste a little olive oil."

Two of these nouns you can count on your fingers. Two you cannot. Which are which?

MeaningCountable (tomatoes, stalls)Uncountable (fish, oil, wine)
A large quantitymany tomatoes
a lot of tomatoes		much oil
a lot of oil
A small quantitya few tomatoesa little oil
Almost nonenot many tomatoesnot much oil
The safe onea lot of works with BOTH, and some works with both in a positive sentence. When in doubt at the table, use those two.

Note the verb: there are not many tomatoes but there is not much seafood. And much and many live mostly in negatives and questions — in a positive sentence, English prefers a lot of.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I ate much tomatoes at the market."
"I ate a lot of tomatoes at the market."
"There are not many seafood left."
"There is not much seafood left."
"She gave me a few olive oil to taste."
"She gave me a little olive oil to taste."

Count it on your fingers and you get many, a few, are. Cannot count it and you get much, a little, is. In a positive sentence, a lot of saves you either way.

Refuses politely and still says yes to tasting

Chapter 4: The Green Market

Eight in the Morning

Before the tourists, before the heat

Dialogue

At the Green Market

E
We are early, and that is the whole trick. After ten there is not much seafood left, and the good stalls are empty.
R
There are not many people yet either. Could you recommend a good place for grilled fish afterwards?
E
I will take you there myself. But first, come to the back. Those women sell local produce from their own gardens, and their prices are half of the square.
R
I would love to try the olive oil. Is it really from a vineyard near here?
E
From her olive trees, not a vineyard — the vineyard is her son's, up the hill. Ask her for a tasting. She will give you a little on bread and she will not let you leave.
R
And the shellfish over there? I am not a big fan of shellfish, but I would give it a try.
E
Then buy a few, not many. Cook them the way my mother does: a little olive oil, garlic, and absolutely nothing else.
Comprehension

About Elena

1. Why does Elena come to the market so early?
Because after ten there is not much seafood left and the good stalls are empty.
2. Where does the olive oil come from, and where is the vineyard?
The oil comes from the woman's own olive trees. The vineyard belongs to her son, up the hill.
3. How does Elena say you should cook the shellfish?
The way her mother does: a little olive oil, garlic and nothing else.
Listening

Rules of the Green Market

A guide explains how the market works. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Why should you bring small notes?
Most stalls only take cash, and there is not much change early in the morning.
2. What time does the fish arrive, and what is left by ten?
The fish arrives at six. By ten there is not much seafood and almost no shellfish.
3. Where should you buy olive oil, and why?
From the women at the back, because they press it themselves and let you taste a little first.
Listening 2

Fish by the Water, Meat in the Hills

A cook explains what most visitors miss. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does he say people wrongly believe about Croatian food?
That it is only grilled fish.
2. What changes twenty minutes inland?
There are not many tourists, portions are much bigger, and you find slow cooked meat, cheese and homemade wine.
3. What is his advice in one sentence?
Eat fish by the water, and eat everything else in the hills.
