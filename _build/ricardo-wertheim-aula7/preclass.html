Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

How Much, How Many -- Food, Wine and Local Markets

Buying food and ordering a meal in Croatia and Slovenia. Key words: seafood, grilled, portion, local produce, vineyard, olive oil, market stall, tasting. Structure: quantifiers with countable and uncountable nouns -- much, many, a lot of, a few, a little.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Seafood -- fish and shellfish, everything you eat from the sea
"By ten there is not much seafood left at the market."
Grilled -- cooked directly over fire or hot metal bars
"The best fish here is simply grilled, with olive oil and nothing else."
Portion -- the amount of food served to one person
"Inland the portions are much bigger than on the coast."
Local produce -- fruit and vegetables grown by farmers near the town
"Everything on that stall is local produce from the next valley."
Vineyard -- a field where grapes are grown to make wine
"Her family has a small vineyard twenty minutes from here."
Olive oil -- the oil pressed from olives, used on almost everything here
"They press the olive oil themselves and let you taste a little."
Market stall -- one small table or stand where a seller works in a market
"Do not buy at the first market stall -- walk to the end first."
Tasting -- trying a small amount of wine or food before you buy
"They offer a free tasting of three wines from the vineyard."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

We arrived at the green market before eight, when there were not many people. The fish comes in at six, so after ten there is not much seafood left and almost no shellfish at all.

We walked to the end of the row first, because there are a lot of stalls and the prices at the entrance are the worst. At the back an old woman gave me a little olive oil on bread, and it was the best thing I ate all week. We bought a few tomatoes, some fresh cheese and one bottle of her oil. Then we sat down near the harbor and ordered one large portion of grilled fish for two, because inland the portions are much bigger and we were not very hungry.

1. Why does the text say "not many people" but "not much seafood"?
A Because you can count people one by one, but you cannot count seafood that way.
B Because people are more important than fish.
C Because "seafood" is a plural word in English.
2. The text says "a few tomatoes" and "a little olive oil". What decides which one you use?
A "A few" is for cheap things and "a little" is for expensive things.
B "A few" goes with things you can count, "a little" with things you cannot.
C "A little" is always a smaller amount than "a few".
3. Why does the text say "there are a lot of stalls" but "there is not much seafood"?
A Because one sentence is in the past and the other is in the present.
B Because countable nouns take a plural verb and uncountable nouns take a singular one.
C Because "stalls" is a longer word than "seafood".

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Much, Many, A Few, A Little

GRAMMAR

One question decides everything: can you count it on your fingers?

MeaningCountable (tomatoes, stalls, people)Uncountable (seafood, oil, wine, cheese)
A large quantitymany tomatoes · a lot of tomatoesmuch oil · a lot of oil
A small quantitya few tomatoesa little oil
Almost nonenot many tomatoesnot much oil
The verb changes tooThere are a few stalls.There is a little oil.
The safe choicea lot of and some work with BOTH. In a positive sentence English prefers them: say "I ate a lot of tomatoes", not "I ate much tomatoes". Much and many live mostly in negatives and questions.

Careful with words that look plural but are not: seafood, bread, cheese, fruit and advice are all uncountable in English.
