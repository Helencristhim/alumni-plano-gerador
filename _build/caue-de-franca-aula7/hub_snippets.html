Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To turn down -- to say no to an offer or an invitation
"She turned down the invitation, but very politely."
To take a rain check -- to say no to an invitation now, but suggest doing it another time
"Can I take a rain check on dinner? I’m exhausted."
I’m afraid -- a polite way to start bad news or a no
"I’m afraid I can’t go on Friday."
To be swamped -- to have too much work to do
"I’m swamped this week, sorry."
To make it -- to manage to go to an event, or to arrive on time
"I don’t think I can make it to the party."
Short notice -- very little warning before something happens
"Sorry for the short notice, but can you come tomorrow?"
To be up for something -- to want to do something, to feel like doing it
"Are you up for a movie tonight?"
To talk someone into -- to persuade someone to do something
"She talked me into going to the karaoke."
To let someone down -- to disappoint someone because you do not do what they hoped
"I promised to help, and I don’t want to let him down."
No hard feelings -- I am not angry; there is no problem between us
"You can’t come? No hard feelings."
To make up for -- to do something good to fix something bad you did
"I missed your birthday, so let me make up for it with dinner."
To bail on someone -- informal: to cancel plans with someone at the last minute
"He bailed on us again. Third time this month!"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Hannah invites Cauê to a hike at five thirty in the morning. “I’m afraid I can’t make it,” he says. “I’d rather sleep on Saturdays.” Then she invites him to karaoke, but he is swamped, so he asks to take a rain check.

“No hard feelings,” Hannah says, “but you’d better come next time. Don’t let me down!” Cauê does not want to bail on his new friends, so he offers something else.

“I’d prefer to invite you all for dinner on Sunday, to make up for it.” Hannah tries to talk him into karaoke after dinner, but he politely turns it down.

1. Why does the text say “I’d rather sleep” and not “I’d rather to sleep”?
A Because would rather is followed by the verb with no to.
B Because sleep is a short verb, and short verbs never take to after them in English.
C Because the sentence is in the past, and to is not used in the past tense.

B A dinner on Sunday.
C A karaoke night at his place on Friday, after he finishes work.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “You’d better to come next time.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Would Rather, Had Better, Would Prefer To

GRAMMAR

Use would rather for preferences and polite refusals, had better for advice with a consequence, and would prefer to for a more formal preference. The short form ’d can be would or had.

JobFormExample
A preferencewould rather (’d rather) + verb, no toI’d rather stay in.
A polite nowould rather not + verbI’d rather not go, if that’s okay.
Two optionswould rather + verb + than + verbI’d rather watch a movie than go out.
Advice or a warninghad better (’d better) + verb: or something bad happensYou’d better leave now.
A formal preferencewould prefer (’d prefer) + to + verbI’d prefer to meet on Sunday.
The trapno to after rather and better; the negative is better notNot I’d rather to stay or you’d better don’t. Say I’d rather stay, you’d better not.

Never: I’d rather to stay · You’d better to go · You’d better don’t · I’d rather stay than going

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A friend invites you to a party you do not want to go to. You say:
A "No, I don’t want to go to your party on Saturday night."
B "I’m afraid I can’t make it. Maybe next time?"
C "I’d rather to stay home than to go to the party."
You want to give a friend advice with a consequence. You say:
A "You’d better leave now, or you’ll miss the train."
B "You’d better to leave now, because the train leaves soon."
C "You’d rather leaving now, or the train will go without you."
You cannot go tonight, but you want to go another time. You say:
A "Can I make it up tonight and go another time with you?"
B "Can I bail on you tonight and come another time?"
C "Can I take a rain check?"
You prefer a movie to a club. You say:
A "I’d rather watching a movie than going to a club tonight."
B "I’d rather watch a movie than go to a club."
C "I’d prefer watch a movie than to go to a club with you."
