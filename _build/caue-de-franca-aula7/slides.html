Saying No Without Sounding Rude

In Portuguese, you have a hundred ways to say no without saying no. In English, a direct no can sound cold, and a vague answer can sound like yes. Today you learn the expressions that say no kindly, and the grammar of preferences.

Chapter 3: The Code

I’d Rather, You’d Better

Two small words with an apostrophe

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Have in Common?

"I’d rather stay in tonight, if you don’t mind."

"You’d better leave now, or you’ll miss the last train."

"I’d prefer to meet on Sunday."

"I’d rather watch a movie than go to a club."

Look at the red words: three of them have ’d. Is it always would? In sentence 2, it is something else. Now look at the blue verbs: which ones have to before them, and which ones do not?

JobFormExample
A preferencewould rather (’d rather) + verb, no toI’d rather stay in.
A polite nowould rather not + verbI’d rather not go, if that’s okay.
Two optionswould rather + verb + than + verbI’d rather watch a movie than go out.
Advice or a warninghad better (’d better) + verb: or something bad happensYou’d better leave now.
A formal preferencewould prefer (’d prefer) + to + verbI’d prefer to meet on Sunday.
The trapno to after rather and better; the negative is better notNot I’d rather to stay or you’d better don’t. Say I’d rather stay, you’d better not.

In one line: ’d rather and ’d better take the verb with no to; ’d prefer takes to.

Dialogue

Hannah’s Invitations

H
Hey, Cauê! A few of us are going hiking on Saturday. We’re leaving at five thirty. Are you up for it?
C
Five thirty in the morning? I’m afraid I can’t make it. To be honest, I’d rather sleep on Saturdays.
H
Ha, fair enough. What about Friday night, then? We’re doing karaoke.
C
I’d love to, but I’m swamped this week. Can I take a rain check?
H
Sure, no hard feelings. But you’d better come next time. Don’t let me down!
C
I won’t. And let me make up for it. I’d prefer to invite you all for dinner. How about Sunday?
H
Sunday works for me. But don’t try to talk us into anything too spicy.
C
Deal. Nothing spicy. I’d rather keep my new friends.

Listening 1 · An Etiquette Coach

The British No

A British etiquette coach explains how people say no politely in the United Kingdom. Sound first — no text.

1. What do British people almost never say, according to her?
A simple, direct no.
2. Which three words does she say can save any refusal?
I’m afraid.
3. What is her advice after you say no?
Offer something else, like another day.
Listening 2 · A Manager

No at Work

An American manager talks about saying no to extra work. Sound first — no text.

1. What happened when she said yes to everything?
She was swamped, worked every weekend and did everything worse.
2. What does she say now when someone asks for a favor at short notice?
That she would prefer to help next week, or that something else will have to wait.
3. What did her boss tell her?
That he would rather have three things done well than ten things done badly.
Listening 3 · A Man Who Always Said Yes

The Day I Bailed

An Australian man tells how saying yes to everything made him lose a friend, for a while. Sound first — no text.

1. Why did he bail on his friend’s birthday?
He had said yes to three other plans that same night.
2. How did his friend feel?
Really let down; he did not answer his messages for a week.
3. How did he make up for it?
He cooked dinner for his friend and promised to say no earlier next time.
