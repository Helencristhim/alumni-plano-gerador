Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

The Notice Nobody Signs -- The Form That Removes the Person

A public notice with seven verbs and no people in it, and the two very different reasons that happens. Key words: to issue, a batch, to trace something back to, to flag something, wording, to sign off on something, a recall, oversight, to be held accountable, deliberate. Structure: the passive voice.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To issue -- to officially send out or announce something
"The notice was issued on a Friday afternoon, which was not an accident."
A batch -- a group of things produced or handled at the same time
"Only one batch was affected, and it was traced in nine hours."
To trace something back to -- to follow the evidence until you find where it started
"The whole thing was traced back to a single supplier in March."
To flag something -- to mark something so that other people notice it
"It was flagged in February and nothing happened until June."
Wording -- the exact words chosen to say something
"Three lawyers spent two days on the wording of one sentence."
To sign off on something -- to give official approval to something
"Nobody wanted to sign off on it, so it sat there for three weeks."
A recall -- an official request to return a product that has a problem
"The recall covered eleven thousand units in four countries."
Oversight -- the job of checking that something is done properly
"There was oversight on paper, and nobody had read the file in a year."
To be held accountable -- to be the person who has to answer for a result
"Somebody has to be held accountable, and the notice names nobody."
Deliberate -- done on purpose, not by accident
"The date was deliberate. Friday afternoon always is."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Two sentences about the same afternoon. We submitted the report on Tuesday. The report was submitted on Tuesday. The second one has lost a person and gained two words. The thing that was acted on has moved to the front, and the verb has become be plus a past participle. That is the whole shape of the passive, and there is nothing else to it.

The detail worth memorising is where the tense lives. It never sits in the participle — it sits in be. The figures are checked every month. The notice has been issued. The file will be reviewed on Friday. Three tenses, one participle each, and only the first word changing. This is why the notice has issued is wrong: the been is not decoration, it is the verb.

The agent — the person who acted — comes back only with by, and only when it matters: it was flagged by the tax team. Most of the time it is left out, and for four honest reasons: nobody knows who did it, it does not matter who did it, it is obvious who did it, or the sentence is about the thing rather than the person. A safety notice is about the product, so no name appears in it. The internal record about the same incident names everybody, because that document has the opposite job.

There is a fifth reason, and everybody recognises it: mistakes were made. Same grammar, different purpose. And there is one hard limit worth knowing before you write anything: a verb with no object cannot go into the passive at all. Something happened, it arrived, it went wrong. Never it was happened. If there is nobody the action was done to, there is no passive to build.

1. Where does the tense live in a passive sentence?
A In the past participle.
B In be — is, was, has been, will be.
C In the word that follows by.
2. Why is "it was happened last week" impossible?
A Because "happen" is an irregular verb.
B Because the verb has no object, so there is nothing to move to the front and no passive to build.
C Because the sentence is missing by.
3. According to the text, why does a safety notice name nobody while the internal record names everybody?
A The two documents have opposite jobs: one is about the product, the other is about who approved what.
B Because public documents are not allowed to contain names.
C Because the internal record is written in a different tense.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive Voice

GRAMMAR

The thing moves to the front, the person leaves, and the tense stays in be.

JobFormExample
the shapebe + past participleThe report was submitted.
routinepresent simple passiveThe figures are checked monthly.
done, result standspresent perfect passiveThe notice has been issued.
a finished momentpast simple passiveIt was flagged in February.
still aheadwill be + participleThe file will be reviewed on Friday.
the agentby + person, only if it mattersIt was flagged by the tax team.
the hard limitNo object, no passive. Not it was happened. Say it happened.

Never: The report was submit · The notice has issued · It was happened last week · The figures was checked

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the sentence a careful writer would send.

The review is finished and the result still stands. You write:
A The review has completed.
B The review has been completed.
C The review was complete.
Something went wrong and nobody caused it directly. You write:
A It was happened during the migration.
B It happened during the migration.
C It was happening by the migration.
You want to make clear which team found the problem. You write:
A It was flagged by the tax team in February.
B It was flagged from the tax team in February.
C It was flag by the tax team in February.
A summary you received says everything "was decided" and names nobody. The most useful reply is:
A This report is dishonest.
B Just so I have it right — who signed off on it, and when?
C Thank you, that is very clear.
