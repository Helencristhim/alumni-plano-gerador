Chapter 4: Where the Person Went

Same Event, No Author

Two words do all of it

Grammar Discovery

What Did the Second Sentence Lose?

"We submitted the report on Tuesday."  /  "The report was submitted on Tuesday."

"Somebody checks the figures every month."  /  "The figures are checked every month."

"They have issued the notice."  /  "The notice has been issued."

"The team traced it to one supplier."  /  "It was traced to one supplier by the team."

In every pair, the blue sentence starts with the person who acted. The red one starts with the thing, and two words carry the change.

JobFormExample
the shapebe + past participleThe report was submitted.
the tense lives in beis / was / has been / will beIt has been issued. It will be reviewed.
routine, every monthpresent simple passiveThe figures are checked monthly.
done, result standspresent perfect passiveThe filing has been completed.
the agentby + person, only if it mattersIt was flagged by the tax team.
why leave the agent outunknown · irrelevant · obvious · hidden on purposeMistakes were made. (by whom?)
the trapA verb with no object has no passive. Not it was happened. Say it happened.

In one line: the thing takes the front of the sentence, the person leaves, and the tense stays in be.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Two Recordings, One Notice

The same form, defended and attacked

Listening 1 · A Compliance Officer

Why the Notice Names Nobody

A compliance officer on a form of writing people love to criticise. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say people accuse this kind of writing of?
Of being cowardly, and of existing so that nobody has to take responsibility.
2. What is his argument for leaving the name out of a notice?
The notice is about the product, not the person. Naming an individual makes the reader look for somebody to blame instead of checking whether they own one of the affected units.
3. What does he say the internal report looks like?
The opposite: names, dates, and who signed off on what. He says the two documents have different jobs and should not be confused.
Listening 2 · Science Radio

What “Mistakes Were Made” Does to a Reader

A short piece on what happens to blame when the sentence has no author. Sound first — no text.

1. What were the two versions of the report the participants read?
One said a named manager approved the batch. The other said the batch was approved. Everything else was identical.
2. What difference did the researchers measure?
The group who read the version with no name estimated a lower financial cost and were less likely to say anybody should lose their job.
3. What does the piece say the finding does not prove?
That the writers intended it. It says the effect is the same whether the choice was deliberate or just habit, which is exactly what makes it worth knowing.
Real Document

The Notice, Verb by Verb

CUSTOMER SAFETY NOTICE
ref. 4471 · issued 14 March

A fault has been identified in one batch of units sold between January and March.

The batch was traced to a single production line and has been removed from sale. Approximately eleven thousand units are affected.

Customers are asked to check the reference number on the base of the unit. Affected units will be replaced at no cost.

The fault was flagged internally in February and the review was completed on 12 March.

Seven passive verbs, and not one by. Internal record IR-4471, which names the reviewer and the date of each approval, was signed off by J. Piffer on 12 March.
1. Which two verbs in the notice are in the present perfect passive?
"Has been identified" and "has been removed" — both use has been plus the participle.
2. How many people are named in the body of the notice, and where does a name appear?
Nobody is named in the body. One name appears only in the footer, on the internal record.
3. What does the footer tell you about the difference between the two documents?
The public notice has no agents at all, while the internal record names the reviewer and the date of each approval. They have different jobs.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I build the passive with be plus the full participle, and I never drop the -ed or the -en.
I know the tense lives in be: is done, was done, has been done, will be done.
I add by only when the person matters, and I leave it out when they do not.
I can hear when a passive is hiding somebody, and ask who did it without accusing anyone.
I know the words: to issue, a batch, to trace something back to, to flag something, wording, to sign off on something, a recall, oversight, to be held accountable, deliberate.
