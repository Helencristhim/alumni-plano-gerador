Talk
Dan Ariely — “Our buggy moral code” (TED)

Sixteen minutes on how far people will bend a rule when the distance between the act and the consequence grows. It is the mechanism behind the study in this lesson’s second recording, told with experiments rather than opinions.

Tip: he describes each experiment in the passive — participants were asked, the money was placed. Write down four of those sentences and say who the agent was in each one.

Radio
99% Invisible — “The Titanic’s Best Lifeboat”

A story about rules that were written after the damage, by people who were never named in them. The regulation vocabulary of this lesson — issued, flagged, signed off, held accountable — runs through the whole episode.

Tip: listen once for the story. Then write four sentences summarising it, all in the passive, and four more in the active. Notice which version you would rather send to a manager.

Podcast
Criminal — “Riverwalk” (episode 368)

Thirty minutes in which an official account and a personal account of the same events sit side by side. The official one is almost entirely passive; the personal one is not. Hearing them back to back is the whole lesson in one episode.

Tip: every time you hear an official document quoted, ask yourself who is missing from the sentence, and whether their absence is honest.
