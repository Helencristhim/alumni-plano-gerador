Lesson 03 — Before class

Two Suppliers, One Slot

Read the two quotes, meet the language, and bring a real decision of your own. In class you will recommend one supplier to someone who disagrees with you — and nobody will give you the words first.
0%
▼

Priming 1: The two quotes

Input

Read it once. Do not decide anything yet — deciding is the task, and the task is in class.

One production slot, two ways to fill it

You have one free production slot in October and two quotes on your desk. Both suppliers can make the bracket. Only one of them can have the slot, and purchasing wants your recommendation by Friday.

Delta Metal has supplied you for six years. Their unit price is 4.20 dollars, with a minimum order of 5,000 pieces and a lead time of four weeks. They hold the ISO certification your American customer asks for, and in six years they have missed two dates, both by less than a week. Their quote ends with a line you have read many times: any delay above five days carries a penalty clause of 2% of the order value.

Krammer is new. Their unit price is 3.65 dollars, thirteen percent below Delta, with a minimum order of 12,000 pieces and a lead time of six weeks. Their capacity is larger and they say they can absorb a second order later in the year. Their quote says nothing about certification, and there is no penalty clause in it — in either direction.

You called two companies who use Krammer. The first said they are good and cheap and answer the phone. The second said the first six months were difficult, and then it settled. That is the track record you have.

The saving, if you go with Krammer, is about seven thousand dollars on this order. Your customer's audit is in March, and you do not yet know whether they will ask for the certificate of every supplier in the chain, or only of yours.

Priming 2: The words of a quote

Vocabulary

Ten words that live inside any supplier quote. Tap Listen once for each. You do not have to produce them yet.

Quote — a written offer with prices and conditions
"Two quotes on your desk, one slot."
Unit price — the price of one single piece
"Their unit price is 3.65 dollars."
Minimum order — the smallest quantity a supplier will accept
"A minimum order of twelve thousand pieces."
Capacity — how much a factory can produce in a period
"Their capacity is larger than ours."
Certification — an official document proving a standard is met
"They hold the ISO certification our customer asks for."
Penalty clause — the part of a contract that says what a late supplier pays
"A penalty clause of two percent of the order value."
Trade-off — what you give up to get something else
"The main trade-off is price against certainty."
Downside — the bad part of an option that is otherwise good
"The downside is six weeks instead of four."
Track record — what someone has actually done in the past
"Six years is a track record. Two phone calls is not."
To commit — to promise something in a way you cannot easily undo
"I don't want to commit before Monday."

Priming 3: Notice, do not answer

Noticing

There is nothing to submit here, and no right answer. Read the twelve lines below and go back to the text with them in your head. In class you will need them — nobody will hand them to you first.

• A is cheaper, but…

• The main trade-off is…

• What worries me is…

• If we go with Krammer, we…

• I'd go with Delta, and here is why.

• It should save around seven thousand.

• On paper it looks better than it is.

• The risk I can't cover is…

• That would only matter if…

• I'd rather pay more and sleep.

• We've never had a problem, which is not the same as never having a risk.

• I can live with the price if we fix the lead time.

One thing to notice while you read: three of them say a number, and none of them say it as a certainty. Ask yourself why.

Priming 4: Say them once

Speaking

Once each, no more. This is not drilling — it is letting your mouth meet the shape before you have to use it under pressure.

The main trade-off is price against certainty.
What worries me is the audit in March.
It should save around seven thousand dollars.
The risk I can't cover is the certificate.

Priming 5: Bring a decision of your own

Reflection

The task in class is about choosing under pressure. Come with a real one, or the second half of the lesson will be about a company that does not exist.

Think of a supplier, a machine or a process you had to choose between two options. Record 60 seconds: what the two options were, which one you picked, and what you gave up. Do not organize it — the shape is what we work on in class.
Now the other half. Record 40 seconds: who disagreed with that decision — or who would have, if they had known — and what they would say to you.
