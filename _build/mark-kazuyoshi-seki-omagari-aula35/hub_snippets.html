The Trip Was Organized By... -- Advanced Passive

"They booked the flights, somebody checked our bags" is correct, but the spotlight is on people nobody cares about. This lesson gives you the passive forms that put the spotlight on the thing and say exactly when it happened -- being, been, must be, to be, being told and get -- and you use them to tell the story of a trip. Key words: tour operator, travel insurance, boarding pass, luggage allowance, excess baggage, upgrade, security check, mix-up, sort out, reschedule, lost and found, hand over.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. These are the words people use for the part of a trip that happens behind the scenes.

Tour operator -- a company that organizes trips and sells them as a package
"Our school trip was organized by a tour operator in Tokyo."
Travel insurance -- a plan that pays you back if something goes wrong on a trip
"Get travel insurance before you fly, just in case."
Boarding pass -- the card or code you show to get on a plane
"Our boarding passes had been printed with the wrong names."
Luggage allowance -- the amount of luggage you can take without paying extra
"Check your luggage allowance before you pack."
Excess baggage -- luggage that is over the limit, so you have to pay more
"We paid for excess baggage on the way home."
Upgrade -- to move somebody to a better seat, room or service
"We were upgraded to a bigger room for free."
Security check -- the place at the airport where bags and people are checked before the gate
"My bag got stopped at the security check because of a bottle of water."
Mix-up -- a mistake where two things are confused with each other
"There was a mix-up with the names on our tickets."
Sort out -- to deal with a problem until it is fixed
"The problem was sorted out in twenty minutes."
Reschedule -- to change the time or date of something that was planned
"Our transfer has been rescheduled for the afternoon."
Lost and found -- the office where things that people have lost are kept
"My jacket was handed in at the lost and found."
Hand over -- to give something to somebody who asks for it
"Hand over your passport at the counter."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read Nina's trip report and answer the questions. Notice how each passive form in bold says WHEN the thing happened, without saying who did it.

Our school trip to Japan was organized by a tour operator, so almost nothing had to be done by our parents. The flights had been booked months before, and our hotel had already been paid for.

The problems started at the airport. Our boarding passes had been printed with the wrong names, and while the mix-up was being sorted out, the whole group had to wait at the desk. It took twenty minutes.

Then my bag got stopped at the security check because of a bottle of water. Liquids must be packed in a clear plastic bag, and I had forgotten. I hate being told off in front of everybody, but I deserved it.

In the end, everything was fixed, and we even got upgraded to a bigger room in Kyoto. Next time, I want to be told about the rules before I pack.

1. "The flights had been booked months before." When were the flights booked?
A Before the trip started, a long time earlier.
B At the airport, during the mix-up.
C After the trip ended.
2. "While the mix-up was being sorted out, the whole group had to wait." What does "was being sorted out" tell you?
A The mix-up was never fixed.
B Somebody was fixing it at that moment.
C Nina fixed it herself.
3. "Liquids must be packed in a clear plastic bag." What kind of sentence is this?
A A rule.
B A question.
C A story about the past.
4. "I want to be told about the rules before I pack." What does Nina want?
A To tell other people the rules.
B Somebody to tell her the rules.
C To forget the rules.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Putting the Spotlight on the Thing

GRAMMAR

When WHO did it is not important, English uses the passive. The form of be tells you when it happened: in progress, finished, a rule, or something you want.

FormWhat it saysExample
was / were + being + past participlein progress"Our bags were being checked."
has / have / had + been + past participlefinished"The hotel had already been booked."
modal + be + past participlea rule or a possibility"Passports must be shown."
to be + past participleafter want, expect, need, hope"We expected to be picked up."
being + past participleafter hate, avoid, enjoy, prepositions"I hate being told to wait."
get + past participleinformal, unplanned or unpleasant"My bag got stopped at security."
Three trapsBeing (in progress) is not been (finished). No "to" after a modal: "must be", never "must to be". Verbs like happen and arrive have no passive: "it happened", never "it was happened".

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen to each sentence, then record yourself saying it. In the passive, the stress falls on the past participle, not on be: have been BOOKED, were being CHECKED, must be SHOWN. That stress is what tells the listener what happened.

The flights have already been booked.
Our bags were being checked when the alarm went off.
Passports must be shown at the gate.
We expected to be picked up at the airport.
My bag got stopped at the security check.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the prompt below. There is no right or wrong answer — bring the recording to class.

Think of a real trip you took with your family or your school. Tell the story in about one minute, but tell it through what was done: what was booked, what was forgotten, what went wrong and how it was sorted out. Use at least five different passive forms -- was being, had been, must be, to be, being told and get -- and say who did something only when it really matters.
