Four Ways to Tell the Same Moment

The Person or the Thing?

"They checked my bag at security."

Correct, and the spotlight is on "they", who nobody cares about.

"My bag was being checked when my flight was called."

The spotlight is on the bag, and the checking was still in progress.

"My bag had already been checked, so I went to the gate."

Finished before something else happened.

"My bag got stopped at security. Again."

Informal, unplanned and a little annoying.

When the person who did it is not important, English moves the spotlight to the thing. The verb be then changes form to say when it happened.

Chapter 3: Who Did It?

Who Did It? It Does Not Matter

The forms English uses when the thing is more important than the person

Grammar Discovery

Four Sentences, Four Forms of Be

Our bags were being checked when the alarm went off.

The hotel had already been booked by the tour operator.

Passports must be shown at the gate.

We expected to be picked up at the airport.

In every sentence something was done, but we do not say who did it. Find the verb be in each one: which form is it in, and what does that form tell you?

FormWhat it saysExample
was / were / is / are + being + past participlean action in progress"Our bags were being checked."
has / have / had + been + past participlean action already finished"The hotel had already been booked."
modal + be + past participlea rule, a possibility or an obligation"Passports must be shown at the gate."
to be + past participleafter want, expect, need, hope"We expected to be picked up."
being + past participleafter hate, avoid, enjoy and after a preposition"I hate being told to wait."
get + past participleinformal, often for something unplanned or unpleasant"My bag got stopped at security."
... by + personOnly when WHO did it really matters: "The trip was organized by my dad." Most of the time, leave the person out.

The Tool

Three Places It Goes Wrong

Trap 1 · being and been are not the same word

"My bag was being checked" is still happening. "My bag has been checked" is finished. They sound alike, so say them slowly: BEE-ing, BIN.

Trap 2 · no "to" after a modal

A modal is followed by be, never by "to be" or "been": "Passports must be shown." Never "must to be shown" or "must been shown".

Trap 3 · some verbs have no passive at all

Verbs with no object, like happen, arrive and die, cannot be passive: "The mix-up happened at the gate." Never "was happened". And do not add "by somebody" when you do not know who: "The flight was cancelled." is enough.

Choose the right form of be and the listener knows exactly when it happened. Choose the wrong one and the story loses its order.

Comprehension

About Nina

1. Who organized Nina's school trip?
A tour operator. It was all done by a tour operator.
2. What was the mix-up at the airport, and how long did it take to fix?
Their boarding passes had been printed with the wrong names. It was sorted out in twenty minutes.
3. Why was her bag stopped, and what does she tell Mark to get?
Because of a bottle of water. She tells him to get travel insurance, because bags can be lost.
Listening

When the Thing Matters More

Your teacher on the forms of the passive and the three traps. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Why does she say the passive is useful when you tell the story of a trip?
Because most of the time nobody cares who did things; the thing matters more than the person.
2. What is the difference between "being" and "been" in the passive?
"Being" is for an action in progress; "been" is for an action that is already finished.
3. What comes after a modal like "must", and what never comes after it?
"Be": "must be shown". Never "to".
4. Why is "was happened" wrong?
Because some verbs, like happen and arrive, have no passive at all.

"Don't worry, the hotel has already been booked." (the hotel did not book anything: it has been booked)

"I hate being told to wait at the airport." (after hate, the passive gerund: being told)
