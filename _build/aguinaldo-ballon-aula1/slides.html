Chapter 1: First Words

Five Years as a CEO, One Sentence

You have been a lawyer and a CEO, and now you are back at the firm. In Portuguese that story takes ten seconds. Today we build the English version, one that you can say without notes.

In one sentence: what do you do now, and what did you do before?

Today’s Goal

Three Missions

1

Ten words for a career in law, business and infrastructure.

2

Choose between "I have done" and "I did" without stopping to think.

3

Introduce yourself to a panel in under one minute.

The structure of the day is the one every introduction needs: I led two companies for five years is a finished chapter, and I have advised infrastructure clients since then is a chapter that is still open. English marks the difference, and listeners notice when it is missing.

One Question

What Is Finished, and What Is Still Open?

Two sentences, out loud, right now.

One about something in your career that is finished. One about something that started in the past and is still true today. Do not worry about the grammar yet: just say them.

Chapter 3: The Code

Finished or Still Open?

Two tenses for one professional story

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Show?

"I led two companies for five years."

"I have advised infrastructure clients since 2015."

"I stepped down three months ago."

"I have worked with international funds many times."

Look at the time words in red. Some of them close a period: three months ago. Others leave it open until now: since 2015. The verb in blue changes with them.

JobFormExample
A finished period, with a clear endpast simpleI led two companies for five years.
A moment in the pastpast simple + ago / in 2020 / last yearI stepped down three months ago.
Started in the past and still true nowhave / has + past participle + since / forI have advised clients since 2015.
Experience, with no datehave / has + past participleI have worked with international funds.
The question about experienceHave you ever + past participle?Have you ever spoken on a panel?
The trapa finished time word never goes with haveNot I have stepped down last year. Say I stepped down last year.

In one line: a closed period takes the past simple; a period that is still open takes have + past participle.

Listening 1 · The Moderator

How I Introduce a Speaker

A conference moderator explains what she needs from each speaker. Sound first — no text.

1. How much time does she give each speaker for the introduction?
About one minute.
2. What is the most common mistake speakers make?
They read their whole CV, with every job and every date.
3. What does she ask speakers to tell her instead?
Three things: what they do now, one thing they did before, and why they are on this panel.
Listening 2 · The Lawyer

From the Firm to the Boardroom

A lawyer describes how his career changed direction. Sound first — no text.

1. What did he do for twelve years?
He worked at a law firm in contracts.
2. Why did he leave the company in 2023?
Because he wanted more time with his own clients and wanted to go back to legal work.
3. What does he say is the advantage of his mixed career?
He understands what the board is worried about, not only the legal risk.
Listening 3 · Business Radio

Why Investors Look at Regulation First

A short radio report on infrastructure investment in Latin America. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the first question investors ask, according to the report?
Who is the regulator, and how stable are the rules?
2. How long do many concessions last?
Twenty or thirty years.
3. Who do investors want on their team, according to the report?
People who understand both the law and the business.
