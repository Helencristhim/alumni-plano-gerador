The Panel You Did Not Take -- Introducing Your Career

A moderator gives you one minute to say who you are. Key words: a panel, a moderator, a background, to advise, a regulator, a concession, a stakeholder, a board, to step down, a track record. Structure: present perfect vs past simple.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A panel -- a group of speakers who discuss a topic in front of an audience
"I was invited to a panel on infrastructure in New York."
A moderator -- the person who controls a panel and asks the speakers questions
"The moderator gave each speaker two minutes to introduce themselves."
A background -- your education and work experience until today
"My background is in law, but I have also worked as a CEO."
To advise -- to tell a client what they should do, as a professional
"I advise companies on infrastructure and regulation."
A regulator -- a public agency that makes and controls the rules of a sector
"The regulator decides the rules for the whole electricity sector."
A concession -- a contract that lets a private company run a public service for many years
"The highway concession lasts thirty years."
A stakeholder -- any person or group affected by a decision
"Every stakeholder in the project has a different priority."
A board -- the group of people who make the main decisions for a company
"I reported to the board every month when I was CEO."
To step down -- to leave an important job by your own decision
"I stepped down as CEO three months ago."
A track record -- the results a person has achieved over the years
"She has a strong track record in public contracts."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At an infrastructure forum, the moderator asks each speaker for a one-minute introduction. The first speaker was a minister for six years. She left the government in 2021, and since then she has advised three international funds.

The second speaker has worked as a lawyer for more than twenty years. He was also CEO of two companies, but he stepped down three months ago. He has worked with the regulator on several highway concessions.

Notice the pattern: when the period is finished, the speakers use the past simple. When the story continues until today, they use have or has with the past participle.

1. Is the first speaker still a minister?
A Yes, she is still a minister.
B No. She was a minister, and she left the government in 2021.
C The text does not say.
2. Why does the text say "he has worked as a lawyer for more than twenty years"?
A Because he is still a lawyer today.
B Because he stopped working as a lawyer.
C Because the work happened at one moment.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "He has stepped down three months ago."
B "He stepped down three months ago."
C "He is stepped down three months ago."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect vs Past Simple

GRAMMAR

A finished period takes the past simple. A period that continues until now takes have + past participle.

JobFormExample
A finished period, with a clear endpast simpleI led two companies for five years.
A moment in the pastpast simple + ago / in 2020 / last yearI stepped down three months ago.
Started in the past and still true nowhave / has + past participle + since / forI have advised clients since 2015.
Experience, with no datehave / has + past participleI have worked with international funds.
The question about experienceHave you ever + past participle?Have you ever spoken on a panel?
The trapa finished time word never goes with haveNot I have stepped down last year. Say I stepped down last year.

Never: I have stepped down last year · I am a lawyer since twenty years · I worked here during five years · Did you ever speak on a panel? (for experience, say Have you ever spoken...?)

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A moderator asks what you do now. You say:
A "I have been CEO of two companies last year."
B "I advise clients on infrastructure and regulation."
C "I am advising since three months."
You want to describe a job that is finished. You say:
A "I was CEO for five years."
B "I have been CEO for five years, until 2024."
C "I am CEO during five years."
An investor asks about your experience with regulators. The best answer is:
A "Yes, I worked with them since 2015."
B "Yes, I have worked with regulators on several concessions."
C "Yes, I have work with regulators many time."
You want to ask about experience, with no date. You say:
A "Did you ever work in Brazil?"
B "Have you ever worked in Brazil?"
C "Are you ever working in Brazil?"
