Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A shared service center -- one office that does the same finance work for many countries
"I work in the shared service center in Sao Paulo."
A counterpart -- the person who has your job in another country or another team
"My counterpart in Mexico closes on the same day."
To report to -- to have somebody as your manager
"I report to the accounting manager here in Brazil."
The month-end close -- the few days when the accounts of the month are finished
"The month-end close takes us five working days."
A ledger -- the official record where every transaction of the company is written
"Nothing exists for the auditors until it is in the ledger."
To handle -- to be the person who deals with something and decides about it
"I handle the close for Brazil, and my counterpart handles Mexico."
A deadline -- the day and time by which the work has to be finished
"The deadline is nine in the morning, my time."
To walk somebody through -- to explain something step by step, from the beginning
"Let me walk you through the calculation, line by line."
To be based in -- to have your office in a particular city
"I am based in Sao Paulo, and I work from home four days a week."
A time zone -- the clock a country runs on, compared with yours
"Chicago is in another time zone, three hours behind us."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Nobody has ever lost a job because of a preposition. But something smaller happens, and it happens every week. A finance analyst writes to a colleague in another country: “The file will be ready in Monday, at March.” The colleague understands, answers, and moves on — and then, without deciding to, reads the next three sentences a little more slowly. The information was all there. Only the confidence in it moved.

English uses three small words for time, and each one has its own size. At is a point: at nine, at half past four, and, for a reason nobody can explain, at night. On is a square on a calendar — a day or a date: on Monday, on the fifth working day, on 3 March. In is a box you are inside of, and it is the biggest of the three: in March, in 2026, in the morning. Place works exactly the same way. At is one building or one address: at the Morumbi office. In is a city, a country, a room you can walk into: in Sao Paulo, in the shared service center.

Two traps survive the rule. The first is the part of the day. Alone, it takes in: in the morning. With a day in front of it, the day wins and it takes on: on Monday morning. The second is a pair that looks identical and is not. In time means early enough for something to still work: the file arrived in time for the review. On time means exactly at the hour agreed: the call started on time. A close can finish on time and still not be in time, and anybody who has run a month-end knows the difference is not academic.

1. According to the text, what changes when the preposition is wrong?
A The colleague does not understand the message.
B The information still arrives, but the confidence in it drops.
C The message has to be written again from the beginning.
2. Why does the text say that in is the biggest of the three words?
A Because it has more letters than the other two.
B Because it is used more often in business English.
C Because it goes with a box you are inside of: a month, a year, a city.
3. What is the difference between in time and on time, in the text?
A In time means early enough for something to still work; on time means exactly at the hour agreed.
B They mean the same thing, and both are correct.
C On time is for people and in time is for documents.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- At, On and In

GRAMMAR

Three words. One decision, taken faster than you can think.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

Somebody on a global call asks what you do. The strongest answer is:
A Keitma Gomes. I am based in Sao Paulo, and I handle the month-end close for Brazil.
B I am based at Sao Paulo and I am responsible for a lot of things in the accounting.
C My work is the accounting of the Brazil, in the shared service.
You want to give the exact moment your deadline falls. You say:
A The deadline is in the fifth working day, in nine hours.
B The deadline is on the fifth working day, at nine in the morning.
C The deadline is at the fifth working day, on nine of the morning.
The other person spoke too fast and you did not follow. You say:
A Yes, yes, no problem.
B Sorry, could you say that again, a little slower?
C I am sorry, my English is not very good.
You have finished your answer and you want the conversation to keep going. You say:
A That is all from my side. Thank you.
B And you -- what do you handle on your side?
C Okay. I think you understood me.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Sixty seconds, no notes. You are the first person to speak on a call with eleven people who have never met you. Say who you are, where you are based, what you handle, when your deadline is, and one thing you need from them. Finish with one question back.
