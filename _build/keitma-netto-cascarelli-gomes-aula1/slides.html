Chapter 3: Where and When

Three Small Words, One Decision

At, on and in — and the moment you have to choose one

Grammar Discovery

Why Do These Three Words Change?

"The call is on Monday, at nine, in the morning."

"I am based in Sao Paulo, at the Morumbi office."

"We close the ledger in March, on the fifth working day."

"My counterpart calls me on Friday afternoon, never at night."

In one line: at is a point, on is a surface you can put a finger on, in is something you are inside of. A clock time is a point. A day is a square on a calendar. A month, a year and a city are things you are inside.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Same Words, At Full Speed

A call, a voice note, and a meeting invitation

Dialogue

Two Minutes on a Global Call

M
Sorry, before we start — Mark Ellery, global process lead. I do not think we have met. You are the one who handles Brazil, is that right?
K
That is right. Keitma Gomes. I am based in Sao Paulo, in the shared service center, and I handle the close for Brazil.
M
Good. And who do you report to on your side?
K
I report to the accounting manager here, and I work with the controllership team every month.
M
Right. We are three hours behind you, so I will keep this short. When do you close the ledger?
K
We close on the fifth working day, in the morning. The deadline is nine o'clock, and that is six in your time zone.
M
That is tighter than I thought. Could you walk me through the last two days of the close, at some point this week?
K
Of course. Thursday at ten, in your time. And you — how many countries does your team look after?
Comprehension

About Mark

1. How does Mark introduce himself, and what does he say about meeting her before?
He says he is Mark Ellery, the global process lead, and that he does not think they have met.
2. What does he say about the time difference, and what does he decide because of it?
His team is three hours behind her, so he says he will keep the call short.
3. What does he ask her to do at the end, and when?
He asks her to walk him through the last two days of the close, at some point this week.
Listening 1 · A Voice Note

What the Other Side Never Says Out Loud

A global process lead recording a note to his own team, the Friday before the close. Sound first — no text.

1. On which working day does the Brazil team close, and what does he say about anything sent after the second working day?
They close on the fifth working day, and anything sent after the second working day is already late for them.
2. What does he say about the time difference, and what should nobody do at four in the afternoon?
Brazil is three hours ahead, so their nine o'clock deadline is six in the morning for his team; nobody should write to them at four in the afternoon and expect an answer the same day.
3. What does he ask people to say when they join the call, and why?
Their name and what they handle, because half the people on the call have never spoken to each other and they lose ten minutes every time.
Listening 2 · Advice

Ten Seconds, Three Sentences

A global controller explaining what she tells every new person on her team.

1. What happens, according to her, to somebody whose name nobody catches?
For the rest of the hour that person is just “the lady from Brazil”.
2. What do people do when they do not know who to ask about a number?
They ask nobody, and the question comes back a week later in an e-mail.
3. What are the three things she tells every new person on her team to say?
Their name, slowly; the city they are based in; and the one thing they handle.

Chapter 6: Your Turn

The Same Two Minutes, Three Times

Guided, half free, and then on your own

Role-Play 1 of 3 — Guided

Mark Asks Again

Scenario: Mark finds you again at the end of the call and asks the same four things: what you handle, where you are based, who you report to, and when you close. Answer all four, and finish with a question back to him.

Keyword chips:

to handle to be based in to report to the fifth working day
Role-Play 2 of 3 — Semi-free

Ask Before You Tell

Scenario: A new counterpart from Mexico has just joined the team. Ask him two questions about his side before you say a single word about yours — then connect his answer to yours in one sentence.

Keyword chips:

What do you handle? Where are you based? When do you close? Sorry, could you say that again?
Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

Sixty Seconds, No Notes

Scenario: You are the first person to speak on a call with eleven people who have never met you. Say who you are, where you are based, what you handle, when your deadline is, and one thing you need from them. Finish with a question back.

No keywords, no notes, sixty seconds.
