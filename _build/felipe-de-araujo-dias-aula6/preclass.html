Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

Have You Ever...? -- Networking at a Conference

The words of the part nobody puts in the program: keynote, booth, attendee, to network, small talk, business card, icebreaker, to catch up. Structure: the present perfect for experience, with ever and never, and what happens the moment you say a date. Mission: open a conversation with a stranger and keep it alive.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Keynote -- the main talk of an event, given to everybody at the same time
"The keynote was better than last year."
Booth -- the small stand a company builds at a fair to receive visitors
"I have never seen a booth that big."
Attendee -- a person who is taking part in an event
"Three hundred attendees stood in the room with a coffee."
To network -- to meet people at an event in order to build useful contacts
"She comes to network, not to look at the booths."
Small talk -- light conversation about nothing important, used to open a door
"Small talk is not the conversation. It is the way in."
Business card -- the small card with your name, your role and how to reach you
"Here is my business card. Write to me next week."
Icebreaker -- something you say or do to start a conversation with a stranger
"The coffee upstairs is a terrible icebreaker, and it works every time."
To catch up -- to talk with somebody you have not seen for a long time, to hear their news
"We caught up for ten minutes before the keynote."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

I have been to four trade fairs and I have never enjoyed the coffee break. This year somebody sat next to me and asked whether I had a spare seat, and twenty minutes later I had three new contacts. She has come to this fair three times. She has worked with two Brazilian retailers. She has never been to Sao Paulo, and she wants to go.

Notice what none of those sentences has: a date. I have been to four trade fairs is my life, not my calendar. The moment I put a time in, everything changes: I went to my first fair in 2019, and I saw the keynote yesterday at nine. Both of those are finished moments with a place on the clock, so they take the past simple. If you can answer the question when?, you cannot use have.

1. Why does the text say I have been to four trade fairs and not I went to four trade fairs?
A Because four is a plural number and plurals take have.
B Because it is an experience in his life with no date attached to it.
C Because trade fairs happen every year.
2. Why is it I saw the keynote yesterday and not I have seen?
A Because yesterday is a finished time, and a date forces the past simple.
B Because see is an irregular verb and irregular verbs avoid have.
C Because the keynote is over and the fair is not.
3. What is the test the text gives you?
A Count the words before the verb.
B Ask yourself whether you can answer the question when. If you can, use the past simple.
C Check whether the sentence is positive or negative.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect for Experience

GRAMMAR

One tense for your life and one for your calendar. The hard part is not the form. It is noticing the second a date walks into the sentence.

FormUse it forExample
have / has + participleAn experience, with no date. Your life, not your calendar.I have been to Chicago. She has met him.
everQuestions only. It means at any time in your life.Have you ever worked with a French supplier?
neverNot once. Already negative, so never add not.I have never seen a booth that big.
been or gonebeen = went and came back. gone = went and is still there. Brazilians almost always want been.
The participleNot the past. go → gone, see → seen, meet → met, speak → spoken, be → been.
The date testIf you can answer when?, use the past simple: I saw it yesterday. If you cannot, use the present perfect: I have seen it.

Portuguese lets you say eu fui and eu tenho ido almost anywhere. English does not. Before you speak, ask yourself whether there is a date in the sentence, even a hidden one.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are at a conference coffee break. Choose the best thing to say.

You want to know whether the person beside you has visited your country. You ask:
A Have you ever went to Brazil?
B Have you ever been to Brazil?
C Did you ever have been to Brazil?
Somebody asks whether you have used a speed meeting before. You have not. You say:
A No, I have never done one. How does it work?
B No, I did never do one.
C No, I have not never done one.
They ask when you saw the keynote. You say:
A I have seen it this morning at nine.
B I saw it this morning at nine.
C I have seen it yesterday at nine.
You want to know what has changed for somebody since last year. You ask:
A What has changed for you since last year?
B What did change for you since last year?
C What is changing for you since last year?

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Imagine you are at the coffee break of an international event and nobody knows you. Record about ninety seconds. Open with one boring question. Then tell three experiences from your career using have or has and no dates at all. Then tell one thing that happened at a specific moment, with the date, and notice how the verb changes. Use at least four words from this lesson and finish by asking two questions with ever. Do not stop to correct yourself.
