Chapter 4: A Life With No Dates

Have You Ever Done It?

The present perfect, and what happens the moment you say when

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Have you ever been to Chicago?"

"I have never seen a booth that big."

"She has met three suppliers this morning."

"I went to the keynote at nine."

FormUse it forExample
have / has + past participleAn experience in your life, with no date attached to it.I have been to Chicago.
everOnly in questions. It means at any time in your life.Have you ever worked with a French supplier?
neverNot once. The sentence is already negative, so no not.I have never seen a booth that big.
been or gonebeen = went and came back. gone = went and is still there.He has been to the fair. / He has gone to the fair.
The moment you say whenyesterday, last year, at nine, in 2019 force the past simple.I went to the keynote at nine.

The present perfect has no date. Add one and the sentence changes tense, every time, with no exceptions.

Grammar Practice

With or Without a Date

Say the full sentence, then click to compare

1. ______ you ever ______ (be) to a trade fair in Europe?
"Have you ever been to a trade fair in Europe?"
2. I ______ (never / meet) a supplier from Vietnam.
"I have never met a supplier from Vietnam."
3. She ______ (give) me her business card this morning.
"She has given me her business card this morning."
4. I ______ (see) the keynote yesterday, not today.
"I saw the keynote yesterday, not today."
Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"Have you ever went to Chicago?"
"Have you ever been to Chicago?"
"I did never meet him."
"I have never met him."
"I have seen the keynote yesterday."
"I saw the keynote yesterday."

After have or has the verb goes into the participle, never into the past. And the second a date appears, the whole sentence goes back to the past simple.
