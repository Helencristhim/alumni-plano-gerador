Chapter 3: The Code

Your Protocol, On One Page

Nothing new. Everything you already own

The Move

Four Seconds, Two Reasons, One Line

1. The first four seconds

“There are two parts to that. Let me give you the number first.”

2. The shape of every answer

“Headline, then two reasons, then stop.”

3. The closing statement

“Three sentences: what I do, the proof, and what I would do here.”

Never blame the market, the parent company or the previous CFO. Every one of those may be true, and every one of them costs you the room.

Comprehension

About the Feedback Call

1. What is the first piece of feedback?
That his first sentence is now the strongest part of the answer, which was not true in June.
2. What is the smaller point they raised?
On working capital he gave three examples, and two would have been better.
3. What does he say he will do that afternoon?
Send the audited page, because he said he would.
Listening

What They Repeat About You

A search consultant on the sentence that travels. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he ask after a final interview?
What the interviewer made of the candidate.
2. What comes back, usually?
One sentence, almost always one the candidate said in the room.
3. When should the candidate say that sentence?
Once, slowly, in the middle of an answer where it belongs — not three times and not in the first minute.
Listening 2

The Afternoon After

A CFO on what she does in the four hours after an interview. Sound first — no text.

1. What three things does she do after an interview?
Sends what she promised the same afternoon, writes down every question in their words, and writes one line about what she would change.
2. Why does she write down the questions?
Because the next process will ask half of them again.
3. What does she say most candidates do instead?
They replay the worst thirty seconds for two days, which makes the next interview worse.
