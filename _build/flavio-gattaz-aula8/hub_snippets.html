The Decision That Cost Money -- Reading a Short Case

A firm takes one project too many. Nobody is stupid, nothing is hidden, and it costs eleven months. Key words: a trade-off, to overcommit, working capital, a contingency, to write something off, exposure, a margin, to escalate, a red flag, to cut your losses, a sunk cost, a post-mortem. Structure: third conditional and past modals.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A trade-off -- a gain you accept in exchange for a loss somewhere else, chosen on purpose
"Taking the fourth project was a trade-off, and we knew which side we were paying on."
To overcommit -- to promise more than the structure can actually deliver
"We overcommitted in March and spent the rest of the year paying for it."
Working capital -- the money a company needs to keep operating between paying its costs and being paid
"The contract was profitable on paper and it ate all of our working capital."
A contingency -- money or time set aside for what you cannot predict but know will happen
"There was a contingency of eight per cent, and we used all of it in the first quarter."
To write something off -- to accept that money is gone and take it out of the accounts
"We wrote off the deposit in June. It was the cheapest decision of the year."
Exposure -- how much a company stands to lose if one particular thing goes wrong
"Our exposure to a single client was forty per cent, and nobody had said it out loud."
A margin -- what is left after the costs, expressed as a share of what came in
"The margin was eleven per cent on paper and closer to four by the end."
To escalate -- to raise a problem to somebody with more authority to decide about it
"The site manager escalated it twice. Both times it stopped one level below me."
A red flag -- an early sign that something is going wrong, visible before the damage
"The first red flag was a supplier asking for payment in advance."
To cut your losses -- to stop something that is failing before it costs you any more
"We should have cut our losses in April. We stopped in November."
A sunk cost -- money already spent that cannot come back, and that should not decide anything now
"The eight months were a sunk cost, and we kept paying for them for another four."
A post-mortem -- the meeting after a project where a team works out what actually went wrong
"We hold a post-mortem on every project now, and nobody enjoys the first ten minutes."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Half of what gets said in a case discussion is about a past that did not happen, and English builds all of it out of the same two words: have plus a past participle. "If we had seen the red flag, we would have stopped in April." Both halves take a step further back than a Portuguese speaker expects, and that is the whole difficulty. In Portuguese one form of the verb carries this; in English the condition uses had plus the participle and the result uses would have plus the participle, and neither half can borrow the other's shape.

Change the word in front and you change what you are doing with the sentence. Should have is a regret or a criticism: we should have cut our losses eight months earlier. Could have is a possibility nobody took: somebody could have escalated it, and nobody did. Must have is a conclusion you are drawing now about then: the margin must have been wrong from the start. And might have leaves the door open: with a bigger contingency it might have gone the other way.

Two warnings. The first is that the condition never takes would: not if we would have seen. The second is about writing. In speech the have contracts until it sounds exactly like the word of, and native speakers themselves write should of by mistake because of it. Say it contracted and write it in full. Used well, this is the structure that turns a sunk cost from something you regret into something you can explain, which is what a post-mortem is for.

1. Why is "if we would have seen the red flag" wrong?
A Because the condition takes "had" plus the participle; "would have" belongs only in the result.
B Because "see" is an irregular verb.
C Because the sentence should be in the present.
2. What is the difference between "could have escalated" and "should have escalated"?
A There is none; the two are interchangeable.
B "Could have" is a possibility nobody took; "should have" is a regret or a criticism.
C "Could have" is formal and "should have" is informal.
3. Why does the text warn about writing "should of"?
A Because "of" is only used with nouns.
B Because in speech the contracted "have" sounds exactly like "of", and even native speakers write it wrongly.
C Because "should" cannot be contracted at all.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Past That Did Not Happen

GRAMMAR

Everything that did not happen carries have plus the participle. The word in front decides what you are doing.

JobFormExample
A past that did not happenIf + had + participle, would have + participleIf we had seen it, we would have stopped.
Regret, or criticismshould have + participleWe should have cut our losses earlier.
A possibility nobody tookcould have + participleSomebody could have escalated it.
A conclusion about the pastmust have / cannot have + participleThe margin must have been wrong.
Leaving the door openmight have + participleIt might have gone the other way.
Negative resultwould not have + participleThe delay would not have mattered.
In speechhave contracts to 've, never written as ofWe should've stopped. (never should of)

Never: if I knew, I would not take it · if we would have seen it · we should of cut our losses · somebody could escalate it and nobody did · the margin must be wrong from the start.

Stage 2: Put the Case in Order

Order

You have been asked to summarize the case in the discussion. Put your answer in a logical order.

?"The firm took a fifth project because the margin looked the same as the fourth."
?"Nobody put the working capital on the table, and that was the whole decision."
?"The first red flag came in month two and was read as a supplier problem."
?"If somebody had escalated it to the board in month three, we would have stopped."
?"By month eight the money was a sunk cost, and it was treated as a reason to continue."
?"What would you have needed to know in February to decide differently?"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to say the decision would have been different with better information. You say:
A "If I knew that in April, I would not take the project."
B "If I had known that in April, I would not have taken the project."
C "If I would have known that, I would not take it."
You want to criticize a decision without naming anybody. You say:
A "We should have cut our losses eight months earlier."
B "We should of cut our losses earlier."
C "We should cut our losses eight months earlier."
You are drawing a conclusion now about a number from last year. You say:
A "The margin must be wrong from the start."
B "The margin must have been wrong from the start."
C "The margin should be wrong from the beginning."
A colleague says the eight months already spent are a reason to continue. You answer:
A "You are right. We cannot waste what we have already paid."
B "That money is a sunk cost. It cannot come back, so it should not decide the next step."
C "We must have continued because of the eight months."
