Chapter 3

Would, Back Then

One word for many repeated mornings

Grammar Discovery

One Day, or Many?

"Back then, I would call my mother every Sunday."

"On winter mornings my father would make coffee before anyone woke up."

"We would leave the office at nine and nobody thought it was strange."

"I used to be very disorganized. (a state — "would" does not work here)"

FormUseExample
would + base verbA repeated ACTION in the past, when the time is already clearBack then, I would call my mother every Sunday.
used to + base verbA past habit OR a past state — always safeI used to be very disorganized.
would with a stateDoes NOT work. Use "used to" insteadNOT "I would be shy" → "I used to be shy."
Nowadays + present simpleThe contrast that makes the story landNowadays I call her twice a week.
⚠ Careful'Would' only works for repeated ACTIONS, never for states: say 'I used to be shy', never 'I would be shy'. After 'would', the verb stays in its base form: 'would call', not 'would called'. And these two take the -ing form: 'stick to running', 'cut down on eating out'.

Comprehension

Did You Catch It?

1. What was Daniel's routine before?
Back then he would work until midnight and sleep until nine.
2. What changed his routine, and when?
He picked up running in 2019. Nowadays he is up at six and sticks to it, even on Sundays.
3. Which part of his routine does he still fail to keep?
Winding down at nine thirty — he still skips that part when the week gets heavy.
Listening

The Habit That Stuck

Listen first. No text. Sound only.

1. What would the speaker do every January, back then?
She would buy four books, read eleven pages of the first one, and feel guilty until December.
2. What changed — motivation or something else?
Not motivation: size. She decided to read only two pages after brushing her teeth at night.
3. What is her advice at the end?
Make the habit so small that a bad week cannot kill it — skipping one night should not end the whole thing.
Listening

Would and Used To

A different voice, a different message. Listen carefully.

1. Which of the two is the safe one, and why?
"Used to" — it works for both actions and states.
2. When does a story usually switch from "used to" to "would"?
After the first sentence sets the scene and the listener knows which period is being described.
3. Why can you not say "I would be terrible at mornings"?
Because being terrible is a state, not something you did repeatedly — and states belong to "used to".
