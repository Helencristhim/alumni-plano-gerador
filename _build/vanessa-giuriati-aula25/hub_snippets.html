Lesson 25 -- Pre-class

Routines and Habits with Depth

Talk about the week you actually live: "would" for repeated past habits ("Back then I would call every Sunday"), "used to" for past states, and "nowadays" for the contrast. Key words: back then, nowadays, stick to, cut down on, wind down, creature of habit, skip, pick up.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Back then -- At that time in the past — used with past forms
"Back then, I would call my mother every Sunday."
Nowadays -- In the present time, compared with how things were before
"Nowadays I hardly ever check email at night."
Stick to -- To keep doing something and not give it up
"I made a rule in January and I have stuck to it."
Cut down on -- To reduce the amount of something you do or consume
"I cut down on coffee last year."
Wind down -- To relax slowly at the end of the day
"I need twenty minutes to wind down before bed."
Creature of habit -- A person who always does the same things, in the same order
"I am a creature of habit — same route, every day."
Skip -- To not do something that you usually do
"I skipped the gym twice this week."
Pick up -- To start a new habit or skill, often without much effort
"I picked up running during the pandemic."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Back then, my week looked completely different. I would answer email until midnight, and I would leave the office last almost every day. I used to be proud of that, which tells you something. Then, in 2022, I picked up walking — thirty minutes, before work, no phone. It was small enough that I actually stuck to it. Nowadays I stop at eight, I cut down on coffee after two in the afternoon, and I try to wind down before bed. I am a creature of habit, so once something is in the week, it stays. The walk has survived three years. The winding down has not: I still skip it whenever the week gets heavy.

C She would wind down before bed.
2. Why did the walking habit survive?
A Because her manager asked her to do it.
B Because it was small enough that she actually stuck to it.
C Because she cut down on coffee.
3. Which sentence needs "used to" and NOT "would"?
A "I would answer email until midnight."
B "I used to be proud of that."
C "I would leave the office last."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Would for repeated actions

GRAMMAR

"Would" paints a past routine — but only for actions, and only once the period is clear.

StructureUseExample
would + base verbA repeated ACTION in the pastBack then, I would call my mother every Sunday.
used to + base verbA past habit OR a past state — always safeI used to be very disorganized.
would with a stateDoes NOT work — use "used to"NOT "I would be shy" → "I used to be shy."
Nowadays + present simpleThe contrast that makes the story landNowadays I call her twice a week.

'Would' works only for repeated ACTIONS: say 'I used to be shy', never 'I would be shy'. After 'would', keep the base form: 'would call'. And these take the -ing form: 'stick to running', 'cut down on eating out'.
