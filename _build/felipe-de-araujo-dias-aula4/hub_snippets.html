The Trip to Chicago -- Immigration, Hotel, Trade Fair

The words a business trip runs on: boarding pass, baggage claim, customs, to check in, booking, aisle, trade fair, badge. Structure: the past simple, regular and irregular, and the -ed that disappears after did. Mission: tell four days of your own life without stopping to translate.

Customs -- the government desk that checks what you bring into a country
"I walked through customs with nothing to declare."
To check in -- to register at an airport desk, at a hotel or online
"I could not check in before three in the afternoon."
Booking -- an arrangement you made in advance for a room, a table or a seat
"I made the booking in January and asked for a quiet room."
Aisle -- the space you walk along between two rows of seats
"Ten hours is a long time, so I always ask for an aisle seat."
Trade fair -- a large event where companies show their products to buyers
"The trade fair in Chicago happens every March."
Badge -- the card with your name that you wear at an event
"Show your badge to the driver and he will let you on."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last March I flew to Chicago for the first time. I printed my boarding pass on Sunday night, I checked in online, and I asked for an aisle seat. The flight was long but quiet. At immigration the officer asked me three questions and I did not catch the last one, so I asked him to repeat it slowly.

Then everything went quickly. I collected my suitcase at baggage claim, I walked through customs with nothing to declare, and I took a taxi to the hotel. The hotel did not have my booking at first, but they found it. The next morning I collected my badge and I spoke English for three days with people from nine countries. Nobody laughed. Nobody walked away.

1. Why is it I did not catch and not I did not caught?
A Because catch is irregular and irregular verbs never change.
B Because did already carries the past, so the verb goes back to the base form.
C Because the sentence is negative, and negatives use the present.
2. Which verbs in the text are irregular?
A printed, checked, asked, collected, walked
B flew, went, took, found, spoke
C was, laughed, walked, declare
3. Why does the text say the flight was long and not the flight did be long?
A Because to be never uses did. It has its own past: was and were.
B Because long is an adjective and adjectives block did.
C Because the flight is a thing, not a person.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Past Simple

GRAMMAR

One tense for everything that is finished. The hard part is not the -ed. It is remembering that the past only shows up once per sentence.

FormUse it forExample
Regular: verb + -edMost verbs. The spelling is regular, the sound is not.I asked · I printed · I walked
Irregular: a new wordA short list you use every day. Learn them in pairs, not alphabetically.go → went · take → took · speak → spoke · fly → flew
Negative: did not + baseSame for regular and irregular. The verb loses its past because did already has it.I did not catch it · I did not go
Question: did + subject + baseNo -ed anywhere in the question.Did you check in online? · Did he take the shuttle?
To be: was / wereThe one verb with no did. I was, you were, he was, we were, they were. Negative: was not, were not.
The sound of -edThree sounds, never a full extra syllable unless the verb ends in t or d: asked = askt, arrived = arrivd, printed = PRIN-tid.

A habit worth building: before you say a past sentence, decide which single word will carry the past. If two words are fighting for it, one of them is wrong.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are travelling for work. Choose the best thing to say.

The immigration officer asks how long you are staying. You say:
A I stay four days for a trade fair.
B Four days. I am here for a trade fair.
C I did stayed four days.
A colleague asks about your flight. The most natural answer is:
A The flight was long, but I slept for six hours.
B The flight did be long, but I did slept six hours.
C The flight is long, but I sleep six hours.
The hotel cannot find your reservation. You explain:
A I did booked in January through the fair.
B I booked in January, through the fair, and I asked for a quiet room.
C I am booking in January through the fair.
Somebody asks whether you enjoyed the trade fair. You say:
A Did you enjoy? Yes, I enjoy very much.
B Yes. I met nine suppliers and I spoke English for three days.
C Yes, I did enjoyed and I did met nine suppliers.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think about the last time you travelled, for work or not. Record about ninety seconds, all in the past. Start with the day before you left and what you prepared. Then say what happened at the airport, what happened when you arrived, and one thing that did not go as planned. Use at least four words from this lesson, and at least three irregular verbs. Finish with what you would do differently next time. Do not stop to correct yourself.
