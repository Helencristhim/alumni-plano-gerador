Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. Do not translate — the example sentence is the meaning.

Rubric -- the chart that shows how a piece of work will be marked
"Read the rubric before you write anything, not after."
Grade -- the mark that is given for a piece of work
"My grade was better than I expected, which never happens."
Peer review -- when students read and mark each other's work
"Half of the individual mark comes from the peer review form."
Feedback -- comments that tell you how to improve
"Comments are returned within a week, so read the feedback before the final version."
Plagiarism -- using somebody else's work as if it were your own
"Everything is checked for plagiarism automatically, so list your sources."
Group project -- work done by a small team of students together
"The group project is worth thirty percent of the term."
Due -- the moment when a piece of work must be finished and sent
"The final version is due at nine in the evening on the twenty-ninth."
Participation -- how much you join in during class
"Participation is part of the mark here, which surprises everybody."
Expectations -- what people think you should do or achieve
"Ask what the expectations are. Nobody says them out loud."
Workload -- the total amount of work you have to do
"The workload is lighter here, but the deadlines are stricter."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Nobody explains the rubric out loud. It is handed to you on the first day and after that you are expected to have read it. Ours is out of one hundred. Fifty marks are given to the group for the presentation and fifty are given to each of us individually. That individual half is decided by the teacher's notes and by the peer review form, which is filled in by the other three people in my group.

The biggest section is not the one everybody works on. Content and research is twenty marks; analysis is forty. We had written a lot of facts and almost no argument. Everything is submitted through the portal and everything is checked for plagiarism automatically, so the sources are listed at the end.

Drafts are collected on the fifteenth and the feedback is returned a week later. Mine said my section was the clearest one but that the argument was not developed. Marks are taken off if the talk goes over twelve minutes. And participation is part of the grade here, which surprised me: at home nobody counts how often you speak.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Sentence Where the Person Disappears

GRAMMAR

Two pieces, always: a form of to be, plus a past participle.

FormUseExample
Present passiveam / is / are + past participleThe drafts are collected.
Everything is checked.
Past passivewas / were + past participleThe data was collected in 2024.
The forms were returned.
Why use itBecause the action matters and the person does not, or because everybody already knows who does it. Rules, instructions and reports live here.
by + personAdd by only when the doer is the surprising part: "filled in by the other three people". Most of the time you leave it out.
NegativePut not between to be and the participle.The argument was not developed.
QuestionStart with the form of to be.When are the drafts collected?
Was the mark decided by the teacher?

You will read this form far more often than you will say it. Recognising it is the skill; producing it comes after.
