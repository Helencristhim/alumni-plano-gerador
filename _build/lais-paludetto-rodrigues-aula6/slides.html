Chapter 3: The Code

Who Did It?

The sentence where the person disappears on purpose

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The drafts are collected on the fifteenth."

"Marks are taken off if you go over twelve minutes."

"The data was collected in 2024."

"The form is filled in by the other three people in your group."

In three of these you cannot say who does the action. In one you can. Which one, and why?

FormHow it is builtExample
Present passiveam / is / are + past participleThe drafts are collected.
Everything is checked.
Past passivewas / were + past participleThe data was collected in 2024.
The forms were returned.
Why use itBecause the action matters and the person does not, or because everybody already knows who does it. Rules, instructions and reports live here.
by + personAdd by only when the doer is the surprising part: "filled in by the other three people". Most of the time you leave it out.
QuestionWhen is the work collected? — Was the mark decided by the teacher?

Two pieces, always: a form of to be, and a past participle. Nothing else.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"The drafts collected on the fifteenth."
"The drafts are collected on the fifteenth."
"The data was collect in 2024."
"The data was collected in 2024."
"The form is filled in for the other students."
"The form is filled in by the other students."

The passive is always two words: a form of to be plus a past participle. And the person who does the action comes after by, never after for.

Dividing the Project

O
Okay, so I read the rubric properly last night. We've been doing this wrong.
L
Wrong how? I thought the content section was the big one.
O
It isn't. Analysis is forty marks on its own. We've written facts, and facts aren't analysis.
L
So we need an argument, not a list. What about the individual half — how is that decided?
O
Half of it is her notes, half is the peer review. The form is filled in by the three of us about each other.
L
Then we should decide the sections now, in front of everybody. When are the drafts collected?
O
The fifteenth, and the feedback comes back a week later. Would you take the comparison? Your draft section was the clearest one.
L
I'll take it. And I'll write the argument first and the facts after, since that's where the marks are.
Comprehension

About Owen

1. What did he discover when he read the rubric properly?
That analysis is worth forty marks on its own, and that they had written facts instead of an argument.
2. How is the individual half of the mark decided?
Half by the teacher's notes and half by the peer review form, which the group members fill in about each other.
3. Why does he ask her to take the comparison?
Because her section in the draft was the clearest one.
Listening

How the Project Is Marked

The teacher explains the part everybody gets wrong. Sound first — no text.

1. How is the mark divided?
Half to the group for the presentation, half to each student individually.
2. What two things decide the individual half?
The teacher's notes and the peer review form filled in by the other three group members.
3. What are the two dates she gives?
Drafts on the fifteenth, final version on the twenty-ninth.
Listening 2

A Voice Note from Your Group

Owen has read the rubric and he is not happy. Sound first — no text.

1. Which section is the biggest, and what have they written instead?
Analysis, worth forty percent. They wrote a lot of facts and almost no argument.
2. How long must the presentation be, and what happens if you go over?
Twelve minutes, not twenty, and marks are taken off if you go over.
3. What does he ask her to do, and why?
To take the comparison section, because her part of the draft was the clearest one.
