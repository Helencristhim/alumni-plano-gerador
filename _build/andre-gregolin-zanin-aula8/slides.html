Correction requested byA reader, not the source
A correction is printed on page two and the error was printed on page one. Nobody in the trade pretends this is fair. The rule survives anyway, because a newspaper that corrects itself in public can be argued with, and one that never does cannot.
1. What exactly did the paper print, and what had actually been said?
It printed a sentence inside quotation marks. What was actually said was a longer remark, paraphrased by a reporter in 1953.
2. How was the quotation verified, and where did the error enter?
It was verified against the original lecture recording. The error entered in a reprint two months later, which added the quotation marks.
3. Who asked for the correction, and why is that detail interesting?
A reader, not the source. The person misquoted never complained.
Chapter 4: The Code

Everything Steps Back One

The verb, the pronoun, the day — and the one case where nothing moves

Grammar Discovery

What Changed on the Way?

"I never said it." → "He said that he had never said it."

"People assume the claim has been checked." → "She explained that people assumed the claim had been checked."

"What do you want to do after the exam?" → "She asked me what I wanted to do after the exam."

"Push through the pain." → "The coach told me to push through the pain."

Everything steps back one. The verb moves into the past, the pronouns change owner, and in the question the word order goes back to normal — no do, no inversion, no question mark. Look at the third pair twice: that is the one almost nobody gets right.

JobFormExample
A statementsay (that) + one step back"I study every day." → He said he studied every day.
Present perfect and past simpleboth become the past perfect"I have seen it." → She said she had seen it.
A question with a question wordask + question word + normal word order"Where do you live?" → He asked me where I lived.
A yes or no questionask + if or whether"Do you agree?" → She asked whether I agreed.
An order or a piece of advicetell · advise + object + to + verb"Push through it." → He told me to push through it.
Time and place move toonow→then · today→that day · tomorrow→the next day"I will call you tomorrow." → He said he would call me the next day.
Beyond saidtold · explained · admitted · claimed · warned · insisted · pointed outShe admitted that she had not checked it.
No step back neededwhen the fact is still true right now"The exam is in January." → He said the exam is in January.

In one line: everything steps back one — except when the fact itself has not moved.

Listening 1 · An Interview

A Brazilian Student in Boston

One side of a podcast interview. Listen once, then be ready to report it. Sound first — no text.

1. What was she least prepared for, and what was she prepared for?
She was least prepared for how much of the work is talking. The language itself was not the problem.
2. What happened in week two of her first semester?
She was asked to defend a position in front of fourteen people, and nobody had warned her.
3. What is her advice, and what does she say arrives on its own?
Not to spend the last six months polishing grammar, but arguing out loud with somebody who disagrees. She says the grammar arrives on its own if you use it enough.
Listening 2 · A Podcast

The Twelfth Person in the Chain

A researcher on what happens to a sentence when it is passed along. Sound first — no text.

1. What was the experiment, and how many people were in each chain?
Twelve people, each reading the previous version once and then writing it from memory.
2. What disappears first, and what survives to the end?
Numbers go first, almost immediately, and names go second. What survives is the shape of the story and anything that sounds like a moral.
3. What did the twelfth version contain in four of the five chains?
A sentence that appeared in none of the first eleven, and in every case it was the sentence that explained the point.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I know that tell takes a person directly and say does not. I never write he said me.
When I report a question, I put the words back in normal order and drop the do.
I move the verb one step back, and I move tomorrow to the next day as well.
I use more than one reporting verb: explained, admitted, pointed out, warned, insisted.
I know the words: to quote somebody, to summarize, to paraphrase, to assert, to contradict, to verify, to misquote, according to, to put it differently, to take somebody word for it.
