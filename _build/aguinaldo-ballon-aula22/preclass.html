Lesson 22 -- Pre-class

Reading the Room -- Saying Exactly How Sure You Are

Why the best experts sound less certain, and how to measure a claim. Key words: to hedge, a caveat, tentative, an assumption, to overstate, a cue, to gauge, a disclaimer, body language, a claim. Structure: tend to, be likely to, it could be argued that, to some extent.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To hedge -- to avoid saying something too directly or too strongly
"Good lawyers hedge when the law is still open."
A caveat -- a warning that a statement has limits or conditions
"The forecast is good, with one caveat: the license is not ready."
Tentative -- not certain or final, said with some doubt
"This is only a tentative answer. I will confirm it in writing."
An assumption -- something you believe is true, but you have not proved
"Our main assumption is that the tariff will not change."
To overstate -- to say something is bigger or truer than it really is
"Do not overstate the return. The investors will check it."
A cue -- a small signal that tells you what to do or what someone feels
"When the chair stopped writing, that was my cue to finish."
To gauge -- to judge or measure what people think or feel
"I asked one question first to gauge the room."
A disclaimer -- a short statement that limits your responsibility
"The opinion ends with a short disclaimer about future changes in the law."
Body language -- the way people show feelings with their face, arms and position
"His words were polite, but his body language said no."
A claim -- a statement that something is true, which other people may doubt
"Every claim in the opinion needs a source."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A foreign fund asks Aguinaldo a simple question: will the new port rule survive in court? Before he answers, he gauges the room. The chair is skeptical, and nobody is smiling.

So he does not overstate his case. He says that the rule is likely to be challenged, and that courts tend to accept the regulator's view on tariffs. He explains his main assumption: there was no public consultation.

Then he adds one caveat. "To some extent, the result depends on the judge." The chair starts writing again: a good cue. Notice: tend to and likely to + a base verb; to some extent softens the whole sentence.

1. Why does Aguinaldo not overstate his case?
A Because he does not know anything about the new port rule and wants to hide it from the fund.
B Because the room is skeptical.
C Because the chair of the fund asked him to speak only about the tariff and nothing else.
2. Why does the text say "courts tend to accept" and not "courts tend to accepting"?
A Because "tend to" is only used in the past tense, and "accepting" is a present form.
B Because "tend to" is followed by a base verb.
C Because "courts" is a plural noun, so every verb after it must lose the -ing ending.
3. What tells Aguinaldo that his answer worked?
A The chair starts writing again.
B The chair says yes.
C Everyone smiles.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Tend To, Likely To, To Some Extent

GRAMMAR

Hedging says exactly how sure you are. Tend to and likely to take a base verb; it is likely that and it could be argued that take a full sentence.

JobFormExample
What usually happenssubject + tend(s) to + base verbInvestors tend to prefer stable rules.
What will probably happensubject + be (un)likely to + base verbThe rule is likely to be challenged.
The same, with a sentenceIt is (un)likely that + subject + verbIt is unlikely that the court will cancel it.
An opinion, not a factIt could be argued that + sentenceIt could be argued that the tariff is too low.
Only partly trueto some extent / in most cases / somewhatTo some extent, the delay was predictable.
Negativedon't tend to / be unlikely toCourts don't tend to change tariffs. The fund is unlikely to invest.

Never: tend to preferring · It is likely the court accept · Maybe it will probably fail · It could be argue that...

Stage 2: Put the Answer in Order

Order

A skeptical investor asks if the project is safe. Put your answer in a logical order.

?"The short answer is yes, with one caveat."
?"Projects with stable tariffs tend to perform well."
?"My main assumption is that the tariff will not change."
?"The caveat is the license, which is likely to take six more months."
?"To some extent, that delay is already in the budget."
?"So I would invest, but I would not promise the first-year return."
