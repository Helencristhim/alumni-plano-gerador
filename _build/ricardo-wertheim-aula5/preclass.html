Lesson 05 -- Pre-class

Starting the Conversation -- How to Break the Ice and Keep It Going

Opening a conversation with a stranger and keeping it alive. Key words: approach, strike up, curious, mention, in common, background, fascinating, get to know. Structure: the present continuous for what is temporary and happening now, in contrast with the present simple for your permanent life.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To approach -- to walk up to someone in order to speak to them
"A woman approached me and asked where the walls were."
To strike up -- to start a conversation with someone you do not know
"I struck up a conversation with the man at the next table."
Curious -- wanting to know more about something or someone
"I was curious about the stone, so I asked her."
To mention -- to say something quickly, without explaining it in detail
"She mentioned that her grandfather was born here."
In common -- something that two people share, an interest or an experience
"We are both architects, so we have a lot in common."
Background -- where a person comes from and what they did before
"Tell me about your background -- how did you become an architect?"
Fascinating -- so interesting that you want to keep listening
"That is fascinating -- tell me more about that."
To get to know -- to slowly learn what a person is really like
"I would like to get to know the people, not only the buildings."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

I live in Sao Paulo and I work as an architect. I travel to Europe once or twice a year, usually in the summer. That is my life, and it does not change much.

But this month everything is different. Right now I am sitting on a terrace in Dubrovnik. I am traveling through Croatia for two weeks, and I am staying in this town for four nights. The woman at the next table is looking at the old wall, not at the sea, so I am curious. She is not reading her phone. In a moment I am going to approach her and strike up a conversation, because I know one thing already: we probably have something in common.

1. Why does the text say "I live in Sao Paulo" but "I am staying in this town"?
A Because living there is permanent, and staying in the town is only for these four nights.
B Because "stay" is an irregular verb and "live" is regular.
C Because one is the past and the other is the future.
2. The text says "I know one thing already", not "I am knowing". Why?
A Because the sentence is a question.
B Because "know" is a state, not an action you can watch, so it does not take -ing.
C Because the writer is not sure about it.
3. "The woman at the next table is looking at the old wall." What does the -ing form tell you here?
A That she looks at that wall every day of her life.
B That it is happening right now, at this moment.
C That she looked at it yesterday and finished.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple and Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

Your life takes the simple form. Your trip takes the -ing.

FormRuleExample
Present simple
I live / she lives		Permanent: facts about your life and things you repeat. Remember the -s for he, she and it.I live in Sao Paulo.
I travel twice a year.
Present continuous
am / is / are + -ing		Temporary: what is true now, this week, this trip. Never drop the am, is or are.I am traveling through Croatia.
She is looking at the wall.
Question
Are you + -ing?		The question word comes first, then are, then you. This is the most natural opener on a trip.How long are you staying here?
Are you visiting the old town today?
NegativeThe not attaches to am, is or are. The verb keeps its -ing.She is not reading her phone.
The trap: state verbsSome verbs describe a state, not an action you can watch, and they almost never take -ing: know, want, like, need, believe, understand. You say "I know this city", never "I am knowing this city".

A useful test: could someone film it? If yes, it probably takes -ing. Nobody can film you knowing something.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are meeting someone for the first time. Choose the best thing to say.

You want to open a conversation with someone at the next table. The best opener is:
A "Do you like it here?"
B "What brings you to Dubrovnik?"
C "Why are you here?"
She asks what you do and how long you are in Croatia. You answer:
A "I am being an architect and I travel here two weeks."
B "I am an architect, and I am traveling here for two weeks."
C "I am architect and I am travel here for two weeks."
She tells you something surprising about her grandfather. You want her to continue. You say:
A "That is fascinating -- tell me more about that."
B "Yes. Very good."
C "I am knowing this story already."
You discover you are both architects. The natural thing to say is:
A "We have much things in common."
B "We are the same person, then."
C "Then we have something in common."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Imagine you are on a hotel terrace in Split and a stranger sits down at the next table. Take about ninety seconds and do the whole first minute out loud: open the conversation, say what you are doing in Croatia this month and what you do back home, and ask that person two questions. Use the -ing form at least three times and two of the phrases from this lesson. Do not stop to correct yourself.
