Chapter 3: Right Now

Always, or Just This Week?

I travel every year · I'm traveling through Croatia

Grammar Discovery

Permanent or Temporary?

"I live in Sao Paulo and I work as an architect."

"I am traveling through Croatia this summer."

"I travel to Europe twice a year."

"I am staying in Split for three days."

Two of these are true every year of your life. Two are true only this month. Which is which?

FormUseExample
present simple
I live / he lives		Permanent: facts about your life, and things you repeatI live in Sao Paulo. I travel twice a year.
present continuous
am / is / are + -ing		Temporary: what is true now, this week, this tripI am traveling through Croatia. I am staying for three days.
Question
Are you + -ing?		The most natural opener with a stranger on a tripAre you visiting the old town today?
The trapSome verbs almost never take -ing, because they are not actions you can watch: know, want, like, need, believe. You say "I know this city", never "I am knowing this city".

Your life takes the simple form. Your trip takes the -ing.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I travel through Croatia this summer."
"I am traveling through Croatia this summer."
"I am knowing this city very well."
"I know this city very well."
"You are staying here how long?"
"How long are you staying here?"

A trip is temporary, so it takes -ing. But know, want, like and need never do. And in a question, are comes before you.

Toolkit

Six Phrases That Do All the Work

"What brings you to Dubrovnik?"

opens — better than "why are you here?"

"Have you been here before?"

opens — works with anyone, anywhere

"That's really interesting — tell me more."

keeps it alive — the other person continues

"Actually..."

corrects a wrong impression, politely

"By the way..."

adds something new without changing the subject

"Speaking of which..."

jumps to a new topic from what was just said

Chapter 4: The Wine Bar

A Stranger at the Next Table

Dubrovnik, eight in the evening

Dialogue

Two Architects, One Wall

R
Excuse me, I am curious — you are looking at that wall, not at the view. Are you an architect?
C
I am, actually. I restore old stone buildings in Chicago. Most people look at the sea, so nobody ever approaches me.
R
Then we have something in common. I am an architect too, and I am traveling through Croatia for two weeks. What brings you here?
C
My grandfather was born about an hour from here. He never came back, so I am spending three weeks looking for the village.
R
That is fascinating — tell me more about that. Did he ever mention the name of the place?
C
Only once, and I wrote it down. By the way, I am walking the walls tomorrow at seven, before the crowds arrive. You are welcome to come.
R
I would like that. Speaking of which, what is your background — how did you start working with old stone?
Comprehension

About Claire

1. What does Claire do in Chicago?
She restores old stone buildings.
2. Why is she spending three weeks in Croatia?
Her grandfather was born near there and never came back, so she is looking for his village.
3. What is she doing tomorrow at seven, and why so early?
She is walking the walls, before the crowds arrive.

Listening 2

How to Talk to Strangers

A travel writer gives three rules. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is his first rule about where to look?
Talk to the person who is looking at something, not at a phone.
2. Why does he say not to ask "do you like it here?"
Because the answer is yes or no, and then the conversation stops.
3. What is the third rule, about yourself?
Give one small thing about yourself, so the other person can ask you something back.
