Lesson recap

A quick reference to the situation and the language you used.

SituationA short briefing to a coordinator who has read neither of two documents that should agree.
What you are doingNaming where the two stop agreeing, and saying which document each claim comes from.
Quoting a documentThe syllabus says… · According to the plan,… · That comes from…, not from…
Naming a divergenceThese two do not line up on… · One says X, the other says Y.
Marking your own readingMy reading is that… · Neither of them actually says it, but…
Key vocabularyto allocate · to run beyond · a commitment · approval · to state something
The test that settles it Before any sentence, ask one question: could someone with the document in front of them disagree with this?
If no, say it flat — it is a quotation. If yes, mark it as yours.

Optional practice

Speak More

About 60 to 90 seconds

Record a short voice message, if you would like to.

The situation Think of two documents in your own school that are supposed to agree — a plan and a syllabus, a syllabus and a coursebook map, a course description and what is actually taught.

What you are trying to do: tell a colleague what does not line up, in a way she could repeat to someone else without checking.

You could cover:

  • what each document is, in one sentence;
  • one point where they stop agreeing, and where each side of it is written;
  • one thing you concluded that neither document actually says;
  • what you think should be decided, and by whom.

Three things a sentence can be

Use this whenever you want to review what you did in class.

What you are doingHow it tends to soundWhat goes wrong without it
QuotingThe syllabus says… · According to the plan,…Without the source, a fact sounds like an opinion, and the other person has nothing to check.
ConcludingMy reading is that… · Putting the two together,…Unmarked, your conclusion is heard as something the document says — and someone will look for it.
RecommendingI would… · What I would suggest is…Unmarked, it sounds like a decision that has already been taken.
