Lesson 28 -- Pre-class

Eating Out Abroad -- From Casual Lunch to Business Dinner

Get ready for two meals in New York: a quick lunch and a business dinner with a supplier. Learn to report what people asked you to do with ask / tell + person + (not) to ("The host asked me to choose the wine", "He told me not to talk about prices"). Key words: walk-in, booth, to grab a bite, business casual, small plates, to treat, to make a toast, to expense, picky eater, corkage fee.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Walk-in -- a guest who arrives at a restaurant without a reservation
"At lunch, most places in Midtown take walk-ins."
Booth -- a table with fixed seats, like a small sofa, on two sides
"Can we have a booth by the window, please?"
To grab a bite -- to eat something quickly, usually a light meal
"Let's grab a bite before the meeting."
Business casual -- a dress code that is not a suit, but not jeans and sneakers
"The dress code for the dinner is business casual."
Small plates -- little dishes that people at the table share
"We ordered six small plates for the table."
To treat -- to pay for someone else's meal or drink
"Put your card away. I'm treating you tonight."
To make a toast -- to raise your glass and say something nice about a person or an event
"At the end of the dinner, the host made a toast to the new partnership."
To expense -- to ask your company to pay for something you bought for work
"Keep the receipt, so you can expense the dinner."
Picky eater -- a person who eats only a few kinds of food
"My son is a picky eater. He only eats pasta and chicken."
Corkage fee -- the money a restaurant charges when you bring your own wine
"The corkage fee is thirty dollars a bottle."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

On Tuesday, Lucimara has forty-five minutes before a meeting, so she and a colleague grab a bite at a small restaurant in Midtown. They are walk-ins, and the host asks them to wait five minutes for a booth.

On Thursday, the dinner with the supplier is very different. Before the dinner, Greg tells her not to bring wine, because the corkage fee is high, and he asks her to confirm the names of her colleagues. At the restaurant, everybody shares small plates, and at the end, Greg asks Lucimara to make a toast. When she tries to pay, he says, "No, no. Put your card away. We are treating you tonight."

1. What did the host ask Lucimara and her colleague to do on Tuesday?
A To come back in the evening, because all the tables in the restaurant were reserved for lunch.
B To wait five minutes for a booth.
C To order quickly.
2. Why did Greg tell her not to bring wine?
A Because the corkage fee is high.
B Because he does not drink wine, and the restaurant does not serve wine at business dinners.
C Because she is a picky eater.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "Greg said me to make a toast at the end of the dinner with the supplier."
B "Greg told to her not bring wine."
C "Greg asked her to make a toast."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reporting Requests and Instructions

GRAMMAR

How to tell someone what other people asked you to do, or told you not to do.

FormUseExample
ask + person + to + verbreport a request ("Could you...?", "Please...")"Could you choose the wine?" → He asked me to choose the wine.
tell + person + to + verbreport an instruction or an order"Arrive on time." → She told me to arrive on time.
ask / tell + person + not to + verbreport a negative request or order ("Don't...")"Don't bring wine." → He told me not to bring wine.
say (that) + sentence (Lesson 16)report a statement, not a requestShe said that the food was great.
RuleAfter ask and tell, say the person first, then to + verb: "He told me to arrive". Never "He said me to..." and never "He told to me...". In the negative, not goes before to: "not to talk", not "to not talk".

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think about a real lunch or dinner with a client or a colleague. Where was it, who paid, and how was it different from a meal with friends? Then report three things people asked you to do or told you not to do, before or during the meal. Use asked me to / told me (not) to at least three times. Take your time.
