Before You Sign -- Paraphrase and Reformulation

How to check, in real time, that everybody agreed to the same thing before a deal is signed. Key words: to paraphrase, to recap, to spell out, to be on the same page, to pin down, to iron out, to get your wires crossed, to read between the lines, a nuance, to sign off on, watertight, to have second thoughts. Structure: reformulation frames -- so what you're saying is (that), in other words, if I've understood correctly, are you saying (that)...?

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To paraphrase -- to say what somebody said again, using your own words
"Let me paraphrase to make sure I've got it."
To recap -- to repeat the main points of what was said, briefly
"To recap: three years, São Paulo only, and a minimum volume."
To spell out -- to explain something clearly and in detail, so nothing is left unsaid
"Could you spell out what happens if they miss the target?"
To be on the same page -- to understand a situation in the same way as the other person
"Before I sign, I want to be sure we're on the same page."
To pin down -- to find out or decide something exactly
"We still need to pin down the delivery date."
To iron out -- to solve the last small problems or disagreements
"Let's iron out the territory clause before Friday."
To get your wires crossed -- when two people understand something in different ways, without noticing
"I think we got our wires crossed about the deadline."
To read between the lines -- to understand what somebody means, even when they do not say it directly
"Reading between the lines, they are not ready to sign."
A nuance -- a very small difference in meaning that is easy to miss
"The nuance is in one word: may, not shall."
To sign off on -- to give your final, official approval to something
"The board has to sign off on the final draft."
Watertight -- so well made that nobody can find a mistake or a gap in it
"I won't sign until the clause is watertight."
To have second thoughts -- to start to doubt a decision you have already made
"The client is having second thoughts about the price."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At half past four, Dana Whitfield called Rodrigo. At five, she was going to sign a distribution agreement with Vale Norte, a Brazilian distributor, and she wanted to be sure they were on the same page. Rodrigo offered to recap the deal: exclusive rights in the state of São Paulo for three years, and a minimum volume of 200,000 units a year.

Dana explained that if Vale Norte missed the minimum, Kestrel could end the exclusivity. So what you're saying is that the exclusivity isn't automatic, Rodrigo said, and he asked her to spell out the details. Ninety days to catch up, and then the exclusivity ends. In other words, they lose the exclusivity but keep the contract. That was the nuance he wanted to pin down.

Then came the surprise. The final draft gave Vale Norte the whole Southeast region. They had got their wires crossed, and Dana started having second thoughts. If I've understood correctly, everything else is fine, Rodrigo said. He would iron out the territory clause with the other lawyer, and Dana could sign off on the agreement the next day, once it was watertight.

1. "So what you're saying is that the exclusivity isn't automatic." What is Rodrigo doing?
A He is telling Dana that she made a mistake when she negotiated the minimum volume with Vale Norte.
B He is giving Dana's idea back in his own words, to check it.
C He is adding a new condition to the deal.
2. Why did Dana start having second thoughts?
A Because the final draft gave Vale Norte a bigger territory than she agreed to.
B Because Vale Norte wanted a lower minimum volume and a longer notice period than the one in the draft.
C Because Rodrigo told her not to sign.
3. What did Rodrigo decide to do?
A Cancel the agreement.
B Sign the agreement for Dana that afternoon and correct the territory clause later with an amendment.
C Fix the territory clause with the other lawyer, so Dana could sign the next day.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Paraphrase and Reformulation

GRAMMAR

The frame tells the other person you are checking. What comes after it is always a normal statement.

JobFormExample
Give it backSo what you're saying is (that) + statementSo what you're saying is that they have to earn it.
Shorter versionIn other words, + statementIn other words, the contract stays.
CheckIf I've understood correctly, + statementIf I've understood correctly, it's three years.
QuestionAre you saying (that) + statement order?Are you saying the price is final?
Invite a correctionCorrect me if I'm wrong, but + statementCorrect me if I'm wrong, but the fee is fixed.
Ask for moreWhat exactly do you mean by + noun?What exactly do you mean by "reasonable efforts"?
When you got it wrongThat's not quite what I meant. What I meant was...What I meant was the state, not the region.

Never: If I understood well · So you're saying does it change? · Are you saying me that... · In other words, that they keep it

Stage 2: Run the Closing Call in Order

Order

Align, recap, ask, give it back, flag the problem, fix it. Put the call in that order.

?"So what you're saying is that the exclusivity isn't automatic."
?"Before you sign, let's make sure we're on the same page."
?"Let's iron out that clause before anyone signs off on it."
?"Correct me if I'm wrong, but the draft says the whole Southeast."
?"Let me recap: São Paulo, three years, and a minimum volume."
?"Could you spell out what happens if they miss the target?"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

On a call, the other side says: "We'll pay within thirty days." You want to check what they mean. You say:
A "Are you saying thirty calendar days from the invoice?"
B "If I understood well, you pay in thirty days, so we can put that date in the draft today."
C "Are you saying me thirty days?"
A client explains a long, complicated worry. You want to show you have understood, in fewer words. You say:
A "In other words, that you're worried about the price and about what could happen to it later."
B "You are wrong about the price."
C "In other words, you're worried the price could change after signing."
The other side says they will use "reasonable efforts" to deliver on time. That is too vague. You say:
A "Fine, no problem."
B "What exactly do you mean by reasonable efforts? Could you spell it out?"
C "So what you're saying is do you deliver on time or not, because we need to know that before Friday?"
Your paraphrase was wrong, and the other person corrects you. Now they paraphrase you, and they are wrong. You say:
A "That's not quite what I meant. What I meant was the state, not the region."
B "No."
C "You didn't understand anything I said on this call, so let's start again from the very beginning."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Choose a recent email or clause from your work. In about sixty seconds, give it back in your own words, as if you were checking it with the other side on a call. Use at least three frames: so what you're saying is, in other words, if I've understood correctly, or are you saying that.
