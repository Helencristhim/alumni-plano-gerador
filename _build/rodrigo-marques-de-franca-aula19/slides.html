Chapter 3: The Code

Give It Back

Five short frames that turn a nod into an agreement

Grammar Discovery

Nothing New. Everything Checked.

So what you're saying is that the exclusivity isn't automatic.

In other words, they lose the exclusivity but keep the contract.

If I've understood correctly, everything else is fine.

Are you saying that you only agreed to São Paulo?

None of these sentences adds new information. What do they do? And what comes after the red words: a question or a statement?

JobFormExample
Give it back in your wordsSo what you're saying is (that) + statementSo what you're saying is that they have to earn it.
A shorter, simpler versionIn other words, + statement (no that)In other words, the contract stays.
Check your understandingIf I've understood correctly, + statementIf I've understood correctly, it's three years.
Turn it into a questionAre you saying (that) + statement order?Are you saying the price is final?
Invite a correctionCorrect me if I'm wrong, but + statementCorrect me if I'm wrong, but the fee is fixed.
Ask them to say moreWhat exactly do you mean by + noun?What exactly do you mean by "reasonable efforts"?
The trapsno do / does after the frame · say + no person · correctly, not wellNot you're saying does it change, not are you saying me, not if I understood well.

In one line: the frame announces the check; what follows is a normal statement.

Chapter 4: Real Voices

Signing at Five

One call, two recordings, one email

Dialogue

Thirty Minutes to Sign

D
Rodrigo, I'm signing the distribution agreement with Vale Norte at five. I just want to make sure we're on the same page before I do.
R
Of course. Let me recap, and stop me if I get anything wrong. They get exclusive rights in the state of São Paulo for three years. Is that right?
D
Yes. And the minimum volume is two hundred thousand units a year. If they miss it, we can end the exclusivity.
R
So what you're saying is that the exclusivity isn't automatic: they have to earn it every year. Could you spell out what happens if they miss the target?
D
We send a notice, they have ninety days to catch up, and if they don't, the exclusivity ends. The contract itself stays in place.
R
In other words, they lose the exclusivity but keep the contract. That's the nuance I wanted to pin down.
D
Exactly. Their lawyer was very clear about that on the last call.
R
Then I think we may have got our wires crossed somewhere. The final draft gives them the whole Southeast region, not just São Paulo.
D
The whole Southeast? That's not what I agreed to. Now I'm having second thoughts about signing today.
R
If I've understood correctly, everything else is fine. I'll call their lawyer, we'll iron out the territory clause, and you can sign off on it tomorrow, once it's watertight.
Comprehension

About Dana

1. What is Dana going to sign, and when?
A distribution agreement with Vale Norte, at five o'clock that afternoon.
2. According to Dana, what happens if Vale Norte misses the minimum volume?
Kestrel sends a notice and Vale Norte has ninety days to catch up. If it doesn't, the exclusivity ends, but the contract stays in place.
3. Why does Dana start having second thoughts?
Because the final draft gives Vale Norte the whole Southeast region, and she only agreed to the state of São Paulo.
Listening 1 · The Interpreter

Meaning, Not Words

A conference interpreter on what her job really is. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say her job really is?
To carry meaning from one language to another — not to translate words.
2. What does she do in her private work when something is unclear?
She asks. She paraphrases with "so what you're saying is" or "if I've understood correctly".
3. Why is the moment when both sides nod dangerous, according to her?
Nodding feels like agreement, but often each person only heard what they expected to hear.
Listening 2 · The Negotiator

Shorter and Calmer

A former police negotiator on the skill he teaches first. Sound first — no text.

1. What did he do before he started teaching executives?
He was a police negotiator, for twelve years.
2. What do people under pressure need, according to him?
Not agreement: they need to know that you have understood them.
3. What happens if your paraphrase is wrong?
That's fine: they correct you, and you get better information.
Real Document

The Summary Email

Re: Kestrel / Vale Norte · agreed terms
From: Beatriz Antunes, counsel for Vale Norte · To: Rodrigo Marques de França

Dear Rodrigo, as discussed, here are the terms we agreed on today's call:

1. Exclusive distribution in the Southeast region for three years.

2. Minimum volume: 200,000 units per year.

3. If the minimum is missed, Vale Norte will use reasonable efforts to catch up within the next quarter.

4. Either party may terminate on 180 days' notice.

We look forward to signing at 5 p.m. · Best regards, Beatriz
1. Which point is different from what Dana understood?
Point 1: the email says the whole Southeast region. Dana agreed to the state of São Paulo only.
2. What is missing from point 3, compared with what Dana said?
The consequence: Dana said the exclusivity ends after ninety days. The email only says "reasonable efforts" and never says what happens if they fail.
3. What would you say to Beatriz on the phone about point 3?
For example: "What exactly do you mean by reasonable efforts? Correct me if I'm wrong, but we agreed that the exclusivity ends after ninety days."
