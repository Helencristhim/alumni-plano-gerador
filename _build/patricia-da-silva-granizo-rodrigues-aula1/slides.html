Chapter 3: The First Two Minutes

Greg Halloran, Thursday Morning

A first conversation with the VP who will chair your monthly call

Dialogue

A First Call With Atlanta

G
Greg Halloran, operations. We have not spoken before. So, what do you do?
P
Patricia Granizo. I run HR and internal communication for JEM System in Brazil.
G
Both of them? That is unusual. How big is the operation down there?
P
Our headcount is just under two hundred, and about half of that workforce is in the field. The plant managers report into operations, not into me.
G
Good, that is the same structure we have here. And what are you working on right now?
P
At the moment I am rolling out a new performance cycle across both sites, and my team is redesigning onboarding before we open in Texas.
G
Texas is mine. We should be talking every month, not every quarter. I will move you onto the people call.
P
That works for me. And what are you working on at the moment?
Comprehension

About Greg

1. How does Greg open the conversation, and what does he say about the two of them?
He gives his name and his area, operations, and says they have not spoken before.
2. What does Greg say when he hears that the plant managers report into operations?
He says it is good, because it is the same structure they have in the United States.
3. What does Greg decide by the end, and what is his reason?
He decides to move Patricia onto the monthly people call instead of the quarterly one, because Texas is his and they should be talking every month.

Chapter 4: The Code

Two Verbs, Two Different Jobs

Why an American hears a permanent role or a temporary one in your first sentence

Grammar Discovery

What Changes and What Does Not

"I run HR and internal communication for JEM System."

"At the moment I am rolling out a new performance cycle."

"The plant managers report into operations, not into me."

"This quarter we are hiring for two new sites in the United States."

Two of these are true every week. Two are only true right now. Which is which, and what in the verb tells you?

FormWhat it doesExample
present simpleyour remit: what is true every week, with or without a projectI run HR for JEM System.
present continuouswhat is on your desk right now, and what will endAt the moment I am rolling out a new cycle.
at the moment, this quarter, right nowthe time words that force the continuousThis quarter we are hiring for two new sites.
usually, every week, alwaysthe time words that force the simpleI take the Atlanta call every Monday.
the mistake that costs youI am running HR tells an American your job is temporary. Say what you run first, then switch to the continuous for the project.

An American asks two questions in the first minute: what do you do, and what are you working on. They are two different forms, and answering both in ten seconds is what makes an introduction sound senior.

Chapter 5: The Ear

At Their Speed, Not Yours

Two recordings at natural pace, with no text on the screen

Listening 1

The Voicemail From Atlanta

Greg leaves a message the night before. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What is changing in September, and what does Greg want from Patricia?
The people review becomes a monthly call instead of a quarterly one, and he wants her on it every time: twenty minutes, no deck, just the numbers and what she is working on.
2. What does Greg say nobody expects, and what does he expect instead?
Nobody expects a presentation. He expects her to own the number and to be able to say what is behind it.
3. What does he say about waiting to be asked?
She does not have to wait for him to ask. The people who do well on that call bring the problem before somebody else finds it.
Listening 2

How an American Director Introduces Herself

A colleague from the eastern region, talking about her own introduction.

0:000:00
1. What are the two sentences she uses to describe her job?
One sentence about what she runs, people operations for the eastern region, which never changes, and one line about what is on her desk this quarter, a benefits change that affects about nine hundred people.
2. What did she used to do, and what did she notice while she was doing it?
She used to tell people her entire history, and she could see them waiting for her to stop.
3. Why does she say Americans cut in, and what does she advise?
They are not being rude, they are trying to find the hook they can ask about. She advises giving the hook early and asking the same question back.
