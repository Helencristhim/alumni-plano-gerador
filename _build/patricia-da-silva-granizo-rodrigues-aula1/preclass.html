Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

The Two Minutes That Introduce You

The ten words your American counterparts use without explaining them, the two verb forms that decide whether your job sounds permanent or temporary, and the two sentences that open every conversation in Atlanta. Key words: remit, headcount, onboarding, retention, workforce, stakeholder, rollout, to report into, cross-functional, to oversee. Structure: present simple for what you run, present continuous for what you are working on.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read it inside a real sentence. These are the words Atlanta uses without stopping to explain them.

Remit -- the area of work a person or a department is officially responsible for
"Internal communication sits inside my remit as well as HR."
Headcount -- the total number of people a company or a team employs
"Our headcount in Brazil is just under two hundred."
Onboarding -- the process of settling a new employee into the company
"We redesigned onboarding so a new hire is useful in three weeks."
Retention -- keeping the people you already employ instead of losing them
"Retention is the first number the American team asks about."
Workforce -- everybody who works for a company, taken together
"Half of our workforce is in the field, not in an office."
Stakeholder -- a person or group with an interest in the decision you are making
"Every stakeholder saw the policy before it went out."
Rollout -- the launch of a new policy or system across the company
"The rollout starts in Brazil and reaches the US sites in October."
To report into -- to have somebody as your direct manager
"The plant managers report into operations, not into me."
Cross-functional -- involving people from more than one department
"It is a cross-functional project, so finance is in the room too."
To oversee -- to be in charge of work that other people carry out
"I oversee four teams and I run none of them day to day."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

Patricia runs HR and internal communication for JEM System in Brazil. Around two hundred people report into her structure, and about half of that workforce sits in the field rather than in an office. That part of the description does not change from one quarter to the next.

This quarter is different. She is rolling out a new performance cycle, she is preparing the first monthly call with the American leadership team, and her team is redesigning onboarding for the two sites that open next year. None of those three things will be true in twelve months, and that is exactly why she describes them with a different form.

1. Why does the text say Patricia runs HR and not Patricia is running HR?
A Because the continuous is too informal for a written profile.
B Because running HR is her permanent remit, not a temporary assignment.
C Because the verb run has no continuous form.
2. She is rolling out a new performance cycle. What does that form tell an American listener?
A That the rollout is on her desk now and will end.
B That she rolls out a performance cycle every quarter.
C That somebody else is doing the rollout for her.
3. Which sentence would make an American ask whether your job is temporary?
A I run HR for JEM System.
B I am running HR for JEM System.
C This quarter I am rolling out a new performance cycle.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- What You Run and What You Are Working On

GRAMMAR

Two forms, two different questions. An American counterpart asks both in the first minute, and answering them with the wrong form changes what they hear about your position.

FormWhat it doesExample
present simpleyour remit: what is true every week, with or without a projectI run HR for JEM System.
present continuouswhat is on your desk right now, and what will endAt the moment I am rolling out a new cycle.
at the moment, this quarter, right nowthe time words that force the continuousThis quarter we are hiring for two new sites.
usually, every week, alwaysthe time words that force the simpleI take the Atlanta call every Monday.
the mistake that costs youI am running HR tells an American your job is temporary. Say what you run first, then switch to the continuous for the project.

Say the permanent sentence first and the current one second. Reversed, the introduction sounds like a project description instead of a job.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

One right answer each. Read the situation before you look at the options.

You are introduced to the VP of Operations in Atlanta. He asks: So, what do you do? Which answer sounds like a director?
A I work with people things at JEM System.
B I run HR and internal communication for JEM System in Brazil, and at the moment I am rolling out a new performance cycle.
C I am running the HR department since 2019, when I entered in the company.
D My remit is very large and complex, it is difficult to explain quickly.
On the monthly call, retention has dropped and nobody has asked about it yet. What does Greg expect you to do?
A Wait until he raises it, so that you do not sound negative.
B Bring it up yourself and say what is behind the number.
C Prepare a deck about it for the following month.
D Send it by email after the call instead.
Which sentence tells an American that the assignment is temporary?
A I oversee four teams.
