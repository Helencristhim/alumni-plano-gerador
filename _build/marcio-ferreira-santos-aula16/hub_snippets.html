The Other Side of the Argument

Reading an analyst note with two sides and summarizing both. Key words: bull case, bear case, counterargument, caveat, to concede, to offset, valid, on balance. Structure: contrast and concession -- although, whereas, despite, however.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Bull case -- the argument that a company or a plan will do well
"The bull case is volume growth in the new stores."
Bear case -- the argument that a company or a plan will do badly
"The bear case is a long price war."
Counterargument -- a reason that goes against another argument
"Every good note has a counterargument."
Caveat -- a warning that limits what you just said
"One caveat: the data is only from one region."
To concede -- to admit that the other side has a good point
"I concede that the app has a small reach."
To offset -- to balance a loss with a gain of a similar size
"Higher volume will offset the lower price."
Valid -- logical and reasonable, so you must take it seriously
"That is a valid point."
On balance -- after you consider all the arguments on both sides
"On balance, we recommend the loyalty price."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

On Tuesday Marcio read two analyst notes about the same retailer. The first note gave the bull case: although food inflation was high, the loyalty prices in the app were bringing customers back. The second note gave the bear case. Despite the growth of the app, it said, only a third of the customers used it, whereas the main competitor reached almost everybody with paper flyers. However, the second analyst added a caveat: her data came from only one month. In the pricing committee, Marcio presented both sides. He conceded that the reach was a valid problem. Then he gave his counterargument: the margin gain would offset the technology costs within a year. On balance, the committee kept the strategy.

1. Why does the text say "Despite the growth of the app" and not "Despite the app grew"?
A After despite you need a noun or an -ing verb, not a full sentence.
B Because "grew" is in the past tense, and despite is only used with the present tense in business texts.
C Because "the app" is a plural noun.
2. In the text, "whereas" compares:
A two analysts who disagree about the price war and about the margin of the company next year
B two months of data
C the reach of the app and the reach of the competitor
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "Although the reach is small, but the margin is better."
B "Although the reach is small, the margin is better."
C "Despite of the small reach, the margin is better, so the committee kept the strategy."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Contrast and Concession: although, whereas, despite, however

GRAMMAR

The four words have a similar meaning. The difference is the grammar that comes after them.

WordRuleExample
although+ a full sentence. You accept one fact, but the other fact is more important. Never although ... but in the same sentence.Although the risk is real, the upside is bigger.
whereas+ a full sentence. Two facts side by side, with the same weight. Comma before it.Option A protects volume, whereas Option B protects margin.
despite / in spite of+ a noun or an -ing verb. Never a full sentence, and never despite of.Despite the price war · despite being cheaper
howeverStarts a new sentence: full stop before it, comma after it.The test was positive. However, it was small.
Concede, then counterAccept the other side first, then give your point: "That is a valid point. However, the payback is twice as fast." Negative and question forms do not change: "Although it is not cheap, ..." · "Despite not having the data, ..." · "However, is it enough?"

Quiz

A "No, I do not agree with you, because I already checked all the numbers in my model last week."
