Chapter 3: Concede, Then Counter

Concede, Then Counter

The words that tell the listener where your argument is going

Grammar Discovery

What Job Does Each Colored Word Do?

"The exam treats everybody the same. However, not everybody arrives the same."

"Medicine is about other people. What’s more, it uses the part of basketball I loved."

"Although I miss the game, I do not regret leaving it."

"I moved to São Paulo for the cursinho. As a result, I stopped training completely."

Each colored word tells the listener what is coming before it arrives: something opposite, something more, something you admit, or a consequence. Now look at where each one sits and at the commas around it.

JobConnectorsExample
Contrasthowever · on the other hand · that said · even soIt is objective. However, it is not fair.
Additionfurthermore · what’s more · on top of thatIt is long. What’s more, it is expensive.
Concessionalthough · even though · while · grantedAlthough I miss it, I do not regret it.
Resultas a result · consequently · which means thatI moved. As a result, I stopped training.
Positionhowever, what’s more, as a result start a NEW sentence + commaNot It is objective, however it is not fair.
One is enoughalthough joins two clauses — never with butNot Although I miss it, but I do not regret it.
The moveconcede, pause, counterGranted, it is objective. That said, it measures speed.

In speech, that said, to be fair and on top of that sound natural. However, furthermore and consequently belong more to writing.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Defend the Decision

One devil’s advocate, two recordings, one scoring sheet

Dialogue

Why Throw That Away?

H
Let me play devil’s advocate for a minute. You were good at basketball, really good. Why throw that away for six years of medical school?
A
I did not throw it away. To be fair, basketball gave me everything I know about discipline. That being said, I could not see myself doing it at thirty-five.
H
Plenty of people coach at thirty-five. That is a career too. What is your counterargument to that?
A
Coaching is a good career. However, it is not the one I want. What’s more, medicine uses the part of the game I liked most: deciding fast, under pressure.
H
Okay, but that undermines your point a little, doesn’t it? If you like pressure, sport has plenty of it.
A
Granted, it does. Even so, in medicine the pressure is about somebody else, not about me. As a result, it feels like it matters more.
H
That is a real stance, and I cannot rebut it. Would you still say it at two in the morning, after a thirty-hour shift?
A
Ask me again in six years. But yes, I think I would stand by it. And you — was there ever a decision you had to defend like this?
Comprehension

About Hannah

1. What role does Hannah say she is going to play, and why does it matter?
Devil’s advocate. She announces that she will argue the other side on purpose, so the attack is not personal.
2. What other career does she suggest for somebody of thirty-five?
Coaching. She says plenty of people coach at thirty-five and that it is a career too.
3. Why does she say his point about pressure is weaker than it sounds?
Because sport has plenty of pressure too, so liking pressure is not a reason to leave it.
Listening 1 · A Debate Coach

The First Thing I Taught Them

A school debate coach on the move that wins arguments. Sound first — no text.

1. What did she teach every new student first?
Not how to attack, but how to agree.
2. Why does she say an argument that admits nothing is the weakest?
Because the judge can see the point you are hiding from.
3. What happened to the students who rushed the pause?
They sounded defensive. The ones who took their time sounded like they had already considered everything.
Listening 2 · A Radio Interview

It Depends on What You Mean by Fair

An admissions advisor on whether one exam can choose future doctors. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say a single exam does well?
It treats every candidate the same way. Nobody gets in because of who their parents know.
2. What is the problem he gives on the other hand?
The test rewards the students who could afford two years of preparation, so equal rules do not always produce equal chances.
3. What does he say about people who defend one system without admitting its weakness?
That they have not thought about it for very long.
