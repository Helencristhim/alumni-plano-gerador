Lesson 9 -- You Should, You Could, You Might

Conversation
"What is the best advice you have ever received?" — British Chat, BBC Learning English

Real people answering the exact question of this lesson. Every answer is a piece of advice, and most of them use a modal in the first five words.

Tip: pause after each person and repeat their advice out loud in your own words, changing the modal. Strong advice becomes a soft option, and you hear the difference immediately.

Listening
"What is your dream job?" — Real Easy English, BBC Learning English

Short, clear answers about work and study from people at different points in their lives. It is the vocabulary of applying, portfolios and choosing, without any pressure to have decided.

Tip: listen once, then answer the same question out loud. If your honest answer is that you do not know yet, say that in English — it is a full sentence and a real answer.

Grammar
"May, might and could" — 6 Minute Grammar, BBC Learning English

Six minutes on the two least certain modals of this lesson, with the difference between possibility and advice made explicit.

Tip: after the video, give three pieces of advice about your own course — one with should, one with could, one with might. Say them out loud and notice how the force changes.
