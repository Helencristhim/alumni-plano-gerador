You Should, You Could, You Might -- When Somebody Asks You

Giving advice and choosing how strong to sound. Key words: pack, carry-on, terminal, currency, apply, portfolio, networking, advice. Structure: should, could and might, with the verb in the base form.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. Four are about a trip, four about the year after this one.

To pack -- to put your things into a bag before a trip
"You should pack light. You always take too much."
Carry-on -- the small bag you take with you into the plane
"Put anything important in your carry-on, not in the big bag."
Terminal -- one of the buildings of an airport
"You could check the terminal again before you leave home."
Currency -- the money a country uses
"You should change a little currency before you land."
To apply -- to ask officially for a job, a course or a place
"She might apply for the internship next semester."
Portfolio -- the collection of work you show to prove what you can do
"You could put the last project in your portfolio."
Networking -- meeting people who may be useful to you later
"Half of that course is networking, and nobody says it out loud."
Advice -- what you tell somebody they should do
"That is the best advice anybody gave me."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

My friend flies abroad for the first time in January, so I sent her a list. She should check which terminal she is flying from on the morning of the flight, because terminals change and the ticket is not updated. She should keep her passport and her medicine in the carry-on, never in the big bag. She could change a little currency before she lands, but she should not change everything at the airport. And the queue at passport control might take an hour, so she should not plan anything for that first morning. That is the best advice anybody gave me.
Why is it "she should check" and not "she should to check"?
A Because after a modal the verb has no to and no -s
B Because check is an irregular verb
C Because the sentence is about the future
Why does the text use "might" for the queue and "should" for the passport?
A Because the passport is advice and the queue is only a possibility
B Because might is used for questions
C Because they mean exactly the same thing

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Should, Could and Might

GRAMMAR

All three are modals. The grammar is identical: modal + verb, with no to and no -s. What changes is the force.

shouldadvice — the strongestYou should pack light.
coulda soft optionYou could try the other terminal.
mightonly possible, not certainIt might be cheaper on a Tuesday.
negativesshould not · might notYou should not leave it in the big bag.
ready-made phrasestrong, but politeIf I were you, I would apply.

Careful 1: never should to, and never she shoulds or she should applies.

Careful 2: advice has no plural, exactly like information and luggage. It is two pieces of advice.

Stage 2: How Strong Is It?

Choose

Read each situation and choose the modal that fits.

You are certain this is the right thing to do. "You ___ keep your passport in your carry-on."
A should
B might
It is only one possible option among several. "You ___ try the other terminal."
A should
B could
You are not sure at all. "The queue ___ take an hour."
A should
B might

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
Your friend is nervous about immigration. Which answer is advice, not an order?
A "Go to the queue and answer."
B "You should write your three answers on your phone before you land."
C "You should to write your answers."
Which sentence is correct?
A She should applies before the deadline.
B She should apply before the deadline.
C She should to apply before the deadline.
You want to soften a strong opinion. What do you say?
A "You must apply."
B "If I were you, I would apply."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Somebody is travelling to the United States for the first time and is nervous about immigration. Record two minutes of advice: what they should do, what they could do, and what might happen at passport control. Use should at least three times, could twice and might twice. Do not write a script first — just speak.
