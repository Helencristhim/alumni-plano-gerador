Chapter 3: The Code

Should, Could, Might

Three words, three different amounts of certainty

Discover the Rule

What Is Different Between These Three?

You should book your flight early. Prices only go up.
You could try the other terminal. There is usually a shorter queue.
It might be cheaper if you fly on a Tuesday. I am not sure.
She should apply for the internship, and she might get it.
Click to reveal the rule
All three are modals, and the grammar could not be simpler: modal + verb, with no to and no -s. She should apply, never she should to apply and never she shoulds. What changes is the force. Should = advice, the strongest: this is what I think you must do. Could = a soft option: here is a possibility, decide for yourself. Might = uncertainty: maybe yes, maybe no, I do not know. Negatives: shouldn't, couldn't, might not. And a ready-made phrase worth having: If I were you, I would...

The Best Advice He Ever Got

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What is the advice about the carry-on?
Anything you cannot replace in twenty-four hours should be in the carry-on, not in the big bag.
2. What does he say about changing currency at the airport?
You should change a little before you travel, but not everything, because the airport rate is the worst one.
3. What does he say is the advice most people ignore?
Checking the terminal on the morning of the flight, because terminals change and the ticket is not updated.

Choosing How Hard to Push

Same advice, three different strengths

Listening 2

What She Tells First-Year Students

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What does she say students should start doing in their first year?
Keeping a portfolio, even of work they do not think is good enough.
2. What does she say about networking?
That it is not about being charming; it is about being remembered by two or three people who know what you do.

They wait until they are ready, and there is no such moment.
