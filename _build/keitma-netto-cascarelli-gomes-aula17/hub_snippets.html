Lesson 17 -- Pre-class

What Did They Actually Mean?

Relaying the questions, requests and instructions of auditors, directors and the BPO, so that the message arrives clear. Key words: request, advise, instruction, specify, ambiguous, clarification, interpret, misunderstanding, wonder, paraphrase. Structure: reported questions, instructions and requests (They asked whether the accruals had been reviewed.).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To request -- to ask for something in a formal way, often in writing
"The auditors requested the supporting documentation by Wednesday."
To advise -- to tell somebody what you think they should do, as a professional
"The director advised us not to send the slides before the call."
An instruction -- a sentence that tells somebody what to do and how to do it
"The instruction came from the controller, not from the auditors."
To specify -- to say exactly what you want or what you mean
"Could you specify which entity you mean?"
Ambiguous -- with more than one possible meaning, so it is not clear
"The question was ambiguous, so I asked him what he meant."
Clarification -- an explanation that makes something clear and easy to understand
"I sent a short email to ask for clarification."
To interpret -- to decide what something means
"How do you interpret this finding: a problem, or only a comment?"
A misunderstanding -- a situation where two people understand the same thing in different ways
"The delay was a misunderstanding, not a mistake."
To wonder -- to want to know something, or to ask yourself a question
"I wonder whether the BPO received the file."
To paraphrase -- to say what somebody said, but with different words
"Let me paraphrase your question to make sure I understood it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

In a shared service center, a question rarely travels in a straight line. An auditor asks a controller, the controller tells the team, and the team writes to the BPO. At every step, somebody has to relay the question — and that is where it changes.

When you relay a question, it is not a question anymore. A yes / no question gets if or whether: Have the accruals been reviewed? becomes They asked whether the accruals had been reviewed. A wh- question keeps the question word, but the verb goes after the subject, like in a normal sentence: Why is the variance so high? becomes She wanted to know why the variance was so high. There is no question mark at the end.

Requests and instructions are different. For a request, you use asked + person + to: The auditor asked us to send the file. For an instruction, you use told + person + (not) to: The director told us not to send the slides. If you relay a question as an instruction, you create a misunderstanding — and somebody may do three days of work for nothing.

1. According to the text, where does a question usually change?
A When the auditor writes it in the final audit report at the end of the year.
B In the ERP system.
C When somebody relays it from one person to the next.
2. Which sentence relays Why is the variance so high? correctly?
A She wanted to know why the variance was so high.
B She wanted to know why was the variance so high, and she asked me twice.

3. What is the risk of relaying a question as an instruction?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Questions, Instructions and Requests

GRAMMAR

A reported question is not a question anymore: no inversion, no question mark.

TypeThe patternExample
yes / no questionasked / wanted to know + if / whether + subject + verbThey asked whether the figures were final.
wh- questionquestion word + subject + verbShe asked what the issue was.
do / does / didthey disappearHe asked where we kept the files.
a requestask + person + to + verbThe auditor asked us to provide a breakdown.
an instructiontell + person + (not) to + verbShe told us not to send the slides.
