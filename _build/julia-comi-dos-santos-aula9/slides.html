The Frame

Which one is strongest?

Advice has a volume knob

must = no choice. should = my advice. could = one option. might = maybe. Same sentence, four different strengths — and after all four, the verb never changes.

The Frame

Turn the volume up or down

mustYou must have a valid passport. — no choice
shouldYou should apply three months before. — my advice
couldYou could contact them again. — one option
mightThey might answer tomorrow. — maybe, I do not know

After all four the verb never changes: no to, no s, no -ed. "She should apply", not "she should to applies".

Careful: in English must sounds like an order. When you are helping a friend, should is almost always the right one.

Because the appointment takes six weeks and January is close.

Listening 1

Advice from somebody who went

1. What does she say you must do first?
Book the visa appointment — it takes six weeks.
2. What is her advice about money?
You should not take much cash, and you could open a card that works abroad.
3. What does she warn you about?
The first week — you might feel lost, and that is normal.
Listening 2

At the consulate

1. What must you bring to the appointment?
Your passport, the printed form and the payment receipt.
2. What do they recommend, but not require?
Arriving thirty minutes early and bringing a copy of everything.
3. What happens if you arrive without one required document?
You lose the appointment and you have to book another one.
