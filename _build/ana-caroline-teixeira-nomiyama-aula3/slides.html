Chapter 3: The Code

Have To, Don’t Have To

Necessary, not necessary, not allowed

Grammar Discovery

Necessary or Not Necessary?

"We have to submit the evidence by Friday."

"The client needs to sign a power of attorney."

"We don’t have to pay a filing fee for this claim."

"You mustn’t sign the document before you meet the notary."

Look at the red words: they say that something is necessary. Now look at the blue ones. Sentence 3: is it a problem if we pay? No, it is only not necessary. Sentence 4: is it a problem if you sign before? Yes, it is not allowed. Same word must, very different meaning.

JobFormExample
Necessary (a rule)have to / has to + verbWe have to submit the evidence by Friday.
Necessary (neutral)need to / needs to + verbThe client needs to sign the power of attorney.
Necessary (strong, formal)must + verb, without toAll documents must have a certified translation.
Not necessarydon’t / doesn’t have to, don’t need toWe don’t have to pay a filing fee.
Not allowedmustn’t + verb (in the U.S., people often say can’t)You mustn’t miss the deadline.
The trapno to after must; and mustn’t is not the same as don’t have toNot “We must to sign.” Say We must sign.

In one line: have to / need to / must = necessary; don’t have to = not necessary; mustn’t = not allowed.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Documents at Real Speed

One call with Miami, three recordings, one checklist

Dialogue

The Documents Call with Miami

D
Hi, Ana. Our client wants to file a claim in the Florida case. Can you help me with the documents?
A
Of course, David. What do we have to submit?
D
First, the bank needs to sign a power of attorney. It has to be signed in front of a notary public in Brazil.
A
And then it needs an apostille, right? It’s a requirement for a Brazilian document in a U.S. court.
D
Exactly. And all the evidence in Portuguese has to have a certified translation.
A
Do we have to pay a filing fee?
D
No, we don’t have to pay anything for this claim. But we mustn’t miss the deadline: everything has to be in by Friday.
A
Then I’m making a checklist today, and I’ll send it to you tonight.

Listening 1 · A Notary Public

What a Notary Does

An American notary public explains what she has to do, and what she does not do. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she have to do when someone signs in front of her?
She has to check the person’s ID and watch the person sign.
2. What mustn’t people do before they come?
They mustn’t sign the document before they come.
3. What doesn’t she have to do?
She doesn’t have to read the whole contract, and she can’t give legal advice.
Listening 2 · A London Lawyer

Why Your Document Needs an Apostille

A British lawyer explains the apostille in one minute. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does a foreign court need an apostille?
It doesn’t know your notary, so it can’t be sure the signature is real.
2. What don’t you have to do anymore?
Go to an embassy.
3. Where do you have to get the apostille?
In the country where the document was made.
Listening 3 · A Paralegal

Half of My Job Is Checklists

A paralegal in Germany explains how she prepares documents for courts in other countries. Sound first — no text.

1. What is different from court to court?
The requirements: translations, filing fees, documents.
2. What are the three columns of her checklist?
What they have to send, what they don’t have to send, and the deadline.
3. Why doesn’t her boss laugh at her lists anymore?
Because a missing translation almost cost them a big case.
