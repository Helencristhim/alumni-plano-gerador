Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

Before We File -- Have To, Don’t Have To, Mustn’t

Preparing the documents for a claim in a U.S. court. Key words: a power of attorney, to sign, a certified translation, a notary public, an apostille, a filing fee, a requirement, to submit, evidence, a checklist. Structure: have to / need to / must x don’t have to x mustn’t.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A power of attorney -- a legal document that gives a person the right to act for someone else
"The bank signed a power of attorney, so our firm can act for it in Miami."
To sign -- to write your name on a document to show that you agree with it
"The director has to sign every page."
A certified translation -- a translation with a statement that it is complete and correct
"The court needs a certified translation of the contract."
A notary public -- an official who checks who you are and watches you sign a document
"We signed the documents in front of a notary public."
An apostille -- a certificate that makes a public document valid in another country
"Without an apostille, the U.S. court may not accept the document."
A filing fee -- the money that you pay to a court to file a document or start a case
"The filing fee for this motion is about three hundred dollars."
A requirement -- something that is necessary because a rule says so
"Every court has its own requirements for documents."
To submit -- to give a document to a court or an authority so that it can be considered
"We have to submit the claim by Friday."
Evidence -- documents or facts that show that something is true
"The contracts and the invoices are our main evidence."
A checklist -- a list of things that you have to do or check
"I always make a checklist before a filing."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Monday, David sends Ana a message: the bank has to file a claim in the Florida case, and everything must be ready by Friday. Ana opens a new checklist. First, the bank needs to sign a power of attorney in front of a notary public in Sao Paulo.

Then the power of attorney has to get an apostille, because it is a requirement for a Brazilian document in a U.S. court. All the evidence in Portuguese needs a certified translation. The good news? They don’t have to pay a filing fee for this claim.

On Thursday night, Ana checks the list one last time. She knows the rule: they mustn’t submit a document without its translation. On Friday morning, the claim goes to Miami.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Have To, Need To, Must x Don’t Have To, Mustn’t

GRAMMAR

Use have to, need to or must when something is necessary (must has no to after it). Use don’t have to or don’t need to when something is not necessary: you can do it if you want. Use mustn’t when something is not allowed. In American English, people often say can’t for this.

JobFormExample
Necessary (a rule)have to / has to + verbWe have to submit the evidence by Friday.
Necessary (neutral)need to / needs to + verbThe client needs to sign the power of attorney.
Necessary (strong, formal)must + verb, without toAll documents must have a certified translation.
Not necessarydon’t / doesn’t have to, don’t need toWe don’t have to pay a filing fee.
Not allowedmustn’t + verb (in the U.S., people often say can’t)You mustn’t miss the deadline.
The trapno to after must; and mustn’t is not the same as don’t have toNot “We must to sign.” Say We must sign.

Never: We must to submit... · She have to sign... · I don’t must... · Do we need pay...?
