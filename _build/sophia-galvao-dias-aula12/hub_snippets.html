Checkpoint: Have You Ever...?

Test week, so this one is short: about ten minutes. Eight words you needed in our classes — braid, mushroom, jam, sprinkles, condensed milk, still life, warm colours, screen time — and one small fix that changes everything: I have already done it, not I had already done it.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Eight words you needed in our classes and did not have. Listen to each one and read the example out loud.

Braid -- hair that is divided into three parts and crossed over and over
"I have never had a braid this long."
Mushroom -- a small soft food with a round top that grows in dark, wet places
"Have you ever had pasta with mushrooms?"
Jam -- a sweet food made with fruit and sugar that you put on bread
"I have tried sushi with strawberry jam. It was weird!"
Sprinkles -- tiny coloured pieces of sugar that you put on top of cakes
"We have put sprinkles on every cake we have made."
Condensed milk -- very thick, very sweet milk that comes in a can
"Have you ever eaten condensed milk with a spoon?"
Still life -- a painting of things that do not move, like fruit or a vase
"I have painted a still life at school this year."
Warm colours -- red, orange and yellow, the colours of fire and the sun
"I have used only warm colours in this drawing."
Screen time -- how many hours a day you spend looking at your phone
"My screen time has gone up this week."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. The words in bold are the ones that talk about your life up to now, and the ones that talk about a finished time.

Test week is almost over, and I am alive. I have already done maths and Italian. I have not done history yet, and it is the one I am worried about.

Yesterday my friend asked me: "Have you ever studied in a coffee shop?" I said no, so after school we went to the new coffee shop near the park. I have never eaten so much cake in one afternoon. We did not study very much.

My screen time has gone up this week, which is funny, because I have not watched a single series. It was all group chats about the tests.

"I have already done maths." When did she do it?
A Yesterday at nine o'clock: the sentence tells us.
B We do not know, and it does not matter: it is done now.
C She is doing it right now.
"After school we went to the new coffee shop." Why is there no have here?
A Because after school yesterday is a finished time.
B Because went never takes have.
C Because she did not like the coffee shop.
Somebody asks you "Have you ever studied in a coffee shop?" and the answer is no. What do you say?
A No, I hadn't.
B No, I didn't ever.
C No, I haven't.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Have, not had

When you talk about your life up to now, and you do not say when, use have / has + the third form of the verb.

  • I have already done it. · I have not done it yet. · Have you ever tried it?
  • Yes, I have. · No, I have not (haven't).

When you say WHEN, there is no have

  • I went there last July. · We ate it yesterday.

The third form

  • be → been · go → gone / been · eat → eaten · see → seen · make → made · try → tried

Two traps. I have went does not exist: it is I have been or I went. And ever and already do not go together: "Have you ever watched it?"
