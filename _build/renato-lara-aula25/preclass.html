Lesson 25 — Pre-class

Road Trip

Eight words for a road trip (road trip, pack, snacks, roof rack, sleeping bag, rest stop, carsick, split the cost) and the difference between the plan (going to) and a quick offer or promise: I'll bring the snacks!

Stage 1.1: On the Road

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes. "Split the cost" is one expression: learn it together.

Road trip — a long trip by car or van, with stops on the way
"It's a three-hour road trip."
Pack — to put your things in a bag before you travel
"Pack something warm."
Snacks — small things you eat between meals
"I'll bring the snacks."
Roof rack — bars on top of a car or van that carry boards and bags
"The boards go on the roof rack."
Sleeping bag — a warm bag you sleep in when you go camping
"Don't forget your sleeping bag."
Rest stop — a place on a long road where you stop to eat or rest
"We're going to stop at a rest stop."
Carsick — feeling sick because a car is moving
"I get carsick in the back seat."
Split the cost — to share the money you pay, so every person pays a part
"We're going to split the cost."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read Renato's message to his parents in Brazil. Then answer the questions.

Big news: the first round is in Margaret River, and it's a three-hour road trip! Here's the plan. Dana is going to drive the van, and we are going to leave at five on Saturday. We're going to stop for breakfast at a rest stop, and we're going to sleep at a campsite, so I need to pack a sleeping bag. I haven't got one, but Dave said, "No problem, I'll lend you mine." The campsite is eighty dollars, and we're going to split the cost. I get carsick, so Jess said, "You'll sit in the front." When she asked about food, I said, "I'll bring the snacks!" And Kai needs help with the roof rack, so I'll be at school early. Don't worry, I'll call you on Sunday night. I promise I won't forget!
"Dana is going to drive the van." Why "going to"?
A It's the plan. Dana decided it before the message.
B Because Dana is the coach, and coaches always use "going to" when they talk to the team.
"I'll bring the snacks!" Why "I'll"?
A Because snacks are food, and we always use "will" when we talk about food on a trip.
B Renato decides right now, and offers to help.
Which sentence is a promise?
A I won't forget!
B We're going to sleep at a campsite.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

The plan (going to) or a quick decision (will)?

Decided before: be going to + verb. Decided now — an offer, a promise, an answer to a problem: will ('ll) + verb. Negative: won't.

The plan

We're going to leave at five.
Dana is going to drive.
We're going to split the cost.

Decided now

I'll bring the snacks. (offer)
I won't be late. (promise)
It's cold. I'll take a hoodie. (decision)

Positive: I'll help you.  ·  Negative: I won't be late.  ·  Question: Will you help me?
Never: I will to bring.  ·  An offer is not "I bring": say I'll bring.
Your voice: "I'll" is one short sound (AIL). "Won't" rhymes with "don't".

Stage 5: Plan your trip

Reflection
You and two friends are planning a surf trip in Brazil this weekend. Record 30 seconds: say three things that are already the plan (We're going to...), and offer three things to your friends (I'll...). Check one thing before you send it: going to for the plan, I'll for what you decide now — and never "I will to".
