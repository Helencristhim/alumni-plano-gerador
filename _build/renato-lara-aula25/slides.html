Grammar Discovery

The plan, or a quick decision?

Dana is going to drive the van. (She decided last week.)
We are going to leave at five. (It's on the trip sheet.)
"Who's bringing food?" — "I'll bring the snacks!"
"I haven't got a sleeping bag." — "Don't worry, I'll ask Dave."

What do the orange words have in common? When did each person decide?

decided before: be going to + verb · decided now: I'll + verb

Use going to for a plan that already exists. Use will ('ll) when you decide in the moment: to offer help, to promise, or to answer a problem. Negative: won't.

The Code

Offer, promise, decide

I'll + verb

Offer: I'll carry your board.
Promise: I won't be late.
Decision now: It's cold. I'll take my hoodie.
Question: Will you help me?

Never

I will to bring the snacks.
will + verb, no "to": I'll bring
"Who's driving?" — "I drive!"
an offer needs will: I'll drive!
Dana going to drive.
don't forget be: Dana is going to drive

I'll = I will · won't = will not — in conversation, always the short form.

The Code

The plan, and the help

The coach tells you the plan. You see a problem, and you offer to help.

The plan

"We're going to leave at five."
"We're going to sleep at a campsite."
"The boards are going to go on the roof."

Your offer

"Okay. I'll set two alarms."
"I'll bring an extra sleeping bag."
"I'll help Kai with the roof rack."
Don't worry, I'll help you with the boards.

See a problem, offer help: the fastest way to be a good teammate.

Sound Check

Short and fast

I'll bring the snacks.

"I'll" is one sound: AIL

We're going to leave at five.

in fast speech, "going to" sounds like GONNA

I promise I won't be late.

"won't" rhymes with "don't" — not "want"
