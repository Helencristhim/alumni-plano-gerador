Could You Say That Again?

Somebody on the call speaks fast, with an accent you do not know, and you lose the next ten minutes. Today you learn what to say in the first five seconds, so you stay in the meeting.

Chapter 1: First Words

The Five Seconds

On a global call, not understanding is normal. Native speakers ask for repetition all the time. The problem is not the accent: it is staying quiet and trying to translate in your head.

Think of the last time you did not understand someone on a call. What did you do?

Chapter 3: The Code

Can You? Could You?

Polite requests that keep you in the meeting

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Questions Show?

"Could you repeat that, please?"

"Can you speak more slowly, please?"

"Could you spell that for me?"

"Can I ask a quick question?"

Look at the red words: can and could. Now look at the blue verbs after them. Do they have -s, -ing or to? No: the verb is always in the simple form.

JobFormExample
Ask someone to do somethingCan you + verb?Can you speak more slowly?
More polite, for a customer or a managerCould you + verb?Could you repeat that, please?
Ask for permissionCan I + verb?Can I ask a quick question?
Answer yesSure. / Of course. / No problem.Could you spell it? — Sure. N-I-N-G-B-O.
Make it softeradd please and a little / moreCould you speak a little more slowly, please?
The trapno to, no -ing after can and couldNot Could you to repeat. Say Could you repeat.

In one line: can or could + you + simple verb; could is softer, so use it with customers and managers.

Chapter 4: On the Call

The Call With Shanghai

One call, two voices, one chat

Dialogue

The Call With Shanghai

W
Hello, Fernanda. Sorry, I have bad news. The shipment of samples has a delay of five days.
F
Sorry, Wei, I didn’t catch that. Could you repeat that, please?
W
Of course. The shipment is late. Five days. The samples arrive on the twentieth, not on the fifteenth.
F
OK, thank you. Can you speak up a little? The connection is not very good.
W
Is this better? The problem is at the port in Ningbo.
F
Yes, much better. Could you spell the name of the port, please?
W
Sure. N-I-N-G-B-O. Ningbo. I can write it in the chat, too.
F
Great, thank you. So, just to confirm: the samples arrive on the twentieth?
W
Yes, that is right. I am sorry for the delay.
Comprehension

About Wei

1. How long is the delay?
Five days.
2. Where is the problem, according to Wei?
At the port in Ningbo.
3. What does Wei offer to do at the end?
To write the name of the port in the chat.
Listening 1 · The Voice Message

A Message From the Plant

A quality engineer at the plant in Shanghai leaves a voice message. Sound first — no text.

1. How many boxes of samples are in the new shipment?
Twelve boxes.
2. When can the customer test the samples?
Next Wednesday.
3. What does he ask Fernanda to do?
To confirm by email, and to send one question in writing if she has one.
Listening 2 · The Project Manager

Nobody Understands Everything

A project manager in India explains what he does when he does not understand someone. Sound first — no text.

1. How many countries are on his calls?
Six or seven.
2. What does he say when he does not catch something?
Sorry, could you repeat that, please?
3. What does he ask people to do with names and numbers?
To write them in the chat.
