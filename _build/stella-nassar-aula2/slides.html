Chapter 4: The Style Code

Praise That Is Precise, Not Loud

The adverb that calibrates the adjective

Discover the Style

What Is the Adverb Doing?

"I left the museum profoundly unsettled and entirely unable to say why."

"How could something so deceptively simple produce such a response?"

"He had grown acutely aware of how lighting altered his canvases."

"That is the quietly revolutionary promise of the discipline."

Remove each colored adverb. What does the sentence lose, and why is very never a good replacement?

FormWhat it doesExample
degree adverb + adjectivemeasures the intensity exactly instead of turning up the volumeprofoundly unsettled, acutely aware
contrastive adverb + adjectivebuilds a tension between how something looks and what it isdeceptively simple, quietly revolutionary
viewpoint adverb + adjectivenames the dimension in which the adjective is trueintellectually demanding, emotionally honest
fixed collocationssome adverbs only sit naturally with certain adjectivesbitterly disappointed, acutely aware, deeply moving
The C2 trapwrong partners and stacked intensifiers. "Highly aware" and "utterly interesting" sound translated; "really extremely important" sounds panicked. One precise pair beats three loud words.

Chapter 5: Voices Behind the Application

Who Reads Your Why?

A counselor, an admissions reader and a student who switched majors

Dialogue

The First Meeting with the College Counselor

D
So, Stella. Art history. Let me guess: art has always been your passion.
S
I was going to say exactly that, which is probably a bad sign.
D
It is the sentence I read more than any other. Every reader has seen it hundreds of times, so it tells them nothing about you.
S
Then what should I say? It is true, even if it is a cliche.
D
Tell me about one specific moment instead. Not your whole life with art. One afternoon.
S
Last year I saw a very small painting in a museum that was deceptively simple, and I could not stop thinking about it for weeks.
D
Good. Now, what did you actually see? Leave out the adjectives for a second.
S
A woman at a window, mostly in gray. The light came from a place I could not find in the picture.
D
That sentence is worth ten passions. When I applied to college, I wrote two pages about loving history and never mentioned a single document I had read.
S
So the essay is not about proving that I love art. It is about showing that I look.
Comprehension

About Mr. Brooks

1. Why does Mr. Brooks say the sentence about art being a passion does not help an applicant?
Because readers have seen it hundreds of times, so it tells them nothing about the applicant.
2. What does he ask Stella to describe instead, and what does he tell her to leave out?
One specific moment, one afternoon rather than her whole life with art, and he tells her to leave out the adjectives.
3. What mistake does he admit he made in his own college essay?
He wrote two pages about loving history and never mentioned a single document he had read.
Listening 1

What Admissions Readers Remember

An admissions officer talks to a room of applicants. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. How much experience does the speaker have, and how long does she say she spends on a first read of an essay?
She has read applications for eleven years, and she spends only a few minutes on a first read.
2. What does she say about essays that open with a famous quotation or a dictionary definition?
They borrow someone else's voice at the exact moment she wants to hear the applicant's, so she usually forgets them.
3. According to her, what makes an essay memorable, and what example does she give?
Specific, honest detail that only that applicant could write, like the student who wrote about reorganizing her grandmother's spice cabinet.
Listening 2

A Voice Memo from a Sophomore

A college student records advice for his younger sister. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. What did Marcus plan to study when he arrived at college, and why did he take an art history course?
Economics. He took the course only because it fit his schedule and satisfied a requirement.
2. What happened in the course that changed his plans?
His professor spent an entire class on one sculpture, and Marcus realized he had never looked at anything that carefully. He switched majors the next semester.
3. What advice does he give his sister about her essay?
Not to pretend she has always known what she wants. Readers trust a question more than a performance of certainty.
