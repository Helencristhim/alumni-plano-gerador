Lesson 18 -- Pre-class

The Story Behind the Object -- Who, Which, That, Whose, Where

A suitcase from Hamburg, a desk with a letter from 1916 and a clock on a market stall: relative clauses to tell the story of old things. Key words: an heirloom, a flea market, vintage, second-hand, craftsmanship, patina, to pass something down, sentimental value, to haggle, a diamond in the rough, worth its weight in gold, one man's trash is another man's treasure. Structure: defining and non-defining relative clauses with who, which, that, whose and where.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An heirloom -- a valuable object that a family keeps for many generations
"This ring is an heirloom. It was my great-grandmother’s."
A flea market -- an outdoor market that sells old and used things
"I found this mirror at a flea market in Hamburg."
Vintage -- old, but of good quality and still valued today
"She only wears vintage dresses from the 1960s."
Second-hand -- owned by someone else before you
"Most of our furniture is second-hand, and I love it."
Craftsmanship -- the skill of making things carefully and beautifully by hand
"Look at the craftsmanship in this drawer. There is not a single nail."
Patina -- the soft shine that an old surface gets with time and use
"Please do not polish the table. The patina is part of its story."
To pass something down -- to give something to a younger person in your family
"My grandmother passed the recipe book down to my mother."
Sentimental value -- importance that comes from memories, not from money
"The watch is not expensive, but it has a lot of sentimental value."
To haggle -- to discuss the price with the seller to pay less
"At a flea market, everybody haggles a little."
A diamond in the rough -- something with great hidden value that needs some work to shine
"The chair was dirty and broken, but it was a diamond in the rough."
Worth its weight in gold -- extremely useful or valuable
"A good carpenter is worth his weight in gold."
One man's trash is another man's treasure -- something one person throws away can be precious to someone else
"My neighbor threw the old door away, and I took it home. One man's trash is another man's treasure."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Greta lives in Hamburg, where there is a big flea market every Sunday. Three years ago she found an old suitcase that was covered with hotel labels. Inside, there was a name, and she found the woman whose grandmother had owned it. It was an heirloom, and Greta gave it back.

Tom restores furniture in York. One day he opened a desk drawer, which had been stuck for decades, and found a letter from a soldier who was writing to his mother in 1916. The desk was not expensive, but now it has enormous sentimental value.

Ana loves objects like these. For her, the patina on an old table is not a problem to fix. It is the story of the people who used it.

1. "She found the woman whose grandmother had owned it." Whose shows:
A possession — the woman’s grandmother
B a place where something happened
C a question
2. "He opened a desk drawer, which had been stuck for decades." The part between commas:
A tells you which drawer, out of many drawers in the workshop
B adds an extra detail
C is a mistake
3. Why does Ana not want to remove the patina?
A Because it is difficult and expensive to remove from old furniture.
B Because the table is new.
C It is part of the story of the people who used the table.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Relative Clauses

GRAMMAR

Who, which, that, whose, where — and the two commas that change the meaning.

FormWhat it doesExample
who / thatPeoplethe man who sold me the chair
which / thatThingsthe clock that my grandfather bought
whosePossessionthe woman whose table I restored
wherePlacesthe market where I found it
no commasDefining: tells you WHICH oneThe chair that I found is from 1940.
commasNon-defining: an extra detail, never thatMy clock, which is old, still works.

Never: The man which sold me the chair · my clock, that is old, still works · the woman who her table... · the market where I found it there. Who for people, whose for possession, and never that between commas.
