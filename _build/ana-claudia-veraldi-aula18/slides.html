Chapter 4: The Code

Which One, or One More Detail?

who · which · that · whose · where — and the two commas

Grammar Discovery

Five Small Words and Two Commas

"The clock that my grandfather bought still works."

"This is the man who sold me the chair."

"My grandmother's sewing machine, which is almost a hundred years old, still works."

"The woman whose table I restored sent me a photo."

Look at the colored words. In which sentences do they tell you WHICH clock, man or woman? In which one do they only add an extra detail? And what do the two commas do?

FormWhat it doesExample
who / thatFor people.the man who sold me the chair
which / thatFor things.the clock that my grandfather bought
whosePossession: his, her, their.the woman whose table I restored
whereFor places.the market where I found it
defining — no commasTells you WHICH one. Without it, the sentence is not clear.The chair that I found is from 1940.
non-defining — commasOnly extra information. Never that. In speech: a small pause.My clock, which is very old, still works.

In one line: No commas = which one (that is fine). Commas = one more detail (never that).

Dialogue

The Clock on the Stall

H
Good morning! That clock you are looking at is my favorite thing on the stall.
A
It is beautiful. Look at the patina on the wood. How old is it?
H
About a hundred years. It belonged to a family who lived in Bath. It was an heirloom.
A
Really? So why did they sell it?
H
The grandson, who lives in Canada now, had no space for it. He was very sad to let it go.
A
I understand. Some things have a sentimental value that money cannot pay. How much is it?
H
Eighty pounds. But it is the clock that everybody stops to look at.
A
In Brazil we always haggle a little at a market. Would you take sixty?
Comprehension

About Harriet

1. Where did the clock come from, according to Harriet?
From a family who lived in Bath. It was an heirloom.
2. Why did the family sell it?
The grandson, who lives in Canada now, had no space for it.
3. How much does Harriet want, and what does she say about the clock?
Eighty pounds. She says it is the clock that everybody stops to look at.
