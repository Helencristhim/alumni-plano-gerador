Stage 1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Eight words from the market you are moving into. In four weeks they will be ordinary.

carbon credit -- one tonne of carbon dioxide that was not released, sold as a unit
"The whole value of the project depends on the carbon credits it can sell."
offset mechanism -- the system that lets one company pay for a reduction made somewhere else
"An offset mechanism does not reduce your own emissions: it pays for somebody else's reduction."
reforestation -- planting trees again on land where the forest was cut down
"In this region the activity behind the credit is usually reforestation."
regulatory framework -- the whole set of rules that govern an activity in a country
"The regulatory framework for carbon markets here is still being written."
concession agreement -- a contract in which the state lets a company use public land or a public service
"It is a concession agreement with the state, and it runs for thirty years."
sustainability -- using land, water and people in a way that can continue for decades
"Sustainability is not about being green: it is about lasting."
to certify -- to check something against a standard and give it an official document
"An independent body has to certify that the tonne is real."
permit -- the official paper that allows an activity the law would otherwise forbid
"Without the environmental permit, the project cannot start."

Stage 3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about what it says.

Four weeks is not long, and yet something specific has changed. In the first lesson the answer to what do you do? was three short sentences. Now it can be one: "I am the lawyer who looks after contracts for an agribusiness group." Same information, one breath, and the listener does not have to wait for the third sentence to know who is speaking.

The other three structures did the same kind of work. I have never worked on a carbon project puts a whole career on the table without a single date. We have been reading the framework document all week explains why there is no answer yet, and it does it without sounding like an excuse. And if the permit came late, the project would lose a planting season shows a consequence that nobody wants, which is the only way to refuse an idea and keep the person.

None of this is advanced English. What is advanced is using the four in the same answer, one after the other, without stopping to choose. That is what a fluent professional sounds like: not somebody with rare vocabulary, but somebody whose sentences do different jobs.

And now there is a new subject to say it about. A carbon credit is a unit: one tonne of carbon dioxide that was not released. An offset mechanism is what lets a company pay for a reduction made somewhere else. The activity behind the credit is often reforestation, and the ground under it is often a concession agreement with the state. Somebody independent has to certify that the tonne is real, and no activity starts without the environmental permit.

The vocabulary is new. The structures are four weeks old. That combination is the whole point of a checkpoint.
