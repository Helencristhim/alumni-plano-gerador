Video
Stanford GSB -- Application Week: School Research, Fit, and "Why"

The admissions team of an American business school answering, on camera, exactly the question this lesson is built on: why this school and why now. This is not advice about the language. It is the people who will be on the other side of the table telling you what they listen for.

Tip: write down every phrase they use for the reason behind an application. Then rewrite your own first sentence using two of them.

NPR Life Kit -- "Find your side quest"

An episode about taking on something demanding that sits outside the job you already have, and about the honest arithmetic of where the hours come from. It is the same conversation you had in the second half of this lesson, in everyday spoken English.

Tip: listen for the reasons people give for starting now rather than later, and note two that are stronger than "I wanted to grow".

Talk
TED -- Ruth Chang, "How to make hard choices" (15 min)

A philosopher on why the decisions that keep us on the fence for years are hard for a reason, and what actually settles them. Fifteen minutes, careful delivery, and a useful vocabulary for the part of your answer that has to explain a two-year hesitation without apologizing for it.

Tip: after the talk, say out loud in one sentence what pushed you off the fence. That sentence is the one admissions is waiting for.
