Lesson 34 — Pre-class

The Night Before

Eight words for the night before a big day (scenery, sunset, tent, early night, restless, pep talk, visualize, alarm) and how to make a feeling stronger: so tired, such a long drive, such nice people.

Stage 1.1: The Night Before

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes. "Scenery" has no plural: never "sceneries".

Scenery — the natural things you see around you, like hills, trees and the sea
"The scenery on the drive south was so beautiful."
Sunset — the time in the evening when the sun goes down
"We arrived just in time for the sunset."
Tent — a small house made of cloth that you sleep in outside
"I am writing this in my tent at the campsite."
Early night — when you go to bed earlier than usual
"I need an early night before the semifinal."
Restless — not able to relax, stay still or sleep
"I feel so restless tonight."
Pep talk — a short talk to give someone energy and confidence
"Dana gave us a pep talk next to the van."
Visualize — to see a picture of something in your mind
"Close your eyes and visualize your first wave."
Alarm — a clock or phone sound that wakes you up
"My alarm is set for five thirty."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read Renato's message to his friend Lucas in Brazil, on Friday night. Then answer the questions.

Lucas, I'm at Margaret River! We drove here today in the club van. It was such a long drive, but the scenery was so beautiful: green hills, old trees and kangaroos next to the road! We arrived just in time for the sunset. It was such an amazing sunset. After dinner, Dana gave us a pep talk. She was so calm. The people at the campsite are such nice people. Now I'm in my tent, and I feel so restless. My alarm is set for 5:30, so I need an early night. Dana says: close your eyes and visualize your first wave. The semifinal is tomorrow!
How was the drive to Margaret River?
A Short and boring, because they took the fast highway and didn't stop at all.
B Long, but beautiful.
How does Renato feel in his tent?
A Restless. He can't relax.
B Very sleepy, so he is already asleep before nine o'clock.
"It was such an amazing sunset." Why "such an" and not "so"?
A Because "such" is for things that happen in the evening, and "so" is for the morning.
B Because there is a noun: sunset.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

So and such for emphasis

Both make a feeling stronger, like "very". Use so before an adjective with no noun. Use such when there is a noun.

so + adjective

I'm so tired.
The scenery was so beautiful.

such a / an + adjective + noun

It was such a long drive.
It was such an amazing sunset.

such + plural noun

They're such nice people.
We had such good waves.

Same idea, two ways: The drive was so long.  =  It was such a long drive.
Remember: never "so long drive" and never "such a nice people". One thing: such a / such an. Many things: such.
Your voice: "such a" is one sound: SUCH-uh. Make "so" long and strong: "I'm sooo tired!"

Stage 4: Friday night at the campsite

Quiz
Bev asks: "How was the drive?" It was very long.
A It was such a long drive.
B It was so long drive, but we stopped one time at a rest stop on the road.
Jess shows you a photo of the sunset.
A Wow! It was such amazing sunset over the water at the beach yesterday.
B Wow! What a sunset! It was so beautiful.
Kai asks: "Why are you still awake?"
A I'm so restless.
B I'm such restless because the semifinal is tomorrow morning at eight.
Tell Tom about the family from Busselton.
A They are such a nice people, and they gave us hot chocolate after dinner.
B They're such nice people.
Dana says: "Go to bed before ten." What does she want?
A She wants you to set your alarm for ten o'clock tomorrow morning, after breakfast.
B She wants you to have an early night.

Stage 5: My own big night

Reflection
Think of a big night in your life: the night before a test, a trip or a game. Record 30 seconds: where were you, how did you feel, and what was it like? Use "so" two times and "such" two times. Check one thing before you send it: after "such a", there is always a noun.
