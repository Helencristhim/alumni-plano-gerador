Chapter 4: The Code

How Long Have You Been Doing It?

A habit that started in the past and is still going

Grammar Discovery

Started in the Past, Still Going

"For the last three years, she has been waking up at five."

"Sleep scientists have been studying this for years."

"I’ve been going to bed at nine-thirty since I started."

"How long have you been working out like this?"

Did these activities start in the past? Yes. Are they finished? No, they are still happening. Look at the blue words: have or has, then been, then a verb with -ing. And the red words: for a period of time, since a starting point.

JobFormExample
Still happeninghave / has been + verb-ingShe has been waking up at five.
A period of timefor + a periodfor three years, for a long time
The starting pointsince + a moment or a datesince 2023, since I started
The questionHow long have you been + verb-ing?How long have you been working out?
Short formI’ve / she’s been + verb-ingI’ve been going to the gym.
The trapthe simple present does not say how longNot I go to the gym since 2020. Say I’ve been going.

In one line: started in the past and still going: have been + -ing, with for or since.

Listening 1 · A Personal Trainer

Training at Dawn

An Australian personal trainer talks about his clients who work out very early. Sound first — no text.

1. How long has he been training people at six in the morning?
For about ten years.
2. Why do many of his clients prefer the early hour?
Because after work they are too tired, and in the morning nothing can interrupt them.
3. What is his advice for beginners?
Start with three days a week, not seven, and be consistent.
Listening 2 · A Sleep Researcher

Sleep Comes First

A British sleep researcher explains what really matters for our energy. Sound first — no text.

1. What has his team been studying?
How sleep affects energy and mood at work.
2. What happens to people who sleep less than six hours, according to him?
They make more mistakes and feel more stressed, even when they think they are fine.
3. What is his main advice?
Go to bed and wake up at the same time every day.
Listening 3 · A Working Mom

My Hour Before Everyone

An American woman explains why she wakes up an hour before her family. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she do in her quiet hour?
She drinks coffee, walks for thirty minutes and reads a few pages.
2. How did she feel before she started this routine?
She was always in a hurry and she felt she never had time for herself.
3. What is the hardest part for her?
Going to bed early when there is a good series on TV.
