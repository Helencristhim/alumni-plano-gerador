Lesson 04 -- Pre-class

The 5 a.m. Club -- I’ve Been Going, Since, For

People who wake up before the sun, and how to talk about your routine and your energy. Key words: an early bird, a night owl, to work out, to stick to, worn out, sleep-deprived, to snooze, to recharge, consistent, willpower. Structure: the present perfect continuous.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An early bird -- a person who likes to wake up very early
"I’m an early bird: I love the quiet of six a.m."
A night owl -- a person who likes to stay awake until late at night
"My husband is a night owl. He reads until one."
To work out -- to do physical exercise, for example at the gym
"I work out four or five times a week."
To stick to -- to continue doing something you decided, even when it is hard
"It’s easy to start a routine, but hard to stick to it."
Worn out -- so tired that you have no energy left
"After a day in the office and two hours in traffic, I’m worn out."
Sleep-deprived -- not having enough sleep for a long time
"Many parents of small kids are sleep-deprived."
To snooze -- to press the button that makes the alarm ring again a few minutes later
"I never snooze. I get up at the first alarm."
To recharge -- to rest so that you have energy again
"I need a quiet weekend to recharge."
Consistent -- always doing something in the same way, again and again
"Being consistent is more important than training hard."
Willpower -- the strength of mind to do something difficult
"Waking up at five in winter takes a lot of willpower."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Mirela has been going to the gym almost every day for a long time. She is an early bird: on home office days, she works out early, helps her daughter get ready for school, and then starts work. She never snoozes.

The office days are different. She wakes up at five, drives to São Paulo and comes back at nine or ten at night. At the end of those days, she is worn out. It takes willpower to stick to the gym the next morning.

Her secret? “I’ve been trying to be consistent, not perfect,” she says. On weekends, a barbecue with the family helps her recharge.

1. Why does the text say “Mirela has been going to the gym”?
A Because she started in the past and she still goes now.
B Because she stopped going to the gym last year, after a long and busy period at work.
C Because she is going to the gym at this exact moment.
2. How does she feel at the end of an office day?
A Full of energy, because she likes to drive and she loves the city at night.
B Worn out.
C Sleep-deprived, because she snoozes every morning.
3. What helps her to recharge on weekends?
A Working out twice a day.
B Sleeping until noon on Saturday and Sunday, with no alarm clock and no plans at all.
C A barbecue with the family.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect Continuous

GRAMMAR

Use have / has been + verb-ing for an activity that started in the past and is still happening now. Use for with a period of time and since with a starting point.

JobFormExample
Still happeninghave / has been + verb-ingShe has been waking up at five.
A period of timefor + a periodfor three years, for a long time
The starting pointsince + a moment or a datesince 2023, since I started
The questionHow long have you been + verb-ing?How long have you been working out?
Short formI’ve / she’s been + verb-ingI’ve been going to the gym.
The trapthe simple present does not say how longNot I go to the gym since 2020. Say I’ve been going.

Never: I go to the gym since 2020 · I am working here for years · for 2023 · How long are you working here?

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A new colleague asks how long you have been at the company. You say:
A "I work here since many years, more or less since the company opened its first office."
B "I’ve been working here for many years."
C "I am working here for many years."
You want to know how long a friend has been running. You ask:
A "How long have you been running?"
B "How long are you running every morning before you go to work at the office?"
C "How long do you run since?"
You slept badly every night this week. You say:
A "I’m a bit sleep-deprived this week."
B "I’m a bit consistent this week, because I didn’t sleep well on any night."
C "I’m a bit willpower this week."
