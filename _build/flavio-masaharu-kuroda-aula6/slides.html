A requirements document almost never says who does the work. That is not carelessness — it is the point. The action matters, the person does not, and the whole document is written in the one structure that allows that.

Chapter 4: The Code

The Voice With No Person In It

Two words, and the person disappears

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"The data is sent to the ERP every night."

"All the changes are approved by the finance team."

"Access is granted only after the security review."

"Nothing is deleted without a written request."

Ask each one the same question: who does it? Three of them do not tell you. One of them does, and it needs an extra two words to say so.

JobFormExample
The action matters, the person does notis / are + past participleThe data is sent every night.
Say who, when it matters+ by + personapproved by the finance team
Singular and pluralis / are must agree with the subjectAccess is granted · Changes are approved

Irregular participlelearn it as a pairsend → sent · give → given · keep → kept

In one line: is or are plus the participle, and the person only appears if somebody decided to name them.

RESPONSE TO SECTION 4
one line per requirement · nothing left blank
4.1 Nightly transferSupported as standard
4.2 Data encrypted at restSupported, certificate attached
4.3 Two-hour maintenance windowAccepted as a constraint
4.4 Historic data migrationNot in scope — priced separately
4.5 Firewall rule changeDependency on your team
4.6 AssumptionSupplier files arrive in one format
Six lines and six different answers. Two are supported and cost nothing. One is accepted as a limit we will work inside. One is honestly excluded, which is worth more than a vague yes. One is a dependency that belongs to them, and it is the line that decides whether the date holds. The last one is an assumption: if supplier files arrive in three formats instead of one, the estimate is wrong, and this line is where we said so before anybody signed.
1. Line 4.4 says not in scope. Why does the note call that worth more than a vague yes?
Because a vague yes is agreed to at signature and argued about later, at your cost. An honest exclusion is priced separately and can still be bought.
2. Which line belongs to the customer, and what happens if nobody notices it?
4.5, the firewall rule change. It is a dependency on their team, and if nobody owns it the project waits, and the delay is blamed on the vendor.
3. Why is the assumption written down at all, if it is only an assumption?
Because an estimate is built on it. Writing it down means that if supplier files arrive in three formats, the conversation is about the assumption, not about the vendor being wrong.
Chapter 6: Your Turn

Two Recordings, Then You

Listening 1 · Three Passes

How He Reads Eleven Pages in One Hour

A solutions engineer on reading a requirements document. Sound first — no text.

1. What are the three passes he makes over the document?
First he looks for what is excluded. Then he marks every sentence with no person in it. Then he looks for dates and dependencies.
2. Why does he say the sentences with no person in them are the expensive ones?
Because a sentence that does not say who does the work will be assigned to whoever signed, and that is usually the vendor.
3. What does he say about the word standard in these documents?
That standard means standard for them, not for you, and that it is the word he asks about most often.
Listening 2 · What Slips

The Assumption Nobody Wrote Down

A project manager on why projects run late. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say is almost never the reason a project is late?
The work itself. She says the work is usually estimated correctly, and the delay comes from something nobody wrote down.
2. What example of a dependency does she give, and why is it dangerous?
A firewall rule change on the customer side. It takes twenty minutes of work and six weeks of waiting, because it is on nobody's list.
3. What does she say about the sentence the estimate was wrong?
That the estimate was not wrong, the assumption was, and that the difference matters because one is a mistake and the other is a conversation.

What I Can Do Now

I can read a requirements document and find the lines that do not say who does the work.

I know the words: a requirement, scope, to comply with, a constraint, an assumption, to validate, a milestone, to sign off, a dependency, an exception.
