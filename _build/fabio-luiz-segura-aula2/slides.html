Chapter 4: For and Since

Your Whole Career in One Sentence

Two small words decide whether the story is finished

Grammar Discovery

Two of These Close. Two Do Not.

"She has run the international desk since two thousand and fifteen."

"Last year she attended the same conference and arrived late."

"I have worked in trade finance for nine years, and I have just come from the panel."

"In two thousand and twenty-three we sent two people and booked nothing."

The red sentences name a finished time, and the story closes. The green ones name no finished time at all, because they are still true right now.

JobFormExample
How LONG something has lastedhas / have + past participle + for + a length of timeI have worked in trade finance for nine years.
WHEN something startedhas / have + past participle + since + a starting pointI have led this team since March.
The difference in one linefor = how long · since = from whenfor six years · since two thousand and nineteen
News, with the result still herejust · already · yetI have just come from the panel. I have not read the schedule yet.
Experience, with no dateever · never · a number of timesHave you been here before? I have been three times.
Finished, and the time is namedpast simple + a closed timeLast year I attended the same conference.
The trapsince never takes the present simpleNot I am in this role since 2019. Say I have been in this role since 2019.
The other trapa named time kills the present perfectNot Last year I have attended. Say Last year I attended.

In one line: if it is still true today, use have plus the participle — for how long, since from when. Name the year and the sentence closes.

Chapter 5: Three Accents

Three Strangers, Thirty Seconds Each

American, British, Indian — the room you are actually walking into

Listening 1 · American

Somebody Stops You on the Floor

Thirty seconds of somebody introducing himself, at full speed. Sound first — no text.

1. What is his name, his company and his role?
Marcus Feld, from Aperture Clearing in Chicago, and he runs the bank partnerships side.
2. How long has he been doing this, and in how many markets are they live?
He has spent the last four years talking to people like the ones in the hall, and they have been live in eleven markets since twenty twenty-two.
3. What does he say his board asks about, and where has he just come from?
His board asks about Brazil every quarter, and he has just come from the settlement panel.
Listening 2 · British

The Same Thirty Seconds, Different Vowels

Same task, second speaker. Sound first — no text.

1. What is her name, her company and her role?
Priya Sandhu, head of product at Levenford Rail, and they are based in London.
2. How long has she been in payments, and which side has she worked on?
About twelve years, mostly on the infrastructure side rather than the customer side.
3. Why is she at the conference, and what has she noticed this morning?
They have not launched in Latin America yet, and she has already had three conversations where people said the same thing about settlement times.
Listening 3 · Indian

Third Speaker, Same Three Questions

Same task, third accent. Sound first — no text.

1. What is his name, his company and his role?
Rohit Menon, from Cardinal Systems in Bangalore, and he looks after their correspondent banking product.
2. How many banks have they worked with, and how long has he been on that product?
About forty banks since the company started, and he has personally been on the product for six years.
3. What has changed at this conference, according to him?
The questions people ask. Two years ago everybody wanted to talk about cost, and now everybody wants to talk about speed.

The Email You Will Actually Get

To: Fabio Luiz Segura, Bradesco · Delegate reference SB-40182

Dear Fabio,

Thank you for registering. Your badge and lanyard can be collected from the registration desk in Hall A from seven o'clock on the morning of the first day. Please bring photo identification. The desk is busiest between eight and nine.

The opening keynote begins at nine o'clock sharp and the doors close five minutes before. The full session schedule is now live and can be filtered by track from your delegate account.

The networking floor opens at ten and stays open until six each day. Delegates arriving on an overnight flight may use the quiet room on level two.

Nobody reads an email like this from start to finish. You read it to find three things, and then you close it.
1. Where and from what time can you collect the badge, and what do you have to bring?
From the registration desk in Hall A, from seven in the morning, and you have to bring photo identification.
2. What happens five minutes before nine?
The doors of the opening keynote close.
3. You want the badge, a coffee, and a seat at the keynote. What time do you arrive, and why not eight?
Around seven or half past seven: the desk is busiest between eight and nine, and the keynote doors close at five to nine.

Rebuild

Put Her Method Back Together

Role-Play 1 of 3 — Guided

In the Queue, Before Anything Counts

Scenario: Seven in the morning, day one. You are in the queue at the registration desk next to somebody you have never met. Nothing is at stake. Start the conversation, keep it going for two minutes, and find out one useful thing before you both get your badges.

Keyword chips:

Is this your first time here? Where have you come in from? I have worked in trade finance for What have you seen so far?
Role-Play 2 of 3 — Semi-free

Too Fast, On Purpose

Scenario: The same queue, but this person talks fast and does not slow down. Your job is not to understand everything. Your job is to keep the conversation alive and get the three things: who they are, what they do, and why they came. Ask for anything you miss, out loud, as many times as you need.

Keyword chips:

Sorry, one more time? Which company was that? So you have been doing this for...? Let me make sure I got that.
Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

Ninety Seconds, No Notes

Scenario: Ninety seconds telling a stranger who you are, using time. How long you have worked in this field, how long you have led this team, what your bank has launched recently, what you have not done yet, and what you have just come from. Finish with a question back.

No keywords, no notes, ninety seconds.
