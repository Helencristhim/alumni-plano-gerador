Talk
Georgie Nightingall — “Talking to strangers: how to have a meaningful conversation” (TEDx)

Thirteen minutes on the exact move this lesson asked you to practise: turning a queue, a coffee line or a corridor into a real conversation with somebody you have never met. She is specific about which questions open people and which ones close them.

Tip: pick one of her questions and add it to the two opening lines you already have. Use it this week, on somebody in your own building, before you use it abroad.

Video
“Comparing Indian English with British, Australian & American English”

The same sentences, spoken four ways. In class you did this cold with three speakers; here you can do it slowly, with the text in front of you, and hear exactly which sounds move.

Tip: after each pair, close your eyes and play it again. The point is not to imitate any of them — it is to stop being surprised by them on a Tuesday morning.

Podcast
Fintech Insider — “How do we get to the holy grail of cross-border payments?”

British hosts, mixed guests, no simplification for a non-native listener, and your own subject from end to end. This is what a session at the conference sounds like when nobody is being polite about the pace.

Tip: give it the first ten minutes only. Write three phrases you understood completely and one you lost, and bring the one you lost to the next class.
