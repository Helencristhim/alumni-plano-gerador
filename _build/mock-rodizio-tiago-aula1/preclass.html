Before class 1: The glossary

Vocabulary

Tap Listen and say each word out loud. Eight words — you will need all of them in class.

Update — short news about how something is going
"Here is the update: both batches left on Monday."
On track — going as planned, at the expected speed
"We're on track for the 12th."
Behind schedule — later than planned
"The second batch is two days behind schedule."
Blocker — the one thing that is stopping the work from moving
"The blocker is the tooling supplier."
Lead time — the time between the order and the delivery
"Their lead time went from three weeks to five."
Batch — a quantity of products made or sent together
"The first batch left the factory on Monday."
Shipment — goods sent from one place to another
"The shipment arrives on the 14th."
Sign-off — the formal approval you need before the next step
"We start production the day we get your sign-off."

Before class 2: The reading

Input

Read it once, without a dictionary. In class we will work on it — you only need the general idea now.

Four hundred words nobody read

Sofia coordinates production at a metal parts factory in Joinville. Every Friday afternoon, for two years, she wrote a status report for her American client. It was thorough. It had numbers, dates, the name of every supplier, and it was usually four hundred words long. She was proud of it, and she never missed one.

In March the client called her, upset. A shipment had slipped by ten days and he had found out from his own warehouse, not from her. Sofia opened her sent folder and found the report from three weeks earlier. The delay was there. It was in the fourth paragraph, in the middle of a long sentence about tooling.

So the information had been sent, and the information had not arrived. Those are two different things, and she had been measuring the wrong one.

She changed the format that same week. Not four hundred words on Friday, but sixty seconds on Monday, on the phone, always in the same order: where we are, what is stuck, what I need from you, when it closes. If nothing was stuck, she said so, and the call took twenty seconds.

Nobody ever thanked her for the long reports. Four months into the short calls, the client asked her to explain the format to his other two suppliers.

Before class 3: Did you get it?

Quiz

Two quick questions. This is not a test — it just tells you whether to read it once more.

Why did the client find out about the delay from his warehouse?
A Sofia decided not to report it.
B It was in her report, buried inside a long paragraph.
What does Sofia do now when nothing is stuck?
A She still calls, and the call is very short.
B She skips the call that week.
