Lesson 23 -- Pre-class

The Condo Board

The annual meeting of Milton's condo association in the US: a special assessment, a closed pool, and an election. Key words: bylaws, quorum, proxy, nominate, ballot, amenity, contractor, reserve fund. Structure: mixed conditionals -- if the board had hired a better contractor, the pool would be open today.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Bylaws -- the official rules that an association or building must follow
"The bylaws say the board has five members."
Quorum -- the minimum number of people needed for a meeting to make official decisions
"We need fifty-one percent of the owners for a quorum."
Proxy -- a document that lets another person vote for you
"I can't come, so I sent a proxy."
Nominate -- to officially suggest a person for a position or an election
"Linda wants to nominate Milton for the board."
Ballot -- the paper or list that people use to vote in an election
"There are four names on the ballot this year."
Amenity -- something in a building that makes life easier or more pleasant, like a pool or a gym
"The pool is the amenity owners use the most."
Contractor -- a person or company paid to do a specific job, like a repair
"The contractor promised to finish the pool in May."
Reserve fund -- money a building saves for big repairs in the future
"Our reserve fund was too small to pay for the roof."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Milton owns an apartment in a building in the US. This year every owner is paying a special assessment for the new roof. If the building had saved more money in the past, the reserve fund would be healthy today. And the pool is still closed: if the board had checked the contractor's references, it would be open now.

Milton never went to the meetings, because he didn't feel ready in English. If he were less shy, he would have spoken up years ago. Tonight, Linda, the board president, wants to nominate him. If he had read the bylaws, he would feel more ready, but he says yes.

1. "If the board had checked the contractor's references, the pool would be open now." Is the pool open now?
A Yes, it opened last week after the new contractor finished the repairs on time.
B No. The board did not check, and the pool is still closed.
C Yes, but only on weekends.
2. In "If he were less shy, he would have spoken up years ago", which half is about the past?
A The if-part, because were is a past form that tells us when the action happened.
B Both halves.
C The result: he would have spoken up years ago.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A If he had read the bylaws, he would feel more ready.
B If he would have read the bylaws, he would feel much more ready tonight at the annual meeting.
C If he read the bylaws, he would have felt ready now.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Mixed Conditionals

GRAMMAR

The two halves of the sentence are in different times.

FormRuleExample
If + past perfect, would + verbpast cause → result nowIf the board had hired a better contractor, the pool would be open today.
If + past simple, would have + past participlepresent fact → result in the pastIf we had a reserve fund, we would not have needed the assessment.
Third conditional (Lesson 17)past → pastIf they had hired him, the pool would have opened in May.
Time signalsnow, today, still = present · last year, in May = pastI would know the rules now.
The trapno would in the if-partIf I had known (not If I would have known).

Look for the time signal in each half: it tells you which form to use.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are at a condo association meeting. Choose the best thing to say.

You want to say that a bad decision in May is the reason the pool is closed today. You say:
A "If the board had hired a better contractor, the pool would be open today."
B "If the board would hire a better contractor in May, the pool would have been open all summer and today."
C "If the board hires a better contractor, the pool was open."
You will be traveling on the day of the meeting. You tell Linda:
A "I will send a ballot to the front desk, so somebody can vote in my place at the meeting on Tuesday."
B "I will be a quorum."
C "I will sign a proxy and leave it at the front desk."
You want to speak during the discussion. You say:
A "Wait, I want to talk now, because this is important and nobody is listening to the owners."
B "If I may, I would like to add something."
C "Give me the word."
Linda asks if you want to be a candidate. You say:
A "Yes. Please nominate me."
B "Yes, please nominate me to the bylaws, and I will read the ballot before the meeting starts tonight."
C "Yes, I nominate."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Imagine you are at the next annual meeting of your building in the US. Take about ninety seconds. Introduce yourself to the owners, explain one problem the building has today and the past decision that caused it, and say what you would do if you were on the board. Use at least two mixed conditionals. Do not stop to correct yourself.
