Chapter 3: The Mixed Code

Yesterday's Decision, Today's Problem

had hired · would be · had · would have needed

Grammar Discovery

What Do the Orange Words Do?

"If I had read the bylaws, I would know the rules now."

"If the board had hired a better contractor, the pool would be open today."

"If we had a real reserve fund, we would not have needed a special assessment."

"If I were less busy, I would have run for the board last year."

Look at the orange words. Is the if-part about the past or the present? And the result? Are the two halves in the same time?

FormUseExample
If + past perfect, would + verbA past action (or no action) with a result NOW.If the board had hired a better contractor, the pool would be open today.
If + past simple, would have + past participleA situation that is always true and that changed the past.If we had a reserve fund, we would not have needed the assessment.
Compare: third conditionalPast with past (Lesson 17).If they had hired him, the pool would have opened in May.
Time signalsnow, today, still = present result · last year, in May = past resultI would know the rules now.
The trapNo would in the if-part.If I had known (not If I would have known).

Mixed conditionals connect yesterday's decision with today's result -- the language of every board meeting.

Chapter 4: The Lobby

Before the Meeting

The lobby, Tuesday, 6:50 p.m. Ten minutes before the annual meeting.

Dialogue

Milton Meets Linda

L
Milton! I'm so glad you're here. If you hadn't come, we wouldn't have a quorum tonight.
M
Really? How many owners do we need?
L
Fifty-one percent. Twelve people sent a proxy, so with you we have exactly enough.
M
Good. To be honest, I'm here because of the special assessment. If the board had planned better, we wouldn't be paying it now.
L
You're right. If we had a real reserve fund, we would have paid for the roof without asking the owners.
M
And the pool? It's been closed since May.
L
The contractor disappeared. If we had checked his references, the pool would be open today. It's the amenity everybody misses.
M
So what can I do? I don't want to only complain.
L
Run for the board. The election is tonight, and I'd like to nominate you. Your name could be on the ballot in ten minutes.
M
Me? Well, if I had read the bylaws, I'd feel more ready. But yes. Nominate me.
Comprehension

About Linda

1. Why is Linda glad that Milton came?
Because without him they would not have a quorum tonight.
2. According to Linda, why is the pool still closed?
The contractor disappeared. The board did not check his references.
3. What does Linda want to do at the meeting?
She wants to nominate Milton for the board, so his name is on the ballot.
