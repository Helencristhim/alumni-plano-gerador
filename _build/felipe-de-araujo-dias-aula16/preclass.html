Lesson 16 -- Pre-class

The City That Surprised Me -- Culture and Travel

The words of a trip: reputation, stereotype, neighborhood, crowded, local, to wander, breathtaking, hidden gem. Structure: defining relative clauses with who, which, that and where. Mission: describe a city that surprised you.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Reputation -- what most people think about a person or a place
"Naples has a reputation that is much worse than the city."
Stereotype -- a fixed, simple idea about a group or a place, and it is often wrong
"The stereotype says the city is dangerous and dirty."
Neighborhood -- an area of a city where people live
"We stayed in a neighborhood where only locals eat out."
Crowded -- full of people, with very little space
"The streets were crowded, noisy and full of scooters."
Local -- a person who lives in a place, not a tourist
"The best restaurant was one that a local showed us."
To wander -- to walk slowly around a place, with no fixed plan
"On Sunday we just wandered around the old city."
Breathtaking -- so beautiful that it surprises you
"The view of the bay from the hill was breathtaking."
Hidden gem -- a very good place that not many people know about
"The pizzeria was a hidden gem with six tables and no menu."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Naples was the city that surprised me most this year. We stayed in a neighborhood where people sit outside until midnight. On the first night we met a taxi driver who told us the history of every street. On Saturday we found a small pizzeria which has no menu, and on Sunday we took the funicular, a little train that goes up the hill.

Look at the small words in bold. Each one joins two ideas in one sentence and replaces a word: who replaces a person, which a thing, where a place. That can replace a person or a thing, and it is the most common when you speak. Because the relative word already does the job, you never repeat he, it or there after it.

1. Why does the text say a taxi driver who and not a taxi driver which?
A Because the taxi driver was the only man in the text who knew the history.
B Because a taxi driver is a person.
C Because who is more formal than which.
2. What does where replace in a neighborhood where people sit outside?
A A place: in that neighborhood.
B A person: the people who live there and sit outside every night of the week.
C A time: at midnight.
3. What is the funicular, according to the text?
A A small pizzeria near the old center of the city, which has no menu.
B A little train that goes up the hill.
C A taxi.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Defining Relative Clauses

GRAMMAR

Two ideas, one sentence. A small word joins them and tells you what you are talking about: a person, a thing or a place.

FormUse it forExample
whoPeople.a driver who told us the history
whichThings and animals.a pizzeria which has no menu
thatPeople or things. The most common when you speak.the city that surprised me
wherePlaces. It replaces there.the street where she grew up
No relative wordWhen the next part has its own subject.the hotel (that) we booked
No repetitionDo not repeat he, it or there.the street where she lived, not where she lived there

The most common mistake is which for people. A person always takes who or that: the driver who took us, never the driver which took us.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are telling a colleague about a trip. Choose the best thing to say.

You want to talk about the taxi driver. You say:
A We had a taxi driver which told us the history of every street in the city.
B We had a taxi driver who told us everything.
C We had a taxi driver who he told us everything.
You want to describe the area of your hotel. You say:
A We stayed in a neighborhood where the locals eat out.
B We stayed in a neighborhood where the locals eat out there every night of the week.
C We stayed in a neighborhood which the locals eat out.
You want to describe the pizzeria. You say:
A It was a small pizzeria who has no menu and only six tables near the church.
B It was a small pizzeria which has no menu.
C It was a small pizzeria where has no menu.
You want to say which city surprised you. You say:
A Naples was the city what surprised me most, more than Milan or Florence.
B Naples was the city where surprised me most.
C Naples was the city that surprised me most.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think about the city that surprised you most in your life, in Brazil or abroad. Record about ninety seconds. Start with its reputation: what people told you before you went. Then describe one person who you met there, one place where you ate or walked, and one thing which was different from what you expected. Finish with a hidden gem that you would recommend to a friend. Use who, which, that and where at least once each, and at least four words from this lesson. Do not stop to correct yourself.
