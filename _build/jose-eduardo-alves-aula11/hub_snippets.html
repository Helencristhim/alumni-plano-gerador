Connecting Ideas -- A Decision, Its Reason and Its Result

A decision explained with its reason and its result. Key words: a decision, a reason, a result, an advantage, a drawback, to choose, to delay, to justify, an argument, a paragraph. Structure: because, so, but, although and however.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A decision -- a choice you make after you think about it
"I made the decision on Monday, after a long call with the client."
A reason -- the thing that explains why you do something
"Give me one reason, and I can accept the delay."
A result -- what happens because of something else
"The result of the new clause was a lower fee."
An advantage -- a good point about one option
"The big advantage of Paranagua is the short queue."
A drawback -- a bad point about an option that you still like
"The only drawback of the evening class is the traffic."
To choose -- to take one option and not the others
"Students choose two seminars in the first week."
To delay -- to do something later than the plan
"The bank delayed the payment by three days."
To justify -- to give the reasons for a decision
"In the seminar, you justify every answer."
An argument -- a reason that you give to show you are right
"Her best argument was a number, not an opinion."
A paragraph -- a few sentences about one idea, together on the page
"Start every paragraph with the idea, then give the reason."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A list of facts is not an answer. In a seminar, the professor wants the facts connected: the decision, the reason and the result. Five small words do that work, and you already know all of them.

Because gives the reason: "The bank delayed the payment because one document was missing." So gives the result: "One document was missing, so the bank delayed the payment." Same two facts, two directions: because looks back, so looks forward.

The other three words keep a decision standing next to a problem. But is the simple one, in the middle: "The evening class is late, but I can go." Although does the same job inside one sentence, at the start or in the middle: "Although the evening class is late, I can go." However starts a NEW sentence, with a comma after it: "The evening class is late. However, I can go."

One trap for Portuguese speakers. In your language, the two contrast words can live together in one sentence. In English, although and but never live in the same sentence. Choose one.

1. Which word gives the RESULT?
A Because
B So
C Although
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "Although the class is late, but I can go."
B "However the class is late, I can go."
C "Although the class is late, I can go."
3. Where does however go?
A At the start of a new sentence, with a comma after it.
B In the middle of one sentence, with no comma.
C At the end of the sentence.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Because, So, But, Although, However

GRAMMAR

Because looks back to the reason. So looks forward to the result.

JobFormExample
Reasonbecause + subject + verbThe bank delayed it because a document was missing.
Resultso + subject + verbA document was missing, so the bank delayed it.
Simple contrastbut, in the middleThe class is late, but I can go.
Contrast in one sentencealthough + subject + verbAlthough the class is late, I can go.
Contrast in a new sentenceHowever + commaThe class is late. However, I can go.
The trapnever although ... but together — choose one

Never: although the class is late, but I can go · however the class is late, I can go · because of the price was low

Stage 2: Put the Decision in Order

Order

A client explains a decision about two ports. Put the sentences in a logical order: options, problem, decision, result.

?"Santos was cheaper. However, the queue there was eleven days."
?"We had two options for the March cargo: Santos or Paranagua."
?"Next time I want to justify the decision in one paragraph, with the numbers."
?"The result was simple: the ship left on time."
?"Although Paranagua is smaller, it is faster."
?"Eleven days cost more than the freight, so we chose Paranagua."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A colleague asks why you chose the evening group. You say:
A "I chose it, so it starts later."
B "I chose it although it starts later."
C "I chose it because it starts later."
The price was low, and you want to say what you did next. You say:
A "The price was low, because we waited."
B "The price was low, so we waited."
C "Although the price was low, but we waited."
You want to say a problem, then keep your decision, in two sentences. You say:
A "The fee is high. However, I accept it."
B "The fee is high, however I accept."
C "However the fee is high, I accept it."
You want one sentence with a problem and a decision. You say:
A "Although the trip is long, but I am going to go."
B "The trip is long, although. I am going to go."
C "Although the trip is long, I am going to go."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Think of one decision you made at work this year. Say what you decided, the reason, the result, and one drawback. Use because, so and although or however. Ninety seconds.
