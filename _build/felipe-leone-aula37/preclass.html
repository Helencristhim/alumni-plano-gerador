Lesson 37 -- Pre-class

Planning the Next Trip -- Plans, Wishes and Deadlines

Ten to twenty minutes before class. How to talk about plans (going to), wishes (would like to) and deadlines (in, within, over the next). Key words: a flight, a booking, an itinerary, to book, a layover, a deadline.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A flight -- a trip on a plane
"My flight to Atlanta leaves at ten at night."
To book -- to reserve a seat, a room or a table before you go
"I booked the hotel near the office."
An itinerary -- a plan of a trip, with the places and the times
"The travel team sent me the itinerary this morning."
A layover -- a short stop between two flights
"There is a three-hour layover in Miami."
Within -- before the end of a period of time
"Please confirm the booking within two days."
Over the next -- during the period of time that is starting now
"Over the next few weeks, I'm going to visit three clients."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Carla works in credit risk in Sao Paulo. Her plans for next month are clear: “I'm going to fly to Chicago in April. I'm going to stay for five days and meet two clients.” These are decided plans.

She also has wishes: “I'd like to visit the new office, and I'd like to present our results in English within a year. Over the next few weeks, I'm going to practice every day.”

"I'm going to fly to Chicago in April." What does it mean?
A It is only a wish.
B She flew to Chicago last April.
C It is a plan that Carla has already decided.
"Within a year" means:
A exactly one year from today.
B before one year ends, at the latest.
C every year.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Plan, Wish, Deadline

GRAMMAR

Decided plan: going to. Wish: would like to. Time: in, within, over the next.

JobFormExample
Decided planbe going to + verbI'm going to travel in March.
Wishwould like to + verbI'd like to speak more fluently within a year.
Deadline, at the latestwithin + periodPlease reply within two days.
During the coming periodover the next + periodOver the next few months, we're going to grow.

Never: I go to travel in March · I would like travel · I'd like to speak more fluent

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

Two colleagues talk about a business trip. Put their conversation in a natural order.

?"I'm going to fly there in May."
?"I'd like to, but I need to confirm within a week."
?"So, when are you going to Atlanta?"
?"Great. Let me know over the next few days."
?"Are you going to visit the new office?"

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen, then record yourself. Going to often sounds like gonna; I would sounds like I’d.

I'm going to fly to Chicago next month.
I'd like to visit the new office.
We're going to finish within two weeks.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You have already booked a flight for June. You say:
A "I'd like to travel in June."
B "I'm going to travel in June."
C "I go to travel in June."
You want to visit Atlanta one day, but there is no plan. You say:
A "I would like visit Atlanta."
B "I'm going to visit Atlanta tomorrow."
C "I'd like to visit Atlanta one day."
The client must answer before Friday at the latest. You say:
A "Please answer within the next three days."
B "Please answer in Friday."
C "Please answer over the next Friday."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Record a one-minute message about your next trip: where you are going to go, when, what you would like to do there, and one thing you need to finish within the next two weeks.
