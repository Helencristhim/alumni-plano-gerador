The Bracelet Stall

About ten minutes. Eight stall words — stall, beads, thread, knot, handmade, browse, customer, pattern — and the verbs that tell what you saw and heard: I saw her buy it or I saw her looking.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud.

Stall -- a small table where you sell things at a fair
"I saw a lot of people stopping at our stall."
Beads -- small colored balls with a hole, for making bracelets
"I watched her choose the beads one by one."
Thread -- a long, thin string you use to make bracelets
"I heard the thread snap in the middle of the knot!"
Knot -- the place where two pieces of thread are tied together
"Can you see me tying the last knot?"
Handmade -- made by a person, not by a machine
"Every bracelet on our table is handmade."
Browse -- to look at things slowly, with no plan to buy
"I noticed a boy browsing for ten minutes."
Customer -- a person who buys something from you
"I saw our first customer come back for another one."
Pattern -- a design that repeats, like stripes or zigzags
"I watched her copy the pattern from a video."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read Sophia's notes from the school fair. The words in bold tell what she saw and heard.

10:15 — I heard the bell ring, and the fair started. I saw a lot of people walking in at the same time.

11:30 — I watched a little girl choose her colors, one by one. Then she put the bracelet on and smiled.

4:00 — At the end of the day, I heard two boys talking about our stall. They said it was the best one at the fair!

"I watched a little girl choose her colors." Did Sophia see the whole choice?
A Yes, from start to finish.
B No, she only saw a small part of it, in the middle.
C She did not see it. She only heard about it.
"I saw a lot of people walking in." What does the -ing show?
A It shows that the action happened on a different day, not at the fair.
B The action was in progress when she saw it.
C Sophia was walking too.
Why is it "I heard the bell ring" and not "I heard the bell to ring"?
A Because ring is an irregular verb, so it never takes to after it.
B Because it happened a long time ago.
C After hear + a person or thing, the verb comes with no to.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

The whole thing, or only a part?

  • see / watch / hear / notice + person + base form = the whole action, start to finish: I saw her buy three bracelets.
  • see / watch / hear / notice + person + -ing = a part of the action, in progress: I saw her looking at the beads.

The person

  • Use me, you, him, her, us, them: I watched them make it. There is no to before the verb.

Remember: your life up to now is still have: Have you ever watched someone make a bracelet?
