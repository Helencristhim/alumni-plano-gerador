Chapter 3: The Code

He Transferred... He Had Transferred...

What happened first, and what happened later

Grammar Discovery

Which Action Came First?

"When we filed the lawsuit, he had already transferred the house."

"The bank found out that the couple had signed the divorce two weeks before."

"By the time the judge froze his accounts, he had moved the money to Florida."

"She said she hadn’t seen him since the divorce."

Look at the blue verbs: a moment in the past. Now look at the red verbs: they happened BEFORE that moment. Red = had + past participle. Blue = the simple past.

JobFormExample
The earlier pasthad + past participle (the same for I, you, he, she, we, they)He had transferred the house.
The later pastsimple past...when we filed the lawsuit.
Signal wordsalready, before, by the time, when, after, never ... beforeBy the time we filed, he had already left.
Negativehadn’t + past participleShe hadn’t seen him since the divorce.
QuestionHad + subject + past participle?Had he transferred the house before the lawsuit?
The trapif the order is clear (one action after the other), the simple past is enoughHe signed the divorce and then transferred the house.

In one line: two past actions: the one that happened first = had + past participle.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Case at Real Speed

One call with Miami, three recordings, one property record

Dialogue

A Call About the Florida House

D
Ana, I have news about the Harris case. We checked the property records in Florida this morning.
A
Great. Is the house still in his name?
D
No. He had transferred it to his ex-spouse three weeks before we filed the lawsuit.
A
Three weeks? That’s a red flag. Was it part of a divorce settlement?
D
Yes. She kept the house, the car and two bank accounts. He kept all the debts.
A
So he had planned it. He wanted to hide the assets from the creditors.
D
Maybe. But first we need to investigate. Some divorces are real, even when the timing looks bad.
A
Agreed. If it was fraud, we can ask the court to void the transfer. I’ll prepare a timeline today.
Comprehension

About David

1. What did David’s team check this morning?
The property records in Florida.
2. When had the debtor transferred the house, according to David?
Three weeks before they filed the lawsuit.
3. Why does David want to investigate before accusing anyone?
Because some divorces are real, even when the timing looks bad.

The Yacht That Changed Owners

A lawyer in London remembers a case where the assets had disappeared before the trial. Sound first — no text.

1. What had the businessman done before the trial started?
He had transferred his yacht and his apartment to his brother.
2. How much did the brother pay?

Listening 2 · A Forensic Accountant

Three Red Flags

An accountant from India explains what she looks for when she investigates a debtor. Sound first — no text.

1. What is her first red flag?
A transfer to a family member, just before a lawsuit.
2. What is the second one?
A very low price, like ten dollars for a house.
3. What did she find in one case?
The debtor had opened a new bank account in his mother’s name.
Listening 3 · A Family Lawyer

Not Every Divorce Is a Trick

A family lawyer from Australia explains why a divorce settlement is not always fraud. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say about most divorces?
Most of them are real: people separate for real reasons.
2. What had happened in his client’s case?
She had separated from her husband two years before his company had problems.
3. What helped to show that the divorce was real?
Messages, a new apartment and witnesses.
