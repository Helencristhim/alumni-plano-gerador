The House in Her Name -- He Had Already Transferred It

A debtor, a divorce and a suspicious transfer. Key words: fraud, to transfer, a property, a divorce settlement, an ex-spouse, to hide, a bank account, a red flag, to investigate, to void. Structure: the past perfect (had + past participle).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Fraud -- a dishonest action to get money or to cause a loss to someone
"The court said the transfer was fraud against the creditors."
To transfer -- to move money or property from one person to another
"He transferred the house to his ex-wife for ten dollars."
A property -- a house, an apartment or a piece of land that someone owns
"The property is in Miami, but the debtor lives in Sao Paulo."
A divorce settlement -- the agreement that decides what each person keeps after a divorce
"In the divorce settlement, she kept the house and the car."
An ex-spouse -- the person you were married to before
"All the assets are now in the name of his ex-spouse."
To hide -- to put something where other people cannot find it
"He wanted to hide his money from the bank."
A bank account -- an arrangement with a bank to keep your money there
"We found a bank account in Florida with two hundred thousand dollars."
A red flag -- a sign that something is probably wrong
"A transfer for ten dollars is a big red flag."
To investigate -- to try to discover the facts about something
"We need to investigate every transfer from the last two years."
To void -- to say officially that something has no legal effect
"We asked the court to void the transfer of the house."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last month, a bank asked Ana’s firm to recover an outstanding debt of one million dollars. The debtor, Mr. Harris, lived in Florida. When the team looked for his property, they found out that he had transferred his house to his ex-spouse a few weeks before.

The couple had signed a divorce settlement in April. She kept the house and two bank accounts, and he kept the debts. The price of the house in the transfer was ten dollars. For Ana, that was a big red flag: maybe he had planned everything to hide his assets.

But Ana investigated before she accused anyone. If the divorce was fraud, the firm can ask the court to void the transfer. If it was real, the house stays with the ex-wife.

1. Why does the text say “he had transferred his house” and not “he transferred his house”?
A Because the transfer happened before the team looked for his property.
B Because the transfer is still happening now, and had is the form we use for actions in progress.
C Because transfer is a verb of state.
2. What did the ex-spouse keep in the divorce settlement?
A The debts and the car, because the house was too expensive for her to keep after the divorce.
B The house and two bank accounts.
C Nothing.
3. What did Ana do before she accused anyone?
A She asked the court to void the transfer and to give the house back to the bank in Florida.
B She called the ex-spouse.
C She investigated the facts.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Past Perfect

GRAMMAR

When you tell a story in the past and one action happened BEFORE another, use had + past participle for the earlier action, and the simple past for the later one. Already, before and by the time often go with it. If the order is already clear (and then...), the simple past is enough.

JobFormExample
The earlier pasthad + past participle (the same for I, you, he, she, we, they)He had transferred the house.
The later pastsimple past...when we filed the lawsuit.
Signal wordsalready, before, by the time, when, after, never ... beforeBy the time we filed, he had already left.
Negativehadn’t + past participleShe hadn’t seen him since the divorce.
QuestionHad + subject + past participle?Had he transferred the house before the lawsuit?
The trapif the order is clear (one action after the other), the simple past is enoughHe signed the divorce and then transferred the house.

Never: He had transfer... · Did he had moved...? · When we filed, he already transferred... · The money has disappeared (in a past story)
