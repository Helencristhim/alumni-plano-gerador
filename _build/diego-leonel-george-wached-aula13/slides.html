Chapter 3: The Code

Has Had or Has Been Having?

Present perfect, simple and continuous

Grammar Discovery

Red or Blue?

"He has had the lesion for six months."

"He has been applying an ointment for three weeks."

"The lesion has bled twice."

"It has been growing since April."

Look at the red verbs, then at the blue ones. Which ones are about an activity that is still going on? Which ones count, or describe a state?

JobFormExample
A state that started in the past and is still truehas / have + past participle (have, be, know, own)He has had the lesion for six months.
How many times, or a finished resulthas / have + past participleIt has bled twice. We have done two biopsies.
An activity still going on, with for / sincehas / have + been + -ingHe has been applying a cream for three weeks.
A visible effect of a recent activityhas / have + been + -ingHer hands are red because she has been washing them all day.
The question about durationHow long have you + past participle / been + -ing?How long have you had this rash?

In one line: the continuous tells you the activity is still going on; the simple counts, gives a result, or describes a state — and have, be and know never go into the continuous.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Case, at Full Speed

One attending, two recordings, one vignette

Dialogue

Presenting to the Attending

P
Okay, Diego, present the vignette to me. Short version.
D
A fifty-eight-year-old farmer presents with a nonhealing lesion on his nose. It has been growing for six months.
P
Has he tried anything?
D
He has been applying an antibiotic ointment for three weeks, but the lesion has not resolved. It has bled twice.
P
Any pertinent negatives?
D
No pain, no fever, no weight loss. His past medical history is unremarkable, but he has had two lesions removed from his arms.
P
Good. So what is on your differential?
D
Basal cell carcinoma first. Squamous cell carcinoma is possible, because he has worked outdoors for forty years.
P
And the next best step?
D
A shave biopsy. We cannot narrow it down without tissue.
Comprehension

About Dr. Parker

1. What does Dr. Parker ask Diego to do first?
To present the vignette to her, in a short version.
2. What does she want to know right after the timeline?
Whether the patient has tried anything for the lesion.
3. What are the last two things she asks for?
His differential, and the next best step.
Listening 1 · A Step 3 Tutor

How to Read a Vignette

A tutor on the order in which she reads every case. Sound first — no text.

1. Which part of the vignette does she tell you to read first, and why?
The last line, because it tells you the task: the most likely diagnosis or the next best step.
2. What example does she give of two different patients?
A man who has had a rash for two years, and a man who has been scratching for two days.
3. Why does she say to read the pertinent negatives slowly?
Because they narrow down the differential more than half of the positive findings.

Question 14 of 40

PRACTICE BLOCK · QUESTION 14 OF 40
timed mode · candidate: Diego Leonel George Wached

A 58-year-old man comes to the clinic because of a nonhealing lesion on his nose. The lesion has been growing slowly for six months and has bled twice after minor trauma. He has been applying an over-the-counter antibiotic ointment for three weeks without improvement. He has worked as a farmer for 40 years and has had two lesions removed from his forearms. He denies pain, fever and weight loss. Past medical history is otherwise unremarkable. Examination shows a 1-cm pearly papule with rolled borders and small surface blood vessels.

Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

A. Reassurance and follow-up in one year  ·  B. Shave biopsy  ·  C. Topical corticosteroid  ·  D. Cryotherapy without biopsy

Teaching vignette written for this lesson in the style of USMLE practice questions. It is not from a real question bank.
1. How long has the lesion been growing, and what has it done twice?
It has been growing for six months, and it has bled twice.
2. Which two details in the history increase the risk?
He has worked outdoors as a farmer for 40 years, and he has had two lesions removed before.
3. Which option is the next best step, and why?
B, a shave biopsy: you cannot confirm the diagnosis without tissue.
