Podcast
Morning Edition (NPR) -- "Groopman: The Doctor's In, But Is He Listening?"

Jerome Groopman, the author of How Doctors Think, explains why most diagnostic errors come from thinking, not from technology, and why doctors anchor on the first idea. It is the reasoning behind every vignette, in a short interview at natural American speed.

Tip: listen for the question he tells patients to ask, What else could it be? Then say it about the case from this lesson, with one present perfect.

Abraham Verghese -- "A doctor's touch" (TED)

A physician and writer on the physical exam, and on what doctors miss when they read the chart instead of examining the patient. Clear, slow, beautifully told medical English, with interactive subtitles.

Tip: watch once with English subtitles, then a second time without. Write down every sentence with has or have + past participle.

Talk
Kathryn Schulz -- "On being wrong" (TED)

Why it feels exactly the same to be right and to be wrong, until somebody shows you the evidence. It is the attitude of the resident in Listening 2: a normal result does not end the story.

Tip: pause after each example she gives and say it as a case: who, what has happened, and what has changed.
