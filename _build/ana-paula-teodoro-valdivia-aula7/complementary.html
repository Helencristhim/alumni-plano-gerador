Lesson 7 -- Defining What Matters

Series
Explained (Vox) -- any episode, 18 minutes each

Every episode takes a subject the audience knows nothing about -- cryptocurrency, water, credit -- and makes it clear without making it childish. Watch the narrator's sentences: the technical word almost never arrives alone, it arrives with a short clause that says what it means, and the episode never stops moving.

Tip: watch with English subtitles and write down three sentences where a term is defined inside the sentence. Then rewrite one of them with a legal term of yours.

Podcast
New Solo (Legal Talk Network) -- "Here's to Plain Speaking and Clear Understanding"

Two American lawyers on why clients stop listening: the jargon that makes us sound expensive and leaves the other person guessing. It is the same problem you worked on today, discussed by people who live it, in natural professional English.

Tip: listen once for the argument, then once with a pen. Every time they use a legal term with a non-lawyer, note how they define it -- and how few words it takes.

YouTube
BBC Learning English -- "Using defining and non-defining relative clauses"

A short masterclass on exactly the structure of this lesson: when the clause tells you which one, when it only adds information, and what the two commas are doing there. Watch it after the lesson, not before -- it will confirm what you already worked out.

Tip: pause after each example and say the same sentence with one of today's words. If you can add the commas and keep going, the habit is starting to stick.
