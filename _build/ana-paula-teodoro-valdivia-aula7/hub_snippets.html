Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

Defining What Matters -- Explaining Complex Concepts to Non-Lawyers

The manager is not a lawyer: say the term and its meaning in the same breath and he stays with you. Key words: force majeure, boilerplate, subsidiary, affiliate, assignment, novation, annex, severability, recital, milestone, deliverable, undertaking. Structure: defining and non-defining relative clauses -- that, who and which, and the two commas that change what the sentence means.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the definition. You already read these words every week -- the goal now is to say what each one means, out loud, in one line.

Force majeure -- an event nobody can control that stops a party from performing
"Force majeure covers a flood, not a bad quarter."
Boilerplate -- the standard clauses that appear in almost every contract
"Nobody reads the boilerplate until something goes wrong."
Subsidiary -- a company owned and controlled by another company
"The Mexican subsidiary is not a party to this contract."
Affiliate -- a company in the same group, without direct ownership
"An affiliate is in the family, but nobody owns it."
Assignment -- passing your rights under a contract to somebody else
"Assignment needs the other side's consent."
Novation -- replacing one party with a new one, with everyone's consent
"A novation moves the obligations too, so everybody signs."
Annex -- a document attached at the end of the contract
"The prices are in the annex, not in the body."
Severability -- the rule that keeps the rest valid if one clause fails
"Severability is why one bad clause does not kill the deal."
Recital -- the opening part that explains why the parties are signing
"The recital tells you what the parties thought they were doing."
Milestone -- a point in the project that has to be reached by a date
"The second milestone is the pilot, in March."
Deliverable -- the concrete thing a party has to hand over
"The deliverable is the report, not the meeting about it."
Undertaking -- a formal promise to do something
"They gave an undertaking to fix it in ten days."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Ana has ten minutes with a board that has never read a contract. She does not stop to explain anything. She defines as she goes. Force majeure, which means an event nobody can control, is in the boilerplate, together with severability, which keeps the rest of the contract valid if one clause fails. Then she moves to the transfer. An assignment, which passes our rights to somebody else, needs the other side's consent. A novation, which replaces one party with a new one, needs everybody to sign, including the affiliate that takes over. The clause that worries her is the one on assignment, and the manager who signed the annex works for the Mexican subsidiary. Nobody in the room asked her to repeat a word. The meaning arrived with the word, in the same breath, every single time.

1. Why is "which means an event nobody can control" between two commas?
A It is extra information. We already know which term she is talking about, and the sentence survives without it.
B Because the sentence is long and she needs to breathe.
C Because "force majeure" is a French expression.
2. "The clause that worries her is the one on assignment." Why is there no comma before "that"?
A Because "that" never takes a comma anywhere in English.
B Because the clause tells us which one. Take it out and we no longer know which clause she means.
C Because the sentence is short.
3. The text says "the manager who signed the annex". Why "who" and not "which"?
A A manager is a person, and people take "who".
B Because "which" only appears after a comma.
C Because the manager works for a subsidiary.
4. Why did nobody in the room ask her to repeat a word?
A Because she avoided every technical term.
B Because she spoke very slowly.
C Because each term arrived with its meaning inside the same sentence.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Defining and Non-Defining Relative Clauses

GRAMMAR

One clause says which one. The other one adds information. In English, the commas decide which is which.

FormUseExample
noun + that / who / which
(no commas)		Defining. It tells you which one. Remove it and the sentence stops making sense.The clause that worries me is fourteen.
noun, who / which ...,
(two commas)		Non-defining. We already know which one. This is extra information, and the sentence works without it.Clause fourteen, which worries me, is the indemnity.
who / whichwho for people, which for things and ideas. A company can take either; a manager is always who.The manager who signed the annex · the annex, which is attached at the end,
The one-breath definitionPut the meaning inside two commas and keep going. The term and the meaning arrive together.A novation, which replaces one party with a new one, needs everyone to sign.
QuestionWhich one are you talking about? · What does that mean?Which clause is the problem? / What does severability mean?
Four trapsNever "the manager which signed" -- people take who. Never put a comma in a defining clause ("the clause, that worries me,") -- in English the comma changes the meaning, it is not a breath. Never use that after a comma ("clause fourteen, that is the indemnity") -- it has to be which. And never repeat the object ("the clause which I mentioned it") -- which is already the object.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

A manager on the call has never heard the word "novation". You say:
A "We need a novation. I can send you an article about it later."
B "We need a novation, which replaces one party with a new one, and that means everybody has to sign."
C "We need something complicated that I will not explain now."
There are twenty clauses in the contract and you want to point at one of them. You say:
A "The clause that worries me is the one on assignment."
B "The clause, that worries me, is the one on assignment."
C "The clause, which worries me, is the one on assignment."
Everybody already knows you are talking about clause fourteen. You want to add one detail. You say:
A "Clause fourteen, that is the indemnity, is the expensive one."
B "Clause fourteen, which is the indemnity, is the expensive one."
C "Clause fourteen which is the indemnity is the expensive one."
You want to identify a person in the group. Which one is correct?
A "The manager which signed the annex works in Mexico."
B "The manager who signed the annex works in Mexico."
C "The manager, that signed the annex, works in Mexico."
You are referring back to a clause you talked about earlier. Which one is correct?
A "The clause which I mentioned it is on page nine."
B "The clause which I mentioned is on page nine."
C "The clause who I mentioned it is on page nine."
