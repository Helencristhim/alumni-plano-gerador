Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Request and the Rule

A manager asking, and the CFO explaining the criteria

Listening 1

A Manager Asks for an Exception

A manager leaves a message asking for a new position. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. What exactly is the manager asking for?
An exception to open a new position, not a replacement.
2. What justification does he give?
The team took on the Chile portfolio and the workload grew, and one person does the work of two.
3. What does he not say in his message?
He does not say the cost of the position or the date when he needs it.
Listening 2

The CFO Explains the Criteria

The CFO records a short message about approvals this quarter. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. What are the two criteria for an exception this quarter?
A critical role, and a cost that is already inside the approved budget.
2. What does the CFO say about new positions?
New positions are on hold until January, and only replacements are considered.
3. What does he ask HR to send with every request?
The business justification in three lines and the monthly cost.

Chapter 5: The Code

Approve, Approval, Approved

The word family of the decision you make every day

Grammar Discovery

Verb, Noun or Description?

"I approve the request today."

"The position is waiting for my approval."

"The request was approved last Friday."

"I'm afraid we can't approve it this quarter."

Three of these words are the same family. The fourth one shows why English uses be and not have. What is the pattern?

FormWhat it doesExample
approve (verb)the action you doI approve the request.
approval (noun)the thing itself, the official yesThe request needs my approval.
approved (past participle)describes the request after the decisionThe request was approved on Friday.
be + adjectiveafraid, sure, sorry and ready go with be, never with haveI am afraid we can't. I am sure about the date. I am sorry about the delay.
Watch outthe verb have with an adjective. "I have afraid" and "I have sure" do not exist in English: it is always I am afraid and I am sure.

Sound Check

Say It Clearly

-ed and -s endings · point 6 of 14

Why this one is hard for a Brazilian speaker

In Portuguese: The past is a full syllable of its own (falou, pediu), so -ed is read as one too.

In English: -ed is a syllable ONLY after t or d (requested, decided). Everywhere else it is a sound glued to the end.

If it slips: "Approved" becomes "approve-ed", and every past verb slows the sentence down.
