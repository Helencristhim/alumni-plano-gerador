Sound

The Three Sounds of -ed

Same three letters. Three different sounds.

asked, stopped, worked — a short T at the end, no extra syllable
called, signed, arrived — a soft D at the end, still no extra syllable
attended, wanted, needed — here, and only here, you hear a whole new syllable
A colleague of mine postponed it.

Only after t or d does -ed become a syllable of its own.

Chapter 3: The Code

Two Letters, and the Word That Steals Them

One tense, three shapes

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"A client called at seven in the morning."

"The exporter sent the wrong document."

"The terminal did not release the cargo."

"Did you find out why?"

The first two red verbs are in the past, but only one of them shows it with two letters. In the last two the past is gone from the verb — and the blue word is carrying it.

JobFormExample
Regular verb, positiveverb + -ed, for every subjectI called the client. She called the client.
Irregular verb, positivea new word you have to knowsend → sent · go → went · take → took
Negativedid not + bare verbThey did not accept the clause.
QuestionDid + subject + bare verbDid you attend the meeting?
Question word in frontWhat / When / Why + did + subject + bare verbWhen did they file it?
When it happenedyesterday, last week, two days ago, on Monday, in MarchWe signed it two days ago.
To be is differentwas / were — no did at allHe was not happy. The clauses were new.
The trapnever two pasts in the same sentenceNot did you went. Say did you go.

In one line: the past belongs to the sentence, not to the verb — and did takes it whenever did turns up.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Somebody Else’s Week, at Full Speed

One conversation, two recordings, one week on paper

Dialogue

How Was Your Week in Miami?

E
You look tired. How did last week go in Miami?
N
It was a long week. On Monday a client called at seven in the morning about a shipment that never left the port.
E
Did you find out why?
N
We did, on Wednesday. The exporter sent the wrong document, so the terminal did not release the cargo.
E
And the client? Did he accept that?
N
Not at first. I spent Thursday on the phone with him and with two colleagues in Houston. We postponed the delivery and agreed on a new date.
E
So when did you finally close it?
N
On Friday afternoon. Three drafts, one bad document, and one signature at four o’clock. It went well, in the end.
Comprehension

About Nora’s Week

1. What happened on Monday, and at what time?
A client called her at seven in the morning, about a shipment that never left the port.
2. Why did the terminal keep the cargo?
Because the exporter sent the wrong document, so the terminal did not release it.
3. What did she do on Thursday, and how did the week end?
She spent Thursday on the phone with the client and two colleagues in Houston. They postponed the delivery, agreed on a new date, and closed it on Friday afternoon.
Listening 1 · Monday Morning

One Week in Five Minutes

A partner opening the week with a report on the last one. Sound first — no text.

1. What arrived on Monday, and what was wrong with it?
The first draft from the buyer in Rotterdam arrived, and it came with two clauses nobody expected.
2. What stopped the week for two days, and what did the other side do about it?
The ship was late. Nobody called and nobody explained why, until the other side finally answered on Friday.
3. How does she summarise the whole week, and why does she want the team to remember it?
One week, three drafts, one delay and one signature — because next month one of them runs a matter like it alone.
Listening 2 · Business History

Five Contracts, Five Signatures

The week the wheat left the country and nobody counted. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the Soviet buyers do, and why did nobody notice?
They went to five different trading houses in New York in the same month, and each house thought it sold a normal cargo, because nobody talked to anybody else.
2. What had already happened by the time the government counted the ships?
The grain was already at sea, and the price of bread went up in every American supermarket that year.
3. The speaker says the lesson is not about wheat. What is it about?
Information. Every contract was correct and the deal was still a disaster, because nobody asked the same question at the same time.
