Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

Last Week at the Office -- Reporting What Already Happened

A week told day by day, in the past. Key words: an agreement, a draft, a signature, a negotiation, a colleague, a business trip, to attend, to file, to postpone, to close a deal. Structure: past simple — the -ed, the ten verbs that ignore it, and the did that takes it away.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An agreement -- what two sides decided together, before anybody wrote it down
"We reached an agreement on Friday, and the contract came on Monday."
A draft -- a first version of a document, written so that people can change it
"They sent the first draft on Monday and we answered on Wednesday."
A signature -- your name written by hand at the end of a document
"One week, three drafts, one delay, and one signature."
A negotiation -- the conversation in which two sides move until they can agree
"The negotiation took three days and two phone calls."
A colleague -- a person you work with, not necessarily a friend
"I spent Thursday on the phone with two colleagues in Houston."
A business trip -- travel you do for work, not for a holiday
"He went on a business trip to the port on Friday morning."
To attend -- to go to a meeting or a class, and be there in person
"I attended the meeting at the terminal and filed the papers the same afternoon."
To file -- to send a document officially to a court or to a public office
"We filed the documents on Friday, one hour before the office closed."
To postpone -- to move something to a later date, without cancelling it
"They postponed the call to Thursday, and nobody explained why."
To close a deal -- to finish an agreement so that the work on it is done
"We closed the deal on Friday afternoon, three drafts later."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last week nothing went the way it was planned. On Monday the buyer sent the first draft, and it arrived with two clauses nobody expected. On Tuesday a colleague called the client. On Wednesday the other side did not answer at all. On Thursday the negotiation finally started, and the delivery was postponed. On Friday the client signed. Look at the verbs: most of them end in -ed, and that ending never changes, whoever the subject is.

Some verbs refuse the -ed and become a different word instead: send becomes sent, go becomes went, read stays read on paper but sounds like "red". There are about a hundred of them, and the ten you will use every week are enough to start.

Now the part that costs points. To say no, the past leaves the verb and goes to did: "They did not accept the clause", never did not accepted. The same happens in a question: "Did you attend the meeting?", never did you attended. One past per sentence, and did takes it whenever did turns up. The verb to be is the exception: it uses was and were, and it never asks did for help — "He was not happy", not he did not was happy.

Finally, the past almost always travels with a time expression: yesterday, last week, two days ago, on Monday, in March. If the time expression is there, the verb has to follow it into the past.

1. "They did not accept the clause." Why is there no -ed on accept?
A Because accept is an irregular verb.
B Because did already carries the past, and there is only one past per sentence.
C Because negative sentences have no tense.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "He did not was happy with the draft."
B "He was not happy with the draft."
C "He was not happied with the draft."
3. What does a time expression like "two days ago" tell you about the verb?
A Nothing. The verb can stay in the present.
B That the verb needs an -s.
C That the verb has to be in the past too.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A colleague asks how last week went. You answer:
A "Last week is difficult. We send two drafts and the client sign on Friday."
B "It was a long week. We sent two drafts, and the client signed on Friday."
C "It was long week, we did sent two drafts and he did signed."
You want to say the other side stayed silent. You say:
A "They did not answer for two days."
B "They did not answered for two days."
C "They not answer during two days."
You want to ask whether your colleague went to the meeting in person. You say:
A "Did you went to the meeting?"
B "Did you attend the meeting yourself, or did somebody go for you?"
C "You attended the meeting, no?"
You want to ask whether the client was satisfied. You say:
A "Did the client was happy with the draft?"
B "Was the client happy with the draft?"
C "The client did happy with the draft?"

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.
