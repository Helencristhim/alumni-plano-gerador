Lesson 27 -- Pre-class

The First Concession -- Never Give, Always Trade

How to answer a request for a discount: make a small counteroffer and put a condition on every concession. Key words: a list price, a counteroffer, wiggle room, in return, to meet halfway, to throw in, a sweetener, to match, a multi-year deal, a bundle. Structure: first conditional for trade-offs (if you sign..., we will...; if we..., will you...?).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A list price -- the official price of a product, before any discount
"Our list price is forty euros per user per month."
A counteroffer -- an offer you make as an answer to another offer
"She asked for fifteen percent, so I made a counteroffer of eight."
Wiggle room -- a little space to change a price or a plan
"There is some wiggle room on the price, but not much."
In return -- as an exchange for something you give
"We can lower the price, but we need a longer contract in return."
To meet halfway -- to accept part of what the other side wants, so both sides give something
"If you can meet us halfway, we will sign today."
To throw in -- to add something extra, for free, as part of a deal
"If you sign this week, we will throw in the training."
A sweetener -- something extra you offer to make a deal more attractive
"Free onboarding is a good sweetener for a finance team."
To match -- to offer the same price or the same thing as someone else
"We can't match their price, but we can match their payment terms."
A multi-year deal -- a contract that lasts more than one year
"A multi-year deal gives us a better price and gives them stability."
A bundle -- a group of products or services sold together for one price
"The bundle includes the software, the training and the support."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

When a buyer asks for a discount, a good seller never says just yes. He makes a trade. If you sign a multi-year deal, we will reduce the fee by eight percent. The first part is the condition: what the customer gives. The second part is the concession: what the seller gives.

The condition uses if + present simple, even when the idea is about the future: if you pay annually, if Giulia signs by Friday. The concession uses will, or 'll when you speak: we'll throw in the training. For a softer offer, use can: we can do eight percent.

You can also put the concession first: we'll do ten percent if you sign by Friday. And you can ask for the trade with a question: if we do ten percent, will you sign this week?

Brazilian speakers often say if you will sign, because in Portuguese the idea feels like the future. In English, there is no will after if. Another common mistake is to forget will in the concession: if you pay annually, we throw in the training.

1. Which sentence is correct?
A "If you will sign a multi-year deal with us this week, we will reduce the fee."
B "If you sign, we reduce."
C "If you sign for three years, we will reduce the fee."
2. In "If you pay annually, we'll throw in the training", who gives what?
A The seller pays for the training every year, and the customer gives nothing in return for it.
B The customer pays annually, and the seller gives the training.
C Nobody gives anything.
3. Why does a seller use if before a concession?
A To get something in return for what he gives.
B Because it is more polite, and the buyer will not ask for anything more after that.
C Because it is a rule of grammar.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The First Conditional for a Trade

GRAMMAR

If + present for what they give. Will for what you give.

JobFormExample
affirmativeif + present, will + verbIf you sign for three years, we will reduce the fee.
negativeif + present, won't + verbIf you sign for one year, we won't change the list price.
interrogativeif + present, will you + verb?If we do ten percent, will you sign this week?
softerif + present, can + verbIf you commit today, we can do eight percent.
concession firstwill + verb + if + presentWe'll throw in the training if you pay annually.

Never: if you will sign · if you pay annually, we throw in · OK, I give you ten percent

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The CFO asks for fifteen percent off. The best first answer is:
A "OK, fifteen percent."
B "If you sign for three years, we can do eight percent."
C "No, our price is fair, and we never give a discount to any customer, because our product is the best."
The buyer asks you to match the other finalist's price. You say:
A "We can't match it, but if you pay annually, we'll throw in the training."
B "If you will commit to three years with us, we will try to match their price, maybe."
C "Yes, no problem."
The buyer says, "Meet me halfway: twelve percent." Your limit is ten. You say:
A "OK, twelve percent is fine, and if you want, we can talk about the support price later too."
B "If you sign, we give ten."
C "I can do ten. If we do ten, will you sign by Friday?"
You want to add free training to close the deal. You say:
A "We give you the training."
B "We'll throw in the training if you sign this week."
C "If you will sign this week, we will throw in the training for all the people in your team."
