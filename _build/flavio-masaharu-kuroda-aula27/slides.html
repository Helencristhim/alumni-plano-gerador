One Ask, One Trade

Dialogue

Paola Asks for Fifteen Percent

P
Mr. Kuroda, your customer in Turin was very convincing. But your list price is still fifteen percent above the other finalist.
F
I'm glad the call went well. Fifteen percent is a lot. If you sign a multi-year deal, we will reduce the fee by eight percent.
P
Eight is not fifteen. Can you match their price?
F
We can't match it, but there is some wiggle room. If you pay annually, we'll throw in the training for your finance team.
P
That is a nice sweetener. And what happens if we only sign for one year?
F
If you sign for one year, we will keep the list price. The discount comes with the three years.
P
Then meet me halfway. Three years, the training, and twelve percent.
F
I can do ten, not twelve. If we do ten percent, will Giulia sign by Friday?

Listening 1 · The Negotiation Trainer

Never Give, Always Trade

A trainer who coaches sales teams, on the first discount. Sound first — no text.

1. Why is the first discount the most important one?
Because it teaches the buyer how you negotiate.
2. What happens if you start with twenty percent?
The buyer will think your list price was never real.
3. What should you do when the buyer asks you to meet halfway?
Not say yes at once: ask a question first, like "If we do that, will you sign this week?"
Listening 2 · The Buyer

Every Meeting, a Discount

A software buyer on why she always asks for a lower price. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she ask for a discount in every first meeting?
To see how much wiggle room the seller has.
2. What did she do when the first seller gave fifteen percent?
She asked for more, because she knew his list price was not serious.
3. Why did she sign with the second seller?
The seller gave her something, but always asked for something in return: a multi-year deal, then a signature by Friday.
