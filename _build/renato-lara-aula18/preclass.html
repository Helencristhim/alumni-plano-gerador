Lesson 18 — Pre-class

The Saturday Trip

Eight words for reading the sea (offshore, onshore, choppy, glassy, current, beginner, experienced, ride) and how to say something is a problem or the right amount: too + adjective, and adjective + enough.

Stage 1.1: Sea Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes. You know these things from the water — now you have the English words.

Offshore — wind that blows from the land out to the sea
"The wind is offshore until ten, so go early."
Onshore — wind that blows from the sea onto the land
"With a strong onshore wind, the waves are messy."
Choppy — with a lot of small, messy waves because of the wind
"After ten the water gets choppy."
Glassy — very smooth and flat, like a mirror, with no wind on it
"At six in the morning the sea was glassy."
Current — water in the sea that moves strongly in one direction
"There is a strong current near the rocks."
Beginner — a person who is starting to learn something
"This beach is good for a beginner."
Experienced — good at something because you have done it for a long time
"The reef is only for experienced surfers."
Ride — a free trip in somebody else's car
"Can I get a ride to the beach on Saturday?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. Then answer the questions.

Friday night the club talked about Saturday for two hours. The reef at the point was two meters with an onshore wind: too big and too messy for most of us, and honestly I'm not experienced enough for that reef yet. Our home beach was too small — the waves were not big enough to surf. So we chose the beach break an hour south. The wind was offshore until ten and the sea was glassy, but it was choppy by eleven. There was a strong current near the rocks, so we stayed in the middle. Jess's dad's van was not big enough for twelve people, so I asked Bev for a ride. The water was too cold for my short wetsuit. Next time I'm bringing enough food. Even the beginner in our group caught waves.
"The waves were too big for most of us." What does this mean?
A The waves were bigger than is good for them. It was a problem, so most of them did not surf there.
B The waves were very big and very good, so everybody surfed there.
Why is it "not big enough" and not "not enough big"?
A Because "enough" always goes at the end of the sentence.
B Because "enough" goes after an adjective: big enough, experienced enough.
Which sentence is correct?
A The water was too much cold for my wetsuit.
B The water was too cold for my wetsuit.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Too big · big enough · enough water

Too = more than is good (a problem). Enough = the right amount. Not ... enough = less than you need.

The form

too + adjective: too big
adjective + enough: big enough
not + adjective + enough: not old enough
enough + noun: enough water

Never

The waves are enough big
The water is too much cold
The reef too dangerous
→ The reef is too dangerous

Positive: The waves are big enough.  ·  Negative: I'm not experienced enough.  ·  Question: Is it too cold?
Too is not very: "very big" can be good; "too big" is always a problem.
Add a reason: It's too choppy to surf. · It's too big for me.

Stage 4: On the beach

Quiz
The waves are only knee-high. You want to tell the group.
A The waves are enough big today.
B The waves aren't big enough today.
The wind is blowing from the sea and the water is messy. What do you say?
A It's onshore and too choppy. Let's wait.
B It's offshore and glassy. Let's wait.
A teammate says your last wave was amazing. You agree it was very good.
A Yes, it was too good!
B Yes, it was really good!
The van has seven seats and there are twelve members.
A There aren't enough seats. Can I get a ride with you?
B There are too enough seats. Can I get a ride with you?
A beginner wants to paddle out at the reef at low tide.
A Wait. It's too shallow, and it's only for experienced surfers.
B Go. It's shallow enough for experienced surfers only.

Stage 5: Choose the beach

Reflection
Look at the surf forecast for your favorite beach in Brazil this weekend. Record 30 seconds: is it too big, too small, too windy, or good enough? Would you go, and would it be good enough for a beginner? Use "too" twice and "enough" twice. Check one thing before you send it: every sentence has a verb — "the waves are too big", never "the waves too big".
