Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the decision system. Then answer the questions.

If the wind is offshore, the waves are clean. If it is onshore, the water gets choppy and most people stay in bed. If the tide is too low, the waves break all at once and you get closeouts. If the flags are red, nobody goes in. And the decision for tomorrow: if it is onshore, I will stay home and do my assignment. If the swell arrives on time, I will be in the water before sunrise.

Why do the last two sentences use "will" and the first four do not?
A Because the last two are about one specific tomorrow.
B Because they are longer sentences.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Zero and first conditional

Same little word, two different jobs. What changes is the second half.

Zero conditional -- a fact

If the tide is low, the wave breaks on the bank.
If the flags are red, nobody goes in.
If you paddle too late, you miss the wave.
present + present

First conditional -- one time

If it is onshore tomorrow, I will stay home.
If I miss the bus, I will be late.
If the swell arrives, we will go at dawn.
present + will

Zero conditional (always true): if + present, present. If the tide is low, the wave breaks on the bank.
First conditional (one future moment): if + present, will. If it is onshore tomorrow, I will stay home.
The one rule to memorise: never "will" after "if". Not in either type.
You can flip it: "I will stay home if it is onshore" is the same sentence, and then there is no comma.
