Grammar Discovery

Two kinds of if

If the tide is low, the waves break on the sandbank.
If the flags are red, nobody goes in.
If it is onshore tomorrow, I will stay home.
If I miss the bus, I will be late.

One is a fact. One is a plan.

The first two are always true, every time: that is the zero conditional, present simple on both sides. The last two are about ONE future moment that has not happened yet: that is the first conditional, present simple after if and will in the other half. And in both, the verb after if is present — never will.

The Code

Always true, or one time

Zero conditional -- a fact

If the tide is low, the wave breaks on the bank.
If the flags are red, nobody goes in.
If you paddle too late, you miss the wave.
present + present

First conditional -- one time

If it is onshore tomorrow, I will stay home.
If I miss the bus, I will be late.
If the swell arrives, we will go at dawn.
present + will

The one rule that covers both: never put "will" after "if". And you can flip the halves — "I will stay home if it is onshore" means exactly the same thing, and then the comma disappears.

Sound Check

The if half is one breath

If the tide is low, the waves are better.

the first half runs together and the voice rises at the comma

If it is onshore, I will stay home.

"I will" becomes "I'll" almost every time

I will go if the swell arrives.

flipped around, no comma, and no pause

Listening 1

Tomorrow, hour by hour

1. What happens if you are in the water before eight?
You get the best of it, because the wind is light and offshore early.
2. What will happen by eleven, and why?
It will be unsurfable, because the wind swings onshore and the gusts get strong.
3. What is the advice if the swell is bigger than forecast?
The north end will be the safe option.
Listening 2

Thirty seconds to decide

1. What are her first two rules?
If the wind is onshore she does not get out of bed, and if the tide is under half a metre she skips it too.
2. What does she check on the webcam?
The flags. If they are red she stays home.
3. What is her last rule, and what does she admit?
If she has an assignment due the next day she goes anyway and does it at eleven at night. She has never once chosen the assignment.
