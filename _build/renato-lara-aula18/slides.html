Grammar Discovery

Too much, or not enough?

Two meters at the reef is too big for most of us.
The waves at home are not big enough today.
The beach break is small enough for everybody.
There isn't enough room in the van.

What do the orange words have in common? Where does each word go?

too + adjective  ·  adjective + enough  ·  enough + noun

Too means more than is good — it is a problem, and it goes before the adjective: too big. Enough means the right amount, and it goes after the adjective (big enough) but before a noun (enough room). Not ... enough means less than you need.

Never

The waves are enough big.
enough goes after the adjective: big enough
The sea is too much choppy.
too + adjective, no "much": too choppy
The waves     too big.
the verb cannot be missing: the waves are too big

Very big = a lot, maybe good. Too big = a problem, you can't surf.

Sound Check

Two small words, big stress

The waves aren't big enough today.

"enough" ends with an f sound: i-NAF

It's too choppy to surf.

the stress goes on "too": it's TOO choppy

I'm not experienced enough for the reef.

"not" and "enough" hold the whole sentence together

Listening 1

Friday night, a message from Kai

1. Why are they not going to Trigg?
The forecast is two meters with a strong onshore wind. It is too big and too messy for most of them.
2. What is the problem with Jess's dad's van?
It isn't big enough for everybody and all the boards, so they need one more car.
3. What time do they meet, and what do they have to bring?
At half past five, not six, in the school parking lot. They have to bring enough water and food for the whole morning, because there is no shop there.
Listening 2

Three conditions from Bev

1. Why does an adult have to drive?
Renato and his friends are not old enough to drive.
2. Why must he stay close to the group?
The currents on that coast are strong, and he doesn't know the beach well enough yet.
3. What does she tell him to take, and why?
His long wetsuit and a warm jacket. It is winter, the water is colder than he thinks, and he was too cold on Tuesday.
