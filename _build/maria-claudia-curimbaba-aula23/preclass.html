Lesson 23 -- Pre-class

Giving Feedback -- Constructive and Direct

Say the difficult thing and keep the relationship. Soften the pressure without softening the message: "I think we could", "it might be worth" + the -ing form, and "What if we..." to turn a verdict into a shared question. New vocabulary: constructive, blunt, praise, shortcoming, acknowledge, reinforce, tactful, actionable, defensive, sugarcoat.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Constructive -- critical but useful: it gives the person a way forward
"Her comments were hard, but entirely constructive."
Blunt -- very direct, with nothing softening it
"That was blunt. True, but blunt."
Praise -- to say clearly that someone did something well
"He deserves praise for the way he handled the questions."
Shortcoming -- a weakness or fault in a person or a piece of work
"The one shortcoming of the deck was the opening."
Acknowledge -- to accept openly that something is true or was said
"Acknowledge the effort before you name the problem."
Reinforce -- to make a behavior stronger by recognizing it
"Naming what worked reinforces it better than a general thank you."
Tactful -- careful not to hurt, without changing the message
"She was tactful and completely clear at the same time."
Actionable -- clear enough that the person can act on it immediately
"Cut the first three slides is actionable; be more engaging is not."
Defensive -- protecting yourself from criticism instead of hearing it
"He went defensive the moment I said the word problem."
Sugarcoat -- to make bad news sound nicer than it is, so it is missed
"Do not sugarcoat it. She would rather hear the truth."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

David presented the quarterly numbers to the board, and Maria Claudia has to talk to him about it. She starts with praise, but not the general kind: "The way you handled the questions at the end was excellent" -- naming the behavior is what reinforces it. Then the one shortcoming, said once: the opening lost the room. She is tactful and she does not sugarcoat it, because those are two different things. "I think we could open with the revenue line", she says, and "it might be worth cutting the first three slides" -- notice that after worth the verb takes the -ing form, while after could and might it stays in the base form. David gets defensive and defends the context, so she does not repeat the verdict. She acknowledges his point and turns it into a question: "What if we kept the context but moved it after the first number?" The talk ends actionable: three slides out, revenue first, rehearsal on Tuesday. Hard, constructive, and not blunt at all.

1. Which form follows "it might be worth"?
A The -ing form ("worth cutting the slides").
B "to" plus the base verb ("worth to cut the slides").
C The past form ("worth cut the slides").
2. Why does she say "What if we kept the context...?" instead of repeating herself?
A Because she has changed her mind about the opening.
B Because he is defensive, and a question shares the decision instead of pushing.
C Because the message was wrong and she is taking it back.
3. According to the text, what is the difference between being tactful and sugarcoating?
A There is none: both make the message softer.
B Being tactful means saying nothing until the person asks.
C Being tactful lowers the pressure; sugarcoating hides the message itself.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Softening a Verdict

GRAMMAR

The difference between blunt and tactful is three or four words, not your personality. COULD instead of should turns an instruction into an option. IT MIGHT BE WORTH + the -ing form suggests without naming who was wrong. I THINK marks it as your view. WHAT IF WE + the past form makes it a shared question. And WE instead of YOU shares the problem instead of assigning it.

MarkerWhat it doesExample
could + base verban option, not an instruction"We could open with the numbers."
it might be worth + -ingsuggests without naming who is wrong"It might be worth cutting the opening."
I think / one thing I would suggestmarks it as your view, not a verdict"One thing I would suggest is a shorter opening."
What if we + past forma question, so the decision is shared"What if we opened with the numbers?"
we, not youshares the problem instead of assigning it"We could tighten it" beats "you should tighten it"

Key point: softening changes the pressure, never the message. If the person leaves without knowing what to change, that was not tact -- that was sugarcoating. Watch the forms: "worth to cut" → "worth cutting"; "could to open" → "could open"; "I suggest you to cut" → "I suggest cutting" or "I suggest that you cut".

Stage 2: The Feedback Talk in Order

Order

Listen first, then put the steps of a feedback conversation in the correct order.

?If the person becomes defensive, you stop, acknowledge their point, and ask a question.
?You reinforce one specific thing that worked, naming the behavior and not the person.
?You say what you are NOT saying, so nothing is left for them to imagine afterwards.
?You name the one shortcoming, once and clearly, softening the pressure but not the message.
?You make it actionable: exactly what you would change, so they can act tomorrow morning.
