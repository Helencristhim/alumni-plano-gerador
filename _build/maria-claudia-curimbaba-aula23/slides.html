Chapter 3 -- The Code

Turning a Verdict into a Suggestion

could / might be worth / I think / what if

Discovery

Same Message, Four Levels

All four say the same thing: the deck opens badly. Which words do the moving?

"You should cut the first three slides."

"I think we could cut the first three slides."

"It might be worth cutting the first three slides."

"What if we opened with the numbers instead?"

MarkerWhat it doesExample
could (not should)turns an instruction into an option"We could open with the numbers."
it might be worth + -ingsuggests without naming who is wrong"It might be worth cutting the opening."
I think / one thing I would suggestmarks it as your view, not a verdict"One thing I would suggest is a shorter opening."
What if we + past forma question, so the decision is shared"What if we opened with the numbers?"
we, not youshares the problem instead of assigning it"We could tighten it" beats "you should tighten it"

Key idea: softening changes the PRESSURE, never the MESSAGE. If the person walks away without knowing what to change, you did not soften it -- you sugarcoated it. Watch the form: after worth the verb takes -ing, and after could and might it is the base verb.

The Structure

Three Parts, in This Order

Not a sandwich. Each part does a different job.

1 · Reinforce something specific

"The way you handled the questions at the end was excellent."

Name the behavior, not the person. A general "well done" reinforces nothing.

2 · Name the shortcoming, once

"The opening lost the room, and I think we could fix that."

Once, clearly, with a softener on the pressure and none on the message.

3 · Make it actionable

"It might be worth cutting the first three slides and opening with the numbers."

If they cannot do it tomorrow morning, it is not feedback yet.

Tactful and blunt are not opposites. The opposite of tactful is careless, and the opposite of blunt is sugarcoated.

Chapter 4 -- The Conversation

Feedback in Action

Listen, read the note, and have the talk

Listening 1

The One Rule

Sound first -- no text on screen.

0:000:00
1. What is the one rule he applies to every piece of feedback?
If the person cannot act on it the next morning, it is not feedback yet -- it has to be actionable.
2. What does he say about the feedback sandwich?
He does not use it: the praise exists only to cushion, so people hear the two nice parts and miss the middle.
3. Why does he name the behavior instead of the person?
Because naming a behavior reinforces it or changes it, while naming the person makes them defensive.
4. What does he do when someone goes defensive anyway?
He stops, acknowledges the effort out loud, and asks a question instead of repeating the verdict.
Artefact

The Feedback Note

AFTER OUR CONVERSATION

From: Maria Claudia Curimbaba · To: David · Q3 board presentation

What worked

The way you handled the questions at the end was excellent -- you took the hardest one first and answered it in two sentences.

What did not

The opening lost the room. Six minutes of context before the first number, and by slide four two people were on their phones.

What I would suggest

It might be worth cutting the first three slides and opening with the revenue line. We could rehearse the new opening on Tuesday.

What I am not saying

That the analysis was wrong. It was not. This is about the first five minutes only.

Your line: "I think we could open with the numbers, and it might be worth rehearsing it together on Tuesday."

Dialogue

The Talk After the Presentation

M

"David, before anything else: the way you handled the questions at the end deserves praise. You took the hardest one first."

D

"Thank you. I can hear a but coming, so please do not sugarcoat it. Just tell me."

M

"All right. The opening lost the room -- that is the one shortcoming. I think we could open with the revenue line instead."

D

"The context matters, though. Without it the numbers mean nothing. I built those six minutes for a reason."

M

"I acknowledge that, and I am not saying the analysis was wrong. What if we kept the context but moved it after the first number?"

D

"That I can work with. I was getting defensive and you did not push -- you asked. That is the difference."

M

"Then let us make it actionable: three slides out, the revenue line first, and we rehearse on Tuesday. Nothing blunt about that -- it is just clear."

Dialogue Review

About the Conversation

1. What does David ask for at the very beginning?
That she does not sugarcoat it -- he can hear a "but" coming and would rather have it straight.
2. How does David defend the six minutes of context?
He says the numbers mean nothing without it, and that he built those minutes for a reason.
3. What does he say made the difference at the end?
That when he got defensive she did not push -- she asked a question instead.

Listening 2

The Feedback That Landed

Sound first -- no text on screen.

0:000:00
1. What did the feedback that did nothing sound like?
"Be more strategic" -- kind, general, and impossible to act on the next morning.
2. What made the other piece of feedback land?
It named one behavior and one change: she was answering questions she had not been asked, and she should stop at the answer.
3. How did she feel in the moment, and how did she feel a week later?
In the moment she was defensive and did not sleep well; a week later she called it the most useful thing anyone had said to her.
4. What does she now ask for at the end of every review?
One thing she should keep doing and one thing she should change -- and she refuses to leave with a general "well done".
