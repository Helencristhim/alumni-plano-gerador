Lesson 5 -- First Impressions

Conference Talk
"Why you should talk to strangers" -- Kio Stark, TED

Twelve minutes on the exact thing you practised today: walking up to somebody you do not know and saying the first sentence. She explains why it feels impossible and why it almost never goes badly.

Tip: watch it once with English subtitles. Then write the ONE opening sentence you are going to use in Bangkok, and say it out loud ten times, until it comes without thinking.

Listening
"The art of conversation" -- 6 Minute English, BBC Learning English

Six minutes on what makes a conversation work and what kills it, at a speed built for learners. Listen for how often the two presenters ask each other something instead of just talking.

Tip: listen once with your eyes closed, no text. Count the questions they ask each other. Then compare with your own recording from today: how many did you ask?

Conference Talk
"10 ways to have a better conversation" -- Celeste Headlee, TED

A radio journalist gives ten rules for talking to people. Rule number four is the one that matters most for you in Bangkok: ask open questions and then actually listen to the answer.

Tip: choose the three rules that are hardest for you, write them down, and use exactly those three in your homework recording. Ignore the other seven for now.
