Lesson 05 -- Pre-class

First Impressions -- The First Thirty Seconds

Walking up to somebody you have never met, and leaving with a contact. Key words: to introduce, to approach, to mention, to exchange, connection, impression, to follow up, contact details. Structure: present continuous for now and for temporary situations.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. Six of these are things you do in the first minute. Two are what is left after you walk away.

To introduce -- to say your name to somebody new
"Let me introduce myself. I am Dejair, from Brazil."
To approach -- to walk towards somebody and start talking
"I approach two or three people every day at a congress."
To mention -- to say something quickly, without explaining it all
"She mentioned a study on nerve damage, so I asked about it."
To exchange -- to give something and get something back
"We exchanged contact details before the session started."
Connection -- a link between two people or two ideas
"There is a clear connection between her work and mine."
Impression -- the idea somebody forms about you in the first minutes
"A short, clear sentence makes a better impression than a long one."
To follow up -- to contact somebody again after the first meeting
"I always follow up in the first week, or the contact is lost."
Contact details -- your email, your phone and where people find you
"Can we exchange contact details? Here is my card."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

It is the first coffee break. Ingrid is standing alone with a cup, and Dejair decides to approach her. He does not start with his name. He starts with the room: "This queue is not moving at all." She laughs, introduces herself, and he asks the only question he prepared: "What are you working on at the moment?" She works on nerve damage in diabetes -- that is her field, always -- but right now she is collecting data in four hospitals until December. He mentions leprosy in one sentence, and she sees the connection immediately.
Why does the text say "she works on nerve damage in diabetes" but "right now she is collecting data"?
A Because collecting data is more important than her field
B Because her field is always true, and the data collection is only until December
C Because the two sentences are about different people
Why is the opening question "What are you working on at the moment?" and not "What do you work on?"
A Because it asks about the project of this period, which is the easiest thing for anybody to talk about
B Because it is more polite
C Because at the moment always needs a question

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

Use the present continuous (am / is / are + verb + -ing) for what is happening right now, and for what is true only for a while: this week, this year, until December. Keep the present simple for what is always true.

always truepresent simpleI work on nerve damage caused by leprosy.
happening right nowam / is / are + -ingShe is presenting in room two right now.
true only for a whileam / is / are + -ingWe are collecting data until December.
questionam / is / are + subject + -ingWhat are you working on at the moment?
state verbs stay simpleknow, want, need, understandSorry, I do not know that group.

Verbs of state -- know, want, need, understand, believe -- do not take -ing. "I am not knowing that group" is wrong.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
Somebody asks what you are working on right now. Which answer is best?
A "I work on nerve damage, and at the moment I am collecting data in three centres."
B "I am working on nerve damage since fifteen years."
C "My work is difficult to explain."
You did not hear a word she said. What do you say?
A "Sorry, my English is very bad."
B "Say that again, please. I am not sure I heard it right."
C "What?"
The session is starting and you want to keep the contact. What do you say?
A "Goodbye."
B "I have to go, but I will follow up next week."
C "I am going to follow you up next week."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
You are at the networking dinner and you sit down next to somebody you have never met. Record three minutes: open the conversation, introduce yourself, ask what they are working on, mention your own work in one sentence, name the connection between the two, and ask for contact details. Do both voices if you want. And do not apologise for your English once.
