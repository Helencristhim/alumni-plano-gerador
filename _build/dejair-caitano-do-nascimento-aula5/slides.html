Chapter 3: The Code

Always True, or True This Week?

The tense that tells people what is happening right now

Discover the Rule

Both Are True. What Is the Difference?

I work on nerve pain.  /  I am working on a new study this year.
She teaches pharmacology.  /  She is presenting in room two right now.
We collaborate with two hospitals.  /  We are collecting data until December.
He lives in Munich.  /  He is staying at the hotel next door this week.
Click to reveal the rule
The left column is the present simple: always true, your job, your field, your routine. The right column is the present continuous (am / is / are + verb + -ing): happening right now, or true only for a while — this week, this year, until December. At a congress you need both in the same breath: I work on leprosy, and at the moment I am collecting data in three centres.

Chapter 5: Practice

Stop Apologising for Your English

A voice message from somebody who did it for years

Listening 2

A Message from Ayse

Read the questions, then listen.

1. Where is she, and what is she doing while she speaks?
In the coffee area on the first floor, looking at him standing alone by the window with his phone.
2. Why does she say the table is his and not hers?
Because two of the four people there work on nerve damage.
3. What is the one thing she asks him to stop doing, and why?
Apologising for his English before he speaks. People do not hear the mistake, they hear the apology -- and then they doubt the science too.
