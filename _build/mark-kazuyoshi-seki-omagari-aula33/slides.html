Chapter 1: When Plans Break

The Day Something Went Wrong

A trip, a school day or a match. One thing went wrong. What caused it, and what happened next because of it?

Four Ways to Tell the Same Story

Same Rain, Four Connections

"It rained. We stayed in the hostel."

Two true facts, and no connection. The listener has to guess that one caused the other.

"We stayed in because of the rain."

Now the cause is named, and it is a noun: the rain.

"The rain was so heavy that the trains stopped."

The cause and how big it was, in one sentence.

"The trains stopped, which is why we stayed in."

The whole first half becomes the reason for the second.

A travel story is a chain: this happened, so that happened, which is why the next thing happened. Today is about the links in the chain.

Chapter 3: The Links

Cause Here, Result There

The words that connect what happened to why it happened

Grammar Discovery

Four Links in the Chain

All the flights were grounded because of the wind.

Due to the typhoon, the airport closed at two.

The downpour was so heavy that the trains stopped.

It was such a long delay that we missed our connection.

In two of them the cause is a noun. In the other two, one word measures how big the cause was. Which word goes with an adjective, and which with a noun?

LinkWhat follows itExample
because of / due to / owing toa noun (the cause as a thing)"because of the wind"
because / since / asa full clause: subject + verb"because the wind was too strong"
so + adjective / adverb + thathow big the cause was, then the result"so heavy that the trains stopped"
such (a) + adjective + noun + thatthe same, when there is a noun"such a long delay that..."
..., which is why + clausethe whole first sentence becomes the cause"It was overbooked, which is why we got a voucher."
lead to / result in + nounOne thing produces another: "The storm led to three hundred cancellations." Both are verbs, so they change tense: leads to, led to, will lead to.

The Tool

Three Places It Goes Wrong

Trap 1 · because of + noun, because + clause

"Because of it was raining" does not exist. Choose one: "because it was raining" (a clause) or "because of the rain" (a noun). Due to and owing to work like because of: always a noun after them.

Trap 2 · so with the adjective, such with the noun

"So a long delay" and "such heavy" do not exist. So goes with an adjective alone: "so long", "so heavy". Such goes with a noun: "such a long delay", "such bad weather".

Trap 3 · "so that" is not a result

So that means in order to: "I left early so that I would not miss the train" is a purpose. For a result, use so alone: "It rained all day, so we stayed in", never "so that we stayed in".

Get the link right and the listener follows the whole chain. Get it wrong and the story stops at the mistake.

Chapter 4: In Real Life

Three Days Late

A trip that went wrong, told by somebody who was there

Dialogue

Why Nina Came Home Late

N
Did I ever tell you why we got home three days late last summer?
M
No. I thought you just decided to stay longer.
N
I wish. A typhoon hit Osaka, and all the flights were grounded because of the wind.
M
So you were stranded at the airport?
N
For one night. The downpour was so heavy that the trains stopped too, which is why we slept on the floor of the terminal.
M
That sounds awful. Did the airline rebook you?
N
In the end. But we landed in Dubai too late, and that missed connection had a knock-on effect on everything else.
M
Did you at least get some compensation?
N
A voucher for dinner. It was such a good dinner that I almost forgave them, so we decided to make the best of it.
Comprehension

About Nina

1. Why were all the flights grounded?
Because of the wind from a typhoon that hit Osaka.
2. Why did she sleep on the floor of the terminal?
Because the downpour was so heavy that the trains stopped too, so there was no way to leave.
3. What did the airline give her, and how did she feel about it?
A voucher for dinner. It was such a good dinner that she almost forgave them, and they decided to make the best of it.
Listening

Linking Cause and Result

Your teacher on the links and the three traps. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What comes after "because of" and "due to", and what comes after "because"?
A noun after "because of" and "due to"; a full clause, with a subject and a verb, after "because".
2. How does she explain the difference between "so" and "such"?
"So" goes with an adjective alone ("so long"); "such" goes with a noun ("such a long delay").
3. Why is "It rained, so that we stayed in" wrong?
Because "so that" means "in order to" — it is a purpose, not a result.
4. What does "which is why" do to the first half of the sentence?
It turns the whole first sentence into the cause of what comes next.

One Link at a Time

Spot the Error

Fix the Link

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "We were stuck because of the flight was cancelled."
"We were stuck because the flight was cancelled." (a full clause takes because; because of needs a noun: "because of the cancellation")
2. "It was so a long delay that we missed the connection."
"It was such a long delay that we missed the connection." (a noun is coming, so such)
3. "The downpour was such heavy that nobody went out."
"The downpour was so heavy that nobody went out." (an adjective alone takes so)
4. "It rained all day, so that we stayed in the hostel."
"It rained all day, so we stayed in the hostel." (so that is a purpose, not a result)
5. "Due to it was overbooked, they gave us a voucher."
"Because it was overbooked, they gave us a voucher." (or "Due to the overbooking" — due to takes a noun)
