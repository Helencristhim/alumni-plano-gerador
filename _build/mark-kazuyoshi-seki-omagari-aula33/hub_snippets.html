Lesson 33 -- Pre-class

Because of the Rain... -- Cause and Result

"It rained. We stayed in." Two true facts, and no link between them. This lesson gives you the links that turn facts into a story -- because of, due to, so ... that, such ... that, which is why, lead to -- and you use them to explain what happens when a trip goes wrong. Key words: cancellation, stranded, overbooked, missed connection, rebook, compensation, voucher, downpour, grounded, knock-on effect, make the best of, refund.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. These are the words you need when a trip does not go to plan.

Cancellation -- when a flight or a booking is officially stopped and will not happen
"There were three hundred cancellations at the airport that afternoon."
Stranded -- stuck somewhere with no way of leaving
"We were stranded at the airport for one whole night."
Overbooked -- with more tickets sold than there are seats
"The flight was overbooked, so two people could not get on."
Missed connection -- when you arrive too late to catch your next flight
"The delay meant a missed connection in Dubai."
Rebook -- to book something again, usually for a different time
"The airline had to rebook everybody on the next morning's flights."
Compensation -- money or something useful you receive because something went wrong
"Did you at least get some compensation for the lost day?"
Voucher -- a piece of paper or a code you can use instead of money
"They gave us a voucher for dinner at the airport."
Downpour -- a lot of very heavy rain in a short time
"The downpour was so heavy that the trains stopped."
Grounded -- kept on the ground and not allowed to fly
"Every afternoon flight was grounded because of the wind."
Knock-on effect -- a result that causes another result, and then another
"One small delay had a knock-on effect on the whole trip."
Make the best of -- to enjoy a bad situation as much as you can, because you cannot change it
"We could not fly, so we decided to make the best of it."
Refund -- money that is given back to you when you do not get what you paid for
"If you prefer not to travel, you can ask for a full refund."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read Nina's story and answer the questions. Notice how each link in bold connects a cause to a result.

Last summer Nina came home from Japan three days late, and none of it was her fault. On the last Friday of the trip, a typhoon hit Osaka. Because of the wind, every flight that afternoon was grounded.

The downpour was so heavy that the trains stopped too, which is why she and her family spent the night on the floor of the terminal. It was such a long night that nobody really slept.

The airline rebooked them for Sunday, but due to the delay they missed their connection in Dubai, and that single missed connection led to two more days of waiting.

In the end, since they could not change anything, they decided to make the best of it. They got a voucher for dinner, and it was such a good dinner that Nina almost forgave the airline.

1. "Because of the wind, every flight was grounded." Why is it "because of" and not "because"?
A Because "the wind" is a noun, and "because of" is followed by a noun.
B Because the sentence is in the past.
C Because "because" can never start a sentence.
2. "The downpour was so heavy that the trains stopped." What does "so ... that" tell you?
A That the trains stopped in order to avoid the rain.
B How heavy the rain was, and what that heaviness caused.
C That the rain and the trains had nothing to do with each other.
3. Why is it "such a long night" and not "so a long night"?
A Because "night" is a noun, and "such" goes with a noun.
B Because "long" is a short adjective.
C Because "so" is only used in questions.
4. "...which is why she spent the night on the floor." What is the cause of her night on the floor?
A The voucher for dinner.
B The missed connection in Dubai.
C Everything in the first half of the sentence: the heavy rain that stopped the trains.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Linking Cause and Result

GRAMMAR

A story is a chain of causes and results. The word you choose depends on what comes after it: a noun, a full clause, an adjective or a noun phrase.

LinkWhat follows itExample
because of / due to / owing toa noun"The flight was cancelled due to the typhoon."
because / since / asa full clause (subject + verb)"We stayed in because the trains had stopped."
so + adjective + thathow big the cause was, then the result"The rain was so heavy that nobody went out."
such (a) + adjective + noun + thatthe same, when there is a noun"It was such a long delay that we missed the connection."
..., which is why + clausethe whole first sentence becomes the cause"It was overbooked, which is why we got a voucher."
lead to / result in + nounone thing produces another"The storm led to three hundred cancellations."
Three trapsNever "because of it was raining" -- a clause takes because. Never "so a long delay" or "such heavy" -- so with the adjective, such with the noun. Never "so that" for a result -- so that means in order to.

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen to each sentence, then record yourself saying it. In "so ... that" and "such ... that" the stress falls on the word that measures the cause: SO long, SUCH a bad day. "That" is weak and fast.

Our flight was cancelled because of the storm.
The delay was so long that we missed our connection.
It was such a bad day that we just stayed in.
They overbooked the flight, which is why we got a voucher.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the sentence that says exactly what you mean.

Which sentence is correct?
A "We were late because of the bus broke down."
B "We were late because the bus broke down."
C "We were late due to the bus broke down."
Which sentence is correct?
A "It was so a long queue that we gave up."
B "It was such long queue that we gave up."
C "It was such a long queue that we gave up."
You want to say what the rain CAUSED. Which one is right?
A "It rained all morning, so we cancelled the match."
B "It rained all morning, so that we cancelled the match."
C "It rained all morning, such we cancelled the match."
Your flight is cancelled and you are given money back. What did you get?
A A layover.
B A refund.
C A missed connection.
Your first flight was late, so you missed the second one, so you lost a hotel night. That was:
A a knock-on effect.
B a voucher.
C a downpour.
Which sentence uses "which is why" correctly?
A "Which is why the airport was closed, we slept there."
B "The airport was closed, which is why we slept there."
C "The airport was closed which is why, we slept there."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the prompt below. There is no right or wrong answer — bring the recording to class.

Think of a real day when one small thing went wrong and caused everything else -- a trip, a school day, a match or a family plan. Tell the story from the very first cause to the last result. Use at least four different links: because of or due to, because, so ... that or such ... that, and which is why. Finish by saying how you made the best of it, or what you would do differently now.
