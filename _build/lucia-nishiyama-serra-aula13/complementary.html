Lesson 13 -- Third Conditional: The Decision You Would Make Differently

YouTube · BBC Learning English
How to use the third conditional -- 6 Minute Grammar

Six minutes on exactly what you practiced tonight: how "if" plus "had" plus a past participle sets up a past that never happened, and how "would have" plus a past participle delivers the imagined result. Two BBC presenters build the pattern live, at natural speed, and drill the contracted forms you will actually hear in a meeting.

Tip: watch it twice. The second time, pause after each example and say one sentence about a real decision of your own: "If we had ..., we would have ..."

YouTube · BBC Learning English
The third conditional -- Conditionals with Georgie

A short, recent lesson that puts the third conditional beside the second one, which is the exact choice you made in class: an imagined present takes "would plus verb", an imagined past takes "would have plus a past participle". It also names the trap directly -- "would" never belongs inside the "if" half.

Tip: after each of her examples, rewrite it as a second conditional out loud. If the meaning moves from March to today, you have understood the difference.

TED Talk · Decision-Making
Kathryn Schulz: On being wrong

A journalist explains why being wrong feels exactly like being right until the moment you find out, which is the cleanest description of a blind spot you will hear. It is the natural context for tonight's language: assumption, hindsight, fallout and the question of whether to second-guess a decision made with the information available at the time.

Tip: turn on English subtitles, then record two minutes on one assumption of your own that turned out to be wrong, and what would have happened if you had tested it.
