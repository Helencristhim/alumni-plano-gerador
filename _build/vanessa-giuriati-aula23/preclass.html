Lesson 23 -- Pre-class

Making and Responding to Requests

Ask for things the long way round: "Could you...?", "Would you mind + -ing?" and "I was wondering if you could..." — plus how to say no early, with one reason. Key words: bother, impose, short notice, swamped, bear with me, sort out, owe, turn down.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Bother -- To interrupt or disturb someone who is busy
"Sorry to bother you — do you have a minute?"
Impose -- To ask too much of someone else's time or help
"I do not want to impose, but could you look at this?"
Short notice -- Very little warning before something happens
"I know this is short notice, but can we meet today?"
Swamped -- Extremely busy, with far too much to do
"I am completely swamped this week."
Bear with me -- Please be patient with me for a moment
"Bear with me — I am almost done."
Sort out -- To fix or organize something that is a problem
"I will sort it out before Friday."
Owe -- To need to give something back to someone
"Thank you — I owe you one."
Turn down -- To refuse an offer or a request, usually politely
"I had to turn down the invitation."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

On Thursday evening Daniel wrote to me: "Sorry to bother you this late. I know it is short notice, but the client moved the call to Friday. I was wondering if you could take it. I do not want to impose — if you are swamped, just turn me down and I will sort it out." I read it twice. Would you mind is easy to refuse, and so is that message, which is exactly why I said yes. I answered: "Bear with me — let me check my calendar." Ten minutes later I took the call, and he wrote back: "I owe you one."

1. Why does Daniel say "if you are swamped, just turn me down"?
A Because he does not really need the help.
B Because he wants to give her an easy way to say no.
C Because he is angry about the change.
2. What does "Bear with me" mean here?
A Please be patient for a moment while I check.
B I cannot help you at all.
C Please send me the file.
3. Which request is the softest?
A "Send me the report."
B "Could you send the report?"
C "I was wondering if you could take the call."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The long way round

GRAMMAR

The longer the request, the more room the other person has to say no — and the more likely they are to say yes.

StructureUseExample
Could you + base verb?Everyday polite — safe with anyoneCould you send the report, please?
Would you mind + verb-ing?More polite; easy to refuseWould you mind sending the report?
I was wondering if you could + base verbThe softest — for a big favor or a busy personI was wondering if you could look at this today.
I would love to, but ...A clean no that keeps the door openI would love to, but I am swamped this week.

'Would you mind' takes the -ing form: 'Would you mind sending?', never 'to send'. 'I was wondering' takes if you could plus the base verb. And after 'sorry to', use the base verb: 'Sorry to bother you'.

Stage 2: Make a Request in Order

Order

Listen first, then put the steps of a request that lands in the right order.

?First, I check whether it is a good moment: sorry to bother you, do you have a minute?
?After that, I make the request the long way round: I was wondering if you could take the call.
?Then I name the situation honestly, including the fact that it is short notice.
?In the end, I say thank you and I admit that I owe them one.
?Next, I give the other person a way out: if you are swamped, just turn me down.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

You need a big favor from a colleague who is very busy. Best choice:
A "Send me the numbers today."
B "I was wondering if you could send me the numbers today."
C "You must send me the numbers today."
You want to ask politely and make it easy to refuse. Best choice:
A "Would you mind sending the file?"
B "Would you mind to send the file?"
C "Do you mind send the file?"
You have to refuse an invitation from your manager. Best choice:
A "No."
B "I would love to, but I am swamped until Thursday. Could I join the second half?"
C "Maybe. I do not know. We will see."
You need a moment to check your calendar before answering. Best choice:
A "Bear with me — let me check my calendar."
B "Bear me with, let me check my calendar."
C "Wait there quiet, please."
