Chapter 3: Where Was I?

I Was Following You Until...

Getting back into a conversation that kept moving

Grammar Discovery

Where Did You Lose It?

"I was following you until you mentioned Brussels."

"You were saying that the banks are not ready."

"What were you saying about the testing phase?"

"Sorry, I was thinking about your first point when you moved on."

Are the red actions finished, or were they in progress at a moment in the past? What stopped them?

UseFormExample
An action in progress at a past momentwas / were + verb-ingI was following you. · You were saying that...
Interrupted by a short actionpast continuous + when / until + past simpleI was following you until you mentioned Brussels.
Asking to go backWhat were you saying about...?What were you saying about the testing phase?
Restarting after a breakWe were talking about...Where were we? We were talking about the deadline.
The trapno -ing with understand, knowNot I was understanding. Say I was with you until...

In one line: the past continuous shows where the conversation was when you lost it, so the other person knows exactly where to go back to.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Fast English, Tired Ears

A German banker in the corridor, a coach, and a schedule change

Dialogue

Off the Record, Four O'Clock

K
Fabio, right? From the panel this morning. Look, off the record, half of the banks in Europe are not ready for the new ISO 20022 rules, and the regulators know it.
F
That's a lot to process. Give me a second to think through the implications. Are you saying that the deadline could move?
K
Not move, exactly. The deadline stays, but the enforcement gets softer. Banks will run the old and the new format in parallel for another year, maybe two.
F
I want to make sure I'm tracking this correctly. You were saying that the old format stays alive longer?
K
Exactly. And for a bank like yours, that changes the cost of the whole project. Sorry, I'm talking very fast. It's day two.
F
Don't worry. I was following you until you mentioned the cost. Is it the price of the software that worries you, or the time?
K
It's the time. Two formats means two sets of tests. What about Brazil? Are your regulators in the same place?
F
In Brazil, the central bank has pushed instant payments very hard, so our clients expect real-time settlement. It's a different kind of pressure.
K
That's fascinating. Listen, I have a side conversation with our compliance team in five minutes. Can we pick up where we left off tomorrow?
F
Absolutely. And please keep me posted on the enforcement question, so I can stay in the loop. Coffee area at nine?
Comprehension

About Katrin

1. What does Katrin say off the record about the banks in Europe?
Half of them are not ready for the new ISO 20022 rules, and the regulators know it.
2. What does she think will happen to the deadline?
The deadline stays, but the enforcement gets softer. Banks will run the old and the new format in parallel for one or two more years.
3. What worries her most, and what does she suggest at the end?
The time: two formats means two sets of tests. She suggests picking up where they left off the next day.
Listening 1 · The Coach

Three Rules for Day Two

A communication coach explains how to survive the second day. Sound first — no text.

1. What changes between day one and the afternoon of day two?
On day one the listening is slow. By day two it warms up, but the brain is full: the problem is cognitive load.
2. Where should the important meetings go on day two?
Before lunch. He says to protect your morning.
3. What should you do if you lose the thread, and how long should you pause before a hard answer?
Say so early, with a clear phrase. Pause about two seconds: to the other person, it sounds like thinking.
