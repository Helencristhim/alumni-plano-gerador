Chapter 3: The Code

Never Have I Seen

Putting the surprise at the front of the sentence

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Never have I seen a city organize itself so quietly."

"Not only did the food surprise me, but the silence did too."

"Rarely does a trip change what you do when you get home."

"Only when I stopped planning did I start enjoying it."

Each sentence begins with a negative or limiting expression, and then something strange happens to the verb. What is it, and where have you seen it before?

FormWhat it saysExample
Never / Rarely / Seldom + auxiliary + subjectThe negative word moves to the front, and the sentence takes question order after it."Never have I seen anything like it."
NOT "Never I have seen"
Not only... but alsoTwo surprises in one sentence. The first half inverts, the second half does not."Not only did the food surprise me, but the silence did too."
Only when / Only after / Not untilThe inversion lands on the MAIN clause, not on the only when part."Only when I stopped planning did I start enjoying it."
Where the auxiliary comes fromIf the sentence has no auxiliary, English borrows do, does or did — exactly as in a question."I rarely travel alone" → "Rarely do I travel alone."
When to use itOnce or twice in a story, never three times. It is a spotlight, and a spotlight left on is just a lamp.Ordinary: "I have never seen that."
Spotlight: "Never have I seen that."

You already know this order — it is the order of a question. The whole trick is that the negative word at the front tells English to use it.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"Never I have seen a city like this one."
"Never have I seen a city like this one."
"Not only the food surprised me, but also the silence."
"Not only did the food surprise me, but the silence did too."
"Only when I stopped planning I started to enjoy it."
"Only when I stopped planning did I start enjoying it."

After the negative word the sentence takes question order: auxiliary first, then the subject. In the second pair the auxiliary did appears and the main verb goes back to its plain form — did the food surprise, never did the food surprised. And with only when the inversion lands on the SECOND half of the sentence. Read the correct versions aloud twice.

Chapter 4: Breakfast in Copenhagen

Breakfast in Copenhagen

The woman at the next table has been to Japan four times

Dialogue

The Table Next to the Window

I
You were at the harbour yesterday, were you not? I saw you taking a photograph of the bicycles.
B
I was. Never have I seen a city where everybody gets around on a bicycle, including the ministers.
I
Wait until you see Tokyo. Are you going?
B
Possibly next year. My wife has been asking for ten years.
I
Then take her. I have been four times, and not only did the food surprise me, but the silence did too.
B
The silence?
I
A packed station at eight in the morning and nobody talking. It is the culture shock nobody warns you about.
B
Then that is the part I want. Where would you go that is off the beaten track?
Comprehension

About Ingrid

1. Where had Ingrid seen Braulio the day before, and what was he doing?
At the harbour, taking a photograph of the bicycles.
2. How many times has she been to Japan?
Four times.
3. What two things surprised her there?
The food and the silence.
4. What does she describe as the culture shock nobody warns you about?
A packed station at eight in the morning with nobody talking.
Listening

Gate Change

An airport announcement. Sound first -- no text.

1. Which gate has the flight moved to, and when does boarding now begin?
Gate thirty-two, and boarding begins at nine forty.
2. What has changed for passengers with a connecting flight in Doha?
Their layover is now under two hours.
3. Who may board first?
Passengers traveling with small children.
Listening 2

Where the Tourists Are Not

A travel podcast segment. Sound first -- no text.

1. Why does she say not to look for the hidden gem?
Because it is in every guide and it is packed by ten in the morning.
2. What does she suggest doing instead?
Picking one ordinary thing you do at home and doing it there, like buying bread or sitting in a park.
3. What is her final sentence?
Only when you stop performing the trip does the trip start.

The Habit

One Scene, Out Loud

Before the next class, choose one photograph from a trip and describe it out loud in English, to nobody, for sixty seconds. One scene, one surprise, one inversion.
