People and Places

The locals you meet on a trip, and how they behave. Key words: a local, custom, gesture, misunderstanding, hospitality, reserved, expressive, encounter. Structure: adverbs of manner -- she explained the custom clearly, you speak English really well.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A local -- a person who lives in the place you are visiting
"A local showed me the way to the market."
Custom -- something people in a place always do, a tradition
"It is the custom here to say good morning when you enter a shop."
Gesture -- a movement of your hands or head that says something
"In Italy, every sentence comes with a gesture."
Misunderstanding -- when two people understand different things, and nobody notices
"It was a small misunderstanding, and in the end we both laughed."
Hospitality -- being warm and generous with visitors and guests
"Their hospitality surprised me: they invited me to dinner."
Reserved -- quiet and slow to show feelings, especially with strangers
"People in the north are more reserved at first."
Expressive -- showing feelings clearly, with the face, the voice and the hands
"She is very expressive: you understand her even without words."
Encounter -- a meeting with somebody that you did not plan
"My best encounter of the trip was with a fisherman at six in the morning."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On my last trip I had a small encounter at a fish market. The woman at the stall was very expressive. She talked quickly, laughed loudly and moved her hands all the time. When I touched a fish, she stopped me gently and explained the custom clearly: you point, and she chooses.

Her brother was the opposite. He was reserved and looked serious at first, and he answered my questions briefly. But on Saturday he gave me the best fish of the morning and said: "You speak English well. Come back next year."

1. The text says "she stopped me gently". What does "gently" describe?
A The woman herself, and what kind of person she is in general, at home and at work.
B The fish at the stall.
C How she stopped him.
2. Why does the text say "he looked serious" and not "he looked seriously"?
A Because "seriously" is a word that only exists in British English, not in American English.
B Because after look, seem and be, English uses the adjective.
C Because it is in the past.
3. The brother says "You speak English well". Why not "good"?
A Because "well" is the adverb of "good", and it says how you speak.
B Because "good" can only be used to talk about food and the weather.
C Because he is speaking very formally to a foreigner he does not know.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Adverbs of Manner

GRAMMAR

An adjective describes a person. An adverb describes HOW the person does something.

FormRuleExample
adjective + lymost adverbs of mannerslow → slowly · warm → warmly
-y → -ily / -le → -lysmall spelling changeshappy → happily · gentle → gently
Irregulargood → well · fast → fast · hard → hard. Never goodly or fastly.
PositionAfter the verb, or after the verb and its object: She spoke quietly. · He explained the custom clearly.
Strongervery / really / quite + adverb: He spoke really slowly.
The trapAfter be, look, seem, use the adjective: He looked serious. And friendly ends in -ly but is an adjective.

Telling a story about a person is mostly two things: what they said, and HOW they did it. "He said: Come back next year" -- and he said it warmly.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are meeting locals on a trip. Choose the best thing to say.

A shop owner speaks English to you, and you want to say something nice about it. You say:
A "You speak English very good, better than me."
B "You speak English really goodly."
C "You speak English really well."
You touched the fish, and the woman at the stall looks surprised. You say:
A "Sorry, I think there was a small hospitality here with the fish."
B "Sorry, I think there was a small misunderstanding."
C "Sorry, it was a custom."
A family in a small town invites you to dinner on the first day. Later, you tell a friend:
A "Their hospitality was incredible."
B "Their custom was very reserved, and they invited me to dinner on the first night."
C "Their gesture was very expressive."
The coffee shop owner was quiet and did not smile at first. You describe him:
A "He was quite reservedly at first, but he became very friendly later in the week."
B "He was quite reserved at first."
C "He was quite expressive at first."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think of one encounter with a local person on a trip, in any country. Take about ninety seconds. Say where you met, how the person behaved, and one thing they said to you, exactly. Use at least three adverbs (slowly, warmly, clearly...), three words from this lesson, and start with "Once in a while, you meet someone who..." Do not stop to correct yourself.
