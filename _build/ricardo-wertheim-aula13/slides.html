Chapter 3: The Code

Not Who, But How

slowly · warmly · clearly · well

Grammar Discovery

What Do the Orange Words Do?

"She explained the custom clearly."

"He spoke very quietly and smiled warmly."

"The waiter reacted quite unexpectedly."

"You speak English really well."

What do the orange words describe: the person, or HOW the person does something? How do you make most of them? And the last one — why is it different?

FormRuleExample
adjective + lyMost adverbs of manner. They say HOW somebody does something.slow → slowly · warm → warmly · clear → clearly
-y → -ilyThe y changes to i.happy → happily · easy → easily
-le → -lyDrop the e and add y.gentle → gently · simple → simply
Irregulargood → well · fast → fast · hard → hard · late → late. Never goodly or fastly.
PositionUsually after the verb, or after the verb and its object: She spoke quietly. · He cooks the fish simply. To make it stronger: very / really / quite slowly.
The trapfriendly, lovely, lonely end in -ly but are adjectives. And after be, look, seem use the adjective: She seemed reserved. He looked happy — never he looked happily.

An adjective describes the person. An adverb describes what the person does.

Chapter 4: A Market in Split

You Point, I Choose

A fish stall, an old custom and a very patient local

Dialogue

At the Fish Stall

A
Good morning! You are looking at the sardines very carefully. Are you a chef?
R
No, just a curious tourist. Can I choose the fish myself?
A
You point, and I choose. That is the custom in this market. Locals never touch the fish.
R
Oh, I am sorry! I did not know. I hope it was only a small misunderstanding.
A
It is fine. You asked politely. Most tourists just grab a fish and pay quickly.
R
And how do you cook sardines at home?
A
Very simply: salt, olive oil and a hot grill. My grandmother used to say: Fish swims three times, in the sea, in the oil and in the wine.
R
That is a wonderful way to say it.
A
Come back on Saturday at seven. My brother brings the best fish, and I can introduce you. That is our hospitality.
Comprehension

About Ana

1. What is the custom at Ana's stall?
The customer points, and Ana chooses. Locals never touch the fish.
2. What do most tourists do, according to Ana?
They just grab a fish and pay quickly.
3. What did Ana's grandmother use to say?
Fish swims three times: in the sea, in the oil and in the wine.
The Note

What Ana Wrote Down

Pazar fish market · stall 14

Saturday, 7:00. Come early, the best fish goes fast.
Ask for Ivo, my brother. Say: Ana sent me.
Do not touch the fish! Point, and he chooses.
Bring a bag. We do not use plastic here.

— Ana

Chapter 5: Practice

Two Travelers, Two Encounters

A quiet coffee shop in Ljubljana, and a market where yes means no

Listening

Slow to Open the Door

A traveler and one very reserved coffee shop owner. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. How did the owner behave on the first day?
He served him silently, and he didn't look at him once.
2. What did the owner ask on the third day, and how?
He asked where he was from, in careful English.
3. What did the traveler learn that week?
People in some places are not cold, they are just slow to open the door. If you come back patiently, the door opens.
Listening 2

When Yes Means No

A traveler and a misunderstanding at a market in Sofia. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What did she ask the woman at the market, and what did the woman do?
She asked if the tomatoes were fresh. The woman shook her head.
2. What can a shake of the head mean in Bulgaria?
It can mean yes. And a nod can mean no.
3. What is her advice when a gesture confuses you?
Ask politely, with words. Locals are usually happy to explain, and the misunderstanding becomes a conversation.
