Ready for the Meeting -- Verb + -ing or Verb + to

Preparing an international meeting with two verb patterns: verb + -ing (avoid, consider, suggest, postpone, practice) and verb + to + base verb (plan, decide, agree, manage, offer). New words: postpone, avoid, consider, suggest, practice, look forward to, manage to, hesitate, afford, tend, decline, offer.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example.

Postpone -- to move something to a later time
"Let's postpone discussing the bonus until May."
Avoid -- to stay away from doing something
"I avoid scheduling calls late on Friday."
Consider -- to think carefully about doing something
"We are considering hiring two more recruiters."
Suggest -- to propose an idea for others to think about
"I suggest starting with the turnover data."
Practice -- to do something many times to get better
"I practice presenting before every big meeting."
Look forward to -- to feel happy about something in the future
"I look forward to meeting the Sydney team."
Manage to -- to succeed in doing something difficult
"We managed to finish the report on time."
Hesitate -- to stop for a moment because you are not sure
"Please don't hesitate to ask questions."
Afford -- to be able to accept a cost or a risk
"We can't afford to lose our best people."
Tend -- to usually do something
"Australian executives tend to speak quite fast."
Decline -- to say no politely
"She declined to give a date for the decision."
Offer -- to say you are willing to do something
"I offered to send a summary after the call."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Tomorrow I have a call with the Sydney team, so today I am preparing. First, I decided to write one clear goal. Then I practiced saying my key points out loud. My colleague Ethan suggested starting with the turnover data, and I agreed. We want to avoid discussing the bonus policy, so we will postpone it until the numbers are final. Australian executives tend to speak fast, but I will not hesitate to ask them to repeat. We cannot afford to misunderstand a decision. I offered to send the agenda today, and I really look forward to meeting the team.

1. Which form comes after "suggest" and "avoid"?
A to + base verb, as in suggested to start with the data.
B The base verb only.
C The -ing form, as in suggested starting.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "I decided to write one clear goal."
B "I decided write one clear goal for the call."
C "I decided writing one clear goal."
3. Why is it "look forward to meeting" and not "look forward to meet"?
A Because it is a past action that happened yesterday.
B Because to is a preposition here, so -ing follows.
C Because meet is an irregular verb.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Verb Patterns (-ing / to)

GRAMMAR

Some verbs are followed by -ing (avoid, consider, suggest, postpone, practice). Other verbs are followed by to + base verb (plan, decide, agree, need, manage, offer). After a preposition (look forward to, before, after), always use -ing.

PatternVerbsExample
verb + -ingavoid, consider, suggest, postpone, practiceI suggest starting with the data.
verb + to + base verbplan, decide, agree, need, manage, offerWe decided to hire two people.
preposition + -inglook forward to, before, afterI look forward to meeting you.
negativenot + -ing / not + toI decided not to postpone it.

Tip: learn each verb with its pattern, as one chunk: suggest + -ing, decide + to. In look forward to, the word to is a preposition, so the next verb takes -ing.
