Lesson 17 -- Pre-class

Which Way to the Palace?

How to ask for and give directions in a new city. Key words: crossroads, roundabout, block, signpost, platform, get lost, head for, shortcut. Structure: the imperative with along, across, through and past -- walk along the harbor, go through the gate.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Crossroads -- a place where two roads meet and cross each other
"Turn right at the next crossroads."
Roundabout -- a round place where roads meet and cars drive around in a circle
"Take the second exit at the roundabout."
Block -- the part of a street from one cross street to the next
"The museum is two blocks from here."
Signpost -- a sign at the side of a road that shows the way to a place
"Follow the signposts for the old town."
Platform -- the place in a station where you wait for the train or the bus
"The bus to Zagreb leaves from platform 4."
To get lost -- to not know where you are or how to find your way
"I always get lost in old towns."
To head for -- to go in the direction of a place
"We are heading for the harbor."
Shortcut -- a shorter and faster way to go somewhere
"There is a shortcut through the park."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Welcome to Piran! Here is the way from the bus station to the apartment. Walk along the sea, toward the big square. Go past the small harbor with the fishing boats.

At the square, turn left at the statue and walk through the narrow street next to the church. Don't take the stairs on the right: they go up to the old walls. Our door is the green one, two blocks after the church. You can't miss it!

1. "Walk along the sea." What does along tell you?
A Go into the sea and swim to the other side, then walk to the big square.
B Stop at the sea.
C Follow the line of the sea, next to it.
2. Why does the message say "Don't take the stairs" and not "You don't take the stairs"?
A Because it is an instruction, and instructions use the plain verb with no person.
B Because the writer forgot a word, and the correct sentence needs you at the start.
C Because the stairs are old.
3. "Turn left at the statue." Why at?
A Because the statue is very big and you can see it from far away, from the bus station.
B Because at is the small word for the point where you turn.
C Because the statue is inside the church.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Giving Directions

GRAMMAR

The plain verb, and four small words that show how you move.

FormRuleExample
verb, with no personno you, no to, no mustWalk along the river. · Turn right.
Don't + verbthe negativeDon't take the bridge.
along / acrossfollow the line of it / from one side to the otheralong the harbor · across the square
through / pastinto one side and out of the other / go by it, and do not stopthrough the gate · past the hotel
turn ... atat for the point where you turnTurn left at the crossroads.
The trapNever go until the church. Say go to the church, or walk until you see the church.

One verb, one small word, one place: walk along the harbor. That is a direction.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are asking for and giving directions in a new city. Choose the best thing to say.

A tourist asks for the cathedral. It is very near and easy to see. You say:
A "You don't can miss it, because it is very near of the square, on the left side."
B "Is near."
C "You can't miss it. It's right next to the square."
You are not sure you are walking the right way. You ask:
A "Am I heading in the right direction?"
B "I am going in the correct way or not, please, can you say me?"
C "Where am I go?"
The shop is on the other side of the road. You say:
A "Go along the road at the traffic light, and then the shop is there for you."
B "Go across the road at the traffic light."
C "Go past the road."
Someone asks: Is it far? The answer is different for different people. You say:
A "It is depending of from where you come, because the city is very big."
B "Depends where."
C "It depends on where you're coming from."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

A friend is in Sao Paulo for the first time and calls you. He wants to walk from one famous place to another, and he is lost. Take about ninety seconds and explain the whole route, step by step. Use at least five directions with the plain verb, four words like along, across, through and past, and "It depends on where you're coming from" once. Do not stop to correct yourself.
