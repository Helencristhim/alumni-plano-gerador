Chapter 3: It Is

The Two Words the Sky Needs

it is · it rains · it will be

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"It is cold this morning."

"It is raining."

"It rains a lot in London."

"It will be hot tomorrow."

PatternUseExample
It is + adjectivethe weather nowIt is cold. / It is hot.
It is + verb-inghappening right nowIt is raining.
It rains / it snowsin general, alwaysIt rains a lot in London.
It will be + adjectivetomorrowIt will be hot tomorrow.

That it means nothing and points at nobody — but English cannot drop it. Portuguese says chove; English needs it rains, always two words.

The Four in the Lift

Four Sentences for the Most Banal Subject in the World

"It is very cold today, isn't it?"

the opening — and everybody in England says it

"Yes, and it is raining again!"

you agree and you ADD one thing — never just yes

"Is it always like this in October?"

you ask — and now they talk for two minutes

"What is the weather like tomorrow?"

the practical one: it decides the coats

Dialogue

Four Seconds in the Lift

C
Morning. Terrible weather today, isn't it?
P
Good morning. Yes, it is! And it is raining again.
C
It has not stopped since Tuesday. Are you here on holiday?
P
Yes, with my family. Is it always like this in October?
C
Pretty much. Half the days, if you are lucky. Nobody stays at home for it, though.
P
Sorry, I don't understand. Can you say that again, please?
C
Sorry. It rains a lot, but people go out anyway.
P
I see. What is the weather like tomorrow? We want to go to the park with the children.
C
Tomorrow is better. Sunny, but cold in the morning.
P
So a coat for the children, and no umbrella?
C
Coats, definitely. And take the umbrella anyway. This is England.
P
That is good advice. And you? Do you live here?
C
Thirty years, and I still complain about the weather every morning. Have a good day.
Comprehension

What Did Colin Say?

1. How often does it rain in October, according to Colin?
About half the days — but nobody stays at home for it.
2. What is the weather like tomorrow?
Sunny, but cold in the morning.
3. What is his advice about the umbrella?
Take it anyway — because this is England.
Listening

The Forecast on the Radio

The weather report, in the morning. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. When does the rain arrive today, and what should you take?
In the afternoon — take an umbrella if you go out after two.
2. How cold is it tomorrow at eight in the morning?
Only seven degrees — put a coat on the children before school.
3. What is Sunday like?
Warm for October, twelve to nineteen degrees, no rain at all.
Listening 2

The Trip Might Be Cancelled

The tour company calls about the weather on Saturday. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. When do they decide, and what do they do?
On Friday evening at six — they call everybody.
2. If they cancel, what are the two options?
Come on Sunday at the same time, or get all the money back.
3. Why is an umbrella no use, and what should she bring?
It is no use on the boat — bring a coat with a hood.

Chapter 6: Your Turn

From Guided to Free

the lift · the forecast · the decision

Role-Play — Guided

Four Seconds in the Lift

Scenario: A stranger comments on the weather. Answer, add one thing, and ask something back. Keep it going until the lift arrives.

Keywords:

Yes, it is!and it is rainingIs it always like this?And you?
Role-Play — Semi-free

Decide the Coats

Scenario: Read tomorrow's forecast to me, and then decide out loud: what do you wear, what do the children wear, and do you take an umbrella?

Keywords:

It will be...in the morninga coat for...an umbrella
Role-Play — Free

Twenty Minutes at the Bus Stop

Scenario: You are waiting for the bus in the rain with another parent. There is nothing to do and nothing to solve. Talk about the weather, and let it become a conversation about anything.

No keywords this time. Two minutes, no stopping. Different from the model is not wrong — if they understood you, it worked.
