Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

More, Less, Better -- Comparing Cities, Architecture and Travel Experiences

Comparing two cities and defending the opinion. Key words: underrated, compact, overwhelming, accessible, authentic, well-preserved, vibrant, iconic. Structure: comparatives and superlatives with short and long adjectives, plus the irregular forms good, bad and far.

Vocabulary

Underrated -- not as famous as it deserves to be

Compact -- small enough to walk across in a short time

Overwhelming -- so big or intense that it is hard to take it all in
"In August the main street is completely overwhelming."
Accessible -- easy to reach, or easy to understand and enjoy
"The lookout point is beautiful, but it is not very accessible."
Authentic -- real and traditional, not made for tourists
"The food there is more authentic than anything on the coast."
Well-preserved -- old but still in very good condition
"The old town is better preserved than almost any city I know."
Vibrant -- full of colour, energy and life
"It is quieter in the day, but far more vibrant in the evening."
Iconic -- instantly recognised as the symbol of a place
"The walls are the most iconic thing on the whole coast."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

People always ask me which one is better, Dubrovnik or Ljubljana. Dubrovnik is the more famous of the two, and the walls are the most iconic thing on the coast. The old town is better preserved than almost any city I have visited.

But it is also more crowded than I expected. Ljubljana is smaller, greener and much more compact. It is far less famous, so it is cheaper, and the food is more authentic than anything I ate on the coast. It is not as dramatic as Dubrovnik, but it is the most underrated capital in Europe.

1. The text says "smaller" but "more compact". Why the different form?
A Because "small" is a short word and takes -er, while "compact" is longer and takes "more".
B Because one is a city and the other is a building.
C Because "compact" has no comparative form in English.
2. Why does the text say "the most underrated capital" and not just "most underrated capital"?
A Because the writer is comparing only two cities.
B Because a superlative always needs "the" in front of it.
C Because "underrated" is an irregular adjective.
3. The text says the old town is "better preserved", not "more well preserved". Why?
A Because "preserved" is a short word.
B Because the writer is talking about the past.
C Because "well" is irregular: it becomes "better", never "more well".

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Comparatives and Superlatives

GRAMMAR

Count the syllables and the rule chooses itself.

AdjectiveComparativeSuperlative
Short (1 syllable)
small, green, old, cheap		+ er · smaller thanthe smallest
Ending in -y
easy, busy, pretty		y becomes ier · easier thanthe easiest
Long (2+ syllables)
crowded, iconic, authentic		more crowded thanthe most crowded
Spelling trap
big, hot, thin		double the last letter · biggerthe biggest
Irregulargood → better → the best · bad → worse → the worst · far → further → the furthest

Three things to keep: after a comparative comes than (never that); before a superlative comes the; and one ending is enough — more cheaper does not exist.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are comparing places at a dinner table. Choose the best thing to say.

You want to say Ljubljana costs less than Dubrovnik. You say:
A "Ljubljana is more cheaper than Dubrovnik."
B "Ljubljana is cheaper than Dubrovnik."
C "Ljubljana is the cheaper that Dubrovnik."
You want to say one city is the symbol of the whole coast. You say:
A "The walls are most iconic thing on the coast."
B "The walls are the most iconic thing on the coast."
C "The walls are the more iconic thing of the coast."
The restaurant was worse than you hoped. The natural sentence is:
A "The food was more bad than I hoped."
B "The food was badder than I hoped."
C "The food was worse than I hoped."
Someone expected a very expensive city. You want to correct that impression:
A "Actually, it is not as expensive as I thought."
B "Actually, it is not so expensive than I thought."
C "Actually, it is the less expensive that I thought."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Choose two cities you know well from your fifty countries. Take about ninety seconds and compare them: which is bigger, which is more authentic, which is better preserved, and which one you would go back to tomorrow. End with the most underrated place you have ever visited, and say what makes it stand out. Use at least six comparatives and two superlatives, and do not stop to correct yourself.
