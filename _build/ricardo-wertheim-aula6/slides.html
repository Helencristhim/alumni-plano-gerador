Chapter 4: The Code

Short Words and Long Words

smaller · more crowded · the best

Grammar Discovery

Count the Syllables

"Ljubljana is smaller and greener than Dubrovnik."

"Dubrovnik is more crowded than I expected."

"The walls are the most iconic thing on the coast."

"The river is the best place in the city to do nothing."

Two of them add a small ending. Two put a whole word in front. Count the syllables — what decides?

AdjectiveComparativeSuperlative
Short (1 syllable)
small, green, old		+ er · smallerthe + est · the smallest
Short ending in -y
easy, busy, pretty		y → ier · easierthe easiest
Long (2+ syllables)
crowded, iconic, authentic		more + word · more crowdedthe most crowded
Spelling trap
big, hot		double the letter · biggerthe biggest
Irregular
good, bad, far		good → better → the best · bad → worse → the worst · far → further → the furthest

After a comparative you say than. Before a superlative you say the. Never both endings at once: more smaller does not exist.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"Ljubljana is more cheaper than Dubrovnik."
"Ljubljana is cheaper than Dubrovnik."
"The old town is more crowded that I expected."
"The old town is more crowded than I expected."
"It is most underrated capital in Europe."
"It is the most underrated capital in Europe."

One ending is enough: more or -er, never both. After a comparative comes than, not that. And a superlative always needs the in front.
