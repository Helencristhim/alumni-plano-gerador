Chapter 3: The Code

Three Ways to Say Next Year

will · going to · present continuous

Grammar Discovery

Which One Is Already Decided?

"They are applying to six schools next year."

"We are going to visit two campuses in October."

"Look at that number. That is going to be a problem."

"It will be expensive, but do not worry — I will call the aid office tonight."

The first two are already decided. The third is a guess with the evidence in front of you. The blue one was decided one second before it was said.

FormWhen you use itExample
Present continuousAn arrangement already fixed: a date, a booking, a form sent"They are applying next year."
going to + verbA plan you already have in your head"We are going to visit two campuses."
going to + verbA prediction with the evidence right in front of you"That is going to be a problem."
will + verbA prediction, an opinion about the future"It will be expensive."
will + verbA decision you make while you are speaking"I will call the office tonight."

Key idea: the more fixed the future is, the more the sentence sounds like the present. A booked tour is present continuous; a plan is going to; a guess is will. And after when, if or before, English uses the present, never will: "I will send the form when I get her tax number."

Listening 1

The Year It Happened

A mother on the application year: deadlines, four campuses in one week, and one expensive mistake. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What did she and her daughter do in October, and would she do it again?
They drove to four campuses in one week. No — she says she would not do that again.
2. What was their real mistake, and what did it cost them?
They filed the financial aid form in February. By then the money at her first-choice school was already gone.
3. Where is the daughter studying now, and how does she feel about it?
At an in-state school she never even visited — and she is happy there.
Listening 2

A Message from the School

A recorded message for the families of seniors. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. When is the senior parent night, and how long is it going to run?
Thursday, October fourteenth, at seven in the evening, in the main auditorium — about ninety minutes.
2. What two things does the message ask families to bring, and why?
The student's course list and last year's tax return, because the financial aid workshop in the second half is hands-on.
3. Who do you write to about deadlines, and who needs the extra Saturday session?
Ms. Ortiz, at counseling at Wilson dot edu. The extra session is recommended for students applying to more than five schools.
Listening 3

Is a Gap Year Worth It?

Both sides of the argument, and where the speaker landed. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is the argument against the gap year, in her words?
You lose momentum. A student who stops after high school sometimes never starts again, and a year out is a year of tuition paid later, at a higher price.
2. What does she say about the students who are on track on paper?
That half of them have no idea why they are there, and they are going to spend forty thousand dollars a year finding out.
3. What is her final position, and what is the one condition she adds?
Take the year, but make it a real one — work, save, do something difficult — and apply before you go, so the seat is waiting for you.

The merit scholarship and the need-based financial aid — fifteen thousand five hundred dollars together.
2. The letter says forty-one thousand four hundred and forty. Is that what the family pays?
No. The aid comes off after that line, so the family pays twenty-five thousand nine hundred and forty dollars a year.

Books and supplies, and transportation and personal expenses — those are estimates of what the student will spend, not charges the school sends you.
