Chapter 3

So Do I

Agreeing and disagreeing in three words

Grammar Discovery

Copy the Little Verb

"I love working from home. — So do I."

"I've never been to Chile. — Neither have I."

"I think meetings are too long. — Really? I don't."

"I can't stand small talk. — Oh, I can! I love it."

FormUseExample
So + auxiliary + IAgree with a POSITIVE sentenceI love it. — So do I.
Neither + auxiliary + IAgree with a NEGATIVE sentenceI haven't seen it. — Neither have I.
I + auxiliary (+ n't)Disagree: say the oppositeI like it. — I don't. / I can't swim. — I can.
Copy the auxiliarydo / does / did, have, can, am / is, would...She's tired. — So am I.
⚠ CarefulCopy the auxiliary of the first sentence: 'I've finished.' — 'So have I', not 'So do I'. The auxiliary comes BEFORE 'I': 'So do I', never 'So I do'. After 'neither', the verb stays positive: 'Neither have I', never 'Neither haven't I'. 'Me too' and 'Me neither' are fine in casual talk.

Chapter 4

Real Voices

A real debate, and two people who learned to disagree

Dialogue

Three Days in the Office?

V
So, what do you think about going back to the office three days a week?
D
Honestly, I'm on the fence. I like seeing people, but the commute is awful.
V
So do I. I like seeing people. But I think three days is too many.
D
I don't. I think three is fine. Let me play devil's advocate: new people learn faster in the office.
V
You have a point. I hadn't thought about new people.
D
But I see where you're coming from. Two hours in traffic is a lot.
V
So maybe there's some common ground. Two days, and flexible hours?
D
I couldn't agree more. Let's suggest that, and nobody has to take sides.
Comprehension

Did You Catch It?

1. Why is Daniel on the fence at first?
He likes seeing people, but the commute is awful.
2. What argument does Daniel use when he plays devil's advocate?
New people learn faster in the office.
3. What do they agree on in the end?
Two days in the office, with flexible hours.
