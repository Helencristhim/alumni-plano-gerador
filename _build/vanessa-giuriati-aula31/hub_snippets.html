Lesson 31 -- Pre-class

Agreeing and Disagreeing with Confidence

React to opinions in real time: agree and disagree in three words with "So do I", "Neither have I" and "I don't", and disagree politely without ending the conversation. Key words: couldn't agree more, see where you're coming from, on the fence, have a point, play devil's advocate, beg to differ, take sides, common ground.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Couldn't agree more -- Used to say you agree completely
"Shorter meetings? I couldn't agree more."
See where you're coming from -- To understand the reasons for someone's opinion, even if you disagree
"I see where you're coming from, but I see it differently."
On the fence -- Not able to decide which side or opinion you support
"I'm still on the fence about the new policy."
Have a point -- To say something that is reasonable or partly true
"You have a point. I hadn't thought about that."
Play devil's advocate -- To argue the other side, even if you don't believe it, to test an idea
"Let me play devil's advocate for a second."
Beg to differ -- A polite but firm way to disagree
"I'm sorry, but I beg to differ."
Take sides -- To support one person or opinion in an argument
"I don't want to take sides between two friends."
Common ground -- The ideas or interests that two people share
"We disagree on the details, but we have common ground."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

At lunch, my colleagues started a debate about the four-day week. Paulo said, "I think it's a great idea." Ana answered, "So do I." Then Marcos said, "I don't. Who answers the clients on Fridays?" Somebody had to play devil's advocate, and he did it well. To be honest, I was on the fence. I could see where both sides were coming from, and I did not want to take sides too early. When Ana said, "I've never worked a four-day week," I said, "Neither have I, but my sister has, and she loves it." Marcos had a point about the clients, so I suggested a rotation. Paulo said, "I couldn't agree more." Marcos still begged to differ, but at least we had found some common ground.

1. What did Ana answer when Paulo said, "I think it's a great idea"?
A "Neither do I."
B "So do I."
C "I don't."
2. Why didn't the writer take sides early?
A She was on the fence and could see both sides.
B She was not at lunch.
C She agreed with Marcos from the start.
3. Which answer agrees with a NEGATIVE sentence?
A "So do I."
B "I couldn't agree more."
C "Neither have I."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- So do I / Neither have I / I don't

GRAMMAR

In conversation, agree or disagree in two or three words by copying the auxiliary verb of the first sentence.

StructureUseExample
So + auxiliary + IAgree with a positive sentenceI love it. — So do I.
Neither + auxiliary + IAgree with a negative sentenceI haven't seen it. — Neither have I.
I + auxiliary (+ n't)Disagree: say the oppositeI like it. — I don't.
Copy the auxiliarydo / did / have / can / am...She's tired. — So am I.

The auxiliary comes BEFORE 'I': 'So do I', never 'So I do'. After 'neither', the verb stays positive: 'Neither have I', never 'Neither haven't I'. Copy the right verb: 'I've seen it.' — 'So have I'.
