Chapter 1: The News

One Change at Your Bank

Where You Start

Who Did It, or What Happened

Who did it

"The board approved the deal on Thursday." -- the sentence is about the board

What happened

"The deal was approved on Thursday." -- the sentence is about the deal

The difference

The same news, and a different first word

News, reports and bank announcements are full of the second kind of sentence. You read them every morning. Today you start saying them.

Chapter 3: The Code

Is Done, Was Done

The structure that every news story is written in

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The deal was approved by both boards."

"The accounts are managed by the same team."

"Interest rates were raised again yesterday."

"A new fund is launched almost every month."

Look at the blue words. In two sentences we know who did it, and in two we do not. What do all four blue phrases have in common?

FormWhat it saysExample
am / is / are + past participlePresent: something that happens regularly, or is true now."The accounts are managed in New York."
was / were + past participlePast: something that happened at a finished time."The deal was approved on Thursday."
NegativePut not after the verb be."A new director was not appointed."
QuestionMove be before the subject."Was the deal approved?"
"When was it decided?"
by + the personOnly when the person matters. Most of the time, leave it out."The deal was approved by both boards."

Why use it at all? Because the news is what happened, not who did it. When the person is not important, is obvious, or nobody wants to say, the passive puts the news first.

Before the Clients Call

R
Have you seen the headline this morning? Harbor Capital and Northgate are going to merge.
J
I saw it on my way in. When was it announced?
R
At seven. It was decided behind closed doors last week, and the shareholders were told last night.
J
Were any of our clients affected?
R
Two of them. Both of their accounts are managed by Northgate at the moment.
J
And who is in charge now? Was a new director appointed?
R
Not yet. A name is expected in the spring, so nothing is changed for now.
J
Then I will call both clients today, before they read it in the news.
Comprehension

About Rachel

1. What news does Rachel bring?
Harbor Capital and Northgate are going to merge.
2. Where was the decision made, according to Rachel?
Behind closed doors, last week.
3. When were the shareholders told?
Last night.
4. Why are two of the clients affected?
Their accounts are managed by Northgate at the moment.
Listening

Things Simply Happen

A short radio segment. Sound first -- no text.

1. Why do journalists use the passive, according to the speaker?
Because in the news, what happened is more interesting than who did it.
2. What are her three reasons for leaving the person out?
The person is not important, everybody already knows, or nobody wants to say.
3. What does she recommend when you explain a decision to a client?
Start with the decision, not with the people in the meeting.

The Habit

One Headline, Out Loud

Before the next class, read one business headline each day and say the news out loud in one sentence, starting with what happened: rates were cut, a fund was launched, a director was appointed.

You already read this structure every morning. Saying it is the next step, and it takes one minute.
