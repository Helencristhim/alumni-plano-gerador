Lesson 16 -- Pre-class

Next Season, Next Deadline

A season calendar, a plan, an arrangement and a promise: how to talk about the future with the right form. Key words: upcoming, to pencil something in, tentative, to firm something up, a slot, to clash, to be due, on track, to fall behind, to wrap something up. Structure: future forms -- going to for plans, the present continuous for arrangements, and will for decisions, offers, promises and opinions.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Upcoming -- happening soon
"We are planning the upcoming season."
To pencil something in -- to plan something that may still change
"A studio session is penciled in for Friday."
Tentative -- not final yet, and it can still change
"Friday is tentative. We will know on Wednesday."
To firm something up -- to make a plan final
"Two hours later, the new dates were firmed up."
A slot -- a fixed time in a schedule for one thing
"We have a two-hour slot in the studio on Monday."
To clash -- to happen at the same time as something else
"The studio slot clashed with another recording."
To be due -- to be expected or planned for a certain time
"Twenty arrangements are due by the end of March."
On track -- making progress as planned, at the right speed
"Don't worry. The team is on track."
To fall behind -- to do things more slowly than planned
"We are going to fall behind if we lose this week."
To wrap something up -- to finish something completely
"We will wrap everything up before the first rehearsal."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

It is January, and Pedro is planning the upcoming season of the show. He has three kinds of future in his calendar, and English uses a different form for each one. Look at the words in bold.

First, the plans he already has in his head. This season, he is going to record all the backing tracks in-house, and he is going to start a month earlier. Nobody else is involved yet: it is his intention. Then, the arrangements: dates that other people have agreed to. The band is coming in on the fifteenth, and he is meeting the vocal coaches on Monday at ten. A recording slot is penciled in for February, but it is still tentative.

Then, in February, the slot clashes with another recording. The producer looks at the calendar: “We are going to fall behind.” Pedro decides on the spot: “I’ll call the studio myself.” That is the third kind of future: a decision at this moment, an offer or a promise, with will. Opinions about the future use will too: “The arrangements will probably arrive late.”

By the evening, the new dates are firmed up, the twenty arrangements are due in March, and the team is on track. “We’ll wrap everything up before the first rehearsal,” Pedro says. One small trap: after will, the verb comes alone. Never will to send, and never will going to.

1. Why does the text say “the band is coming in on the fifteenth”?
A Because it is an arrangement: other people have agreed to that date.
B Because the band is on its way to the studio at this exact moment, and Pedro can see the van outside.
C Because it is a promise.
2. Pedro says “I’ll call the studio myself.” Why does he use will?
A Because he planned the call a long time before, and it was already written in his calendar for that afternoon.
B Because he decides at that moment.
C Because the call is tentative.
3. Which sentence is correct, according to the text?
A I will going to record all the backing tracks in-house this season.
B Don’t worry about the file, I will to send it to you again tonight, before the rehearsal.
C I’m going to record all the backing tracks in-house.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Future Forms

GRAMMAR

One question: when was it decided, and who else is involved?

JobFormExample
A plan you already haveam / is / are going to + verbI am going to study online next year.
An arrangement: a date and other peopleam / is / are + verb-ingWe are meeting the coaches on Monday.
A decision now, an offer, a promisewill (’ll) + verbI’ll send it tonight.
An opinion about the futurewill + verb (I think, probably)I think the audience will love it.
A prediction you can see nowam / is / are going to + verbLook: we are going to fall behind.
Negativewon’t · am not going to · am not + -ingI won’t be late. We aren’t recording on Sunday.
QuestionWill ... ? · Are you going to ... ? · Are you + -ing ... ?What are you doing on Friday?

Remember: after will, the base verb comes alone (I will send, not I will to send), and you never put will and going to together.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A colleague asks about your Tuesday. You have a meeting with the producer at three, and she has already confirmed it. You say:
A "I meet the producer at three, and after that I go back to the studio to finish the mix."
B "I'm meeting the producer at three."
C "I will to meet the producer at three."
The singer says she cannot find the new arrangement in her email. You offer to help right now. You say:
A "I'm sending it again tomorrow at nine, after the meeting with the vocal coaches."
B "No problem, I send it again."
C "No problem, I'll send it again."
A Berklee classmate asks about your plans for next year. You decided months ago to study music production. You say:
A "I'm going to study music production."
B "I will going to study music production, and I hope to finish the course before the end of the year."
C "I study music production next year."
You look at the schedule: two sessions are on the same day, and there is no time to move them. You say:
A "These two sessions clash, so we fall behind next week, and the producers get angry with us."
B "These two sessions are on track."
C "These two sessions clash. We're going to fall behind."
