Lesson 05 -- Pre-class

The Status Call -- Done, Not Done, and the Risk

How to give a clear status of the close to the global team: what is done, what is not done yet, the risk, and the date, in the right tense and with the right small word for time. Key words: on track, behind schedule, a bottleneck, to flag, pending, to sign off on, a workaround, to catch up, to escalate, an estimate. Structure: have plus the third form with already and yet, the past simple with a date, and in, on, at, by for time.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

On track -- going as planned, with no delay
"The close is on track for Thursday."
Behind schedule -- later than the plan said
"We are two days behind schedule because of the bank."
A bottleneck -- the one step that slows down the whole process
"The approval of the invoices is always the bottleneck."
To flag -- to tell people early that there may be a problem
"I want to flag a risk before it becomes a problem."
Pending -- waiting to be done, answered or approved
"Two invoices are still pending."
To sign off on -- to give the final approval for something
"The CFO signs off on the numbers on day five."
A workaround -- a temporary way to solve a problem until the real fix arrives
"We found a workaround, so the file goes out today."
To catch up -- to reach the point where you should be after a delay
"We lost a day, but we will catch up by Friday."
To escalate -- to send a problem to a more senior person to decide
"If the bank does not answer today, I will escalate it."
An estimate -- a number or a date that you calculate but are not sure about
"My best estimate is Thursday at noon."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Every month Carlos, a controller in Bogota, used to say the same thing on the global close call: “It is almost done.” His manager in Miami finally told him: “Almost done is not a status. Tell me what is done, what is not, and when.”

Now Carlos uses three sentences. The first is about the finished work, with no date: “We have already reconciled the bank accounts.” The second is about the work that is still open: “We have not received the last two invoices yet, so the file is pending.” When he gives a date, the verb changes: “The bank sent the file on Monday.”

The third sentence is the risk and the promise, and here the small word matters. “You will have the final file by Thursday at noon” means Thursday at noon is the latest moment. “I am on the call until noon” means he is there the whole time before noon. His manager now answers in one word: “Perfect.”

Why does Carlos say "We have already reconciled the accounts"?
A Because it happened on Monday.
B Because it is finished and he does not give a date.
C Because it is not finished yet.
Why does the verb change in "The bank sent the file on Monday"?
A Because the bank is in another country.
B Because the sentence has a finished date, Monday.
C Because it is a question.
What does "by Thursday at noon" mean?
A Every day until Thursday.
B After Thursday.
C Thursday at noon is the latest moment.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Done, Not Done, and When

GRAMMAR

Finished, no date: have plus the third form. Finished, with a date: the past form. Open: not yet. Time: in, on, at, by.

JobFormExample
Done, no datehave already + third formWe have already reconciled the accounts.
Not donehave not + third form + yetWe have not received the invoices yet.
Done, with a datepast formThe bank sent the file on Monday.
Day · hour · monthon · at · inon Monday · at noon · in March
The latest momentbyYou will have it by Thursday.
The whole time beforeuntilI am on the call until noon.

Never: we have fixed it yesterday · we already closed? · in Monday · until Thursday (for a deadline) · it is almost done
