Chapter 3: The Code

One Second, One Small Promise

A decision you make on the spot

Grammar Discovery

Decide It on the Spot

"Roberto, I’ll take your question in a second."

"Good point. I’ll come back to that on slide six."

"I’ll send you the full numbers after the meeting."

"Don’t worry. It won’t take more than two minutes."

What do the orange words have in common? Look at the verb after will. Is there a to? Is there an -s? And did Marcos plan these sentences before the meeting, or decide them in that second?

JobFormExample
A decision in that secondI’ll + base verbGood point. I’ll come back to that.
An offerI’ll + base verbI’ll send you the numbers.
A promise (not)won’t + base verbIt won’t take long.
Same form for everybodywill = ’ll · will not = won’tI’ll, we’ll, she’ll, it won’t
A plan made BEFORE (lesson 5)going to + base verbWe’re going to launch in March.
The trapnever to, never -s, never the presentNot I come back to that or I will to send. Say I’ll come back to that, I’ll send.

In one line: going to is the plan you brought to the meeting; I’ll is the decision you make inside it.

Listening 1 · A Board Member

The Next Five Seconds

A board member on presenters who lose the room. Sound first — no text.

1. According to him, when does a presenter lose the room?
Not when somebody interrupts, but in the next five seconds, by what the presenter does.
2. What does the weak presenter do?
He stops, answers everything, and forgets where he was.
3. When does he start to trust a presenter?
When the presenter really comes back to the question ten minutes later.
Listening 2 · A Presentation Coach

One Very Small Word

A coach on going to, will, and keeping a promise. Sound first — no text.

1. When do you use going to, according to her?
For a plan you made before the meeting, like we are going to launch in March.
2. What two things does an I'll sentence do?
It tells the other person that the question matters, and it tells the room that you are still the one leading.
3. What is her warning at the end?
Do not say I'll if you will not do it. One broken promise costs you more than ten good answers.
