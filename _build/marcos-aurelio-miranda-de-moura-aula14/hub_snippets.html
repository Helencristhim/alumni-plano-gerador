Lesson 14 -- Pre-class

Keeping the Room -- Somebody Interrupts, You Keep the Room

The interruption is not the problem. The next five seconds are. Key words: a tangent, a side conversation, a parking lot, to acknowledge, to redirect, to take over, on track, firm, calm, a commitment. Structure: will for decisions made on the spot, offers and promises, contrasted with going to.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A tangent -- a topic that goes away from the main subject of the meeting
"Carla's question about the margin was a tangent."
A side conversation -- a private talk between two people while somebody else is presenting
"Roberto started a side conversation with the finance team."
A parking lot -- a list of questions you write down to answer later, at the end of the meeting
"I wrote her question on the parking lot."
To acknowledge -- to show somebody that you heard them and that their point matters
"You acknowledged her point and kept going."
To redirect -- to send the conversation back to the main topic
"Give a short answer, then redirect the room."
To take over -- to take control of a meeting from the person who is leading it
"The CEO tried to take over the discussion."
On track -- going in the right direction, and at the right time
"A short answer keeps the agenda on track."
Firm -- strong and clear about what you want, but not angry
"It was calm and it was firm, and he waited."
Calm -- quiet and in control, not nervous and not angry
"You did not get angry. You stayed calm."
A commitment -- a promise to do something, that people expect you to keep
"A promise in a meeting is a commitment."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

In every meeting, somebody interrupts. A board member starts a side conversation, somebody asks about a tangent, or a senior person tries to take over. You cannot stop it. What you can control is the next five seconds. Stay calm, stay firm, and make a small decision on the spot: Good question, I’ll come back to that. I’ll send you the numbers after the meeting.

For a decision you make in that second, an offer or a promise, English uses will: will, or just ’ll, then the verb with nothing added. I’ll take your question, not I take your question and not I will to take. The negative is won’t: It won’t take long. For a plan you made BEFORE the meeting, use going to: We’re going to launch in March.

A good presenter does three things. First, acknowledge the question. Then write it on the parking lot, and redirect the room so the agenda stays on track. Last, and most important, really come back to it. Every I’ll is a commitment, and the room remembers.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Will on the Spot

GRAMMAR

Going to is the plan you brought to the meeting. I’ll is the decision you make inside it.

JobFormExample
Decided nowI’ll + base verbI’ll come back to that.
An offerI’ll + base verbI’ll send you the file.
A promise (not)won’t + base verbIt won’t take long.
Same for everybodyno -s, no to, no -ingI’ll, she’ll, we’ll, it won’t
Plan made before (lesson 5)going to + base verbWe’re going to launch in March.

Never: I come back to that · I will to send · it won’t takes · I’ll sending

Stage 2: Keep the Room in Order

Order

A board member interrupts your presentation. Put your answer in order: stay calm, acknowledge, promise, redirect, and later keep the promise.

?"That is a good point about the margin."
?"I'll put it on the parking lot for now."
?"Sorry, Carla, let me finish this slide."
?"OK, Carla, here is the margin I promised."
?"I'll come back to that on slide six."
?"So, back to the two options for the south."

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen, then record yourself. I’ll sounds like one short word, not "I will".

Let me finish this slide, and then I'll take your question.
Good question. I'll come back to that on slide six.
I'll put it on the parking lot, and we'll look at it at the end.
I'll send you the full numbers after the meeting.
Can we go back to the proposal?

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

In the middle of your second slide, a board member asks about the margin. It is on slide six. You say:
A "Good question. I come back to that later, on slide six of the presentation."
B "Wait."
C "Good question. I’ll come back to that on slide six."
The CEO asks for numbers you do not have with you. You offer to send them. You say:
A "I’ll send them to you after the meeting."
B "I’m going to send you the numbers, as I planned last week before the meeting."
C "I will to send them."
Two people start a side conversation while you present. You stay calm and firm. You say:
A "Please be quiet, everybody, because I am presenting now and this is very rude."
B "Let me finish this slide, and then I’ll take your questions."
C "I’ll stop here and we can talk about something else."
Somebody asks how long your part of the meeting is going to take. You promise it is short. You say:
A "Don’t worry, it won’t take more than ten minutes."
B "Don’t worry, it won’t takes more than ten minutes of the meeting."
C "Long."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Think of your next real presentation at work. Who usually interrupts you, and how? Record yourself giving the opening, and imagine two interruptions. For each one, say a short sentence with I’ll, then go back to your presentation.
