The Settlement Call -- If He Pays, We’ll Waive

Negotiating a settlement with the debtor’s lawyer. Key words: a settlement, to negotiate, an offer, a counteroffer, an installment, a lump sum, to waive, interest, to default, a lawsuit. Structure: first conditional with if, unless and as long as.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A settlement -- an agreement that ends a legal problem without a decision from the court
"Both sides want a settlement before the trial."
To negotiate -- to discuss something to reach an agreement
"We negotiated for two hours, and finally we agreed."
An offer -- an amount or a deal that one side says it can accept
"Their first offer was sixty percent of the debt."
A counteroffer -- a new offer that you make after you say no to the first one
"We rejected their offer and made a counteroffer."
An installment -- one of several payments that together pay a debt
"He can pay the debt in six monthly installments."
A lump sum -- all the money paid at one time, in one payment
"If he pays a lump sum, we can give a discount."
To waive -- to say officially that you will not ask for something that you have the right to
"The bank agreed to waive the late fees."
Interest -- the extra money that you pay when you borrow money, as a percentage
"The interest on this debt is twelve percent a year."
To default -- to fail to make a payment that you agreed to make
"If he defaults again, we will go to court."
A lawsuit -- a case that one person or company starts in court against another
"Our client does not want a long lawsuit in Florida."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Mr. Carter owes a bank two hundred and forty thousand dollars, and he does not want a lawsuit. His lawyer calls Ana to negotiate a settlement. His first offer is low: sixty percent of the debt, in twelve installments.

Ana says no and makes a counteroffer. “If he pays a lump sum of eighty percent, we’ll waive the interest.” Mr. Carter does not have that much cash, so she offers a second option: six installments, as long as he pays every one on time.

There is one more condition. If he defaults, the deal will end, and the bank will go to court. And the offer is not open forever: the bank will file the lawsuit unless the lawyer answers by Friday.

1. Why does the text say “If he pays” and not “If he will pay”?
A Because after if, we use the simple present, even when we talk about the future.
B Because the sentence is about something that happened in the past, at the start of the negotiation.
C Because will is never used in a negotiation.
2. What does “unless the lawyer answers by Friday” mean?
A If the lawyer answers by Friday, the bank will go to court on Monday morning without another call.
B The lawyer has to answer on Friday.
C If the lawyer does not answer by Friday, the bank will file the lawsuit.
3. What is the condition of the installment option?
A Mr. Carter has to pay the interest first.
B Mr. Carter has to pay every installment on time.
C Mr. Carter has to pay a lump sum of eighty percent before the first installment, and then six more.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The First Conditional: If, Unless, As Long As

GRAMMAR

Use if + simple present for a possible condition in the future, and will / won’t + verb for the result. Unless means if not. As long as means only if. Never use will after if, unless or as long as.

JobFormExample
Condition + resultIf + simple present, will / won’t + verb“If he pays a lump sum, we’ll waive the interest.”
Result firstwill + verb + if + simple present (no comma)“We’ll accept the deal if he signs today.”
Unless = if notunless + affirmative verb“We’ll go to court unless he pays.” = if he doesn’t pay
As long as = only ifas long as + simple present“As long as he pays on time, we’ll wait.”
QuestionsWhat will happen if...? / Will you accept... if...?“Will you waive the interest if he pays today?”
The trapnever will after if, unless or as long asNot If he will pay... Say If he pays...

Never: If he will pay... · Unless he doesn’t pay... · we not will go... · If your client accept...

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The debtor’s lawyer makes a very low offer. You say:
A "No. That offer is too low, and if he will not pay more, we file a lawsuit tomorrow."
B "No, too low offer."
C "I’m afraid that’s too low. Here’s our counteroffer."
You want to offer a discount for a payment in one go. You say:
A "If he pays a lump sum, we’ll waive the interest."
B "If he will pay a lump sum, we waive the interest for him and for his company."
C "If he pay a lump sum, we waive the interest."
You accept installments, but only with payments on time. You say:
A "We’ll accept installments, unless he pays on time every month of the year."
B "We’ll accept installments, as long as he pays on time."
C "We accept installments as long as he will pay."
You want to give the other side a final deadline. You say:
A "We’ll file a lawsuit unless he pays by Friday."
B "We’ll file a lawsuit unless he doesn’t pay by the end of the week, on Friday."
C "We file a lawsuit if he not pays by Friday."
