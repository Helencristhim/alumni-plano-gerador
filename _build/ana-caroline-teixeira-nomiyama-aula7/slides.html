Chapter 3: The Code

If He Pays, We’ll Waive

A condition in the present, a result with will

Grammar Discovery

The Condition and the Result

"If your client pays a lump sum, we’ll waive the interest."

"We’ll file a lawsuit unless he pays by Friday."

"As long as he pays every installment, we won’t go to court."

"If he defaults again, the full debt will be due."

Look at the red parts: they are the condition, and the verb is in the simple present (pays, defaults), even though it is about the future. Now look at the blue parts: they are the result, with will or won’t. Unless means if not. As long as means only if.

JobFormExample
Condition + resultIf + simple present, will / won’t + verb“If he pays a lump sum, we’ll waive the interest.”
Result firstwill + verb + if + simple present (no comma)“We’ll accept the deal if he signs today.”
Unless = if notunless + affirmative verb“We’ll go to court unless he pays.” = if he doesn’t pay
As long as = only ifas long as + simple present“As long as he pays on time, we’ll wait.”
QuestionsWhat will happen if...? / Will you accept... if...?“Will you waive the interest if he pays today?”
The trapnever will after if, unless or as long asNot If he will pay... Say If he pays...

In one line: if / unless / as long as + present, then will: If he pays, we’ll waive.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Deal at Real Speed

One phone call, three recordings, one proposal

Dialogue

The Call from the Other Side

M
Good afternoon, Ana. This is Mark Reynolds. I represent Mr. Carter. He wants a settlement, not a lawsuit.
A
Good afternoon, Mark. We’re happy to negotiate. What’s your offer?
M
He can pay sixty percent of the debt, in twelve monthly installments.
A
I’m afraid that’s too low. Here’s our counteroffer: if he pays a lump sum of eighty percent, we’ll waive the interest.
M
He doesn’t have that much cash right now. His business is slow this year.
A
Then we can accept eighty-five percent in six installments, as long as he pays every one on time.
M
And what will happen if he misses one payment?
A
If he defaults, the deal ends, and we’ll file the lawsuit. Unless I hear from you by Friday, we’ll go ahead.
Comprehension

About Mark

1. What does Mark’s client want, and what does he want to avoid?
He wants a settlement; he wants to avoid a lawsuit.
2. What is Mark’s first offer?
Sixty percent of the debt, in twelve monthly installments.
3. Why can’t his client pay the lump sum?
He does not have that much cash right now; his business is slow this year.
Listening 1 · A Mediator

Why Most Cases Never Reach a Judge

An Australian mediator explains why so many disputes end in a settlement. Sound first — no text.

1. What happens to most of the cases that he sees?
They end in a settlement, not in a decision from a judge.
2. Why do both sides often prefer a settlement?
Because a lawsuit takes years and costs a lot of money, and nobody knows the result.
3. What is his advice to people who negotiate?
Know your limit before the meeting starts.
Listening 2 · A Collections Manager

The Rules of a Payment Plan

A collections manager at a bank in India explains how her team negotiates with debtors. Sound first — no text.

1. What does her bank usually offer to a client who cannot pay?
A payment plan in monthly installments.
2. When does the bank waive part of the interest?
When the client pays a lump sum.
3. What happens if a client defaults on the plan?
The plan ends, and the full debt is due again.
Listening 3 · A German Lawyer

Negotiating in My Second Language

A German lawyer talks about her first negotiations in English. Sound first — no text.

1. What was her problem in her first negotiations in English?
She accepted the first offer too fast, because she wanted to finish the conversation.
2. What does she prepare before every call now?
Three sentences: one for no, one for her counteroffer and one for her condition.
3. What does she do when the other lawyer speaks too fast?
She asks him to repeat the numbers, slowly.
