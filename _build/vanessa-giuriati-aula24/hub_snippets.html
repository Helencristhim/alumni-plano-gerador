Lesson 24 -- Pre-class

Handling Uncertainty with an Honest Answer

Answer well when you do not know: give a ballpark figure, show how sure you are with must / might / cannot be, and commit to a deadline for the exact number. Key words: off the top of my head, ballpark, double-check, get back to you, look into, presumably, as far as I know, educated guess.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Off the top of my head -- Said from memory, without checking anything
"Off the top of my head, it is about forty."
Ballpark -- An approximate number or figure, not the exact one
"Can you give me a ballpark figure?"
Double-check -- To check something a second time to be sure
"Let me double-check the numbers."
Get back to you -- To come back later with the answer
"I will get back to you by four o'clock."
Look into -- To investigate something to find the answer
"I will look into it this afternoon."
Presumably -- Probably, judging by what you already know
"Presumably they signed the contract yesterday."
As far as I know -- Based on the information you have, which may not be complete
"As far as I know, the deadline has not moved."
Educated guess -- A guess based on knowledge and experience, not on nothing
"That is an educated guess, not a promise."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Daniel asked me for two numbers before lunch, and I had neither of them in front of me. So I answered anyway. Off the top of my head, we closed around forty accounts last quarter. It must be close to that, because we hired eight people in the same period, and it cannot be more than fifty — we only had four people on the team until May. That is a ballpark figure, an educated guess, and I said so. As far as I know, none of the June contracts are signed, so presumably they land next cycle. The churn rate I would not guess at all: I am going to look into it, double-check with finance, and get back to him by four.

1. How sure is she that the number is around forty?
A She has no idea at all.
B She is almost sure, and she gives a reason for it.
C She read it in the final report this morning.
2. Why does she refuse to guess the churn rate?
A Because she does not care about it.
B Because it is the kind of number that will be checked, so she prefers to verify it first.
C Because Daniel did not ask for it.
3. Which sentence marks the limit of what she knows?
A "It must be close to that."
B "As far as I know, none of the June contracts are signed."
C "I will get back to him by four."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- must / might / cannot

GRAMMAR

Three small words that tell the other person exactly how sure you are.

StructureUseExample
It must be + base verbAlmost sure, and you have a reasonIt must be around forty.
It might / could bePossible; you genuinely do not knowIt might be closer to fifty.
It cannot beAlmost sure it is NOT trueIt cannot be more than sixty.
Modal + base verbNo "to", no -s, no past form after the modalIt must be, it might cost, it cannot take that long.

After a modal, the verb stays in its base form: 'it must be', never 'must to be'. Use cannot be for a strong negative deduction. And the expression is 'get back to you'.

Stage 2: Answer Without the Answer, in Order

Order

Listen first, then put the steps of an honest, useful answer in the right order.

?First, I say what I do know, straight away: off the top of my head, it is around forty.
?In the end, I promise a real deadline: I will double-check and get back to you by four.
?After that, I mark how sure I am: it must be close to that, and it cannot be more than fifty.
?Then I add the reason behind it, so the number is not floating in the air.
?Next, I name the limit of what I know, so nobody treats my guess as a fact.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

Your manager asks for a figure you do not have. Best choice:
A "I do not know."
B "Off the top of my head, it must be around forty. I will confirm by four."
C "Forty-one point three percent."
You are almost sure the number is NOT above sixty. Best choice:
A "It cannot be more than sixty."
B "It must not be more than sixty."
C "It cannot to be more than sixty."
You want to mark the limit of your information. Best choice:
A "As far as I know, the deadline has not moved."
B "As far as I knowing, the deadline has not moved."
C "For all I am knowing, the deadline has not moved."
You need one more day before you can confirm. Best choice:
A "I will look into it and get back to you tomorrow morning."
B "I will get back you tomorrow morning."
C "Maybe tomorrow, maybe not, we will see."
