Chapter 3

How Certain?

must / might / cannot — the three levels

Grammar Discovery

Order Them by Certainty

"It must be around forty — we hired eight last quarter."

"It might be closer to fifty, but I would have to check."

"It could be either, honestly."

"It cannot be more than sixty. We do not have the seats."

FormUseExample
must be + base verbYou are almost sure, and you have a reasonIt must be around forty.
might / could beIt is possible; you are genuinely unsureIt might be closer to fifty.
cannot beYou are almost sure it is NOT trueIt cannot be more than sixty.
Modal + base verb, alwaysNo "to", no -s, no past form after the modalIt must be / it might cost / it cannot take that long.
⚠ CarefulAfter a modal, the verb stays in its base form: 'it must be', never 'must to be' or 'must is'. Use cannot be for a strong negative deduction — 'it must not be' means something else. And it is 'get back to you', not 'get back you'.

Comprehension

Did You Catch It?

1. What does Daniel ask for at the beginning?
He asks how many accounts the team closed last quarter.
2. What does Daniel want to know about the number he is given?
Whether it is a ballpark figure or a real, exact number.
3. What does Daniel ask her to do before the board meeting?
To look into it — and she promises to double-check with finance and get back to him by four.
Listening

The Three-Part Answer

Listen first. No text. Sound only.

1. What three things does the speaker give when she does not know?
What she does know, how sure she is, and a deadline for the real answer.
2. Why does she say "that is an educated guess, not a promise"?
To be clear about how sure she is — it is based on experience, but it is not the final figure.
3. What does she say kills a meeting?
Not "I do not know" itself, but "I do not know" said alone, with nothing after it. The room needs a number to work with and a time when the real one arrives.
Listening

Must, Might, Cannot

A different voice, a different message. Listen carefully.

1. What are the three words the speaker talks about?
Must, might and cannot.
2. What is the difference between "must be" and "might be"?
"Must be" is a strong deduction with a reason behind it; "might be" means the speaker genuinely does not know.
3. Why does he say this matters at work?
Because people do not mind when you are unsure — they mind when they cannot tell how unsure you are.
