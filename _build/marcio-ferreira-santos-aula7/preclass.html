Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

What They Said -- Reporting Decisions and Evidence

Relaying what a board, a study or a regulator said. Key words: stakeholder, endorsement, consensus, briefing, rationale, escalation, mandate, precedent. Structure: reported speech, with the tense one step back and reporting verbs beyond "said".

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Stakeholder -- anyone affected by a decision, inside or outside the company
"Every stakeholder was informed before the release."
Endorsement -- public support that someone important gives to a plan
"The plan moved fast because it had the endorsement of the board."
Consensus -- a position that everybody in the room can accept
"We reached consensus on the timeline, not on the budget."
Briefing -- a short meeting or document that tells a team what it needs to know
"The briefing was sent to everyone who missed the meeting."
Rationale -- the reason behind a decision, written down
"The rationale for every price change has to be recorded."
Escalation -- taking a problem to a higher level because you cannot decide it
"The escalation went to the commercial director on Friday."
Mandate -- formal permission to do something, given by the people in charge
"We have a mandate to rebuild the model, not to change the team."
Precedent -- an earlier decision that becomes the reference for the next one
"If we accept this discount, we create a precedent."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

This is the briefing from yesterday's board meeting. The chief executive confirmed that the southern plan had been approved, with one condition. The finance director warned that the budget would not be increased before the second quarter. Two directors argued that the timeline was too short, and the board noted their position in the minutes. Finally, the board asked whether the model could be extended to the fresh categories, and we said that it could, but not before September.

1. The chief executive said "the plan has been approved". Why does the briefing say "had been"?
A In reported speech the tense moves one step back.
B Because the plan is no longer approved.
C Because "approve" is an irregular verb.
2. Why does the briefing use "asked whether" and not "asked that"?
A The original was a yes or no question, so it takes "whether" or "if".
B "Whether" and "that" mean the same thing in reports.
C Because the board is plural.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "She asked what was the rationale."
B "She asked what the rationale was."
C "She asked what did the rationale be."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

When you repeat what somebody said, the tense moves one step back.

DirectReportedExample
present simplepast simple"It is approved" → he said it was approved
present perfect or past simplepast perfect"Margins have improved" → she noted they had improved
will / canwould / could"We will start" → they confirmed they would start
yes or no questionwhether / if, normal word order"Can it be extended?" → they asked whether it could be extended
Reporting verbsBeyond "said": stated, confirmed, indicated, suggested, warned, argued, noted, emphasized. And remember: you tell someone, but you say something.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each real professional situation.

A colleague asks what the board decided. You answer:
A "The chief executive said the plan has been approved yesterday."
B "The chief executive confirmed that the plan had been approved."
C "The chief executive said me the plan is approved."
You want to report a yes or no question from the meeting. Which is correct?
A "They asked whether the model could be extended."
B "They asked that the model could be extended."
C "They asked could the model be extended."
You want to show that somebody said something with concern. The best verb is:
A "The finance director warned that the timeline was too short."
B "The finance director suggested that the timeline was too short."
C "The finance director confirmed that the timeline was too short."
You are not sure what was agreed. The professional move is:
A "I want to be accurate, so let me confirm the rationale before I report it."
B Report it anyway and correct it later if somebody complains.
C Say nothing and let another team write the summary.
