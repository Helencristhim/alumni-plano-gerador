The Language of Investment Risk

An investor does not ask if a project has risk. Every project has risk. The question is how sure you are, and what somebody has to do about it. Today you learn to say exactly that.

Chapter 4: The Code

Must, May, Likely

Certainty, probability and obligation

Grammar Discovery

How Sure Is the Speaker?

"With this level of debt, the lenders must be worried."

"The concessionaire has to publish its accounts every year."

"The project may need a second guarantee."

"Tariffs are likely to rise next year."

Two of the blue words are about how sure the speaker is. One is about a rule. One is about a probability. Which is which? And look at the verb after each one: is it be, to be or being?

JobFormExample
A rule or a law (obligation)have to / must + verbThe concessionaire has to publish its accounts.
Almost sure (deduction)must + verbWith this debt, they must be worried.
Almost impossible (deduction)can’t + verbWith this debt, the rating can’t be AAA.
Possible, but not suremay / might + verbThe project may need a second guarantee.
Probable or not probablebe likely / unlikely to + verbTariffs are likely to rise.
The trapnot necessary is don’t have to; mustn’t means it is not allowedYou don’t have to sign today. You mustn’t share the data.

In one line: the modal shows how sure you are or how necessary it is, and the verb after it stays in the base form.

Every line answers two questions: how likely the risk is, and who has to do something about it. The committee approves the loan only if every risk has an owner.
1. Which risk is likely, and what will they do about it?
The currency risk. They will hedge 70% of the dollar debt.
2. Why is a change in the tariff rules unlikely?
Because contracts have been respected for twenty years.
3. Who has to update the register, and how often?
The legal team, every quarter.
Listening 1 · The Analyst

How I Rate a Toll Road

A credit analyst at a rating agency explains what she looks at first. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she do with most traffic forecasts?
She reduces them by twenty percent, because they are usually too optimistic.
2. According to her, when is the risk lower?
When the contract says clearly who has to pay if the costs go up.
3. What may happen before every election in some countries?
The government may change the tariff.
Listening 2 · The Fund Manager

The Quarterly Risk Update

A fund manager speaks to the investors in an infrastructure fund. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the good news about the projects?
None of them is close to a default, traffic is higher than expected and both concessionaires are creditworthy.
2. What is likely to happen if the dollar continues to go up?
The returns of the fund are likely to fall next year.
3. When will they decide about the hedge?
At the next board meeting, in March.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I can say how sure I am with must, may and can't.
I can say what is probable with be likely to and be unlikely to.
I use have to for rules, and don't have to when something is not necessary.
I use the base form after the modal, never to after must, may or can't.
