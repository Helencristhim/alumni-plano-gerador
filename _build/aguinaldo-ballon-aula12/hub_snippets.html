The Language of Investment Risk -- How Sure Are You?

How investors price risk in infrastructure, and how to say how sure you are and who has to act. Key words: a downside, volatility, a default, a currency risk, a rating, collateral, to mitigate, a worst-case scenario, creditworthy, a return. Structure: modals of certainty and obligation.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A downside -- a negative part or a possible bad result of something
"The main downside of the project is the currency risk."
Volatility -- how fast and how much prices or rates go up and down
"Volatility in the exchange rate scares long-term investors."
A default -- a situation in which a borrower does not pay back a loan
"One default can damage the reputation of a whole sector."
A currency risk -- the risk that a change in the exchange rate reduces the value of an investment
"The fund earns in reais and pays in dollars, so the currency risk is high."
A rating -- a grade from an agency that shows how safe a loan or a company is
"The new rating made the bonds cheaper to issue."
Collateral -- property or money that a borrower promises to give if the loan is not paid
"The bank asked for the toll revenue as collateral."
To mitigate -- to make a risk or a problem less serious
"A longer contract can mitigate the regulatory risk."
A worst-case scenario -- the worst thing that could possibly happen in a situation
"In the worst-case scenario, the project loses its license."
Creditworthy -- able to pay back money that you borrow
"The concessionaire must show that it is creditworthy."
A return -- the money that an investment earns
"Investors accept a lower return when the rules are stable."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A foreign fund is studying a metro project in Rio. The lenders asked for more collateral, so they must be worried about the debt. The project earns in reais and borrows in dollars, and the volatility of the exchange rate is high.

Aguinaldo explains the rules to the fund: the concessionaire has to publish its accounts every year, and the contract has to say who pays if costs go up. A change in the tariff rules is unlikely to happen, because contracts have been respected for twenty years.

He does not promise that there is no risk. He says the project may need a second guarantee, and that a better rating is likely to reduce the cost of the loan. Notice how each modal shows how sure he is.

1. Why does Aguinaldo say the lenders "must be worried"?
A Because he is almost sure.
B Because there is a rule that says lenders always have to worry about debt.
C Because the lenders told him directly in a meeting that they were worried.
2. How sure is he that the tariff rules will change?
A He is completely sure that the rules will change before the next election.
B He thinks it is unlikely.
C He thinks the change is necessary and has to happen this year.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "The project may needs a second guarantee from the bank."
B "The project may to need a second guarantee from the bank."
C "The project may need a second guarantee."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Modals of Certainty and Obligation

GRAMMAR

The modal shows how sure you are, or how necessary something is. After the modal, the base form of the verb.

JobFormExample
A rule or a law (obligation)have to / must + verbThe concessionaire has to publish its accounts.
Almost sure (deduction)must + verbWith this debt, they must be worried.
Almost impossible (deduction)can’t + verbWith this debt, the rating can’t be AAA.
Possible, but not suremay / might + verbThe project may need a second guarantee.
Probable or not probablebe likely / unlikely to + verbTariffs are likely to rise.
The trapnot necessary is don’t have to; mustn’t means it is not allowedYou don’t have to sign today. You mustn’t share the data.

Never: They must to be worried · Tariffs are likely rise · The project may needs · You mustn’t sign today (when you mean it is not necessary)

Stage 2: Put the Risk Presentation in Order

Order

You present one risk to a credit committee. Put your sentences in a logical order.

?"We earn in reais, but the debt is in dollars."
?"To mitigate this, the company has to hedge most of the debt."
?"In the worst-case scenario, we may need a second guarantee."
?"So the risk is real, but it is under control."
?"If the dollar goes up again, returns are likely to fall."
?"The main downside of this project is the currency risk."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A rating agency gave the company AA. You tell the committee:
A "A default is very likely to happen, because the rating is AA and the collateral is strong."
B "A default is very unlikely."
C "A default must to happen next year if the rating stays the same as now."
The law says the concessionaire publishes its accounts every year. You say:
A "The concessionaire may publish its accounts every year if it wants to."
B "The concessionaire is likely publishing its accounts every year, I think."
C "It has to publish its accounts every year."
Signing today is not necessary. You tell your client:
A "You don't have to sign today."
B "You mustn't sign today, it is completely forbidden by the contract."
C "You haven't to signing today, but maybe you can sign next week."
The lenders suddenly asked for more collateral. You think they are worried. You say:
A "They have to be worried, because the law says lenders have to worry about it."
B "They can't be worried, because they asked for more collateral this morning."
C "They must be worried."
