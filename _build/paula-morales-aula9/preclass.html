Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

Can You Do This? -- Ability, Permission and Requests

This is the lesson where you find out that you already know. Open your survival card from lesson 1: it says “Can you repeat that, please?”. And the one from lesson 5: “Excuse me, can you help me?”. That is eight lessons of saying can without knowing its name. In this lesson the box opens: one little word with three jobs (what I am able to do · may I? · can you?), six new verbs (explain, sign, wait, join, print, move) and could, which is only can in a tie. This Pre-class is a first contact: come to class with can in your ear, not on the tip of your tongue.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Six verbs — and you do all six every week, only not in English. Listen to each one and repeat it out loud, alone. Two sound traps: sign is said SAIN — the G is silent, as in design and campaign (this is the only hard word today, repeat it five times) — and explain is ex-PLAIN, stressed on the second syllable, rhyming with rain.

Explain -- to make something clear to someone
"Can you explain the numbers?" Say ex-PLAIN, with the stress on the second syllable — the classic mistake is to say "EX-plain".
Sign -- to write your name on a document
"I can't sign it without the report." Watch the sound: SAIN. The G is not pronounced — never say "sig-ne".
Wait -- to stay until something happens
"Could you wait a moment, please?" The sound is the same ay as in late, from lesson 7 — you already have that sound in your mouth.
Join -- to come into a meeting with the others
"Can I join the meeting?" The verb for a meeting is join — never "enter the meeting", which sounds as if you had only walked through the door.
Print -- to put a document on paper
"Could you print two copies?" The verb wants an object in English: print the report.
Move -- to change a meeting to another day
"Can we move the meeting to Monday?" To reschedule is move — the meeting does not die, it only walks to another day. The sound is a long U: muuv.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions. The words in bold are the grammar of this lesson — look at what comes right after each can and each could.

Tuesday, eight in the morning. Paula needs three things from Ricardo, the broker — and writes the email in four lines.

"Can you send me the report on the Grupo Ferreira account? I can explain the numbers to the client on Friday, but I can't sign the document without your report."

"Could you also print two copies for Thursday? I can be at your office at ten. One more thing: can I join your meeting with the client on Friday? If Friday is difficult, I can wait until Monday."

Paula reads the email again and counts: five requests, zero apologies. No “sorry to bother you”, no “sorry for my English”. And the email is still polite. (Notice one more thing: after can the verb comes bare. Can you explain — never can you to explain. That single turn is half the lesson.)

1. Why does it say "Can you explain" and not "Can you to explain"?
A Because the sentence is short and there is no room for to.
B Because after can the verb is always bare — no to, no -s, nothing.
C Because explain already ends in a consonant.
2. The text has "I can explain", "can I join?" and "can you send?". Why the same can three times?
A Because can has three jobs: saying what I am able to do, asking for permission, and asking someone for something.
B Because Paula likes the word and repeated it by accident.
C Because every email sentence in English starts with can.
3. Why did Paula write "Could you print" and not "Can you print"?
A Because "can you print" would be a grammar mistake.
B Because could is the past, and the printing is for Thursday.
C Because could is can in a tie — it adds a gentle distance. Both would be correct.
4. How many times does Paula apologize in this email?
A Twice — once at the start and once at the end.
B Not once — and the email is still just as polite.
C Five times — one for each request.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- can / can't / could: one word, three jobs

GRAMMAR

Three different ideas — I am able to, may I, would you — and in English all three are the same word. For once, English is simpler, not harder.

O empregoInglêsWhen you use it
1. HabilidadeI can explain the numbers.Ability — what you are able to do.
2. PermissãoCan I join the meeting?Permission — you do it, but you ask first.
3. PedidoCan you send me the report?Request — the other person does it.
NegativeI can't sign it today.The negative — one word, no don't.
Pedido educadoCould you repeat that, please?Você poderia repetir, por favor?
Can never changesI can · you can · Sofia can · we canNo -s in the third person. Ever.

The rule, in three words: can + bare verb. No to, no -s, nothing in between. And in a question can jumps in front of the subject — exactly as is and does did in lesson 8. It is the same helper, with another name: Can you help me?, never You can help me?

The most common mistake here: saying “Can you to explain that?”. In your first language the verb after “can” carries an infinitive ending, and the hand wants to bring that ending across. It is not carelessness — it is your first language at work. After can, the verb goes bare: leave the ending at home.

Mistake number 2: “I don't can explain”. This one only happens to people who learned lesson 3 well — the negative asks for don't, right? Not here: can is already the helper of the sentence. It takes no partner and carries no luggage: it becomes can't on its own. Same with the -s: “She can sign”, never “she cans” or “she can signs” — one S per sentence, just like does in lesson 8.

Can or could? When in doubt, use can. Could is only can in a tie: it adds a gentle distance, and it works for clients and managers. But can is not rude — it is what your colleagues say all day long. The practical rule: colleague = can, client = could. That is all. Do not freeze choosing the word: nobody has ever been offended by a can.

Why this matters: open your survival card from lesson 1. It is there: “Can you repeat that, please?” and “Can you speak slowly, please?”. And the one from lesson 5: “Excuse me, can you help me?”. You have been using this grammar since day one — you only did not know its name. In this lesson you will learn nothing new about those sentences. You will find out that you were right all along.

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen to each sentence, then record your voice. Nobody is grading you — it is only you and your microphone. Careful with two of them: sign is SAIN (silent G) and explain is ex-PLAIN. And notice something that will help you LISTEN: in the middle of a sentence can almost disappears, it becomes a quick kn — while can't sounds strong and dry. That is how an American tells them apart: not by the T, by the weight.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best option for each real situation — the broker, your manager, the Miami client.

You need Ricardo to send you the report. He is a colleague you speak to every week. You say:
A "You can send me the report?"
B "Can you send me the report?"
C "Can you to send me the report?"
The Miami client speaks too fast and you caught nothing. You say:
A "Yes, yes." (pretending you understood)
B "Could you repeat that, please?"
C "Sorry, sorry, my English is very bad, sorry, could you repeat?"
Sofia wants the update on Friday, but Ricardo's report only arrives on Monday. You say:
A "I don't can send it on Friday."
B "OK." (and you stay up all night trying)
C "I can't send it on Friday without the report. Can I send it on Monday?"
The call with Miami is on Friday and the account is yours. You want to be in that room. You ask:
A "Can I join the call on Friday?"
B "Can I enter in the call on Friday?"
C "I can join the call on Friday?"
In an interview, they ask: "What can you do in your job?" The best answer is:
A "My English is not very good, but I try."
B "I can handle the account and I can explain the numbers to the client."
C "I cans handle the account."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record your answer. Speak for 60 seconds, with no script. Do not redo it more than twice. This is exactly the exercise you will do in class — and the homework audio afterwards.

Answer out loud the question a Zurich interview would ask: “What can you do in your job?” Say five things you are able to do, each one starting with “I can...”. For example: "I can handle the account. I can explain the numbers to the client. I can talk to any broker in the country." Five. Not four. And here is the rule of this recording, the only one that matters: no sentence may contain a but. No "I can explain the numbers, but my English is not good". Not today. You have spent eight lessons listing what you cannot do — this recording is the other list, and it is longer than you think.
