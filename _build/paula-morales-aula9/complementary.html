Lesson 9 -- Can You Do This? Ability, Permission and Requests

Film
Hidden Figures · 2016 · Disney+ — included in the subscription

This film is here because of one scene, and that scene is the whole of today's lesson. Katherine Johnson is the best mathematician at NASA and she is not allowed into the room where the decisions happen. She is able to do the work — she can — and she still stands outside, because nobody invited her. Until the day she asks. It is a film about three competent women who spend two hours asking to be in the room, and about what happens when they ask: nothing bad. The distance between being able to do the work and being heard is not capability — it is a request. You have a career behind you; this film is not about NASA.

Tip: watch it with English subtitles — the goal here is not to understand everything (there is a lot of maths and rocket vocabulary you do not need). Do ONE thing only: every time somebody asks for something, hit pause and look at the can or the could — and at what comes right after it (always a bare verb, with no to). If you only want one piece: the scene where Katherine explains why she needs to be in the briefing room is the one that counts for the task.

Podcast
Speak English Now with Georgiana — #345 “Stop Apologizing So Much in English!” (free, with transcript)

This episode is not about grammar. It is about you. Georgiana spends the whole episode on a single habit — putting a sorry in front of everything — and explains what it does to the listener: it does not make you more polite, it makes you sound unsure, and the person on the other side starts doubting your work before you open the spreadsheet. She gives ready-made swaps, and one of them is our lesson: instead of “Sorry for the question”, you say “Can I ask something quickly?”. The same thing — without the apology in front.

Tip: listen the first time without stopping and without reading anything — listening is your strength, you understand more than you think, and Georgiana speaks slowly on purpose. The second time, do the task that counts: take the three swaps she teaches and write next to each one the last time you said the version with sorry. You will remember all of them, and fast. Open and free, with no sign-up to listen: the player is on the page itself.

YouTube
How to Pronounce Can vs. Can't — American English Accent · Rachel's English (~4 min, free)

There is a problem with can that nobody warns you about, and it is not about speaking: it is about listening. When an American says “I can send it”, the can almost disappears — it becomes a kn so fast it barely exists. Can't, on the other hand, sounds strong, heavy, and often with no T at the end. So the two can sound almost the same, and they mean the opposite. Rachel is American and settles it in four minutes, with a rule that works: do not look for the T — listen for the weight. What is strong is can't. What disappears is can.

Tip: this is the shortest video of the week and the most useful one for Friday's call — watch it to the end, it is only four minutes. Do this: close your eyes during the examples and try to guess which of the two she said BEFORE she shows it on screen. You will get some wrong — and that is exactly the point: your ear is not the problem, the two really do sound alike. Knowing that already gives you the right to ask: “Could you repeat that?”.
