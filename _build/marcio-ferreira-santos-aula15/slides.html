Chapter 3: The Code

The Size of the Difference

significantly cheaper · twice as fast · by far the safest

Grammar Discovery

Read and Notice

"Option B is significantly cheaper than Option A."

"Option A brings twice as much volume as Option B."

"The margin cost of B is far smaller."

"Doing nothing is by far the most expensive option in six months."

What do the orange words have in common? What do they tell you that cheaper alone does not? And how many options does the blue sentence compare?

StructureRuleExample
ComparativeShort adjective + -er than; long adjective: more ... than. Never both.cheaper than · more expensive than
Small differenceslightly / a little + comparativeslightly higher
Big differencesignificantly / much / far / a lot + comparative. Never very.far smaller · much cheaper
Exact sizetwice / three times + as ... astwice as fast as Option A
Superlativethe -est / the most for three or more options. By far makes it stronger: by far the safest.

One line to remember: never just cheaper. Say how much cheaper.

Listening 2

A or B? One Minute

A case-discussion coach. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What do most people do wrong, according to the speaker?
They compare everything, and the answer gets longer and weaker with every sentence.
2. What are the three things a stronger answer does?
It starts with the choice, compares on one or two dimensions with the size of the difference, and names the biggest downside of its own option.

Because you sound like someone who has already done the analysis, not someone who is selling.
