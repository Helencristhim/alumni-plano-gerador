Video
"Are we in control of our decisions?" -- Dan Ariely, TED

A behavioral economist shows how a third option that nobody wants changes which of the other two people choose. His main example is a magazine subscription page: pure pricing, and pure comparison.

Tip: every time he says "better", "more" or "than", pause and say one sentence about Option A and Option B with a modifier: slightly, significantly, twice as.

"Stressful things" -- Learning English Grammar, BBC Learning English

A short episode on exactly the grammar of this lesson: comparative and superlative sentences, explained with everyday examples. The transcript is on the same page.

Tip: listen once without the transcript. Then read it and mark every comparative with C and every superlative with S.

Talk
"The paradox of choice" -- Barry Schwartz, TED

A psychologist explains why more options can make a choice harder and the result less satisfying. Useful for any retailer who decides how many products and prices to put on the shelf.

Tip: open the transcript after the first listen and find three sentences that compare. Say one of them about your own shelf at work.
