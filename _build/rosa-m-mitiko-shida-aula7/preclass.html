Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

You Should, You Could, You Must -- The Rule and the Exception

Modals for obligation, advice and deduction, in the call where the client wants an exception. Key expressions: policy, compliance, waiver, refund, to escalate, guidelines, fare, to comply with, grace period, penalty, to flag, approval chain. Structure: must, have to, should, could and might -- and why must not and do not have to are opposites.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. These twelve stay here in your material -- they do not disappear after the class.

Policy -- an official rule that a company follows
"Company policy says we must charge the fee, and I cannot change that."
Compliance -- following the rules and laws that apply to your work
"Compliance has to approve any discount over that limit."
Waiver -- an official document that lets one case not follow a normal rule
"I can request a waiver, but I cannot promise it will be approved."
Refund -- money given back to a customer
"A full refund is not possible, but the ticket could stay open for a year."
To escalate -- to pass a problem to somebody more senior
"I cannot approve it, but I can escalate it today."
Guidelines -- advice about how something should be done, not a strict rule
"The guidelines say ten days, but you can depart from them with a reason."
Fare -- the price a passenger pays for a flight
"The fare rules are in the contract, and they apply to every ticket."
To comply with -- to do what a rule or a law requires
"Every corporate contract has to comply with the same fare rules."
Grace period -- extra time you are allowed after a deadline before a penalty applies
"There is a twenty-four hour grace period, and you are still inside it."
Penalty -- an amount you pay or a consequence when a rule is broken
"After that date the penalty is higher than the ticket."
To flag -- to mark something so that other people notice it needs attention
"I will flag the invoice so that finance looks at it today."
Approval chain -- the sequence of people who must approve something
"The approval chain has three names on it, and mine is not one of them."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A client calls on a Monday morning with twelve tickets on the wrong day. He knows what the answer is going to be, and he calls anyway, which is a good sign: he would rather learn the rule than argue with it.

There are three levels, and mixing them up is what makes these calls go badly. Level one is the policy. If the contract says the change fee applies, it applies, and nobody can remove it by being sympathetic. Every corporate contract has to comply with the same fare rules. Level two is the guidelines: recommendations you should follow, and may depart from if you can explain why. Level three is the exception, which must be requested, must have a reason attached, and must be approved by somebody in the approval chain.

So Rosa does three things on the call. She states the rule first, because a client who hears the rule early stops hoping. Then she offers what she could do instead: no refund, but the tickets stay open for twelve months. Then she is honest about the limit — she can request a waiver and she can escalate it today, but she must not promise it before compliance approves it.

The last thirty seconds are the ones that keep the client. He asks what he should do differently, and she gives him exactly one thing: call before booking more than six seats, because there is a grace period on the group fare that almost nobody knows about, and after it the penalty is higher than the ticket. Then she says she will flag the invoice so that finance looks at it the same day.

1. Why does the text say "we must charge the fee" and not "we should charge the fee"?
A Because it is advice, and he can ignore it.
B Because it is a rule in the contract, with no choice involved.
C Because it is a guess about the invoice.
2. What is the difference between guidelines and policy, according to the text?
A Guidelines are recommendations you may depart from with a reason; policy is a rule.
B They are two words for the same thing.
C Guidelines are stricter than policy.
3. "She must not promise it before it is approved." What does that mean?
A It is optional, and she can decide.
B It is forbidden until the approval comes.
C She is not required to, but it would help.
4. Why does she tell him about the grace period?
A Because it is the answer to what he should do differently next time.
B Because it removes the fee on this booking.
C Because compliance asked her to.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Modals -- Obligation, Advice, Options and Guesses

GRAMMAR

Five small words, and each one sets a different temperature. You already know all of them. What this lesson trains is choosing the right one without stopping to think.

FormWhat it saysExample
must · have toObligation, no choice. Must often comes from the rule itself, have to from an outside circumstance. In most business sentences they are interchangeable."We must charge the fee."
"Compliance has to approve it."
should · ought toAdvice and recommendation. Strong enough to be useful, soft enough not to offend."You should call me before you book."
could · mightAn option, or a possibility you are not sure about. The polite way to put an idea on the table."We could keep the ticket open."
"It might be the fare class."
must, for a guessMust also means I am almost certain, when you are reading evidence instead of stating a rule."That must be the old fare — the contract changed in March."
The trap: two negativesmust not = forbidden. do not have to = optional. They are opposites, and confusing them changes the whole message."You must not promise it." (forbidden)
"You do not have to copy me." (optional)

After every modal comes the base verb: no to, no -s, no -ing. She must call, never she must to call and never she musts call.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

A client wants an exception and a new colleague wants to know the rules. Choose the best thing to say.

1. The client asks you to remove a fee the contract applies. Your first sentence is:
A No, that is impossible.
B Under the contract we must charge the fee, and here is what we could do instead.
C We must to charge the fee, sorry.
2. You want to advise him about next time. You say:
A You should call me before you book more than six seats.
B You shoulds call me before you book more than six seats.
C You must to call me before you book more than six seats.
3. A colleague asks whether he needs to copy the regional office on every email. He does not. You say:
A You must not copy the regional office on every email.
B You do not have to copy the regional office on every email.
C You cannot copy the regional office on every email.
4. You want to tell him that promising an unapproved exception is forbidden. You say:
A You do not have to promise the exception before it is approved.
B You must not promise the exception before it is approved.
C You should not to promise the exception before it is approved.
5. The invoice shows a price you do not recognize. You are almost sure why. You say:
A That should be the old fare.
B That must be the old fare -- the contract changed in March.
C That must to be the old fare.
