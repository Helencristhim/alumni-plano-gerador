The Cost of Choosing

Why the hundredth small decision of the day is harder than the first — and what that says about your routine.

Stage 1: Context and Purpose

By Eight in the Evening, What Is Left?

Think about an ordinary Tuesday. Not a hard day — an ordinary one. By the end of it, are you still able to decide things well? Or does something change?

What is the last decision you postponed simply because you were tired?

Stage 1: Context and Purpose

Before You Read: Where Do You Stand?

Choose a position for each statement. Then say why in one sentence.

Being tired affects my decisions more than being uninformed.
Having more options available makes people happier with what they choose.
A person with strong willpower does not get worn down by small choices.

Stage 4: Content Organization

What Is the Writer Doing Here?

Same text, different jobs. For each sentence, decide whether it states the argument, illustrates it, or tells you what to do.

Every choice, however small, spends a little of the same limited supply.
Shoppers at the end of a long day reach for whatever is nearest to hand.
Settle the trivial questions once, and stop asking them every morning.
Some people wear more or less the same clothes every working day.
Keep the decisions that matter for the hours when you are still fresh.
Stage 4: Content Organization

Your Idea Map — Keywords Only

Four nodes, three to five words each, in your notebook. Click a node if you want the prompt.

Node 1
The claim
In your own words: what is the writer actually arguing? One line, no quoting.
Node 2
The proof that convinced you
Which single example did most work on you? Why that one and not the others?
Node 3
Where it meets your week
One moment in your own routine where you recognise this. Be concrete: a place, an hour.
Node 4
The change worth making
One change, small enough that you would actually do it this week.
Stage 4: Content Organization

Order Them for Your Life

Most realistic for you at the top, least realistic at the bottom. There is no right order — only your reason.

Decide the small daily things once, then leave them alone.
Move the hardest decision of the week to the first hour of the day.
Cut the number of options before you start comparing them.
Accept a good enough answer and stop weighing up the rest.

Stage 5: Language Bridge

Two Verbs, Two Behaviours

I am weighing up two options right now.

By six o’clock I know that I will not decide well.

She usually defaults to the first thing she sees.

That seems reasonable to me.

Two of these can take the -ing form. Two cannot. Which, and why?

Stage 5: Language Bridge

Actions Happen. States Just Are.

Action verbs

Things you do. They can be in progress.

weigh up · compare · postpone · decide
I am weighing up two options.

State verbs

Things that simply are. No progress, no -ing.

know · seem · prefer · matter
I know I will not decide well.

How often

Where frequency sits in the sentence.

I usually default to…
I default to it most evenings.
Nine times out of ten, I…

Stage 6: Oral Preparation

Rehearse Only the First Ten Seconds

The opening is where fluent people sound hesitant. Fix that one piece and the rest follows. Choose an opening, adapt it, and say it twice.

What stands out to me is how ordinary the examples are.
I tend to leave the harder decisions until the end of the day.
One change I could make is deciding the small things the night before.
