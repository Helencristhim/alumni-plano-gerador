Chapter 4: The Code

The Four Pairs That Change the Operation

stop, remember, try, regret

Grammar Discovery

Same Verb, Opposite Fact

"He stopped monitoring the alarm after the second minute."

"He did not stop to read the fault code."

"He remembered signing it but did not remember checking anything."

"The master elected to try running the section on the emergency generator."

For each pair, say what actually happened and what did not. Then answer the one question the whole lesson turns on: which of the two forms describes an action that was COMPLETED?

FormWhat it doesExample
stop doing / stop to docease the activity / pause in order to do something elseHe stopped monitoring it. / He stopped to read the code.
remember doing / remember to dorecall a past action / not forget a future obligationHe remembered signing it. / He must remember to sign it.
try doing / try to doexperiment with a method / attempt something difficultHe tried running it on the generator. / He tried to restart it.
regret doing / regret to informbe sorry about a past action / formal notification of bad newsHe regretted not reading the code. / We regret to inform you.
The reading trapthe two forms are not stylistic variants: they describe DIFFERENT EVENTS. An option that swaps stopped monitoring for stopped to monitor reverses the fact while keeping every word of the sentence, and that is the cleanest distractor in the whole paper.

In a procedure text these pairs are not grammar: they are the record of what was done. Read the second verb before you read the noun, because the second verb is the fact.

Chapter 6: Say It Back

Say It Back

Two recordings, then the report in your own words

Listening 1

Eleven Hours on Watch

The officer of the watch, interviewed six weeks later. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What is the distinction he insists on at the start?
That he did not ignore the alarm. He watched it for two minutes, decided it was the sensor, and then stopped watching it -- which is a different thing.
2. What does he say he regrets, and how long would it have taken?
Not stopping to read the fault code. About fifteen seconds, and it would have told him the fault was in the circuit.
3. What does he say about the eleven hours and about the checklist?
He is not offering the eleven hours as an explanation. He remembers signing the checklist but does not remember checking a single item on it.
Listening 2

A Signature Is Not a Check

A superintendent on why checklists stop working. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she say happens to a checklist over about two years?
It starts as a procedure and ends as a signature. The first time somebody walks the deck with it; the fiftieth time somebody signs it in the office.
2. Why does she say adding items makes the problem worse?
Because the longer the form, the faster it is signed.
3. What do they do instead, and how does it reveal who really checked?
They change one item every month without announcing which. Whoever actually worked through the list notices; whoever only remembered to sign it does not.
