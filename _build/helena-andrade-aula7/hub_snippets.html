Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read it inside a real sentence. These are the words the exam text will use.

Procedure -- the official list of steps for doing a job on board
"The procedure says two people must check the reading."
Malfunction -- a failure of a machine to work as it should
"The malfunction was in the circuit, not in the sensor."
Alert -- an official message that something needs attention now
"An alert was sent to all ships in the area."
Deviation -- a change from the planned route or from the approved procedure
"The deviation was small but expensive."
Contingency -- a plan made in advance for something that may go wrong
"The contingency plan did not include this step."
Drill -- a practice exercise for an emergency
"The crew do a fire drill every month."
Hazard -- a source of danger that exists before anything goes wrong
"The hazard was known to the company."
Checklist -- an official list of steps that you check one by one
"He signed the checklist but did not check anything."
Precaution -- an action taken in advance to stop an accident
"Reading the code was a simple precaution."
Notification -- the official act of telling an authority that something happened
"The notification to the company was four hours late."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Gerund or Infinitive (Reading)

GRAMMAR

With four verbs, the second verb changes the fact: stop, remember, try, regret. Read the second verb first. You only need to read it and know what really happened.

FormWhat it doesExample
stop doing / stop to dothe activity ended / pause in order to do somethingHe stopped monitoring it = he was not watching any more. He stopped to read the code = he paused and read it.
remember doing / remember to doremember a past action / do not forget a future jobHe remembered signing it = he signed it before. Remember to sign it = sign it later.
try doing / try to dotest a method / make an effort to do something difficultHe tried running it on the generator = he tested this method.
regret doing / regret to informbe sorry about the past / formal start of bad newsHe regretted not reading the code. We regret to inform you...
The reading trapthe two forms are two different events. A wrong option in the exam changes stopped monitoring into stopped to monitor and keeps all the other words.

You do not need to write these pairs perfectly. You need to read the second verb and know what really happened.
