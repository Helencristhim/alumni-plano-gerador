Reading ASON Under Pressure -- Gerunds, Infinitives and the Pairs That Change Meaning

The vocabulary of operational procedure and the four verb pairs where the form changes the fact. Key words: procedure, malfunction, alert, deviation, contingency, drill, hazard, checklist, precaution, notification. Structure: gerund and infinitive with a change of meaning.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read it inside a real sentence. These are the words the exam text will use.

Procedure -- the official step-by-step sequence for doing something on board
"The procedure requires two people to confirm the reading."
Malfunction -- a failure of equipment to work as it should
"The report suggests the malfunction may have occurred before departure."
Alert -- a formal notification that something needs attention now
"An alert was issued to all vessels in the sector."
Deviation -- a departure from a planned route or an approved procedure
"It would appear that the deviation was unintentional."
Contingency -- a plan prepared in advance for something that may go wrong
"The contingency put forward was never tested."
Drill -- a practice exercise that rehearses an emergency procedure
"A drill is not a record: it is an operation."
Hazard -- a source of danger that exists before anything has gone wrong
"The company must identify the hazards its own operation creates."
Checklist -- an official list of steps that has to be verified item by item
"The pre-departure checklist had been signed but not worked through."
Precaution -- an action taken in advance to stop an incident from happening
"Sounding the whistle was the precaution nobody took."
Notification -- the formal act of telling an authority that something has happened
"Notification to the company was delayed by four hours."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

The officer stopped monitoring the alarm after the second minute. He did not stop to read the fault code, which would have taken about fifteen seconds and would have told him the opposite.

He said that he remembered signing the checklist but did not remember checking anything. The master elected to try running the affected section on the emergency generator, a step the contingency plan does not contain. The report notes that the officer regretted not reading the code, and closes with the sentence every company dreads: we regret to inform you.

1. He stopped monitoring the alarm. Was he still watching it afterwards?
A Yes. He paused and then continued.
B No. Stop + -ing means the activity ended.
C The sentence does not say.
2. He remembered signing the checklist. When did the signing happen?
A Before the remembering. The action is already complete.
B After the remembering. It was an obligation he fulfilled.
C At the same moment.
3. The master elected to try running the section on the generator. What does try + -ing tell you?
A That it was very difficult and he may have failed.
B That running it on the generator was a method he used to see whether it would help.
C That he refused to do it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Gerund or Infinitive -- When the Form Is the Fact

GRAMMAR

Most verbs take one form or the other and nothing changes. Four of them change the EVENT, and all four appear in procedure writing. Read the second verb first: it is where the fact lives.

FormWhat it doesExample
stop doing / stop to docease the activity / pause in order to do something elseHe stopped monitoring it. / He stopped to read the code.
remember doing / remember to dorecall a past action / not forget a future obligationHe remembered signing it. / He must remember to sign it.
try doing / try to doexperiment with a method / attempt something difficultHe tried running it on the generator. / He tried to restart it.
regret doing / regret to informbe sorry about a past action / formal notification of bad newsHe regretted not reading the code. / We regret to inform you.
The reading trapthe two forms are not stylistic variants: they describe DIFFERENT EVENTS. An option that swaps stopped monitoring for stopped to monitor reverses the fact while keeping every word of the sentence, and that is the cleanest distractor in the whole paper.

In a procedure text these pairs are not grammar: they are the record of what was done. Read the second verb before you read the noun, because the second verb is the fact.
