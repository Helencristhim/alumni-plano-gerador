Chapter 3: The Code

How Many? How Much?

many · much · a few · a little · a lot of

Grammar Discovery

Can You Count It?

"There are only a few seats left in the mezzanine."

"We still have a little time before the curtain goes up."

"How many tickets do you need?"

"There is not much information about the understudy."

Seats and tickets: you can count them. Time and information: you cannot. Which orange words go with which?

With nouns you CAN countWith nouns you CANNOT countMeaning
How many tickets?How much money?the question about quantity
a few seatsa little timesome — positive, there is enough
few seatslittle timealmost none — negative
not many touristsnot much informationa small quantity, in negatives and questions
a lot of / plenty of — with BOTH: a lot of seats, a lot of timea big quantity

In one line: many and a few count, much and a little measure — and money, time, information and advice never take an s.

Listening 1 · The Announcement

Before the Curtain

The voice everybody hears and nobody listens to. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. How long is the show, and how long is the intermission?
Two hours and thirty minutes, with one intermission of fifteen minutes.
2. Who is playing Anna tonight, and where can you find her name?
The understudy, Rachel Moore. Her name is on a small white slip inside the playbill.
3. What does the announcement say about drinks?
There is not much time at the break, so order the drink at the bar in the lobby before the show, and it will be waiting for you at the intermission.
Listening 2 · A Story

She Stole the Show

A Wednesday afternoon and a little white slip. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. How did she try to get tickets first, and what happened?
She entered the online lottery, because every evening performance was sold out, and she lost three times.
2. Why was the Wednesday matinee a good idea, according to her friend?
Fewer tourists, cheaper tickets, and a lot of seats in the mezzanine.
3. What happened at the curtain call, and why was it a surprise?
The whole theater stood up: a standing ovation for an understudy, on a Wednesday afternoon. She had never seen that before.
Listening 3 · The Box Office

Thank You for Calling

A recorded message full of days, numbers and rules. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. When are the matinees, and when is there still a little availability?
On Wednesdays and Saturdays at two o'clock. There is still a little availability on Wednesday afternoons, mostly in the mezzanine.
2. How many tickets can one person buy?
Up to four tickets per person, and not more.
3. What happens if you arrive late?
An usher seats you at a good moment in the show, and sometimes that means waiting until the intermission.
