Chapter 4: If Only...

A Different Past

If I had... · I would have...

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"If I had left the hotel earlier, I would have caught the first flight."

"If the snow hadn't started, they wouldn't have canceled the flight."

"If I had read the fine print, I would have known my rights."

"She would have flown that night if she had joined the waitlist."

What do the orange words have in common? Did these things really happen?

FormUseExample
If + had + past participlethe imaginary condition in the pastIf I had left earlier,
would have + past participlethe imaginary result in the pastI would have caught the flight.
hadn't / wouldn't havethe negative formsIf it hadn't snowed, we wouldn't have waited.
If + past, would + verb (Lesson 18)an imaginary present or futureIf I had time, I would travel.

Use the third conditional to talk about a past that did not happen: regrets, lessons, and "what if". Never say "If I would have": use If I had. In speech, "would have" sounds like would've.
