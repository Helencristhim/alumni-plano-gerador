Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Cancellation -- when a flight or an event is stopped and will not happen
"There were over two hundred cancellations at JFK last night."
Ground stop -- an order that no planes can leave an airport for a period of time
"The snowstorm caused a ground stop, so no planes could leave."
Missed connection -- when you arrive too late for your next flight
"Our first flight was late, so we had a missed connection in Miami."
Stranded -- stuck in a place with no way to leave
"Hundreds of passengers were stranded at the airport."
Waitlist -- a list of people waiting for a free seat or place
"I am third on the waitlist for the morning flight."
To be entitled to -- to have the right to have or to do something
"If the airline cancels your flight, you are entitled to a refund."
Reimbursement -- money that you get back for something you paid for
"I sent my hotel receipt to get a reimbursement."
To file a claim -- to make an official request for money or help
"I filed a claim for my damaged suitcase."
Fine print -- the small but important details in a contract or a ticket
"Read the fine print before you buy a cheap ticket."
Beyond our control -- something that we cannot change or stop
"I am sorry, but the weather is beyond our control."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

On Thursday night, Lucimara is at JFK, ready to fly home to São Paulo. Then the snow starts, and the airport announces a ground stop. At 9 p.m., she gets an email: cancellation. Hundreds of passengers are stranded, and the line at the desk is very long.

Lucimara calls the airline while she waits, and the agent puts her on the waitlist for the morning flight. Later, at the hotel, she thinks about the day. "If I had checked the weather, I would have flown on Wednesday. And if I had read the fine print, I would have known that the airline does not pay for the hotel when the weather is the problem." She keeps every receipt, just in case.

1. Why are hundreds of passengers stranded?
A Because the airline sold too many tickets for the flight, and there are no more seats on the plane.
B Because the snow caused a ground stop and cancellations.
C Because the desk is closed.
2. What would Lucimara have done if she had checked the weather?
A She would have flown on Wednesday.
B She would have stayed in New York for one more week and worked from the hotel near Times Square.
C She would have called her director.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "If I would have read the fine print, I would have known my rights as a passenger."
B "If I had read the fine print, I would know it yesterday."
C "If I had read the fine print, I would have known."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Third Conditional

GRAMMAR

How to talk about a past that did not happen: lessons, regrets, and "what if".

FormUseExample
If + had + past participlethe imaginary condition in the pastIf I had checked the weather,
would have + past participlethe imaginary result in the pastI would have flown on Wednesday.
hadn't / wouldn't havethe negative formsIf it hadn't snowed, we wouldn't have waited.
If + past, would + verb (Lesson 18)an imaginary present or futureIf I had time, I would travel more.
RuleThe third conditional is about the PAST, and both parts are imaginary: they did not happen. Never use "would" in the if part: say "If I had known", not "If I would have known". The two parts can change places: "I would have called you if I had known." In American English, the participle of "get" is gotten.

Quiz

A "My flight was canceled. What are my options?"

C "Why you cancel my flight?"
