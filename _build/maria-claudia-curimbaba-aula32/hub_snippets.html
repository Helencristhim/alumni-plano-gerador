Diplomatic Disagreement -- Conflict Resolution

Say no without closing the door: acknowledge the person, then disagree with the idea ("I understand why..., however", "I see your point, but", "I am not sure I agree", "with respect"). New vocabulary: tension, grievance, sore point, take sides, see eye to eye, mediate, de-escalate, clear the air, compromise, agree to disagree.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Tension -- a nervous, unfriendly feeling between people who disagree
"You could feel the tension in the room when the dates came up."
Grievance -- a complaint about something you think was unfair
"Houston's grievance is that nobody warned them about the delay."
Sore point -- a topic that makes somebody angry or uncomfortable because of the past
"Late shipments are a sore point with Tom since last year."
Take sides -- to support one person or group in a disagreement
"As the mediator, I cannot take sides."
See eye to eye -- to agree with somebody completely
"Tom and Carlos do not see eye to eye on the June dates."
Mediate -- to help two people or groups who disagree find a solution
"I offered to mediate between Houston and the plant."
De-escalate -- to make a conflict less serious or less angry
"A short pause helped to de-escalate the call."
Clear the air -- to talk honestly about a problem so that people can stop feeling upset
"We need one honest call to clear the air."
Compromise -- an agreement in which each side accepts less than it wanted
"The compromise is half the parts in June and half in July."
Agree to disagree -- to accept that two people have different opinions and stop arguing about it
"On the price, we agreed to disagree and moved to the dates."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

On Thursday Maria Claudia has to mediate a call between Tom in Houston and Carlos at the plant. There is tension between them: Houston's grievance is that nobody warned them about the delay, and late shipments are a sore point since last year. She decides not to take sides. She will start with the person: "I understand why this is frustrating, Tom." Only then will she disagree: "However, the line is full in June." If Tom says the plant ignored his order, she will say, "I am not sure I agree that it was ignored." She knows they will never see eye to eye on whose fault it was, so they will agree to disagree on the past and look for a compromise on the dates.

1. Why does she start with "I understand why this is frustrating"?
A Because she agrees that the plant made a mistake and wants to apologize for it.
B Because she wants Carlos to change the dates.
C To acknowledge Tom before she disagrees with him.
2. What will she say if Tom says the plant ignored his order?
A You are wrong about that.
B I am not sure I agree that it was ignored.
C I agree, the plant ignored the order and we will fix it this week for you.
3. What will they do about whose fault it was?
A They will agree to disagree and focus on the dates.
B They will ask the family board to decide who was wrong, and then they will discuss the dates.
C They will not talk at all.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Diplomatic Disagreement

GRAMMAR

In a conflict, the first half of your sentence is for the person and the second half is for the problem. Acknowledge first, then disagree with the idea -- never with the person.

JobPhrasesExample
acknowledgeI understand why..., I see your point, I can see why...I understand why Houston is upset.
then disagreehowever, but, even soHowever, the plant cannot ship earlier.
disagree softlyI am not sure I agree that..., I see it a little differentlyI am not sure I agree that it was ignored.
disagree firmlywith respect, with all due respectWith respect, the dates were in the contract.
move forwardcould we agree to..., what if we...Could we agree to half in June?

Key point: "I understand" is not "I agree". Say "with respect" or "with all due respect", never "with all respect". And use one contrast word only: "but however".

Stage 2: A Mediation in Order

Order

Listen first, then put the steps in the correct order.

?You disagree with the ideas that do not work, politely, without taking sides.
?You end with a compromise that both sides can accept, and you put it in writing.
?You listen to each side alone and write down the grievance and the position.
?You agree to disagree on the past, so the call does not go back to old sore points.
?You open the call by acknowledging both people before you talk about any solution.
