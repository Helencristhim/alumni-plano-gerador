Chapter 3 -- The Code

No, Without Closing the Door

I understand... however... I see your point, but...

Discovery

Four Sentences, Two Halves

Read the highlighted part of each sentence. Who is it for -- the person, or the problem?

"I understand why Houston is upset. However, the plant cannot ship earlier."

"I see your point, but the line is already full in June."

"I am not sure I agree that the plant ignored the order."

"With respect, the dates were in the contract."

JobFormExample
acknowledge the personI understand why... · I see your point · I can see why..."I understand why Houston is upset."
then disagreeHowever, ... · but ... · even so, ..."However, the plant cannot ship earlier."
disagree softlyI am not sure I agree that... · I see it a little differently"I am not sure I agree that it was ignored."
disagree firmly but politelyWith respect, ... · With all due respect, ..."With respect, the dates were in the contract."
move forwardWhat if we... · Could we agree to..."Could we agree to half in June?"

Key idea: the first half of the sentence is for the person, the second half is for the problem. Two traps: "I understand" is not "I agree" -- you can understand somebody and still say no; and it is "with respect" or "with all due respect", never "with all respect".

The Structure

Three Jobs in a Conflict

The same small phrases, doing three different jobs for the people in the room.

1 · Lower the temperature first

"I understand why this is frustrating, Tom."

Nobody listens to a solution while they feel ignored.

2 · Disagree with the idea, not the person

"I see your point. However, the line is already full in June."

The person keeps their dignity; the problem stays on the table.

3 · Move from positions to a way forward

"Could we agree to half in June and half in July?"

A compromise ends the argument; being right does not.

Notice what is missing: "you are wrong". A diplomatic speaker disagrees with the plan, never with the person.
