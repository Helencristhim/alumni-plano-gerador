Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Leonardo is a partner at a boutique law firm in Brazil. He works in tax law, and almost all of his clients are financial institutions: banks, insurers, fintechs and investment funds. Every month he presents projections to those clients, and he answers hard questions about them. These days he is working on the tax reform. Right now he is building three scenarios, because every stakeholder in the room wants a number before the rules change. He knows the technical side very well. What he is improving this year is the English that carries it.

1. Why does the text say "he is working on the tax reform" and not "he works"?
A It is a current project, in progress these days (present continuous).
B It is something he does every single day of his life.
C It finished a long time ago.
2. "Every month he presents projections" uses the present simple because:
A It is happening at this exact second.
B It is a routine that repeats.
C It is temporary, just for this week.
3. The text says "he knows the technical side", never "he is knowing". Why?
A Because the sentence is about the past.
B Because "know" is an irregular verb.
C Because "know" is a state verb, and state verbs do not take -ing.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple vs Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

How to say what is always true about your practice and what you are working on right now.

FormUseExample
Present Simple
I / you / we + verb (he / she + -s)		Facts, routines and permanent situations - always true.I advise financial institutions.
Present Continuous
am / is / are + verb-ing		Right now, today, these days - temporary or unfinished.I am reviewing the reform impact.
Negativedo not / does not + verb · am not / is not / are not + -ingWe do not advise individuals. / She is not leading that project now.
QuestionDo / Does + subject + verb · Am / Is / Are + subject + -ingDo you advise banks? / Are you working on the reform?
State verbsknow, want, need, understand, believe, mean, prefer almost never take -ing: "I need two more days" (not "I am needing").
Time wordsevery day, always, usually, never → simple · right now, today, this month, these days → continuous
