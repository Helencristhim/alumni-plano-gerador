Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

Back From the Trip -- Washington and Chicago

You are back from the United States, and everybody asks how the trip was. Key words: a landmark, a monument, a guided tour, to book, a neighborhood, crowded, a view, to walk around. Structure: past simple with was, were and regular verbs.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A landmark -- a famous building or place that people know
"The Lincoln Memorial is a famous landmark in Washington."
A monument -- a big structure that remembers a person or an event
"We visited the Washington Monument on the first day."
A guided tour -- a visit where a guide explains everything to a group
"We booked a guided tour of the Capitol."
To book -- to reserve a ticket, a table or a room before you go
"I booked the boat tour two weeks before the trip."
A neighborhood -- a part of a city where people live and work
"We stayed in a quiet neighborhood near the river."
Crowded -- with too many people in one place
"The museum was very crowded on Saturday."
A view -- what you can see from a high or open place
"The view from the tower was amazing."
To walk around -- to walk with no plan, just to see a place
"We walked around the city for hours."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Tom was in Washington for two days. On Saturday he walked around the National Mall. The museums were free, so he visited the Natural History Museum. In the afternoon he booked a guided tour of the Capitol.

On Sunday the Air and Space Museum was very crowded, so he stayed only one hour. In the evening he walked to the Lincoln Memorial, his favorite landmark.

Priya was in Chicago for a conference. She booked a boat tour on the river. The view of the tall buildings was amazing, and her hotel wasn’t in a busy neighborhood.

3. Which sentence is correct?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Simple -- Was, Were and Regular Verbs

GRAMMAR

Use the past simple for finished stories, like a trip. Was goes with one thing, were with two or more. Regular verbs take -ed.

JobFormExample
I / he / she / itwasThe trip was amazing.
you / we / theywereThe museums were free.
Negativewasn’t / weren’tThe tour wasn’t expensive.
Regular verbsverb + ed (for everybody)We visited the monuments.
Verbs that end in -e+ dWe arrived on Monday.
The trapthe past is the same for I and sheNot she visiteds. Say she visited.

Never: The museums was free · The trip were great · We visit the Capitol yesterday · She walkeds around
