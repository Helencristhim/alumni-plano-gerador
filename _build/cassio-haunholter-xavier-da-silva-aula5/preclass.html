Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Reinforced concrete -- concrete with steel bars inside it, so that it also resists tension
"The whole substructure is reinforced concrete."
Structural steel -- steel sections used to build the frame that carries a building
"A structural steel frame goes up much faster on site."
Masonry -- walls built from blocks or bricks laid one on top of the other
"We can do the external walls in masonry or in precast panels."
A load -- the weight or force a structure has to carry
"The equipment load on that slab is far higher than usual."
A span -- the distance between two supports of a beam or a roof
"At a thirty-metre span, steel becomes the obvious answer."
A foundation -- the part of the structure that transfers the loads into the ground
"The foundation depends on what the soil report says."
Precast -- made in a factory and brought to site ready to install
"Precast panels cut two months from the programme."
Cladding -- the outer layer that covers and protects the structure
"The cladding is metal, not masonry, on the east face."
Durability -- how well a material resists wear and the environment over time
"In a humid process area, durability matters more than cost."
A trade-off -- something you accept losing in order to gain something else
"The trade-off is cost against speed on site."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The design review opens with two options for the main frame. Option A is reinforced concrete; option B is structural steel. Everybody in the room already knows that steel is considerably faster to erect. The interesting part is everything else.

The load from the process equipment is far higher than usual, and the client may add more in three years. At an eighteen-metre span, the two options are nearly equal on the foundation, and steel is only slightly cheaper on the frame itself.

So the team recommends concrete, and says the trade-off out loud: four weeks on site, in exchange for future capacity. On the external walls the choice is easier. Precast panels are by far the quickest option, and their durability in a humid area is significantly better than masonry.

1. Why does the team recommend concrete even though steel is faster?
A Because steel is more expensive on the frame.
B Because the equipment load is far higher than usual and the client may add more later.
C Because concrete is quicker to erect.
2. What does “steel is only slightly cheaper on the frame” tell you?
A The difference in cost is small.
B The difference in cost is very large.
C Steel is more expensive than concrete.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “Precast is the most quickest option for the walls.”
B “Precast is by far the quickest option for the walls.”
C “Precast is more quicker option for the walls.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Comparatives, Superlatives and Degree

GRAMMAR

The comparison says which one. The degree word says by how much, and that is the part an engineer is listening for.

JobFormExample
A small differenceslightly / marginally / a little + comparativeConcrete is slightly heavier on the foundations.
A large differenceconsiderably / significantly / far / much + comparativeSteel is considerably faster to erect.
The clear winnerby far + the + superlativePrecast is by far the quickest option.
Two things that are equalas + adjective + asPanels are as durable as masonry here.
A difference in the other directionnot as + adjective + as / nowhere near asMasonry is nowhere near as durable as concrete.
The trapnever more + -er, and never the most + -estNot more cheaper. Say cheaper. Not the most fastest. Say the fastest.

Never: more faster · the most quickest · not so durable than · more heavy that steel

Stage 2: Put the Technical Argument in Order

Order

You are defending a design choice in a review. Put your argument in a logical order.

?"We are recommending the reinforced concrete frame."
?"The reason is the load: the process equipment is far heavier than usual."
?"And the client may add more equipment in three years."
?"The trade-off is about four weeks on site, because steel is considerably faster to erect."
?"On cost, the steel frame is only slightly cheaper, and the foundations are nearly the same."
?"So we would rather lose the weeks than oversize the frame."
