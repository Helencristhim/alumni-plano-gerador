Lesson 24 -- Checkpoint: Making the Case

Talk
Daniel H. Cohen -- "For argument's sake" (TED, 10 min)

A philosopher asks why we treat every argument as a war that somebody must win, and suggests that the loser of a good argument often gains the most. A short, funny talk about the same skills as this lesson.

Tip: write down his three models of argument. Then decide which one you use in a hearing, and which one you use at a dinner table.

Watch on TED ↗
Film
A Few Good Men (1992)

Two military lawyers defend two young Marines in a murder trial, and the case depends on who carries the burden of proof and on one witness under cross-examination. The courtroom scenes are a masterclass in poking holes in an argument.

Tip: watch the final cross-examination twice. The second time, note every question the lawyer asks and how each one prepares the next.

Podcast
NPR Hidden Brain -- "Is arguing with passion the most effective way to persuade opponents?"

A short episode about research on persuasion: arguments work better when they are framed in the other side's values, not in your own. A useful idea before you make a case to a client from another culture.

Tip: after listening, rewrite one argument you used this month so that it starts from the other side's values.
