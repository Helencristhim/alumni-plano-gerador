Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To make a case for -- to give reasons why something should be done or accepted
"Before you attack their plan, make a case for yours."
A premise -- an idea that an argument is based on and that the listener has to accept first
"If the premise is wrong, the conclusion does not matter."
To substantiate -- to show that a claim is true by giving evidence or facts
"Can you substantiate that figure, or is it an estimate?"
To rebut -- to show, with reasons, that a claim or an argument is wrong
"She rebutted every point in less than five minutes."
A straw man -- a weaker version of the other side's argument, invented so that it is easy to attack
"Nobody said we should close the office. That is a straw man."
A red herring -- a point that has nothing to do with the real question and takes attention away from it
"The CEO's salary is a red herring. The question is the delivery date."
To cherry-pick -- to choose only the facts that support you and ignore the others
"They cherry-picked three good months from a very bad year."
A slippery slope -- the claim that one small step will lead to a chain of bad results
"If we accept this clause, we will accept anything? That is a slippery slope."
To poke holes in -- to find the weak points in an argument, a plan or a story
"Before the hearing, ask a colleague to poke holes in your argument."
The burden of proof -- the responsibility to show that a claim is true
"The burden of proof is on the party that makes the claim."
To stand up to scrutiny -- to remain convincing when somebody examines it carefully
"His numbers did not stand up to scrutiny for more than ten minutes."
A line of reasoning -- a series of connected ideas that lead to a conclusion
"I follow your line of reasoning until the third step."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Thursday, a partner at Rodrigo's firm told the board that the firm should stop taking foreign clients. "We lost one international case last year," he said, "and next year it will be ten." Rodrigo did not attack him. First he asked a follow-up question: "Who told you the case was lost because the client was foreign?" Then he checked that he had understood: "So what you're saying is that one case tells us about all of them?"

Then he made his own case. "It could be argued that international work is risky," he said, "but what worries me is the evidence. The figures quoted in your note come from one year only. Our foreign revenue rose by 18% last year, to almost a third of the total. Not only did we win nine of our ten international cases, we also won two new clients because of them."

The partner had clearly cherry-picked his data, and his "next year it will be ten" was a slippery slope. "He can't have looked at the full report," Rodrigo thought. But he did not say it. He simply asked the board to read the numbers, and the proposal was never mentioned again.

1. "Who told you the case was lost?" Why is there no did in this question?
A Because who is the subject: the person who told him.
B Because Rodrigo is speaking to a partner, and formal questions in a board meeting never use an auxiliary verb.
C Because it is a negative question.
2. "He can't have looked at the full report." What does Rodrigo think?
A He is not sure if the partner looked at the report, so he plans to ask him about it after the meeting.
B He is almost certain that the partner did not look at it.
C He thinks the partner was not allowed to see the report.
3. Why was "next year it will be ten" a slippery slope?
A Because the partner used a number that was false.
B Because the partner attacked a weaker version of Rodrigo's argument instead of the real one.
C Because it claims that one lost case will lead to many more.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Block 2 Toolkit

GRAMMAR

No new grammar today. These are the eleven tools from lessons 13 to 23. In an argument, each one has a job.

Lesson and toolFormExample
13 -- Hedgingcould / might / it could be argued thatIt could be argued that the risk is small.
14 -- Trendsrise / fall + by (the change) + to (the result)Revenue rose by 18%, to 4 million.
15 -- Cleft sentencesWhat ... is ... / It is ... that ...What worries me is the evidence.
16 -- Reduced clausesnoun + past participle / -ingThe figures quoted in your note are old.
17 -- Past modalsmust / might / can't + have + past participleHe can't have looked at the report.
18 -- Compound nounsnoun + noun, number-nouna two-year supply agreement
19 -- ParaphraseSo what you're saying is ... / In other words ...So what you're saying is that one case is enough?
20 -- Modal passiveshall / may / must + be + past participleThe deposit shall be returned in 30 days.
21 -- Negative inversionNot only / Never / Only later + auxiliary + subjectNot only did we win nine cases, ...
22 -- Nominalizationthere has been a + noun / upon + nounThere has been a delay in the payment.
23 -- Follow-up questionssubject: who + verb / object: who + did + subjectWho told you? Who did you tell?

Never: Not only we won · shall returned · Who did tell you? · must have forget

Stage 2: Build the Argument

Order

Position, premise, evidence, the objection, the answer, the conclusion. Put the argument in that order.

?"My position is simple: the contract should have an English version."
?"The premise is that both parties must understand what they sign."
?"Last year, two of our disputes began with a translation problem."
?"Admittedly, a second version costs time and money."
?"But one dispute costs far more than any translation."
?"So, on balance, an English version is the safer choice."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose what a careful speaker would say.

In a meeting, the other side says: "You want to cancel the whole contract." You only asked to change one clause. You say:
A "That is a red herring."
B "With respect, that is a straw man. We only asked to change one clause."
C "You are completely wrong, and I think that everybody in this meeting can see it, including your own team."
A colleague shows you three good months from a very bad year to prove the strategy works. You say:
A "I'm afraid those months have been cherry-picked. What about the other nine?"
B "That is a slippery slope, because one good month will not necessarily lead to another good month next year."
C "Can you poke holes in the figures?"
The other side claims your client broke the contract, but they bring no evidence. You say:
A "The burden of proof is on us, so we will prepare every document before the hearing."
B "We must have broken it."
C "The burden of proof is on the party making the claim."
You want to show the board you have thought about the strongest objection. You start with:
A "Let me make the case for the other side first."
B "Not only I have thought about it, but I also discussed it with the whole team during the last two weeks."
C "What I want is that you agree."
