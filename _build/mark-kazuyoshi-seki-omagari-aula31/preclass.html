Lesson 31 -- Pre-class

By Then I Will Have... -- Future Perfect

A plan is a promise with a date on it. The future perfect stands on a day that has not arrived yet and looks back at what is already finished: by the time we land, we will have been in the air for twenty-six hours. The form, the two traps -- "by the time" with the present, and "by" against "until" -- and a trip planned backwards from the day it starts. Key words: itinerary, layover, long-haul, jet lag, set off, detour, off the beaten track, peak season, budget, pack light, fully booked, get around.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. These are the words a trip needs while it is still being planned, not the ones you use in the photographs afterwards.

Itinerary -- the day-by-day plan of a trip: where you are on each day and when you move
"Our itinerary says three days in Tokyo, then the train south."
Layover -- the wait between two flights, in an airport you are not there to visit
"We have a four-hour layover in Dubai on the way out."
Long-haul -- a flight of many hours, usually right across an ocean
"It is a long-haul flight, so I sleep for the first eight hours."
Jet lag -- the tired, wrong-hour feeling your body has after crossing time zones
"The jet lag will be over by the third morning."
Set off -- to start a journey
"We set off at five in the morning, before the traffic starts."
Detour -- a longer way round, either because you choose it or because you have to
"The best afternoon of the trip was a detour we did not plan."
Off the beaten track -- away from the places every tourist goes
"One day off the beaten track is worth three days in a line."
Peak season -- the weeks when everybody travels, and prices are at their highest
"We are flying in peak season, because school breaks do not move."
Budget -- the money you decide in advance that you are allowed to spend
"The whole budget is four thousand, food included."
Pack light -- to take one small bag and nothing you have to check in
"Pack light: you carry that bag through four subway stations."
Fully booked -- with no places left at all
"Half the hostels were fully booked four months in advance."
Get around -- to travel from place to place inside a city
"The transport card is the cheapest way to get around."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the plan and answer the questions. Notice every sentence in bold: each one stands on a day that has not arrived yet and looks back at what is already over.

Six months out, and the trip finally has dates. We fly on the fourth of July, which is peak season and therefore the worst possible week, but school breaks do not move. By the time we land in Tokyo, we will have been in the air for twenty-six hours, because the cheap flight is a long-haul one with a four-hour layover in Dubai. The first day is for the jet lag. Nothing is planned for it.

Here is the part I am proud of. I have been saving since February, and by the end of June I will have paid for the trains and the food myself. The flights are the deadline that frightens me, though. Half the hostels were fully booked in March, so by Sunday night I will have booked all three cities, and not one day later. We will not have decided the last two days, and that is on purpose.

The itinerary is Tokyo, then Kyoto, then Osaka, with one empty afternoon in every city. My cousin says every good thing that happened to her out there happened on a detour, somewhere off the beaten track that was in no plan at all. She also says two things I have written down: pack light, because you carry that bag through four subway stations, and buy the transport card on the first morning, because it is the cheapest way to get around.

The whole budget is decided, down to the last meal. By the time we set off, there will be nothing left on the list, which is the only way I know to enjoy the airport instead of surviving it.

1. In "By the time we land in Tokyo, we will have been in the air for twenty-six hours", when do the twenty-six hours end?
A Now, as the sentence is written.
B At the landing -- a moment in the future that the writer is looking back from.
C Some time after the landing, once they leave the airport.
2. Why is it "by the time we land" and not "by the time we will land"?
A Because the landing is not certain yet.
B Because after "by the time" English uses the present -- the other half already puts the sentence in the future.
C Because "land" is an irregular verb with no future form.
3. "By Sunday night I will have booked all three cities, and not one day later." What does "by" do here?
A It sets a point: the booking is finished on Sunday night at the latest.
B It means the booking continues all the way through Sunday night.
C It means the booking started on Sunday night.
4. Why does the writer say "We will not have decided the last two days" instead of leaving it out?
A Because he ran out of time and is apologizing for it.
B Because leaving those days open is part of the plan, and the negative says so on purpose.
C Because the last two days belong to somebody else's itinerary.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Future Perfect

GRAMMAR

The future perfect puts you on a day that has not arrived yet and lets you look back from it. The form never changes; what changes is the word you use to name the deadline.

PieceFormExample
affirmativewill have + past participle, identical for every person"By July I will have saved enough for the flights."
negativewill not have (won't have) + past participle"We will not have finished the itinerary by Friday."
questionWill + subject + have + past participle"Will you have booked the hostel by then?"
the deadline with byby + a point in time, or by then"By Sunday night I will have booked the flights."
the deadline with by the timeby the time + the present simple, never will"By the time we land, we will have been in the air for twenty-six hours."
alreadybetween will and have, for the part that surprises"By the time you wake up, I will already have packed."
by against untilby is a point and the thing is finished: "I will have booked it by Friday". until is a stretch and the action runs all the way there: "I will keep looking until Friday". Portuguese uses one word for both.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the prompt below. There is no right or wrong answer — bring the recording to class.

Take a trip you actually want to take -- where, when, how long, and with whom -- and stand on the morning you set off. From there, looking back, tell me four things: what you will have booked, what you will have saved, what you will not have decided yet, and what will already have gone wrong. Use a different time marker in every sentence -- by July, by then, by the time we set off -- and finish with the first deadline that is your responsibility this week.
