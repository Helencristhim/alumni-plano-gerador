Machines That Think -- Will, Likely, Might

What artificial intelligence is changing, and how sure we can be about the future. Key words: a chatbot, to automate, a breakthrough, to replace, reliable, accurate, a task, to keep up with, a skill, data. Structure: predictions with will, be likely to, might.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A chatbot -- a computer program that answers you in a conversation, in writing or by voice
"I asked a chatbot to plan my trip."
To automate -- to make a machine or a program do a task that people used to do
"The bank automated most of its customer service."
A breakthrough -- an important discovery or development
"The new medicine was a breakthrough."
To replace -- to take the place of someone or something
"Will robots replace drivers?"
Reliable -- something or someone you can trust, because it works well
"The answers are not always reliable."
Accurate -- correct and exact, without mistakes
"The translation was surprisingly accurate."
A task -- a piece of work that you have to do
"AI is good at repetitive tasks."
To keep up with -- to learn about new things fast enough to not stay behind
"It’s hard to keep up with technology."
A skill -- an ability to do something well, that you learn and practice
"Listening is an important skill for a leader."
Data -- facts and numbers that computers collect and use
"These programs learn from huge amounts of data."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Mirela enjoys reading about technology and the future. Imagine she asks a chatbot to plan a spring trip to Scotland. The plan is ready in ten seconds, but not everything is accurate: two museums on the list are closed in April. It is fast, but it isn’t always reliable.

Will machines replace people? Experts say AI will probably automate many repetitive tasks, and some jobs are likely to change a lot. But human skills, like leading a team, will still matter.

There is a new breakthrough every month, and it is hard to keep up with everything. One idea, though, is clear: AI learns from data, and people learn from experience. In the future, both might work together.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Predictions -- Will, Likely, Might

GRAMMAR

Use will when you are sure, will probably or be likely to when something is probable, and may or might when it is only possible. Probably goes after will and before won’t.

JobFormExample
Very surewill / won’t + verbNew jobs will appear.
Probablewill probably / probably won’tIt will probably change. It probably won’t stop.
Probable (formal)be likely to / be unlikely to + verbJobs are likely to disappear.
Possiblemay / might + verbIt might invent a fact.
An opinionI think... will / I don’t think... willI don’t think it will replace us.
The trapno to after might and will; maybe does not go with willNot it might to change or it will maybe. Say it might change.

Never: It will maybe change · It might to change · Jobs are likely disappear · It won’t probably

Stage 2: Put the Opinion in Order

Order

A manager gives her opinion about AI at a meeting. Put the ideas in a logical order.

?"Let me share what I think about AI and our team."
?"First, it will probably automate some repetitive tasks."
?"Second, our reports are likely to be faster."
?"But the answers aren’t always accurate, so we’ll check them."
?"I don’t think it will replace anybody here."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You are almost sure, but not 100%. You say:
A "AI will probably change my job."
B "AI will maybe change my job a lot in the next five or ten years, I think."
C "AI probably will to change my job."
Something is only possible. You say:
A "It might happen."
B "It will happen, I am completely sure about it, there is no doubt."
C "It might to happen."
You cannot trust a tool because it often fails. You say:
A "It’s not very reliable."
B "It’s not very automated, so it gives a different answer every time I use it at work."
C "It’s not very breakthrough."
You want to say technology changes too fast for you. You say:
A "It’s hard to replace technology, because it changes every single month."
B "It’s hard to keep up with technology."
C "It’s hard to automate technology."
