Chapter 4: The Code

Will, Likely, Might

How sure are you?

Grammar Discovery

How Sure Is the Writer?

"New jobs will appear."

"AI will probably automate many repetitive tasks."

"Some jobs are likely to disappear."

"It might give you a perfect summary, or it might invent a fact."

Put the sentences in order, from the most certain to the least certain. Green: very sure. Blue: probable, but not certain. Red: possible, maybe yes, maybe no. Look where probably goes: after will.

JobFormExample
Very surewill / won’t + verbNew jobs will appear.
Probablewill probably / probably won’tIt will probably change. It probably won’t stop.
Probable (formal)be likely to / be unlikely to + verbJobs are likely to disappear.
Possiblemay / might + verbIt might invent a fact.
An opinionI think... will / I don’t think... willI don’t think it will replace us.
The trapno to after might and will; maybe does not go with willNot it might to change or it will maybe. Say it might change.

In one line: sure: will; probable: likely to / will probably; possible: might / may.

Listening 1 · A Tech Journalist

Three Predictions

A British technology journalist makes three predictions for the next ten years. Sound first — no text.

1. What is her first prediction, and how sure is she?
Everybody will have a personal assistant on the phone; she is almost sure.
2. What does she say about driverless cars?
They are likely to be common in some cities, but not everywhere.
3. What is her least certain prediction?
That people might work only four days a week.
Listening 2 · A Doctor

AI in the Hospital

An American doctor explains how he uses artificial intelligence at work. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the AI tool do in his hospital?
It looks at X-rays and shows him areas that might be a problem.
2. Why does he still check everything?
Because the tool is fast, but it is not always accurate.
3. Does he think AI will replace doctors?
No: he thinks it will help them, but patients will still need a person.
Listening 3 · A Teacher

Learning in the Age of AI

A teacher from India talks about how AI is changing the way people learn. Sound first — no text.

1. How do her students use AI?
To explain difficult ideas, to practice conversation and to check their writing.
2. What is she worried about?
That students might stop thinking for themselves.
3. What will become more important, in her opinion?
Asking good questions and checking if the answer is true.
