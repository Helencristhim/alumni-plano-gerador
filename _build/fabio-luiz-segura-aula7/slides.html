Chapter 3: Who Did It, and Who Cares

The Fact in Front, the Author Behind

And the moment when doing that is a mistake

Grammar Discovery

Where Is the Person?

"Our team identified the gap in 2022."

"The gap was identified by our team in 2022."

"The pilot was approved last quarter, and Brazil is projected to grow again."

"I am responsible for every number on this page."

In the red lines the fact comes first and the person is late or missing. In the blue ones the person comes first, and the last one would be ruined by moving them.

JobFormExample
The fact matters, the author does notbe + past participleThe pilot was approved last quarter.
The author matters, but comes secondbe + past participle + byThe gap was identified by our team in 2022.
A forecast, with no author at allis projected to / is expected toTrade flow is projected to grow again this year.
Present, general institutional factis / are + past participlePayments are settled in two days on that corridor.
Ownership and leadershipACTIVE, alwaysI am responsible for every number. My team built it.
The trapthe passive needs be, and the participle, not the pastNot the gap was identify. Say was identified.
The other traptoo much passive hides youA whole presentation in the passive sounds like a report nobody wrote.

In one line: passive for the data, active for the decision. Facts do not need an author. Ownership does.

Listening 1 · The Baseline Rule

A Number on Its Own Is Decoration

Somebody who reads these presentations for a living. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say "forty per cent" tells her nothing?
Because she does not know from what, over how long, or compared with whom.
2. What does she say about growing forty per cent in a market that grew fifty?
That it is a loss of share described as a win.
3. What does she say to do if you cannot supply the three references?
Do not use the number at all, because the person opposite will remember that you could not defend it long after they have forgotten the number.
Listening 2 · The Two Seconds

Say the Number, Then Stop

What happens to people presenting data in a second language. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does he say people read the numbers fast?
Because the numbers are the part they are sure of, and reading them gives them somewhere safe to look.
2. What do strong presenters do instead?
They say one number, stop, say what it means, and stop again.
3. What is the second benefit of the two seconds of silence?
In those two seconds you are not speaking, so if the number was hard to say, nobody noticed.
Listening 3 · The Third Number

The One Nobody Volunteers

A research analyst on the number she almost never gets. Sound first — no text.

1. Which two numbers does she always get, and which one does she not?
She gets the size of the market and the bank's share of it. She almost never gets market penetration.
2. What does eighty per cent penetration tell her about a growth figure?
That the growth is about to stop, and everybody in the room can see it except the presenter.
3. Why does she take a presenter more seriously when they give penetration without being asked?
Because they have shown her the number that limits their own story, and she says that is not modesty but the strongest thing they can do.
