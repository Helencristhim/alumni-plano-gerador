Chapter 3 -- The Code

What If You Paid More?

Testing ideas without making a promise

Grammar Discovery

What Do the Purple Words Have in Common?

"If you paid a hundred million upfront, the committee would never approve it."

"If the division hit its targets, you would pay ten million more."

"We can agree, provided that the targets are realistic."

"As long as you keep the team, we can meet halfway."

Two test an idea. Two set a condition. What is the pattern?

UseFormExample
Test an idea (not real yet)If + past simple, would + verb"If you paid in cash, we would lower the price."
Firm, real offerIf + present, will + verb"If you sign this week, we will include the warranty."
Firm conditionprovided that / as long as + present"We agree, provided that the team stays."
Negative conditionunless + present"We will not sign unless the earn-out stays."
Neverwould after ifNot "If you would pay..."
Grammar Rule

Idea or Offer?

Second conditional (testing an idea)First conditional (a firm offer)
"If you paid in cash, we would lower the price.""If you pay in cash, we will lower the price."
"If we extended the deadline, would you sign?""If we extend the deadline, will you sign?"
"If part of the price were an earn-out, it would work.""If part of the price is an earn-out, it will work."
Sounds like a question: no commitment yet.Sounds like a promise: you are ready to deliver.

Chapter 4 -- Getting There

Project Vertex: The Price Gap

One call, two recordings, one proposal

Dialogue

Eduarda and Daniel

D
Eduarda, let's be honest. We're in a deadlock. We offered eighty million, and your client wants a hundred.
E
I understand, Daniel. But eighty was an anchor, and we both know the business is worth more.
D
Maybe. But we have leverage. Your client needs to close before the debt matures.
E
True, but my client has a fallback option. Another buyer called us last week.
D
Okay. If we paid a hundred million upfront, our investment committee would never approve it.
E
Then let's look for a trade-off. What if part of the price were an earn-out?
D
An earn-out could work, provided that the targets are realistic.
E
They are. If the division hit its targets next year, you would pay an extra ten million.
D
And what would the deal be contingent on?
E
On one thing: you keep the management team. As long as you do that, we can meet halfway at ninety million upfront.
Comprehension

About Daniel

1. How does Daniel describe the talks at the start?
As a deadlock: his side offered eighty million and the seller wants a hundred.
2. What leverage does Daniel think his side has?
The seller needs to close before the debt matures.
3. On what condition does Daniel accept an earn-out?
Provided that the targets are realistic.

Listening 2 -- The Trainer

The Golden Rule

A negotiation trainer explains one rule and the grammar behind it. Sound first -- no text.

1. What is the simple rule for any negotiation?
Never give something without getting something back.
2. When do you switch to the first conditional?
When you are ready to commit.
3. What should you know before you sit down, and why?
Your fallback option, because without it you have no leverage.

Thank you for a constructive call. Following our discussion, my client would be ready to proceed on these terms:

As long as we receive your confirmation by Friday, we will share the draft agreement next week.

Best regards,
Eduarda
