Conditional Structures in Negotiations

Project Vertex: the buyer offers eighty million and the seller wants a hundred. Key words: leverage, trade-off, anchor, deadlock, fallback option, earn-out, contingent, meet halfway. Structure: the second conditional to test an idea (If you paid..., we would...) and provided that / as long as to set a condition.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Leverage -- the power that gives one side an advantage in a negotiation
"A second buyer gives us leverage."
Trade-off -- giving up one thing in order to get another
"A lower price for a faster closing is a fair trade-off."
Anchor -- the first number put on the table, which frames the whole discussion
"Their offer of eighty million is just an anchor."
Deadlock -- a situation where neither side is willing to move
"After two hours, the talks were in a deadlock."
Fallback option -- what you will do if no agreement is reached
"Our fallback option is the second buyer."
Earn-out -- part of the price paid later, only if the business hits its targets
"Ten million of the price will be an earn-out."
Contingent -- depending on something else happening first
"The offer is contingent on board approval."
Meet halfway -- to compromise so that each side gives up the same amount
"Why don't we meet halfway at ninety million?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Northgate Capital wants to buy a division of Eduarda's client, but the two sides are eighty million and a hundred million apart. Daniel Price, the buyer's advisor, calls it a deadlock. Eduarda knows the first offer is only an anchor, so she does not accept it. She also knows her leverage: her client has a fallback option, a second buyer.

Instead of making promises, she tests ideas with the second conditional: If you paid ninety million upfront, my client would listen. If part of the price were an earn-out, both sides would win. These sentences sound like questions, not commitments, so nobody loses face if the answer is no.

When the idea starts to work, she sets firm conditions: provided that the targets are realistic, and as long as Northgate keeps the management team. The final deal is contingent on that team. In the end, both sides meet halfway at ninety million upfront, with an extra ten million if the division hit its targets.

1. Why does Eduarda use the second conditional to propose the earn-out?
A To test an idea without making a promise.
B Because the earn-out was already signed by both sides in the past, before the call started.
C To make a firm promise.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "If you would pay ninety million upfront, my client would listen to you."
B "If you paid ninety million, my client will listen."
C "If you paid ninety million upfront, my client would listen."
3. What is the final deal contingent on?
A On the second buyer making a better offer than Northgate before the end of the week.
B On Northgate keeping the management team.
C On a price of a hundred million.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Second Conditional and Firm Conditions

GRAMMAR

Use the second conditional to test an idea. Use provided that / as long as to set a condition.

JobFormExample
Test an ideaIf + past simple, would + verbIf you paid in cash, we would lower the price.
Ask about an ideaIf + past simple, would + subject + verb?If we extended the deadline, would you sign?
NegativeIf + past simple, would not + verbIf they stayed at eighty, we would not accept.
BeIf + were (all persons)If part of the price were an earn-out, it would work.
A firm offerIf + present, will + verbIf you sign this week, we will include the warranty.
A firm conditionprovided that / as long as + presentWe agree, provided that the team stays.

Never: If you would pay... · provided that you will keep... · If we lowered the price, they will sign.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose what a confident negotiator would say.

The buyer opens with a very low number. You say:
A "That number is an anchor, not a real valuation."
B "Okay, we accept eighty million, because it is your first number and it is fair for everybody."
C "Your anchor is our deadlock."
You want to test an earn-out without promising anything. You say:
A "If part of the price will be an earn-out, it would work."
B "If part of the price were an earn-out, would that work for you?"
C "If part of the price would be an earn-out next year, would that work for your committee?"
You accept ninety million, but only if the team stays. You say:
A "We accept ninety million, provided that you will keep the management team for two years."
B "We accept, unless the team stays."
C "We accept ninety million, as long as the team stays."
The buyer says your client needs them. You answer:
A "My client has a fallback option."
B "You are right, we have no leverage, so please make us any offer you think is fair."
C "If you would leave, we would call the other buyer."
