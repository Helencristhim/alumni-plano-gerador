Chapter 3: The Code

The Moment That Is Over

One past, and the little word that carries it in questions

Grammar Discovery

Where Did the Past Go?

"It started on my right hand three weeks ago."

"When did it start?"

"I did not change anything that week."

"The cream made it worse and two blisters appeared last Friday."

The blue words all name a time that is closed. Look at the red verbs: in the first and the last, the past sits on the verb itself. In the middle two, it moved somewhere else — where?

JobFormExample
Statement, regular verbverb + edIt started three weeks ago.
Statement, irregular verbits own past formThe cream made it worse. It got bigger.
Questiondid + subject + base verbWhen did it start?
Negativedid not + base verbI did not change anything.
The past moves ONCEdid already carries itNot did it started. Say did it start.
Time words that take the pastago, last week, yesterday, in March, when I wasTwo blisters appeared last Friday.
Irregular verbs to know herebegin → began, get → got, make → made, come → came, take → tookIt began after a flight.

In one line: the past lives on the verb in a statement, and on did in a question or a negative — never on both.

Comprehension

About Mrs. Nakamura

1. When was the onset, and where did the patches appear first?
About six weeks ago. Two patches on her elbows first, then one on her knee.
2. What changed in her life at that time, and what does she believe her trigger is?
She changed jobs in March and did not sleep for about a month. She believes stress is her trigger, and says it always was.
3. What did she put on the patches, and what happened?
A cream her sister gave her. It did not help and it burned.
Listening 1 · A Dermatologist

Eight Questions, In Order

A dermatologist teaching the sequence he uses every day. Sound first — no text.

1. Which question does he always ask first, and why?
When did it start. He says the date organises everything else, and the patient almost always remembers it more precisely than they expect.
2. What does he say about asking what makes it worse?
That patients rarely volunteer a trigger, so you have to ask directly, and you have to ask about the week before the onset, not about the general habits.
3. What is the question he says residents forget most often?
What have you already put on it. He says half of what he sees on the skin was caused by something the patient tried first.
Listening 2 · A Patient

The Story, Out of Order

A patient telling the same history the way people actually tell it. Sound first — no text.

1. In the correct order, when did the problem begin and what happened first?
It began in the spring, about two months ago, with one small dry patch behind her ear.
2. What did she try, in what order, and what happened each time?
First a moisturiser, which did nothing. Then her mother’s old cream, which helped for four days and then stopped working. Then nothing for three weeks.
3. What does she mention at the very end that she says she almost forgot?
That she started a new tablet for her blood pressure at the beginning of the spring.

The Note You Write Afterwards

Thirty-five year old woman with a six-week history of dry, scaly patches on both elbows and the right knee. The onset followed a job change in March and a month of poor sleep, which the patient identifies as her trigger. She used an unlabelled cream given by a relative; it did not help and it burned. The skin around one patch was swollen last week. Plan: examine the whole body surface, prescribe a topical steroid, and rule out infection.
1. The note says treatment tried: unlabelled cream, no benefit. Say that line as a full sentence in the past.
She used an unlabelled cream that a relative gave her, and it did not help.
2. What three things does the plan line contain?
To examine the whole body surface, to prescribe a topical steroid, and to rule out infection.
3. The paragraph says the onset followed a job change. Which word in the note tells you this is the patient’s belief and not a proven cause?
Identifies: the patient identifies it as her trigger. The note reports what she believes, and does not claim it was proved.
