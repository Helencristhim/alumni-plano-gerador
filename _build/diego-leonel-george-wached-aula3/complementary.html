NPR -- "Meet Lisa Sanders, The Doctor Behind House"

An interview with the physician whose diagnosis column inspired the series House, on the history as the diagnostic instrument and on how often the story arrives out of order. Clear American English, with the full transcript on the same page.

Tip: listen with a pen. Every time the episode quotes something a patient says, write down the past question you would ask next.

Audiobook
Lisa Sanders -- "Every Patient Tells a Story" (audiobook preview)

The physician behind the New York Times Diagnosis column, on the history as the diagnostic instrument. Every chapter is a case told twice: the way the patient told it, and the order in which it actually happened. That is the exercise of this lesson, in book form.

Tip: after one chapter, close the book and retell the case out loud in five past sentences, in chronological order. Then check how many details you moved.

Music
Johnny Cash -- "Hurt"

Four minutes of the slowest, clearest diction in American music, and a lyric written almost entirely in the past simple with irregular verbs: I hurt myself today, I wear this crown of thorns, what have I become. Every line is a finished moment, which is exactly the tense this lesson trains.

Tip: read the lyrics while you listen and list every past verb. Mark which ones are regular and which are irregular, and say the irregular ones out loud three times each.
