Chapter 3: The Code

Who, Which, Where

How to add context without stopping your sentence

Grammar Discovery

Necessary or Extra?

"Tom, who runs finance at Brightline, saw the first warning sign."

"The payroll bridge, which went live in March, had zero errors."

"We spent a week in Chicago, where Brightline has its main office."

"We didn't listen to him at first, which was a mistake."

Cover the colored part of each sentence. Does the sentence still work? Then the colored part is extra information. Look at the commas, and at the word after the first comma: when is it who, which or where? And what does which refer to in the last sentence?

ForFormExample
People, who + verb ,Tom, who runs finance, saw it.
Things and companies, which + verb ,Kestrel, which invests in logistics, ...
Places, where + subject + verb... in Chicago, where we met the team.
A whole ideasentence + , which + verbWe waited, which was a mistake.
The testremove it: the sentence still worksTom saw it. (still a full sentence)
The trapnever that between commasNot Tom, that runs finance,. Say Tom, who runs finance,.

In one line: extra information goes between commas with who (people), which (things or a whole idea) or where (places), never that.

Chapter 4: The Panel

Lessons from the Front Line

One panel, two recordings, one program

Dialogue

On Stage

D
Welcome back. Carlos, you led the payroll bridge, which went live with zero errors. What made the difference?
C
Thank you, Diana. I'd like to build on what Daniel said. The real difference was testing, which nobody wanted to pay for.
D
Can you give the audience a case in point?
C
Sure. In December, Tom, who runs finance at Brightline, saw some strange numbers in the old data.
D
And the team listened to him?
C
Not at first, which was a mistake. But I won't digress. The point is simple: listen to the people who know the data.
D
That's a thought-provoking point. Would anyone on the panel like to chime in?
C
Before they do, one quick anecdote. Our first payday, which was on March fifth, was the quietest day of my career. Nobody called.
Comprehension

About Diana

1. What does Diana say about the payroll bridge when she introduces Carlos?
That it went live with zero errors.
2. What does Diana ask Carlos to give the audience?
A case in point: a clear example.
3. What does Diana invite the other panelists to do?
To chime in with a comment.
Listening 1 · The Moderator

What the Best Panelists Do

An experienced moderator explains what good panelists do. Sound first — no text.

1. How many panels has she moderated?
More than two hundred.
2. How long should you speak when you chime in?
About thirty seconds, not three minutes.
3. Where should you put the extra information in a sentence?
In the middle, between commas.
Listening 2 · The Opening

A Confession on Stage

Another panelist opens with a story about his first acquisition. Sound first — no text.

1. What does Kestrel Partners invest in?
Mid-size logistics companies.
2. Why did their first acquisition almost fail?
They forgot about people: the CEO left after three months, and half of his team followed him.
3. What do they do now before they sign?
They spend a full week in the company and meet everyone.
