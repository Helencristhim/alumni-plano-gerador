Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Moderator -- the person who leads a discussion and gives the turns to speak
"The moderator gave each speaker two minutes to start."
Panelist -- one of the speakers who sit on a panel and answer questions
"Each panelist came from a different industry."
Build on -- to add to an idea that someone else has already shared
"I'd like to build on what Daniel said about people."
Chime in -- to join a conversation with a short comment
"Can I chime in here for a second?"
Digress -- to move away from the main topic while you are speaking
"Sorry, I digress. Back to the question."
Anecdote -- a short, interesting story about something that really happened
"He opened with an anecdote about his first deal."
Case in point -- a clear example that shows your point is true
"Data is the biggest risk. The payroll bridge is a case in point."
Thought-provoking -- making people think seriously about something
"That was a thought-provoking question from the audience."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At the M&A Integration Forum in New York, Carlos sits on a panel with three other speakers. Diana, who sits on the Northway board, is the moderator. The audience does not know the deal, so Carlos gives context in the middle of his sentences.

“The payroll bridge, which went live in March, had zero errors,” he says. “Tom, who runs finance at Brightline, saw the first warning sign. We spent a week in Chicago, where Brightline has its main office. We did not listen to Tom at first, which was a mistake.”

Notice the pattern. The extra information goes between commas. Use who for people, which for things or a whole idea, and where for places. If you remove it, the sentence still works. Never use that between commas.

1. Why does Carlos add context in the middle of his sentences?
A Because the audience does not know the deal.
B Because the moderator asked him to speak for a very long time.
C Because he wants to digress.
2. Who is the moderator of the panel?
A Tom, from Brightline finance.
B Diana, who sits on the Northway board.
C Daniel, who leads integration at another company.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “Tom, that runs finance, saw the numbers.”
B “Tom, which runs finance, saw the numbers.”
C “Tom, who runs finance, saw the numbers.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Non-Defining Relative Clauses: Who, Which, Where

GRAMMAR

Put extra information between commas, in the middle or at the end of a sentence. The sentence still works without it.

ForFormExample
People, who + verb ,Tom, who runs finance, saw it.
Things and companies, which + verb ,The bridge, which went live in March, worked.
Places, where + subject + verbWe met in Chicago, where Brightline has its office.
A whole ideasentence + , which + verbWe waited, which was a mistake.
Possession, whose + noun ,Mark, whose company we bought, joined us.

Never: Tom, that runs finance, · Diana, which sits on the board, · Kestrel, who invests in logistics, · The bridge, which went live in March had zero errors (one comma missing)

Stage 2: Put the Opening in Order

Order

Carlos gives his two-minute opening on the panel. Put his sentences in a logical order.

?"Tom, who runs finance at Brightline, was the first to see it."
?"I'm Carlos, and I work at Accenture Strategy, where I lead integration projects."
?"Our biggest risk was the old data, not the strategy."
?"This year we integrated Brightline, which Northway acquired last year."
?"So my point is simple: listen to the people who know the data."
?"Thank you, Diana, and good afternoon, everyone."
