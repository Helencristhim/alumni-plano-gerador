Stage 1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Four words for duties, four for rights and positions. Listen to each one and read it in context.

to undertake -- to promise formally, often in a contract, to do something
"The seller undertakes to deliver a monitoring report within sixty days."
to refrain from -- to not do something because a rule or an agreement says so
"We must refrain from selling the same credits to anybody else."
to commit to -- to promise to do something and to keep doing it
"We commit to keeping the registry up to date."
to postpone -- to move an event or an action to a later date
"Rafael wanted to postpone signing until the spring."
to be entitled to -- to have the legal right to have or to do something
"The buyer is entitled to terminate the agreement if we deliver late."
to seek -- to try to get or to achieve something (formal)
"The buyer is already seeking other projects."
to object to -- to say that you do not agree with something or do not accept it
"Their counsel may object to reviewing the price."
to waive -- to decide not to use a right or a rule that you have
"We can waive the price review for the first year."

Stage 3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about what it says.

A contract is a list of promises: things you will do, and things you will not do. In English, many of those promises are two verbs together, and the second verb has a fixed form.

Some verbs want to. The seller undertakes to deliver a report. The buyer agrees to pay within thirty days. The buyer is entitled to terminate if the credits arrive late. Other verbs want -ing. Amanda would avoid postponing the signature, and she recommends accepting the clause, never "recommends to accept".

After a preposition, the verb is always -ing. The seller must refrain from selling the same credits. Both sides commit to reviewing the price. And the buyer may object to paying before the verification.

The difficult word is to. In "entitled to terminate", to is part of the verb. In "object to paying", to is a preposition: you could also say "object to the price". If a noun can follow it, use -ing.

1. According to the text, what form comes after a preposition like from?
A The -ing form.
B The infinitive with to.
C The base form, with no to and no -ing.
2. Why is it "object to paying" but "entitled to terminate"?
A Because object is a formal verb and entitled is an informal one.
B Because in object to, to is a preposition, and in entitled to, it is part of the verb.
C Because paying is a noun and terminate is an adjective.
3. Which sentence does the text say is correct?
A She recommends to accept the clause as soon as possible.
B She recommends accept the clause.
C She recommends accepting the clause.

Stage 4: Grammar Tip -- doing or to do

GRAMMAR

Some verbs are followed by -ing, some by to, and after a preposition the verb is always -ing.

PatternVerbsExample
verb + -ingavoid, consider, recommend, suggest, postpone, delay, riskI would avoid postponing the signature.
verb + toagree, undertake, seek, refuse, fail, decide, intendThey agreed to link the payment to the issuance.
preposition + -ingrefrain from, commit to, object to, look forward toWe must refrain from selling the same credits.
verb + somebody + towant, ask, require, expectThey want us to deliver the report in sixty days.
Negativenot goes before the second verb: "We agreed not to sell the credits." "I suggest not waiting."
Questionthe pattern does not change: "Did they agree to link the payment?" "Would you consider waiving the review?"

Quick test before you speak: is there a preposition before the verb? If a noun could go there, use -ing.
