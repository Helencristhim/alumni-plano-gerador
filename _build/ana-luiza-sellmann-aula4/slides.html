Chapter 3: The Code

Two Halves, One Sentence

The condition, and the consequence

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"If it rains, we will move to my place."

"If I finish early, I will make it to the class."

"If the client approves it today, we will start on Monday."

"What will we do if nobody comes?"

Every sentence has two halves. One half never takes will. Which one, and why?

HalfFormExample
The conditionif + present simple. Never will, even though it is about the future.If it rains, ...
The consequencewill + base verb. In speech it is almost always the contraction...., we'll move to my place.
Either order"If you send the brief, I'll review it today." = "I'll review the brief today if you send it." With if first, use a comma. With if second, no comma.
Softer optionsInstead of will you can say might (maybe), can (possible) or should (advice): "If they change the brief, we can adjust the plan."
unlessUnless means "if not": "Unless it rains, we meet at the park."

One shape, and it never changes: if + present, will + verb. The only thing you have to remember is that will never lives in the if half.

Detail

How Sure Do You Want to Sound?

A PROMISE

"If you send me the brief today, I will review it before Friday."

A POSSIBILITY

"If we launch in March, we might reach a younger audience."

AN OFFER

"If they cut the budget, we can move to the smaller venue."

ADVICE

"If the client is nervous, we should send the plan in writing."

Same shape, four levels of commitment. Choosing the word is choosing how much you promise.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"If it will rain, we will move to my place."
"If it rains, we will move to my place."
"If the client approve it, we start Monday."
"If the client approves it, we'll start on Monday."
"If you will send the brief, I will review it."
"If you send the brief, I'll review it."

The word will never goes inside the if half, even when the whole sentence is about the future. And the verb after if follows the normal present rules: he approves, she sends.

About Leo

He will move the whole thing to his place. That is the backup plan.

3. When will he give her a heads-up?
If two more people say yes tonight.

Free Practice

Two Options, One Recommendation

Scenario: The client has cut the budget by twenty per cent and asks what happens now. Present both options as conditions, say what you will lose in each one, recommend one, and finish with what you need from them and by when.
