Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

There is one question shape that almost never gets a one-word answer. "Have you ever been to Brazil?" "Have you ever worked with an American team?" It asks about a whole life, with no date attached, and the other person has to look back and find something. That is why it keeps a conversation alive when "Do you like your job?" kills it. The answer comes in two parts. If it is no, the word is never, and the verb stays positive: "I have never seen a race live." If it is yes, the moment you add a date the sentence changes tense: "Yes, I went there in 2019." Have and a date cannot live in the same sentence.

1. Why does "Have you ever been to Brazil?" work better than "Do you like Brazil?"
A Because it is a longer question.
B Because it asks about a whole life, so the other person has to find an experience to tell.
C Because it is more polite.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "I have not never worked abroad."
B "I have never worked abroad."
C "I never have not worked abroad."
3. What happens when you add a date?
A Nothing. "I have seen that race last year" is correct.
B The sentence goes into the past simple: "I saw that race last year."
C You have to add ever as well.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present perfect with ever and never

GRAMMAR

Experience with no date. Have or has plus the past participle.

ShapeFormExample
QuestionHave / Has + subject + ever + participleHave you ever been to Brazil?
Answer: nosubject + have / has + never + participleI have never seen it.
Answer: yesYes, + past simple for the detailYes, I went there in 2019.
Third personhas, never haveShe has never tried it.

The participles you need most here: be → been, see → seen, do → done, go → gone, eat → eaten, write → written. Regular verbs simply take -ed: worked, tried, enjoyed. And never already means no, so the verb after it never carries a second negative.
