Chapter 3: Ever and Never

The Question That Opens Doors

Experience, not facts

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Have you ever been to Brazil?"

"I have never seen a Formula 1 race live."

"Have you ever worked with an American team?"

"She has never tried Brazilian food."

ShapeFormExample
QuestionHave / Has + subject + ever + participleHave you ever been to Brazil?
Negative answersubject + have / has + never + participleI have never seen it.
Positive answerYes, + past simple for the detailYes, I went there in 2019.
Never says nono not with neverI have never been. (not have not never)

The present perfect asks about a whole life, with no date. As soon as you say when, the sentence switches to the past simple: "Yes, I went there in 2019."

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

✗ WRONG

"Have you ever went to Brazil?"

✓ RIGHT

"Have you ever been to Brazil?"

✗ WRONG

"I have seen that race last year."

✓ RIGHT

"I saw that race last year."

After have comes the participle, not the past: been, seen, done, tried. And a date kills the present perfect: with last year, in 2019 or yesterday, use the past simple.

Chapter 4: The Conversation

Coffee, and Nobody You Know

Twenty-five minutes, one stranger

Dialogue

At the Coffee Table

T
That last talk was longer than I expected. Have you been to this conference before?
M
Three times now. I am Megan, by the way. Good icebreaker.
T
Thiago. Honestly, I never know what to say in these breaks. It always feels awkward.
M
For everybody. The trick is to ask about experience. Have you ever worked on a project abroad?
T
Not yet, but I work for an American company. Have you ever been to Brazil?
M
I have never been to South America. But my brother went to Sao Paulo last year and he never stopped talking about it.
T
What did he like most? People always bring up the food.
M
The food, and a race. He went to the Interlagos circuit.
T
There is our common ground. I have followed Formula 1 since I was a child.
M
Then you two would get along. He is here somewhere.
T
Introduce us at dinner and we can catch up properly. This was a good chat.

A conference announcement. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. How long is the coffee break, and why does it matter?
Twenty-five minutes, and it is where most delegates find the people they work with later.
2. What is the first piece of advice for a first-time delegate?
Not to stand with the colleagues they came with.
3. What should you ask if the conversation stops?
What the other person has enjoyed most so far.
