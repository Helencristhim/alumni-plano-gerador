The Real Problem

It Is Not the Speed

Everybody says fast English is the problem. It is not really the speed. It is that at speed, English glues words together and throws sounds away.

Whaddaya gonna do about it? That is six words. Say it slowly and you already know every single one of them.

Chapter 3: Two Fast Voices

Two People Talking Normally

Nobody is going to slow down for you first

Dialogue

The Frenchman Who Also Loses Them

M
Ana, be honest with me. On those calls with the Americans, do you actually keep up, or do you just wait for the email afterwards?
A
The first two minutes usually go completely over my head. After that I get about half.
M
Same. And it is not the vocabulary. When they write it down it is crystal clear. When they say it, everything is glued together.
A
Because they are not saying going to. They say gonna. And they swallow the sound in the middle of twenty.
M
So it is not that they mumble. It is that the sounds are genuinely not there.
A
Exactly. And I made it worse for years by listening word for word. If I lose one word I stop, and then I lose the next twelve.
M
That is a very good description of my Monday mornings. So what do you do instead?
A
I let the small ones go. If somebody rattles off a whole sentence I take the two words that carry the meaning and I keep going.
Comprehension

About Marc

1. What does Marc admit about calls in English?
That he is not sure he keeps up either — he asks Ana whether she actually follows or just waits for the email afterwards. It is his Monday mornings too.
2. What is Marc’s explanation for the problem, and what does Ana add to it?
Marc says it is not the vocabulary, because written down it is crystal clear; everything is glued together in speech. Ana adds that the sounds are genuinely missing: gonna, and the swallowed t in twenty.
3. What does Marc conclude at the end, in his own words?
That the speakers are not mumbling — the sounds are genuinely not there. It is a different problem from bad pronunciation, and it needs a different solution.

Chapter 4: The Code

What Happened to the Spaces?

gonna · wanna · didja · lotta — four reductions, most of your problem

Grammar Discovery

Say the Right-Hand Side Out Loud

"What are you going to do? — Whaddaya gonna do?"

"I want to talk to you about it. — I wanna talk to ya about it."

"Did you see the beams yet? — Didja see the beams yet?"

"It is a lot of work. — It’s a lotta work."

Read the left-hand side. Now read the right-hand side out loud, fast. They are the same sentence. Which sounds actually disappeared, and which ones only moved?

In one line: English is not written with the spaces it is spoken with.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I am going to gonna call him tomorrow."
"I am gonna call him tomorrow."
"I wanna to see the house on Saturday."
"I wanna see the house on Saturday."
"He gonna come at eight."
"He is gonna come at eight."
"We gotta to leave in ten minutes."
"We gotta leave in ten minutes."

Three of these four are the same error: gonna, wanna and gotta already contain the to, so adding another one makes the sentence collapse. The third is different — gonna replaces going to, never is going to, so the verb be stays exactly where it was. And one warning: these are spoken forms. Say them, hear them, and do not write them in an email.

Listening 2

Twelve Years Before I Heard It

A French woman on the day the noise turned into words. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What could she do well, and what made her believe her English was fake?
She could read anything and write reports, but on the telephone she understood almost nothing.
2. What did the colleague do, and what did she discover?
The colleague showed her a transcript while the recording played. Every word on the page was a word she already knew — she simply had not heard them, because they were not said the way they are written.
3. What is her advice about order, and why?
Listen to the fast version first and read the transcript afterwards. The other order teaches you nothing, and slow clear teachers were kind but useless.

The Voicemail, As You Heard It

Duration 0:34
Not edited

1. Say the 0:04 line the way it is written in a book. What has been reduced?
"We are going to have to move Thursday." Going to became gonna, have to became hafta, and we are became we’re. Three reductions in six words.
2. The 0:13 line has two reductions. Name both.
"Did you get the quote I sent you on Monday?" Did you fused into didja, and you flattened to ya. Neither sound was lost — they were joined.
3. What does gimme a ring mean, and what has happened to it?
"Give me a call." Give me collapses into gimme, and a ring is simply British and Australian for a phone call. The reduction is the sound; the ring is the vocabulary.
