Chapter 3: The Code

One Question, Four Speeds

Nothing is added. Things are only taken away.

Discovery

What Went Missing?

Word by word

"What, are, you, going, to, do, on Saturday?"

The same sentence, normal speed

"What are you going to do on Saturday?"

Faster

"Whaddaya gonna do on Saturday?"

Talking to somebody he knows

"Whatcha doin Saturday?"

Four versions of one question, and no new word in any of them. Play the first two one after the other: written out, it is going to; said at normal speed, the very same sentence already arrives as gonna. From there the orange part is where several words collapse into a single sound, and the blue word is gone altogether.

MachineWhat it doesSlowFast
1. LinkingA final consonant jumps onto the next vowelpick it uppi-ki-tup
2. Weak formsto, of, and, for, can, are lose their vowela cup of coffeea cuppa coffee
3. ReductionsFixed spoken forms, learned as single wordsgoing to · want to · got to · let megonna · wanna · gotta · lemme
4. Vanishing soundsA t between vowels softens; an h at the start of a small word dropswater · did you · what are youwader · didja · whatcha
Who reduces mostNative speakers, at home, with people they know well—Todd, in Chicago
Who reduces leastAnybody speaking English as a second language, anywhere—Camille, and you

In one line: the more at home a speaker feels, the more of the sentence disappears — which is why the French colleague is often easier to follow than the American.

Chapter 4: Many Englishes

Same Weekend, Two Speeds

Todd from Chicago, and Camille from Lyon

Dialogue

Todd Books the Car

T
Hey, so the visa thing worked out, I am gonna be in the city on the ninth and I wanna come out your way on Friday if that works for you.
A
Todd, you are talking a mile a minute. Say the second half again, slowly.
T
Sorry. Friday. I want to come and see you on Friday.
A
Friday is fine. And there was a date before that which I did not get — you slurred it a little.
T
The ninth. I land on the ninth, ten forty in the morning. Lemme send you the flight, it is faster than saying it.
A
Please do. When you rattle off numbers they go in one ear and out the other.
T
Everybody tells me that. My sister says I mumble. I dunno, I have talked like this my whole life.
A
You do not mumble. You take the joints out of the sentence. Say the date once more and I will write it down.
Comprehension

About Todd

1. Todd mentions two different days. What happens on each one?
He lands in the city on the ninth, at ten forty in the morning. He wants to come and see her on Friday.
2. What does he offer to do instead of saying the flight details out loud, and why?
He offers to send them in writing, because it is faster than saying them.
3. What does his sister say about the way he speaks, and does Ana agree with her?
His sister says he mumbles. Ana does not agree: she says he does not mumble, he takes the joints out of the sentence.
Listening 1 · Chicago

A Voice Note from Todd

Thirty seconds, at full speed. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. GIST: what is the one thing Todd wants you to confirm?
Whether Friday still works, so that he can book the car tonight.
2. DETAIL: why can he not come straight to you on the ninth?
He lands at ten forty in the morning but has to stay in the city that first night because of the car.
3. He warns you about two things. What are they?
He does not eat fish, and his phone will be useless out there, so if he does not answer it is nothing personal.
Listening 2 · Lyon

And Then Camille Replies

Same weekend, half the speed. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. GIST: what does Camille want to know from you?
Whether Saturday is convenient for you. She asks you to send a message when you have time.
2. DETAIL: when does she arrive, and how does she get to you?
On the tenth, one day after Todd. She takes the bus, so nobody needs to come and collect her.
3. What is the one thing she says she must tell you?
That she goes to bed extremely early, so if you go out at night she will disappear before midnight, and it is nothing personal.

1. Both of them want the market. Is that a problem, and what do you propose?
Todd wants it on Friday and Camille on Saturday. It is not a problem, because they arrive a day apart. You can go twice, or ask Todd to wait one day and take both of them on Saturday.
2. One line on this page is a guess, not something you heard. Which one, and why?
"Friday: the market + ?????" — Todd said he was not going to plan the afternoon, so there is nothing to write there. The question marks are hers, not his.
3. One note says "played twice". Which voice was it, and what exactly made it hard?
Todd. Not the vocabulary — every word in that message is a word you already know. It was the speed, the reductions (gonna, gotta, wanna, lemme) and the linking between them.
