Lesson 9 -- Understanding Fast American English

Video
Rachel’s English -- "FAST ENGLISH: Everything You Need To Speak Fast English Like a Native Speaker"

Eighteen minutes on exactly tonight’s code: gonna, wanna and gotta, the linking between words, and the sentence stress that makes Americans sound so fast. Every example is played slowly first and then at natural speed.

Tip: do not only listen. Say each reduced version out loud with her. Producing it is what teaches your ear to recognize it.

Listening
NPR Short Wave -- "Can dogs understand the meaning of words? Scientists are trying to figure it out"

Nine minutes on whether the dogs pressing soundboard buttons online really understand the words. Two American hosts at full natural speed, on a subject you actually care about.

Tip: listen once with no help and write down the gist in one sentence. Only then open the free transcript on the same page and see how much you had.

Talk
TED -- John McWhorter, "Txtng is killing language. JK!!!"

A linguist explains why speech was never meant to sound like the written page: it is loose, fast and full of shortcuts, and that is normal. It is the idea behind tonight’s whole lesson, and he is a fast American speaker himself.

Tip: watch it once with no subtitles and aim only for the gist. Turn the English subtitles on the second time and count how many reductions you had already caught.
