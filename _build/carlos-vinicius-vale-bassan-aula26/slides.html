Chapter 4: The Code

Must, Might, Can’t

How to say how sure you are

Grammar Discovery

How Sure Are You?

"The delay must be in the old data again."

"The next integration might take six months, but I need to check."

"That number can’t be right. We checked it twice."

"The new CFO must have read the post-mortem."

In each sentence Carlos does not have all the facts, so he makes a deduction. The blue word shows how sure he is. Look at the red words: when is it base form, and when is it have + past participle?

How sure?FormExample
Almost sure: yesmust + base formIt must be the old data.
Maybemight / could + base formIt might take six months.
Almost sure: nocan’t + base formThat can’t be right.
About the pastmust / might / can’t + have + past participleShe must have read it. They can’t have seen it.
Trap 1negative deduction = can’tNot It mustn’t be true. Say It can’t be true.
Trap 2no to after the modalNot It must to be. Say It must be.

In one line: must = almost sure yes; might / could = maybe; can’t = almost sure no; + have + past participle for the past.

Grammar Practice

Make a Deduction

Say it first, then click to check

"Everyone in finance is smiling. The payroll report ___must be good." (almost sure: yes)
"I am not sure. The CFO ___might ask about the cost." (maybe)
"Twelve integrations in a year? That ___can't be right." (almost sure: no)
"She knows every number. She must ___have read the post-mortem."
"Tom is not here. He might have ___missed the email." (miss)
"The new process ___could save us two weeks, but we need data." (maybe)
Chapter 5: Real Speed

The Leadership Offsite

One agenda, one coach, one heads-up

Real Document

The Offsite Agenda

NORTHWAY LEADERSHIP OFFSITE -- DAY 1
Session 3 · Open Q&A with the Integration Team · Carlos Vinicius Vale Bassan, Accenture Strategy
Time9:00 to 9:20 -- twenty minutes
FormatNo slides. Questions from the floor.
In the room12 leaders, including the new CFO
TopicsPayroll bridge results; next integration; team capacity
RuleAnswers under one minute. Estimates are welcome.
Twenty minutes, twelve leaders: that is less than two minutes per person. Short answers win.
1. Can Carlos use slides in this session?
No. There are no slides: the questions come from the floor.
2. Why must the answers be short?
Because there are twelve leaders and only twenty minutes, and the rule is under one minute per answer.
3. Make a deduction: what might the new CFO ask about?
She might ask about the cost of the payroll bridge or the next integration.
