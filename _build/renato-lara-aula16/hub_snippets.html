Stage 1.1: School Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Timetable — the paper or the app that tells you which class you have and when
"My timetable says science is in room 12."
Subject — one thing you study at school, like maths or geography
"How many subjects are there in Year 10?"
Canteen — the place at school where you buy hot food at lunch
"There is always a line at the canteen."
Locker — the small tall door at school where you leave your books and your bag
"My locker is upstairs in C block."
Uniform — the clothes every student at the school has to wear
"Is there a uniform for sport?"
Assembly — the morning meeting where the whole school sits together in one hall
"There is an assembly every Monday."
Recess — the short break in the middle of the morning, before lunch
"Recess is only twenty minutes."
Slow down — to speak or move more slowly than before
"Sorry, could you slow down a bit?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the tour of the school. Then answer the questions.

Your first morning in Perth. There is a timetable in the folder with your name on it, and there are six periods on it, one after the other, with a bell at the end of each one. There are five subjects before lunch on a Monday, and two of them are science. There is an assembly at half past eight in the hall, the whole school in one room. Recess comes at eleven, and there is always a line at the canteen then, so nobody goes at recess — they go at lunch. Your locker is upstairs, because there are no lockers on the ground floor. There is a uniform, and there is a hat rule with it: no hat, no play. And when somebody talks too fast, there is one sentence that fixes it: sorry, could you slow down a bit?
Why is it "There are six periods" and not "There is six periods"?
A Because the verb agrees with what comes after it, and "six periods" is more than one.
B Because "there" is plural.
What job is the word "there" doing at the start of these sentences?
A It points at a place far away, like "over there".
B It holds the front of the sentence so that "is" or "are" can say that something exists.
The text says "there are no lockers on the ground floor". What is another way to say the same thing?
A There aren't any lockers on the ground floor.
B There isn't no lockers on the ground floor.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

There is, there are — saying what a place has

"There" holds the front of the sentence and is or are comes straight after it. The verb agrees with the thing that comes next: one thing → there is, two or more → there are.

The form

There is a canteen.
There are two labs.
There isn't a pool.
There aren't any lockers left.
Is there a surf club?
Are there two breaks?

three traps

Never My school has a canteen
for a place, say There is
Never There is two labs
Never In my school a canteen

To ask, the two words swap: There is a pool → Is there a pool? — There are lockers → Are there lockers?
Counting: "How many subjects are there in Year 10?" — the two words stay together and go to the end.
In speech: "there is" comes out as there's and "there are" as there're. It sounds like one word, but the verb is still in there.

Stage 4: The first morning

Quiz
You want to tell your host sister that your school in Brazil has a big canteen.
A In my school has a big canteen.
B There is a big canteen at my school.
You want to know if the school has a surf club.
A Is there a surf club at this school?
B Have a surf club at this school?
Three subjects come before recess. How do you say it?
A There is three subjects before recess.
B There are three subjects before recess.
A teacher is speaking so fast that you catch nothing. What do you say?
A Sorry, could you slow down a bit?
B Sorry, you speak very fast for me.

Stage 5: Your school, room by room
