Chapter 3: Verbs That Choose

The First Verb Decides

-ing or to: you do not choose, the verb in front does

Grammar Discovery

What Comes After the Red Verb?

"I enjoy training new people on the team."

"We decided to move the close to Thursday."

"She keeps postponing the call with legal."

"They agreed to send the figures on Friday."

The red verbs talk about the activity itself. The blue ones point forward, to something that comes after the decision.

Verb in frontWhat comes afterExample
enjoy, avoid, keep, finish, mind, consider, suggestverb + -ingI enjoy training people.
decide, plan, agree, hope, expect, refuse, manageverb + to + base verbWe decided to move the close.
look forward to, be worth, can't help, can't stand-ing (the to in look forward to is a preposition)I look forward to meeting you.
stop-ing = you no longer do it · to = you stop in order to do itI stopped checking email at night.
remember-ing = a memory · to = a task still to doRemember to send the pack.
try-ing = an experiment · to = an effortI tried to call him twice.
the trapNot I suggest to move the call. Say I suggest moving the call, or I suggest we move it.

In one line: a verb about the activity itself takes -ing; a verb that points forward takes to. Stop, remember and try take both — with two different meanings.

Dialogue

So What Do You Do for Fun?

G
Okay, no more indicators tonight. What do you actually do when you are not working?
J
Honestly? I make a point of reading something every night. At the moment I am really into books about how the brain makes decisions.
G
That sounds like work with a different cover. Do you enjoy cooking? I keep promising myself I will learn.
J
I gave up trying to cook during the week. But I decided to take Spanish lessons, and that one I have managed to keep.
G
Spanish on top of English? I can't help admiring that. I had a go at Italian once and quit after three weeks.
J
Three weeks is not bad. My pet peeve is people who say they are too old to start. What made you stop going?
G
The class was at seven in the morning. I can't stand getting up early, and I am not ashamed to say it.
J
Then you should try listening to a podcast at night instead. It is worth trying for a month before you give up again.
Comprehension

About Greg

1. What does Greg keep promising himself?
That he will learn to cook.
2. What happened when Greg tried Italian?
He had a go at it once and quit after three weeks.
3. Why did Greg stop going to the class?
It was at seven in the morning, and he can't stand getting up early.
Listening 1 · A Habit Researcher

Why Next Monday Never Comes

On the difference between deciding to do something and actually doing it. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say most people do on a Sunday night?
They decide to start something new on Monday.
2. According to him, what is the problem with deciding to start?
Deciding feels like progress, so people stop there and never start.
3. What does he suggest doing instead?
Making the first step so small that you cannot avoid doing it, like reading one page.
Listening 2 · A Language Columnist

The Verb That Points Forward

A short piece on why English verbs choose -ing or to. Sound first — no text.

1. According to the columnist, what does the to form usually point at?
Something that comes after the verb, in the future: a plan, a decision, a hope.
2. What does the -ing form usually point at?
The activity itself, something that is already happening or already known.
3. What example does she give for stop?
Stopping to buy coffee means you go and buy it; stopping buying coffee means you never buy it again.
Real Document

The Feedback Form

TRAINING SESSION · FEEDBACK
filled in by J. Piffer after the indirect tax workshop
What I enjoyed → explaining the new rules to people outside the tax team
What I would avoid next time → starting at 8 a.m. — half the room arrived late
What I suggest changing → sending the slides two days before, not after
What I have decided to do → run a shorter session every month instead of one long one
What I keep putting off → writing a one-page guide people can keep on their desk
Every line on this form uses a verb that chooses its company: enjoyed explaining, avoid starting, suggest sending, decided to run, keep putting off.
1. What did she enjoy about the workshop?
Explaining the new rules to people outside the tax team.
2. What does she suggest changing, and why does it need -ing?
Sending the slides two days before. Suggest is always followed by -ing, never by to.
3. What has she decided to do from now on?
To run a shorter session every month instead of one long one.

What I Can Do Now

I use to after verbs that point forward: decide, plan, agree, hope, manage, refuse.
