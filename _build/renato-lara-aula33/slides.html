Grammar Discovery

Necessary, or forbidden?

Dana: "You must stay near the peak."
Dana: "You mustn't waste waves in the first minute."
Dana: "You mustn't paddle behind a surfer with priority."
Dana: "You don't have to catch ten waves. Two good waves are enough."

What do the orange words have in common? Which one says "don't do it", and which one says "it isn't necessary"?

must = it's necessary · mustn't = don't do it · don't have to = it isn't necessary

After must and mustn't, use the base verb with no "to": you must stay, you mustn't waste.

The Code

Three jobs

must: it's necessary

You must check in at seven.
He must wear a jersey.
I must stay calm.

mustn't: don't do it

You mustn't be late.
Surfers mustn't drop in.
I mustn't panic.

don't have to: not necessary

You don't have to bring a jersey.
She doesn't have to come.
We don't have to hurry.
Watch out: no "to" after must: "I must go", not "I must to go". No "-s" with he or she: "He must wear". In questions, say "Do I have to check in?"
Sound Check

Three sounds

You mustn't waste waves.

"mustn't" has no first "t" sound: MUS-nt

You must stay near the peak.

"must" is short and quick in the middle of a sentence

You don't have to catch ten waves.

"have to" sounds like "haf-ta"
