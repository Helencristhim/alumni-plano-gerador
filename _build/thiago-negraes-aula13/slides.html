Chapter 3: He Does, She Does

One Letter That Matters

He does, she does, the team does

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"My team designs the treatment plant."

"The client decides in the end."

"The water utility supplies water to the city."

"What does your manager do? He oversees the whole project."

SubjectWhat happensExample
he, she, it, the team, the clientthe verb takes -sThe client decides.
verb ending in -o, -s, -sh, -ch, -xthe verb takes -esShe goes to the site. He watches the data.
consonant + yy becomes -iesThe utility supplies water.
negative and questiondoes / doesn't take the -s, the verb goes back to the baseWhat does he do? He doesn't design.

One question decides it: is the subject one person, one company or one team that is not me or you? Then the verb takes the -s. In the question and the negative, the -s moves to does, and only once.

Chapter 4: The New Director

So, What Exactly Do You Do?

The first call with the new director

Dialogue

The New Director

K
Hi Thiago, I am Karen. I just joined the team in Houston. So, what exactly do you do?
T
I am a civil engineer at our office in Brazil. I work on sanitation projects for the consultancy.
K
Sanitation, so water and sewage? I come from transportation projects, so this is new for me.
T
Exactly. Our client is usually a water utility, the company that supplies water to a city.
K
And what does your team deliver?
T
My team studies the network and designs the treatment plant. My manager oversees the whole project.
K
Who decides in the end?
T
The client decides, but a lot of stakeholders give their opinion: the city, the regulator and the community.
K
That sounds complex. How long does a project usually take?
T
A study usually takes six months, and each deliverable goes to the client every month.
K
Perfect. Can you explain that to the whole team on Thursday? Nobody here really understands it.
Did You Catch It?

About Karen
