Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

When you talk about yourself, the present simple is easy: "I work on sanitation projects." But most of a job description is about other people and other companies, and there the verb changes. After he, she, it, or any single subject like the team, the client or the utility, the verb takes an -s: "my team designs the plant", "the client decides", "my manager oversees the project". Some verbs take -es ("she goes", "he watches"), and a consonant plus y becomes -ies ("the utility supplies water"). In questions and negatives, the -s moves to does and the main verb goes back to its base form: "What does your team deliver?", "The client doesn't design anything." Portuguese has no letter for this, which is why the -s disappears in meetings.

B Because the team is one subject, like it, so the verb takes -s.
C Because the sentence is in the past.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "What does your team delivers?"
B "What your team delivers?"

3. What is the correct form of supply after "the utility"?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The present simple with he, she and it

GRAMMAR

One question decides everything: is the subject one person or one thing that is not I or you? Then the verb takes the -s.

FormRuleExample
Affirmativeverb + -s / -es / -iesThe client decides. The utility supplies water.
Negativedoesn't + base verbShe doesn't design the plant.
Questiondoes + subject + base verbDoes your manager oversee the project?
Wh- questionwh- + does + subject + base verbWhat does your team deliver?

The -s appears only once in the sentence. If there is does or doesn't, the main verb has no -s: "Does she work?", never does she works.
