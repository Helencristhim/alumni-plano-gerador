The First-Month Review -- What Used to Slow Us Down

One month after the kickoff, Rafael writes a review: how the team used to work, and how it works now. Key words: retrospective, bottleneck, workaround, manual, automate, streamline, turnaround time, lesson learned. Structure: used to + verb for the past that changed, and the present simple for now.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Retrospective -- a meeting where a team looks back at what went well and what did not
"We have a short retrospective after the first month."
Bottleneck -- one point in a process that slows down everything after it
"The approval step was the bottleneck."
Workaround -- a quick way to avoid a problem without fixing it
"We found a workaround and fixed the real problem later."
Manual -- done by people, not by a machine
"The access report is still manual."
Automate -- to make a machine or a system do a task, not a person
"We automated the server requests last month."
Streamline -- to make a process simpler and faster
"We plan to streamline the access report in month two."
Turnaround time -- the time between a request and the result
"The turnaround time for a new server is now two hours."
Lesson learned -- something useful you understand after a mistake or an experience
"Our biggest lesson learned: one point of contact for each workstream."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

One month after the kickoff, Rafael writes a short review for the steering committee. Before the migration, the team used to create new servers by hand. One engineer approved every request, and the turnaround time used to be five days.

When something broke at night, people used to find a workaround and fix the real problem later. The team didn't use to have a regular meeting with the vendor.

Today, things are different. Most of the work is automated, and a new server takes two hours. The team has a short check-in every Monday. One task is still manual: the monthly access report. Rafael plans to streamline it in month two.

1. How long does a new server take today?
A Five days, because one engineer still approves every request by hand.
B Two hours.
C One month.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "The team used to creating servers by hand before the migration."
B "The team use to create servers by hand."
C "The team used to create servers by hand."
3. Which task is still manual?
A The monthly access report.
B The weekly check-in with the vendor, every Monday morning.
C New servers.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used to and the Present Simple

GRAMMAR

Used to + verb: a habit or a situation in the past that is not true now. For today, use the present simple.

JobFormExample
Before, not nowused to + verbIt used to take five days.
Negativedidn't use to + verbWe didn't use to meet the vendor.
QuestionDid ... use to + verb?Did you use to approve every request?
Nowpresent simpleToday it takes two hours.

Never: used to creating · didn't used to · I use to work at home now

Tom Wujec -- "Build a tower, build a team" (TED, 7 min)

Teams build a tower with spaghetti and a marshmallow, and the results show why testing early beats planning everything first. A short, clear talk about what teams learn after each try.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Then say two sentences: what teams used to do in the test, and what the best teams do.

Talk
Tim Harford -- "Trial, error and the God complex" (TED, 20 min)

An economist explains why complex systems improve through small tests and honest reviews, not through one perfect plan. A good companion to any retrospective.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Write down one example he gives and say how it compares with your migration project.
