Grammar Discovery

Before, or Now?

"A new server used to take five days."

"Today it takes two hours."

"We didn't use to have a meeting with the vendor."

"Now we have a check-in every Monday."

What do the orange words have in common? Which ones are still true today?

Chapter 6: Your Turn

Two Stories, Then Your Review

From guided to free

Listening 1 · The Engineering Manager

I Was the Bottleneck

An engineering manager on how her team used to work. Sound first — no text.

1. Why was she the bottleneck?
Because she approved every request for a new test environment.
2. How long did a new environment use to take, and how long does it take now?
About a week before; twenty minutes now.
3. What was her biggest lesson learned?
If one person is in the middle of every request, that person is the problem.
Listening 2 · The IT Director

Thirty Minutes, Three Questions

An IT director on how his retrospectives changed. Sound first — no text.

1. What were retrospectives like in his old company?
A two-hour meeting at the end of every project, nobody talked about mistakes, and nobody read the document.
2. When does he have the meeting now, and why?
After the first month, because there is still time to change things.
3. What are the three questions?
What used to slow us down, what is better now, and what we want to streamline next month.
