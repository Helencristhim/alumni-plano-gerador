Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Anniversary -- the same date as an important event in an earlier year
"The network celebrates its fifteenth anniversary this year."
Decade -- a period of ten years
"She has worked in oncology for more than a decade."
Retire -- to stop working, usually because you are older
"Dr. Reis wants to retire next year."
Mentor -- an experienced person who helps a newer person at work
"Marta was my mentor when I joined the team."
Survivor -- a person who is still alive after a serious illness or a very bad event
"Jorge is a cancer survivor, and now he helps other patients."
Dedicated -- working very hard for something because it is important to you
"The nurses in this unit are very dedicated."
Award -- a prize that you get because you did something very well
"The team got an award for patient care."
Look back -- to think about things that happened in the past
"When I look back, I see a lot of progress."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

This month, the Oncology Network celebrates its fifteenth anniversary. Marta Lopes is a patient navigator, and she has worked in the network for fifteen years. Jorge Matos is a cancer survivor. He has been a volunteer since 2020, and Marta was his mentor.

Dr. Paulo Reis has worked at the hospital since 2001, for more than two decades. He will retire next year. A journalist asks him: "How long have you worked here?" He smiles: "For a long time. When I look back, I see a lot of progress."

1. "Marta has worked in the network for fifteen years." Does Marta work in the network now?
A No. She worked there for fifteen years, and then she stopped, so now she is at a different hospital in Sao Paulo.
B We don't know.
C Yes. She started fifteen years ago, and she still works there.
2. "Jorge has been a volunteer since 2020." Why since and not for?
A Because 2020 is a period of time, like six years, and we use since with every period of time in the past.
B Because 2020 is the start point.
C Because Jorge is a volunteer.
3. Which question is correct?
A How long have you worked here?
B How long do you work at the hospital, and since when do you work in the network?
C How long you have worked here?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- For, Since, How Long

GRAMMAR

It started in the past, and it is still true now: use have or has + past participle.

PartRuleExample
Affirmativehave / has + past participleShe has worked here for fifteen years.
for+ a period of timefor six years · for a decade · for a long time
since+ the start pointsince 2011 · since March · since my first day
Negativehaven't / hasn't + past participleI haven't seen Marta since Monday.
QuestionHow long + have / has + subject + past participle?How long have you worked here?

Is it still true now? Then do not use the present simple: I have worked here since 2002 (not I work here since 2002). For is how long; since is from when.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

At the anniversary dinner. Choose the best thing to say.

A journalist asks how long you have worked at Einstein. You say:
A "I work at Einstein since 2002, and I am in the Oncology Network since the first year of the project."
B "I have worked at Einstein since twenty-four years."
C "I have worked at Einstein for twenty-four years."
You want to know about a new colleague. You ask:
A "How long are you here?"
B "How long have you worked here?"
C "How long you work here, and how long you are in this team with Marta?"
Marta gets her award. You say:
A "Congratulations on your award, Marta! You have helped so many families."
B "Congratulations for your award, Marta! You helped so many families since fifteen years, and we are very proud."
C "Congratulations, Marta! You help families since 2011."
Sarah asks about Jorge. You say:
A "He is a volunteer for six years, and he is cancer-free since 2016, so he knows Marta for ten years now."
B "He has been a volunteer for six years."
C "He has been a volunteer since six years."

Reflection
