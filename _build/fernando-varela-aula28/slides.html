Chapter 4: The Code

Since When? For How Long?

for · since · How long...?

Grammar Discovery

What Do the Orange Words Do?

"Marta has worked in the network for fifteen years."

"Jorge has been a volunteer since 2020."

"How long have you worked here?"

"Marta started in 2011, and she has worked here since then."

Look at the orange words. Did these things stop, or are they still true now? Which word comes before a number of years? Which word comes before a date?

PartRuleExample
Formhave / has + past participleShe has worked here for fifteen years.
MeaningIt started in the past, and it is still true now.I have known Marta since 2011.
for+ a period of time: fifteen years, six months, a decade, a long timefor a decade
since+ the start point: 2011, March, Monday, my first daysince 2020
QuestionHow long + have / has + subject + past participle?How long have you worked here?
The trapFor something that is still true, do not use the present simple: I have worked here since 2002 — never I work here since 2002. And for fifteen years — never since fifteen years.

For is HOW LONG. Since is FROM WHEN. Both tell us that the situation is still true today.
