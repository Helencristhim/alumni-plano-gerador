Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Plenary -- a session for all the people at a congress
"The summit closes with a plenary on the future of robotic nursing."
Breakout session -- a smaller session about one topic, at the same time as other sessions
"I went to the breakout session on nursing teams."
Showcase -- to show the best of something to other people
"The panel showcased what their teams had learned."
Insight -- a clear and deep understanding of a situation
"My main insight from this summit is simple: the team matters most."
Benchmark -- a number or standard that you use to compare results
"Setup time is a good benchmark for a robotic team."
Sustainable -- able to continue for a long time without problems
"A robotic program is sustainable when the team stays together."
Momentum -- the energy that keeps something moving forward
"After the first year, it is hard to keep the momentum."
Scale up -- to make a program bigger, for example in more hospitals
"We would like to scale up to two more hospitals next year."
Hindsight -- understanding a situation only after it has happened
"In hindsight, we should have trained the whole team together."
Pay off -- to bring a good result after a lot of work or time
"All the hours on the simulator really paid off."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

It is the last day of the Robotic Surgery Summit. At the coffee break, Andreia meets a nurse manager from Chile: "I was wondering if your team uses the simulator too. I was hoping we could visit your center next year."

On the panel, she tells the story of her team: "When we started, we had never worked with a robot." Then she is careful with the results: "Although every hospital is different, it seems that a fixed team makes the difference." And she sets a condition: "We can scale up, provided that every new center trains its nurses first."

At the closing plenary, she looks back. In hindsight, the first fifty cases were the most important, and the hard work paid off. There is no new grammar here: each moment of the day uses a structure she already knows.

1. Why does Andreia say "I was wondering if your team uses the simulator too"?
A Because it is a polite indirect question for a person she has just met.
B Because she is talking about something that happened in the past, at another congress, before she met this nurse manager.
C Because "I was wondering" is the only way to start a question in a formal situation.
2. "When we started, we had never worked with a robot." What happened first?
A The team worked with the robot first, and the program started three years later.
B The program and the first robotic case happened in the same week.
C The team had no experience with robots before the program.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "We can scale up, provided that every new center will train its nurses first and every single new nurse will practice."
B "We can scale up, provided that every new center trains its nurses first."
C "We can scale up, unless every new center trains its nurses."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- One Structure for Each Moment of the Day

GRAMMAR

No new grammar today: one day at a summit uses the structures you already know.

MomentJobStructureExample
Coffee breakask politelyI was wondering if + normal word orderI was wondering if your team uses the simulator.
Coffee breakmake a soft requestI was hoping we could / I wanted to checkI was hoping we could visit your center.
Paneltell the storywas / were + verb-ing; had + past participleWe had never worked with a robot.
Panelpresent results honestlyit seems that / tend to / be likely toIt seems that a fixed team makes the difference.
Panelaccept a weak pointalthough + clause; despite + noun or -ingAlthough every hospital is different, ...
Panelset a conditionprovided that / as long as + presentWe can scale up, provided that the nurses train first.
Plenaryguide the audiencelet me start by + -ing; that said; coming back toLet me start by thanking my team.

Remember: in an indirect question, there is no do / does after if. After provided that and as long as, use the present, not will. After although, use a full clause; after despite, use a noun or verb-ing.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best sentence for each moment of the day.

At the coffee break, you want to know if a nurse from Chile uses the simulator. You say:
A "I was wondering if your team uses the simulator too."
B "I was wondering if do you use the simulator in your hospital, and also in the other hospitals of your group."
C "You use simulator?"
On the panel, someone says: "Your data comes from one hospital only." You answer:
A "No, that is not important."
B "Despite our data comes from one hospital, I think it is the same everywhere."
C "That's a fair point. Although our data comes from one hospital, it seems that the learning curve is similar in other centers."
Ken asks for your final reflection. You start with:
A "OK, so, I don't know, I am very nervous today, sorry for my English, so I will try to say some things about the program."
B "Thank you, Ken. Let me start by looking back at where we started."
