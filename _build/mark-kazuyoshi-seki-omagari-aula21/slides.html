Why Your Story Sounds Flat

A Story Has Layers

The chain

what happened, then what happened next

The background

what was already going on when it arrived

The layer underneath

what had happened, and how long it had been going on

In Portuguese you switch between these three without thinking. In English you have been using one of them for everything, and that is why a good story comes out sounding like a list.

Chapter 3: The Layers

Four Tenses, One Night

Same story, four different camera positions

Grammar Discovery

Same Night, Four Layers

The lights went out and she turned around.

They were walking through the station when it happened.

Her brother had disappeared two years earlier.

She had been looking for him since the first episode.

Every sentence is in the past. Put the four events in the order they actually happened, and then say what each tense is doing that the others are not.

TenseFormIts job in the story
Past simplethe lights went outThe chain of events, in order. This is the spine of the story.
Past continuousthey were walkingThe background that was already running when the event arrived. Sets the scene, and gets interrupted.
Past perfecthe had disappearedOne step further back: something finished before the story started. This is where backstory lives.
Past perfect continuousshe had been lookingHow long that earlier thing had been going on, up to the moment of the story. It explains the reaction.
The pair to hearShe had waited for him tells you it happened. She had been waiting for him tells you for how long — and that is why she reacted like that.
The connectorswhen + past simple cuts in; while + past continuous runs underneath; by the time and before pull you back to the past perfect.
Practice

Choose the Layer

Say it first, then click to check

"They ___were talking about nothing important when the lights went out." (talk — the background)
"By the time she turned around, he ___had left." (leave — finished before that moment)
"She was exhausted, because she ___had been running for twenty minutes." (run — how long, up to that moment)
"The streets were wet. It ___had been raining all afternoon." (rain — explain what we can see)
"While the camera ___was following them, nobody said a word." (follow — running underneath)
"He ___stopped, turned around and realized she was gone." (stop — the chain of events)
The Tool

Three Questions Before Every Sentence

Question 1

Is this an event in the chain, or the background it happened against?

Chain goes past simple. Background goes past continuous.

Question 2

Did it finish before the moment I am describing?

Then step back with the past perfect. This is the backstory layer.

Question 3

Does the point depend on how long it had been going on?

Then it is the past perfect continuous, and it is doing the explaining.

A retelling that never leaves question one sounds like a list. Answering question three once, at the right moment, is what makes people feel the ending.

Comprehension

About Nina

1. How does she describe the first minutes of the episode?
Two characters were walking through an empty station, and the camera was following them from behind — she sets the scene before any event.
2. What reason does she give for the fact that nothing happens at first?
Because the show had been building that moment for six episodes — the silence is the build-up, not an accident.
3. What does she refuse to tell him, and why?
The last thirty seconds — that is the cliffhanger, and she says he has to earn it.

Chapter 5: Practice

Building the Layers

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "They walked through the station when the lights went out."
"They were walking through the station when the lights went out." (the background is past continuous; the event cuts into it)
2. "By the time she turned around, he left."
"By the time she turned around, he had left." ("by the time" pulls you one step back — past perfect)
3. "She was exhausted because she was running for twenty minutes."
"...because she had been running for twenty minutes." (duration up to a moment in the past is past perfect continuous, not past continuous)
4. "He turned around and realized a great episode."
"He turned around and realized that she was gone." (in English "realize" means to notice or understand — you do not "realize" a thing you made)
