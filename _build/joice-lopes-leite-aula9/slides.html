Chapter 3: The Code

Two Verbs, Two Shapes

like + ing · want + to

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I like working with public schools."

"I enjoy testing new platforms."

"I want to improve my English."

"I would like to present in November."

VerbWhat comes afterExample
like / enjoyverb + -ingI like working. I enjoy testing.
wantto + verbI want to improve.
would like ('d like)to + verbI'd like to present.
preferboth workI prefer small rooms. I prefer to speak slowly.
Key ideawant and would like mean the same thing. Would like is the polite one -- it is what you say in a real meeting.

Two shapes to remember: like + ing, want / would like + to.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I want improve my English."
"I want to improve my English."
"I like to work with schools every day."
"I like working with schools every day."
"I am like to present in November."
"I would like to present in November."

After like and enjoy, the verb takes -ing. After want and would like, it takes to.

Chapter 4: The Conversation

The Conversation That Decides November

what you like, what you want, what you prefer

Dialogue

Would You Like to Present?

L
Hi, Joice. What do you like doing in your work?
J
I like working with public schools. And I enjoy testing new platforms.
L
Very good. And what do you want to improve this year?
J
I want to improve my English. That is my goal.
L
That is a good goal. Would you like to present at the November event?
J
Yes, I would like to present. But I prefer a small room.
L
A small room is possible. Five people, three minutes each.
J
Three minutes I can do. I would like to talk about public schools.
L
Perfect. And what would you like to learn from the other people?
J
I want to learn how they use technology in small schools.
Comprehension

About Luis

1. What does Luis invite Joice to do?
To present at the November event.
2. What does Luis say about the room?
That a small room is possible.
3. How long does each person speak?
Three minutes each, five people.

What I Can Do

I can say what I like doing: "I like working with schools."
I can say my goal: "I want to improve my English."
I can use the polite form: "I would like to present."
After like and enjoy I use -ing; after want and would like I use to.
I can accept an invitation and say what I prefer.
