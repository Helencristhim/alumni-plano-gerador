Chapter 3: The Code

Graduated or Have Graduated?

Past simple and present perfect in one story

Grammar Discovery

Closed or Open?

"Ana graduated from medical school in 2018."

"She has worked in three hospitals since then."

"She shadowed a dermatologist in Miami last summer."

"She has already passed two Step exams."

Look at the red verbs, then at the blue ones. Which ones have a finished time? Which ones connect the past to now?

JobFormExample
A finished time (in 2018, last year, ago, when...)past simpleShe graduated in 2018.
Experience, with no time saidhave / has + past participleI have shadowed two attendings.
From the past until now, with for / sincehave / has + past participleShe has worked here since 2021.
Recent news, with just / already / yethave / has + past participleI have just sent my application.
The questionWhen did...? · Have you ever...?When did you graduate? · Have you ever worked in the US?
A storypresent perfect opens, past simple tells the detailsI have done two rotations. The first one was in Boston.

In one line: if you say a finished time, use the past simple; if the time is still open or not said, use the present perfect.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Story at Full Speed

One mock interview, two recordings, one schedule

Dialogue

The Mock Interview

P
So, Diego, let’s practice the first question. Tell me about yourself.
D
I’m a dermatology resident from Brazil. I graduated from medical school there, and since then I have focused on skin disease.
P
Good start. Why dermatology?
D
The turning point came in my first year, when we caught a melanoma very early. After that, I decided to pursue dermatology.
P
And what have you done to prepare for the United States?
D
I have read dermatology articles in English every week for years, and I have started my Step 3 preparation.
P
Have you ever worked in an American hospital?
D
Not yet. I haven’t done a rotation here, but I would like to shadow a dermatologist next year.
P
That’s honest, and it sounds good. Any setbacks?
D
My speaking was weak, so this year I started English classes twice a week. In hindsight, it was the best decision I made.
Comprehension

About Dr. Parker

1. What is the first question Dr. Parker asks?
Tell me about yourself.
2. What does she want to know after "Why dermatology?"
What Diego has done to prepare for the United States.
3. How does she react when Diego says he has not worked in an American hospital?
She says it is honest and it sounds good, and then she asks about setbacks.
Listening 1 · A Program Director

Give Your Story a Shape

A program director on what she wants to hear when she says "Tell me about yourself." Sound first — no text.

1. What mistake do many international applicants make, according to her?
They start at birth: they tell her where they went to high school.
2. What are the three parts of the shape she wants?
The present, one turning point with real details, and where you want to go next.
3. What stands out more than ten prizes?

Listening 2 · An International Graduate

Forty Programs, Zero Interviews

A doctor from Colombia on the setback that changed his application. Sound first — no text.

1. How long has he lived in the United States?
For six years.
2. What happened with his first application?
He applied to forty programs and did not get a single interview.
3. What has he done since he matched?
He has finished his residency, passed Step 3 and started a fellowship.
