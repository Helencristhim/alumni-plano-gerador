Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Fluency -- the ability to speak easily, without many long pauses
"My fluency is better when I talk about my own patients."
Accuracy -- using the language correctly, without mistakes
"Accuracy matters more in a written case report than in small talk."
A strength -- something that you do well
"Clear case presentations are one of my strengths."
A blind spot -- a problem in yourself that you do not notice
"My blind spot is the s in the third person."
Feedback -- comments about how well somebody did something
"My attending gave me useful feedback after rounds."
Room for improvement -- EXPRESSION — a part that can still get better
"There is still room for improvement in my emails."
To keep track of -- to write down or follow something so that you do not forget it
"I keep track of my mistakes in a small notebook."
A benchmark -- a standard that you use to compare and measure something
"Step 3 is my benchmark for clinical English."
On track -- EXPRESSION — making the right progress to reach a goal
"I am on track to take Step 3 next year."
To build on -- to use what you already have to make more progress
"Let us build on what you did well today."
To brush up on -- to practice something again because you are starting to forget it
"I need to brush up on my biostatistics vocabulary."
Consistent -- always the same, done in the same way every time
"Consistent practice is better than one long weekend of study."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At the end of Block 4, Diego writes a short note about his progress. He compares then and now: My listening is much faster than it was in July, and emails are now the easiest part of my week. He describes his next test in a few words: Step 3 is a two-day, case-based exam.

Then he balances a strength and a weakness: Although my fluency has improved, accuracy is still my blind spot. Therefore, I keep track of my mistakes. And he compresses a lot of information into one sentence, like a journal article: Doctors preparing for Step 3 need consistent practice, and residents trained in two languages often speak too fast.

Four lessons, four tools: than to compare, a compound adjective to describe, although and therefore to connect, and a participle to compress.

1. "My listening is much ___ it was in July." Which words complete the sentence?
A more faster than, because the difference is big and much makes it stronger
B faster than
C the fastest than
2. Which is correct?
A Step 3 is a two-days, case-based exam that many international doctors take.
B Step 3 is a case-based, two-days exam.
C Step 3 is a two-day, case-based exam.
3. "Doctors preparing for Step 3 need consistent practice." What does preparing mean here?
A who are preparing
B because they prepared for the exam in the past, before they started the residency
C after they prepare

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Your Block 4 Toolbox (Review)

GRAMMAR

No new rule today. These are the four structures from Lessons 16-19, and the job each one does when you talk about progress.

JobFormExample
Compare (Lesson 16)much + -er than / the -estMy listening is much faster than before.
Describe (Lesson 17)number-noun, adjective-participle + nouna two-day, case-based exam
Balance (Lesson 18)Although + clause, clause / However, / Therefore,Although I am fluent, I make small mistakes.
Compress (Lesson 19)noun + -ing / noun + past participleDoctors preparing for Step 3... / residents trained abroad...

Never: more faster · a two-days exam · although... but · feedbacks · a feedback

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

Your attending asks: "How is your English going?" You answer:
A Good, good. It is very good now, I think, because I study every day and I watch a lot of series.
B It is much better than in July. Although I am more fluent, accuracy is still my blind spot.
C More better than before.
An attending gives you feedback: you dropped some articles in your presentation. You say:
A Thank you, that is fair. I will keep track of my articles this week.
B No, I always use the articles correctly, and I think you did not hear me very well during the presentation.
C OK.
A program director asks about your goal for the next six months. You say:
A I want to brush up in my written English, and I am on track for take Step 3 next year.
B I want to improve my English.
