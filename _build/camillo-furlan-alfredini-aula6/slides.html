Chapter 4: The Code

Was, Were

The verb to be in the past

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Have in Common?

"Bruno was in Paris last October."

"The halls were very crowded."

"The coffee wasn't cheap."

"Was it a good trip?"

Look at the orange words. Now or in the past? And when is it was, when is it were?

PersonFormExample
I / he / she / itwasI was in Paris. It was amazing.
You / we / theywereThe halls were crowded.
Negativewasn't / weren'tIt wasn't cheap. They weren't tired.
QuestionWas...? / Were...?Was it good? Were you tired?
Time wordsyesterday / last week / last yearI was in Madrid last year.
The trappast = was, not isNot Paris is amazing last year. Say Paris was amazing.

In one line: am / is → was; are → were. For a question, the verb goes first: Was it...? Were you...?

Chapter 5: Real Voices

The Postcard and Two Voices

One document, two recordings

Real Document

A Postcard for You

POSTCARD · PARIS
Paris Motor Show · October
ToCamillo Furlan Alfredini
FromBruno
Hi, Camillo! I was at the Motor Show on Saturday. The electric cars were amazing, but the halls were very crowded. The weather was rainy and cold. The cafes were nice, but the coffee wasn't cheap: six euros! See you at the shop on Monday.
P.S.Were you at a fair this month?
A postcard from a friend. Every sentence is in the past.
1. How was the weather?
It was rainy and cold.
2. Was the coffee cheap?
No, it wasn't. It was six euros.
3. Answer Bruno's P.S. question.
"Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't."
