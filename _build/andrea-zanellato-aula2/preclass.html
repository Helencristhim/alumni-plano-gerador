Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A canal -- a long, narrow river made by people, often in a city
"Amsterdam has more than one hundred kilometers of canals."
A castle -- a very old, big building with strong walls, where kings lived
"Edinburgh Castle is on a big rock in the city center."
The countryside -- the land outside the cities, with fields, hills and small villages
"I would like to drive in the Scottish countryside."
A lake -- a big area of water with land all around it
"Sweden has almost one hundred thousand lakes."
To rent a bike -- to pay money to use a bike for some hours or days
"In Amsterdam, you can rent a bike near the station."
A day trip -- a short trip: you go and come back on the same day
"Loch Ness is a good day trip from Edinburgh."
A local dish -- a typical food from one place
"I always want to try a local dish when I travel."
The northern lights -- green and pink lights in the sky, in the far north of the world
"You can see the northern lights in the north of Sweden in winter."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Do you want to see a different Europe? In Amsterdam you can rent a bike and ride along the canals. If you would like to leave the city, take a day trip to the countryside.

In Scotland, many visitors want to see Edinburgh Castle first. Then they would like to go north, to the Highlands and to Loch Ness, the most famous lake in the country.

In Sweden there are lakes everywhere. In winter, a lot of people would like to see the northern lights. And everybody wants to try a local dish: meatballs, or a cinnamon bun with coffee.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Want to and Would Like to

GRAMMAR

Use want to + verb for a wish or a plan. Use would like to + verb (I’d like to) when you want to be polite. Would like never takes -s.

JobFormExample
A strong wishwant to + verbI want to see the canals.
With he / shewants to + verbShe wants to rent a bike.
A polite wishwould like to + verb (I’d like to)I’d like to visit the castle.
QuestionWould you like to + verb? / Do you want to + verb?Would you like to come?
Negativedon’t want to / wouldn’t like toI don’t want to go in August.
The trapalways to before the verbNot I want go. Say I want to go.

Never: I want go · I would like visit · She would likes to · Do you would like to...?
