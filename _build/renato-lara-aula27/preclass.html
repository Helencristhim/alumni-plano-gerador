What Would You Do?

Eight words for heat day (brave, risk, lucky, imagine, advance, knocked out, calm, hesitate) and how to imagine and give advice: If I were you, I'd wait for a set.

Stage 1.1: Heat Day

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes. "Knocked out" is one expression: learn it together.

Brave — not afraid to do something difficult or dangerous
"Be brave out there."
Risk — a chance that something bad can happen
"Don't take a silly risk near the rocks."
Lucky — when good things happen to you by chance
"If I were lucky, I'd get two good waves."
Imagine — to make a picture of something in your mind
"Imagine you win this heat."
Advance — to go on to the next round of a contest
"The top two surfers advance."
Knocked out — out of a contest because you lost
"Kai got knocked out in heat two."
Calm — relaxed, not nervous or angry
"Stay calm and wait for a set."
Hesitate — to stop for a moment because you are not sure
"Don't hesitate. Just go."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read Renato's message to his friend Lucas in Brazil, the night before Round 1. Then answer the questions.

Lucas, it's the night before my first heat in Australia, and I can't sleep. The waves here are huge. If they were smaller, I wouldn't be so nervous! Kai gave me some advice. He said, "If I were you, I'd stay calm and wait for the big ones." He also said, "Don't hesitate on a good wave." I'm trying to imagine the heat. If I advanced, I'd call you and my parents right away. And if I got knocked out? I'd be sad, but I wouldn't give up. Dana says I have to be brave, but not take a silly risk near the rocks. Honestly, if I were lucky, I'd get two good waves. Tell me: what would you do if you were me?
"If they were smaller, I wouldn't be so nervous." Are the waves small?
A Yes, they were small yesterday, so Renato is talking about the past here.
B No. They are huge. Renato imagines small waves.
"If I were you, I'd stay calm." What is Kai doing?
A He is giving Renato advice.
B He is telling Renato about a heat that he surfed in Albany last year.
"If I advanced, I'd call you." Did Renato advance?
A Yes, he advanced in the morning and called Lucas after the heat.
B Not yet. He is imagining it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Imagined situations: If + past, would + verb

The past after if is not about yesterday. It means not real, only in your mind. The other part uses would ('d) + verb. Negative: wouldn't.

If + past simple

If I won the final,
If the waves were smaller,
If I were you,

would + verb

I'd buy a new board.
I wouldn't be nervous.
I'd wait for a set. (advice)

Positive: I'd call my parents.  ·  Negative: I wouldn't hesitate.  ·  Question: What would you do?
Remember: would goes in the result, never after if. Real and possible? Use if + present, will.
Your voice: "I'd" is one short sound (AID). In "would", the L is silent: WUD.

Stage 4: On the sand

Quiz
Tom is nervous before his heat. Give him advice.
A If I am you, I will stay calm and wait for the big set.
B If I were you, I'd stay calm.
Jess asks about your dream. Answer her.
A If I won the final, I'd buy a new board.
B If I would win the final, I would buy a new board here.
You want advice from Dana. Ask her.
A What you would do in my heat if you were me, Dana?
B What would you do if you were me?
You finished third in your heat. Only the top two go on.
A I got knocked out.
B I got lucky and I advanced to the next round of the contest.
A perfect wave comes to you. What do you do?
A I hesitate for a long time and then I think about the risk.
B I don't hesitate. I go.

Stage 5: Imagine your life

Reflection
Imagine you were a pro surfer on the World Tour. Record 30 seconds: where would you live, what would you do every day, and what wouldn't you do? Check one thing before you send it: if + past, would + verb — and never "would" after "if".
