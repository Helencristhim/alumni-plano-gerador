Near Hamburg. She has lived in Lisbon for six years.

There is a fallback, usually somebody’s garage. People almost never call things off at the last minute.

She believed that if I have time, I will come was a promise, and she waited for months. Now she pencils things in, commits only when she really knows, and brings a book.

Chapter 4: The Code

If, Unless, As Long As, In Case

Four small words, four different promises

Grammar Discovery

Which One Is Not a Condition?

"If it rains, we will call off the hike."

"I will come, unless there is a downpour."

"I will come as long as we leave before eight."

"I will take a jacket in case it gets cold."

After if, unless, as long as and in case, the verb is in the present — even though every sentence is about Saturday. What does each colored phrase do to the promise?

FormIts jobExample
if + present, will + verbA possible future situation and its result. Never will after if.If it rains, we will call it off.
unless + presentThe only exception. It already means if not — no not after it.I will come unless it rains.
as long as + presentA firm condition: only if this is true.I will come as long as we leave before eight.
in case + presentNot a condition. A precaution: you do it anyway.I will take a jacket in case it gets cold.
when vs ifWhen = you are sure it will happen. If = maybe.When I get home, I will call you.
the other halfcan, might or an imperative also work.If it is sunny, take a hat.

In one line: present after the little word, will in the result — and the little word decides how much you are promising.

K

A

K

A

Comprehension

About Katrin

1. What is Katrin’s plan for Saturday, and at what time?
She is going to the waterfall, at seven in the morning.
2. What does Katrin need from Ana, and by when?
A clear answer by Friday. If Ana is not sure, she will ask somebody else, and she says that is fine.
3. How does Katrin understand the sentence if I have time, I will come?
As a real promise. If Ana says it, Katrin will wait for her at seven with a coffee.
