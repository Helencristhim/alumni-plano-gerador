If I Have Time This Weekend -- Plans That Depend

The same if-sentence is a promise in one country and a polite maybe in another. Key words: to call off, a downpour, tentative, to commit to something, a fallback, last-minute, to back out of something, to hinge on something, iffy, to pencil something in, to play it by ear, to take a rain check. Structure: the first conditional, with unless, as long as and in case.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To call off -- to cancel something that was planned
"If there is a downpour, we will call off the hike."
A downpour -- a very heavy fall of rain in a short time
"We were caught in a downpour on the way home from the market."
Tentative -- not definite yet, and it can still change
"It is a tentative plan, so do not buy the tickets yet."
To commit to something -- to promise clearly that you will do it
"I will not commit to Saturday until I see the forecast."
A fallback -- a second plan that you use if the first one fails
"If the waterfall is closed, the lake is our fallback."
Last-minute -- happening at the latest possible moment
"I hate last-minute changes, but here they are normal."
To back out of something -- to decide not to do something you agreed to do
"If you back out of a plan, just say so early."
To hinge on something -- to depend completely on one thing
"The whole weekend hinges on the weather."
Iffy -- uncertain, and possibly bad
"The sky looks iffy, so take a jacket."
To pencil something in -- to arrange something that may still change later
"Let's pencil in Saturday and confirm on Friday."
To play it by ear -- to decide what to do as things happen, without a fixed plan
"We will play it by ear and see how we feel in the morning."
To take a rain check -- to say no for now, but suggest another time
"I will take a rain check on dinner, but I am free next weekend."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Thursday, Katrin, a German neighbor, invited Ana to a hike on Saturday at seven. Ana answered the Brazilian way: if I have time, I will come. For Ana the plan was tentative. For Katrin it was almost a promise.

So Katrin asked for the real condition. Ana thought about it. If it rains, we will call it off anyway, she said, because the trail turns to mud after a downpour. And she added: I will come as long as we leave before eight, because she has lunch plans at one.

In the end she gave Katrin a clear answer: yes, unless it rains. She promised to confirm by Friday, and she said she will take a jacket in case it gets cold at the top. The weekend still hinges on the weather, but now nobody has to guess.

1. Why "if it rains, we will call it off" and not "if it will rain, we will call it off"?
A Because rain is a noun, not a verb.
B Because after if we use the present, even when we talk about the future.
C Because the hike is on Saturday.
2. "Yes, unless it rains." What does Ana mean?
A Yes, if it does not rain.
B Yes, but only if it rains.
C No, because it will probably rain.
3. "I will take a jacket in case it gets cold." Will Ana take the jacket if the morning is warm?
A No, only if it gets cold.
B It depends on Katrin.
C Yes. In case is a precaution: she takes it anyway.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The First Conditional

GRAMMAR

One structure, and four small words that change how much you promise.

FormIts jobExample
if + present, will + verbA possible future situation and its resultIf it rains, we will call it off.
Negativenot can go in either halfIf it does not rain, I will not stay home.
Questionthe question goes in the result halfWill you come if it rains?
unless + presentthe only exception -- it already means if notI will come unless it rains.
as long as + presenta firm condition: only if this is trueI will come as long as we leave before eight.
in case + presentnot a condition: a precaution, you do it anywayI will take a jacket in case it gets cold.

Never: If it will rain, we will call it off · I will come unless it does not rain · Take a jacket in case it will get cold · If I will have time, I come.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

A German friend asks if you are coming to her barbecue. You want to give a clear answer:
A "If I have time, maybe I come."
B "Yes, unless it rains. I will confirm by Friday."
C "If it will not rain, I will come."

A "I will take an umbrella in case it rains."
B "I will take an umbrella unless it rains."

A "The trip hinges on the wind."
