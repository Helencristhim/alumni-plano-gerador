Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last year, there was a long drought in the south of Minas Gerais. Because of the drought, the yield on many farms was lower. Prices went up all over the world. As a result, some smallholders earned more, but many lost part of their crop. When we connect a cause to its effect, we use cause and effect linkers.

Because of and due to come before a noun (because of the drought, due to the new regulation). Because comes before a full sentence (because it did not rain). A common mistake is "because the drought". In English, the correct form is "because of the drought".

As a result and therefore start a new sentence with the effect, and they take a comma: "The farm got a certification. Therefore, it earns a premium." To say what a cause produces, use lead to: "Deforestation leads to higher temperatures."

1. Which sentence is correct?
A Because the drought in the south of Minas, the yield on many of our farms was lower this year.
B Because of the drought, the yield was lower.
C Because of it did not rain, the yield was lower.
2. Why do we say "due to the new regulation" and not "due to the regulation is new"?
A Because due to is only used in formal emails to buyers in Europe and never in conversation.
B Because due to comes before a noun, not a full sentence.
C Because the regulation is from the government.
3. Which word completes the sentence: "The farm got a certification. ___, it earns a premium."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Cause and Effect Linkers

GRAMMAR

Connect a cause to its effect: look at what comes after the linker -- a noun or a full sentence.

LinkerStructureExample
because of / due to+ nounBecause of the drought, the yield fell.
because+ subject + verbThe yield fell because it did not rain.
so+ result, same sentenceIt did not rain, so the yield fell.
As a result, / Therefore,new sentence + commaPrices went up. As a result, farmers earned more.
lead tocause + leads to + effectDeforestation leads to higher temperatures.

Opinion language: In my view, ... · I strongly believe that ... · I’m not convinced that ... And remember: say I agree, never I am agree.
