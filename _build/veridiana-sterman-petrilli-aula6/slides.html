Chapter 4: The Code

If You Can, We Will

One shape, two halves, and the word that already means if not

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Offers Share?

"If you can move us tonight, we will take it."

"Unless you cover the taxi, we will have to pay for it ourselves."

"We will book somewhere ourselves if we do not hear anything by six."

"If that is everything you can authorise, could you ask a supervisor?"

The red half is the condition and the blue half is what happens. All four talk about the future — so look at which half is allowed to say will, and which half is not.

JobFormExample
A real future possibilityIf + present, will + bare verbIf you can move us, we will take it.
The order can flipwill + bare verb, if + presentWe will book somewhere if we do not hear from you.
If not, in one wordunless + present (never a second not)Unless you cover the taxi, we will pay.
A softer consequenceIf + present, could / may / mightIf that is everything, could you ask a supervisor?
Setting a deadlineIf + present + by + timeIf we do not hear anything by six...
Offering two options at onceIf you can..., we will...; if you cannot, we will...Two reasonable choices, and the desk picks one.
The trapno will after if; no second not after unlessNot if you will move us. Say if you can move us.

In one line: the if half stays in the present even when the whole sentence is about tonight, and unless already carries the not.

Listening 1 · A Consumer Adviser

When to Ask for a Supervisor

Somebody who answers complaints for a living. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say the person at the desk often cannot help?
Because the list of things they are allowed to authorise is short. It is not that they do not want to help; they physically cannot.
2. What does she say the first question should be, and what should it not be?
Not "can I speak to a manager", but "what are you able to offer me yourself?"
3. Why does she say to ask for things in writing, if you are not planning to go to court?
Because a company that has written something down remembers it.
Listening 2 · A Duty Supervisor

What It Looked Like from My Side

Nine years behind an airport desk. Sound first — no text.

1. What did he already know when a passenger reached him, and what did he not know?
He already knew the problem. He did not know what would satisfy them, and that is what they almost never said.
2. What kind of sentence did the passengers who got the most finish with?
Two options: if you can get us on the morning flight, we will take it; if you cannot, we will need a hotel and dinner tonight.
3. What happened to the passenger who only said this is unacceptable, and why?
They got nothing, every time, because there was nothing for him to say yes to.
Chapter 6: Say It Back

Now the Desk Is Yours

Guided > Semi-free > Free

Role-Play 1 of 3 — Guided

Two Options, One Deadline

Scenario: Midnight, confirmed reservation, no room. Ask what the agent can offer, then give two options in one sentence, then set a time by which you need an answer, and finish by asking for it in writing.

Keyword chips:

What can you offer us? an alternative to cover in writing
Role-Play 2 of 3 — Semi-free

Move It Up Without Making an Enemy

Scenario: The agent offers the other hotel, but not the taxi and not dinner. Say what that leaves you paying, offer one reasonable alternative, and then escalate in a single sentence that does not blame the person in front of you.

Keywords:

unless reasonable to authorise a supervisor
Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

Midnight, and It Is Your Turn

Scenario: You and your mother, midnight, a confirmed booking and no room. She does not speak English and she is tired. Handle the whole thing: find out what they can do, offer two options, set a deadline, escalate if you have to, and get it in writing.

No keywords, no notes, sixty seconds. You are the only one who can speak.
