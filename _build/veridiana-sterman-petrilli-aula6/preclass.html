Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

No Room at the Hotel -- Negotiating a Way Out

Midnight, a confirmed reservation, and no room. What the person behind the desk is actually allowed to give you, and the sentence that makes them give it. Key words: availability, to guarantee, an alternative, to cover, reasonable, a supervisor, to escalate, in writing, a deadline, to settle. Structure: first conditional and unless.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Availability -- the fact that something is free and can be used
"A late check-out depends on availability that morning."
To guarantee -- to promise something firmly, so that it cannot fail
"Can you guarantee a room for tomorrow night, or not?"
An alternative -- another option that does the same job
"If there is no room here, what alternative can you offer?"
To cover -- to pay for a cost, so the other person does not have to
"Will you cover the taxi as well, or only the room?"
Reasonable -- fair, and not too much to ask for
"Two options, both reasonable, and the agent chooses one."
A supervisor -- the person above the one you are talking to
"Could you ask a supervisor? I know this is not your decision."
To escalate -- to take a problem to somebody with more power
"I only escalate when the person in front of me cannot decide."
In writing -- on paper or in an e-mail, and not only spoken
"Could you send me that in writing, so nobody has to remember it?"
A deadline -- the last moment at which something can happen
"If we do not hear anything by six, we will book somewhere ourselves."
To settle -- to agree on an end to a disagreement
"Most of these settle at the desk, in under five minutes."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Complaining tells the desk what the problem is. Negotiating hands them a yes they are allowed to give. The difference lives in one structure, and English uses it every time it talks about a real possibility in the near future: if plus the present, and then will plus the bare verb. "If you can move us to another hotel tonight, we will take it."

Look at the halves. The if half is about tonight, but the verb stays in the present. This is the trap for Portuguese speakers, because in Portuguese that half is a future form. In English, never if you will move us. The will lives in the other half, and only there. The two halves can also change places: "We will book somewhere ourselves if we do not hear anything by six."

Unless is the same structure in one word: it already means if not. "Unless you cover the taxi, we will have to pay for it ourselves." Because the not is already inside, you never add a second one. Never unless you do not cover.

Two more uses, and they are the ones that end the conversation well. The consequence half does not have to be will: it can be softer. "If that is everything you can authorise, could you ask a supervisor?" That sentence escalates without blaming the person in front of you. And a deadline is simply an if half with a time in it: "if we do not hear anything by six". Two reasonable options, one deadline, and the answer in writing. That is the whole conversation.

1. Why is "If you will move us tonight" wrong?
A Because the if half stays in the present, even when the sentence is about tonight.

C Because the two halves are in the wrong order.
2. What does unless already contain?
A A future form, so the verb after it takes will.
B The word not, which is why you never add a second one.
C A deadline, so it always needs a time after it.
3. According to the text, what does "If that is everything you can authorise, could you ask a supervisor?" achieve?
A It tells the agent that they have not done their job.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- First Conditional and Unless

GRAMMAR

The if half stays in the present. The will lives in the other half, and only there.

JobFormExample
A real future possibilityIf + present, will + bare verbIf you can move us, we will take it.
The order can flipwill + bare verb, if + presentWe will book somewhere if we do not hear from you.
If not, in one wordunless + present (never a second not)Unless you cover the taxi, we will pay.
A softer consequenceIf + present, could / may / mightIf that is everything, could you ask a supervisor?
Setting a deadlineIf + present + by + timeIf we do not hear anything by six...
Offering two options at onceIf you can..., we will...; if you cannot, we will...Two reasonable choices, and the desk picks one.
In speechwe will becomes we’ll · do not becomes don’tIf you can move us, we’ll take it.

Never: if you will move us · unless you do not cover the taxi · if we do not hear by six, we book somewhere · this is unacceptable (with no request after it)

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The hotel is full and you have a confirmed reservation. Your first sentence is:
A "This is unacceptable. We booked in July."
B "We have a confirmed reservation. What can you offer us tonight?"
C "Can I speak to the manager, please?"
You want to give the desk two options in one sentence. You say:
A "If you can move us tonight, we will take it; if you cannot, we will need dinner and a taxi."
B "If you will move us tonight, we take it; if you will not, we need dinner."
C "You move us tonight or we need dinner and a taxi."
They offer the other hotel but not the taxi. You say:
A "Unless you do not cover the taxi, we will pay ourselves."
B "Unless you cover the taxi, we will have to pay for it ourselves."
C "Unless you will cover the taxi, we pay ourselves."
The agent has clearly reached the end of what they can do. You say:
A "You are not helping me at all. Get me a manager."
B "If that is everything you can authorise, could you ask a supervisor?"
C "Fine. We will sort it out ourselves."
