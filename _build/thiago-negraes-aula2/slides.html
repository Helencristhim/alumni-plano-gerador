Chapter 4: Asking Politely

There Is, and Could I

Describe what exists, then ask for what you want

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"There is a safe in the room."

"There are two lifts in the main hall."

"Is there a gym in the hotel?"

"Could I have a late check-out, please?"

StructureUseExample
There is + singularOne thing existsThere is a safe in the room.
There are + pluralTwo or more things existThere are two lifts in the hall.
Is there / Are thereAsk whether it existsIs there a gym in the hotel?
Could I / Could you + base verbAsk politely for somethingCould you send someone up, please?

After could the verb has no to and no -s: "Could you send", never "Could you to send" or "Could you sends".

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

✗ WRONG

"Have a gym in the hotel?"

✓ RIGHT

"Is there a gym in the hotel?"

✗ WRONG

"Could you to send someone up?"

✓ RIGHT

"Could you send someone up?"

Portuguese uses one verb for both ideas. English splits them: there is for what exists in a place, have only for what somebody owns.

Dialogue

At the Front Desk

R
Good evening. Do you have a reservation with us?
T
Yes, under Negraes. Three nights, please.
R
I have it here. There is a deposit of one hundred dollars, and we give it back when you leave.
T
That is fine. Is breakfast complimentary?
R
It is, until ten thirty. All the amenities on the second floor are too.
T
Is there a gym up there as well?
R
There is, and it is open all night. There are two lifts on your left.
T
One more thing. My flight leaves late on Friday. Could I have a late check-out?
R
Until two, yes. Just tell housekeeping the night before.
T
Perfect. Could you send some water up to the room?
R
Of course. Room 812, and enjoy your stay.
