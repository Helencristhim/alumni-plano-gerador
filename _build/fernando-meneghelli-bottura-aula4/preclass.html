Lesson 04 -- Pre-class

The Executive Voice -- Headline First, Detail Second

Framing an answer so it lands: the headline, the signpost, and the one thing they will remember. Key words: a headline, to signpost, a takeaway, to land, concise, understated, compelling, credibility, to frame, delivery, to underscore, to command the room. Structure: cleft sentences, the spotlight that moves the important part to the front.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. Do not translate — the example sentence is the meaning. Every word here is about HOW something is said, not what.

A headline -- the short version you give before any of the detail
"The headline is that we protected the margin and lost a fifth of the revenue."
To signpost -- to tell the listener in advance how your answer is organized
"There are three reasons, and the third is the one people forget."
A takeaway -- the one thing somebody remembers after you stop talking
"If there is one takeaway, it is that I fix the cost base before I touch the revenue."
To land -- to have on the listener the effect you intended
"The number landed. The reasoning behind it did not."
Concise -- short and complete at the same time
"His answers were concise, which is not the same as short."
Understated -- deliberately calm, saying less than you could
"An understated delivery of a very large number is hard to argue with."
Compelling -- convincing enough that people want to keep listening
"It was a compelling case, and it took him ninety seconds to make it."
Credibility -- the reason people believe what you say
"You buy credibility with a number you did not have to give."
To frame -- to present information so that it shapes how it is understood
"He framed a cost cut as an investment, and nobody argued."
Delivery -- how you say it, as opposed to what you say
"The content was there. The delivery gave it away."
To underscore -- to make something stand out as the important part
"What I would underscore is the sequence, not the size of the cut."
To command the room -- to hold everybody's attention without raising your voice
"She commanded the room by answering in one sentence and stopping."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The short answer is that we protected the margin and lost about a fifth of the revenue, and I would take the same decision again. There are three reasons. What made it unavoidable was that the account had been structurally unprofitable for six quarters. It was the timing that shaped the sequence: six weeks of visibility is enough to rebuild a cost base and not enough to replace volume. And the third reason, the one people forget, is that the team needed to see an unpopular decision taken on purpose.

Written down, that is one headline, one signpost and three reasons. Spoken, it takes forty seconds. The version I used to give took three minutes, because I built the case first and put the conclusion at the end, and nobody could tell which parts of the context mattered until it was too late to land.

What I would underscore is the sequence. It was not the size of the cut that saved the margin — it was doing it in that order. And all I needed to make that case was one number I did not have to give them, which is where credibility actually comes from. The delivery was understated on purpose: a very large number said calmly is much harder to argue with than the same number said loudly.

1. "It was the timing that shaped the sequence." What is this sentence quietly denying?
A That something else — the size of the account, for instance — shaped the sequence.
B That the sequence was shaped at all.
C Nothing. It is simply a more formal way of saying "the timing shaped the sequence".
2. Why does the text say "What made it unavoidable was..." instead of "The account was unprofitable, so it was unavoidable"?
A Because the second version is grammatically wrong.
B Because it puts the important part at the front and makes the speaker sound decided rather than descriptive.
C Because it is shorter.
3. According to the text, why did the three-minute version fail?
A The conclusion came at the end, so nobody could tell which parts of the context mattered until it was too late.
B The English was too complicated for the panel.
C It contained too few numbers to be convincing.
4. Where does the text say credibility actually comes from?
A From speaking with confidence and volume.
B From the length and completeness of the reasoning.
C From giving one number he did not have to give — and from an understated delivery.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Spotlight

GRAMMAR

English lets you take any part of a sentence and move it to the front. The part you move becomes the point — and everything else quietly becomes background.

PatternPlain versionWith the spotlight
What ... is / wasI bring twenty years of agency economics.What I bring is twenty years of agency economics.
It was ... thatThe sequence saved the margin.It was the sequence that saved the margin.
The thing that ... is / wasThe reporting line changed everything.The thing that changed everything was the reporting line.
All ... is / wasI needed six weeks of visibility.All I needed was six weeks of visibility.
AgreementAfter what the verb is singular, even when the thing that follows is plural: What I bring is twenty years, never are.
ConnectorAfter it was the connector is that (or who for people), never what. It was the sequence that saved it, never what saved it.
The hidden contrastThe spotlight always denies something. It was the sequence that saved the margin quietly says: not the size of the cut. That implied contrast is what makes the structure sound decided instead of descriptive — and it is why one per answer is enough. Used in every sentence, it stops emphasizing and starts performing.

One spotlight marks one takeaway, and an answer only has one takeaway.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

The panel has thirty seconds of attention per answer. Choose the version that lands.

"Tell us about the hardest decision you have taken." You open with:
A "The short answer is that we let our largest account go and protected the margin. There are three reasons."
B "Well, to understand this properly you need to know that in 2018 the group had nine entities and the market was changing..."
C "That is a difficult question, because there were many hard decisions in those nine years."
You want to mark the one thing you want them to remember. You say:
A "This point is very important, please note it."
B "What I would underscore is the sequence, not the size of the cut."
C "What I would underscore are the sequence and also the size."
You give a headline and the interviewer says it is not what she asked. The best response is:
A "Then the short answer is different, and there are two things behind it."
B "Sorry, sorry. Let me start again from the beginning of the story."
C "Ah, excuse me, my English sometimes makes me answer the wrong thing."
