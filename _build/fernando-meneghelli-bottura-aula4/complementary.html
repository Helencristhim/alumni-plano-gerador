Lesson 4 -- The Executive Voice

Grammar Video
"What is a cleft sentence? It-cleft and wh-cleft"

The two patterns from this lesson, side by side: "It was the sequence that saved the margin" and "What I bring is twenty years". Short, and it shows the plain version next to the emphatic one so the difference is visible rather than explained.

Tip: after each example, rebuild it with your own material. One it-cleft about a decision, one wh-cleft about what you bring. Say them out loud — the structure only works when it sounds natural, and that takes repetition, not understanding.

Talk with transcript
"The secret structure of great talks" -- Nancy Duarte, TED (18 min)

Eighteen minutes on why the order of what you say decides whether it lands. Connection to Lesson 4: she is describing at the level of a whole speech exactly what this lesson does at the level of a single answer — the listener needs to know where you are going before the detail can mean anything. Full transcript beside the video.

Tip: watch once, then take one answer you gave badly in a real interview and rebuild it on her structure. Write the headline sentence only, nothing else, and say it out loud five times.

Executive Communication
"Think Fast, Talk Smart" -- Matt Abrahams, Stanford Graduate School of Business (58 min)

A Stanford lecturer on speaking without preparation: the exact situation that cost you a role. He gives concrete structures for answering on the spot, including the one this lesson uses, and he is explicit that the goal is not to be brilliant but to be finished.

Tip: split it across two days, thirty minutes each, and write down only the structures — not the anecdotes. Then use one of them out loud on a question you have never seen, the same evening.
