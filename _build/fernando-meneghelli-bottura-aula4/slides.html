Chapter 3: The Code

Moving the Important Part to the Front

Same words, same facts, a different point

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

What I bring is twenty years inside agency economics.

It was the sequence that saved the margin, not the size of the cut.

The thing that changed everything was the reporting line moving abroad.

What I would underscore is that we did it in that order.

Each of these could be said in a plain sentence. What do the opening words add?

PatternPlain versionWith the spotlight
What ... is / wasI bring twenty years of agency economics.What I bring is twenty years of agency economics.
It was ... thatThe sequence saved the margin.It was the sequence that saved the margin.
The thing that ... is / wasThe reporting line changed everything.The thing that changed everything was the reporting line.
All / The reason ...I needed six weeks of visibility.All I needed was six weeks of visibility.
The hidden contrastThe spotlight always denies something. It was the sequence that saved the margin quietly says: it was not the size of the cut. That implied contrast is why the structure sounds decided instead of descriptive.
Why it matters in an interviewIt is the cheapest way to sound like somebody who has a view. Use it once or twice in an answer, never in every sentence — overused, it stops emphasizing and starts performing.

One spotlight per answer. Two at most. It marks the takeaway, and an answer only has one takeaway.

The Protocol

Three Steps, Four Seconds

1. Say something true

"There are two parts to that."

2. Name the destination

"Let me start with the working capital side."

3. Start concrete

"Let me give you the number first, and then the reasoning."

Silence is what convinces you the blank is real. Break the silence in under two seconds and the rest comes back on its own.

The Move

Headline, Signpost, Detail

1. Headline

"The short answer is that we protected the margin and lost a fifth of the revenue."

2. Signpost

"There are three reasons, and the third is the one people forget."

3. Detail

"The first is... the second is timing... and the third is what the team needed to see."

"The short answer is that we protected the margin and lost a fifth of the revenue. There are three reasons, and the third is the one people forget."

If the headline is not what they wanted, they will interrupt in fifteen seconds instead of three minutes. That is the point, not the risk.

Chapter 4: In the Room

The Blank, Live

An evaluator who has never met you, and one question you cannot see coming

Dialogue

Four Seconds That Nobody Noticed

N
Let us change direction. Working capital. How did you manage it in a group where the clients paid in ninety days and the media owners wanted thirty?
F
There are two parts to that.
N
Go on.
F
Let me start with the number, and then the structure behind it. The gap was about sixty days across nine entities.
N
Sixty days is a lot of cash. What did you do with it?
F
I lost the thread for a second there. Let me pick it up: we moved the media payment terms first, because that was the side we controlled.
N
And the client side?
F
Not only did we shorten the client terms, we also stopped invoicing at month end, which took four days out on its own.
N
Good. And for what it is worth, I did not notice anything at all.
Comprehension

About Nadia

1. What is the situation she describes in her first question?
Clients paying in ninety days while the media owners wanted to be paid in thirty.
2. How does she react when he says there are two parts to the question?
She simply says "go on" — she treats it as a normal opening, not as hesitation.
3. What does she say at the end, and what does it tell you?
That she did not notice anything at all — the pause that felt long to him was invisible from the other side of the table.
Listening

Why Finance People Bury the Point

A communication coach, on the same mistake every week. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does he say people start with the context, and where does that work?
They want the conclusion to make sense. It works in a written report, and it is fatal in a room.
2. What happens to a listener who cannot tell where the answer is going?
They stop tracking and start waiting — and once somebody is waiting, nothing lands.
3. What does he say is a gift, and why?
Being interrupted after fifteen seconds instead of two minutes, because it means you find out early that your headline was not what they wanted.
Listening 2

A CFO Who Went Blank

In front of a board, in his second year. Sound first — no text.

1. What did he do at the time, and why does he say he would not recommend it?
He kept talking, faster, hoping to arrive somewhere — and after about forty seconds somebody had to offer him a moment.
2. What does he say was the worst part of that episode?
Being given the moment rather than taking it himself.
3. What does he do now instead, and why does it work?
He gives the number first, because a number is easier to retrieve than an argument — and by the time it is out of his mouth, the argument is back.
