Chapter 3: The Code

Moving the Important Part to the Front

Same words, same facts, a different point

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

What I bring is twenty years inside agency economics.

It was the sequence that saved the margin, not the size of the cut.

The thing that changed everything was the reporting line moving abroad.

What I would underscore is that we did it in that order.

Each of these could be said in a plain sentence. What do the opening words add?

PatternPlain versionWith the spotlight
What ... is / wasI bring twenty years of agency economics.What I bring is twenty years of agency economics.
It was ... thatThe sequence saved the margin.It was the sequence that saved the margin.
The thing that ... is / wasThe reporting line changed everything.The thing that changed everything was the reporting line.
All / The reason ...I needed six weeks of visibility.All I needed was six weeks of visibility.
The hidden contrastThe spotlight always denies something. It was the sequence that saved the margin quietly says: it was not the size of the cut. That implied contrast is why the structure sounds decided instead of descriptive.
Why it matters in an interviewIt is the cheapest way to sound like somebody who has a view. Use it once or twice in an answer, never in every sentence — overused, it stops emphasizing and starts performing.

One spotlight per answer. Two at most. It marks the takeaway, and an answer only has one takeaway.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"What I bring are twenty years inside agency economics."
"What I bring is twenty years inside agency economics."
"It was the sequence what saved the margin."
"It was the sequence that saved the margin."
"The thing that changed everything it was the reporting line."
"The thing that changed everything was the reporting line."

After what the verb is singular. After it was the connector is that, never what. And the subject appears only once.

The Move

Headline, Signpost, Detail

1. Headline

"The short answer is that we protected the margin and lost a fifth of the revenue."

2. Signpost

"There are three reasons, and the third is the one people forget."

3. Detail

"The first is... the second is timing... and the third is what the team needed to see."

"The short answer is that we protected the margin and lost a fifth of the revenue. There are three reasons, and the third is the one people forget."

If the headline is not what they wanted, they will interrupt in fifteen seconds instead of three minutes. That is the point, not the risk.

Comprehension

About Claire

1. What does Claire say happens in a room that does not happen in a report?
Nobody knows which part of the context matters, so nothing lands.
2. Does she ask him to cut the reasoning? What does she ask instead?
No — he keeps every word of it. She asks him to signpost it: conclusion, number of reasons, then the reasons.
3. How does she define "concise"?
Not shorter — finished. She can repeat the forty-second version back to him.
Listening

Why Finance People Bury the Point

A communication coach, on the same mistake every week. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does he say people start with the context, and where does that work?
They want the conclusion to make sense. It works in a written report, and it is fatal in a room.
2. What happens to a listener who cannot tell where the answer is going?
They stop tracking and start waiting — and once somebody is waiting, nothing lands.
3. What does he say is a gift, and why?
Being interrupted after fifteen seconds instead of two minutes, because it means you find out early that your headline was not what they wanted.
Listening 2

The Same Answer, Done Right

Conclusion in the first sentence. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the headline, and how many reasons does she signpost?
They protected the margin and lost about a fifth of the revenue, and she would decide the same way again. Three reasons.
2. What is the third reason, the one she says people forget?
That the team needed to see them make an unpopular decision on purpose, rather than have it made for them.
3. What does she underscore at the end, and what does she deny?
She underscores the sequence. She denies that it was the cut that saved the margin — it was doing it in that order.

Chapter 6: Your Turn

Conclusion First

Guided > Semi-free > Free

The Artefact

One Page, Thirty Seconds

Board Paper — One Page
Client Renewal — Recommendation
From: Group CFO
Read time: 30 seconds

Headline

We should let the account go and protect the margin. It is the sequence that makes this work, not the size of the reduction.

Three reasons

  1. The account is structurally unprofitable and has been for six quarters.
  2. Six weeks of visibility is enough to rebuild the cost base, not enough to replace the volume.
  3. The team needs to see an unpopular decision taken on purpose.

What I would underscore: cost first, then volume. In the other order the margin does not survive the quarter.

1. Where is the conclusion on this page, and what does that tell you about the reader?
In the first line. The reader has thirty seconds and may never reach the reasons.
2. Which sentence uses the spotlight structure, and what does it deny?
"It is the sequence that makes this work" — it denies that the size of the reduction is what matters.
3. What is the takeaway the writer wants the board to leave with?
Cost first, then volume — because in the other order the margin does not survive the quarter.
