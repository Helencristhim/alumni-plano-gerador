Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A clue -- a small piece of information that helps you find an answer
"The date on the file was the first clue."
A hunch -- a feeling that something is true, but without any proof
"I have a hunch that file eleven is the one."
To figure something out -- to understand or solve something after thinking about it
"Give me ten minutes and I'll figure it out."
To rule something out -- to decide that something is not possible, so you stop thinking about it
"The band stops in the middle, so we can rule that file out."
A long shot -- an idea or attempt that has only a small chance of being right
"It could be the cable, but that's a long shot."
Far-fetched -- so strange or unlikely that it is very hard to believe
"A ghost in the studio? That's a little far-fetched."
A giveaway -- something that makes the truth very easy to see
"The count-in at the start is a giveaway."
Telltale -- showing clearly that something exists or has happened, usually in “a telltale sign”
"That hiss is a telltale sign of old tape."
To ring a bell -- to sound familiar, but you can't remember exactly why
"That name rings a bell. Did she sing on the show?"
Unmistakable -- so easy to recognize that you cannot confuse it with anything else
"Her laugh at the end is unmistakable."
Muffled -- a sound that is not clear because something is covering it
"The vocal sounds muffled, like the singer is behind a door."
Spot-on -- exactly right, correct in every detail
"Your guess was spot-on. It was the air conditioning."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The label wants the take of Dara’s showcase song that she and Pedro chose in April. The problem: the hard drive has eleven files, and none of them has a name. Pedro has no proof, only clues, so he says how sure he is. File seven can’t be the take, because the band stops in the middle. File nine might be the one — it has the right length — but the vocal is muffled, so it could be a warm-up. File eleven has a count-in at the start, and Pedro only counts in on the takes they keep. So file eleven must be the take.

Look at the small word before the verb. Must means Pedro is almost sure it is true. Might, may and could mean it is possible. Can’t means he is almost sure it is NOT true. After all of them comes the base verb, with no to: it must be, never it must to be. And the opposite of must be is can’t be, not mustn’t be — mustn’t is for rules. When something is happening now, add be + -ing: the red light is on, so they must be recording.

The words of the detective work complete the picture. Pedro has a hunch, and he wants to figure it out step by step. First he rules out file seven. Dara’s idea that file eleven is a copy of the mix is a long shot, because the mix file is much bigger — and the idea that somebody renamed the files at night is just far-fetched. The count-in is a giveaway, and the laugh after the bridge is unmistakable. On an old tape, a hiss is a telltale sign of its age, but these files are digital and have no hiss at all. When Pedro mentions the open door, it rings a bell for Dara, and in the end his guess is spot-on.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Modals of Deduction

GRAMMAR

One question: how sure are you? Almost sure yes, possible, or almost sure no?

How sure?FormExample
Almost sure: yesmust + base verbIt must be the original take.
Possiblemight / may / could + base verbIt might be the cable. It could be the room.
Possibly notmight not / may not + base verbIt might not be the right file.
Almost sure: nocan’t + base verbIt can’t be a live show.
Happening nowmodal + be + verb-ingThey must be recording right now.
Questionscould works in questions; must and might are rareCould it be a copy of the mix?

Never: must to be · mustn’t be (for a guess) · maybe it might be · it can be (for one specific case)

Quiz

B “It might be the cable.”
C “Maybe it might be the cable, or maybe it might be the air conditioning on the left.”

A “It can’t be. There’s no audience.”
B “It mustn’t be from the live show, because there’s no audience in the room at all.”
C “It must be from the live show.”

A “He can be recording.”
