Chapter 3: The Code

Not Yes or No: How Sure?

One small word before the verb says how sure you are

Grammar Discovery

How Sure Are You?

File eleven must be the take we chose.

File nine might be the right one.

It could be a copy from the mixing session.

File seven can’t be the final take.

Pedro has no proof in any of these. What do the blue words tell you? In which one is he almost sure? In which one is he almost sure it is NOT true?

How sure?FormExample
Almost sure: yesmust + base verbIt must be the original take.
Possiblemight / may / could + base verbIt might be the cable. It could be the room.
Possibly notmight not / may not + base verbIt might not be the right file.
Almost sure: nocan’t + base verbIt can’t be a live show. There’s no audience.
Happening nowmodal + be + verb-ingHe must be recording. His phone is off.
The trapsno to after the modal · the opposite of must is can’tNot must to be, not mustn’t be, not maybe it might be.

In one line: the clue tells you the answer; the modal tells people how sure you are.

Comprehension

About Dara

1. Why does Dara need to find the right file today?
The label wants the take they chose in April, and none of the eleven files on the drive has a label.
2. Why does Dara think file nine might be the take, and what does she decide after Pedro's comment?
Because it is the right length. After Pedro mentions the muffled vocal, she decides that file nine must be the warm-up.
3. What is Dara's last doubt about file eleven?
She asks if it could be a copy from the mixing session, and not the original take.
Listening 1 · The Tape with No Label

Detective Work in the Archive

A sound archivist at a TV network and a tape nobody can identify. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say the recording must be older than the year 2000?
Because there is a hiss under the music, and that hiss is a telltale sign of analog tape.
2. Why can't it be a live show, and who might the singer be?
There is no audience at all, so it must be a studio recording. The singer might be a famous singer from the eighties, or it could be a backing singer who sounds like her.
3. What was the clue at the end of the tape, and what is she still figuring out?
Somebody laughs and says "Okay, that's the one," and the voice is the old music director, so it must be one of his sessions. She is still figuring out the date.
Listening 2 · The Buzz in the Left Speaker

Rule It Out, One by One

A mixing engineer and a noise that should not be there. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the engineer's first thought when he hears the buzz?
He thinks the speaker must be broken. It is always the worst idea first, so he tells himself not to panic.
2. How does he rule out the mix and the computer screen?
He plays a different song and the buzz is still there, so it can't be the mix. Then he turns off the screen, and the buzz is still there, so it can't be the screen.
3. What was the giveaway, and what is his advice?
He turned off the air conditioning and the buzz stopped. His advice: rule things out one by one, and don't trust your first guess.
