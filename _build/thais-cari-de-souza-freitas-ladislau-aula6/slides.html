Chapter 4: The Code

Things You Can Count, and Things You Cannot

One idea, two families of words

Grammar Discovery

Which of These Can You Count?

"Many customers never use the whole limit."

"We do not have much data on that cohort yet."

"A few limits are very high, and a little revenue comes from them."

"Most of the losses and a lot of the risk come from one group."

Try putting the number three in front of each red word. Two of them accept it and two do not. Now look at which blue word sits in front of each family, and which two work with both.

The trapsome nouns are uncountable in English and plural in PortugueseNot informations, not datas, not advices.

In one line: if you can put a number in front of it, use many and a few; if you cannot, use much and a little — and a lot of works for both.

Listening 1 · Reading the Numbers

The Quarter, Out Loud

An analyst reading a dashboard to a room. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the default rate, and what was the baseline?
Four point two per cent, against a baseline of three point one.
2. How much of the base is in the pilot, and what does she say about the sample?
Roughly a third. She says the sample is now big enough to trust, which it was not two months ago.
3. What does she say about the average limit?
That hardly any customer is at the average, and that the range matters more than the average does.
Listening 2 · A Podcast

Nobody Is Average

A statistician on the number that describes nobody. Sound first — no text.

1. What example from outside business does he use, and what did they find?
Pilots and cockpit seats. They measured four thousand pilots on ten things, and not one of them was average on all ten.
2. What does he say an average always hides?
The range, and the two ends of it, where the interesting cases are.
3. What does he tell people to ask whenever they see an average?
How many people are actually near it, and what the two ends look like.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I read a decimal with point, never with comma, and a thousand without and.
I use many and a few for things I can count, and much and a little for things I cannot.
I never say informations, datas or advices.
Every figure I say comes with what I am comparing it with.
I know the words: a rate, an average, a share, a gap, roughly, to exceed, to fall short, a range, a sample, a baseline.
