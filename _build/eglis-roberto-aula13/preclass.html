The Night Out -- Then and Now

Our last lesson: a farewell night out with friends, and the question from lesson 1 answered again. Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example.

A night out -- an evening when you go out to have fun with friends
"We had a great night out at the marina."
A regular -- a person who often goes to the same bar or restaurant
"He's a regular here: the barman knows his drink."
A round -- drinks that one person buys for everybody in the group
"It's my round. What are you having?"
To make a toast -- to lift your glass and say something nice about someone
"His wife made a toast to Harry."
To catch up -- to talk with someone and hear their news after a long time
"We caught up over a beer."
To run into -- to meet someone you know by chance
"I ran into an old friend at the airport."
To stay out late -- to come home very late at night
"We stayed out late on Friday."
To call it a night -- to stop what you are doing and go home to bed
"It's two o'clock. Let's call it a night."
To keep in touch -- to keep talking or writing to someone who lives far away
"We have kept in touch for thirty years."
A farewell -- a party or a moment to say goodbye to someone who is leaving
"Harry's farewell is on Friday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Paulo has known Harry for twenty years. Last month Harry told him that he was moving to Portugal, so on Friday the old crew went out for a farewell at the marina bar. Paulo was buying the first round when he ran into Rui, a man he used to sail with in the eighties. They hadn’t seen each other for thirty years.

They caught up for hours. Rui told Paulo about a storm in 1998: “If I had known about the wind, I would have stayed in port.” At two in the morning, Paulo called it a night. “If I were twenty again, I’d stay until sunrise,” he said. Before he left, he made a toast to Harry: “Next summer I’m going to visit you, and this time next year we’ll be sailing in Portugal.”

No new grammar today: every form in bold is one you have practiced in this package. Each one has its own job.

"Paulo has known Harry for twenty years." Why does the text use the present perfect here?
A It is a short action that finished at a clear time in the past, like last Friday at the marina bar.
B It started in the past and is still true now.
C It is a plan for the future.
"If I had known about the wind, I would have stayed in port." What really happened to Rui?
A He didn’t know, and he went out to sea.
B He knew about the wind, so he stayed in port with his boat and waited for the storm to pass.
C He is going to stay in port next time.
Which sentence tells us what somebody said?
A "Harry is moving to Portugal next month with his wife and their old dog."
B "Harry used to sail."
C "Harry told him that he was moving to Portugal."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Whole Package: One Job, One Form

GRAMMAR

A review of the forms from lessons 1 to 12. Choose the form for the job.

JobFormExample
Experience, no datehave / has + past participleI’ve been to Lisbon three times.
A finished timepast simpleI went there in 2019.
The background of a storywas / were + verb-ingI was buying a round when he came in.
An old habitused to + verbI used to stay out until sunrise.
An imaginary presentif + past, would + verbIf I were twenty, I’d stay.
An imaginary pastif + had + past participle, would haveIf I had known, I would have come.
What someone saidtold me (that) + one tense backHe told me he was moving.
The futuregoing to / present continuous / will be + verb-ingI’m flying on Monday. This time next year, I’ll be sailing.

Never: I have been there in 2019 · I am in Brazil since 1990 · If I knew, I would go (for the past) · He told me he is moving · I will fly on Monday (the ticket is booked)

Quiz
