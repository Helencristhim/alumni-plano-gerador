Chapter 3: The Code

I Manage, She Manages

One tense for jobs and routines

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Show?

"I manage the HR team."

"She manages the payroll."

"He doesn’t work in finance."

"Does she report to you?"

Look at the blue verbs. With I the verb is simple; with she and he it takes an -s. Now the red words: with doesn’t and does, the -s goes away from the verb.

JobFormExample
I / you / we / theyverbI manage the HR team.
he / she / itverb + sShe manages the payroll.
Negativedon’t / doesn’t + verbHe doesn’t work in finance.
QuestionDo / Does + subject + verb?Does she report to you?
Special formshave → has · go → goes · do → doesShe has a team of six.
The trapno -s after does or doesn’tNot she doesn’t works. Say she doesn’t work.

In one line: he, she and it take -s; after does and doesn’t, the verb is simple again.

Chapter 4: Meeting People

Meeting People

One breakfast, two professionals, one chart

Dialogue

A Business Breakfast, Table Four

M
Hi, I’m Mark. Nice to meet you. So, what do you do?
A
Hi, Mark. I’m Andrea. I work in human resources. I’m the Head of HR.
M
Oh, interesting. What are you in charge of?
A
Everything about people: payroll, benefits, training, and we hire new people too.
M
That’s a lot! I work in finance. I manage a team of five analysts.
A
Nice. And who do you report to?
M
I report to the finance director. She lives in Chicago, so we talk online every Monday.
A
I report to the CEO. We meet every week, here in the office.
Comprehension

About Mark

1. Which department does Mark work in?
Finance.
2. How many people does Mark manage?
Five analysts.
3. Who does Mark report to, and how do they talk?
The finance director. They talk online every Monday.
