Lesson 13 -- Pre-class

Fluency Under Pressure -- Hesitate Out Loud

What to say in the three seconds when your mind goes blank. Key words: to rephrase, to prompt, to redirect, to acknowledge, to interrupt, bear with me, that is beside the point, to jump in, to lose your train of thought, to get your point across. Structure: fillers, self-repair and time-buying phrases in spontaneous speech.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To rephrase -- to say the same idea again, in different words
"That came out wrong. Let me rephrase it."
To prompt -- to help somebody continue by giving them a small question or a word
"If I get stuck, prompt me with the first word."
To redirect -- to bring a conversation back to the point it should be on
"We got lost in the details, so she redirected us to the main question."
To acknowledge -- to show that you have heard and understood what somebody said
"A quick right or I see is enough to acknowledge the point."
To interrupt -- to start speaking while somebody else is still talking
"Sorry to interrupt, but could you say that again?"
Bear with me -- asks the other person to be patient while you find the words
"Bear with me, I know what I want to say."
That is beside the point -- says that something is true but not important for this discussion
"The weather was terrible, but that is beside the point."
To jump in -- to enter a conversation that is already happening
"Can I jump in here? I have an example."
To lose your train of thought -- to forget, in the middle of a sentence, what you were going to say
"Sorry, I have lost my train of thought. Where was I?"
To get your point across -- to make other people understand what you mean
"My English was not perfect, but I got my point across."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Many students believe that a fluent speaker never hesitates. Record any native speaker for ten minutes and you will hear the opposite. They hesitate all the time. The difference is that they hesitate out loud. Instead of going silent, they say let me think about that, off the top of my head or bear with me. These phrases carry no information. Their job is to tell the listener that you are still in the conversation and that the sentence is coming.

A second family repairs what has already come out. I mean, or rather and what I mean is let you correct yourself without stopping and starting again: It was hard. Well, what I mean is, it was slow. When a word does not come at all, describe it: I am not sure of the exact word, but it is something like a fresh start. And if you forget what you were saying, say so: Sorry, I have lost my train of thought. Where was I?

The line between a good filler and a bad one is short. A filler of one or two seconds keeps you in the conversation. A silence of three seconds or more takes you out, because the listener starts to wonder whether you understood the question. The freeze is not a gap in what you know. It is a gap in speed, and these phrases buy the time.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Fillers, Repair and Time-Buying Phrases

GRAMMAR

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

In an interview, you are asked a question you did not expect. You say:
A Nothing, for ten seconds, until the answer is complete in your head.
B That's a really good question. Let me think for a second.
C I don't know.
You cannot remember the word for something. You say:
A I'm not sure of the exact word, but it's something like a fresh start.
B Wait, wait, wait.
C Sorry, I will stop and start again from the beginning when I remember the word.
You said something and it came out wrong. You say:
A Forget everything I just said.
B Can you repeat again what I said, please, so I can hear it?
C Sorry, let me rephrase that.
Two people are discussing something and you want to add an example. You say:
A That is beside the point, so now I will talk.
B Can I jump in here? I have an example.
C Bear with me while you stop talking, because I want to say something about it.
