Chapter 3: Silence Is Not Fluency

Silence Is Not Fluency

The phrases that carry no information, and why you need them

Grammar Discovery

Delete the Color. What Is Lost?

"That is a really interesting question. Let me think. I would go back to Franca."

"It was hard. Well, not hard, exactly. What I mean is, it was slow."

"I am not sure of the exact word, but it is something like a fresh start."

"Right. I see what you mean. Going back to what you said earlier, why Franca?"

Delete every colored part and the information is still there. So the colored parts are not there for information. Each one does a different job in the conversation. What are the jobs?

JobPhrasesWhen
Buy timelet me think about that · off the top of my head · bear with meThe question was unexpected.
RepairI mean · or rather · what I mean is · let me rephrase thatWhat came out is not what you meant.
Find the wordI am not sure of the exact word, but it is something like…The word does not come. Describe it.
Show you are listeningright · exactly · I see what you mean · fair pointThe other person is talking.
Clarify and redirectcould you say that again? · what do you mean by…? · going back to what you saidYou missed something, or the topic drifted.
Recoversorry, I have lost my train of thought · where was I?You forgot what you were saying.
The linea short filler keeps you in the conversation — three seconds of silence takes you outProductive vs disruptive.

Kind of, sort of and like work as fillers in casual speech. In an interview, choose let me think instead.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

No Time to Think

One fast conversation, two recordings, one recovery toolkit

Dialogue

Quick Question, No Time to Think

H
Quick question, no time to think. If you had a completely free month, starting tomorrow, what would you do?
A
Oh, that is a really good question. Bear with me. Off the top of my head, I would go back to Franca, kind of to see if it still feels like home.
H
Interesting. And what is the hardest part about being eighteen in São Paulo?
A
The hardest part is, I mean, it is not the city. What I am trying to say is that everybody here seems to know exactly where they are going.
H
And you don’t?
A
I do, actually. Or rather, I know where. I just don’t know how long it will take. Sorry, I have lost my train of thought. What was the question?
H
The hardest part of being eighteen here. Don’t worry, you got your point across.
A
Right, exactly. Going back to what you asked, though — what was the hardest part for you, at my age?
Comprehension

About Hannah

1. How does Hannah introduce her first question?
She says it is a quick question and that there is no time to think.
2. What does she ask about life in São Paulo?
What the hardest part is about being eighteen there.
3. What does she do when he loses his train of thought?
She repeats the question in a few words and tells him not to worry, because he got his point across.
Listening 1 · A Podcast

Hesitate Out Loud

A conversation coach on what fluent speakers really do. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he ask his students to do, and what surprises them?
Listen to ten minutes of a podcast and count. They are shocked that native speakers hesitate all the time.
2. What does he say those phrases tell the other person?
That you are still in the conversation and that the sentence is coming.
3. What happens after about three seconds of silence?
The listener starts to wonder whether you have understood the question.
Listening 2 · A Voice Note

The Hardest Decision

Somebody answering an unexpected question, without a script. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say was the hardest decision she ever made?
Leaving her first job — or, as she corrects herself, deciding to leave it.
2. How long had she been in that job?
She first says six years, then corrects it to seven.
3. In the end, how does she describe the decision?
Not as one moment, but as about two years of small moments and then one very ordinary morning.

Role-Play 1 of 3 — Guided

Three Seconds, No More

Scenario: Hannah asks unexpected questions about your life, one after another. You may hesitate, but never in silence. Every pause longer than three seconds has to be filled with a phrase from your toolkit.

Keyword chips:

let me think off the top of my head bear with me what I mean is
Role-Play 2 of 3 — Semi-free

Say It Another Way

Scenario: Your teacher reads a short paragraph out loud. Explain it in your own words, without looking and without repeating its sentences. When a word does not come, describe it.

Keywords:

what I mean is or rather let me rephrase that something like
Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

Three Minutes, No Silence

Scenario: A real conversation for three minutes. Tell your teacher about a moment when you had to make a really difficult decision. If you lose a word, use your toolkit and keep going. Never stop to start again.

No keywords, no notes, three minutes. Hesitate out loud.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

When a question surprises me, I buy time out loud instead of going silent.
When a word does not come, I describe it and keep going.
When something comes out wrong, I repair it without starting again.
I have five default phrases, and I can say them without thinking.
I know the words: to rephrase, to prompt, to redirect, to acknowledge, to interrupt, bear with me, that is beside the point, to jump in, to lose your train of thought, to get your point across.
