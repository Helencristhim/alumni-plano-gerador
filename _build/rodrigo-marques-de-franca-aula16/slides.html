Chapter 4: The Code

Where Did the Words Go?

Reduced relative clauses and participle clauses

Grammar Discovery

What Is Missing?

"the Supply Agreement signed with Nordhaven on 3 March 2025"

"the party wishing to terminate must first send a written notice"

"Having reviewed the delivery notes, we see no evidence of force majeure."

"Any notice not complying with this requirement is likely to be construed as invalid."

Each red word replaces a whole clause. Say the long version of each one. What disappeared?

Short formLong versionExample
noun + past participle (passive)which / that was + past participlethe equipment ordered in June = which was ordered
noun + -ing (active)who / that + verbthe party wishing to terminate = who wishes
not + -ingthat does not + verbany notice not complying = that does not comply
Having + past participleafter we had + past participle (the first action)Having reviewed the notes, we see... = after we had reviewed
The subject ruleThe person who did the participle must be the subject after the comma.Having reviewed the file, we found... NOT Having reviewed the file, the contract seemed...
When NOT to reduceA single finished action in the past, active: keep the full clause.the client who called yesterday NOT the client calling yesterday

In one line: -ed is done to the noun; -ing is done by the noun; having + -ed happened first.

Real Document

Decode the Clause

Supply Agreement · Valença Alimentos / Nordhaven B.V. · reviewed by Rodrigo Marques de França

14.1 Any Party wishing to terminate this Agreement on the grounds of a material breach committed by the other Party shall first serve a written notice, delivered by courier to the address set forth in the Recitals, specifying the breach complained of and granting the defaulting Party a period of fifteen (15) days, counted from receipt thereof, within which to remedy it.

1. Who can send the notice?
"A party that wants to end the contract because the other side committed a material breach."
2. How must the notice be delivered?
"In writing, by courier, to the address in the recitals -- not by email."
3. What must the notice say?
"It must say exactly what the breach is."
4. When do the fifteen days start?
"When the other party receives the notice."

Listening 1 · The Editor

The Budget That Reviewed Nothing

An editor of business reports, on the habit she spends most of her time fighting. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say a good long sentence is efficient?
Because phrases like the documents attached to this email are short versions of whole clauses, and readers process them quickly.
2. What is wrong with her example sentence?
Having reviewed the figures, the budget looked unrealistic: the budget did not review anything -- the person who did has disappeared.
3. What does she check before sending anything?
That the first noun after the comma is the person who did the action.
Listening 2 · The Partner

Two Sentences in Plain English

A partner who writes opinions for international clients. Sound first — no text.

1. What has he changed his mind about?
Not about the formal language itself, but about starting with it.
2. How does every opinion of his firm open now?
With two sentences in plain English: the question and the answer.
3. Who is the long sentence for, according to him?
For the lawyers on the other side and for the judge, if it ever gets that far.
