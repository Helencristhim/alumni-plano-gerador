Lesson 16 -- The Long Sentence

Podcast Clip
Lex Fridman Podcast -- "Why lawyers use hard-to-understand language" (with Edward Gibson, MIT)

A cognitive scientist explains what his lab found when it measured legal sentences against ordinary English, and why center-embedded clauses make contracts hard even for lawyers. The research behind today's lesson, in conversation.

Tip: each time he mentions a structure that makes sentences hard, ask yourself whether the opinion you read in class used it, and where.

Video
BBC Learning English -- "What is 'legalese'? An animated explainer"

A short animated history of legal English: where words like hereinafter and whereas come from, and the plain-language movement that wants to replace them.

Tip: write down every legal word they mention and decide which ones you would keep in an opinion for a foreign client, and which ones you would replace.

A clear lesson on the -ing, -ed and having forms you decoded today, with the subject rule that the editor in Listening 1 talked about.
