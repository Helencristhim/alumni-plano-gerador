Chapter 4: Which One, Exactly

The Half-Sentence That Answers Which One

And the two commas that change what it is doing

Grammar Discovery

Which One Has Commas, and Why?

"The two verticals where we have the deepest experience are banking and logistics."

"The eleven engineers whose tenure is over five years built the platform."

"This is the platform that the incumbent supplier could not replace."

"Our largest client, which arrived through a referral, has renewed four times."

Each red half answers the question which one? — remove it and the sentence stops identifying anything. The orange one is inside commas, and removing it changes nothing: there was only ever one largest client, so the clause is a detail rather than an identification. That is the whole difference, and it is worth two commas.

JobFormExample
Saying which personwho (or that)The partner who ran the call has done eleven deals.
Saying which thingwhich or thatThe platform that the incumbent could not replace.
Saying which place or situationwhereThe two verticals where we have the deepest experience.
Saying whose it iswhose + nounThe engineers whose tenure is over five years.
Adding a detail about something already uniquetwo commas, and never thatOur largest client, which arrived through a referral, has renewed.
Dropping the pronounpossible when it is the object of the clauseThe client (that) we onboarded in March.
The trapthe subject is not repeated after the pronounNot the engineers who they built it. Say the engineers who built it.
The permanent targetthe verb agrees with the noun in frontThe client who has renewed. The clients who have renewed.

In one line: no commas means which one; commas mean by the way.

Grammar Practice

Which One, or By the Way?

Say it first, then click to check

“The two verticals ___where we have the deepest experience are banking and logistics. (a place or a situation)”
“This is the platform ___that the incumbent supplier could not replace. (a thing, and the clause says which platform)”
“The partner ___who ran the call has done eleven deals. (a person)”
“The eleven engineers ___whose tenure is over five years built the platform. (it belongs to them)”
“Our largest client___, which arrived through a referral, has renewed four times. (there is only one largest client)”
“The three clients who ___have renewed twice are all in banking. (have -- the verb agrees with clients, not with who)”

Chapter 5: Real Speed

The Same Habit, From Both Sides

Two recordings and one page of a real data room index

Listening 1 · A Fund Partner

The Founders Who Qualify Themselves

A fund partner on the four-minute test she runs in every first meeting. Sound first — no text.

1. What question does she ask, and what does the unprepared founder answer?
She asks an open question about the clients. The unprepared founder says they work with large enterprises across several sectors, and then stops and waits for the next question.
2. What does the prepared founder add that nobody asked for?
The part of the client base he actually worries about.
3. What does she say changes once she hears that?
She stops testing him and starts thinking with him, which she calls a completely different meeting.
Listening 2 · A Deal Adviser

Why Short Sentences Cost You Money

A deal adviser on the advice founders are given and should ignore in this one room. Sound first — no text.

1. When does he say short sentences are right, and when are they wrong?
Right when you are pitching to a crowd. Wrong in diligence, where you are being examined rather than pitching.
2. What happens when you say you have a strong backlog and stop?
The analyst decides what strong means, and she decides conservatively, because that is her job.
3. What does he say the longer version achieves?
There is nothing left for anybody else to fill in, so the number you said is the number that goes into the model.

Six lines, six clauses. Take the clauses out and every line becomes a question the buyer has to ask, which costs a week each time.
1. Which line uses commas, and what would change without them?
Line 2.2. Without the commas it would be identifying which client out of several arrived through a referral, instead of adding a detail about the one largest client.
2. Rewrite line 2.4 as one sentence with a clause in it.
Any version works if it keeps the split, for example: the part of the backlog that is under signed contract is seventy-one percent, and the remainder is verbal.
3. Which line would you want to expand before the first meeting, and why?
Any answer works if it names the line and says what a buyer would ask about it. What does not work is naming a line without the question attached.
