The Pattern

Which one was decided before now?

I am going to present a case in November. (booked months ago)
We are going to close the budget before I leave. (already the plan)
— Nobody is answering. — Fine, I will answer it. (decided this second)
— I do not have the file. — I will send it to you tonight. (decided this second)

A plan → going to. A decision made now → will

If you already decided before this conversation, use going to. If you are deciding while you speak — offering, promising, volunteering — use will. Both are the future; they say when you decided.

The Pattern

Two futures, two moments

Already decided

I am going to present a case.
We are going to close the budget first.
I am not going to miss a session.

The plan existed before you opened your mouth.

Decided right now

I will answer that one.
I will send it tonight.
I’ll ask him for you.

Offers, promises, volunteering. In speech: I’ll.

In a session, both are useful: "I’m going to talk about our migration" (planned) and "I’ll take that question" (right now).

Did You Catch It?

About their week

1. What is on the agenda, and when is the panel?
Four sessions a day, and a panel on Thursday.
2. What is David’s goal, and why does he say it is a real one?
To ask one question a day — because last year he asked zero.
3. What does David offer to do, and when?
To read Claire’s case that night and send her notes before breakfast.
Listening 1

Two weeks, and a plan

1. What is she not going to do this time, and why?
Try to talk to everybody — last time she came home with forty business cards and no friends.
2. What are her two rules for the sessions and for work?
Ask at least one question a day, and not answer work emails before six.
3. What outcome does she want?
Five people she can call, and one idea she can actually use.
Listening 2

The programme agenda

1. What happens in the mornings, and who leads?
Two sessions, each facilitated by a professor — discussion, not lecture.
2. What is the Wednesday task in week two?
Each team presents a case to the whole room, twelve minutes, no exceptions.
3. What does he say about the panel on the last Thursday?
The good questions come from the floor, not from the panel.
