Stage 1.1: Words for a Programme

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say each word out loud with your own numbers. Eight words, three minutes.

Agenda — the list of what will happen, and when
"The agenda for day one is very full."
Outcome — what you get at the end of something
"The outcome I want is more confidence."
Session — one part of a programme, with a start and an end
"There are four sessions a day."
Panel — a group of people who answer questions in front of a room
"On Thursday there is a panel on risk."
Facilitate — to lead a discussion without giving the answers
"A professor will facilitate the group work."
Participate — to take part, and not only listen
"I am going to participate, not just sit there."
Goal — the thing you are trying to reach
"My goal is to ask one question a day."
Commit to — to promise you will really do something
"I am committing to thirty minutes a day."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the text. Then answer the questions.

I am going to spend two weeks at the programme in November, and I have a plan. Before I leave, we are going to close the annual budget, because I am not going to answer emails about it from California. The agenda has four sessions a day; a professor will facilitate each one, so I am going to have to participate and not just listen. On the last Thursday there is a panel, and my goal is to ask one question a day, even a bad one. Last time I asked zero. The outcome I want is five people I can call afterwards. I am committing to that now, out loud, so somebody can check.
What is going to happen before he leaves?
A They are going to close the annual budget.
B He is going to answer emails from California.
Why is it "a professor will facilitate" and not "is going to facilitate"?
A Because it is a fact about the programme, announced by someone else — not his own plan.
B Because professors always use will.
What is his goal?
A To meet forty people.
B To ask one question a day.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Two futures — the difference is when you decided

Decided before this conversation → going to. Deciding while you speak → will.

going to — the plan

I’m going to present a case.
We’re going to close the budget.
I’m not going to miss a session.

will — right now

I’ll take that question.
I’ll send it tonight.
I’ll ask him for you.

? Are you going to present? — not "Do you going to present?"
× "I will to close the budget" — after will the verb is bare: I will close.
× "I go to participate" — the plan needs going to: I am going to participate.

In real speech: gonna. You do not have to say it — but you will hear it constantly, so recognise it.

Stage 4: In the room

Quiz
You decided months ago to go to the programme. How do you say it?
A "I will go to a programme in November."
B "I’m going to a programme in November."
Nobody volunteers to answer. You decide, right now, to do it. What do you say?
A "I’ll answer that one."
B "I am going to answer that one."
Which goal could somebody check on Friday?
A "I am going to network a lot and improve my English."
B "I am going to ask one question a day and sit with new people at every lunch."

Stage 5: Two minutes on November

Reflection
Record two minutes in English. Tell me your plan for the two weeks: three things that are already decided, one goal that somebody could actually check on the last Friday, and one thing you are not going to do, with the reason. Use going to at least four times. Finish with a question for your teacher.
