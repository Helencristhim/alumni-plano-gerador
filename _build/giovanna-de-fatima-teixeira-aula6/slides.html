Chapter 3: Can, Can't, Could

One Word, Three Jobs

I am able to · I am allowed to · may I, politely

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I can read a technical drawing."
"I can't operate the excavator."
"With this badge you can go into the tunnel."
"Visitors can't operate anything."

Two sentences are about a skill. Two are about a rule. Which is which?

FormWhat it meansExample
can (skill)I know how to do itI can read a technical drawing.
can (rule)I am allowed to do itWith this badge you can go into the tunnel.
can'tno skill, or no permissionVisitors can't operate anything.
QuestionCan + person + verb?Can you sign this report?
Never changesAfter can the verb is naked: no to, no -s, no -ing. She can sign, never "she cans to signs".
Grammar Discovery

Asking Politely

"Open the gate."
"Can you open the gate?"
"Could you open the gate, please?"
"Could I have access to the north area?"

Same request, three temperatures. Which one do you say to your supervisor?

You sayHow it soundsUse it with
Open the gate.an order, not a requestan emergency, and nothing else
Can you...?normal, friendlya colleague you see every day
Could you...?polite, a little more distancea supervisor, a client, a visitor
Could I...?asking for permission for yourselfCould I use the meeting room?
The one ruleHere could is not the past. It is the same request, with warmer manners. And please goes at the end, after a comma.

Your Badge, Your Rules

Role-Play — Guided

Sorry, You Can't

Scenario: A visitor with a green badge wants to go down into the tunnel with you. Tell him what he can do, what he can't do, and what he needs first. Be firm, but friendly.

Keywords:

You can walk I'm sorry, you can't You need the training with a guide
Role-Play — Semi-free

Three Things from Your Supervisor

Scenario: It is Monday morning and you need three things from Ray: access to the north area, a place for the training on Thursday, and his signature on the report. Ask for all three, politely. He will say no to one of them.

Keywords:

Could I Could you sign permission Thursday
Free Practice

Sixty Seconds About Yet

Sixty seconds: three things you can do at work today, and one thing you can't do yet. Then the same about your English: three things you can already do in English, and one thing you can't do yet.

No keywords. No stopping. The word "yet" is the whole point.
