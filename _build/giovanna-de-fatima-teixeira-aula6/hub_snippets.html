What I Can and Can't Do Yet

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen as many times as you want.

Allowed -- when the rules say yes, you can do it
"Only two people are allowed in the tunnel at the same time."
Permission -- the yes that somebody gives you before you do something
"I have permission to go into the north area this week."
Access -- the right to go into a place or to use a system
"My badge gives me access to every area at ground level."
Badge -- the small card with your name and photo that opens the gate
"Show your badge at the entrance, every morning."
Training -- the classes and the practice you do before you are ready for a job
"She has the training for confined spaces."
License -- the official paper that says you are ready to drive or use a machine
"He has the license for the excavator."
Operate -- to make a big machine work
"Only three people on this site can operate the excavator."
Sign -- to write your name on a paper to say yes, I agree
"She can sign the daily report."
Skill -- something you have learned to do well
"Each new skill on his card is one more thing he can do."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the text out loud, then answer the questions below. Read slowly -- nobody is timing you.

Every morning three people stop at the same gate and show a badge. The color of the card says what each one is allowed to do. Marcos has a green badge: he can walk on the yellow walkway with a guide, but he cannot go into the tunnel and he cannot operate anything. Amanda has a blue badge. She has access to every area at ground level, because she has the training for confined spaces, and she can sign the daily report. She cannot operate the big machines: that is a different license. Tomas has a red badge and the license for the excavator, so he can operate it all day. For the office area he needs permission, and he always asks politely: "Could I use the meeting room for ten minutes?" Every new skill on his card is one more thing he can do.

1. The text says "he cannot operate anything". Is that about skill or about rules?
A About skill: Marcos never learned how to use a machine.
B About rules: a green badge does not allow it, and that is all the text tells us.
C About time: he only comes twice a month.
2. Why does Tomas say "Could I use the meeting room?" and not "Use the meeting room"?
A Because "could" means yesterday, and he used it yesterday.
B Because he is not sure the room exists.
C Because he is asking for permission, and "could" is the polite way to ask.
3. What comes after "can" in every sentence in this text?
A The verb with nothing on it: can walk, can sign, can operate.
B "to" and then the verb: can to walk, can to sign.
C The verb with -ing: can walking, can signing.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Can, Can't, Could

Grammar

One small word with three different jobs, and one shape that never changes.

FormWhat it meansExample
can (skill)I know how to do itI can read a technical drawing.
can (rule)I am allowed to do itWith this badge you can go into the tunnel.
can't / cannotno skill, or no permissionVisitors can't operate anything.
QuestionCan + person + verb?Can you sign this report?
Short answersyes / no, with the same wordYes, I can. / No, I can't.
You sayHow it soundsUse it with
Open the gate.an order, not a requestan emergency, and nothing else
Can you...?normal, friendlya colleague you see every day
Could you...?polite, a little more distancea supervisor, a client, a visitor
Could I...?asking permission for yourselfCould I use the meeting room?
The one ruleHere could is not the past. It is the same request, with warmer manners. And please goes at the end, after a comma.

The shape that never changes

After can, can't and could the verb is naked: no to, no -s, no -ing. She can sign — never "she cans to signs". And for questions there is no do: Can you...?, never "Do you can...?"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation. Wrong? Try again -- nobody is watching.

A visitor with a green badge wants to walk down into the tunnel with you. You say:
A "You not can to go in the tunnel."
B "I'm sorry, you can't go into the tunnel. You need the training first."
C "Do you can go in the tunnel? No."
You need your supervisor to sign the report before lunch. You say:
A "Sign this report now."
B "You could to sign this report?"
C "Could you sign this report before lunch, please?"
A colleague asks if you know how to use the excavator. You do not have the license. You say:
A "No, I can't. I don't have the license for it."
B "No, I don't can. I have not the license for it."
C "No, I cannot to operate. The license is not with me."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record your answer. There is no right or wrong here, and nobody corrects anything.
