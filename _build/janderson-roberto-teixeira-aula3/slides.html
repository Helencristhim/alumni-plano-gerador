Lesson 3 · The Interruption

What Were You Doing When…

Every good story has two verbs in it: the long one that was already happening, and the short one that cut across it.

Chapter 1: The Interruption

One Morning That Went Wrong

A day when you were doing something completely normal and something stopped it. Traffic, a phone call, a closed road, a client who arrived early.

Where You Start

A List, or a Story

The list

"I left home. There was traffic. I arrived late."

The story

"I was driving to the office when the road closed."

The difference

One long verb, one short verb, and the word when

A list of finished actions is correct English and nobody listens to it. The moment you put one action inside another, the person in front of you leans in, because now there is a scene and something is about to interrupt it.

Chapter 3: The Code

The Long Verb and the Short One

What was already happening, and what interrupted it

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I was driving to the office when the road closed."

"I was sitting in a traffic jam when my telephone rang."

"While I was reading the email, I realised the time."

"What were you doing when the client arrived?"

Every sentence has two actions. One of them started first and was still going. The other one arrived and cut across it. Which form does each one take?

FormWhat it saysExample
Past continuous: was / were + verb-INGThe long action. It was already happening, and it is the background of the scene."I was driving to the office."
Past simple: the short actionThe interruption. It happened once, quickly, and it finished."… when the road closed."
when and whileWhen usually introduces the short action. While usually introduces the long one. Both sentences mean the same thing."I was reading when he called."
"While I was reading, he called."
Two long actions togetherIf both actions were happening at the same time, both take the -ing form."While I was driving, she was working from home."
The questionMove was or were to the front. Nothing is borrowed — there is no did here."What were you doing when the client arrived?"

The past continuous is not about time. It is about a frame: it paints the scene, and the past simple is what walks into it.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I drove to the office when the road closed."
"I was driving to the office when the road closed."
"What did you doing when he called?"
"What were you doing when he called?"
"I was not knowing that the meeting was at eight."
"I did not know that the meeting was at eight."

The action that was already running takes was or were plus -ing. That form already contains everything it needs, so it never asks for did. And the small group of verbs about the mind — know, understand, want, need — still refuses the -ing form, exactly as it did in Lesson 1. Read the correct versions aloud twice.

Chapter 4: The Bad Morning

The Call You Almost Missed

Rachel is in the London office and she starts on time

Dialogue

Twenty Minutes Late

R
There you are. We started without you. What happened?
J
I am sorry. I was on my way to the office when the road closed, all of a sudden, with no warning.
R
And you were stuck there the whole time?
J
Fifty minutes in the same traffic jam. I was reading your email when I realised I had no signal.
R
That explains it. I was about to call you when you finally walked in.
J
Luckily I made it before the client did. Did he say anything?
R
He was laughing about it. He was sitting in the same traffic jam, apparently. He hung up twice.
J
Then we were all late together. What were you doing while we were both stuck?
Comprehension

About Rachel

1. What did Rachel and the others do when he did not arrive?
They started the meeting without him.
2. What was she about to do when he walked in?
She was about to call him.
3. What does she say the client was doing?
He was laughing about it, and he was sitting in the same traffic jam.
4. How many times did the client hang up?
Twice.
Listening

A station announcement. Sound first -- no text.

1. How late is the eight fifteen service?

2. What was happening when the signal failed?
The train was leaving the depot.
3. Who should speak to a member of staff, and why?

Listening 2

Six Cars Behind You

A short personal story from a podcast. Sound first -- no text.

1. What was he doing when his telephone rang?
He was sitting in a traffic jam, going nowhere.
2. What was he about to do, and what stopped him?
He was about to apologise, and the client told him he was stuck too, six cars behind.
3. Why does he say he was lucky?
Because nobody moved for forty minutes, so they finished the whole meeting.

Chapter 6: Your Turn

Explaining Yourself

Role-Play

Arriving Late

Scenario: You walk into a meeting twenty minutes late and Rachel asks what happened. Apologise once, explain in two sentences with one interruption in them, and ask one question back.

Keywords:

Role-Play

Now You Ask

Scenario: Rachel had a very strange Monday and she is waiting to be asked about it. Find out four things: where she was going, what she was doing when it happened, what interrupted her, and how it ended.

Keywords:

Where were you going? What were you doing? What happened then? How did it end?
Free Practice

The Day It All Went Wrong

Scenario: Tell the story of one day from your life that went wrong: where you were, what you were doing, what interrupted it, and how it ended. Then ask two questions about a day of hers.

No keywords. The conversation is yours.

The Habit

One Scene, Out Loud

Before the next class, describe one moment of your day out loud, once a day, using two verbs: what you were doing, and what interrupted it. Sixty seconds, to nobody.

You already know this structure. What you do not yet have is the habit of reaching for it at speed.
