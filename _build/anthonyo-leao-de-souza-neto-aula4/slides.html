Your Words at Work

Which Word Do You Need?

1. You just walked in, and there are two of you.
Table. "Good evening. A table for two, please."
2. The waiter comes back and you still have not decided.
Ready. "Sorry, we are not ready yet. Two more minutes, please."
3. Dinner is over and you want to go.
Bill. "Could I have the bill, please?"
4. The food is very good and you want to say so.
Delicious. "It is delicious, thank you."
Chapter 3: I Would Like

Asking Without Ordering

I want → I would like

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I want the fish."

"I would like the fish, please."

"Can I have the menu, please?"

"Could I have the bill, please?"

FormHow it soundsExample
I want + thingcorrect, but hard — like an instructionI want the fish.
I would like + thing · short: I'd likethe normal, polite way to orderI'd like the fish, please.
Can I have + thing?easy and friendly — works everywhereCan I have the menu, please?
Could I have + thing?a little more formal than canCould I have the bill, please?

Same request, four ways. Put please at the end of all of them and you are never rude by accident.

What Did Clara Say?

1. What is the fish of the day, and what comes with it?
Salmon, from this morning. It comes with potatoes.
2. What is very good tonight, to start?
The tomato soup.
3. What is important about the bill?
Service is not included.

0:000:00

2. Why is the steak special tonight?
Because it is twenty-five minutes. Better to order it now.
3. When is the moment for the dessert, and why?
With the main dish, because the kitchen is open until ten thirty.

A Friend Recommends a Restaurant

Three things to know before you go. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Why is a reservation important here?
Because it is small, about ten tables, and it is always full at eight.
2. What are the two important things?
They are closed on Mondays, and service is not in the bill.
