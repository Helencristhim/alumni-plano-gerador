Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Pitch -- a short, prepared talk to convince people to accept an idea or a plan
"I have five minutes for my pitch to the deal committee."
Stakeholder -- a person or group that has an interest in a decision and is affected by it
"Every stakeholder wants a different closing date."
Rationale -- the set of reasons that explains why a decision makes sense
"The rationale for the review is simple: the property files are old."
Evidence -- facts, numbers or documents that show something is true
"Bring evidence, not opinions: the dates, the files, the numbers."
Back up -- to support what you say with facts or numbers
"I can back up that point with the data room index."
Compelling -- so strong and clear that people find it hard to say no
"Javier wants one compelling reason, not a long list."
Credibility -- the quality that makes people trust and believe you
"If you exaggerate one risk, you lose credibility on all of them."
Track record -- the results that you or your team have achieved in the past
"Your team has a good track record, and that gives you credibility."
Outweigh -- to be bigger or more important than something else
"The cost of three weeks does not outweigh the cost of a hidden liability."
Buy-in -- real agreement and support from the people who must act on a decision
"Without buy-in from the business team, the plan will not work."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

On Thursday, Simone has five minutes to give her pitch to the deal committee. The business team wants to close in March, and every stakeholder is worried about time. Her rationale is simple. The property files in Campinas have not been checked since 2019, and she can back up that point with evidence from the data room. In addition, the target had a problem with the regulator two years ago, so its track record is not clean. What is more, the antitrust clearance takes ninety days anyway. Therefore, three weeks of review does not change the closing date. Simone knows that one exaggeration can destroy her credibility, so she does not call the risk huge. Her most compelling point is the last one: the cost of three weeks does not outweigh the cost of a hidden liability. As a result, she hopes to get real buy-in from the committee, not only a polite yes.

1. Why does Simone not call the risk huge?
A Because the business team has already decided to close the deal in March without any review at all.
B Because the risk is not on the register.
C Because she does not want to lose credibility with an exaggeration.
2. Why does three weeks of review not change the closing date?
A Because the antitrust clearance takes ninety days anyway.
B Because the board has agreed to move the closing date to the end of April for everybody.
C Because the seller accepted it.
3. What is her most compelling point?
A That old files are normal in every deal.
B That the cost of three weeks does not outweigh the cost of a hidden liability.
C That the target is cheap.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Connectors of Addition and Result

GRAMMAR

A persuasive argument has a shape: a reason, a second reason, and then the request as a result. English marks each step with a connector. In addition, furthermore and what is more add a reason. Therefore, as a result and that is why show the result. These connectors usually start a new sentence and take a comma.

FormUseExample
in addition, / furthermore,add a second reason"In addition, its track record is not clean."
what is more,add the strongest reason, often last"What is more, the clearance takes ninety days."
therefore, / as a result,show the result"Therefore, I am asking for three weeks."
that is why + sentenceshow the result, spoken style"That is why we need the review."
so (inside one sentence)a short result, no new sentence"The files are old, so we need a review."

Key point: the fixed forms are in addition (never "in addition of that") and as a result (never "as result"). And put the request LAST: reason, in addition, therefore.

Stage 2: Build the Pitch in Order

Order

Listen first, then put the steps of a persuasive pitch in the correct order.

?You find evidence to back up each reason: a date, a number, a document.
?You finish with the request, as a result: therefore, I am asking for it.
?You think about each stakeholder and what they are afraid of losing.
?You add the second reason with in addition or what is more.
?You choose one or two clear reasons: that is your rationale.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best sentence for each situation.

Javier asks for your request. Which answer is the most persuasive?
A "Please, can we wait a little more, because I think maybe it is important and I am a bit worried about it?"
B "As result, we wait."
C "Therefore, I am asking for three more weeks, with a clear deadline."
You want to add a second reason. Which sentence is correct?
A "In addition, the clearance takes ninety days anyway."
B "In addition of that, the clearance is slow."
C "In addition the clearance it takes ninety days anyway, so the closing date it does not move."
The CFO says the risk is small. How do you keep your credibility?
A "No, the risk is huge -- probably the biggest risk in the whole history of this company."
B "You are right, it is small, but it is serious, and the evidence is on one page."
C "Therefore, it is small."
