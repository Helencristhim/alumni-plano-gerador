Lesson 04 -- Pre-class

What Is Happening Right Now? -- Live Situations in HR Meetings

Giving a status update in a live meeting: what is moving, where it is, and what is blocked. Key words: update, ongoing, restructuring, merger, integration, headquarters, agenda, minutes, catch-up, workstream. Structures: present continuous (am/is/are + -ing), the negative and the question, and the contrast with the present simple.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the definition. You will hear all of them in the calls with Milan.

Update -- new information about how something is going
"I am sending you a short update every Wednesday."
Ongoing -- happening now and not finished yet
"The integration is ongoing and it is going well."
Restructuring -- changing how the teams and the jobs are organised
"We are restructuring the whole commercial area."
Merger -- two companies joining together and becoming one
"The merger is changing everything in my area."
Integration -- putting two teams or systems together so they work as one
"I am leading the people integration workstream."
Headquarters -- the main office of a company
"Our new headquarters is in Milan."
Agenda -- the list of points a meeting will cover
"Can you send me the agenda before the call?"
Minutes -- the written notes of what a meeting decided
"I am writing the minutes of the board meeting."
Catch-up -- a short informal meeting to share news
"We are having a quick catch-up on Friday."
Workstream -- one part of a big project, with its own team
"There are four workstreams and I am running one of them."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Viviane runs the engagement survey every year. That is her routine. But this quarter she is doing something different: she is leading the culture workstream of the integration with the Italian group. Right now her team is training the store managers, and they are at thirty-one of forty-one. They are not closing the new job titles yet, because they are waiting for the model from headquarters. Marco writes to her every Monday and asks the same question: "Are you still waiting for us?" She knows the answer, and she is putting it in the minutes of every meeting.

1. Why does the text say "she is leading" and not "she leads"?
A Because it is happening now, in this quarter, and it is not her permanent routine.
B Because "lead" is an irregular verb.
C Because the sentence is a question.
2. The text says "she knows the answer", not "she is knowing". Why?
A Because the sentence is in the past.
B Because "know" is a state verb and it does not take -ing.
C Because "know" is always used with the present simple in questions.
3. Why is there "Are you" at the start of "Are you still waiting for us?"
A Because the question inverts the verb to be and the subject.
B Because questions in English always start with a verb.
C Because it is more polite than "Do you wait?"

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Continuous -- Right Now, and Around Now

GRAMMAR

The tense of the live meeting: what is moving today, this week, this quarter.

FormUseExample
am / is / are + verb-ingHappening now or around now: today, this week, this quarter.I am leading the culture workstream.
the verb to be is obligatoryThe -ing alone is not a tense.I am working. (never I working)
negativeThe not goes after am / is / are.We are not closing the job titles.
questionInvert the verb to be and the subject. No do.Are you working on the merger?
state verbs stay simpleknow, need, want, understand, believe do not take -ing.I know the numbers. I need two more people.
simple or continuous?Always true → simple. Only true now → continuous.I run the survey every year. This month I am running it in Italy.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

Marco asks: "What are you working on right now?" You answer:
A "I am responsible for culture and engagement."
B "Right now I am leading the culture workstream of the integration."
C "I work in the culture workstream every day."
You want to ask a colleague about the merger. Which question is correct?
A "You are working on the merger?"
B "Do you working on the merger?"
C "Are you working on the merger?"
The new system is not ready and it is a problem this month. You say:
A "We are working with two systems at the same time."
B "We work with two systems at the same time, always."
C "We are knowing the problem of the systems."
The recruiter asks what you are working on. The strongest answer includes:
A only good news, to sound positive
B one number and one thing that is blocked
C the full description of your job title
Which sentence is correct?
A "I am needing two more people in my team."
B "I need two more people, and I am asking for them this month."
C "I needing two more people this month."
