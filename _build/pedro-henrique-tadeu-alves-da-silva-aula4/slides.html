Note for the group: if the Emotion words line is not empty, read the text again and ask whether the song needs them.
1. Two lines on this page separate two different kinds of time. What is the difference between Scene and Events?
Scene is what was already happening when the song started, and it runs in the background. Events are the finished things that arrive one after another and move the story forward.
2. The Emotion words line says None. Why would a reader write that down as useful information?
Because the absence is the technique. The text never names a feeling, so the reader supplies it, and a feeling you supplied yourself is stronger than one you were handed.
3. What does the page say about the narrator, and why does that matter?
Third person, and by the end the narrator knows less than the reader. That gap is what makes the last line work: the reader sees something the voice in the song does not say.
Chapter 4: The Code

The Background, and What Lands On It

Two past forms, two completely different jobs

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"The rain was falling."

"She was standing in the kitchen with her coat on."

"She put the keys on the table."

"While the radio was playing, he said the one sentence he could not take back."

The red verbs were already running. The blue ones happened and finished. In the last sentence both appear at once, and that is the whole machine.

JobFormExample
Already running, the backgroundwas / were + verb-ingThe rain was falling. She was standing in the kitchen.
Happened and finished, the eventthe past formShe put the keys down. He asked. She did not answer.
Both in one sentenceWhile + continuous, then the past formWhile the radio was playing, he said it.
Two backgrounds at oncecontinuous + and + continuousThe rain was falling and she was standing in the doorway.
Events in a rowpast form, past form, past formShe put the keys down, sat and said nothing.
Negative and questiondid not + base verb · was not + -ingShe did not answer. She was not listening. What was she doing?
The trapthe background needs the continuousNot the rain fell and she stood in the kitchen as an opening. Say was falling, was standing.

In one line: the -ing form is the room, and the past form is what happens in it.

Listening 1 · The First Verse Problem

Remove Every Word That Names a Feeling

A workshop mentor on the one mistake she sees in almost every first verse. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the problem she sees in almost every first verse, and why does she say she understands it?
They start with a feeling: I was so lost, I was broken, I did not know who I was. She understands because that is the true thing, and it is what the song is about.
2. Why does she say a feeling in line one does not work?
Because a feeling is the conclusion, and giving the conclusion in line one leaves the listener nothing to do.
3. What exercise does she suggest, and what was the best opening line she read last year?
Remove every word that names an emotion; what is left is usually a room, a time and an object, and those three things are the craft. The line was six words long: the car was still running outside. Nobody said afraid and nobody said leaving, and everybody knew both.
Listening 2 · Twist and Surprise

A Surprise Takes Something Away. A Twist Gives It Back.

A songwriter on the most misunderstood word in the craft, and the test he uses. Sound first — no text.

1. What difference does he draw between a surprise and a twist?
A surprise takes something away from you; a twist gives you something back. The difference is whether the line was available from the beginning.
2. He gives two examples: one that cheats the listener and one that does not. What are they?
Being told at the end that the whole thing was a dream, with nothing in the first two verses to suggest it, cheats you. Finding out in the last line that she never took off the coat that was mentioned in verse one does not, and you go back and listen again.
3. What is the test he uses, and what does it mean if you fail it?
Write the last line first, then go back into verse one and leave one small object there that makes the last line inevitable. If you cannot find the object, you do not have a twist. You have an ending.
