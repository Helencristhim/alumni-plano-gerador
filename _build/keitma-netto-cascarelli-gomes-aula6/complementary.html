Alan Smith — Why you should love statistics (TED)

Twelve minutes from the data editor of the Financial Times on what happens between a number and the person hearing it. He is talking about exactly the gap you worked on today: the figure is right and the picture in the listener's head is wrong.

Tip: he says several figures out loud. Write down how he says each one, and compare it with how you would have said the same number in a call.

Talk
Mona Chalabi — 3 ways to spot a bad statistic (TED)

Eleven minutes on what a careful listener does with a number: asks where it came from, who is missing from it, and whether it matches what is already known. Worth a second visit, this time listening only for how she says the figures.

Tip: every time a percentage appears, pause and say the same movement in the other two ways — from and to, and as a proportion. Three sentences, out loud, then continue.

Podcast
The Indicator from Planet Money — Trump versus Capital One

Nine minutes of financial journalism where the whole argument rests on comparisons: bigger than, twice as much as, up by. It is the fastest place to hear this lesson's grammar used by native speakers at full speed.

Tip: write down every comparison you hear, exactly as they say it. Then check: did they give both numbers first, or only the movement?
