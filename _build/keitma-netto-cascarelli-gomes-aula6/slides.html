Chapter 4: Bigger, Smaller, By How Much

One Movement, Three Sentences

All three are true. Only one is useful

Grammar Discovery

Same Movement, Different Size

"The rate went from four per cent to six per cent."

"The rate went up by two percentage points."

"The rate is fifty per cent higher than it was."

"The rate went up by two per cent."

Four sentences about one movement, from four per cent to six. Three of them are true. Which one is the red one, and why is it the one that gets people into trouble?

ShapeWhat it saysExample
from X to Ythe safest of all — it gives both numbers and lets the listener do the restIt went from four per cent to six per cent.
up / down by + amountthe size of the movement itselfIt went up by two percentage points.
X per cent higher thanthe movement as a proportion of where it startedIt is fifty per cent higher than it was.
twice · half · three timesmultiples, always followed by as much as or the size ofIt is twice as high as last year.
slightly · roughly · significantlythese go before the comparison, not after the numberIt is slightly above budget, and roughly in line with last year.
the trapa movement between two percentages is in percentage points, not per centFour to six is two percentage points, or fifty per cent — never two per cent.
the second trapcomparatives take than, never that and never ofNot higher that last year. Say higher than last year.

In one line: say both numbers first, from and to. Then say the movement, and say which kind it is. It costs one extra second and it cannot be misread.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Numbers, Spoken

Two voice notes and one page of a pack

Listening 1 · A Voice Note

Two Per Cent, or Fifty

A finance director on the difference that decides how a room reacts. Sound first — no text.

1. If a rate goes from four per cent to six, what are the two correct ways to describe the movement?
An increase of two percentage points, and an increase of fifty per cent.
2. What does he say he has watched a room do?
Approve something because somebody said two per cent, when the honest sentence was that the rate went up by half.
3. What is the rule he uses, and what does he say it costs?
Say the two numbers first, from and to; then say the movement and which kind it is. He says it takes one extra second.
Listening 2 · A Controller

Three Sentences Per Figure

A controller explaining how she presents numbers to people outside finance.

1. What does her first sentence always contain?
The number and what it is a proportion of — not just three point four, but three point four times the monthly fixed costs.
2. What two things does she always give together in the second sentence?
The direction and the size of the comparison — up from two point nine last year, so roughly twenty per cent higher.
3. What has she learned when she cannot say the third sentence?
That she does not understand the figure yet, and no slide is going to fix that for her.

1. How would you describe the movement in gross margin, in two different but correct ways?
It went up by two percentage points, from thirty per cent to thirty-two. That is also an increase of roughly seven per cent.
2. Why is the net movement smaller than the gross one?
Because something on the same line offsets part of it — the gross was plus three hundred thousand and the net is plus two hundred.
3. Why does the note say the benchmark is not directly comparable?
Because the regional benchmark excludes freight, and the margin above it does not.
