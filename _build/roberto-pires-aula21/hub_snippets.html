Stage 1.1: Store Vocabulary

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. These eight words help you buy clothes that fit you.

Fitting room -- the small room in a store where you try clothes on
"Where is the fitting room, please?"
Sleeve -- the part of a shirt or a jacket that covers your arm
"The sleeves are too long for me."
Tight -- very close to your body, with no extra space
"These jeans are too tight."
Loose -- not close to your body, with a lot of extra space
"I like a loose shirt in the summer."
Cotton -- a soft natural material used for shirts and T-shirts
"Is this shirt one hundred percent cotton?"
Hanger -- the object you hang a shirt or a jacket on
"Please put the shirt back on the hanger."
Plain -- with one color and no pattern at all
"I prefer a plain shirt, with no pattern."
To suit -- to look good on a person
"That blue jacket really suits you."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the situation and answer the questions.

The evening is cold, so Roberto walks into a store. "Do you have this jacket in a large?" he asks. The shop assistant takes it off the hanger and shows him the fitting room. Inside, Roberto looks in the mirror: the jacket is too big, the sleeves are too long, and it is very loose on the shoulders. He tries a medium. This one is not too tight, and the sleeves are long enough. It is one hundred percent cotton, dark blue and completely plain. "That color really suits you," the assistant says.

1. What is the problem with the large jacket?
A It is too tight on the arms.
B It is too big and the sleeves are too long.
C It is not cotton.
2. Why is the medium jacket right for him?
A Because it is loose on the shoulders.
B Because it has a pattern.
C Because it is not too tight and the sleeves are long enough.
3. Where does the word "enough" go in a sentence?
A Before the adjective: "enough long".
B After the adjective: "long enough".
C At the start of the sentence.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Too & Enough

Grammar

Use too + adjective when there is more of something than you want: "It's too big". Use adjective + enough when the amount is right: "It's big enough". Use not + adjective + enough when there is less than you need: "It isn't long enough". Remember the position: too goes before the adjective, enough goes after it. To ask the store for another option, use Do you have + noun? with a full sentence: "Do you have this in a smaller size?"

FormExampleMeaning
too + adjectiveIt's too big.more than you want — a problem
adjective + enoughIt's big enough.the right amount — no problem
not + adjective + enoughIt isn't long enough.less than you need — a problem
Do you have + noun?Do you have this in a large?ask the store for another option
