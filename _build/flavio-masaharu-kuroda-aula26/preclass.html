Lesson 26 -- Pre-class

The Reference Call -- Reporting What the Customer Said

A customer talks to your buyer and you are not on the call. How to read what was said and report it to your team, with nothing added and nothing lost. Key words: a reference check, candid, a hiccup, hands-on, responsive, a drawback, to deliver on, to vouch for, a testimonial, a lesson learned. Structure: reported speech (said that, told me that, asked if, tense backshift).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A reference check -- a call where a buyer asks a current customer about a supplier
"Paola asked for a reference check before the decision."
Candid -- honest and direct, even when the truth is not pleasant
"Davide was candid about the connector."
A hiccup -- a small problem that causes a short delay but is not serious
"There was one hiccup in the first month."
Hands-on -- involved in doing the work yourself, not only managing it
"Our project manager is very hands-on."
Responsive -- quick to answer and to help when someone asks
"The support team was responsive."
A drawback -- a disadvantage, the negative side of something
"The main drawback is the reports."
To deliver on -- to do what you promised to do
"We delivered on our promise about approval time."
To vouch for -- to say from your own experience that someone is good and can be trusted
"Davide can vouch for our team."
A testimonial -- a short positive statement from a customer, often used in marketing
"Buyers trust a phone call more than a testimonial."
A lesson learned -- something useful you understood after a mistake or a problem
"Our lesson learned was to start the connector early."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

After a reference call, the salesperson was not on the line, so he has to report what other people said. Davide's words were: "The team is very hands-on." Flávio tells Giulia: Davide said that the team was very hands-on. The verb goes one step back in time: is becomes was, will becomes would, can becomes could.

When you say who heard the words, use told + the person: he told Paola that he would choose Invent again. With said, there is no person after the verb.

Questions change too. Paola asked: "Is there a drawback?" Flávio reports: she asked if there was a drawback. After asked if, or asked what, the word order is the order of a normal sentence, not of a question: she asked what the main drawback was.

A common mistake is he said me. In English, it is he told me, or simply he said.

1. Which sentence is correct?
A "Davide said me that the team was responsive and that the support in Italian was very fast."
B "Davide told me that the team was responsive."
C "Davide told that the team is responsive."
2. Paola asked: "Can I visit a warehouse?" How does Flávio report it?
A She asked if she could visit a warehouse.
B She asked if can she visit one of the warehouses in Turin, maybe next month.
C She asked can I visit.
3. Why does the verb change from is to was in the report?
A Because the information is not true anymore and the team changed completely after the call.
B Because it is a rule only for formal emails.
C Because the words were said in the past, before the report.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

Move the verb one step back. Use told + person. No question order after asked.

He saidYou reportExample
"The reports are not flexible."are → wereHe said that the reports were not flexible.
"I will choose them again."will → wouldHe told Paola that he would choose us again.
"I can vouch for them."can → couldHe said that he could vouch for us.
"Is there a drawback?"asked if + subject + verbShe asked if there was a drawback.
"What is the main drawback?"asked + what + subject + verbShe asked what the main drawback was.

Never: he said me · he told that · she asked if were the reports · she asked what is
