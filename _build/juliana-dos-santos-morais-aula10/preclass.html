Lesson 10 -- Pre-class

The Follow-up -- Reporting the Meeting

The meeting is over, and one short email tells everybody what people said and asked. Key words: a recap, to point out, to mention, a concern, to suggest, feedback, to attach, regarding, to look forward to, to highlight. Structure: reported speech (said that, told me that, asked me to).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A recap -- a short summary of the main points of a meeting
"Here is a quick recap of our call."
To point out -- to tell someone about a fact or a problem that is important
"Daniel pointed out that the hotel was full in November."
To mention -- to say something briefly, without many details
"Emma mentioned that she was traveling next week."
A concern -- a worry about something that could go wrong
"The partner had one concern about the date."
To suggest -- to give an idea for other people to consider
"She suggested a shorter program."
Feedback -- comments about how good or bad something is, to help improve it
"Thank you for your feedback on the draft."
To attach -- to add a file to an email
"I have attached the list of companies."
Regarding -- about; on the subject of
"Regarding the budget, the director will answer on Friday."
To look forward to -- to feel happy about something that will happen
"I look forward to our next meeting."
To highlight -- to show that something is important, so that people notice it
"I want to highlight one point: the deadline is Friday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

One hour after the call, Juliana writes the follow-up. She starts with a short recap. Then she reports the main points: Daniel said that the list was almost ready, and Emma told the group that she was traveling the next week.

The partner asked the chamber to send the logo by Monday. The hotel pointed out that November was a busy month, so Juliana writes it as a concern.

Notice the pattern. When Juliana reports what people said, the verb moves one step back: is becomes was. A request becomes asked + person + to + verb.

1. What did Daniel say?
A That the list was almost ready.
B That he was traveling next week.
C That November was a busy month.
2. What did the partner ask the chamber to do?
A To send the logo by Monday.
B To change the date of the event.
C To find a new hotel.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “Daniel said me that the list was ready.”
B “Daniel told me that the list was ready.”
C “Daniel told that me the list was ready.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

Use said that or told + person + that, and move the verb one step back. For a request, use asked + person + to + verb.

JobFormExample
Reporting a statementsaid (that) + sentenceDaniel said that the list was ready.
With the person who heard ittold + person + (that)Emma told me that she was traveling.
The verb moves backis → was · am doing → was doing · will → wouldHe said he would send it.
Reporting a requestasked + person + to + verbThe partner asked us to send the logo.
Reporting a questionasked + if / what + normal orderShe asked if the date was confirmed.
The trapnever said + personNot He said me. Say He told me or He said that.

Never: He said me · She told that she was traveling · The partner asked us send the logo · He said that he will send it yesterday
