Lesson 7 -- Describing What You See

Talk
Tracy Chevalier -- "Finding the story inside the painting" (TED, 13 min)

A novelist stands in front of three paintings and describes them out loud, one detail at a time, before she allows herself a single interpretation. It is the exact sequence you practiced today, performed by somebody who does it for a living.

Tip: count how many of her sentences hang detail on a noun with an -ing clause. Then describe one work of your own the same way, out loud.

Podcast
The Lonely Palette -- Episode 13: Edward Hopper, "Nighthawks"

Every episode of this show does one impossible thing: it puts a painting in your head using nothing but words. This one takes an image almost everybody thinks they know and describes it until you see it again.

Tip: stop the episode at the end of the description, before the history begins, and write down the order she used. It will be scale, then light, then figures.

Video
The National Gallery -- "A curated look at: A day in the countryside"

A curator walks through paintings by Rubens, Constable and Corot, describing each one in real time in front of the work. British English, unhurried, and full of the structure you learned today: detail hung on a noun, never a new sentence.

Tip: pause after each painting and describe the next one yourself before she does. Then compare her first sentence with yours.
