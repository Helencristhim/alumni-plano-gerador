Chapter 4: The Word After If

Two Halves, Two Tenses

And one of them is not the one you would expect

Grammar Discovery

What Comes Right After If?

"If the storm moves east, we will reroute everything."

"If the crew times out, they will call the flight off."

"Unless we decide now, we will lose the window."

"If that happens, we might need a backup plan."

Every one of these is about the future. Look at the red verbs and say what tense they are in. Then look at the blue ones.

HalfFormExample
the conditionif + present simpleIf the storm moves east...
the resultwill + bare verb...we will reroute everything.
softer resultmight, may, can, should...we might need a backup plan.
order does not mattercomma only when if comes firstWe will reroute if the storm moves east.
unless= if not, so never negative after itUnless we decide now, we will lose it.
same rule afterwhen, as soon as, until, beforeAs soon as it lands, I will call you.
the trapNever if it will rain. Portuguese has a future subjunctive; English does not, and fills the gap with the present.

In one line: the future lives in only one half of the sentence, and it is never the half with if in it.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Two Recordings, One Decision Sheet

The same structure, spoken and written

Listening 1 · A Duty Controller

The Decision You Make at Six Forty

A controller on the one call that shapes the rest of the day. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say is the hardest decision of the day, and when does it happen?
Whether to hold the first departure, and it happens at about six forty in the morning.
2. What does he say happens if he waits for certainty?
By the time he is certain, the window has closed and the decision has been made for him.
3. What does he say about announcing a delay?
It does not fix anything. It buys time, and buying time is often the only move available.
Listening 2 · Science Radio

Why Every Plan Is Too Optimistic

A short piece on the mistake everybody makes when they estimate. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the students predict about their own work, and what actually happened?
They predicted they would finish in about thirty-four days. On average it took fifty-six, and fewer than half finished by the day they had been sure about.
2. What does the piece say is strange about how people predict for other people?
They are much more accurate. The optimism only appears when the plan is their own.
3. What is the fix the piece describes?
Stop asking how long it will take, and ask how long the last five things like it actually took.
Real Document

The Decision Sheet, Line by Line

OPERATIONS · MORNING SHIFT
duty manager: Jessica Piffer · sheet 06:40
If the ceiling stays below four hundred feet → we will hold the 06:50 · decide by 06:45
If the 06:50 is held → we will reroute the Miami connection · decide by 07:10
Unless the crew signs on by 07:30 → we will call the 08:05 off · decide by 07:30
If we call the 08:05 off → there will be a knock-on effect in three cities · notify by 07:40
If nothing changes by 08:00 → the standby crew will go home · decide by 08:00
Every line on this sheet has a time in the last column. A condition with no deadline attached is not a decision. It is a worry.
1. What has to happen for the 08:05 to be called off, and by what time?
The crew has to fail to sign on by 07:30. That is also the deadline for the decision.
2. Which line depends on another line above it?
The Miami reroute: it only happens if the 06:50 is held, which is the line above.
3. According to the note, what turns a condition into a decision?
A deadline. Without a time attached, it is a worry rather than a decision.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I never put will after if, unless, when, until or as soon as.
I can soften a consequence with might, may or should instead of promising will.
I know that unless already means if not, so I never add a second negative to it.
I attach a deadline to every condition, so it is a decision and not a worry.
I know the words: a backup plan, to go ahead, a knock-on effect, to call something off, a window, to reroute, worst case, to buy time, to be on standby, tight.
