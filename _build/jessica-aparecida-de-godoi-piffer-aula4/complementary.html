Talk
Dan Gilbert — “Why we make bad decisions” (TED)

Thirty-three minutes on the arithmetic people actually use when they weigh a risk against a reward, and why it is reliably wrong. It is the argument behind the second recording of this lesson, made at length and with better examples.

Tip: he builds several if-then sentences out loud. Write down four of them, then rewrite each one about a decision of your own.

Radio
99% Invisible — “Gander International Airport”

A town of ten thousand people that had to absorb thirty-eight rerouted aircraft with a few hours of notice. It is the vocabulary of this lesson in its natural habitat: a window, a backup plan, a knock-on effect, and a very long list of decisions made without enough information.

Tip: listen once for the story, then write five first conditional sentences describing what the town had to decide, each one with a deadline attached.

Video
Daniel Kahneman — “The riddle of experience vs. memory” (TED)

Twenty minutes from the researcher whose work sits underneath every study quoted in this lesson. He is slow, precise and completely unhurried, which makes him one of the easiest native speakers to follow at full speed.

Tip: this one is worth pausing. Every time he says “if”, stop and predict how the sentence will end before he says it.
