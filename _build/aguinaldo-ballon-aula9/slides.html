Chapter 3: The Code

Soft Form, Firm Argument

Would, could and might on a stage

Grammar Discovery

Why Do These Sentences Sound Polite?

"I would say that the rules are the real problem."

"Could I add something here?"

"That might be true, but the data shows the opposite."

"Would you agree that stability matters more than price?"

Say each sentence without the blue word: The rules are the real problem. I add something. That is true. You agree. The argument is the same. What changes is how it sounds to the other speaker and to the audience.

JobFormExample
Give a strong opinion politelyI would say (that) ...I would say the rules are the real problem.
Ask to speak or interruptCould I + verb ...?Could I add something here?
Concede, then disagreeThat might be true, but ...That might be true, but the data shows the opposite.
Invite agreementWould you agree that ...?Would you agree that stability matters?
Suggest another viewIt could / might be argued that ...It could be argued that investors care about rules.
The trapthe modal is followed by the base form, never toNot I would to say. Say I would say.

In one line: would, could and might + base form soften the form, not the argument.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Debate, at Real Speed

One conversation, three recordings, one page

Dialogue

Two Speakers, One Stage

D
Honestly, I think investors do not care much about regulation. If the return is high enough, they will come.
A
Could I add something here? That might be true for short-term funds, but not for infrastructure.
D
Why not? Money is money.
A
I would say a concession is different. It lasts thirty years, so the investor needs to trust the rules for thirty years.
D
Okay, I concede that the time is longer. But higher returns can compensate for that risk.
A
They can, but users pay for those returns with higher tariffs. Would you agree that stable rules are cheaper for everybody?
D
That is a good counterargument. Could you elaborate on the tariffs?
A
Of course. Let me give one example, and then I will wrap up.
Comprehension

About Diana

1. What does Diana think investors do not care much about?
Regulation.
2. What does Diana concede?
That the time of an infrastructure investment is longer.
3. What does Diana ask him to elaborate on?
The tariffs.
Listening 1 · The Moderator

What Makes a Good Debate

An experienced moderator explains what she likes to see on a panel. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say the audience remembers?
The moment when two speakers disagree.
2. What do the best speakers do before they disagree?
They concede one point.
3. What does she say about speakers who interrupt all the time?
The audience stops listening to them.
Listening 2 · The Speaker

The First Time I Disagreed on Stage

A Latin American lawyer remembers his first public debate in English. Sound first — no text.

1. Why was he nervous before his first debate in English?
Because his English was not perfect and the other speaker was a native speaker.
2. What phrase did he use to interrupt?
"Could I add something here?"
3. What did he learn from that day?
That the audience cared more about his example than about his accent.
