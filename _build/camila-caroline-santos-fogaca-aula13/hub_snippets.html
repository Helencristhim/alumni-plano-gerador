Stage 2: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

The words for what you have just done: classify a request and say what it does to the budget. Listen to each expression and read it in context.

incremental headcount -- new positions that increase the number of people in the plan
"Two new analysts in collections are incremental headcount."
attrition -- the people who leave a company over a period, as a number or a rate
"Attrition is high in collections this year."
resignation -- the decision of an employee to leave the job, said or written to the company
"Her resignation arrived on Monday."
notice period -- the time an employee works between saying they are leaving and the last day
"The notice period in this company is thirty days."
handover -- the moment when one person passes the work to another
"We plan a two-week handover before her last day."
within budget -- inside the money that the plan already approved
"A replacement at the same level stays within budget."
replace like for like -- to replace a person with somebody in the same role and at the same level
"We want to replace her like for like."

Stage 4: Grammar Tip -- Verb or noun?

GRAMMAR

Many HR words come in pairs: a verb for the action and a noun for the thing. The position in the sentence tells you which one to use.

PairWhere it goesExample
resign / resignationafter the subject: the verb. After a, the, her, eight: the noun."She resigned." · "Her resignation arrived."
replace / replacementafter to or a modal: the verb. After a or this: the noun."We want to replace her." · "This is a replacement."
hand over / handoverthe verb is two words; the noun is one word."She hands over the cases." · "The handover takes two weeks."
attritiona noun with no plural: it takes is, not are."Attrition is high in collections."
Watch outa noun cannot be the action of the sentence: "eight people resigned", not "eight people did resignation". And a verb cannot follow a or the: "it is a replacement", not "it is a replace".

Look at the word before the gap. A subject, to or can asks for the verb; a, the, her or a number asks for the noun.

Stage 8: Explain a Request in Five Moves

Order

Put the five moves in the order a director needs to hear them, from the type of request to what happens next.

?Say what it does to the budget: within budget, or a cost impact.
?Say the type: a replacement or a new position.
?Say the next step and the date.
?Give the reason: a resignation, a step down, or new work.
?Say how the work continues: the notice period and the handover.
