Chapter 3: The Director Who Asks Why

Twelve More Positions, Two Different Stories

A conversation between the HRBP and the global director, line by line

Dialogue

Eight and Four, Not Twelve

M
Camila, I'm looking at the plan for South America. The headcount requests went up by twelve positions. Can you explain that?
C
Of course. Eight of the twelve are replacements. People resigned, and we want to replace like for like: same role, same level.
M
So those eight don't change the budget?
C
No. They are budgeted positions, so they are within budget. The cost impact is zero.
M
And the other four?
C
The other four are incremental headcount. They are new positions, so they are a net increase.
M
Eight resignations in one year is a lot. Why is the attrition so high?
C
Most of the attrition is in collections. The market pays more for analysts, and five resignations came in the same quarter.
M
How much time do we have before they leave?
C
The notice period is thirty days, so we plan a handover for each one. The new person takes over the cases before the last day.
Comprehension

About Mark

1. What does Mark see in the plan for South America?

He thinks it is a lot, and he asks why the attrition is so high.
3. What is Mark's last question about?

Chapter 4: The Code

The Verb, or the Noun?

Resign or resignation, replace or replacement: the place in the sentence decides

Grammar Discovery

What Comes Before the Word?

"Five people resigned in the same quarter."

"Five resignations came in the same quarter."

"We want to replace her like for like."

"This is a replacement, not a new position."

"Attrition is high in collections."

Blue and orange say the same idea. Look at the word before each one: what tells you to use the verb, and what tells you to use the noun?

FormWhere it goesExample
verbafter the subject, after to, after can or willFive people resigned. We want to replace her.
nounafter a, the, her, this or a numberFive resignations. This is a replacement.
two words / one wordthe verb hand over is two words; the noun handover is oneShe hands over the cases. The handover takes two weeks.
no pluralattrition takes is, never are, and no aAttrition is high.
Watch outa noun is not an action: people resigned, never people did resignation. And a verb does not come after a: a replacement, never a replace.

Sound Check

Find the Strong Syllable

Word stress · point 3 of 14

Why this one is hard for a Brazilian speaker

In Portuguese: The words that end in -tion have a twin that puts the strong syllable at the very end.

In English: The strong syllable comes just before -tion: at-TRI-tion, res-ig-NA-tion. The end is weak and short.

If it slips: With the force on the last syllable, the listener does not recognize the word at first, and in a budget review that is the word that carries the number.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

The Rule of the Budget, and a Real Case

The regional manager on the budget review, and a call from Chile

Listening 1

The Budget Review: Same or Different?

Claire, the regional manager, talks to the HR team before the budget review. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. Why is a replacement within budget, according to Claire?
Because the position is already in the plan: it is a budgeted position.
2. When does a replacement have a cost impact?
When the manager wants a more senior person, so it is not like for like.
3. What does Claire expect in collections, and why?
More replacements, not more new positions, because attrition is high there.

Rafael, from the rental company in Chile, leaves a message. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00

2. Why does Rafael want the new person to start two weeks before?
For a proper handover, before the notice period ends.
3. What happens in June?
His operations supervisor steps down, an analyst takes over, and the analyst needs a replacement.
