Warm-up — From Lesson 26

Review

In Lesson 26 you compared two business cultures: the etiquette, the hierarchy, and why Americans tend to be more straightforward and punctual. Now Daniela is in the Chicago office. On her first morning, her laptop cannot open the test system, and she learns the rules of the office. Some things are rules, some things are optional, some are only advice, and some are risks. Today you will learn the words of rules and security, and the small verbs that show the difference: must, have to, don’t have to, mustn’t, should and might.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Policy -- a set of official rules that a company writes for its people
"Our security policy says that visitors need a badge."
Mandatory -- required by a rule: you must do it
"The security training is mandatory for all new employees."
Optional -- not required: you can choose to do it or not
"The Friday lunch with the team is optional."
Compliance -- following the rules, the policies and the law
"The auditors check our compliance every month."
Credentials -- your username and password: the information you use to log in
"Never share your credentials with a colleague."
Guideline -- advice about how to do something, not a strict rule
"Our coding guideline says to add a comment to every change."
Requirement -- something that is necessary: you need to have it or to do it
"A strong password is a requirement for the new system."
Risk -- the possibility that something bad will happen
"Changing the system on the day of the go-live is a big risk."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

On Monday night, Daniela read the visitor guide from the Chicago office. The first rule was simple: visitors must wear a badge at all times. To use the test system, everyone has to connect to the VPN and confirm the login on the phone. Visitors mustn’t share their credentials with anyone, not even with a colleague. Some things are optional: visitors don’t have to use a company laptop. Daniela can use her own laptop, but she should install the latest updates first. The guide also had a tip: you should save your work on the shared drive, because files on the desktop are not safe, and the Wi-Fi in the meeting rooms might be slow in the afternoon. Daniela smiled. At SPF, the rules are similar, but not as strict.

1. According to the guide, what is mandatory for all visitors?
A A badge, at all times.
B A suit and tie on the first day, because the directors are very formal in the Chicago office.
C A company laptop.
2. What happens if Daniela uses her own laptop?
A It is not allowed: she mustn’t bring a personal laptop to the office, not even to a workshop.
B She has to buy a new one.
C It is fine, but she should install the latest updates first.
3. Why should visitors save their work on the shared drive?
A Because the auditors read every file on the desktop at the end of the day.
B Because the Wi-Fi might be slow, and files on the desktop are not safe.
C Because it is mandatory.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip — Rules, Advice and Possibility

GRAMMAR

Modal verbs show HOW strong a sentence is: a rule, no rule, a prohibition, advice or a possibility. After a modal, use the base verb.

UseFormExample
A rulemust / have to + verbYou must wear your badge. Everyone has to use the VPN.
Not necessarydon’t / doesn’t have to + verbYou don’t have to use a company laptop.
Not allowedmustn’t + verbYou mustn’t share your credentials.
Adviceshould / shouldn’t + verbYou should call IT security first.
Possibilitymight + verbThe system might be slow this afternoon.
Remember"must wear", never "must to wear". "should call", never "should to call". Mustn’t = it is not allowed. Don’t have to = it is not necessary, you can choose. And with she / he: "she doesn’t have to".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

A new colleague asks: "Can I use your login today? Mine is not ready." What do you say?
A "Sure, here it is. Please change nothing important and give it back to me after lunch, okay?"
B "You don’t have to log in."
C "Sorry, I can’t. We mustn’t share credentials. You should call IT security."
The Friday meeting is optional. Which sentence is correct?
A "You mustn’t come to the meeting on Friday, but you can watch the recording later if you want."
B "You don’t have to come to the meeting on Friday."
C "You don’t must come on Friday."
You are not sure the update will finish on time. How do you tell the stores?
A "The update might take longer than planned."
B "The update must to take longer than we planned, so all the stores have to close early tonight."
C "The update takes longer."
A colleague wants to change the system on the day of the go-live. What is the best advice?
A "Good idea! Let’s do it on Saturday afternoon, when everyone is at home and the stores are busy."
B "You should to wait."
C "You shouldn’t change it on Saturday. It is a big risk."
