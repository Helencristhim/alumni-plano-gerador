Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"We advised the group since March."
"We have advised the group since March."
"If the ruling will be refused, the client restructures."
"If the ruling is refused, the client will restructure."
"The group could held liable for the difference."
"The group could be held liable for the difference."

Each of these was correct on its own in an earlier lesson. What breaks them is speed: three structures competing inside one sentence.

Listening

A Voicemail from the General Counsel

The client sets the rules for Thursday's call. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she NOT want to hear on the call?
The history of the file — she says she already knows it.
2. What does she want when Leonardo reaches the two scenarios?
Numbers, not adjectives: what each scenario does to the cash position and what the restructuring costs.
3. What will her chief financial officer do during the call?
Interrupt, and ask Leonardo to elaborate on anything vague — so he has to clarify without restarting.
Listening 2

What Changes at the Halfway Point

A senior partner on hearing your own mistakes. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. How does he describe the beginning of learning a language?
Collecting things and filling a box: words, structures, a way to build a conditional.
2. What is the uncomfortable thing that starts happening at the halfway point?
You hear your own mistakes while you are still speaking.
3. What does he say is the right reaction, and the wrong one?
Wrong: slowing down and getting quieter. Right: correct yourself in three words and keep going.
