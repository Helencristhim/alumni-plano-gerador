Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

The first three are new. The rest come back from Lessons 1 to 7 -- say your own definition before you read the card.

Articulate -- to express an idea clearly and in an organized way
"Let me articulate the position in three lines before we go into the detail."
Elaborate -- to add more detail to something you have just said
"Would you like me to elaborate on that scenario?"
Clarify -- to explain something again so that it becomes easier to understand
"Let me clarify one number before we move on."
Scenario -- one possible version of the future that you describe and compare
"Two scenarios are on the table, and the client has to choose one."
Regulatory framework -- the whole set of official rules that govern a sector
"The regulatory framework has been revised twice since we opened the file."
Tax exposure -- the amount of tax risk a company is open to
"The tax exposure has grown, but nobody has recalculated it."
Cash flow impact -- the effect a decision has on the money moving in and out of a company
"The cash flow impact is absorbed inside the current budget."
Flag a risk -- to point out a danger early so that other people can see it
"We flagged the risk in March, before the acquisition was signed."
Advance ruling -- an official answer from the authority, obtained before you act
"An advance ruling was requested in April and has not been granted yet."
Be held liable -- to be made legally responsible for paying something
"Until the ruling is granted, the group could be held liable for the difference."
Subsidiary -- a company owned or controlled by a larger company
"The client acquired a subsidiary in Mexico last November."
Jurisdiction -- the country or authority whose rules apply to a case
"The Mexican entity falls under a different jurisdiction."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Leonardo opens the call with one sentence. "Before the detail, here is where we stand today." He is describing a file that ALMA has advised on since March, and in that time the tax exposure has grown. Last November the client acquired a subsidiary in Mexico, and the structure that his team reviewed in March no longer describes the group. Fifteen percent is withheld at source on payments that leave Brazil, and an advance ruling was requested in April. It has not been granted yet, so the group could be held liable for the difference, and the officer who signs the return may be asked to explain it personally. Then Leonardo gives the two scenarios. If the ruling is granted this quarter, the rate falls to ten percent and the cash flow impact stays inside the budget. If it is refused, the client will have to restructure before December. The general counsel interrupts and asks him to elaborate, and he clarifies one number and picks the thread back up. If she asked him whether the position is safe, he would say no -- and he says so, in one sentence, before he closes.

1. Why does the text say "has advised since March" instead of "advised in March"?
A The advisory work started in the past and is still running today.
B The advisory work finished in March.
C The advisory work has not started yet.
2. What does "the officer who signs the return may be asked to explain it" tell the client?
A That the officer has already been questioned.
B That it is possible, not certain, and it identifies exactly which person.
C That every officer in the group will be questioned.
3. What is the difference between the two "if" sentences and the last one, "if she asked him"?
A There is no difference: all three describe real plans.
B The first two are real possibilities this quarter; the last one is hypothetical and softer.
C The last one describes something that already happened.
4. When the general counsel interrupts, what does Leonardo do?
A He starts the whole explanation again from the beginning.
B He waits for her to finish and says nothing.
C He clarifies one number and picks the thread back up where he left it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Whole Toolkit in One Call

GRAMMAR

Nothing here is new. The only new skill is choosing which structure, in real time, while somebody is waiting for the answer.

The job in the callThe formExample
Open with the relationship, and what has changedpresent perfect
have / has + past participle		"We have advised the group since March, and the exposure has grown."
Anchor one finished event on a datepast simple"The client acquired a subsidiary in Mexico last November."
State the rule without naming who wrote itpassive voice
be + past participle		"Fifteen percent is withheld at source." / "The ruling has not been granted yet."
Project a real future, this quarterfirst conditional
if + present, will / falls		"If the ruling is granted, the rate falls to ten percent."
Soften an opinion, or step into a hypothesissecond conditional
if + past, would		"If you asked me today, I would say the position is not settled."
Say how sure you aremodal verbs
could / may / should		"The group could be held liable." / "We should not describe this as settled."
Say which entity, which rule, which personrelative clauses
who / which / that		"The officer who signs the return." / "Payments that leave Brazil."
Manage the room while you speak"Let me articulate the position." / "Would you like me to elaborate?" / "Let me clarify one number." / "Let me rephrase that."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each real conversation.

The client opens the call with "Where do we stand?" Your first line is:
A "Let me start from the beginning, in March, when the group had one entity."
B "Let me articulate the position in three lines before we go into the detail."
C "It is complicated, so it will take some time to explain."
You said "fifty percent" but you meant "fifteen". You are mid-sentence. You say:
A "Let me rephrase that. The rate is fifteen percent, not fifty."
B Nothing, and hope nobody noticed.
C "Sorry, sorry. Can I start this part again from the beginning?"
The chief financial officer cuts in: "Can you elaborate on that?" The best move is:
A Repeat the same sentence more slowly.
B Add the detail he is missing, then say "coming back to the second scenario" and pick the thread up.
C Ask him to hold all questions until the end.
The client asks whether the position is safe, and you do not think it is. You say:
A "The position is completely fine, do not worry about it."
B "I cannot answer that question."
C "We should not describe it as settled. If you asked me today, I would say no."
