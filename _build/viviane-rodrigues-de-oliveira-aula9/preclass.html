Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

What Was Happening When It All Changed? -- The Acquisition

Narrating the week the Italian group bought the company: what was happening, what changed, and what you did about it. Key words: acquisition, announcement, rumor, uncertainty, transition, redundancy, morale, town hall, due diligence, change management. Structures: past continuous (was/were + -ing), the contrast with the past simple, when and while, and state verbs.

Vocabulary

Acquisition -- when one company buys another one
"The acquisition was announced on a Tuesday morning."
Announcement -- the moment the company tells everybody something official
"After the announcement, nobody worked that afternoon."
Rumor -- a story people tell before the official news arrives
"The rumor was already in the stores two weeks before."
Uncertainty -- not knowing what is going to happen
"Uncertainty is worse for people than bad news."
Transition -- the period between the old situation and the new one
"We are still in the transition, one year later."
Redundancy -- when a job is not needed any more after a change
"There were fewer redundancies than people expected."
Morale -- how a group of people feels about work
"Morale was very low in the first month."
Town hall -- a big meeting where leaders talk to the whole company
"We ran a town hall every Friday for two months."
Due diligence -- the checking process before a company is bought
"During the due diligence I was sending documents every day."
Change management -- how you help people through a big change at work
"Change management is the part nobody plans for."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

It was a Tuesday. At eight in the morning Viviane was sitting in a meeting about the training calendar. At eight thirty her phone started ringing: three store managers were calling to ask about a rumor. At nine the CEO sent the email and the announcement became official. She was standing in the corridor when she read it. By ten o clock nobody was working: people were standing in small groups and everybody was asking the same question. Morale was the lowest of the year. While the leadership team was preparing the first town hall, Viviane collected forty-one questions from the stores. She knew she could not answer most of them, so she said exactly that.

1. Why does the text say "she was standing in the corridor when she read it"?
A Because standing was the long action and reading the email was the short one that happened inside it.
B Because both actions happened at the same speed.
C Because "stand" is an irregular verb.
2. The text says "she knew she could not answer", not "she was knowing". Why?
A Because "know" is a state verb and never takes -ing.
B Because the sentence is a question.
C Because "know" is only used in the present.
3. Why is "while" used with "the leadership team was preparing"?
A Because while introduces the long action, the one in the middle of happening.
B Because while is more formal than when.
C Because while is always used with the past simple.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Continuous -- The Long Action in the Past

GRAMMAR

One action was already happening. Another one cut it.

FormUseExample
was / were + verb-ingAn action in the middle of happening in the past.I was reading the results.
past continuous + when + past simpleThe long action, interrupted by the short one.I was reading when the CEO called.
while + past continuousWhile always takes the LONG action.While we were preparing the town hall, the news leaked.
backgroundTo describe the scene around an event.Everybody was talking. Nobody was working.
negative and questionThe not goes after was/were; the question inverts them.They were not working. Were you working?
state verbs stay simpleknow, want, need, understand: never with -ing.I knew it was serious.
