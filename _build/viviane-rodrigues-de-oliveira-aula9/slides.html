Chapter 3: The Code

One Was Long, One Cut It

Two pasts in the same sentence, doing different jobs

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I was reading the survey results when the CEO called."

"While we were preparing the town hall, the news leaked."

"At nine in the morning everybody was talking about it."

"People were not working that afternoon. They were waiting."

In each sentence one action was long, in the middle of happening, and one was short and sudden. Which verb form does each one take?

FormUseExample
was / were + verb-ingAn action in the middle of happening in the past.I was reading the results.
past continuous + when + past simpleThe long action, cut by the short one.I was reading when the CEO called.
while + past continuousWhile introduces the LONG action.While we were preparing the town hall, the news leaked.
background of a sceneTo paint what was going on around the event.Everybody was talking. Nobody was working.
state verbs stay simpleknow, want, need, understand: no -ing, in any tense.I knew it was serious. (never I was knowing)

The Structure

A Change in Three Sentences

"Tell me about a big change you went through" -- this is the shape of the answer.

1 · What was happening

"We were running the leadership programme and morale was the best it had ever been."

2 · What changed

"Then the group bought the company, and in one morning everything stopped."

3 · What you did

"I ran a town hall every Friday for two months. People needed information more than they needed answers."

Background → the event → what YOU did. Without the third sentence you are telling a story that happened to you, not one you led.

Chapter 4: The Town Hall

One Year Later

Marco · Global People Director · the conversation nobody had yet

Dialogue

What Really Happened That Week?

M
Viviane, I have a question I never asked you. What was happening here the week we announced the deal?
V
Honestly? The rumor was already in the stores. People were talking about it two weeks before the announcement.
M
Two weeks? We thought it was confidential.
V
It never is. While we were preparing the town hall, three store managers called me to ask if they were losing their jobs.
M
And what were you telling them at that point?
V
Sorry, could you say that again, please?
M
At that point, what were you telling people?
V
The truth: that I did not know yet. And then I ran a town hall every Friday for two months. Morale was low, but nobody was inventing stories any more.
M
That is why we kept your team. Most companies lose half of it in a transition.
Comprehension

About Marco

1. What does Marco ask her at the beginning?
What was happening in her company the week they announced the deal.
2. What surprises him about the rumor?
That it was already in the stores two weeks before — they thought it was confidential.
3. What does he say at the end about her team?
That it is why they kept it — most companies lose half a team in a transition.

Listening 2

The Change Question in Interviews

A recruiter on what he listens for in a change story. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What question does he ask in almost every interview?
Tell me about a difficult change you went through.
2. What is wrong with the answers in the first group?
Everything happens to the person — they are never doing anything.
3. What do the answers in the second group have?
A moment where the person says so I did this: a meeting, a call, a document.
4. Is he looking for a hero?
No — half of those decisions did not even work. He wants somebody who was acting and not only waiting.

Your Week

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "I reading the email when the CEO called me."
"I was reading the email when the CEO called me."
2. "I was knowing it was serious, but I had no information."
"I knew it was serious." (know is a state verb)
3. "While the CEO sent the email, I was in the corridor."
"When the CEO sent the email, I was in the corridor." (while takes the long action)
4. "At ten o clock nobody worked, everybody talked in the corridor."
"At ten o clock nobody was working, everybody was talking in the corridor."
The Real Thing

The Day, Hour by Hour

Internal timeline
Acquisition Day -- People Team Log
Tuesday
Prepared for the transition review

What the People team logged that day

  • 08:00 — People team meeting about the training calendar. Nobody knew yet.
  • 08:30 — First calls from three store managers. The rumor was already out.
  • 09:00 — CEO email to the whole company. Announcement official.
  • 10:00 — Nobody working. Groups in every corridor. Morale: lowest of the year.
  • 14:00 — 41 questions collected from the stores. 39 of them about jobs.
  • Friday — First town hall. Answer to 12 of the 41 questions. The other 29: we do not know yet.

Town halls continued every Friday for eight weeks.

1. Which line shows the moment that cut everything else in half?
Nine o clock, the CEO email. Everything before it was a normal Tuesday; everything after it was the transition.
2. Why does the log say "we do not know yet" for 29 questions?
Because saying that is still information. The listening says it: people can accept bad news -- what they cannot accept is silence.
