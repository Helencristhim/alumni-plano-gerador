Lesson 9

Forty-Five Seconds of Opinion

Four questions, no preparation, and the concession that makes an opinion sound mature

Warm-up

Studying the Night Before Class

At GV you study every topic the night before the class. Do you think it works? Give one good thing and one bad thing about it.

The Target

Forty-Five Seconds, Four Moves

1. Your position, in the first sentence.

2. One reason, and one example from your own life.

3. A concession: the strongest point on the other side, in one sentence.

4. Back to your position, so the answer has an end.

Diagnostic

Online or in Person?

An interviewer on a screen asks: “Some people say you can learn a language better online than in a classroom. What do you think?”

You have no preparation time. Start speaking now, and stop at forty-five seconds.

Chapter 3: The Code

The Other Side, in One Sentence

Four ways to accept a point without giving up your opinion

Grammar Discovery

Agree a Little, Then Disagree

"Although online classes are flexible, I learn more in a room with people."

"Despite the heavy workload, I think the Socratic method is worthwhile."

"My brother learned from video games, whereas I learned from videos."

"Cramming works for one exam. However, it does not work in the long run."

Each sentence accepts one point and then moves in another direction. Look at what comes right after each red and blue word: a full sentence, or only a noun? And which one starts a new sentence after a period?

JobFormExample
Accept a point, then disagreealthough / even though + subject + verbAlthough online classes are flexible, I prefer the room.
The same, with a noundespite / in spite of + noun or -ingDespite the workload, it is worthwhile.
Compare two factsclause, whereas / while + clauseHe learned from games, whereas I learned from videos.
Contrast two sentencesSentence. However, + sentenceIt works for one exam. However, it does not last.
Stronger surpriseeven though + subject + verbEven though I read a lot, I still get stuck.
Despite with a verbdespite + -ingDespite studying every night, I felt ready.
The trapnever despite of; never although butNot Although it is hard, but I like it. Say Although it is hard, I like it.

In one line: Although and whereas take a full sentence, despite takes a noun or -ing, however starts a new sentence, and although never needs but.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

A Practice Interview

One conversation, two recordings, one feedback sheet

Dialogue

Question Two, No Preparation

N
Okay, Luiza, this is a practice round. I ask, you answer right away, forty-five seconds each. Ready? Do you think students should study a topic before the class, or after it?
L
Before, in most cases. Although it takes time, the class makes much more sense when you already know the basic ideas.
N
Good start. But is there a drawback? Some students say it is overwhelming.
L
It can be. At my university we do it every night, and the workload is heavy. Despite that, I think the benefits outweigh the drawbacks, at least in the long run.
N
Nice concession. Next question. Is social media good or bad for language learners?
L
Honestly, it is a double-edged sword. I learned most of my English from videos, whereas my friends learned in a classroom. However, you can also spend three hours watching and learn nothing.
N
That example is exactly what the test wants. Last one. Would you rather cram for one big exam, or study a little every week?
L
A little every week, even though it is less exciting. I took it for granted in school, but when I crammed, I forgot everything in a week.
N
Good. You hesitated a bit on the second one. Were you torn between the two sides?
L
A little. But I think the trade-off is worthwhile: I lose two seconds, and I gain a better answer. How long did I take on that one?
Comprehension

About Nathan

1. How long does Nathan say each answer should be, and what does he say about preparation?
Forty-five seconds each, and she answers right away, with no preparation.
2. What drawback does Nathan say some students mention?
That studying before the class is overwhelming.
3. What does Nathan say about her social media example?
That it is exactly what the test wants.
4. What does Nathan notice about her second answer?
That she hesitated a bit, and he asks whether she was torn between the two sides.

Listening 2 · A Radio Debate

Should Law Schools Stop Cold-Calling?

Two sides of a debate on the Socratic method. Sound first — no text.

1. What do supporters say the Socratic method prepares students for, and what do they point out about the reading?
For courtrooms, where nobody waits for you to feel ready. Students who may be called on actually do the reading.
2. What do critics accept, and what is their main argument?
They accept it creates discipline. They argue it rewards confident students, whereas quieter students stay silent.
3. What middle ground have some schools found, and what do most students still say?
They tell a small group in advance that they will be on call that week. Most students still say it is stressful, but worthwhile.
