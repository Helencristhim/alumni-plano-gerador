Grammar Video
"Modifying Comparatives — a lot, far more, much, a little, slightly" — ENGLISH with James, engVid

A free lesson on the exact set of words this class is built on: how to say that a difference is enormous, moderate or barely there. Connection to Lesson 6: this is why "far higher" and "slightly less" are two different pieces of information.

Tip: pause at each example and say the same sentence about Sao Paulo and the city you chose in the warm-up. If you can do it without the word "very", the lesson has landed.

Podcast
"Why are prices going up?" — 6 Minute English, BBC Learning English

Six minutes on inflation and the cost of living, with the vocabulary used to talk about prices rising and money buying less. Connection to Lesson 6: it is the reading of this lesson, in the form you would meet it on the news.

Tip: listen once, then write three sentences comparing prices now and five years ago — and grade every one of them. Far higher, slightly higher, nowhere near as high.

Reading
"London is officially one of the world's most expensive cities for expats" — Time Out London

A short ranking piece placing London among the most expensive cities in the world for people who move there for work, with the cities it is measured against. Connection to Lesson 6: real comparisons, in the register of a magazine rather than a report.

Tip: read it once and underline every comparative. Then say out loud how London compares with Sao Paulo on three of those points, grading each difference. The article gives you the facts; the grading is yours.
