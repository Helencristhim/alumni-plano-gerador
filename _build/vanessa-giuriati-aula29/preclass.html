Lesson 29 -- Pre-class

Health, Wellbeing and Self-Care

Answer "How are you?" honestly and briefly with the present perfect continuous ("I've been feeling a bit run down lately"), and talk about energy, rest and self-care. Key words: run down, burned out, under the weather, take a toll, recharge, pace yourself, look after, switch off.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Run down -- Tired and a little sick because you have done too much
"I've been feeling a bit run down lately."
Burned out -- Completely exhausted after a long period of stress or work
"After two years without a break, she was burned out."
Under the weather -- A little sick, not seriously
"I'm a bit under the weather today."
Take a toll -- To have a bad effect on something over time (take a toll on)
"Late nights are taking a toll on my sleep."
Recharge -- To rest so that you get your energy back
"I need a quiet weekend to recharge."
Pace yourself -- To do things at a speed you can keep for a long time
"It's a long project, so pace yourself."
Look after -- To take care of someone or something
"I've been trying to look after myself better."
Switch off -- To stop thinking about work and relax completely
"I can't switch off until I close my laptop."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present perfect continuous

GRAMMAR

Use have / has been + -ing for something that started in the past and is still going, or has just stopped, with an effect now.

StructureUseExample
have / has been + -ingStarted before and still going (or just stopped)I've been feeling run down lately.
How long have you been + -ing?Ask about the length of something ongoingHow long have you been dealing with this?
for / since / lately / recentlyTypical time wordsShe's been sleeping badly since March.
know, like, want (no -ing)Some verbs are not used in the continuousI've known her for years.

The continuous shows the PROCESS ('I've been working late'); the simple form shows a RESULT or a number ('I've had three coffees today'). Negative: 'I haven't been sleeping well'. With 'since' or 'for', never the simple present: not 'I am tired since Monday'.
