YouTube · BBC Learning English
Defining relative clauses -- 6 Minute Grammar

Six minutes on the exact structure you practiced tonight: who for people, that and which for things, whose for possession and where for places, with no commas at all. Two BBC presenters build the clauses live, at natural speed, and drill them with quick examples.

Tip: watch it twice. The second time, pause after each example and say one sentence about your own team: "The engineer who..."

Relative Clauses -- The Grammar Gameshow, Episode 11

Ten minutes in quiz format: contestants build relative clauses under pressure while the host corrects every mistake out loud. It covers the two traps from class -- putting "that" between commas, and repeating the noun inside the clause.

Tip: answer out loud before the contestants do. If you beat them to "whose", the form is already yours.

TED Talk · Work & Leadership
Margaret Heffernan: Forget the pecking order at work

Fifteen minutes on why the strongest teams are not the ones full of star performers, but the ones where people help each other. It is the natural context for tonight's language -- turnover, mentoring, onboarding and consolidating teams all appear in the working world she describes.

Tip: turn on English subtitles, then record two minutes describing her argument with relative clauses: "The teams that produced the best results...", "The researcher who ran the experiment...".
