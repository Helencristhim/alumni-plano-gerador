Chapter 4: The Relative Code

The Relative Map

One Pattern, Six Doors

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The engineer who built our data pipeline left the company last year."

"The bottleneck that slowed every release was in the review step."

"My counterpart in Dublin, who runs the same platform, reports the same numbers."

"The workaround we used for six months has finally been retired."

What do all the highlighted groups have in common?

PartFormExample
The nounthe person, thing or place you are describingThe engineer ...
The pronounwho (people) · that / which (things) · whose (possession) · where (places)... who built our pipeline ...
No commas = definingthe clause says WHICH one you mean; remove it and the sentence goes vagueThe bottleneck that slowed every release ...
Commas = non-definingextra information about something already identified — use who or which, never thatMy counterpart, who runs the same platform, ...
Drop the pronounOnly when it is the OBJECT of the clause: "The workaround we used lasted six months." Never when it is the subject, and never repeat the noun inside the clause.

Place: the lab where we test · Possession: the team whose turnover was highest

Detail

Commas Change the Meaning

NO COMMAS = which one

Essential. Remove it and the sentence goes vague.

"The engineer who built the pipeline has left."

who · that · which · whose · where

COMMAS = extra detail

Remove it and the sentence still works.

"My counterpart in Dublin, who joined last year, runs the same platform."

who · which · whose · where — never that

In an interview most of your clauses have no commas — nobody in the room knows your projects yet.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"The manager that his team was consolidated is now in Dublin."
"The manager whose team was consolidated is now in Dublin."
"My director, that joined in 2019, approved the plan."
"My director, who joined in 2019, approved the plan."
"The system that we replaced it was ten years old."
"The system that we replaced was ten years old."

Use whose for possession, who after a comma, and never repeat the noun inside the clause.

Grammar Practice

Choose the Right Pronoun

Say the full sentence out loud, then tap to check

"The engineer ___who designed our first pipeline still mentors every new hire." (people -- defining)
"The bottleneck ___that slowed every release was in the review step." (things -- defining)
"The two teams ___whose turnover was highest are now the most stable." (possession)
"The lab ___where the models are tested is in Zurich." (place)
"My counterpart in Dublin, ___who runs the same platform, reports the same numbers." (extra information -- never "that")
Dialogue

Who Owns What

M
Lucia, before the review starts, remind me which team actually owns the release pipeline now.
L
The team that fixed the bottleneck in the review step owns it. It is the group we consolidated in March.
M
And who runs it day to day?
L
Priya, the engineer who designed the first version. She also mentors everyone we onboard.
M
Was that the group with the retention problem last year?
L
It was. The two teams whose turnover was highest last year are now the most stable in the division.
M
What changed?
L
One manager instead of three, and the workaround we used for six months has finally been retired. My counterpart in Dublin, who runs the same platform in Europe, is copying the model to scale it.
M
That is the clearest org chart anyone has given me all year.

About the Conversation

1. What does Mark want to know before the review starts?
Which team actually owns the release pipeline now.
2. What does Mark ask about after he hears who runs the team?
Whether it was the group with the retention problem last year, and then what changed.

Listening 1

Meet the Group

A director introduces his team. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. Who is Priya, and what does she do now?
The engineer who designed the first version of the pipeline. She now mentors every person they onboard.
2. Where are the models tested, and what happened there this year?
In the lab in Zurich, which has doubled its capacity this year.
3. Why are the two groups with the highest turnover now the most stable?
Because they were consolidated into one team with a single manager.
Listening 2

The Two-Second Test

An interview coach shares one test. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What is the test she gives every candidate?
Say the sentence out loud, take the clause away, and listen to what is left.
2. What does she say you must never use between commas?
"That" -- between commas you use who or which.
3. Why will most of your clauses in an interview have no commas?
Because you are naming projects, teams and people that nobody in the room has ever heard of.
