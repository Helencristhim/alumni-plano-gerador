Lesson 31 -- Pre-class

Before the Big Meeting

Preparing an important meeting with a short call, and checking agreement without pushing. Key words: pre-read, talking points, bottom line, ballpark, on the same page, run through, loop in, touch base, sign off, dry run. Structure: question tags -- isn't it? / didn't you? / shouldn't we?

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Pre-read -- a short document that people read before a meeting
"I sent the pre-read on Tuesday, two days before the call."
Talking points -- the few main ideas that you plan to say in a meeting
"I have three talking points for the board."
Bottom line -- the most important fact; the final result, often about money
"The bottom line is that the platform pays for itself."
Ballpark -- a number that is approximately right, not exact
"Can you give me a ballpark for next year?"
On the same page -- agreeing and understanding a situation in the same way
"I want to make sure we are on the same page."
Run through -- to explain or check something quickly, from start to end
"Let me run through the three key numbers."
Loop in -- to include someone in a conversation or an email
"We should loop in Daniel before the meeting."
Touch base -- to talk briefly with someone to check how things are
"Let's touch base on Monday morning."
Sign off -- to give official, final approval to something
"The CFO has to sign off on the budget."
Dry run -- a practice of a presentation or event before the real one
"We did a dry run with Karen on Thursday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Next week, the Brightwave board reviews all software costs, and the CFO, Victor, must sign off on the budget. Karen says the big meeting is not the place for surprises. So Patrícia sends a pre-read and prepares three talking points. Then she calls Victor to be sure they are on the same page. "You got the pre-read, didn't you?" she asks. He did. She runs through the numbers and gives him the bottom line: each store saves four hours a week. "That is a strong number, isn't it?" Victor asks for a ballpark for next year's price. Before they finish, Patrícia says they should loop in Daniel, and they agree to touch base on Monday. On Thursday, she does a dry run with Karen.

1. "You got the pre-read, didn't you?" Why didn't?
A Because got is past simple, so the tag uses did.
B Because every tag uses didn't.
C Because the sentence is negative.
2. "That is a strong number, isn't it?" Does Patrícia expect yes or no?
A Yes.
B No.
C She does not know.
3. Why does Patrícia call Victor before the meeting?
A To avoid surprises in the meeting.
B To cancel the meeting.
C To ask for a discount.
4. Which sentence is correct?
A "We should loop in Daniel, isn't it?"
B "We should loop in Daniel, shouldn't we?"
C "We should loop in Daniel, don't we?"

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Question Tags: isn't it? / didn't you? / shouldn't we?

GRAMMAR

A short question at the end of a sentence, to check that the other person agrees. English has no universal "né": the tag copies the verb of the sentence.

StructureUseExample
positive sentence + negative tagYou expect yes.The numbers are good, aren't they?
negative sentence + positive tagYou expect no.You didn't see the email, did you?
be or a modal: repeat itis, are, can, should, will...We should call him, shouldn't we?
other verbs: do / does / didPresent and past simple.You got the pre-read, didn't you?
present perfect: have / hasRepeat have or has.You haven't seen it, have you?
AvoidNever isn't it after every sentence: not "You got it, isn't it?" → "You got it, didn't you?". Never the name in the tag: not "Victor agreed, didn't Victor?" → "didn't he?". And never negative + negative: not "You didn't see it, didn't you?" → "did you?".

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think about a real important meeting in your calendar. Record the call that you would make one week before it, with the key person: check that they got the pre-read, confirm that they agree with your main points, ask for a ballpark if you need one, and agree on the next step. Use at least four question tags, with falling intonation. Take your time.
