Chapter 3: Language Focus

Didn't You? Isn't It?

question tags — checking without pushing

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"You got the pre-read, didn't you?"

"The numbers are good, aren't they?"

"That is a strong number, isn't it?"

"We should loop in Daniel, shouldn't we?"

Look at the green words at the end of each sentence. Where does each one come from? Compare it with the verb in the first part.

StructureUseExample
positive sentence + negative tagYou expect the answer yesThe numbers are good, aren't they?
negative sentence + positive tagYou expect the answer noYou didn't see the email, did you?
be / modal: repeat itis → isn't it · should → shouldn't weWe should call him, shouldn't we?
other verbs: do / does / didgot → didn't you · works → doesn't itIt works, doesn't it?
IntonationFalling = you are checking. Rising = a real question.You got it, didn't you? (falling)

The tag uses a pronoun, never the name: not "Victor agreed, didn't Victor?" → "didn't he?". And there is no universal isn't it like né: not "You got it, isn't it?" → "You got it, didn't you?"

The Three Sentences Before a Big Meeting

Check, Agree, Align

"You got the pre-read, didn't you?"

1 — check that the other person is ready: got → didn't you

"The numbers look good, don't they?"

2 — check that you agree, without pushing: look → don't they

"We should loop in Daniel before the meeting, shouldn't we?"

3 — align the next step: should → shouldn't we

Chapter 4: The Pre-Meeting Call

The Pre-Meeting Call

Fifteen minutes with the CFO

Dialogue

On the Same Page?

P
Victor, thank you for the time. Before the budget review, I want to make sure we are on the same page. You got the pre-read, didn't you?
V
I did. I read it on the plane. The adoption numbers are good, aren't they?
P
They are. Forty-eight stores are live, and that is ahead of the plan. Let me run through the three key numbers.
V
Please be quick. The board will only ask one question: what is the bottom line?
P
The bottom line is simple: each store saves about four hours a week. That is a strong number, isn't it?
V
It is. And the price for next year? Just give me a ballpark.
P
In ballpark terms, the same price per store. We should loop in Daniel before the meeting, shouldn't we?
V
Yes. Let's touch base with him on Monday, and then I can sign off on the budget.
Comprehension

About Victor

On the plane.

Chapter 6: Your Turn

No Surprises

Guided > Semi-free > Free

Role-Play — Guided

The Pre-Meeting Call

Scenario: I am Victor, the CFO of Brightwave. Before the budget review, check three things with me, one at a time, with a question tag: that I got the pre-read, that I agree with the numbers, and that we should loop in Daniel.

Keywords:

didn't you? don't they? shouldn't we? pre-read on the same page
Role-Play — Semi-free

Find the Surprise

Scenario: I am Victor again, and one thing worries me, but I will not say it unless you ask. Check my agreement on your three talking points, one by one, with question tags. When I say no, find out why and agree on a next step before the meeting.

Keywords:

talking points bottom line ballpark touch base sign off
Free Practice

Ninety Seconds, One Real Meeting

Ninety seconds: think of a real important meeting in your calendar. Imagine the call with the key person one week before: check that they got the pre-read, that they agree with your main points, and what the next step is.

No keywords. One rule: at least four question tags, with falling intonation.
