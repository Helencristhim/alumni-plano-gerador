Chapter 3: The Code

One Fact, Five Shapes

Nothing new. Everything you already have, side by side

Grammar Discovery

Same Fact, Five Sentences

The fact: clause nine caps our liability at one month of fees, and the amendment is not signed.

"We should not sign until the cap is raised."

"Clause nine provides that our liability is capped at one month of fees."

"In short, the cap is the sticking point. Therefore my recommendation is to hold."

"If we sign it as it stands, we will carry the whole exposure."

"The cap was left unchanged in the last round."

One fact, five shapes. Which question is each of them the answer to?

The questionThe shapeFrom
"Can we sign?"A modal: must / must not / should / do not have to.Lesson 1
"What does the clause say?"A reporting verb in the present: states, requires, provides.Lesson 2
"Why do you recommend that?"Connectors: in short, first, second, however, therefore.Lesson 3
"How bad is it?"A conditional: first for a real risk, second for a remote one.Lesson 4
"Who let this through?"The passive: the cap was left unchanged. No name, no blame.Lesson 5

The question that saves you: what is this moment asking for? — not "how do I say this in English?"

Chapter 4: The Mock Call

Five Questions, Eight Minutes

Every one of them needs a different shape

Dialogue

Eight Minutes Before the Board

D
Ana, I have eight minutes. Can we sign the master agreement this week, what does clause nine actually say, and do I need to give the board a heads-up?
A
Three answers. We should not sign this week. Clause nine provides that our liability is capped at one month of fees. And yes — give them a heads-up today, because the sticking point is the cap, not the turnaround.
D
Their counsel told me on Friday they agreed to raise it. And what is an affiliate, by the way? The board will ask.
A
They proposed raising it. Nothing has been agreed, and it is not in the wording. And an affiliate is a company that belongs to the same group as the counterparty — which matters here, because the cap covers them too.
D
Fine. And if I sign it as it stands on Friday, how bad is it?
A
If we sign it as it stands, we carry the whole exposure above one month of fees. My recommendation is to hold until we have their buy-in in writing. Expect pushback on the timing, not on the cap.
D
That is four answers in ninety seconds, and I can use every one of them. Nine weeks ago I used to book a second call after yours. Send me those two lines and I will take it in.
Comprehension

About David

1. How much time does David have, and why does that matter?
Eight minutes, before a board call — so he needs answers he can use, not a full explanation.
2. What does he get wrong in the middle of the call?
He says their counsel agreed to raise the cap. They only proposed it, and it is not in the wording.
3. What does he say has changed since nine weeks ago?
He used to book a second call after hers. Now he can act on the answers straight away.
Listening

The Moment It Stops Being Hard Work

A manager who works with lawyers in six countries. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is NOT the moment a lawyer stops being hard work?
When the accent improves. That is not what he notices.
2. What can he hear in the first months?
The machine: two seconds of silence while the question is translated, then a long sentence that answers something slightly different.
3. What does he get now when he asks how bad something would be?
A condition, not a story.
4. What is his definition of fluent?
Every question comes back in the shape it was asked in, so he can act without booking a second call.
Listening 2

I Expected Beautiful. I Got Short

A lawyer, nine weeks later. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What did she expect to happen to her English?
That it would get more beautiful: longer sentences, better words.
2. What actually happened to her summaries?
They went from three paragraphs to four lines, and people stopped asking what she meant.
3. What did she notice around the ninth or tenth week?
She had stopped building sentences. She was choosing: condition, or reporting the document, or the passive.
4. What still catches her?
Anything she did not prepare for, and an accent she has never heard before.

Fix the Sentence

1. "Clause nine said that our liability is capped at one month of fees."
"Clause nine states that our liability is capped at one month of fees." (the contract is in force)
2. "On the call they agreed sixty days." (they only offered it)
"On the call they proposed sixty days. Nothing has been agreed."
3. "Could you tell me what does the cap cover?"
"Could you tell me what the cap covers?"
4. "They backed the deal out two days before signature."
"They backed out of the deal two days before signature."
