Lesson 10 -- Pre-class

Milestone Review -- Integrating Nine Weeks of Legal English

Nine weeks, nine tools, and one question left: which one does this moment need? Key words: turnaround, handover, sticking point, heads-up, ballpark, red tape, workaround, backlog, bandwidth, rollout, buy-in, pushback. Structures: nothing new -- the same fact said with a modal, with a reporting verb, with connectors, with a conditional and in the passive, so you can choose the shape the question is asking for.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the definition. None of these is in the contract -- they live in the emails and the calls around it.

Turnaround -- the time it takes to complete something and send it back
"The turnaround on a full review is three working days."
Handover -- passing a task or a file to another person
"I will write a short handover before I go on leave."
Sticking point -- the one issue that keeps blocking agreement
"The sticking point is the cap, not the timeline."
Heads-up -- an early warning so nobody is surprised
"Let me give you a quick heads-up before the board sees this."
Ballpark -- an approximate figure, not an exact one
"I can give you a ballpark now and the exact number tomorrow."
Red tape -- official rules and paperwork that slow everything down
"The delay is red tape on their side, not the drafting."
Workaround -- a way of getting past a problem without solving it
"That is a workaround, not a fix -- the clause is still wrong."
Backlog -- work that has accumulated and is still waiting
"There are eleven contracts in the backlog this month."
Bandwidth -- the time and capacity a person or team actually has
"I do not have the bandwidth for a second review this week."
Rollout -- putting something into use across the whole organization
"The rollout of the new template starts in October."
Buy-in -- the agreement and support of the people involved
"We have legal buy-in; we still need finance."
Pushback -- resistance or disagreement from the other side
"Expect pushback on the indemnity -- they refused it twice."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

David has eight minutes and five questions, and every question needs a different shape. He asks whether the master agreement can be signed this week, and Ana answers with a rule: "we should not sign until the cap is raised". He asks what clause nine says, and she reports the document in the present: "clause nine provides that our liability is capped at one month of fees". He asks how bad it would be, and she answers with a condition: "if we signed it as it stands, we would carry the whole exposure". Then he says their counsel agreed to raise the cap. Ana does not let that pass. They proposed it; nothing has been agreed, and it is not in the wording yet. Finally he asks what an affiliate is, and she defines it inside the sentence: "an affiliate is a company that belongs to the same group". Nothing here is new. The sticking point is the turnaround between the question and the shape, and after nine weeks that gap is closing.

1. Why does Ana answer "can we sign this week?" with a modal instead of quoting the clause?
A The question asks for a rule, not for the text of the document.
B Because quoting a clause is grammatically wrong in English.
C Because modals are more polite than reporting verbs.
2. Why is "clause nine provides that our liability is capped" in the present tense?
A Because the call is happening now.
B Because the document is in force, so it still says that today.
C Because "provide" has no past form in legal English.
3. Why does Ana replace "they agreed" with "they proposed"?
A Because "agree" is too informal for a board call.
B Because the counterparty said it in a different language.
C Because an offer is not a commitment, and "agreed" would turn a conversation into a fact.
4. According to the text, what is closing after nine weeks?
A The gap between hearing the question and choosing the shape of the answer.
B The gap between her accent and a native accent.
C The gap between the two versions of the contract.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- One Fact, Five Shapes

GRAMMAR

Nothing new here. The same fact, and the moment on the call that each shape belongs to.

The questionThe shapeExample
"Can we sign?"A modal: must / must not / should / do not have to.We should not sign until the cap is raised.
"What does the clause say?"A reporting verb in the present, because the document is in force.Clause nine provides that our liability is capped.
"Why do you recommend that?"Connectors: position, two reasons, one caveat, position again.In short, the cap is the sticking point. Therefore I would hold.
"How bad is it?"A conditional: first for a real risk, second for a remote one.If we sign it as it stands, we will carry the whole exposure.
"Who let this through?"The passive, for professional distance. No name, no blame.The cap was left unchanged in the last round.
Three trapsNever answer "can we sign?" by reading the clause -- that answers a question nobody asked. Never use the first conditional for a remote risk ("if we sign, we will lose everything") when the second is the honest one. And never write "they agreed" for something that was only proposed -- one word turns a conversation into a commitment.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

The manager asks: "Can we sign the master agreement this week?" You answer:
A "Clause nine says the liability of the parties shall be limited to an amount equivalent to..."
B "We should not sign this week. The cap is still the sticking point."
C "It is difficult to say, there are many aspects in this contract."
He asks how bad it would be to sign anyway. The risk is real, not remote. You say:
A "If we sign it as it stands, we will carry the whole exposure above one month of fees."
B "If we would sign it as it stands, we will carry the whole exposure."
C "It is a very dangerous contract for the company in general."
On Friday their counsel offered to raise the cap. Nothing is in writing. In today's summary you write:
A "They agreed to raise the cap."
B "They proposed raising the cap. Nothing has been agreed, and it is not in the wording."
C "The contract provides that the cap will be raised."
He asks who approved the version with the old cap. You do not want to name anyone. You say:
A "The procurement team forgot to send the amendment, as always."
B "The cap was left unchanged in the last round. I would rather fix it than trace it."
C "I do not know, it is not my responsibility."
He asks a question you did not catch, and you need two seconds. You say:
A "Sorry? Sorry? I do not understand your accent."
B "Bear with me a second -- could you tell me what exactly the board wants to know?"
C "Yes, of course, I will send you an email about that later."
