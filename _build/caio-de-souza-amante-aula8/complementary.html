Complementary Activities

Optional extras to enjoy outside class. Mark each one as done after you watch or listen to it.

Talk
Hans Rosling — “The Best Stats You've Ever Seen” (TED)

Twenty minutes of a statistician making numbers argue for themselves, in front of an audience that came in believing the opposite. Almost every sentence he says about the data is built in the impersonal form you practised today, and you can hear why: he wants the number in front, not himself.

Tip: watch once for the argument. On the second pass, write down five sentences of his where the actor has been removed, and one where it has not.

Watch on TED ↗
Video
VCPete — “Revenue Churn and Net Revenue Retention”

A venture capitalist working through the exact confusion the second listening described: two companies reporting the same churn number and meaning two different businesses. Short, unhurried American English, with the arithmetic on screen while he speaks.

Tip: pause when he defines each metric and say the definition back in the impersonal form, out loud, before you continue.

Watch on YouTube ↗
Podcast
Planet Money — “Accounting, Double Entry and the Last Supper”

Where the whole language of the board pack came from, told as a story about a monk in fifteenth-century Italy. The best half hour there is on why a number means nothing until somebody agrees on how it is counted, which is the argument the analyst makes in the second listening.

Tip: the full transcript is on the same page. Listen first with the page closed, then read it and count how many sentences have no person in them.
