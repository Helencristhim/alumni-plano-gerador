Chapter 4: The Sentence With Nobody In It

When the Actor Leaves the Sentence

And the one rule that decides when he comes back

Grammar Discovery

Where Did Everybody Go?

"The forecast was missed by nine percent."

"Churn is measured on client count, not on revenue."

"The accounts were audited by Vieira and Marsh in March."

"Two deals moved into the next quarter."

Every red piece is built from two parts, and the first part is always the same verb. It is that first part, not the second, that carries the tense and agrees with the subject. Only one sentence adds the green tail, and it does so because the name changes what the sentence is worth. The orange one needs none of this: nothing was done to those deals.

JobFormExample
The result matters, the actor does notbe + past participleThe forecast was missed by nine percent.
A definition or a standing ruleis / are + past participleChurn is measured on client count.
The actor changes the weight, so name it… by + the actorThe accounts were audited by Vieira and Marsh.
Nothing is done to the subjectkeep it activeTwo deals moved into the next quarter.
The trapbe is not optional — there are always two verbsNot the forecast missed by nine percent. Say was missed.
The second trapthe participle, never the plain pastNot was chose. Say was chosen.
The permanent targetbe carries the number, so it still agreesChurn is measured. The accounts were audited.

In one line: take the person out when the person is not the point — and never when the person is you.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Two People Who Read Numbers for a Living

Two recordings and one page of a real board pack

Listening 1 · A Board Member

The First Ninety Seconds

A board member on what she looks for before she reads a single number. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she look for in the first ninety seconds, and why?
The sentences that still have a person in them, because those are the ones somebody fought over.
2. What does she say about the impersonal sentences?
That everything uncontroversial has already been flattened into that form, so nobody is standing behind it and nobody argues about it.
3. Why does she call it a reliable method?
Because the document was written by eight people who all wanted it to look calm, and the one named sentence shows where the disagreement lives.
Listening 2 · A Fund Analyst

The Number Is Not the Message

A European fund analyst on what eight percent actually means. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say a churn number on its own teaches her almost nothing?
Because she does not yet know what was counted — clients or revenue, monthly or annual, and whether a reduced contract counts at all.
2. What does she ask founders to write into the sentence?
The definition itself, in the impersonal form, every time — and she says it costs about eleven words.
3. What does she say about audited numbers?
That you should say who audited them, because the name is doing work the sentence cannot do without it.

Board Pack, Page One

Six lines. Five of them say what was done and not who did it. The last one names the firm, because there the name is the information.
1. Which two lines on this page would a buyer ask about first, and what would he ask?
Recurring revenue, because it rose seventeen points and he will want to know what was reclassified; and the missed forecast, because he will want to know what moved.
2. Why is one quarter excluded from the baseline, and why does the page say so?
Because it was an outlier and it would distort every comparison. The page says so because a buyer who finds it himself assumes it was hidden.
3. Only one line on the page names a company. Why that one?
Because the accounts were audited from outside, and the name of the firm is the information — without it, the line says almost nothing.

What I Can Do Now
