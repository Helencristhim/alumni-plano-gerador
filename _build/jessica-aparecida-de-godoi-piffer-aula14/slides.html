Chapter 4: By, To and From

How Much, and Where To?

One movement, two shapes, and four small words

Grammar Discovery

Same Story, Different Shapes

"Trips rose sharply after May."

"There was a sharp rise after May."

"Trips plunged by almost 70 percent."

"Trips climbed from 40,000 to 65,000 a day."

The red sentences say the same thing in two ways. The blue words tell you something about the numbers.

What you want to sayPatternExample
the movementverb + adverbrose sharply · grew steadily · fell slightly
the same movement as a thingadjective + nouna sharp rise · steady growth · a slight fall
the size of the changebyfell by 70 percent · a rise of 70 percent
the end pointtorose to 92,000
the start and the endfrom … tofrom 40,000 to 65,000
the topatpeaked at 92,000
the timefinished time or up to nowin 2020 it plunged · since 2022 it has edged up
the trapNot increased in 10 percent and not rose sharp. Say increased by 10 percent, rose sharply.

In one line: a verb takes an adverb, a noun takes an adjective; by is how much, to is where it ended.

Start With the Sentence

On how to present a chart so people remember it. Sound first — no text.

2. What should you say before you show the chart?

Listening 2 · A Market Report

A Remarkable Year for Coffee

A short radio report on coffee prices. Sound first — no text.

1. Why did prices climb at the start of the year?
Because of a long dry season in Brazil.
2. What happened in May, and why?
Prices plunged by twenty percent in one week, when the rain finally came.
3. What do analysts expect before the end of the year?
Prices will edge up again, as demand from Asia grows steadily.

1. What happened to the effective rate in Q3?
It rose sharply, to 31.2 percent. It was a spike.
2. By how much did payments rise from Q2 to Q3?
By 1.5 million dollars, from 4.1 to 5.6 million.
3. Why is Q3 not the real trend?
Because it includes a one-off payment after an audit settlement.
