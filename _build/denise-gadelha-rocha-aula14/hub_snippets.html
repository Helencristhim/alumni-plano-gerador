Lesson 14 -- Pre-class

Language Under Pressure -- Advanced Hedging, Epistemic Distance and Executive Credibility in Uncertain Conversations

How to say exactly how much you know, and no more. Key words: overstate, understate, qualify, plausible, working assumption, directionally, order of magnitude, stand behind, provisional, indicative, commit to a number, with confidence. Structure: hedging and epistemic distance -- modals of probability, adverbs of certainty and impersonal structures.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the definition. These are the labels that tell a reader how much you actually know.

To overstate -- to make something sound bigger or more certain than the evidence allows
"If I overstate this once, every number I give them afterwards gets discounted."
To understate -- to make something sound smaller or less certain than it really is
"She understated it so carefully that nobody in the room acted on it."
To qualify -- to add a limit to a statement so that it is not read as absolute
"I will give you the figure, and I want to qualify it before you write it down."
Plausible -- believable on the evidence you have, without being proven
"A harder market in 2027 is plausible. It is not yet a forecast."
Working assumption -- something you treat as true so that work can continue, knowing it may change
"Our working assumption is six per cent. It is an assumption, not a promise."
Directionally -- right about the trend, without claiming the exact figure
"Directionally we are where I said we would be in July. The decimal comes on Friday."
Order of magnitude -- the rough scale of something: tens, hundreds or thousands
"I can give you the order of magnitude today and the figure on Friday."
To stand behind -- to be willing to defend a number in public as your own
"That is the only figure on the page I will stand behind in any month of the year."
Provisional -- good enough to work with now, and expected to change
"Treat the December column as provisional until the run-off closes."
Indicative -- meant to show the shape of something, not to be quoted as final
"The pricing there is indicative, and I would not put it in a board pack."
To commit to a number -- to give a figure you accept being held to later
"I am not going to commit to a number before the December run-off closes."
With confidence -- the top of the scale: said without a caveat, because you would defend it
"There are two things I can say with confidence, and this is one of them."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the account and answer the questions. Watch what happens to the distance between the speaker and the claim.

Denise can say with confidence that retention closed at 84%. The figure has been reconciled twice, so she states it plainly and stands behind it. The rest of the assessment is not like that, and the difference has to be audible in the sentence itself.

Capacity in the region would appear to be tightening. On the evidence we have, a harder market in 2027 is plausible — and the note says so in exactly those words, because "the market will harden" and "the market may harden" are two different promises, and only one of them can be kept in March. The pricing in the appendix is indicative: it shows the shape of the movement rather than the figure, and the figure itself stays provisional until the December run-off closes.

Her working assumption for planning is six per cent, and she says what would move it. Directionally the directorate is where it said it would be in July; the order of magnitude is reliable and the decimal is not. What she refuses to do is commit to a number in October that she would have to defend in March.

The temptation runs both ways. Overstating buys one good meeting and costs you every figure afterwards. Understating costs nothing visible and everything slowly, because nobody tells you that the question has quietly gone to somebody else.

1. "The market will harden" and "the market may harden" — what is the difference?
A "Will" states it as something you can be held to; "may" marks it as one possible outcome among others.
B "Will" is formal and "may" is informal, but the meaning is the same.
C "Will" is used for next year and "may" for this year.
2. "Capacity would appear to be tightening." Why "would appear" and not "appears"?
A Because the sentence refers to the past.
B Because "would" puts one more step of distance between the speaker and the claim: it is what the evidence points to, not what she declares.
C Because "appear" cannot be used in the present tense.
3. Which of these is the strongest claim in the text?
A Retention closed at 84%.
B A harder market in 2027 is plausible.
C Capacity would appear to be tightening.
4. Why does the text say that understating costs "everything slowly"?
A Because an understated figure is always wrong in the end.
B Because it takes several quarters to correct an understated figure.
C Because nobody tells you it is happening — the question simply goes to somebody who answers it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Hedging and Epistemic Distance

GRAMMAR

Three layers do the work, and they stack in a fixed order: the modal sets the likelihood, the adverb marks the degree, the impersonal structure moves the claim off you and onto the evidence.

LayerWhat it doesThe forms you will actually use
Modals of probabilityPut the claim on a scale of likelihoodwill (certain) · should (expected) · may / might (possible) · could (conceivable)
Adverbs of certaintyMark the degree inside the sentenceclearly · evidently · presumably · apparently · arguably · possibly · conceivably
Impersonal structuresMove the claim off you and onto the evidenceit appears that... · the data suggest that... · there are indications that... · what we are seeing is consistent with...
The distancing "would"One step further back, with the structure unchanged: it appears becomes it would appear, the data suggest becomes the data would suggest. Nothing about the fact changes — only your distance from it.
Where the adverb goesMid-position, after the auxiliary and before the main verb: "capacity is apparently tightening", "we can arguably hold the rate". Front position is for emphasis only: "Arguably, we can hold it."
One hedge, not twoStacking reads as evasion, not as precision: "it would possibly maybe harden" becomes "it may harden". One marker per claim, chosen on purpose.
After a modal, bare infinitiveNever an -s and never a "to": would suggests and would to suggest are both wrong. Only would suggest, would appear, may hold.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

Group asks for a 2027 figure in October, before the December run-off closes. You say:
A "Our working assumption is six per cent. I will commit to five, which I can stand behind in any month."
B "Six per cent." Nothing else — they asked for one number.
C "It is impossible to say anything about 2027 at this stage."
The retention figure has been reconciled twice and it is final. The right way to report it is:
A "Retention seems to be around 84%, more or less, I think."
B "Retention closed at 84%. That one I can say with confidence."
C "Retention would appear to have possibly closed at approximately 84%."
Which sentence is correct?
A "The data would suggests that capacity is tightening."
B "The data would suggest that capacity is tightening."
C "The data would to suggest that capacity is tightening."
You want to say that the trend is right but the exact figure is not settled yet:
A "Directionally we are where we said we would be. The exact figure comes on Friday."
B "The number is right, more or less, probably."
C "I cannot answer that until Friday."
A colleague is about to quote your appendix pricing in a board pack. You should have said, when you sent it:
A "Those figures are almost certainly wrong."
B Nothing. An appendix speaks for itself.
C "Those figures are indicative — they show the shape of the movement, not the number."
Every figure you send arrives with three conditions attached. The real risk is:
A People stop asking you for numbers and ask somebody who answers, and nobody tells you it happened.
B There is no risk. Caution is always the safe choice with a head office.
C You will be held to the conditions instead of to the figure.
