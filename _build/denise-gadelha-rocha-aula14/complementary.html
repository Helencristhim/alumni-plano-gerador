Lesson 14 -- Language Under Pressure

Series
Inside Bill's Brain: Decoding Bill Gates -- Part 2

Watch a man who almost never gives a flat number. He works in orders of magnitude, he says what a figure depends on before he says the figure, and he separates what the modelling shows from what he personally believes. The whole episode is an hour of the calibration this lesson is about, done by somebody who has been doing it for forty years.

Tip: write down five sentences of his that contain a number. Next to each one, mark the rung: confirmed, expected, observed or inferred.

Podcast
WorkLife with Adam Grant -- the episodes on confident humility and rethinking

His argument is the one behind this whole lesson: being sure and being credible are not the same thing, and the people who are trusted with the hardest questions are the ones who can say precisely how much they know. Listen for how he marks his own claims — the evidence goes in one sentence and his opinion in another.

Tip: pick one episode and count how many times he says "the data suggest" instead of "the data prove". Then read your last report to Milan and count yours.

YouTube
Hans Rosling -- The Best Stats You've Ever Seen (TED)

Nobody has ever been more certain in public and more careful about the limits of the data at the same time. He states what the numbers show without softening it, and he names what they cannot show without apologising for it. That combination is exactly the top and the bottom of the ladder from today, held by the same person in the same talk.

Tip: find the moment where he tells the audience which figures are reliable and which are estimates. Steal the sentence and use it in your Q4 assessment.
