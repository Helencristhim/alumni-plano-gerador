Chapter 1: What You Actually Know

A Number You Gave, and Then Regretted

Not because it was wrong. Because of how certain you sounded when you said it.

The Real Cost

Two Ways to Lose a Room

Overstating

You give a figure you cannot deliver. Somebody holds you to it. It is loud, it is remembered, and you can see it happening.

Understating

Every figure arrives with three conditions attached. Nothing is ever final. After a year, people stop asking you for numbers.

Only the first one is ever said to your face. The second is never mentioned — the question simply goes to somebody who answers it.

Chapter 3: The Code

One Step Back

Modals, adverbs and the impersonal structures that carry the distance

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Capacity is tightening."

"Capacity is apparently tightening."

"It appears that capacity is tightening."

"On the evidence we have, capacity would appear to be tightening."

The fact never changes. Something else does, and it grows with every line. What is it?

LayerWhat it doesThe forms you will use
Modals of probabilityPut the claim on a scale of likelihoodwill (certain) · should (expected) · may / might (possible) · could (conceivable)
Adverbs of certaintyMark the degree inside the sentenceclearly · evidently · presumably · apparently · arguably · possibly · conceivably
Impersonal structuresMove the claim off you and onto the evidenceit appears that... · the data suggest that... · there are indications that... · what we are seeing is consistent with...
The distancing "would"One step further back, with the structure unchanged: it appears becomes it would appear; the data suggest becomes the data would suggest. Nothing about the fact changes — only your distance from it.
Where the adverb goesMid-position, after the auxiliary and before the main verb: "capacity is apparently tightening", "we can arguably hold the rate". Front position is for emphasis only: "Arguably, we can hold it."
One hedge, not twoStacking reads as evasion, not as precision: "it would possibly maybe harden" becomes "it may harden". One marker per claim, chosen deliberately.
After a modal, bare infinitiveNever an -s and never a "to": would suggests and would to suggest are both wrong. Only would suggest.

The Structure

Four Rungs, Four Different Promises

Same subject, four levels of certainty. Choosing the wrong rung costs you in both directions.

Confirmed · you stand behind it

"Retention closed at 84%. That one I can say with confidence."

Expected · you have not promised it

"The rate should hold at five per cent. That is our working assumption."

Observed · you have seen it, you are not declaring it

"It appears that capacity in the region is tightening."

Inferred · the evidence points there, you do not

"On the evidence we have, a harder market in 2027 would appear plausible."

Most executives own the top rung and the bottom one. The two in the middle are where the work actually lives.

E

D
Five. Five is the figure I will stand behind in any month of the year. Six is where I expect to land, and if six goes into your deck it stops being an expectation and becomes a promise I made in October.
E
That is lower than what the other regions are sending me.
D
It is. And in March it will be the only one still standing. Directionally we are all saying the same thing. The difference is that I am telling you which of my two numbers is which.

Listening

The Quiet Failure

A group underwriting director on the two ways of losing a number. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What are the two ways of losing credibility with a number?
Overstating — giving a figure you cannot deliver — and hedging everything, so that nothing you say is ever final.
2. Why does he say overstating is survivable?
Because it is loud and embarrassing, and you can see it happening.
3. What happens to the people who hedge everything, and how do they find out?
People stop asking them for numbers and route the question to somebody who answers it — and nobody ever tells them it is happening.
4. What does he say good executives do instead?
They are precise about the degree: this I will stand behind, this is our working assumption, this is indicative and I would not quote it.
Listening 2

Sure, or Sorry

A director on reporting to a head office in another language. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she say her two settings in English were?
Sure, and sorry — either a flat number, which was more than she meant, or an apology around it, which was less.
2. How was each of the two misread?
The flat number became a commitment; the apology became doubt about the underlying business, when her only doubt was about the decimal.
3. What was the small thing that fixed it?
She stopped improvising the certainty and started choosing the phrase before the call — four or five phrases, used deliberately.
4. What could the room finally hear?
The difference between a number she was guessing at and a number she would defend.
Chapter 5: Practice

Your Quarter, Labelled

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "The data would suggests that capacity is tightening."
"The data would suggest that capacity is tightening." (after a modal, bare infinitive: no -s, no "to")
2. "It appears that the market to be hardening."
"It appears that the market is hardening." or "The market appears to be hardening." (that + clause, or appear + to be, never both)
3. "Is probable that the rate holds at five per cent."
"It is likely that the rate will hold at five per cent." (English needs the subject "it"; and in this register the word is "likely", not "probable")
4. "In my opinion I think the December column is provisional."
"In my opinion the December column is provisional." or simply "The December column is provisional." (one stance marker per claim, never two)

1. Why does every line carry what would move it?
Because a label without a trigger is just a disclaimer. Saying what would move a figure turns a hedge into information the reader can act on.

Because it is the only one you are personally on the hook for. Everything above is labelled; that line is signed.
