Lesson 15 -- Pre-class

When the Answer Is No -- Refusing Without Closing the Door

Three requests, three refusals, and the words that keep a working relationship alive. Key words: to turn down, to decline, a prior commitment, bandwidth, to overcommit, a counter-proposal, to meet someone halfway, non-negotiable, gracious, flattered, to take a rain check, to leave the door open. Structure: polite refusal and counter-proposal.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To turn down -- to say no to an offer or an invitation
"I had to turn down the residency because of the dates."
To decline -- to refuse politely, usually in a formal way
"She declined the invitation in a short, warm email."
A prior commitment -- something you have already agreed to do at that time
"I am afraid I have a prior commitment in Basel that week."
Bandwidth -- the time and energy you have available for new work
"I do not have the bandwidth for a new piece before June."
To overcommit -- to agree to do more than you can really do
"Last year I overcommitted, and every project suffered."
A counter-proposal -- a different offer you make in reply to a request
"Instead of saying only no, she made a counter-proposal."
To meet someone halfway -- to accept part of what someone wants, so that both sides give something
"If you can move the date, I can meet you halfway on the fee."
Non-negotiable -- fixed; it cannot be discussed or changed
"For me, the consent of every patient is non-negotiable."
Gracious -- polite, kind and generous, even in a difficult moment
"It was a gracious refusal: he thanked me, and I still felt welcome."
Flattered -- pleased because someone has shown that they value you
"I am really flattered that you thought of my work."
To take a rain check -- to say no now, but suggest doing it another time
"Can I take a rain check on dinner? This week is impossible."
To leave the door open -- to refuse now, but make it clear you are interested later
"Always leave the door open: say what you could do next time."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

In one week in March, Liliane received three requests. A curator in Lisbon asked for a new piece in six weeks. A hospital asked for weekly workshops. And a client of the agency wanted a private dinner with an artist in Basel.

She did not say yes to everything, and she did not disappear. To the curator she wrote: I'm afraid I can't make a new piece so quickly, and I'd rather not rush a work about patients. Would it work if I sent you an existing piece instead?

To the hospital she wrote: I was wondering if we could start with one workshop a month. And to the client: How about visiting the artist's studio instead? In the end, all three accepted her counter-proposals.

1. Why does she write would it work if I sent and not if I send?
A Because the past form makes the suggestion sound more tentative and polite.
B Because she had already sent the piece to the museum last month, before the curator wrote to her.
C Because send is an irregular verb.
2. "I'd rather not rush a work about patients." What does she mean?
A She is in a hurry and wants to finish the new work as soon as she possibly can.
B She does not like patients.
C She prefers not to do it quickly.
3. What did the three people do in the end?
A They withdrew their invitations and decided to look for another artist for their projects.
B They accepted her counter-proposals.
C They insisted.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Softening the No

GRAMMAR

A polite refusal in English has three moves: thank the person, refuse softly, and offer something else. Each move has its own structure, and each structure is followed by a specific verb form.

MoveFormExample
Refuse softlyI'm afraid + I can't / I won't be able to"I'm afraid I can't take this on."
Thank firstThank you for + -ing / I'm flattered that..."Thank you for thinking of me."
PreferenceI'd rather (not) + base verb, with no to"I'd rather not rush it."
Counter-proposal: questionWould it work if + past simple?"Would it work if I sent you an older piece?" The past here means distance, not time.
Counter-proposal: suggestionHow about / What about + -ing?"How about showing it in the autumn?"
DistancingI was wondering if + subject + could"I was wondering if we could move it." NOT if could we

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are answering curators, clients of the agency and friends. Choose the best thing to say.

A curator invites you to give a talk on a day you are in Basel:
A No. I am in Basel that day, so it is impossible for me, and I cannot change my plans at all.
B I'd rather to go another day.
C Thank you for asking. I'm afraid I have a prior commitment that day.
A client asks for a discount you cannot give, but you could shorten the program:
A I'd rather not change the price, but would it work if we shortened the trip?
B Discount, no.
C How about to pay less and to see less, if that is what you really want to do on this trip?
You want to move a meeting with a museum, politely:
A Move it to Thursday.
B I was wondering if we could move it to Thursday.
C I was wondering if could we move it to Thursday, because Wednesday is very busy for me.
A friend invites you to dinner this week. You cannot go, but you want to see her soon:
A Can I take a rain? I am really very busy with the agency this week, and the next one too.
B I take the check.
C Can I take a rain check? Next week would be perfect.
A residency asks you to give up the rights to your recordings:
A Thank you, the rights are very negotiable for me, so please take whatever you need for the residency.
B Thank you, but keeping the rights is non-negotiable for me.
C Rights? How about to keep them?

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Think of a real request you will probably receive this year: a talk, a show, a residency, a client of the agency. In one minute, record your answer. Thank the person, refuse clearly with I'm afraid or I'd rather not, give one reason, make a counter-proposal with would it work if or how about, and leave the door open.
