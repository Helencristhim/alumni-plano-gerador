Chapter 3: The Code

The Small Word Goes to the End

Who, whose and which, and a preposition that moves

Grammar Discovery

Where Does the Small Word Go?

"Who does the final decision depend on?"

"Which department does the contract go through?"

"Whose budget does the money come from?"

"Which vendors are you comparing us with?"

In Portuguese the small word comes first: de quem depende? com quem compara? Where does it go in English? And what do whose and which ask about?

JobFormExample
Verb + prepositionquestion word ... verb + preposition at the endWho does it depend on?
Common ones in salesdepend on · go through · report to · compare with · come fromWho do you report to?
The owner of somethingwhose + nounWhose approval do you need?
A choice from known optionswhich + nounWhich vendors are on the shortlist?
The person who decideswho + verb (no do)Who signs the contract?
Softer, in a real callCan I ask + question word + normal orderCan I ask whose budget it comes from?
Whose vs who'swhose = de quem · who's = who isWho's on the committee? Whose team is it?
Neverpreposition at the start, Portuguese styleNot on who does it depend? · Not of who is the budget?

In one line: in a spoken question, the preposition goes to the end; whose asks about the owner, which asks about a choice.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Fifteen Minutes, Four Answers

One call, two recordings, one deal map

Dialogue

Giulia, Tuesday at Three

G
I read your page. My technical lead liked the SAP connector.
F
Great to hear. Can I ask who the final decision depends on?
G
On our managing director, but there is a small buying committee: me, IT and finance.
F
That helps. And whose budget would it come from, yours or finance's?
G
Finance. Anything above fifty thousand needs their approval.
F
Understood. Which other vendors are you comparing us with, if you do not mind me asking?
G
Two. We have a shortlist of three, and a decision in March. Data in Europe is a deal-breaker for us.
F
Our data stays in Frankfurt, so that is fine. What is the March date based on, and who should I talk to in finance?
Comprehension

About Giulia

1. Who does the final decision depend on at Giulia's company, and who else is involved?
On the managing director, with a small buying committee: Giulia, IT and finance.
2. Whose budget does the money come from, and what is the rule?
Finance's. Anything above fifty thousand needs their approval.
3. How many vendors are on Giulia's shortlist, and what is her deal-breaker?
Three, with a decision in March. Data stored outside Europe is a deal-breaker.
Listening 1 · The Sales Director

The Most Expensive Mistake

A sales director on qualifying a deal early. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he call the most expensive mistake in sales?
A long conversation with the wrong person.
2. What are the four things you find out when you qualify?
Who decides and who else it depends on; whose budget the money comes from; the timeline and what the date is based on; and which other vendors are on the shortlist.
3. Why does he say these are not rude questions?
Because, asked politely, they show that you respect the buyer's time.
Listening 2 · Procurement

The Person Vendors Forget

A procurement manager on what vendors should ask her. Sound first — no text.

1. What usually happens with vendors and procurement, in her experience?
Vendors spend weeks with finance, and the contract arrives on her desk when they have never heard her name.
2. What is always the deal-breaker for her company?
Data protection.
3. What do the vendors she likes ask in the second meeting?
What the process looks like and how long each step usually takes, so they plan with her instead of going around her.

Three lines still have a question mark, and each one is a question for the next call. A map with no question marks after two calls is rare; a map that nobody fills in is the reason deals slip.
1. Turn the decision-maker line into a question for the next call.
For example: Could you tell me who your managing director is, and whether I could meet him or her in February?
2. Turn the timeline line into a question with a preposition at the end.
What is the March date based on?
3. Why is Giulia the champion but not the decision-maker?
Because she likes the product and defends it inside, but the final decision depends on the managing director and the money on finance.

What I Can Do Now

I can qualify a deal in the first two calls: who decides, budget, timeline, competitors.
I put the preposition at the end of a spoken question: who does it depend on?

I ask permission before the sensitive questions, so the buyer does not feel interrogated.
I know the words: to qualify, a decision-maker, a budget holder, a champion, a buying committee, procurement, a shortlist, a timeline, a deal-breaker, approval.
