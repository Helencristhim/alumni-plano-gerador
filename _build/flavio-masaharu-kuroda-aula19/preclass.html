Lesson 19 -- Pre-class

Qualifying the Deal -- Who Decides, When and With What Budget

Fifteen minutes with a partner who likes the product: find out who decides, whose budget it is, the timeline and the competition. Key words: to qualify, a decision-maker, a budget holder, a champion, a buying committee, procurement, a shortlist, a timeline, a deal-breaker, approval. Structure: questions ending in a preposition, whose and which.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To qualify -- to find out if a possible deal is real and worth your time
"Qualify the deal in the first two calls, not after the demo."
A decision-maker -- the person who makes the final choice
"Giulia likes us, but she is not the decision-maker."
A budget holder -- the person who controls the money for a purchase
"The budget holder is the finance director, not the IT team."
A champion -- a person inside the buyer's company who wants you to win
"Without a champion inside, the deal goes nowhere."
A buying committee -- a group of people who decide together what to buy
"The buying committee has five people from three departments."
Procurement -- the department that buys things and negotiates the contracts
"Every contract goes through procurement before it is signed."
A shortlist -- the short list of final options a buyer is comparing
"We are on the shortlist with two other vendors."
A timeline -- the plan of dates for each step until a decision
"Their timeline says a decision in March."
A deal-breaker -- one condition that, if it is not met, ends the deal
"For them, data stored outside Europe is a deal-breaker."
Approval -- official permission to go ahead with something
"Anything above fifty thousand needs the CFO's approval."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A buyer who likes your product is good news, but it is not a deal. To qualify the deal, you ask four questions early: who decides, whose money it is, when, and against whom. In Portuguese the small word comes first: de quem depende? In English, a spoken question puts the preposition at the end: Who does the decision depend on?

The same happens with many verbs that sales people use every day: Which department does the contract go through? Who do you report to? Which vendors are you comparing us with? What is the date based on? Putting the preposition at the start, like on who does it depend, sounds like a translation.

To ask about the owner of something, use whose + noun: whose budget would it come from? Whose approval do you need? Do not confuse it with who's, which means who is. To ask about a choice between known options, use which + noun: which vendors are on the shortlist?

These questions are not rude if you ask permission first. Can I ask who the final decision depends on? If you do not mind me asking, which vendors are you comparing us with? A deal you qualify early is a deal you can forecast.

1. Which question is correct in spoken English?
A "On who does the decision depend, and who is the person who signs?"
B "Who does the decision depend on?"
C "Who depends the decision?"
2. You want to know who owns the budget. You ask:
A "Whose budget would it come from?"
B "Of who is the budget that the money will come from in the end?"
C "Who's budget would it come from?"
3. Why does the text say these questions are not rude?

Quiz

A "Who's budget is it?"

A "Why are you looking at other vendors when you already know that ours is the best one?"
