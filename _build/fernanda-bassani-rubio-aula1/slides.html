Chapter 3: The Code

I Work, She Works

One tense for your job and your routine

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Show?

"I deal with global car makers."

"Daniel works in Detroit."

"I don’t travel every month."

"Wei doesn’t speak Portuguese."

Look at the blue verbs. With I, the verb is simple: deal. With Daniel (he), the verb gets an -s: works. For the negative, look at the red words: don’t and doesn’t.

JobFormExample
I / you / we / theyverbI deal with global car makers.
he / she / itverb + sDaniel works in Detroit.
Negative: I / you / we / theydon’t + verbI don’t travel every month.
Negative: he / she / itdoesn’t + verb (no s)Wei doesn’t speak Portuguese.
Your job titleI am + jobI am a commercial manager.
The trapno -s after doesn’tNot he doesn’t works. Say he doesn’t work.

In one line: with he and she the verb gets an -s; after don’t and doesn’t the verb is simple.

Chapter 4: On the Call

The First Minute of the Call

One call, two voices, one profile card

Dialogue

The First Minute of the Call

D
Good morning, everyone. I am Daniel Brooks. I am the new global account manager, and I am based in Detroit.
F
Good morning, Daniel. Nice to meet you. I am Fernanda, from HB Fuller in Brazil.
D
Nice to meet you too, Fernanda. What do you do there?
F
I am a commercial manager. I am in charge of the automotive segment, and I deal with our global car makers.
D
Great. So you are our main supplier contact in South America.
F
Yes, that is right. Your company is one of our key accounts.
D
Perfect. Just one thing: I am in a different time zone. I start work at eight, Detroit time.
F
No problem. I join a call with Germany at seven, so the morning works for me.
Comprehension

About Daniel

1. Where is Daniel based?
In Detroit.
2. What is Daniel’s new job?
He is the new global account manager.
3. What time does Daniel start work?
At eight, Detroit time.

Anna Opens the Call
