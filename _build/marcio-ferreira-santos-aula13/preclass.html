Lesson 13 -- Pre-class

The Decision I Would Have Made Differently

Talking about a decision that went wrong without losing authority. Key words: hindsight, post-mortem, oversight, blind spot, root cause, to backfire, to course-correct, to second-guess. Structure: third conditional and past modals -- the past that did not happen.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Hindsight -- the clear understanding you only get after something has happened
"In hindsight, we should have piloted in one region first."
Post-mortem -- a meeting after a project ends, to learn what to repeat and what to avoid
"The post-mortem is not about blame. It is about the next project."
Oversight -- a mistake you make because you did not notice something
"Excluding the second market was an oversight, not a decision."
Blind spot -- an area you keep missing because you cannot see it from where you sit
"Regional pricing was our blind spot for two quarters."
Root cause -- the real reason behind a problem, under the visible symptom
"The root cause was the data, not the model."
To backfire -- to produce the opposite result of the one you wanted
"The discount backfired: volume rose and margin collapsed."
To course-correct -- to change direction while the work is still running
"We could have course-corrected in March if we had read the weekly data."
To second-guess -- to doubt a decision after it has already been made
"I do not second-guess the call. I would simply take more time now."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

The post-mortem started with one rule: decisions, not people. In March the team had signed off on a rollout in four regions at once. If they had piloted in one region first, they would have caught the problem three months earlier. Regional pricing had never been in the model, and nobody had noticed it was missing, so it was an oversight rather than a bad call. It stayed a blind spot until June. In hindsight, the discount was the wrong tool: it backfired, because volume rose and margin fell at the same time. They could have course-corrected in April if anybody had read the weekly numbers, and somebody in finance must have seen them, because the figures were in the shared folder. But the discount was only the symptom. The root cause was the data. Nobody in the room second-guessed the March decision, because with the information available in March it was reasonable. What they changed was the process: today nobody signs off on a rollout without a pilot.

1. Why is it "If they had piloted" and never "If they would have piloted"?
A The if clause takes had plus the participle. Would have belongs in the other half of the sentence.
B Because "pilot" is an irregular verb.
C Because the sentence is about the future.
2. What does "somebody in finance must have seen them" mean?
A Somebody in finance was obliged to look at them.
B The speaker is almost certain that somebody in finance saw them.
C Nobody in finance saw them.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "They could course-corrected in April."
B "They could have course-corrected in April."
C "They could have course-correct in April."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Third Conditional and Past Modals

GRAMMAR

One structure, and it is the one Brazilian speakers get wrong most often in English.

StructureRuleExample
Third conditionalIf + had + participle, then would have + participle. The past did not happen.If we had tested it, we would have caught it.
Never "would" after ifThe if clause takes had, never would have. Same rule as the first and second conditional.never "if we would have tested"
Regretshould have + participle: the right thing, and it was not done.We should have piloted first.
Possibilitycould have + participle: it was possible, and it did not happen.We could have course-corrected in April.
Deduction and speechmust have + participle: you are almost certain about the past. Cannot have + participle is the opposite. In speech, all of these contract: should've, could've, would've, must've. Never drop the have.
