Chapter 3: The Code

What You Say When the Source Is Not You

Reporting verbs in the passive

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"It is said that the panel sat for a single morning."

"He is believed to be the only arbitrator with criminal-law experience."

"The money is believed to have moved through four jurisdictions."

"Three players are reported to have been cleared months after the season."

In all four the speaker is reporting something he did not see. But one of them starts with it and three start with the person, and in two of them something extra happens to the second verb. What, and why?

FormWhat it doesExample
it is said that + clauseIt + is/was + past participle + that + a full sentence. Nobody is named, the claim goes on the record, and the writer has not signed it. This one always works."It is said that the panel sat for a single morning."
subject + is said to + verbMove the person to the front and the reported verb becomes to + the bare verb. Same time as the reporting. This is the form a professional produces, and the one your first language never suggests, because it would keep a full clause after the verb instead."He is believed to be the only arbitrator with criminal-law experience."
subject + is said to have + participleThe event happened before anybody reported it, so the second half travels back: to have + past participle. The reporting verb stays in the present. This is the one that collapses under pressure."The money is believed to have moved through four jurisdictions."
subject + is said to have been + participleEarlier and done to somebody: the passive goes inside the infinitive. Long, and worth drilling, because it is how every clearance and every suspension is reported."Three players are reported to have been cleared months after the season."
choosing the verb is choosing the distancesay, believe, think, report, allege, claim, understand, know, expect, consider. It is alleged stands further back than it is reported, and both stand much further back than an active sentence with a name in front of it."It is alleged that the federation paid for the panel."

Your first language has no personal version of this at all. It stays impersonal whatever you do to it, so he is said to have taken never arrives on its own. It has to be built deliberately, a few hundred times, until it does.

Register

Choosing How Far to Stand Back

Same fact, four distances

"The federation funds the panel."

You are saying it. If it turns out to be wrong, it is your mistake.

"It is understood that the federation funds the panel."

You know it from somebody who would know, and you are not naming them.

"It is reported that the federation funds the panel."

Somebody has published it. You are passing it on, not confirming it.

"It is alleged that the federation funds the panel."

Somebody claims it, nothing is proved, and you are standing as far back as English lets you.

Nothing changed about the fact. What changed is how much of it you are prepared to carry. In a memo that choice is read as a legal position, so make it on purpose rather than by accident.

It Was Never a Choice Between This and a Perfect Trial

An arbitrator on why sport decides its own disputes. Sound first — no text.

1. What two reasons does she give for the system existing?
Speed and uniformity.
2. What does she say happens when an ordinary judge decides a transfer dispute?
It can take three years, and by then the season it mattered in is history.
3. What does she say the real alternative to her tribunal was?
Not a perfect trial, but twenty-eight different national answers to the same question.
Listening 2

The Brown Envelope Nobody Has Ever Seen

An integrity investigator on what a fixed match actually looks like. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say the first sign of a fixed match usually is?
Betting data: a market moving at three in the morning, before anything has happened on the pitch.
2. Why do most files die in the slow part?
The money is believed to have moved through four jurisdictions and they can compel disclosure in none of them.
3. What does he say the headlines get wrong?
They report people as charged when they have only been provisionally suspended while questions are asked.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I can use it is said that with a full clause when I do not want to name a source.
I can move the person to the front: he is believed to be, they are said to have.
I can use to have plus a participle when the event is earlier than the report.
I can build the passive inside the infinitive: reported to have been cleared.
I have fourteen words for how a sport writes, applies and enforces its own rules.
