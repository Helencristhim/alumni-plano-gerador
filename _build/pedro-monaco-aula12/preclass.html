Lesson 12 -- Pre-class

The Rules of the Game

How professional sport writes, applies and enforces its own rules -- and what you say when the source is not you. Key expressions: a governing body, a sanction, arbitration, a hearing, eligibility, a code of conduct, to hand down, to be cleared, a level playing field, an integrity unit, to strip somebody of, due process, a provisional suspension, match-fixing. Structure: reporting verbs in the passive -- it is said that, he is believed to be, and the form that reaches back in time, is reported to have been cleared.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. All fourteen belong to a legal system that is not a legal system: the private machinery a sport uses to judge its own.

A governing body -- the organisation that writes the rules of a sport and enforces them
"The governing body writes the rulebook, applies it and pays for the panel that reads it."
A sanction -- an official punishment imposed for breaking a rule
"The sanction was two years, reduced to fourteen months on the second reading."
Arbitration -- a private process in which a neutral panel decides a dispute instead of a court
"The clause sends every dispute to arbitration in Lausanne, whatever the passport says."
A hearing -- the meeting at which both sides put their case before a decision is made
"The hearing lasted a morning and the file behind it had taken nine months."
Eligibility -- the conditions you have to meet before you are allowed to compete
"His eligibility was never in doubt. The paperwork behind it was."
A code of conduct -- the written rules about how people in an organisation must behave
"The code of conduct is four pages and the commentary on it is ninety."
To hand down -- to issue an official decision or punishment from a position of authority
"The panel will hand down its decision three weeks after the hearing."
To be cleared -- to be officially declared not to have done anything wrong
"He was cleared in March, by which time the season he cared about was over."
A level playing field -- a situation in which everybody competes under the same conditions
"The whole point of a rulebook is a level playing field, or the appearance of one."
An integrity unit -- the department that investigates cheating and corruption inside a sport
"The integrity unit has investigators and no power at all to compel a bank."
To strip somebody of -- to take a title or an award away from somebody officially
"They can strip somebody of a title four years after the medal was handed over."
Due process -- the proper set of steps that has to be followed before anybody is punished
"Speed is what sport wants and due process is what it pays with."
A provisional suspension -- a temporary ban that applies while a case is still being decided
"A provisional suspension is not a finding, although it reads like one in the headline."
Match-fixing -- the crime of arranging the result of a game before it is played
"Match-fixing cases almost always begin with betting data, never with a witness."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Legal writing and sports journalism share one problem: most of what they report, the writer did not see. English has a specific machine for that, and it has two settings. The impersonal one is it is said that: It is said that the panel sat for a single morning. Nobody is named, the claim is on the record, and the writer has not signed it.

The second setting starts from the person instead. You move the subject to the front and the reported verb becomes an infinitive: He is believed to be the only arbitrator with criminal-law experience. This is the version that sounds professional, and it is the one a Brazilian speaker almost never produces, because the first language keeps a full clause after the verb and there is nothing to copy.

Time is where it breaks. If the event happened before the reporting, the second verb takes to have plus a participle: The money is believed to have moved through four jurisdictions. And if the event was also done to somebody, the passive goes inside: Three players are reported to have been cleared months after the season ended. The reporting verb stays in the present; only the second half travels back.

The verbs that work this way are the ones your files are full of: say, believe, think, report, allege, claim, understand, know, expect, consider. Choosing between them is a decision about distance. It is alleged stands further back than it is reported, and both stand much further back than an active sentence with a name in front of it.

1. Why would a lawyer write "it is alleged that they took the money" rather than "they took the money"?
A Because the passive voice is more polite in English.
B Because it puts the claim on the record without the writer owning it.
C Because the active voice is not allowed in legal writing.
2. Which sentence says the moving happened before anybody reported it?
A The money is believed to move through four jurisdictions.
B The money is believed to have moved through four jurisdictions.
C The money is believed that it moved through four jurisdictions.
3. What happens to the subject in the personal version?
A It moves to the front and the reported verb becomes an infinitive.
B It disappears and is replaced by it.
C It stays where it is and a that-clause follows.
4. Which one is right for something earlier that was done TO the players?
A Three players are reported to be cleared months after the season.
B Three players are reported to have been cleared months after the season.
C Three players are reported that they were cleared months after the season.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reporting Verbs in the Passive

GRAMMAR

Portuguese only has the impersonal version of this, so the personal one -- he is said to have taken -- never arrives on its own. It has to be built on purpose until it stops feeling like translation.

PatternWhen it happensExample
it is said that + clauseIt + is/was + past participle + that + a full sentence. Nobody is named, the claim goes on the record, and the writer has not signed it. This one always works."It is said that the panel sat for a single morning."
subject + is said to + verbMove the person to the front and the reported verb becomes to + the bare verb. Same time as the reporting. This is the form a professional produces, and the one your first language never suggests, because it would keep a full clause after the verb instead."He is believed to be the only arbitrator with criminal-law experience."
subject + is said to have + participleThe event happened before anybody reported it, so the second half travels back: to have + past participle. The reporting verb stays in the present. This is the one that collapses under pressure."The money is believed to have moved through four jurisdictions."
subject + is said to have been + participleEarlier and done to somebody: the passive goes inside the infinitive. Long, and worth drilling, because it is how every clearance and every suspension is reported."Three players are reported to have been cleared months after the season."
choosing the verb is choosing the distancesay, believe, think, report, allege, claim, understand, know, expect, consider. It is alleged stands further back than it is reported, and both stand much further back than an active sentence with a name in front of it."It is alleged that the federation paid for the panel."

One habit for this week: open any English-language report about a case you are following and underline every reporting verb. Then write, next to each one, whether the event happened before the reporting or at the same time. That single question is what decides the form.

Stage 2: Put the Sequence in Order

Order

Listen to how a disciplinary case runs from the first alert to the final award, then put the steps in order.

?An alert is raised, usually by data rather than by a person, and the governing body opens a file.
?The athlete is notified and, under most codes, provisionally suspended while the file is built.
?A hearing is held, on paper or in a room, and both sides put their case.
?A first-instance panel hands down a decision, and either a sanction is imposed or the athlete is cleared.
?Within twenty-one days, either side may take the whole thing to arbitration in Lausanne, where a final award is made.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are on the record about somebody else. Choose the version a professional would actually write.

1. A journalist asks whether your client has been charged. You report what has happened without confirming guilt:
A It is said he to have been charged.
B He is reported to have been provisionally suspended.
C He is reported that he was provisionally suspended.
2. You want to say that the money moved before anybody looked into it:
A The money is believed to move through four jurisdictions.
B The money is believed to have moved through four jurisdictions.
C The money is believed moving through four jurisdictions.
3. You are writing the first line of a memo and you do not want to name the source:
A It is understood that the panel will sit in March.
B It is understood the panel to sit in March.
C Is understood that the panel will sit in March.
4. You want to report an allegation about a person, starting from the person:
A He is alleged that he broke the code of conduct.
B He is alleged to have broken the code of conduct.
C He is alleged breaking the code of conduct.
5. The decision came out last week and you are reporting what you heard:
A The decision is believed to be handed down last week.
B The decision is believed to have been handed down last week.
C The decision is believed being handed down last week.
