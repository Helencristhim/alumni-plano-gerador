Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Eight restaurant words. Listen to each one and read the example out loud.

Recommend -- to tell somebody that something is good and they should try it
"The waiter recommended the moussaka."
Receipt -- the piece of paper that shows what you paid
"Ask for the receipt and check it."
Portion -- the amount of food they put on one plate
"It is one enormous portion, so we shared it."
Substitute -- something you get instead of what you asked for
"They brought a substitute: yoghurt with honey."
Allergy -- when your body reacts badly to a food
"I have an allergy to nuts, so I say it first."
Complain -- to say that something is wrong and you are not happy
"I did not want to complain, but the plate was wrong."
Available -- there, and ready for you to have
"The pistachio dessert is not available tonight."
Request -- to ask for something, in a polite or formal way
"Could I request a table by the window?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the advice. The words in bold are your plan B, said before you need it.

Here is what I tell my cousin before every trip. Say the difficult thing FIRST, at the door, before you sit down. If you have an allergy, you will need one sentence, and you will need it in the first thirty seconds.

Then it gets easy. If the table isn't ready, you 'll wait outside. If the dish you want isn't available, you 'll ask what they recommend. If the portion is enormous, you 'll share it. And if the plate comes out wrong, you 'll say so in one sentence — you do not have to complain, you just have to say it.

"If the table isn't ready, you'll wait outside." Is this a dream or a real possibility?
A Real — it can easily happen tonight.
B A dream, like winning the lottery.
C Something that already happened.
Why is it "if the table isn't ready" and not "if the table won't be ready"?
A Because it is about the past.
B After if we use the present, even when we mean the future.
C Both are fine.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

First conditional — your plan B

For things that can really happen, tonight, in that restaurant.

  • If + present simple  →  will / 'll + verb
  • "If the table isn't ready, we'll wait outside."
  • "If they don't have it, I'll try something else."
  • The other half can be an instruction: "If you have an allergy, say it first."

The trap. Never will after if. Not "if the table will not be ready" — the if half stays in the present, even though you are talking about later.

And the politeness scale

  • "Give me..." — rude, do not use it
  • "I want..." — sounds like a child
  • "Could I have..." — this is the one you want
  • "I was wondering if I could..." — very formal, for special requests

Speaking

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
You are ordering. Which one would you actually say to a waiter?
A "Give me the fish."
B "Could I have the fish, please?"
C "I want the fish now."
The table is not ready. Which sentence is correct?
A "If the table will not be ready, we will wait."
B "If the table isn't ready, we'll wait outside."
C "If the table is not ready, we waiting outside."
They brought the wrong plate. What do you say first?
A "This is wrong! I did not order this!"
B "Sorry, I think this is the wrong plate. I ordered the fish."
C Nothing — you eat it to avoid the conversation.
You have an allergy. When do you say it?
A At the door or before you order.
B After the food arrives.
C Only if they ask you.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Think about a restaurant you know well. In about ninety seconds: order a whole meal out loud — a drink, a starter, a main — using Could I have. Then give three plan-B sentences with if: what you will do if the table is not ready, if your dish is not available, and if the plate comes out wrong. Finish with the sentence about your own allergies, or about a food you cannot eat.
