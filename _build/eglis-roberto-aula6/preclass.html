Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

Where Would You Live? -- The Life You Could Have

How to talk about an imaginary life in another country, what you would gain and what you would lose, and what you wish were different. Key words: to move abroad, to settle down, homesick, the cost of living, to fit in, roots, a dual citizen, a lifestyle, red tape, to miss out on. Structure: if plus the past form, then would, for an imaginary situation now; I wish plus the past form for what you would like to change.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To move abroad -- to go and live in another country
"They moved abroad when their kids finished school."
To settle down -- to start living in one place for a long time, with a stable life
"After years of traveling, he settled down near the beach."
Homesick -- sad because you are far from your home and your people
"In the first winter in Milan, I was very homesick."
The cost of living -- how much money you need for a normal life in a place
"The cost of living in Lisbon has gone up a lot."
To fit in -- to feel that you belong in a group or a place
"It took her two years to fit in with the locals."
Roots -- the place, the family and the culture you come from
"My roots are in Italy, but my life is in Brazil."
A dual citizen -- a person who has the nationality of two countries
"As a dual citizen, he can work anywhere in Europe."
A lifestyle -- the way a person lives: habits, rhythm and choices
"I love the slow lifestyle of small towns."
Red tape -- too many official papers and rules that make things slow
"Moving to a new country means a lot of red tape."
To miss out on -- to not have a good experience that other people have
"If you never travel, you miss out on so much."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Carla lives in Rio, but her grandparents came from Spain, and she has a Spanish passport. Her friends always ask: why don’t you go? “If I lived in Madrid, I would probably earn more,” she says. “And I would travel around Europe every weekend.” She does not live in Madrid. It is an imaginary situation, so she uses if plus the past form, and then would.

“But if I moved, I would miss my mother and the beach. I wouldn’t know anybody.” The past form here does not talk about the past. It talks about now, but in a world that is not real.

There are also things she would like to change in Rio. “I wish the city were safer, and I wish I had more time for the beach.” After I wish, she uses the past form too: were, had, even when she talks about today.

"If I lived in Madrid, I would earn more." Does Carla live in Madrid?
A No. It is an imaginary situation.
B Yes, she lives there now.
C She lived there in the past.
Why does she say "If I moved" and not "If I would move"?
A Because move is an irregular verb.
B Because she moved last year.
C Because would goes in the other part of the sentence, not after if.
"I wish I had more time for the beach." What does it mean?
A She has a lot of time now.
B She does not have much time now, and she would like more.
C She had more time in the past.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- If, Would and I Wish

GRAMMAR

Imaginary now: if plus the past form, then would. A wish about now: I wish plus the past form.

JobFormExample
Imaginary situation nowIf + past, would + verbIf I lived in Italy, I would eat better.
The other orderwould + verb + if + pastI would move if my family came with me.
Be, for every personIf I / he / she wereIf I were younger, I would go.
AdviceIf I were you, I wouldIf I were you, I’d try it for a year.
A wish about nowI wish + past formI wish my kids lived closer.
Short form in speechI would = I’d, would not = wouldn’tI’d miss the food. I wouldn’t go alone.

Never: If I live in Italy, I would · If I would live there · I wish my family lives closer · I wish I can travel more
