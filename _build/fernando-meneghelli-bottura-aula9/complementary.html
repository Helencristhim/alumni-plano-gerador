Lesson 9 -- The Loaded Question

Grammar Video
"Be used to and get used to" -- BBC Learning English, English Class

The two halves of the family that advanced speakers still mix up: the state you are in, and the process of getting there. Connection to Lesson 9: "I used to find that difficult and I am used to it now" answers half the hostile questions in any process.

Tip: the trap is the -ing. After "be used to" and "get used to", the next word ends in -ing. Build five sentences about your own career out loud and check every one of them for that ending.

Talk with transcript
"How to disagree productively and find common ground" -- Julia Dhar, TED (11 min)

A world debate champion on how to disagree without the other person closing down. Connection to Lesson 9: her first technique is the one this lesson is built on — say out loud the part of the other position you accept, before you say anything else. Full transcript beside the video.

Tip: take the three hostile questions you least want to be asked and write only the first sentence of each answer, using her technique. First sentences only. Then say them out loud until they sound calm rather than rehearsed.

Interview under pressure
"Julius Malema" -- BBC HARDtalk, full interview (24 min)

Twenty-four minutes of loaded questions asked back to back by an interviewer who does not accept a first answer. Watch purely as technique: where the guest concedes, where he stands his ground, where he deflects — and how visible each of the three is from the outside.

Tip: pause after each question and label it out loud before he answers: concede, stand your ground, or deflect. Then compare with what he actually does. You are training the eye you will need on yourself.
