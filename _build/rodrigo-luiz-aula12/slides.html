Chapter 3: The Pattern

Two Words at Every Table

some · any

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

I'd like some bread, please.
Do you have any fish today?
We don't have any oysters today.
Would you like some dessert?

Two say SOME. Two say ANY. What is different?

UseFormExample
You can count ita + one, or a number + -sa menu, two starters, three desserts
You cannot count itno a, no -sbread, wine, water, rice, soup
Ask for it (+)some + food or drinkI'd like some bread, please.
Question (?) and negative (−)any + food or drinkDo you have any fish? — We don't have any oysters.
Careful"a bread" and "a wine" do not exist. Say some bread, a piece of bread, a glass of wine. And in an OFFER we say some, not any: "Would you like some dessert?"
The Three Sentences of a Dinner

Three Sentences and Dinner Is Yours

"I'd like some bread, please."

1 — SOME, to ask for what you cannot count

"Do you have any fish today?"

2 — ANY, to ask if they have it

"I'd like a glass of wine, please."

3 — A GLASS OF, to count what you cannot count
