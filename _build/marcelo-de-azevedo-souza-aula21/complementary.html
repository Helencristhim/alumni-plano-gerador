Lesson 21 -- The Art of the Story

Lesson theme: telling a travel anecdote with a scene, a twist and a punchline, and looking forward from the past (was going to, was about to, was supposed to, would). Extra practice outside class -- tick each one when you finish it.

Film
Big Fish (2003) -- directed by Tim Burton

A son tries to find the truth behind his father's famous stories, which the father has embellished for forty years. A whole film about how a story is told, and why people hang on every word of a great storyteller. The father's scenes are slow and clear, full of "I was about to" and "I had no idea it would".

Tip: after each of the father's stories, pause and retell it in one minute: the scene, the twist, the punchline.

NPR Life Kit -- How to tell a good story

The directors of The Moth, the most famous storytelling show in America, explain how to choose a story, find its one-sentence spine and decide where to start. Free to stream, with the full transcript on the same page, in natural American English at real speed.

Tip: listen once without the transcript, then write your Albanian story as one sentence, the way they suggest.

The clues to a great story -- Andrew Stanton (TED)

The writer of Toy Story and WALL-E on what makes an audience care: a promise at the start, and a reason to keep listening. He opens with a joke that is a perfect anecdote in itself, set up, twist and punchline in under two minutes.

Tip: watch the first two minutes twice, then tell the opening joke to someone in your own words.
