Chapter 3: The Code

Looking Forward from the Past

was going to · was about to · was supposed to · would

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

All four sentences are about the past. But where is the highlighted part looking?

A. "We were going to cross the pass that afternoon, but the rain came."

B. "I was about to turn back when a shepherd appeared."

C. "We were supposed to reach Theth by six."

D. "I had no idea that night would become my favorite memory."

Grammar Rule

The Future, Seen from the Past

FormYou use it forExample
was / were going to + verba plan in the past, often one that changedWe were going to cross the pass that afternoon.
was / were about to + verbthe moment just before something almost happenedI was about to turn back when a shepherd appeared.
was / were supposed to + verbwhat was expected or arranged, but did not happenWe were supposed to reach Theth by six.
would + verbthe future, seen from a moment in the pastI had no idea that night would change the trip.

Key idea: stand at a moment in the past and look forward. This is the engine of every good anecdote: the listener hears the plan, and then waits for it to go wrong. After about to and supposed to, always use the base verb: "about to turn", never "about to turning".

Grammar Practice

Choose the Form

Say your answer out loud first, then tap to check.

So, are you ready? Tell it to me the way you are going to tell it on the show. Set the scene first.
M
Right. It was my second week in Albania. We were supposed to reach Theth by six, but at four o'clock we were still climbing, and the rain was coming in sideways.
I
Good, that is enough scene. Now please do not ramble about the bike. What happened next?
M
I was about to turn back. Honestly, I had already turned the bike around. And then, out of the blue, a shepherd came down the path with a mule.
I
That is your twist. Stop there for one second. A pause right there will keep people in suspense. And the punchline?

He walked us over the pass in the dark and refused to take any money. I had no idea that night would become the best memory of the trip. It took me a year to see the funny side of the rain.

Self-Assessment

What I Learned

I use "was going to" for a plan in the past, often one that changed.
I use "was about to" for something that almost happened.
I use "was supposed to" for what was expected but did not happen.
I use "would" to look forward from the past: "I knew it would rain".
I can tell a travel story with a scene, a twist, a pause and a punchline.
