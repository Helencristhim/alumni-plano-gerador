Chapter 3: Read the News

Three Weeks Later

Read for the main idea

Read for the Main Idea

The Strait Everybody Is Watching

The Strait That Everybody Is Watching

The strait, which carries about a fifth of the world's oil, has been partly closed for eleven days. The first number to move was shipping insurance, which has more than doubled since the beginning of the month. That is the part of the story that reaches the headlines. The part that reaches companies is slower: manufacturers whose factories depend on parts from Asia are reporting delays of two to three weeks, and the outlook for the next quarter is described by almost every analyst as volatile, which is the word people use when nobody knows.

None of this looks like a human resources problem, and that is exactly why it becomes one. The plants that are trying to catch up will do it with overtime, and the teams whose managers have already been pushing them for a quarter are the teams that will lose people first. Companies that are bracing for a difficult quarter usually freeze hiring, which is the moment when the workload of everybody who stays goes up. The news that moves a business is rarely about the business. It is about the pressure that arrives three weeks later, in a building nobody was watching.

Chapter 4: The Code

Two Facts, One Sentence

The clause that carries the second half

Grammar Discovery

Why Do Two of These Have Commas?

The strait, which carries a fifth of the world's oil, has been closed.
The factories whose parts come from Asia are reporting delays.
Companies that are bracing for a hard quarter freeze hiring.
Shipping insurance, which was the first number to move, has doubled.
Click to reveal the rule
A defining relative clause says WHICH one, so the sentence needs it: the factories whose parts come from Asia — without it you do not know which factories. No commas, and you can use that or which for things, who for people, whose for possession, where for places. A non-defining clause adds extra information to something already identified: the strait, which carries a fifth of the world's oil,. It goes between commas, and after a comma you can never use that — only which or who. The test: remove the clause. If the sentence still points at the right thing, it takes commas.

TWO SENTENCES · NOT FIVE

Ten minutes before the meeting, by the coffee machine

The Breakfast Conversation

M
Did you see the shipping story this morning?
A
I did. The strait, which carries about a fifth of the world's oil, has been closed for eleven days now.
M
Everyone is talking about the oil price. I am not sure it changes anything for us.
A
Not this month. But the plants whose parts come from Asia are already reporting delays, and a plant that is behind runs on overtime.
M
And overtime is your line, not mine.
A
It is. And the teams whose managers have been pushing them since January are the ones we lose first.
M
What is the outlook, then? Give me your honest one.
A
Volatile, which is the word people use when nobody knows. What I would watch is the overtime line in the two plants we talked about last month.

At breakfast, the chief financial officer asks what you think about the shipping story.

  • Two sentences: what happened, and what it means for us.
  • One relative clause is enough to sound like you read more than the headline.

You want to identify which factories are affected, out of all the factories in the group.

  • This is a defining clause: no commas.
  • Whose works for possession: factories whose parts come from Asia.

You want to add, as extra information, that the strait carries a fifth of the world's oil.

  • Non-defining clause, with commas, and which never that.
  • The sentence still makes sense without it — that is the test.

Somebody says the story has nothing to do with your area.

  • Name the knock-on effect and the delay.
  • Overtime and hiring freeze are the two words that land.

You are asked what you expect for the next quarter and you genuinely do not know.

  • There is an adjective that says exactly that without sounding lost.
  • Follow it with the one thing you would watch.
