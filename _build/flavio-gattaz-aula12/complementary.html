BU Questrom -- "Letters of Recommendation & Work Experience" (7 min)

The admissions office of a Boston business school explaining what it wants from the two letters and from the work-experience section of the file. Seven minutes, from the people who actually read them, and it answers the question this lesson leaves you with: who should write yours.

Tip: after watching, write the names of your two recommenders and, next to each one, the exact result that person could substantiate with a figure.

Podcast
NPR Life Kit -- "Does your resume need a makeover?"

A career coach going line by line through what a resume should say and what it should stop saying. Almost every example she gives is a claim being turned into something a stranger can check, which is exactly the move this lesson asks of you.

Tip: listen with your own resume open. Every time she describes a weak line, find the equivalent line in yours and mark it. Those are the eight sentences for the homework.

Talk
TED -- Adam Galinsky, "How to speak up for yourself" (15 min)

A social psychologist on the narrow space in which a person can advocate for themselves without being read as arrogant. It is the problem behind every line of a resume and behind the phone call in which you ask somebody to vouch for you.

Tip: he describes a "range of acceptable behavior". Ask yourself where the two letters put you inside it, and whether one of them is doing work you should not be doing yourself.
