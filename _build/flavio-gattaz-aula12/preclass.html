Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A screening -- the first pass in which most files are removed without a conversation
"Two thirds of the files never reach a human conversation. That is the screening."
Boilerplate -- the standard wording that appears in every document of that kind and says nothing
"Hard-working, dedicated, a team player. It is all true and it is all boilerplate."
A claim -- a statement about yourself that the reader has no reason to believe yet
"Every line of a resume is a claim until somebody else confirms it."
To substantiate -- to back a statement with evidence that a stranger can check
"The claim was substantiated by two numbers and a date, which is all it needed."
Verifiable -- able to be checked by somebody who does not know you
"Twenty-five employees and three sites is verifiable. Strategic vision is not."
To be overlooked -- to be passed over without anybody actually deciding against you
"He was not rejected. He was overlooked, and those are different failures."
A recommender -- the person who writes a letter about you for a committee to read
"You choose your recommenders once, and the choice is half of the letter."
An endorsement -- a public statement that somebody is good at what they do
"An endorsement from a client is worth three from people who work for you."
To vouch for somebody -- to put your own name behind somebody else's claim
"I will vouch for the numbers. I was in the room when they were signed."
A shortcoming -- a weakness that an honest letter about you has to name
"The letter names one shortcoming, and that is the paragraph the committee believes."
To attribute something to somebody -- to say who a result belongs to, in writing
"The turnaround was attributed to the site team, and it should have been."
To be tasked with something -- to be given a job to do by somebody with the authority to give it
"In 2021 he was tasked with writing the pricing process down, and it took a year."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Open any resume, any recommendation letter, any committee record, and you will find page after page in which nobody does anything. The application was received. The file was reviewed. The candidate was shortlisted. That is the passive voice, and it is built out of two pieces and nothing else: a form of be, and a past participle. The object of the ordinary sentence moves to the front and becomes the subject. "My grandfather founded the company" becomes the company was founded by my grandfather.

The interesting part is the person who did it. In institutional writing that person usually disappears altogether, and it is not carelessness. "Most files are removed at the first pass" does not say who removes them, because nobody needs to know and no one person did it. This is the normal case, and it is why a document written in this register sounds impersonal: the doer has been left out on purpose. You put the name back with by only when the credit or the responsibility is the point of the sentence. The third project was turned around by his team puts the name back, and it does it to give the team the result.

Three traps. The first is the participle: it is was handed, not was hand, and was written, not was wrote. The second is the preposition after certain verbs: you are tasked with something, promoted to a position, appointed to a board. The third is the one Portuguese speakers walk into most often: a verb with no object cannot go into the passive at all. Things happen; they are never happened. In speech, English often replaces be with get: half of those files get overlooked. That belongs to conversation, not to the page a committee reads.

2. According to the text, when do you put a name back after "by"?

3. Which of these is the trap the text says Portuguese speakers walk into most often?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive Voice

GRAMMAR

The object comes to the front, the verb becomes be plus a participle, and the doer appears only on purpose.

JobFormExample
The basic shapebe + past participleThe site work was handed over.
When the doer matters... + by + personThe project was turned around by his team.
When the doer does notno by at all — the normal caseMost files are removed at the first pass.
Present perfect passivehas / have been + participleThe pricing process has been written down.
Fixed prepositionstasked with, promoted to, appointed toHe was tasked with the pricing brief.
Spoken, not writtenget + past participleHalf of those files get overlooked.
The trapa verb with no object has no passiveNot was happened. Say happened.

Never: was hand · was wrote · was promoted for director · was tasked to the pricing brief · the turnaround was happened.
