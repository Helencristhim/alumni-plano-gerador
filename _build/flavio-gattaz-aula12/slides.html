Chapter 4: The Code

Where Did the Person Go?

The passive voice, and the moment you put the name back

Grammar Discovery

What Moved to the Front?

"My grandfather founded the company in 1974." → "The company was founded by my grandfather in 1974."

"Somebody removes most files at the first pass." → "Most files are removed at the first pass."

"The board gave him the pricing brief in 2021." → "He was tasked with the pricing brief in 2021."

"His team turned the third project around." → "The third project was turned around by his team."

Everything in blue is the ordinary sentence. Everything in red is the one a document would print, and two things have happened: what was the object is now at the front, and the person who acted is either gone or moved to the end after by. In the second sentence nobody appears at all. In the fourth, somebody appears on purpose.

JobFormExample
The basic shapebe + past participleThe site work was handed over.
When the doer matters... + by + personThe project was turned around by his team.
When the doer does notno by at all — and this is the normal caseMost files are removed at the first pass.
The resume linewas tasked with / was appointed / was promoted toHe was tasked with the pricing brief.
Two objectsthe person can become the subjectHe was given the brief in 2021.
Spoken, not writtenget + past participleHalf of those files get overlooked.
The trapa verb with no object has no passiveNot the turnaround was happened. Say the turnaround happened.

In one line: the object comes to the front, the verb becomes be plus a participle, and the person appears only when you decide the credit is the point.

Chapter 5: Real Documents

The Sheet, and Two Readers

A screening record, an admissions reader and somebody who writes the letters

Real Document

The Screening Record

FILE RECORD — F. GATTAZ
internal · round 1 · not shared with the candidate
3 Septemberapplication received
9 Septemberfile reviewed by two readers
9 Septembertwo letters received — one client, one partner
12 Septemberheadcount and site data verified
15 Septembercandidate shortlisted for interview
15 Septemberrecommended by A. Prado, second reader
Reader’s note: the partner’s letter is boilerplate and was set aside. The client letter names one shortcoming and substantiates two claims with figures the office was able to check, and it is the reason this file was not overlooked.
1. Five of the six lines do not say who did the action. Which line names somebody, and why does that one need a name?
The last one, recommended by A. Prado, second reader. A recommendation belongs to a person, so the file records who is answerable for it.
2. According to the reader's note, what happened to the partner's letter and why?
It was set aside because it is boilerplate: standard wording that appears in every letter of that kind and says nothing about him.
3. What two things does the client's letter do that the other one does not?
It names one shortcoming, and it substantiates two claims with figures the office was able to check. The note says that is why the file was not overlooked.
Listening 1 · An Admissions Reader

Eleven Minutes With Your File

An admissions reader on what happens before anybody calls you. Sound first — no text.

1. How long does a file get on the first pass, and what is she looking for in that time?
About eleven minutes. She is looking for claims she can check, not for the best-written pages.
2. What does she say happens to a resume that is all adjectives?
It is boilerplate, and it gets overlooked rather than rejected. Nobody decides against it: it simply does not survive the screening.
3. What does she say about the sentences that make her stop?
They have a number, a date and a name in them, and she says a claim that can be verified is worth more than a claim that is well written.
Listening 2 · Somebody Who Writes Them

The Paragraph That Gets Believed

A manager on the letters he has written and the one sentence that makes them work. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say about the letters that praise everything?
That they are read as boilerplate and are worth nothing, because a letter with no limit in it has nobody's judgement in it.
2. Which paragraph does he say gets believed, and what does it contain?
The one that names a real shortcoming and says what the person did about it. He says the rest of the letter is believed because of that paragraph.
3. What advice does he give about choosing a recommender?
Choose somebody who can vouch for a specific result with figures, not the most senior person you know. Seniority without detail reads as a favour.
