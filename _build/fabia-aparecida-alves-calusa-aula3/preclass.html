Scope Before Price

Eight words from the proposal — scope of work, deliverable, credentials, onboard, fee structure, proposal, invoice, prospect — and the four small words that make an answer sound in control: already, just, yet, ever. Around fifteen minutes.

Stage 1.1: Words from the proposal

Vocabulary

These are the words that appear in every contract you already write in Portuguese. Tap Listen and say each one out loud.

Scope of work — exactly what is included in a service, and what is not
"Let’s define the scope of work before we talk about fees."
Deliverable — one concrete thing you promise to hand over, by a date
"The monthly payroll report is our first deliverable."
Credentials — the documents and proof that show you can do the job
"I’ve just sent you our credentials."
Onboard — to take a new client on and set everything up
"We’ve already onboarded twelve international clients."
Fee structure — how the price is built — per hour, per employee, or fixed
"Our fee structure depends on headcount, not on hours."
Proposal — the written offer: what you will do, and for how much
"Can I put a proposal together by Friday?"
Invoice — the document that asks a client to pay
"We issue one invoice a month, on the fifth."
Prospect — a company that might become a client — also the verb: to look for one
"That prospect has already asked for a proposal."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

A message from a prospect after the proposal. Read it once, then answer.

Hi Fábia,

I’ve already been through the proposal twice. The scope of work looks right and the fee structure makes sense — more than the other two we’ve seen.

Two problems. You’ve put six weeks to onboard us and we open in four. And head office hasn’t approved the budget yet, so I can’t sign this week.

Have you ever done a four-week onboarding? I’ve just forwarded your credentials to London, so they know who you are.

David
Has David read the proposal?
A Yes — twice, and sooner than you would expect.
B No — he is waiting for head office first.
Why "hasn’t approved the budget yet" and not "already"?
A Because head office will never approve it.
B Because it has not happened up to now, but it is still expected.
What does "I’ve just forwarded your credentials" tell you about when it happened?
A A moment ago — almost certainly today.
B Some time last month.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Four small words, four different messages

On a call, these are not grammar. They are tone.

already — sooner than expected. Goes between have and the verb.
"We’ve already onboarded twelve international clients." → this is proof.
just — a moment ago. Same position.
"I’ve just sent you our credentials." → this is momentum.
yet — not until now, but still expected. Goes at the end, and only in negatives and questions.
"We haven’t defined the scope yet." → this is a door left open.
ever — at any point in your life. Only in questions.
"Have you ever outsourced payroll before?" → this is discovery.

⚠ "Did you ever work with...?" is not wrong in American English, but on a first call "Have you ever worked with...?" keeps the door open to now. That is the one to use.

Stage 3: Say it out loud

Speaking

Tap Listen, then Record. Say we’ve and I’ve, not the long form — the short form is what buys you the calm tone.

Of course. It depends on the scope. May I ask two quick questions first?
We’ve already onboarded twelve international clients.
So the next step is a proposal by Friday. Does that work for you?

Stage 4: On the call

Quiz
A prospect opens with "just send us your rates". What do you say?
A "I’ll send them today." — and you still do not know what they need.
B "Of course. It depends on the scope — may I ask two quick questions first?"
They ask a tax question you are not sure about, live, on the call.
A "I’d rather check that than guess. Can I come back to you by Friday?"
B Give an approximate answer and correct it later if needed.
Which sentence is correct?
A "We have onboarded already twelve clients."
B "We have already onboarded twelve clients."

Stage 5: The price question

Reflection
Record ninety seconds in English. A prospect opens the call by asking how much it would cost, before telling you anything about their company. Answer: hold the price, ask your two questions, explain the fee structure without a number, and close with a next step and a date. Use already, just, yet or ever at least twice.
