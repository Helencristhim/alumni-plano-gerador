The Pattern

Four small words that change the answer

We have already onboarded twelve international clients.
I have just sent you our credentials.
We have not defined the scope yet.
Have you ever worked with a Brazilian firm?

already = sooner than expected · just = a moment ago · yet = not until now · ever = in your whole life

Already and just sit between have and the verb. Yet goes to the end, and only in negatives and questions.

The Pattern

Getting the conversation back

They say
You say
"Just send us your rates."
"Of course. It depends on the scope, so may I ask two quick questions first?"
"How much per month?"
"Our fee structure depends on headcount rather than hours. How many people would we be running?"
Something you did not follow
"Let me make sure I understood you correctly. You need..."
A question you cannot answer live
"I’d rather check that than guess. Can I come back to you by Friday?"
Closing the call
"So the next step is a proposal by Friday. Does that work for you?"

None of these is a delay. Every one of them is you taking the call somewhere.

Did You Catch It?

How Karen kept control

1. What did David ask for in his first sentence?
The rates, so he could compare three providers.
2. Why did their last outsourcing attempt matter to Karen?
It went badly — which is why they are looking again, and what she now knows to address.
3. What exactly would David get every month?
Three deliverables — the payroll run, the tax filing and one report — and one invoice on the fifth.
4. How many numbers did Karen give about price?
None. She explained the fee structure and ended with a proposal by Friday.
