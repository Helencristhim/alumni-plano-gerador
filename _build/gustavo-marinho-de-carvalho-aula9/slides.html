Chapter 3: Who, Which, That

Which Half Could You Delete?

The comma is not decoration. It changes what the sentence claims.

Grammar Discovery

Which Half Could You Delete?

"The client who handles our account is based in London."

"The report that I sent last week has been approved."

"James, who I spoke to yesterday, confirmed the date."

"We met in the office where the contracts team sits."

Delete the red parts and the sentence stops telling you WHICH client and WHICH report — those halves are doing identifying work, and they take no commas. Delete the blue part and you still know exactly who James is; that is why it sits between commas. The orange one shows that the joining word changes with what you are describing.

JobFormExample
Identifying a personwho / that, no commasThe client who handles our account is based in London.
Identifying a thingwhich / that, no commasThe report that I sent has been approved.
Extra information about somebody already identifiedwho / which, WITH commasJames, who I spoke to yesterday, confirmed the date.
A placewherethe office where the contracts team sits
Possessionwhosethe supplier whose contract ends in March
You may drop it when it is the object(that) I sentthe report I sent last week
The trapno double subjectNot the client who he handles our account.
The other trapnever that with commasNot James, that I spoke to. Use who.

In one line: no commas means the clause tells you WHICH one. Commas mean you already knew, and this is extra.

Comprehension

About Claire

1. According to Claire, what does Daniel do with the contracts, and what does he not do?
He prepares them. He does not sign anything over a certain value.
2. Who is Priya, and why does her holiday stop things?
She is the person who signs contracts over a certain value, and she is based in Manchester. The team that handles the volumes reports to her as well, so nothing moves in the week she is away.
3. What does she tell him at the end that he did not ask about?
That Daniel is covering for somebody until the eighteenth, so his workload is double at the moment.
Listening 1 · Business Radio

The Third Thing Nobody Hands You

A short radio piece on what you actually need in the first month with a new client. Sound first — no text.

1. What two things does everybody hand you, and what is the third?
The process and the calendar. The third is a map of the people.
2. What is the difference she draws between the org chart and the working map?
The org chart tells you the job titles. The working map tells you who answers quickly, whose approval unblocks a thing and which of the people copied on every email will read yours.
3. Why does she say nobody writes it down, and what does she recommend?
Because everybody inside learned it by being there for two years. She recommends asking for it directly in the first week, and says it saves about six months.
Listening 2 · A Voice Note

The Person Who Decides

A voice note about a distinction that costs people a lot of time. Sound first — no text.

1. What distinction does he say nobody explained to him?
That the person you talk to and the person who decides are frequently not the same person, and often not even in the same conversation.
2. What does he say about going around your point of contact?
That it never works and never goes unnoticed.
3. What is the polite version of the question, and what does he say to do with the answer?
“Who else needs to see this before it can move?” Ask it early, ask it of the person you like, and write down the answer.
Real Document

Who Is Who on Our Side

Who is who — your side of the account
Sent after the call · four names, one line each

Daniel Okoye — prepares the contracts and is your first point of contact for anything that has a number in it. He reports to Priya. He is covering for a colleague until the 18th, so his workload is double this month.

Priya Raman — signs anything over fifty thousand and is the person whose approval actually unblocks a contract. She is based in Manchester, which is why the Thursday call happens at nine.

The volumes team — the group that confirms quantities, which sits in a different building and also reports to Priya.

Claire Hensley — me. I am in charge of the relationship, which means I am the one to call when something is stuck rather than wrong.

Sample page written for this lesson. The company and the people are invented.
1. Which line contains information you could delete without losing which person is meant?
Priya’s line: “which is why the Thursday call happens at nine” is extra, and it sits after a comma for exactly that reason.
2. Find the whose and say what it is doing.
“the person whose approval actually unblocks a contract”. It marks possession: the approval belongs to her, and it is the approval that matters, not her title.
3. The last line explains when to call Claire. Say the same distinction in your own words.
She is the one for things that are stuck rather than wrong — a problem of movement, not of content. Daniel is for anything with a number in it.
