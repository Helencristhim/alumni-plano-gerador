Grammar Discovery

Same Client, Six Sentences

Todas falam do mesmo cliente. Então por que os verbos em laranja estão diferentes?

The client moves goods to the port every day.

In 2019 the company was small.

In April a fire started in warehouse 4.

The team was loading a truck at that moment.

The client is going to decide on Thursday.

Option A is safer than Option B for this client.

O verbo não muda por capricho. Ele muda porque cada frase responde a uma pergunta diferente.

A pergunta que a frase respondeA formaExemplo
O que é verdade sempre?present simplethe client moves goods every day
Como as coisas eram?was / werein 2019 the company was small
O que aconteceu e acabou?past simplea fire started in warehouse 4
O que estava acontecendo?was / were + -ingthe team was loading a truck
O que vem depois?going to / willthe client is going to decide on Thursday
Qual das duas é melhor?-er than / the -estOption A is safer than Option B
The One That Still Slips

Already in the Diary, or Decided Now?

Em português os dois viram “vou”. Em inglês, a pergunta é uma só: já estava marcado?

Já estava marcado → going toDecidido agora → will
The client is going to decide on Thursday.I will check that and call you back.
I am going to present in Miami in November.I will send the report before the deadline.
We are going to visit the plant in March.Don't worry — I will answer every question.

I am going to present the renewal in Miami.

I will send the report before the deadline.

O teste da agenda: já está marcado → going to. Você está decidindo agora, na frente da pessoa → will.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I working with a big client in Santos."
"I work with a big client in Santos."
"Yesterday I go to Santos and I send the report."
"Yesterday I went to Santos and I sent the report."

Estes dois erros não vêm de falta de estudo — vêm de pressa. Eles aparecem quando você está com medo, e não quando você não sabe. O conserto é o mesmo nos dois casos: respire e diga a frase mais devagar. A palavra de tempo (yesterday) não marca o passado — quem marca é o verbo.

Comprehension

What Did Greg Say?

1. What is the date of the presentation, and how long does Greg give for it?
November 12, and five minutes with the team.
2. Who is in the room, besides the Miami team?
The reinsurer — and that is why Greg is careful about the fire.
3. What does Greg say the reinsurer always asks about first?
The fire. He says that the line about what the team was doing is the line they read first.
4. Greg asks about two things at the end of the call. What are they?
The renewal — which option to recommend — and the deadline for the final report.
Listening

David's First Time Alone

Sound first — no text. Leia as perguntas antes de ouvir.

0:000:00
1. What happened to David the first time he presented alone in Miami?
He learned a long text by heart, and after two sentences he forgot everything. He stood there in silence for eleven seconds.
2. What did the old broker next to him say after the meeting?
That nobody in the room wanted a speech — they wanted the client, the history, the loss, the numbers and the next step.
3. What does David do before every presentation now, and why does it work?
He writes five sentences on one small card, one for each part of the story. It works because you cannot forget five sentences — and if you lose one, the other four are still there.
The Artifact

Your Card for Miami

Cinco linhas. Uma forma por linha.

The Artifact

The Five-Sentence Card

MIAMI · 12 NOVEMBER · 5 MINUTES Paula Morales
1Who they are today.
"Litoral Cargo is a logistics company in Santos. It has four warehouses and eighty trucks."
2How it was.
"In 2019 the company was small. It had two warehouses, and the first quote was cheap and simple."
3What happened, and what was happening.
"In April a fire started in warehouse 4. The team was loading a truck, and nobody was hurt."
4The two options.
"Option B is cheaper, but the coverage in Option A is more complete. I recommend Option A."
5What comes next.
"The client is going to decide on Thursday, and I will send the report before the deadline."
1. Line 1 and line 2 are about the same company. Why is the verb different?
Line 1 answers "what is true today", so it is present simple: is, has. Line 2 answers "how was it in 2019", so it is was and had.
2. Line 3 has two different past forms in one line. Find them and say why each one is there.
A fire started — one short thing that happened and finished. The team was loading a truck — the long thing that was already happening around it.
3. Line 5 uses going to and will. Which one is already in the diary?
Is going to decide on Thursday — the meeting is already booked. I will send the report is your promise, made here, in this sentence.
4. Now change the numbers: take a real client from your desk and say the five lines again.
There is no wrong answer here — say the five lines out loud with your own client, your own year, your own numbers. That is the whole lesson: five questions, five forms, one story.
