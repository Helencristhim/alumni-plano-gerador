Lesson 20 -- Pre-class

Review and Consolidation -- Twenty Lessons, One Story

Lesson 20 closes the block — and it brings no new form. It brings the six you already have, in one place, for one thing only: choosing the right one without stopping to think. Present for what is always true (the client moves goods every day), was/were for how things were (in 2019 the company was small), past simple for what happened and finished (a fire started), was/were + -ing for what was happening (the team was loading a truck), going to and will for what comes next, and the comparative for choosing between two (Option A is safer than Option B). The key is not to memorize six rules: it is to ask one question — what question does this sentence answer? — because the question chooses the form. The six words are revision too: client, report, deadline, goal, claim and quote, all from earlier lessons. At the end, one thing only: a whole client account told in five sentences.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

None of these six words is new — they have all been here before, from lesson 2 to lesson 19. The goal now is not to learn them, it is for them to come out without a pause. The sound traps: DED-lain (the dead sounds like the e in bed) · ri-PORT (stress on the second syllable) · KLAI-ent (two syllables) · kleim (only one).

Client -- the company or person who buys from you
"The client in Santos wants the renewal this week." Say KLAI-ent, two syllables — the i sounds like the one in my.
Report -- a document with the numbers of the month
"I send the report to Miami every Friday." Say ri-PORT, stress on the second syllable.
Deadline -- the last day to finish something
"The deadline for the Santos file is Friday at six." Say DED-lain — the dead sounds like the e in bed, never “did”.
Goal -- what you want to reach
"My goal for next year is one contract for the four warehouses." Say GOUL, one syllable — and notice: goal almost always travels with going to or will.
Claim -- what a client opens after an accident
"We opened the claim the same morning." Say kleim, one syllable — never “cla-ime”.
Quote -- a price the insurer gives for a risk
"There are two quotes on the table for the renewal." Say KWOUT, one syllable — the qu sounds like “kw”.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the story and answer the questions. All the sentences in bold talk about the same client — and yet the verbs are different. Notice why: each sentence answers a different question.

The Santos account file, told by Paula. “Litoral Cargo is my biggest account. When somebody asks me who they are, the answer is always in the present: the client moves goods to the port every day. That was true yesterday, it is true today and it will be true tomorrow.”

“But if the question is about 2019, the form changes: in 2019 the company was small — two warehouses, thirty trucks. And in April came the hard part: a fire started in warehouse 4. Something that happened and finished, in one minute. But the first thing the reinsurer asks is not that one: it is what was under way in that minute — the team was loading a truck, and nobody was hurt.”

“Now the renewal. There are two quotes: Option A is safer than Option B for this operation. And what comes next takes two forms, both in the same sentence: the meeting is already booked, so the client is going to decide on Thursday; and the report is a promise I am making right now, so I will send it before the deadline.”

1. Why is it “the client moves goods every day” and not “moved”?
A Because the sentence is short.
B Because this sentence answers “what is always true?” — and that asks for the present.
C Because the client is big.
2. “A fire started” and “the team was loading” are in the same morning. Why are the forms different?
A Because they answer different questions: one says what happened and finished, the other says what was going on around it.
B Because one sentence is about people and the other about things.
C Because the fire was more important.
3. In the last sentence, why is it “is going to decide” for the meeting and “will send” for the report?
A Because the meeting is more important than the report.
B Because will only exists in the first person.
C Because the meeting was already booked (calendar → going to) and the report is a promise made now (→ will).
4. In “in 2019 the company was small”, why is it not “is”?
A Because the sentence answers “how was it in 2019?” — and that asks for was, not is. Today the company is big.
B Because “company” is plural.
C Because the sentence starts with a year.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- the question chooses the form

GRAMMAR

No new form here: all six are already yours. What changes today is the way of choosing. Do not ask “which tense do I use?” — ask “what question does this sentence answer?”. The question chooses the form on its own.

The questionIn EnglishWhen you use it
What is always true?the client moves goods every dayo cliente move carga todo dia
How were things?in 2019 the company was smallem 2019 a empresa era pequena
O que aconteceu e acabou?a fire started in warehouse 4a fire started in warehouse 4
O que estava acontecendo?the team was loading a truckthe team was loading a truck
What comes next (already booked)?the client is going to decide on Thursdayo cliente vai decidir na quinta
What comes next (decided now)?I will send the report todayI will send the report today
Which of the two is better?Option A is safer than Option BOption A is safer than Option B

The pair that still slips — going to or will? There is one test only: is it already in the calendar? If yes, going to (“the client is going to decide on Thursday” — the meeting has been booked). If you are deciding now, in front of the person, will (“I will check that and call you back”).

The two mistakes that come back under pressure, and the fix for each one:

1) “I working with a big client.” — the verb is missing. In the present simple the form is “I work with a big client.” (The -ing only comes in with am / is / are / was / were alongside it.)

2) “Yesterday I go to Santos.” — the time word does not mark the past. What marks it is the verb: “Yesterday I went to Santos.”

And the detail about will: after it the verb goes bare, with no to — I will send, never “I will to send”.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record your answer. Speak for 90 to 120 seconds, with no script. Do not redo it more than twice. This is exactly the homework audio — start building it here.

You have five minutes in Miami, and a room with the team and the reinsurer. Present the whole account in five sentences, in this order: (1) who the client is today; (2) how the company was in 2019; (3) what happened in April; (4) what was happening at that moment; (5) what comes next — the comparison of the two quotes, your recommendation and the report deadline. One form per sentence. It does not have to be perfect — it has to be yours.
