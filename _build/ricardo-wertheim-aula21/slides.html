Chapter 3: The Code

You Must, You Don't Have To

must · have to · mustn't · don't have to

Grammar Discovery

Necessary, Forbidden or Free?

"Visitors must respect the monuments."

"Tour groups have to book a time to enter."

"You mustn't walk in the streets in your swimsuit."

"You don't have to pay to walk in the old town."

Look at the blue words. Which ones say it is necessary? Which one says do not do it? Which one says you can choose?

FormMeaningExample
must / have toit is necessaryGuides must have a permit.
has tohe, she, itElena has to open at seven.
mustn'tit is forbidden: do NOT do itYou mustn't sit on the fountain.
don't have toit is not necessary: you can chooseYou don't have to pay.
QuestionsDo I have to...? / Does she have to...?Do I have to book?
The trapmustn't is NOT don't have toNever must to or don't must.

After must, mustn't and have to: the verb with no "to" and no -s. You must respect, she has to book.

Chapter 4: A Call from Elena

News from Dubrovnik

A city council, a swimsuit and a fountain

Dialogue

A Call from Elena

E
Ricardo! I have news from Dubrovnik. The city council made new rules for the old town.
R
Really? After all those crowds in August? What are the new rules?
E
First, you mustn't walk in the streets in your swimsuit. If you do, you get a fine.
R
That makes sense. The old town is not a beach. And the old buildings?
E
You mustn't sit on the fountain or on the church steps. Visitors have to respect the monuments.
R
And do I have to book a time to enter, like at a museum?
E
No, you don't have to. Only big tour groups have to book, and their guides must have a permit.
R
So the city didn't ban tourists. It asked them to behave. And what about you? What do the people who live there think?
E
Most of us are happy. We have to live here every day, after all. Are you allowed to sit on monuments in Sao Paulo?
R
Officially, no. But not everybody follows that rule!
Comprehension

What Did Elena Say?

1. What news did Elena give Ricardo at the start of the call?
The city council made new rules for the old town.
2. According to Elena, who has to book a time to enter?
Only big tour groups. And their guides must have a permit.
3. What do the people who live in the old town think, according to Elena?
Most of them are happy, because they have to live there every day.

Chapter 5: Practice

Two Voices, Two Sides

The guide and the city council

Listening

Before We Go In

A guide at the gate of the old town, with her tour group. Sound first — no text.

1. What mustn't visitors wear in the streets?
Their swimsuits. The police can give them a fine.
2. Why mustn't they sit on the church steps or the fountain?
Because they are monuments, more than five hundred years old.
3. When does the group have to be back at the gate?
At twelve o'clock, because the group has a booked time.
Listening 2

A Voice from the Council

A member of the city council on the local radio. Sound first — no text.

1. What do some people say about the city council?
That they hate tourists. He says it is not true.
2. What do big tour groups have to do now?
They have to book a time to enter the old town.
3. Do visitors have to pay to walk in the old town?
No, they don't have to. It is still free.
