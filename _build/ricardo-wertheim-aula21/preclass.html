Lesson 21 -- Pre-class

The Rules of the Old Town

How to talk about the rules of a city. Key words: city council, rule, fine, allowed, to ban, permit, to respect, monument. Structure: must, have to, mustn't and don't have to -- you mustn't sit on the monuments, you don't have to pay.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

City council -- the group of people who are elected to make decisions for a city
"The city council made new rules for the old town."
Rule -- something that tells you what you can or cannot do in a place
"The new rule is simple: no swimsuits in the streets."
Fine -- money that you pay as a punishment when you break a rule
"If you walk in the old town in a swimsuit, you can get a fine."
Allowed -- if something is allowed, it is OK to do it and nobody will stop you
"Photos are allowed in the cathedral, but not during Mass."
To ban -- to say officially that people cannot do something
"Some cities ban big tour buses from the historic center."
Permit -- an official paper which says you can do something
"Every tour guide in the old town must have a permit."
To respect -- to treat a person or a place with care, because they are important
"Visitors must respect the people who live there."
Monument -- an old building or statue which is important for the history of a place
"The fountain is a monument from the fifteenth century."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last week Elena called Ricardo from Dubrovnik. "The city council made new rules for the old town," she said. "Visitors mustn't walk in the streets in their swimsuits. If they do, they get a fine. And they mustn't sit on the fountain or on the church steps. They have to respect the monuments."

Ricardo asked if he had to book a time to enter. "No, you don't have to," Elena answered. "Only big tour groups have to book, and their guides must have a permit. Walking in the old town is still free."

Ricardo liked the idea. "So the city didn't ban tourists," he said. "It asked them to behave." Elena laughed. "Exactly. We have to live here every day. Are you allowed to sit on monuments in Sao Paulo?"

1. Who made the new rules?
A The tour guides who work at the gate, with a permit from the city.
B The city council.
C Elena and her neighbors.
2. Which sentence from the text says something is NOT necessary?
A "They have to respect the monuments, and they mustn't sit on the fountain."
B "Visitors mustn't walk in the streets in their swimsuits."
C "No, you don't have to."
3. Why are the people of the old town happy with the rules, according to Elena?
A Because they have to live there every day.
B Because the city council gives the money from the fines to the families who live there.
C Because tourists don't come any more.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Must, Have To, Mustn't, Don't Have To

GRAMMAR

Necessary, forbidden, or your choice?

FormMeaningExample
mustit is necessary (a rule)Guides must have a permit.
have to / has toit is necessary (a rule or a situation)Tour groups have to book. Elena has to open at seven.
mustn'tit is forbidden: do NOT do itYou mustn't sit on the fountain.
don't have toit is not necessary: you can chooseYou don't have to pay.
QuestionsDo / Does + subject + have toDo I have to book a time?

Never: you must to respect · you don't must · she have to · you mustn't pay (when you mean: it is free)
