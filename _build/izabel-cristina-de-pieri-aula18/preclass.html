"Attrition fell to zero after the recovery."

"The payback period was eleven months."

"The firmware went back as a proof of concept."

"AVRT now treats the Meridian file as a best practice."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Izabel reads the published case study for the first time. Published six months after the Halo failure, it follows the Meridian account from the day the line went down to the day the contract was renewed. The pain point is stated in one sentence. Having lost the client's confidence in a single week, AVRT chose not to argue; it measured. Taking the worst month as the baseline, the team agreed on three metrics with David Chen and published them every Thursday. Rebuilt by an external firm and tested unit by unit, the firmware went back to Meridian as a proof of concept before any full shipment. Implementation was slow on purpose: onboarding the warehouse staff on the new dashboard, AVRT trained forty operators in three weeks. Adoption climbed to ninety-four percent and attrition fell to zero. Asked for a testimonial, David wrote a single line. Read honestly, the findings show a supplier that recovered because it published its own numbers.

1. "Having lost the client's confidence, AVRT chose not to argue." What does this show?
A Losing the confidence happened BEFORE the decision not to argue.
B The two things happened at exactly the same moment.
C AVRT will lose the confidence in the future.
2. Why "Rebuilt by an external firm" and not "Rebuilding by an external firm"?
A Because "rebuilt" is shorter than "rebuilding".
B Because the firmware received the action — the clause is passive.
C Because the sentence is in the past.
3. "Onboarding the warehouse staff, AVRT trained forty operators." Who did the onboarding?
A The warehouse staff.
B The dashboard.
C AVRT — the subject of the main clause.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Participle Clauses

GRAMMAR

Saying why, when or how in fewer words — the grammar written business English runs on.

TypeStructureExample
-ing clause
active		verb + -ing (= because / while we take)Taking February as the baseline, we agreed on three metrics.
Past participle clause
passive		past participle (= because it was rebuilt)Rebuilt by a firm, the firmware went back as a proof of concept.
Having + past participle
earlier action		having + past participle (= after we had lost)Having lost the account, AVRT chose to measure.
The one ruleThe missing subject of the participle must be the subject of the main clause. "Having lost the account, AVRT published the numbers" — not "the numbers were published" (numbers do not lose accounts).
Watch outUse the past participle, not -ing, when the subject RECEIVES the action: not "Rebuilding by a firm" but "Rebuilt by a firm". And never "Being lost the account" — the form is "Having lost the account".
