Chapter 4: The Code

Participle Clauses

-ing · past participle · having + past participle

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Having lost the account, AVRT chose to measure."

"Taking the worst month as the baseline, the team agreed on three metrics."

"Rebuilt by an external firm, the firmware went back as a proof of concept."

"Asked for a testimonial, David wrote a single line."

The first part has no subject. Who is it, in every sentence?

TypeReplacesExample
-ing (active)because / while we tookTaking the worst month as the baseline, we agreed on three metrics.
Past participle (passive)because it was rebuiltRebuilt by a firm, the firmware went back as a proof of concept.
Having + past participleafter we had lost (earlier action)Having lost the account, AVRT chose to measure.

One rule decides everything: the missing subject of the participle must be the subject of the main clause. Check that subject before you write the comma.

Language Focus

Which Type?

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

✗ WRONG

"Having lost the account, the numbers were published."

✓ RIGHT

"Having lost the account, AVRT published the numbers."

✗ WRONG

"Rebuilding by an external firm, the firmware went back."

✓ RIGHT

"Rebuilt by an external firm, the firmware went back."

✗ WRONG

"Being lost the account, we chose to measure."

✓ RIGHT

"Having lost the account, we chose to measure."

The missing subject belongs to the main clause. Use -ing when the subject acts, a past participle when the subject receives the action, and having + past participle for what happened first.

Dialogue

Signing Off the Case Study

A
Izabel, I read the final draft last night. It is good, but it is brutal. Are you really going to publish the month we lost two accounts?
I
Yes. Having lost the account once, we do not get to choose the flattering half. The attrition number stays in.
A
Fair enough. And the comparison at the start? Where do those percentages come from?
I
Taking February as the baseline, we agreed on three metrics with David. Every percentage in the study is measured against that month.
A
The board will ask about cost. Eleven months is a long time to wait for your money back.
I
Rebuilt by an external firm and tested unit by unit, the firmware was never going to be cheap. An eleven-month payback period is still cheaper than losing Meridian.
A
One last thing. Did David give you anything we can quote?
I
Asked for a testimonial, he sent one line: they told me the date, and then they met it. The findings are ours, but that line is the whole story.

The Numbers from Sofia

Your operations lead sends the figures for the case study. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Which month is the baseline, and which three metrics were agreed with David?
February is the baseline; the metrics are uptime, defect rate and time to replacement.
2. What happened to adoption, and what happened to attrition?
Adoption climbed from thirty-one percent in January to ninety-four percent in May; attrition went from two accounts to zero.
3. Why does Sofia want the unflattering numbers published?
Because publishing them is the whole reason the case study is credible.
Listening 2

A Call from David Chen

The client approves the draft and explains why. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Why is David approving the case study?
Not because the recovery was fast, but because AVRT published the numbers that made it look bad next to the ones that made it look good.
2. What three things does David say AVRT did right?
The proof of concept arrived before the full shipment, his operators were onboarded properly, and he received the whole findings file instead of a summary.
3. What does David ask for by Friday, and what should AVRT do with the file?
The final version for his legal team, and AVRT should treat the file as a best practice for the next account.

What I Learned

I can shorten an active clause with -ing: "Taking February as the baseline, we agreed on three metrics."
I can shorten a passive clause with a past participle: "Rebuilt by an external firm, the firmware went back."
I use having + past participle for the earlier action, and I never leave the subject hanging.
I know the words: pain point, baseline, metric, adoption, findings, attrition, payback period.
