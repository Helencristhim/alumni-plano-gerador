Aula 18 -- The Client Success Story -- Reading a Case Study

Film
Ford v Ferrari (2019)

An engineering team is given one job: fix a car that keeps failing, and prove it on the track. It is the Lesson 18 story in film form — a baseline, a proof of concept, a slow implementation, and a result you can put a number on. Watch how Ken Miles and Carroll Shelby turn a public failure into a documented win.

Tip: after the first Le Mans sequence, write the story of that car as a five-sentence case study. Open three of the five sentences with a participle clause ("Having failed at Le Mans...", "Rebuilt over a single winter...", "Tested lap after lap...").

Talk
Nancy Duarte -- "The secret structure of great talks" (TED)

Duarte takes famous speeches apart and shows the shape underneath: what is, what could be, and the gap between them. That is exactly the shape of a case study — the pain point, the result, and the work in the middle. Free and open on TED, with English transcript and subtitles.

Tip: map the Meridian story onto her structure. Where is "what is" (the pain point), where is "what could be" (the metrics), and where is the reward at the end (the testimonial)?

YouTube
"How to use participle clauses" -- English Masterclass, BBC Learning English

A short, precise masterclass on the exact code from Lesson 18: -ing clauses for active meaning, past participle clauses for passive meaning, and having + past participle for the earlier action. It also shows the trap we practiced — leaving the subject hanging.

Tip: pause after each example and rebuild it with an AVRT fact. Then record a 90-second version of the Meridian case study using at least four participle clauses, one of each type.
