Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read it in context. These are the words of every policy that lands in your inbox from headquarters.

global policy -- a rule written at headquarters that applies to the whole group
"The global policy on remote work comes into effect in January."
guideline -- a recommendation that helps you apply a rule, without being the rule itself
"The guideline explains how to apply the policy in each country."
compliance -- doing what the rules and the law require
"Compliance checks every policy before we send it out."
rollout -- the process of putting something new into practice, step by step
"The rollout starts in Brazil and ends in Chile."
local adaptation -- the change you make so that a global rule works in one country
"Every country sends its local adaptation to headquarters."
effective date -- the day when a rule starts to apply
"The effective date is the first of March."
communication plan -- the plan of who says what, to whom and when
"The communication plan has three steps: leaders, managers, employees."
in line with -- following the same direction as a rule or a decision
"Our local practice is in line with the global policy."
come into effect -- to start to apply, officially
"The new rule comes into effect on the first of March."
apply to -- to affect a person or a group
"The policy applies to all employees with a permanent contract."
bear in mind -- to remember something important while you decide
"Bear in mind that the works council has to be informed first."
roll out -- to put something new into practice across the company
"We roll out the policy in three countries at the same time."
pass on -- to give information to the next person who needs it
"I pass the guideline on to the local managers."
point out -- to say something that the other person has not noticed
"She pointed out that the effective date is a holiday in Chile."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

A global policy is written in one place and lived in many. It leaves headquarters as a clear document and arrives in each country as a set of questions: does this apply to my team, what changes for me, and from when?

The document itself usually has four parts. The scope, which says who it applies to. The rule. The effective date, which is the day it starts, not the day it was written. And a guideline, which is not the rule but the help: how to apply it, with examples.

Then comes the rollout, and this is where the business partner works. Each country compares the rule with the local reality and writes a local adaptation: a signed agreement with a works council, a different notice period, a public holiday on the effective date. The adaptation does not cancel the policy; it makes the policy possible. And a good communication plan makes sure that managers hear it before their teams do, and not five minutes after.

When you explain all this to a manager, one question comes back every time: why? English gives you two structures for the answer, and the difference is simple. Use so that when the purpose belongs to somebody else: "I send you the guideline so that you have the same words in front of you." Use in order to, or just to, when the purpose belongs to the same person who acts: "We changed the policy in order to have one rule in three countries."

What English does not accept is a person after to. "To her understand the policy" is the direct translation of a Portuguese structure, and it stops the sentence. The correct form is so that she understands the policy. It is one of those small structures that decide whether a manager thinks you are explaining or apologising.

1. According to the text, what is a guideline?
A The help that shows how to apply the rule, with examples.
B The rule itself, written by headquarters.
C The date when the policy starts.
2. What does a local adaptation do?
A It makes the global policy possible in one country, without cancelling it.
B It cancels the global policy in that country.
C It delays the effective date for everybody.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A I send the guideline to her understand the policy.
B I send the guideline so that she understands the policy.
C I send the guideline for she understand the policy.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Purpose: so that and in order to

GRAMMAR

Two structures answer the question why. The choice depends on one thing only: who does the second action.

FormWhat it doesExample
so that + subject + verbthe purpose belongs to somebody elseI send the guideline so that the managers have the same words.
in order to + verbthe purpose belongs to the same subject; formalWe changed the policy in order to have one rule.
to + verbthe short everyday versionWe publish it in February to give everybody time.
so that + can / willabout a future possibilityI send it today so that you can prepare the meeting.
Watch outa person after to. "to her understand" and "for the team know" are direct translations. Use so that she understands.

Quick test: if the next word is a person, you need so that. If the next word is a verb, to or in order to works.

Stage 2: Explain a Policy in Five Moves

Order

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Real sentences from a policy rollout. Only one option is both correct and natural.

1. A manager asks why the policy changed. Which answer is correct?
A It changed so that the three countries have the same rule.
B It changed for the three countries have the same rule.
C It changed to the three countries have the same rule.
D It changed so that the three countries having the same rule.
2. Which sentence about the date is correct?
A The policy comes in effect the first of March.
B The policy comes into effect on the first of March.
C The policy enters in effect in the first of March.
D The policy comes into effect at the first of March.
3. You want to say that the local practice follows the global rule. What do you say?
A Our local practice is in line with the global policy.
B Our local practice is in the line of the global policy.
C Our local practice is in line of the global policy.
D Our local practice is on line with the global policy.
4. Which sentence uses the phrasal verb correctly?
A I pass on the guideline to the local managers.
B I pass the guideline for the local managers on.
C I pass to the local managers on the guideline.
D I pass on to the guideline the local managers.
