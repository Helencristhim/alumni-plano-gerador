Chapter 3: The Code

Why, and So What?

The words that connect a cause to a result

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Units fell in August because the shipment was held at customs."

"Units fell in August because of the late shipment."

"Due to the price cut, two clinics switched to a competitor."

"The clinics had no product for three weeks. As a result, units declined by twelve percent."

The blue words introduce a cause. What comes after each one: a full sentence, or only a noun? And the purple words: cause, or result?

JobLinking wordsExample
Cause + full sentencebecause, since, asUnits fell because the shipment was late.
Cause + nounbecause of, due to, owing toUnits fell because of the late shipment.
Result, same sentenceso, which led toThe shipment was late, so the clinics ran out.
Result, new sentenceAs a result, Therefore, ConsequentlyThe clinics ran out. As a result, units declined.
Two trapsNever because of the shipment was late — with a verb, it is just because. And never due to the clinics had no product — due to takes a noun: due to the stockout.

The Structure

Explain a Drop in Three Steps

Start with the number. End with the action. The cause goes in the middle.

1 · What happened

"Units in the South fell by twelve percent in the third quarter."

2 · Why it happened

"The root cause was a stockout in August. As a result, two clinics switched to a competitor."

3 · What we are doing

"We have a recovery plan, so we expect to bounce back in the fourth quarter."

What → why → what next. One or two causes, never ten.

Chapter 4: The Q3 Review

Before the Global Review

Laura Whitfield · Regional Commercial Director · the Q3 call

Dialogue

The Q3 Review

L
Renato, the South is down in the third quarter. Before the global review, I need to understand what happened.
R
Of course. Units in the South declined by twelve percent compared to the second quarter.
L
Twelve percent is a lot. Is it a real downturn, or something else?
R
It is not a downturn in the market. The root cause was a stockout in August. A shipment was held at customs for eleven days.
L
I remember that. So the clinics had no product?
R
Exactly. Because of the delay, some clinics had nothing for almost three weeks. And part of the July dip is just seasonality.
L
Did we lose any clients because of it?
R
Yes, that was the knock-on effect. Due to a price cut from a competitor, two clinics in Porto Alegre switched, and they have not come back yet.
L
That is the part the global team will ask about. Is the damage temporary?
R
The stockout was. For the two clinics, we have a recovery plan: joint visits this month, and growth in Curitiba will offset part of the drop.
L
Good. Start with the number, give one cause, and end with the plan. That is how you say it to the global team.

What the VP Remembers

0:000:00
1. According to him, what does he remember after a bad quarter?
Not the drop itself, but the way the manager explains it.
2. How does the weak explanation start, and how does the strong one start?
The weak one starts with excuses; the strong one starts with the number.

Because it tells him the manager does not know the real cause.

That the manager understands why it happened and has already started to fix it.
Listening 2

The Stockout From the Other Side

A supply planning manager on the August shipment. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Why was the shipment late?
It was held at customs for eleven days.
2. What was the direct effect, and what was the knock-on effect?
Direct: clinics had no product for almost three weeks. Knock-on: a few tried a competitor, and two stayed with it.
3. What two things did her team change as a result?
They keep six weeks of stock instead of four, and the commercial team hears about a late shipment on the same day.
4. What is temporary, and what is not?
The stockout was temporary; the lost clinics are not, unless the commercial team wins them back.
Chapter 5: Practice

Your Quarter

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "Units fell because of the shipment was late."
"Units fell because the shipment was late." (with a verb, just because)
2. "Due to the clinics had no product, sales declined."
"Due to the stockout, sales declined." (due to takes a noun)
3. "We lost two clinics because that the competitor cut the price."
"We lost two clinics because the competitor cut the price." (no that after because)
4. "We pretend to bounce back in the fourth quarter." (you mean: we plan to)
"We intend to bounce back in the fourth quarter." (pretend means to act as if something is true)
5. "Three persons in my team visited the clinics."
"Three people in my team visited the clinics." (people, not persons)

What happened: units in the South declined by 12% compared to Q2.

Why: the main cause was a stockout in August, due to a shipment held at customs for 11 days. A smaller part of the drop is seasonality: July is always quieter. As a result of the stockout, two clinics in Porto Alegre switched to a competitor after a price cut.

What we are doing: joint visits to both clinics before the end of October; six weeks of stock from now on. Growth in Curitiba (+5%) offsets part of the drop. Expected recovery: Q4.

1. How big was the drop, and compared to what?
Units in the South declined by twelve percent compared to the second quarter.
2. What is offsetting part of the drop?
Growth in Curitiba, five percent.
3. Which is the main cause, and which is the smaller one?
The main cause is the stockout in August; the smaller one is seasonality in July.
