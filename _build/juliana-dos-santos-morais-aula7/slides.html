A partner likes your proposal, but not the price, not the date, not every detail. This lesson gives you the words of a friendly negotiation, and the structure that carries every condition.

Yes, If...

Last lesson you compared two offers. Now one partner wants to change your offer: a lower price, a different date, more visibility. A good answer is not yes and not no. It is yes, if...

Chapter 3: The Code

One Condition, One Result

If + present, will + verb

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Offers Show?

"If you sign this week, we will give you a discount."

"If you pay upfront, the price will be lower."

"We will add a second booth if you stay for two years."

"If the director does not agree, we will not change the terms."

Each sentence has two parts. In red: the condition, with if. In blue: the result. Look at the verb after if: is it present or future?

JobFormExample
The conditionif + present simpleIf you sign this week...
The resultwill + verb...we will give you a discount.
Either orderresult + if + condition (no comma)We will add a booth if you stay two years.
Negativedoes not / will not (won’t)If he does not agree, we won’t change the terms.
Softer resultcan / may instead of willIf you pay upfront, we can offer a better price.
The trapnever will after ifNot If you will sign. Say If you sign.

In one line: if + present for the condition, will + verb for the result.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Negotiation, at Real Speed

One call, three recordings, one offer sheet

Dialogue

The Price of the Gold Package

D
Juliana, we like the gold package very much. But the price is a problem for us. Can you give us a discount?
J
I understand. What discount do you have in mind?
D
Twenty percent. Our budget is smaller this year.
J
Twenty percent is difficult. But if you sign for two years, we will give you ten percent.
D
Ten is not enough. How about fifteen, and we sign for two years?
J
Let us meet halfway. Fifteen percent, if you pay the first year upfront.
D
Upfront... OK. If our director agrees, we will send the contract on Friday.
J
Perfect. So we have a deal. It is a win-win for both sides.
Comprehension

About Daniel

1. What discount did Daniel ask for first, and why?
Twenty percent, because his budget is smaller this year.
2. What was Daniel’s counteroffer?
Fifteen percent, and they sign for two years.
3. What does Daniel need before he sends the contract?
His director has to agree.
Listening 1 · The Sales Director

Never Say Just Yes

A sales director explains her rule for negotiations. Sound first — no text.

1. What is her main rule?
Never give something without getting something back.
2. What does she say instead of yes?
Yes, if... and then a condition.
3. What example does she give?
If you sign for two years, we will give you a discount.
Listening 2 · The Buyer

How I Answer a Counteroffer

A buyer at a Canadian company talks about negotiating with Brazilian partners. Sound first — no text.

1. What did he learn about Brazilian partners?
They prefer to build a relationship before they talk about the price.
2. What does he do when he receives a counteroffer?
He takes one day to think and never answers in the same call.
3. What question does he always ask at the end?
So, what do we both need to make this work?
Listening 3 · The Offer

The Membership Offer

A recorded message about a special offer for new member companies. Sound first — no text.

1. What discount do new members get if they join before December?
Twenty percent in the first year.
2. What happens if a company pays the full year upfront?
It gets two free tickets to the business breakfast.
3. What happens if a company signs for two years?
It gets a free booth at the next trade fair.

Because the partner’s director has not approved it yet.
