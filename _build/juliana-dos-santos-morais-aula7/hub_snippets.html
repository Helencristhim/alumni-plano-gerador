Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

Meeting Halfway -- A Friendly Negotiation

A partner likes your proposal, but not every detail. Key words: to negotiate, a discount, the terms, to agree on, a counteroffer, to meet halfway, a commitment, upfront, a win-win, a deal. Structure: first conditional (if + present, will).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To negotiate -- to discuss something to reach an agreement
"We are negotiating the price of the gold package."
A discount -- a lower price than the normal one
"If you sign this week, we will give you a discount."
The terms -- the conditions of an agreement: price, dates, duties
"We agree with the terms, except the payment date."
To agree on -- to decide something together
"We agreed on a date in March."
A counteroffer -- a new offer you make after you receive an offer
"They sent a counteroffer with a lower price."
To meet halfway -- to accept part of what the other side wants, as they accept part of what you want
"You want 20 percent and we offer 10. Can we meet halfway at 15?"
A commitment -- a promise to do something, or to stay in an agreement
"We need a commitment for two years."
Upfront -- paid before the service starts
"If you pay upfront, the price is lower."
A win-win -- a result that is good for both sides
"A longer contract is a win-win for everybody."
A deal -- an agreement between two sides, often about business
"So, do we have a deal?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Daniel asks for a twenty percent discount on the gold package. Juliana does not say yes, and she does not say no. “If you sign for two years, we will give you ten percent,” she says.

Daniel sends a counteroffer: fifteen percent. Juliana decides to meet halfway. “Fifteen percent, if you pay the first year upfront.” Daniel agrees, and they have a deal.

Notice the pattern. After if, the verb is in the present: sign, pay. In the result, the verb uses will: will give.

1. What does Juliana offer if Daniel signs for two years?
A Twenty percent.
B Ten percent.
C A free booth.
2. How do they meet halfway?
A Fifteen percent, if Daniel pays the first year upfront.
B Twenty percent, with no conditions.
C Ten percent, and Daniel signs for one year.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “If you will sign, we give you a discount.”
B “If you sign, we will give you a discount.”
C “If you signed, we will give you a discount.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- First Conditional

GRAMMAR

Use if + present simple for the condition and will + verb for the result. Never use will after if.

JobFormExample
The conditionif + present simpleIf you sign this week...
The resultwill + verb...we will give you a discount.
Either orderresult + if + condition (no comma)We will add a booth if you stay two years.
Negativedoes not / will not (won’t)If he does not agree, we won’t change the terms.
Softer resultcan / may instead of willIf you pay upfront, we can offer a better price.
The trapnever will after ifNot If you will sign. Say If you sign.

Never: If you will sign, we give you a discount · If you pay upfront, the price is going lower · if you will stay two years · If he don’t agree

Stage 2: Put the Negotiation in Order

Order

A partner asks for a discount. Put the conversation in a logical order.

?"We like the package, but can you give us a discount?"
?"I understand. What discount do you have in mind?"
?"Twenty percent."
?"If you sign for two years, we will give you ten percent."
?"How about fifteen?"
?"OK, let us meet halfway: fifteen, if you pay upfront."
