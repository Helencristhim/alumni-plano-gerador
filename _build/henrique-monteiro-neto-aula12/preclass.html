Lesson 12 -- Pre-class

He Would Go On to Become — Telling a Life Backwards

A life narrated from its end, and the tense that exists only because the narrator knows how it finished. Key expressions: a formative experience, to make one's name, an apprenticeship, to fall out of favor, a late bloomer, posthumously, to rise through the ranks, obscurity, a protege, a lasting legacy, self-taught, to be overshadowed by, a false start, to hit one's stride, ahead of one's time, to be written out of history, an obituary, to be vindicated. Structure: the future in the past — was to become, would go on to, was about to, was never to.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. These eighteen stay in your material — they do not disappear after the class.

A formative experience -- something early that shaped the person you became
"The two years abroad were the formative experience and he never wrote about them."
To make one's name -- to become known for the first time, through one particular thing
"She made her name with a book she later described as embarrassing."
An apprenticeship -- a long period of learning a craft under somebody who already has it
"He served a twelve-year apprenticeship and complained about none of it."
To fall out of favor -- to stop being approved of by the people who matter
"He fell out of favor in his fifties and was rediscovered at eighty."
A late bloomer -- somebody whose ability shows itself much later than usual
"A late bloomer, in a profession that only celebrates the young."
Posthumously -- after the death of the person concerned
"The prize was awarded posthumously, which she would have found funny."
To rise through the ranks -- to advance step by step inside an institution
"He rose through the ranks without ever appearing to try."
Obscurity -- the state of being unknown to almost everybody
"Twenty years of obscurity, and then everything at once."
A protege -- somebody whose career is guided and protected by an established figure
"She was his protege and then, quite publicly, his rival."
A lasting legacy -- what continues to exist and matter after somebody is gone
"The lasting legacy is the school, not the buildings."
Self-taught -- having learned without formal instruction
"Entirely self-taught, and he never let anybody forget it."
To be overshadowed by -- to be made to seem less important by somebody or something nearby
"Her work was overshadowed by his for thirty years and then was not."
A false start -- an attempt that begins and then has to be abandoned
"Two false starts in medicine before he found the thing he was for."
To hit one's stride -- to reach the point of working at full ability, comfortably
"He hit his stride at fifty-three, which is later than the story usually allows."
Ahead of one's time -- doing something the world was not yet ready to accept
"Ahead of his time is what people say when they mean nobody listened."
To be written out of history -- to be removed from the accepted account, deliberately or by neglect
"Two of the four were written out of history and one of them wrote the account."
An obituary -- the published account of a life, written after the death
"A good obituary is a small biography with a deadline."
To be vindicated -- to be shown to have been right, after being doubted
"He was vindicated eleven years after he stopped caring."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The obituary called her the founder of a school of thought, and she would have disliked the phrase. She had spent forty years insisting that a body of work is not a movement. The piece was accurate about the facts and wrong about the person, which is the usual outcome and not a criticism of the writer.

She was fifty-three before she hit her stride. Behind that there was an apprenticeship of twelve years, two false starts in a field she left without explanation, and a long stretch of obscurity in which she published almost nothing. She was self-taught in the subject that would make her name, and she was to remain slightly defensive about that for the rest of her life. The two years abroad at twenty-four were the formative experience, and she never wrote a line about them.

The middle is the part biographers find hardest. She fell out of favor in her sixties, publicly, and for a decade she was overshadowed by a former protege who had been her most careful reader. She was about to withdraw the second volume when the institute wrote to her. She would go on to spend eleven years there and never once mention the decade in between.

What survives is not what she chose. The argument she believed was her lasting legacy is now taught as a stage the field passed through; she was, in that narrow sense, ahead of her time about something else entirely. Two of her collaborators were written out of history, and one of them wrote the standard account. A short paper she considered a distraction was to be vindicated thirty years later, posthumously, by evidence she was never to see.

1. "She was to remain slightly defensive about that." What does this form tell us?
A That she intended to remain defensive.

2. "She was about to withdraw the second volume when the institute wrote." What normally follows this form?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Future in the Past

GRAMMAR

These are the forms used by a narrator who already knows the ending. They point forwards from a moment in the past, and they are the grammatical signature of biography, of history and of telling your own life.

FormWhat it doesExample
was / were to
+ base form		A future seen from the past, with a sense of destiny. Formal and literary, and it announces that what follows mattered."He was to become the most quoted economist of his generation."
would + base form
would go on to		A later development narrated from earlier. Would go on to is the standard formula for what somebody did after the moment being described."She would go on to found three institutions."
was / were about to
+ base form		On the point of happening. Almost always followed by when and an interruption — that is the shape the reader expects."He was about to abandon it when the letter arrived."
was never to
was not to + base form		The negative destiny. It states that the thing never happened and that the person could not have known — and it is the most quietly devastating form in the language."Neither of them was ever to see the result."
was / were going toAn intention held at that moment, and usually one that was NOT carried out. The contrast with was to is worth hearing: destiny against plan."He was going to publish it in 1974." (and the manuscript stayed in a drawer)

The mechanical rule is simple and it is where the errors happen: after was to, would and about to, the verb goes in the base form. The time is already carried by the first half. Beyond the mechanics, notice what these forms DO: each one tells the listener that the story has already been decided, and that the narrator has judged what it added up to.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are telling somebody's story, and you already know how it ended. Choose the sentence that works.

You want a future seen from the past, with a sense of destiny:
A "He was to became the most quoted economist."
B "He was to become the most quoted economist."
C "He was to becoming the most quoted economist."
You want to say what she did later, narrated from earlier:
A "She would went on to found three institutions."
B "She would go on to found three institutions."
C "She would going on to found three institutions."
Something was on the point of happening and was interrupted:
A "He was about to abandon it when the letter arrived."
B "He was about to abandoning it when the letter arrived."
C "He was about abandon it when the letter arrived."
You want to say that they never saw the outcome, and could not have known:
A "Neither of them was ever to see the result."
B "Neither of them would never see the result."
C "Neither of them was never seeing the result."
You want to say an intention was held and not carried out:
A "He was to publish it in 1974, and did not."
B "He was going to publish it in 1974, and the manuscript stayed in a drawer."
C "He would publish it in 1974, and did not."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
