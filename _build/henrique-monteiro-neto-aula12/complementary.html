Lesson 12 -- He Would Go On to Become

Podcast
"Fresh Air Remembers Obituary Writer Jim Nicholson" — NPR

A man who spent a career writing about people nobody had heard of, and who explains why those are the best ones to write. Connection to Lesson 12: it is the craft behind today's text, and it is full of the forms in the grammar table. Transcript on the page.

Tip: listen for fifteen minutes. Then write, in your head or on paper, the first sentence of an obituary for somebody in your own family. One sentence is the whole exercise.

"What I Learned From 2,000 Obituaries" — Lux Narayan, TED

Six minutes on what the first sentences of two thousand obituaries have in common, analysed like data. Connection to Lesson 12: it answers the question the lesson ends on — what actually gets written in that first line. English transcript on the page.

Tip: this one is short. Watch it twice, and the second time write down the verbs he says appear most often. They are all in today's vocabulary set.

"How to Talk About the Future in the Past" — BBC English Masterclass

A short treatment of exactly today's forms, including the difference between was to and was going to. Connection to Lesson 12: the grammar kept outside the class, as you asked, in under fifteen minutes.
