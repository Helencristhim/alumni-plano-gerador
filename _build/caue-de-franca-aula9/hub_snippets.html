The Brazilian Bubble -- I Wish I Had Talked to More Locals

A trip to Orlando where everybody spoke Portuguese, and what you would do differently. Key words: a bubble, to stick together, to blend in, to stand out, to feel out of place, a local, a tourist trap, homesick, in hindsight, a missed opportunity, to look back on, to make the most of. Structure: wish and if only.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A bubble -- a small group or place where you feel protected from the world outside
"In Orlando, we lived in a Brazilian bubble."
To stick together -- to stay close to each other as a group
"Tourists from the same country often stick together."
To blend in -- to look or act like the people around you, so nobody notices you
"I tried to blend in, but my accent gave me away."
To stand out -- to be very easy to notice
"With my camera and my map, I really stood out."
To feel out of place -- to feel that you do not belong in a place or situation
"On the first day, I felt out of place."
A local -- a person who lives in the place you are visiting
"Ask a local where to eat, not the hotel."
A tourist trap -- a place that is expensive and full of tourists, and not very good
"That restaurant is a tourist trap."
Homesick -- sad because you are far from home and you miss it
"After two weeks, I felt a little homesick."
In hindsight -- when you think about it later, with more information
"In hindsight, I should have stayed longer."
A missed opportunity -- a chance that you did not use
"Not speaking English there was a missed opportunity."
To look back on -- to think about something in the past
"When I look back on that trip, I laugh."
To make the most of -- to use a situation in the best possible way
"Next time, I’ll make the most of every day."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At the beginning of the year, Cauê went to Orlando. In every store, someone was speaking Portuguese. It was a Brazilian bubble, and the Brazilians stuck together. He did not stand out, but he did not practice either.

In hindsight, it was a missed opportunity. “I wish I had talked to more locals,” he says. “If only we hadn’t gone to every tourist trap together!”

When he looks back on the trip, he smiles. “I wish I spoke more naturally,” he says, “but next time I’ll make the most of every conversation.”

1. Why does the text say “I wish I had talked” and not “I wish I talked”?
A Because had talked is more polite than talked when you speak about other people.
B Because the wish is about the past, about the trip to Orlando.
C Because talk is an irregular verb, and irregular verbs always need had after wish.
2. Why did Cauê not practice his English in Orlando?
A Because the locals did not want to talk to tourists from Brazil or from other countries.
B Because he felt homesick and stayed in the hotel for most of the trip.
C Because he was in a Brazilian bubble, and the Brazilians stuck together.
3. Which wish is about now?
A “I wish I spoke more naturally.”
B “I wish I had talked to more locals when I was there in the past.”
C “If only we hadn’t gone to every tourist trap together on that trip!”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Wish and If Only -- One Step Back in Time

GRAMMAR

After wish and if only, the verb goes one step back. For a wish about now, use the past simple (were for be) or could. For a regret about the past, use the past perfect. For other people’s behavior, use would.

JobFormExample
A wish about nowwish + past simple (were for be)I wish I spoke more naturally. I wish I were there.
A regret about the pastwish + past perfectI wish I had talked to more locals.
An ability you wantwish + could + verbI wish I could tell jokes in English.
Stronger feelingIf only + the same tensesIf only we hadn’t stuck together!
Other people’s behaviorwish + someone + wouldI wish people would speak more slowly.
The trapwish takes one tense backNot I wish I have or I wish I talked for the past. Say I wish I had talked.

Never: I wish I have · I wish I talked (about the past) · I wish I can · We stuck together each other
