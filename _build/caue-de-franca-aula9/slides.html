Chapter 3: The Code

I Wish I Had ...

Regrets and wishes, one tense back

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Wishes Have in Common?

"I wish I spoke more naturally."

"I wish I had talked to more locals in Orlando."

"If only we hadn’t stuck together all the time!"

"I wish I could tell jokes in English."

Look at the blue verbs: spoke, could. Are these wishes about the past, or about now? Now look at the red verbs: had talked, hadn’t stuck. Those wishes are about Orlando, about the past. Which tense do they use?

JobFormExample
A wish about nowwish + past simple (were for be)I wish I spoke more naturally. I wish I were there.
A regret about the pastwish + past perfectI wish I had talked to more locals.
An ability you wantwish + could + verbI wish I could tell jokes in English.
Stronger feelingIf only + the same tensesIf only we hadn’t stuck together!
Other people’s behaviorwish + someone + wouldI wish people would speak more slowly.
The trapwish takes one tense backNot I wish I have or I wish I talked for the past. Say I wish I had talked.

In one line: after wish, go one step back: now > past simple, the past > past perfect.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Story, at Real Speed

One conversation, three recordings, one checklist

Dialogue

Looking Back on Orlando

P
So, Cauê, you’ve been to the States before, right?
C
Yes, to Orlando, at the beginning of the year. But to be honest, I wish I had used more English there.
P
Really? Didn’t you have to speak English all the time?
C
Not really. It was a Brazilian bubble. In every store, someone was speaking Portuguese, so we all stuck together.
P
That happens here in Toronto too. When I arrived, I only spoke to people from my city for months. I felt out of place everywhere else.
C
In hindsight, it was a missed opportunity. I wish I had talked to more locals.
P
Well, if only everybody looked back on a trip and learned something! What would you do differently next time?
C
I’d leave the bubble on day one, and make the most of every conversation. And I wish I could tell jokes in English. That’s my next goal.

Listening 1 · A Travel Writer

The Expat Bubble

A British travel writer talks about why people abroad end up living in a bubble. Sound first — no text.

1. Why do people stick together abroad, according to him?
Because it is comfortable: same language, same jokes, same food.
2. What does he wish he had done in his first year abroad?
Learned the language faster, instead of staying with other British people.
3. What is his advice?
Make one local friend in the first week.
Listening 2 · An American in Brazil

If Only I Had Known

An American woman who lived in Brazil talks about her first months there. Sound first — no text.

1. What did she do in her first months in Brazil?
She only spoke English, with other foreigners.
2. What does she wish she had done?
Taken Portuguese classes from the first week.
3. What does she wish Brazilians would do?
Answer her in Portuguese, not switch to English.
Listening 3 · A Tour Guide

How to Blend In

An Australian tour guide gives advice on how to get out of the tourist bubble. Sound first — no text.

1. What is his first piece of advice?
Stay away from tourist traps and ask a local where they eat.
2. What does he say about standing out?
You will stand out anyway, and that is fine; just try.
3. What does he say about feeling homesick?
It is normal, and talking to people helps.
