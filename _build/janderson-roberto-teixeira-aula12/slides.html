Lesson 12 · Plan B

If This Happens

Half of a professional conversation is about things that have not happened yet and might. English has one structure for all of them, and it takes about four minutes to learn.

Chapter 1: Plan B

The Thing That Might Go Wrong This Week

One thing in your week that could fall apart, and what you will do if it does. Two sentences.

Where You Start

Real, and Still Possible

The condition

"If the client says no..." -- possible, and not decided yet

The consequence

"...we will go back to the first option."

The point

You sound prepared instead of hopeful

Anybody can describe a plan. What makes somebody sound senior in a meeting is describing the plan and the branch off it in the same breath: if this happens, we do that. It is one small structure, and in your job you need it in almost every conversation about money.

Chapter 3: The Code

If It Happens, We Will

One structure for everything that has not happened yet and might

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"If he asks about the fees, I will send him the table."

"If they turn it down, we will go back to the first option."

"Unless somebody objects, we will go ahead on Friday."

"I will call you as soon as I know."

Every one of these talks about the future. Look at the verb right after if, unless and as soon as. Something is missing there that you would expect. What is it?

FormWhat it saysExample
if + present simple, will + verbA real possibility in the future. The condition half never takes will."If he asks, I will send it."
NOT "if he will ask"
Either orderYou can start with either half. If the if half comes first, it takes a comma."If it rains, we will move it."
"We will move it if it rains."
unless = if notOne word instead of two, and the verb after it is positive."Unless somebody objects, we will go ahead."
= if nobody objects
may / might / canThe consequence does not have to be certain. Swap will for a weaker verb."If he asks, I may need the table."
"If we wait, we might lose it."
when, as soon as, before, afterSame rule: no will after these either, even though the meaning is future."I will call you as soon as I know."
NOT "as soon as I will know"

The whole structure is one habit: the half with if stays in the present, and the future lives in the other half. Portuguese puts the future in both, which is exactly why this takes practice rather than explanation.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"If he will ask about the fees, I will send the table."
"If he asks about the fees, I will send the table."
"I will call you as soon as I will know."
"I will call you as soon as I know."
"Unless they do not accept, we will go ahead."
"Unless they accept, we will go back to the first option."

After if, unless, when, as soon as, before and after, English uses the present even when it is clearly talking about the future. And unless already means if not, so a second negative inside it turns the sentence upside down. Read the correct versions aloud twice.

J
Thursday at ten, unless he calls it off. Chances are he will not, but I want a backup plan.
O
Fine. If he calls it off, what do we do?
J
If he calls it off, we send the summary anyway and we ask for fifteen minutes on Monday.
O

J
Worst case, two weeks. That is a setback, not a disaster. Will you be around on Monday if we need it?

Listening

If the Market Moves

A short radio segment. Sound first -- no text.

1. What does the speaker say most people do when a number falls?
They decide in the first hour, which is the worst hour to decide anything in.
2. What does she recommend writing down in advance?
What you will do if it drops, and at which point -- before it drops, not after.
3. What is her final point about a plan with no branch in it?
That it is a hope, not a plan.
Listening 2

Two Things Could Still Go Wrong

A voice message about a meeting. Sound first -- no text.

1. What are the two things that could go wrong?
The client may call it off, and the second signature may not arrive in time.
2. What will they do if the meeting is called off?
Send the summary anyway and ask for fifteen minutes on Monday.
3. What does he ask her to do before Thursday?
To make sure the numbers are in the file, so that nobody has to look for them during the call.

The Habit

One Branch, Out Loud

Before the next class, take one thing in your diary each day and say the branch out loud: if this does not happen, I will. One sentence, present in the first half, future in the second.

You already build these branches all day in your head, in Portuguese. Today you only moved the future to the correct half of the sentence.
