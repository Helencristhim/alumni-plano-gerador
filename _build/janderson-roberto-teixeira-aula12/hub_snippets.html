If This Happens -- Plans, Branches and the Word That Never Takes Will

How to describe a plan and what you will do if it changes, in one structure. Key expressions: a backup plan, to go ahead, to call off, to make sure, a drop, chances are, to turn down, worst case, a setback, to bear in mind. Structure: the first conditional with if and unless.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. These ten stay here in your material -- they do not disappear after the class.

A backup plan -- a second plan you use if the first one fails
"If there is no backup plan, there is no plan."
To go ahead -- to continue with something as it was agreed
"If nobody objects by Friday, we will go ahead."
To call off -- to cancel something that was planned
"They called off the meeting an hour before it started."
To make sure -- to check that something will really happen
"Make sure he has the numbers before he opens the call."
A drop -- a fall in a number, a price or a level
"A drop of two per cent is not a crisis."
Chances are -- it is probable that
"Chances are he will ask about the fees first."
To turn down -- to refuse an offer or a request
"If they turn it down, we will know by Wednesday."
Worst case -- the worst thing that could realistically happen
"Worst case, we lose two weeks and nothing else."
A setback -- a problem that delays progress
"It is a setback, not a disaster, and there is a difference."
To bear in mind -- to remember something when you make a decision
"Bear in mind that he decides nothing without his brother."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Every plan Janderson makes has two halves, and only one of them is ever written down. The first half is Thursday at ten. The second half is what happens if Thursday at ten does not survive until Thursday, and that half lives in his head, in Portuguese, where nobody else can see it.

This week there are two things that could go wrong. The client may call off the meeting, which he has done twice before and which is nobody's fault. And the second signature may not arrive in time, in which case nothing goes out at all. Neither is a disaster. Both are a setback, and there is a difference worth defending out loud.

So the backup plan is simple. If he calls it off, they send the summary anyway and ask for fifteen minutes on Monday. If he turns the whole thing down, they go back to the first option and lose two weeks. Worst case, two weeks. Chances are none of it happens and the meeting goes ahead exactly as agreed.

One thing to bear in mind: this client decides nothing without his brother, who will not be in the room. So the real work is not the call. It is making sure the numbers are in the file before it starts, so that whatever happens next happens without anybody looking for a document. A drop of two per cent will not change that. A missing table will.

1. "If he calls it off, they will send the summary anyway." Why is there no will in the first half?
A Because English never uses will in a sentence with if.
B Because the half that starts with if stays in the present, and the future lives in the other half.
C Because calling off is a past action.
2. According to the text, what is the difference between a setback and a disaster?
A A setback delays progress; a disaster ends it. The text says the difference is worth defending out loud.
B There is no real difference.
C A setback is always somebody's fault.
3. What is the real work before the call, according to the last paragraph?
A Persuading the brother to attend.
B Making sure the numbers are in the file, so nobody has to look for a document.
C Rewriting the summary.
4. What does chances are mean in the third paragraph?
A It is certain that.
B It is probable that.
C It is impossible that.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The First Conditional

GRAMMAR

One structure covers everything that has not happened yet and realistically might. The only difficulty is that Portuguese puts the future in both halves and English puts it in one.

FormWhat it saysExample
if + present simple, will + verbA real possibility. The if half never takes will."If he asks, I will send it."
NOT "if he will ask"
Either orderBoth halves can come first. Starting with if adds a comma."If it rains, we will move it."
"We will move it if it rains."
unless = if notOne word for two, and the verb after it stays positive."Unless somebody objects, we will go ahead."
may / might / canThe consequence can be uncertain too. Swap will for something weaker."If we wait, we might lose it."
when, as soon as, before, afterSame rule: present after all of them, even for the future."I will call you as soon as I know."
The imperative versionVery common at work: an instruction plus a condition."Call me if anything changes."

In a meeting this structure does one specific thing for you: it makes you sound prepared. Anybody can present a plan. Presenting the plan and its branch in the same breath is what senior people do, and it is one sentence.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are planning something at work that might still change. Choose the best thing to say.

You want to describe a real possibility:
A If he will ask about the fees, I will send the table.
B If he asks about the fees, I will send the table.
C If he asks about the fees, I send the table.
You want to say you will call the moment you have the answer:
A I will call you as soon as I will know.
B I will call you as soon as I know.
C I call you as soon as I will know.
You want to say the plan continues if nobody objects:
A Unless somebody does not object, we will go ahead.
B Unless somebody objects, we will go ahead.
C Unless somebody will object, we will go ahead.
You want to make the consequence less certain:
A If we wait, we might lose the window.
B If we will wait, we lose the window.
C If we wait, we lose maybe the window.
A client asks what happens if the number falls:
A In the eventuality of a negative variation we would evaluate the alternatives.
B If it drops below that level, we will move half of it. Worst case, we lose two weeks.
C It is impossible to know what will happen.
