"The first conditional" -- 6 Minute Grammar, BBC Learning English

Six minutes on today's exact structure, including the two things that cost most: no will after if, and what unless really means. Connection to Lesson 12: it drills the same sentence shape with different examples until the present tense in the first half stops feeling strange.

Tip: pause after each example and build the same branch about your own week. Then say it again starting with the other half, to prove the order does not matter.

"Would you like a robot companion?" -- 6 Minute English, BBC Learning English

Six minutes of speculation about something that has not happened yet, which means it is full of conditionals from beginning to end. Connection to Lesson 12: you hear if, unless and might doing exactly the work you practised, in a conversation rather than a table.

Tip: listen once without the transcript. Then find three conditional sentences and check where the future is in each one. It is never in the if half.

"How frustration can make us more creative" -- Tim Harford, TED

Fifteen minutes from an economist about what happens when the plan falls apart. Connection to Lesson 12: today you built the branch; he argues that the branch is often where the good outcome was hiding. He is also unusually clear and slow for a TED speaker.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Then think of one setback from your own year that turned out better than the original plan, and describe it out loud in three sentences.
