Warm-up — From Lesson 20

Review

In Lesson 20 you worked on how you sound: your pace, stress, and a confident voice, never a flat monotone. Today you are halfway through the program. You will look back at Lessons 11-20, learn the words to talk about your own fluency, and get ready for a short speaking check in class. (Fluency is not perfect English. Fluency is keeping going.)

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Fluency -- the ability to speak easily, without stopping all the time
"My fluency is much better than six months ago."
Hesitate -- to stop for a moment because you are not sure
"I hesitated when they asked about the budget."
Filler -- a short word or phrase that fills a pause and gives you time to think
"Let me think is a smart filler for a meeting."
Rephrase -- to say the same idea again, in different words
"If you forget a word, rephrase and keep going."
Progress -- moving forward and getting better at something
"You have made real progress since March."
Setback -- a problem that slows you down for a time
"The London call was a setback, but I learned from it."
Comfort zone -- the situations where you feel safe, with no challenge
"Leading a call in English is outside my comfort zone."
Goal -- something you want to achieve and work toward
"My goal is to lead the status call in English."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Before this program, Daniela used to plan every sentence in Portuguese before a meeting. In March she started her English lessons, and last month she gave the SAP talk at the retail tech conference. Since July she has run three project calls in English — a big step outside her comfort zone. There was one setback: on the London call, she hesitated on the budget question. Since then, she has been using a smart filler: "Let me think." Her next goal is clear. In November she is going to lead the weekly status call with the US team, and if she prepares three key points before each call, she will feel confident.

1. What did Daniela use to do before this program?
A She planned every sentence in Portuguese.
B She led every status call with the US team in English.
C She gave talks at retail tech conferences every month.
2. Which sentence is about something that started in the past and is still going?
A Last month she gave the SAP talk at the conference.
B She has been using a smart filler.
C In November she is going to lead the weekly status call.
3. What is Daniela going to do in November?
A Give another SAP talk at the retail tech conference in London.
B Start her English lessons.
C Lead the weekly status call.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip — The Time Map (Lessons 11-20)

GRAMMAR

No new grammar today. This is one map for the tenses you already know.

TimeFormExample
Old habitused to + verbI used to plan every sentence.
Finished, with a timepast simpleLast month I gave the talk.
Up to now, no timehave / has + past participleI have run three calls.
Started and still goinghave been + -ingI have been using fillers since July.
Planam / is / are going to + verbI am going to lead the call.
Real conditionif + present, will + verbIf I prepare, I will feel confident.
RememberA finished time (last month, in March, two years ago) takes the past simple, never "have". Since and so far go with have. Negative: I didn't / I haven't / I'm not going to. Question: Did you...? / Have you...? / Are you going to...?
