Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A good tutorial has a simple structure: the result first, then the steps in order, each one with a signal word like first, then or once. But real processes are rarely done by one person. Part of the work is done for you, and English has a special structure to say that.

When somebody else does a service for you, use have + object + past participle: “I had my hair cut last Friday.” If you say “I cut my hair”, English understands that you held the scissors yourself. In informal speech, get works the same way: “She got her transcript translated.” If it matters who did the work, add by: “by a certified translator”.

The tense goes on have or get, never on the main verb: “We are getting our documents checked,” “I will have the letter notarized.” The same structure also describes something bad that happened to you: “He had his phone stolen on the first day.”

Be careful with the order. “I had my hair cut” is a service; “I had cut my hair” is the past perfect, and it means you did it yourself, earlier. A certified translation, a transcript sent from Brazil and a letter stamped by a notary: none of them is done by you, and all of them need the causative.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Causative: Have and Get Something Done

GRAMMAR

Have or get, then the object, then the past participle: the work was done for you.

JobFormExample
A servicehave + object + past participleI had my hair cut.
Informalget + object + past participleShe got it translated.
Who did itcausative + by + persontranslated by a certified translator
Futurewill have / am going to get + object + participleI will have it notarized.
In progressam / are getting + object + participleWe are getting it checked.
Something badhave + object + participleHe had his phone stolen.

Never: I cut my hair at the salon (if somebody else did it) · she had translated her transcript by a translator · get notarize the letter · we are having check our documents
