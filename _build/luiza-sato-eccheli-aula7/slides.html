Warm-up

The Target

A Process in Three Moves

1. Say what the result will be before the first step.

2. Give the steps in order, with one clear signal word for each.

3. Say what you do yourself, and what you have somebody else do for you.

Chapter 3: The Code

Who Actually Did It?

Four sentences where the speaker did not do the work, and one where she did

Grammar Discovery

Did She Cut It Herself?

"I had my hair cut last Friday."

"She got her transcript translated by a certified translator."

"I need to have the letter notarized before I apply."

"We are getting our documents checked this week."

In each sentence, who did the work: the speaker, or somebody else? Look at the order of the words after had and got. Where is the object, and what form is the verb?

JobFormExample
Somebody does a service for youhave + object + past participleI had my hair cut.
The same, more informalget + object + past participleShe got her transcript translated.
Say who did itcausative + by + personI had it translated by a certified translator.
Any tensethe tense is on have or getWe are getting it checked. I will have it notarized.
Something bad that happened to youhave + object + past participleI had my phone stolen in the metro.
A sequence of stepsfirst, then, once, after that, finallyFirst I get it translated, then I have it notarized.
The trapnever I cut my hair if somebody else did it; never had cut my hairNot I had cut my hair. Say I had my hair cut.

In one line: Have or get, then the object, then the past participle: the work was done for you, not by you.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

A Salon in London

One conversation, two recordings, one receipt

Dialogue

Just a Trim, Please

M
Hi there. Do you have an appointment, or are you a walk-in?
L
I booked online for three o’clock. Luiza. I would like to have my hair cut, but only a trim.
M
Of course. It is a bit long on the sides. Do you want to keep the layers?
L
Yes, please. And no bangs. I had bangs cut once before a trip, and I spent the whole month regretting it.
M
No bangs, understood. Are you here on holiday, or are you studying?
L
Studying. I am on an exchange at the law school, so this week is all paperwork. I had my transcript sent from Brazil, and now I need to get it checked.
M
That sounds painful. Did they ask for a certified translation as well?
L
They did. I got it translated in São Paulo, but they also want one letter notarized here, so first I have it stamped, and then I take it to the office.
M
Well, at least your hair will look good in the photos. Some clients ask for highlights before a new start. Maybe next time?
L
Maybe next time. Can you show me how you blend the ends? I learn everything from tutorials, but I can never do it the same way.
Comprehension

About Marco

1. What does Marco ask Luiza first?
Whether she has an appointment or is a walk-in.
2. What does Marco notice about her hair, and what does he ask about the layers?
That it is a bit long on the sides. He asks if she wants to keep the layers.
3. What does Marco ask about the paperwork?
Whether they asked for a certified translation as well.
4. What does Marco suggest for next time, and why does he mention the photos?
Highlights. He jokes that at least her hair will look good in the photos.
Listening 1 · A Creator

How I Film a Tutorial

A beauty creator explains her process. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she decide before she touches a brush, and why?
The result, because if she does not know where she is going, the video wanders.
2. What does she do before she starts filming?
She does the whole look once off camera and writes every step on a card.
3. What does she have other people do for her, and what does she say makes the best tutorial?
Her lighting is set up by a friend and her videos are edited by someone else. The best tutorial is one where every step has a reason.
Listening 2 · A Voicemail

Your Documents, In Order

A message from an exchange office. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the first step, and what happens if the transcript is not in English?
Request the official transcript. If it is not in English, it must be translated by a certified translator.
2. What kind of translation will not be accepted?
A simple translation made by a friend or an app.
3. How must the documents be sent, and what happens to students whose documents are incomplete?
All three together in a single email. They will have to register late, with fewer electives to choose from.
