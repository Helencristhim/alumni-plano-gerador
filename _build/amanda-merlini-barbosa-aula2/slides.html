Chapter 3: The First Call

Thirty Minutes With the Other Side

A first conversation with the legal team you are joining, line by line

Dialogue

The Call Before the Move

M
Amanda, good to meet you. I am Martin Reis, from the legal team here. Tell me where you are now.
A
I am the legal manager of an agribusiness group, and I have been there for four years.
M
Four years. And before that? I would rather hear the story than read the profile.
A
I started in a law firm in two thousand and thirteen, and I stayed for three years. Then I moved in-house, and I have never gone back.
M
Why not? Most people go the other way when the money is better.
A
Because in a firm I wrote the opinion and somebody else decided. In-house I am in the room, and I sign off on the contract myself.
M
Fair. Tell me about one contract that went wrong.
A
In two thousand and twenty-two a buyer stopped paying. The jurisdiction clause sent us abroad, so the case was slow and expensive. Since then I have read that clause first, in every contract.
M
That is the answer I was hoping for. Have you ever worked on a carbon project?
A
Not yet. But I have spent eleven years on contracts in a sector that lives on land, and the regulation is where the work is going.
Comprehension

About the Conversation

1. What does Martin say he would rather hear?
The story behind the profile, not the profile itself.
2. What reason is given for staying in-house instead of going back to a firm?
In a firm you write the opinion and somebody else decides; in-house you are in the room and you sign off on the contract yourself.
3. What went wrong with the contract in two thousand and twenty-two, and what changed after it?
A buyer stopped paying and the jurisdiction clause sent the case abroad, so it was slow and expensive. Since then the jurisdiction clause is the first thing she reads.
Chapter 4: The Code

Is There a Date in the Sentence?

That is the whole decision

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I have worked in agribusiness for eleven years."

"I started in a law firm in two thousand and thirteen."

"I have never gone back to a law firm."

"The buyer stopped paying in two thousand and twenty-two."

Two of them say when it happened. Two do not. Which is which?

FormUseExample
Present perfect
have / has + past participle		the time is not finished, or you do not say when: experience, a period that reaches today, a result you still haveI have worked here for four years. I have never worked on a carbon project.
Past simplethe time is finished and usually said: a year, a date, last month, when I was at the firmI started in 2013. The buyer stopped paying in 2022.
for and sincefor + how long (for four years). since + when it started (since 2021). Both live with the present perfect, never with the present: say "I have worked here since 2021", never "I work here since 2021".
already, yet, justalready before the verb, yet at the end of a negative or a question, just before the verb: "I have already read it", "I have not signed it yet", "He has just called".
The one questionIs there a finished time in the sentence? Yes, past simple. No, present perfect. When the answer starts general and then gives a date, the tense changes mid-answer, and that is correct.

Sound Check

Say It Clearly

The -ed ending · the verbs of this lesson

Why this one is hard for a Portuguese speaker

The habit: every written ending gets a vowel, so worked comes out with three syllables and signed with two.

The rule: the -ed adds a syllable only after a t or a d sound. started, decided, wanted. Everywhere else it is a single consonant glued to the word: worked, signed, moved, stopped.

Why it matters: an extra syllable makes a past sound like a present, and the whole career story loses its timeline.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

The Brief and the Model

What they will ask you, and how another lawyer tells the same story

Listening 1

A Message Before the Call

Martin tells you what Friday is going to be. Sound first, no text.

1. How long is the call, and what does Martin say it is not?
About thirty minutes, and it is a conversation, not an interview.
2. What three things is he interested in?
Where she started, why she moved from the law firm to the company side, and what she has been doing since she took over the department.
3. Why does he ask about a contract that went wrong?
Because that is where he learns the most about a lawyer.
Listening 2

Somebody Else's Two Minutes

A lawyer tells the short version of her career. Sound first, no text.

1. What did she learn in the law firm, and what did she dislike about it?
She learned to draft, and she never met the client twice.
2. What changed when she moved in-house?
She was suddenly in the room where the decision happened.
3. What work turned out to be the most interesting she has ever done, and why is that surprising?
Environmental compliance, and it is surprising because nobody in her team wanted it.

1. Which two lines need the present perfect, and which two need the past simple?
Currently and Track record take the present perfect (I have been, I have negotiated). Before that and First role take the past simple, because the years are finished.
2. The card says "no carbon project yet". Turn that line into a full sentence you could say out loud.
Example: "I have not worked on a carbon project yet, but I have spent eleven years on contracts in a land-based sector."
3. Why is a track record more useful on this card than a list of skills?
Because a track record is proof of what somebody already did, and a list of skills is only a claim.
