Podcast
NPR -- "Hockey fan wins scholarship for warning Canucks staffer of cancerous mole"

A stranger in the crowd held a phone against the glass to tell a team manager that the mole on his neck looked wrong, and it was a melanoma. The whole story is the chronology this lesson trains -- what happened, what had already been there, and what would have happened without the warning. Clear American English, with the transcript on the page.

Tip: listen once. Then retell the whole story out loud in the past, using at least three sentences with had plus the participle for what happened before the appointment.

Audiobook
Siddhartha Mukherjee -- "The Emperor of All Maladies" (audiobook preview)

A biography of cancer, written by an oncologist, and the best long-form model in English of the thing you practised today: a chronology told so that the reader always knows what came before what. Every chapter is a case report at book length.

Tip: listen to the preview and count the sentences built with had plus the participle. In a narrative this dense they are everywhere, and once you hear them you stop losing the order in your own.

Music
The Beatles -- "She's Leaving Home"

Three and a half minutes that are pure narrative tense. The song tells a story in strict chronological layers -- what she did that morning, what had already been decided, what the parents were doing while it happened -- and it is sung slowly enough to hear every auxiliary.

Tip: read the lyrics and mark every verb. Separate them into three columns: the main line, what had happened before, and what was still going on. That is the same table you filled in class.
