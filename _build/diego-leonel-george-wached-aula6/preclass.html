Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

The Case Report -- The Chronology of a Melanoma

Eighteen months of story behind one lesion, and one paragraph that has to put it all in order. Key words: a lesion, a biopsy, a margin, to excise, pigmented, asymmetry, a border, a delay in presentation, to note, a stage, to metastasise, to be lost to follow-up. Structure: narrative tenses -- past perfect and past continuous.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A lesion -- any area of the body that is damaged or abnormal
"The lesion measured nine millimetres at presentation."
A biopsy -- taking a small piece of tissue to examine under a microscope
"A biopsy was performed at the first visit."
A margin -- the healthy skin removed around a tumour, to be sure nothing is left
"The margins were clear on histology."
To excise -- to cut something out completely, with surgery
"The lesion was excised under local anaesthetic."
Pigmented -- coloured by the pigment that gives skin its colour
"A pigmented lesion on the left shoulder."
Asymmetry -- having two halves that do not match
"Asymmetry and an irregular border were noted."
A border -- the border of a lesion, seen from outside
"The border was irregular and poorly defined."
A delay in presentation -- the time a patient waits before coming for help
"There had been an eighteen-month delay in presentation."
To note -- to write down something you observed
"Asymmetry was noted at the first examination."
A stage -- the stage a cancer has reached when it is found
"At the time of excision the disease was stage two."
To metastasise -- to spread from the first place to another part of the body
"By then the tumour had metastasised to a regional node."
To be lost to follow-up -- when a patient stops attending and cannot be reached
"She had been lost to follow-up for eleven months."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

In a case report the tense is not decoration. It is the only thing telling the reader what came first -- and in oncology what came first is most of the diagnosis. Read the same four facts twice and you will see it.

The main line of the story sits in the past simple: a fifty-four year old woman presented to the clinic in March with a pigmented lesion on the left shoulder. A biopsy was performed at the first visit. The lesion was excised with clear margins.

Everything that happened before that line goes into had + participle. The lesion had changed eighteen months earlier. Her daughter had noticed that the border was no longer regular. She had shown it to a pharmacist and had been told that it was probably nothing. And, crucially: by the time she was seen, the tumour had metastasised. Two years before, at another service, she had been lost to follow-up for eleven months, which somebody noted and nobody acted on.

And what was still running at the time takes was + -ing: while she was waiting for the appointment she had booked in January, the lesion began to bleed. Background, then interruption. Put those three tenses in the right places and eighteen months fit into one paragraph without a single date being needed twice. Get them wrong and the reader cannot tell whether the delay in presentation came before or after the stage was known -- which is the whole point of the report.

1. "The lesion had changed eighteen months earlier." Why not "changed"?
A Because change is an irregular verb.
B Because that event is earlier than the main line of the story, and had plus the participle is what marks it.
C Because eighteen months is a long period.
2. "While she was waiting for the appointment, the lesion began to bleed." What is the job of was waiting?
A It is the background that was already running when the other event interrupted it.
B It means the waiting happened before everything else.
C It is a polite way of saying she waited.
3. Why would "She had presented to the clinic in March and then we excised it" be wrong?
A Because present cannot take the past perfect.
B Because March is a named month.
C Because nothing in that sentence happened earlier than the main line -- the past perfect marks anteriority, and here there is none.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Narrative Tenses -- Past Perfect and Past Continuous

GRAMMAR

Three pasts, three jobs: what happened, what had happened before it, and what was still running at the time.

JobFormExample
The main line of the storypast simpleShe presented in March.
Earlier than that linehad + past participleThe lesion had changed eighteen months earlier.
Still running at the timewas / were + -ingWhile she was waiting, it began to bleed.
Background + interruptionwas -ing + past simpleShe was travelling when the referral arrived.
Time words for the earlier pastby then, by the time, before, earlier, alreadyBy the time she was seen, it had spread.
Negative and questionhad not + participle / had + subjectShe had not attended the appointment.
Irregular participles herespread → spread · show → shown · tell → told · take → takenShe had been told it was nothing.

Never: she came in March and the mole changed before · by then the tumour had spreaded · she had presented in March and then we excised it (nothing earlier here) · while she was knowing about the risk · the biopsy was performing at the first visit

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to say that the mole changed BEFORE the patient came to you. You say:
A The patient came in March and the mole changed before.
B The patient presented in March. The lesion had changed eighteen months earlier.
C The patient was coming in March when the mole changed.
The bleeding happened during the wait for the appointment. You say:
A While she waited for the appointment, it was beginning to bleed.
B While she was waiting for the appointment, it began to bleed.
C She had waited for the appointment and it bled.
Nothing happened earlier than the main line. You say:
A She had presented in March and then we excised it.
B She presented in March and the lesion was excised the following week.
C She was presenting in March and we had excised it.
A reviewer asks about a gap in the record. The right answer is:
A She had been lost to follow-up for eleven months at another service.
B She disappeared, but it is not important for this case.
C There was a period without information which we did not include.
