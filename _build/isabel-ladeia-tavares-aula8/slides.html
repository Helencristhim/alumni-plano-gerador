Chapter 3: The Code

The Small Words Next to the Number

Direction, size, and compared with what

Grammar Discovery

By, To, At, As

"Germination rose by fifty percentage points in three days."

"It rose sharply from ten percent to sixty, and peaked at eighty-five."

"The soaked group was roughly twice as high as the control."

"The first site fell slightly, whereas the third more than doubled."

The verbs are simple. The meaning lives in the small words next to the numbers. What does each one tell you?

UseFormExample
The size of the changerise / fall + by + amountIt rose by twenty percent.
Where it endedrise / fall + to + valueIt fell to nineteen.
Start and endfrom X to YIt rose from ten to sixty.
The highest pointpeak at + valueIt peaked at eighty-five.
How fastverb + sharply / steadily / slightlyIt rose sharply.
Comparing two groupstwice as high as / three times higher than / whereasTwice as high as the control.
The traprise has no object; raise needs oneThe values rose. (Not: the values raised.)

In one line: by is how much, to is where it ended, and as…as compares.

Listening 1 · Figure 3

Tell Me What It Shows

A supervisor describes a figure to the group. Draw the two lines while you listen. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is on each axis, and what are the two groups?
Days, from zero to fourteen, on the horizontal axis, and the percentage of seeds that germinated on the vertical one. The control, and the seeds soaked overnight.
2. Describe the green line from day zero to day ten.
Almost nothing for three days, then a sharp rise from about ten to about sixty percent between day three and day six, then it slows down and levels off at roughly eighty-five by day ten.
3. What does he say about day eight, and what does he refuse to say?
Day eight in the control is an outlier, one tray, and he would not build an argument on it. He refuses to say why the soaking works, because that comes later.
Listening 2 · A Voice Message

Three Sites, One Sentence

A colleague reads you her results table before she sends it. Write down all six numbers. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What did she count, where, and when?
The same species at three sites, in March and again in August.
2. What happened at each of the three sites?
The first fell slightly, from forty-two to thirty-eight. The second fell sharply, from fifty-five to nineteen, losing roughly two thirds. The third rose from twelve to thirty, more than double.
3. What was wrong with her first summary sentence, and what does she suggest instead?
It said the population declined across all sites, which is not true. She suggests: it fell sharply at one site, barely changed at another, and more than doubled at the third.

Figure 2. Number of individuals of the same species counted at three sites (S1–S3) in March and August. Counts fell slightly at S1 (42 to 38) and sharply at S2 (55 to 19), whereas they more than doubled at S3 (12 to 30).

Supervisor’s note: good caption. Now write the sentence that goes above it in the text.
1. What does the caption say that a reader needs, without opening the text?
What was counted, at which sites, in which two months, and the direction and size of the change at each site.
2. Which site changed by the largest proportion, and in which direction?
Site 3. It rose from twelve to thirty, which is more than double. Site 2 lost roughly two thirds, which is the largest fall.
3. Say the summary sentence for the text, describing without explaining.
Counts declined at two of the three sites, sharply at S2 and only slightly at S1, whereas they more than doubled at S3.
