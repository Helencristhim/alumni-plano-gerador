Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

Numbers on the Page -- Tables, Figures and Captions

Most researchers read the figures before the text. Today you learn to describe what a figure shows, before anybody explains it. Key words: a trend, a peak, a decline, to fluctuate, to level off, steadily, sharply, roughly, an outlier, a caption, an axis, a proportion. Structure: rise and fall by and to, peak at, and twice as high as.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Draw the shape of each trend word with your finger.

A trend -- the general direction in which numbers move over time, ignoring the small ups and downs
"Look for the trend before you look at any single point."
A peak -- the highest point that a value reaches before it stops rising or starts to fall
"The peak came on day nine, at about eighty percent."
A decline -- a fall in a number or amount, usually over a period of time
"There was a sharp decline at the second site."
To fluctuate -- to go up and down again and again, without a clear direction
"The temperature fluctuated between eighteen and twenty-two degrees."
To level off -- to stop rising or falling and stay at about the same level
"After day ten, the green line levels off."
Steadily -- at a regular, even speed, without sudden jumps
"The control group rose steadily for two weeks."
Sharply -- suddenly and by a large amount
"It rose sharply between day three and day six."
Roughly -- approximately, not exactly, when the exact number does not matter
"The soaked group was roughly twice as high as the control."
An outlier -- one value that is very far from all the others and does not fit the pattern
"There is one outlier, on day eight in the control."
A caption -- the short text under a figure or table that says exactly what it shows
"A good caption lets the figure stand on its own."
An axis -- one of the two lines of a graph, with the scale for what is being measured
"Check where the vertical axis starts before you look at the lines."
A proportion -- a part of a whole, usually given as a fraction or a percentage
"A large proportion of the seeds did not germinate."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Isabel’s supervisor shows the group a figure about seed germination. The soaked seeds do almost nothing for three days. Then germination rises sharply from about ten percent to sixty, and it peaks at roughly eighty-five on day ten before it levels off. The control rises steadily and finishes at about forty.

Two small words do most of the work. By tells you the size of the change: germination rose by fifty percentage points in three days. To tells you where it ended: the control rose to forty. And when two groups are compared, the comparison has a fixed frame: the soaked group was roughly twice as high as the control, whereas the control never jumped at all.

What her supervisor does not say is just as important. He does not say why soaking works. A results section describes what happened. The explanation belongs to the discussion, and a sentence that tries to do both usually claims more than the figure can show.

1. "Germination rose by fifty percentage points." What does by tell you?
A Where the line ended at the very end of the experiment, after fourteen days.
B Who carried out the measurement, as in a passive sentence with an agent.
C The size of the change.
2. The control "rose to forty." What do you know?
A That it rose by forty percentage points, which is a very large change for a control.
B Where the control ended: at about forty percent.
C That the control was twice as high as the soaked group at the end.
3. Why does the supervisor not say why soaking works?
A Because explaining belongs to the discussion. The results section only describes.
B Because he does not know the answer and does not want the group to find out.
C Because the figure clearly proves it, so there is no need to say it out loud.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Small Words Next to the Number

GRAMMAR

By is how much, to is where it ended, and as…as compares.

UseFormExample
The size of the changerise / fall + by + amountIt rose by twenty percent.
Where it endedrise / fall + to + valueIt fell to nineteen.
Start and endfrom X to YIt rose from ten to sixty.
The highest pointpeak at + valueIt peaked at eighty-five.
How fastverb + sharply / steadily / slightlyIt rose sharply.
Comparingtwice as high as / three times higher than / whereasTwice as high as the control.

Never: it increased 20% · the values raised · two times higher as · the figure proves that…

Stage 2: Put the Description in Order

Order

Put this description of a figure in the order the reading recommends: what it shows, the trend, the comparison, and the outlier last.

?"It peaked at roughly eighty-five percent and then leveled off."
?"The control, by contrast, rose steadily to about forty."
?"There is one outlier, on day eight in the control."
?"Figure 3 shows the percentage of seeds that germinated over fourteen days."
?"The soaked group rose sharply between day three and day six."
?"By the end, the soaked group was roughly twice as high as the control."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

Your supervisor asks you to describe a figure at the group meeting. The best first sentence is:
A "This figure proves that our treatment works much better than anything else we tried."
B "So, there is an outlier on the right, which is really strange and interesting."
C "Figure 2 shows the number of individuals counted at three sites in March and August."
A value went from 42 to 38. You want to say how much it changed. You say:
A "It fell slightly, by four."
B "It fell to four, which is a small change compared with the other sites in the table."
C "It raised by four individuals between March and August."
One group finished at 80 percent and the other at 40. You say:
A "The first group was two times higher as the second group at the end of the experiment."
B "The first group was twice as high as the second."
C "The first group was more high than the second."
A colleague wrote: "The population declined across all sites." Two sites fell and one more than doubled. You suggest:
A "Counts fell at two sites, whereas they more than doubled at the third."
B "It is fine. Nobody reads the results section carefully, so a general summary is enough."
C "Write that the population increased, because the third site had the biggest change."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Choose one graph from a paper in your area, or from your placement. In one minute, describe it out loud in the order from the reading: what it shows, the trend, one comparison with by, to or twice as, and the outlier, if there is one. Describe only. No explanations.
