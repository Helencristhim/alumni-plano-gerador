Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

Used to, and Now -- Who You Were Before You Left

The interesting part of a change is never the new habit. It is the old one you cannot believe you had. Key words: a routine, a comfort zone, homesick, gradually, a turning point, independent, self-discipline, to take something for granted, to get used to something, to look back on something. Structure: used to for past habits and states, and the trap next to it.

Vocabulary

A routine -- the things you do at the same time every day without deciding to
"My routine there had four training evenings in it, and everything else moved around them."
A comfort zone -- the set of situations where nothing surprises you any more
"I did not leave my comfort zone by flying. I left it in week two, on a bus."
Homesick -- missing your own home so much that you can feel it in your week
"I was homesick for about eleven days, and then the season started."
Gradually -- a little at a time, so slowly that you only notice afterwards
"Nothing changed on a particular day. It changed gradually, and then it had changed."
A turning point -- the moment after which nothing goes back to how it was
"The turning point was the first away game, when nobody sat next to me."
Independent -- able to do things without needing somebody else to organise them for you
"Living alone does not make you independent. Solving a problem at eleven at night does."
Self-discipline -- making yourself do something at the moment nobody is checking
"Self-discipline is not a personality. It is a small number of decisions made in advance."
To take something for granted -- to stop noticing something good because it is always there
"I took my own language for granted until the day nobody in the room spoke it."
To get used to something -- to slowly stop finding something strange or difficult
"It took me three weeks to get used to a school where nobody repeated anything."
To look back on something -- to think about a period of your life that is already over
"When I look back on that first month, the hard part was not the language."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Before he left, his week had three training evenings in it. He used to train on Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday, he used to be the quiet one on any new team, and he did not use to read anything in English at all. Every one of those sentences describes something that is over, and that is the whole job of used to plus the base form. Notice the negative: it is did not use to, with no d, because the past is already carried by did. The question works the same way: Did you use to play competitively?

Two words away there is a structure that means something almost opposite. After a month abroad, he got used to training in the dark at six, and today he is used to reading in English without noticing that it is English. Here used to is not a past tense at all: it is an adjective phrase about being adapted, and what follows it is a noun or a verb in -ing, never the base form. He got used to train is wrong; he got used to training is right.

One more structure belongs here, and it is useful in a story. Would plus the base form describes a repeated past action: every Saturday we would take the same bus. It cannot be used for states, so I would be the quiet one is wrong and I used to be the quiet one is right. And the vocabulary of change fits around all of this: nothing happened at a turning point, it happened gradually; he was homesick for eleven days; and he had taken his own language for granted until the day nobody in the room spoke it.

1. Why is it "did not use to read" and not "did not used to read"?
A Because "read" is an irregular verb.
B Because the past is already carried by "did", so the -d on "use" disappears.
C Because the sentence is negative, and negatives never take -d.
2. What is the difference between "I used to train" and "I got used to training"?
A The first is a habit that is over. The second is something that became normal and still is.
B They mean the same thing, and the second is simply more formal.
C The first is about the present and the second is about the past.
3. The text says "I would be the quiet one" is wrong. Why?
A Because "would" is only used for the future.
B Because "quiet" cannot follow the verb to be.
C Because "would" describes repeated actions and cannot be used for states. The sentence needs "used to be".

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used to, and the Trap Beside It

GRAMMAR

Used to plus the base form is a habit that ended. Be or get used to plus -ing is a habit that became normal.

JobFormExample
A past habit that is overused to + base formI used to train three times a week.
A past state that is overused to + be / have / liveI used to be the quiet one on any team.
Negativedid not use to + base form (no -d)I did not use to read in English.
QuestionDid you use to + base form?Did you use to play competitively?
Becoming adaptedget used to + noun or verb-ingI got used to training in the dark.
Being adapted nowbe used to + noun or verb-ingI am used to reading in English now.
A repeated past action, in a storywould + base form (never for states)Every Saturday we would take the same bus.

Never: I got used to train · I did not used to read · before Canada I train three times a week · I would be the quiet one.

Quiz

A "Before Canada, I train three times a week."

C "Before Canada, I was used to train three times a week."

A "After a month I got used to training in the dark."
B "After a month I got used to train in the dark."
C "After a month I used to train in the dark."
You are saying that something was not part of your life before. You say:
A "I did not used to read anything in English."
