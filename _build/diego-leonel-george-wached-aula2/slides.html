Chapter 4: The Code

The Voice That Hides the Doer

Why the paper says were enrolled and not we enrolled

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"Two hundred and forty adults were enrolled at four centers."

"Severity was assessed by two dermatologists."

"The cream is applied once a day."

"The authors conclude that once a day is enough."

In the first three, the red verb has two parts and the sentence starts with the thing that received the action. In the fourth one it starts with the people who did it. Which three are built the same way?

JobFormExample
The doer does not matterbe + past participleAdults were enrolled at four centers.
A finished study, pastwas / were + participleSeverity was assessed at week twelve.
A general instruction, presentis / are + participleThe cream is applied once a day.
The doer DOES matterby + the doer, at the endassessed by two dermatologists
Negativewas / were not + participleNine patients were not included in the analysis.
Questionwas / were + subject + participleWas adherence measured?
The trapnever drop the verb beNot the patients enrolled in four centers, if you mean somebody enrolled them.

In one line: put the thing that received the action first, and build the verb with be plus the participle.

Listening 1 · A Researcher

The Study, in Ninety Seconds

A researcher presenting his own trial at a congress. Sound first — no text.

1. How many people were enrolled, and where?
Two hundred and forty adults, at four centers.
2. What was the main outcome, and when was it measured?
The change in severity from baseline, measured at week twelve.
3. What limitation does he admit, and what does he say about adherence?
The follow-up was short, only twelve weeks. He says adherence was better in the once-daily group, and that better adherence was associated with a better outcome, without claiming it caused it.
Listening 2 · An Editor

Why Papers Get Sent Back

A journal editor on the sentence that gets a paper rejected. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the single most common reason she sends a paper back?
The conclusion claims that one thing caused another, when the study only shows that the two appeared together.
2. What does she say about the word significant?
That authors use it as if it meant large, when it only means unlikely to be chance, and that a significant difference can be far too small to matter to a patient.
3. What does she say she likes to see in a paper?
A limitations section written by authors who found the weakness themselves, before a reviewer had to point it out.
