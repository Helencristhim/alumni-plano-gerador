Podcast
NPR Short Wave -- "The toll of social media and smartphones on teen mental health"

Thirteen minutes in which two science journalists take a set of correlational studies apart on air, and keep repeating the sentence this lesson turns on: correlation is not causation. The topic is teenagers and screens, but the reasoning is the reasoning of any results section, in plain, slow American English, with the transcript on the same page.

Tip: after listening, take the abstract you read this week and find one sentence that reports an association. Say out loud, in English, what the study shows and what it does not prove.

Audiobook
David Spiegelhalter -- "The Art of Statistics" (audiobook preview)

A Cambridge statistician explaining, in ordinary English, what a study can and cannot show -- including the word significant, which he spends real time on. The free preview is the opening of the book, read at a comfortable speed, and it is the vocabulary of every results section you read.

Tip: listen with the passive voice in mind. Write down five sentences built with was or were plus a participle, and say each one out loud.

Music
Simon & Garfunkel -- "The Boxer"

Five minutes of extremely clear diction, and a lyric that turns on a passive line most people never notice: I have squandered my resistance for a pocketful of mumbles, such are promises -- and later, all lies and jest, still a man hears what he wants to hear. Slow, sung, and full of narrative past.

Tip: read the lyrics while you listen. Find every line where the person who acts is not named, and say who it could be. That is the same choice the paper makes.
