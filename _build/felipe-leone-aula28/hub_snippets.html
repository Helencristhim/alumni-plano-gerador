Lesson 28 -- Pre-class

Leading and Inspiring the Team -- Difficulty, Help and Encourage

Ten to twenty minutes before class. How to say what people have difficulty doing, and how you help and encourage them. Key words: to delegate, a deadline, to encourage, a mentor, feedback, to lead, motivated.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To delegate -- to give a task to another person on your team
"I had to learn to delegate the weekly reports."
A deadline -- the last day or time to finish a piece of work
"We moved the deadline to Friday."
To encourage -- to give a person the confidence to do something
"My first manager encouraged me to present in English."
A mentor -- a more experienced person who gives you advice at work
"My mentor helped me to plan my career."
Feedback -- the comments that you get about your work
"I give my analysts feedback every two weeks."
To lead -- to be the person in charge of a group or a meeting
"She led the meeting with the client."
Motivated -- with a strong wish to do something well
"The team is very motivated this quarter."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Tom is a new team leader at a bank in Boston. In his first month, he had difficulty saying no to his old colleagues, and the team had difficulty finishing the reports on time.

His manager helped him to organize the work and encouraged him to talk to each person on the team. Now Tom encourages his analysts to bring a solution, not only a problem.

"Tom had difficulty saying no." What does it mean?
A He said no to everybody.
B He never said no.
C Saying no was hard for him.
What did his manager do?
A She helped him to organize the work.
B She did the reports for him.
C She told the team to work faster.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Difficulty, Help, Encourage

GRAMMAR

After difficulty, use the -ing form. After help and encourage, use a person and then to + verb.

JobFormExample
A problem with an actionhave difficulty + -ingI have difficulty following fast calls.
Support from a personhelp someone (to) + verbShe helped me to prepare the presentation.
Confidence from a personencourage someone to + verbI encourage my team to ask questions.

Never: I have difficulty to speak · I have difficult in speaking · I encourage my team share ideas

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

Listen first, then put the lines in order. A manager and an analyst talk after a meeting. Put their conversation in a natural order.

Step 1: listen to the whole conversation. Step 2: put the lines in the order you heard.
?"Thanks. Can I lead the call, or should you do it?"
?"You looked worried in the meeting. Is everything OK?"
?"You lead it. I encourage you to try, and I will be there."
?"I can help you to prepare for the next call."
?"Not really. I have difficulty explaining the model to this client."

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen, then record yourself. Say difficulty with the stress on the first syllable: DI-fficulty.

My team has difficulty meeting deadlines.
She encouraged me to lead the call.
Can you help me to plan the week?

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A colleague asks why the report is late. You say:
A "We had difficult to get the data."
B "We had difficulty getting the data."
C "We had difficulty to get the data."
You want an analyst to share ideas in meetings. You say:
A "I encourage you sharing your ideas."
B "I encourage you share your ideas."
C "I encourage you to share your ideas."
Your manager supported you before a big presentation. You say:
A "She helped me to prepare it."
B "She helped to me prepare it."
C "She helped me preparing it."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Think of a person who helped you at work. Answer in about one minute: what did you have difficulty doing then, how did this person help you, and what do you encourage your team to do today?
