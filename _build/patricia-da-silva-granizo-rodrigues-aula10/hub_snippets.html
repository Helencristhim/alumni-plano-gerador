Lesson 10 -- Pre-class

Checkpoint -- The Full Picture

The ten expressions that let you agree and disagree in the same breath, and the linkers that make a claim survive a room full of objections. Key words: granted, having said that, whereas, by and large, more often than not, to be fair, in practice, all things considered, to put it another way, on balance. Structure: concessive and contrastive linkers. This lesson also reviews the nine before it.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read it inside a real sentence. All ten do the same job: they let you hold two ideas that pull against each other.

Granted -- used to accept a point before you argue against it
"Granted, the benchmark lags. It does not explain the pattern under one year."
Having said that -- used to introduce a qualification to what you have just said
"Attrition is down. Having said that, two of the leavers were the two I could not lose."
Whereas -- used to contrast two facts of equal weight in one sentence
"Attrition is flat in the plants, whereas it is rising in the office."
By and large -- generally, with some exceptions
"By and large the rollout landed, and the exceptions are both in Marietta."
More often than not -- in most cases, but not always
"More often than not, the problem is the first ninety days."
To be fair -- used before you concede a point to the other side
"To be fair, nobody had ever told him Friday was the deadline."
In practice -- how something actually works, as opposed to how it is designed
"On paper it is at-will. In practice California gives the employee much more."
All things considered -- after weighing everything up
"All things considered, I would take the seven-month date and defend it."
To put it another way -- used to restate something more clearly
"To put it another way: we are not losing people, we are failing to keep new ones."
On balance -- after weighing the arguments on both sides
"On balance, I would move the budget rather than ask for more."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

Although the budget is flat, we are opening two sites this year. Attrition under one year of service has come down eleven points since March, whereas attrition over three years has not moved -- which is what we expected and what we wanted. By and large the onboarding redesign has done what we said it would do.

Having said that, it has worked in three sites out of five, and the two that are missing are the two that need it most. Granted, we lost the two people who were building the engineering pipeline. That is true and it does not solve anything. To put it another way: the problem is not that we do not know what to do, it is that we have not staffed it. All things considered, I would rather have this problem than the one we had in January.

1. Although the budget is flat, we are opening two sites. What would be wrong with adding but before we?
A Nothing -- it would make the contrast stronger.
B English uses although or but, never both in the same sentence.
C But cannot begin a main clause.
2. Granted, we lost two people. That is true and it does not solve anything. What is the writer doing?
A Conceding a point and immediately limiting how much work it is allowed to do.
B Apologising for a result and moving on.
C Blaming the departure of two people for the whole problem.
3. Attrition under one year has come down, whereas attrition over three years has not moved. What does whereas signal?
A That the second fact contradicts the first.
B That the two facts are contrasted and both stand.
C That the second fact is more important than the first.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Concession and Contrast

GRAMMAR

Conceding is not politeness and it is not weakness. It is what makes the part you do NOT concede impossible to dismiss -- and it is the single most reliable way to be believed in an American meeting.

FormWhat it doesExample
although / even though + clauseconcedes and continues in ONE sentenceAlthough the budget is flat, we are opening two sites.
whereascontrasts two facts of equal weight, without either one winningAttrition is flat in the plants, whereas it is rising in the office.
having said that / that saidconcedes AFTER a full stop, at the start of the next sentence... Having said that, the trend is not the biggest problem.
granted, ... butconcedes your opponent point OUT LOUD before you answer itGranted, benchmarks lag. But the leavers are on market pay.
the trapAlthough ... but ... in the same sentence. Portuguese allows the pair; English uses one or the other, never both. Same for despite of, which does not exist: it is despite or in spite of.

Two traps that survive B2 in Portuguese speakers: although ... but in the same sentence, and despite of, which does not exist. It is despite or in spite of.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

One right answer each. Read the situation before you look at the options.

You want to accept a point and then answer it, in one sentence.
A Although the benchmark lags, but the leavers are on market pay.
B Although the benchmark lags, the leavers are on market pay.
C Despite of the benchmark lagging, the leavers are on market pay.
D The benchmark lags, however but the leavers are on market pay.
Two facts, both true, pulling in different directions. Which shape?
A Attrition is flat in the plants, whereas it is rising in the office.
B Attrition is flat in the plants, whereas in the office is rising.
C Attrition is flat in the plants, however it is rising in the office.
D Attrition is flat in the plants but in the office not.
You open a mid-year review with an executive team. Where do you start?
A With the results that went well, to build credit.
B With what has not worked, in the first thirty seconds.
C With the plan for next year.
D With the context, so that everything is understood.
You concede that you lost two key people. How do you stop that excuse doing too much work?
A Explain in detail why losing them was so damaging.
B Say it is true and that it does not solve anything.
C Avoid mentioning it at all.
D Say it was outside your control.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record your answer. This is the one your teacher will build lesson eleven around.

Record about two minutes -- longer than usual, on purpose. Give your own mid-year review: start with what has not worked, concede one thing and limit it, say what has worked with a number, attach one qualification, and close with a judgement. Use at least five of the ten expressions. Then, in one extra sentence, say what you want lessons eleven to thirty to do. Do not read -- speak.
