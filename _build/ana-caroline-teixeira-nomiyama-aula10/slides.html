Chapter 4: The Code

The Recipe That..., the Kitchen Where...

Who, which, that, where and whose

Grammar Discovery

Who, Which or Where?

"Yeast is the small living thing which makes the dough rise."

"More and more people who work long hours are baking on Saturdays."

"The dough needs a warm place where it can rise."

"The result is a loaf whose crust is crispy."

Look at the blue words, then at the red word after each one. A thing takes which. People take who. A place takes where. And whose is like its or their: the crust of the loaf. Each red word joins two ideas in one sentence.

JobFormExample
Whofor peopleA baker who teaches weekend classes.
Whichfor things and animalsThe yeast which I use is dry.
Thatfor people or things; very common when we speakThe bread that I baked was crispy.
Wherefor placesThe kitchen is the place where I relax.
Whosefor possession, like his, her, its or theirA friend whose bread is amazing.
The trapdo not repeat the person or the thing at the endNot the recipe that I made it. Say the recipe that I made.

In one line: people: who; things: which; people or things: that; places: where; possession: whose.

Listening 1 · A London Baker

The Quietest Morning of the Week

A British baker talks about the busy people who come to her Saturday classes. Sound first — no text.

1. Who usually comes to her Saturday classes?
Busy people: lawyers, doctors and teachers.
2. What changes when the students knead the dough?
They stop talking about work.
3. Which part of the class surprises them most, and why?
The waiting: they want to do something, but bread needs time.
Listening 2 · A Home Baker

My Grandmother’s Notebook

An Australian man tries to bake the bread that made his grandmother famous. Sound first — no text.

1. Why was his grandmother famous in her town?
Because of her bread: people came to her house every Sunday to buy a loaf.
2. What was wrong with his first loaf?
It did not rise, and it was as hard as a rock.
3. What does he think is the secret of her bread?
Her oven: a big old one which she used for everything.
Listening 3 · A Chef in New York

Bread With No Yeast

A Dutch chef remembers the first time he tried Brazilian cheese bread. Sound first — no text.

1. Where did he try Brazilian cheese bread for the first time?
In a small cafe in New York where a Brazilian couple worked.
2. What two things are NOT in cheese bread, according to the owner?
Wheat flour and yeast.
3. When does he make it today?
On Sundays, for friends who visit him.
