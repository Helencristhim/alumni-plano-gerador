Weekend Baking -- The Recipe That..., the Kitchen Where...

Why busy people bake bread on weekends, and how to describe recipes, people and places. Key words: dough, to knead, flour, to rise, an oven, a recipe, to bake, yeast, a loaf, crispy. Structure: relative clauses with who, which, that, where and whose.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Dough -- a soft mix of flour and water that you make into bread
"The dough is sticky, so add a little more flour."
To knead -- to press, push and fold dough again and again with your hands
"Knead the dough for ten minutes, until it is smooth."
Flour -- a white or brown powder made from wheat or other plants, used for bread and cakes
"You need five hundred grams of flour for one loaf."
To rise -- to grow bigger and go up, like dough in a warm place
"Leave the dough in a warm place to rise for an hour."
An oven -- a hot box in the kitchen where you cook or bake food
"Heat the oven before you put the bread in."
A recipe -- a list of ingredients and the steps to cook something
"This is a recipe that my grandmother wrote by hand."
To bake -- to cook bread or cakes in an oven
"I bake bread almost every Saturday morning."
Yeast -- a very small living thing that makes dough grow bigger
"Without yeast, the bread stays flat and hard."
A loaf -- bread that is baked in one piece, in one shape
"I baked two loaves: one for us and one for my neighbor."
Crispy -- hard and dry on the outside in a good way; it makes a noise when you bite it
"I love bread with a crispy crust and a soft inside."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

From Monday to Friday, Ana is a lawyer who works until eight at night. On Saturdays, after her English class, she goes to the place where she relaxes the most: her kitchen. She opens an old notebook which has all her favorite recipes.

This week she wants to bake a loaf of bread. She mixes the flour, the water and the yeast, and she kneads the dough for ten minutes. While it rises, she walks her dog in the park.

In the afternoon, her husband, whose favorite snack is cheese bread, asks for something more. So she makes cheese bread too, in the same oven. The bread that comes out is crispy outside and soft inside.

1. Why does the text say “a lawyer who works” and not “a lawyer which works”?
A Because which is only for questions, and the sentence about Ana is not a question at all.
B Because a lawyer is a person, and we use who for people.
C Because works is in the present.
2. What does “her husband, whose favorite snack is cheese bread” mean?
A Her husband makes cheese bread for her every Saturday afternoon, in the same oven as the loaf.
B Her husband does not like cheese bread.
C Cheese bread is the favorite snack of her husband.
3. What does Ana do while the dough rises?
A She walks her dog in the park.
B She works on a case.
C She reads the recipes in her old notebook and writes a new one for the cheese bread.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Who, Which, That, Where, Whose

GRAMMAR

A relative clause joins two ideas in one sentence. Use who for people, which for things, that for people or things, where for places and whose for possession. Do not repeat the person or the thing at the end of the clause.

JobFormExample
Whofor peopleA baker who teaches weekend classes.
Whichfor things and animalsThe yeast which I use is dry.
Thatfor people or things; very common when we speakThe bread that I baked was crispy.
Wherefor placesThe kitchen is the place where I relax.
Whosefor possession, like his, her, its or theirA friend whose bread is amazing.
The trapdo not repeat the person or the thing at the endNot the recipe that I made it. Say the recipe that I made.

Never: the baker which teaches · the recipe that I made it · the kitchen which I bake · a friend who her bread

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A colleague asks who taught you to bake. You say:
A "My mother. She is the person which taught me everything that I know about the kitchen."
B "My mother. She is the person who taught me."
C "My mother. She is the person where taught me."
You want to describe your favorite place at home. You say:
A "The kitchen is the place which I relax every Saturday morning after my English class."
B "The kitchen is the place who I relax."
C "The kitchen is the place where I relax."
You show a friend an old recipe. You say:
A "This is the recipe that my grandmother wrote."
B "This is the recipe that my grandmother wrote it by hand in her old notebook."
