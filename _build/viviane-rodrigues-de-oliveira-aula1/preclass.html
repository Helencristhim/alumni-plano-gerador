Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

What You Actually Do -- The Language of Your Role

Saying what you do at work, not just your job title. Key words: onboarding, engagement, performance, turnover, hiring, recognition, survey, retention, workplace culture, leadership development. Structures: present simple for your role and routine, "I am responsible for", and how to make a question with do and does.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the definition. You use all of these in Portuguese every day.

Onboarding -- the first weeks of a new person in the company
"I take care of the onboarding of every new leader."
Engagement -- how connected and motivated people feel at work
"We measure engagement twice a year."
Performance -- how well a person does their job
"Every leader has a performance conversation with me."
Turnover -- the number of people who leave the company
"Turnover is high in the stores, not in the office."
Hiring -- the process of choosing and bringing in new people
"Hiring a new manager takes about six weeks."
Recognition -- showing people that their work is valued
"Our recognition programme is very simple."
Survey -- a set of questions you send to people to collect opinions
"I read every comment in the survey."
Retention -- keeping good people in the company
"Retention is my biggest problem this year."
Workplace culture -- the way people behave and work together in a company
"My job is workplace culture, not paperwork."
Leadership development -- helping managers learn how to lead a team
"Leadership development is my main project now."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Viviane works as an HR manager at a retail group. She is responsible for workplace culture, performance and engagement. Every year she runs the survey and she reads every comment. She also takes care of the onboarding of new leaders. Turnover is high in the stores, so retention is her biggest problem this year. Her team works on two things: better onboarding in the first month, and recognition for the store leaders. When a recruiter asks her "What do you do?", she does not say only her job title. She gives three sentences and one example.

1. Why does the text say "she works" and not "she work"?
A With he, she and it, the present simple always adds an -s.
B Because the sentence is in the past.
C Because "work" is an irregular verb.
2. The text says "she is responsible for culture", not "she responsible for culture". Why?
A Because "responsible" is a verb.
B Because "responsible" needs the verb to be: am, is or are.
C Because the sentence is a question.
3. Why is there a "do" in "What do you do?"
A Questions in the present simple need do or does.
B It is a mistake in the text.
C It makes the question more polite.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple and Questions

GRAMMAR

How to say what you do, and how the question comes back to you.

FormUseExample
I / you / we + verbYour job and your routine -- things that are always true.I work in HR. We measure engagement.
he / she + verb + sThird person always takes an -s.She works in HR. He leads the team.
am / is / are + responsible forWith responsible, use the verb to be. Never I work responsible.I am responsible for engagement.
Question: Do / DoesQuestions need do or does, and the verb goes back to the base form.Do you work in HR? / Does she lead the team?
Negativedo not / does not + base formI do not work with payroll. She does not lead that team.
Two trapsNever I am work in HR -- choose one verb. Never What you do? -- the question needs do.
