Lesson 05 -- Pre-class

Stage Left, Please -- Instructions at the Ceremony

You imagine the night of the award ceremony and the instructions of the staff. Key words: a badge, the entrance, an usher, a seat, backstage, to line up, a cue, a host, a rehearsal, a dress code. Structure: first conditional with if, when and as soon as.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A badge -- a card with your name that you wear at an event
"As soon as you get your badge, go to the entrance."
The entrance -- the door or place where you go into a building
"The winners’ entrance is on the left side."
An usher -- a person who shows people where to go or where to sit at an event
"If you get lost, ask an usher."
A seat -- a place where you sit
"Your seat is in row F, seat twelve."
Backstage -- the area behind the stage, where people wait before they go on
"Please wait backstage until we call you."
To line up -- to stand in a line, one person after another
"The winners line up at the side of the stage."
A cue -- a signal that tells you it is time to do something
"Your cue is your project name."
A host -- the person who presents an event and speaks to the audience
"The host will read the name of each project."
A rehearsal -- a practice of an event before the real event
"There is a short rehearsal at five o’clock."
A dress code -- the rules about what clothes to wear at an event
"The dress code is smart, not formal."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The stage manager talks to the winners one hour before the show. “As soon as the host reads your category, go backstage. There, you will line up in the order of the screen.”

“When the host says your project name, walk up to the middle of the stage. That is your cue. You will have about thirty seconds for a photo.”

“And if you miss your cue, don’t worry. An usher will tell you when to go. Please wear your badge all night.”

1. What do the winners do as soon as the host reads their category?
A They go backstage.
B They go to the bar.
C They make a speech.
2. Why does the text say “if you miss your cue” and not “if you will miss your cue”?
A Because after if, the verb stays in the present, even for the future.
B Because the cue is in the past.
C Because the stage manager is British.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “When the host will say your name, walk up.”
B “When the host says your name, walk up.”
C “When the host said your name, you will walk up.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- First Conditional -- If, When and As Soon As

GRAMMAR

For a real future, use if (possible), when (sure) or as soon as (immediately) + present. In the other part, use will or an instruction.

JobFormExample
A possible futureif + present, will + verbIf they call your name, you will go up.
A sure futurewhen + presentWhen the music starts, walk to the left.
Immediately afteras soon as + presentAs soon as you get your badge, go in.
Instructionsif / when + present, imperativeIf you get lost, ask an usher.
Negativedon’t / doesn’t ... won’tIf you don’t have a badge, they won’t let you in.
The trapno will after if, when or as soon asNot if they will call. Say if they call.

Never: If they will call your name · When the music will start · As soon as you will arrive · If you don’t have it, they don’t let you in tomorrow

Quiz
