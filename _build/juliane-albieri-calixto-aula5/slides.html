Chapter 3: The Code

If They Call Your Name

Three words for the future: if, when, as soon as

Grammar Discovery

Four Instructions for the Night

"If they call your name, you will go up to the stage."

"When the music starts, walk to the left."

"As soon as you get your badge, go to the entrance."

"If you don’t have your badge, they won’t let you in."

All four sentences talk about the future: the night of the ceremony. But look at the verb right after the red words: call, starts, get, don’t have. It is in the present, not with will. The other part has will or an instruction (walk, go). And the red words are different: if is possible, when is sure, as soon as is immediately.

JobFormExample
A possible futureif + present, will + verbIf they call your name, you will go up.
A sure futurewhen + presentWhen the music starts, walk to the left.
Immediately afteras soon as + presentAs soon as you get your badge, go in.
Instructionsif / when + present, imperativeIf you get lost, ask an usher.
Negativedon’t / doesn’t ... won’tIf you don’t have a badge, they won’t let you in.
The trapno will after if, when or as soon asNot if they will call. Say if they call.

In one line: if, when and as soon as + present; will or an instruction in the other part.

Chapter 4: The Briefing

The Briefing

Dialogue

Lena’s Briefing

L
Hi, you must be Juliane. I’m Lena. I’m in charge of the winners tonight. Do you have your badge?
J
Yes, here it is. Sorry, I’m a bit nervous. Where do I go first?
L
Don’t worry, everybody is nervous. First, there is a short rehearsal at five. As soon as it finishes, an usher will take you to your seat.
J
OK. And when do I go up?
L
When the host starts your category, go backstage and line up on the left side.
J
Sorry, could you say that again, a little more slowly?
L
Of course. When your category starts, go backstage. Line up on the left. Your cue is your project name.
J
So if the host says my project name, I’ll walk up to the stage. Is that right?
L
Exactly. I’ll be at the side of the stage all night. If you need anything, look for me. II’ve done this for eight years: it always works.rsquo;ve done this for ten years: it always works.

Listening 1 · The Stage Manager

Instructions for the Winners

A stage manager talks to a group of winners at the rehearsal, one hour before the show. Sound first — no text.

1. What happens as soon as the host reads the category?
The winners in that category go backstage.
2. How long does each winner have on the stage?
About thirty seconds.
3. What should winners do if they miss their cue?
Stay calm and wait: an usher will tell them when to go.
Listening 2 · Last Year’s Winner

Ananya Remembers

Ananya, a designer from Delhi, won an award last year. She gives advice for the night. Sound first — no text.

1. What mistake did Ananya make last year?
She went to the wrong side of the stage.
2. What will she do if she wins again?
She will arrive early for the rehearsal.
3. What is her advice about the instructions?
If you don’t understand, ask the usher to repeat; everybody does it.
