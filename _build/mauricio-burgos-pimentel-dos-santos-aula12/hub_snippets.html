Lesson 12 — Pre-class

The First Forty-Eight Hours

Eight words for the first days of the program (orientation, jet lag, cohort, assigned, cold call, put on the spot, pre-reading, wing it) and the grammar that turns every surprise into a plan: if + present, will + verb. Around 30 minutes, in one sitting.

Stage 1.1: Words for the First Days

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say each word out loud. Eight words, three minutes.

Orientation — the first session, which explains how the program works
"Orientation starts at eight thirty in the main hall."
Jet lag — feeling tired after a long flight to a different time zone
"If I arrive on Saturday, the jet lag will be gone by Monday."
Cohort — the group of people who do the program together
"Our cohort has people from twenty countries."
Assigned — given to you by someone else — you do not choose it
"Your assigned study group is on the back of your name tag."
Cold call — when a professor asks you a question without warning
"If the professor cold calls you, answer in one sentence."
Put on the spot — to make someone answer with no time to prepare
"She put me on the spot in front of forty people."
Pre-reading — the texts you read before a session
"The pre-reading for Monday is forty pages."
Wing it — to do something without preparing
"I did not read the case, so I had to wing it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the text. Then answer the questions.

My flight lands on Saturday morning. If I sleep on the plane, the jet lag will be gone by Monday. On Sunday I will finish the pre-reading, because if the professor cold calls me, I do not want to wing it. The orientation is on Monday at eight thirty. If I am late, I will sit in the back and find my assigned study group at the break. I already know what I will say to my cohort: who I am, what my bank does, and what I want to take home. If someone puts me on the spot, I will give one sentence and one reason. And if a word disappears, I will say "hold on" and describe it.
When will his jet lag be gone, if he sleeps on the plane?
A By Monday.
B On Saturday night, before the welcome dinner.
Why is it "if the professor cold calls me" and not "if the professor will cold call me"?
A Because the professor is a person, and will is only used for things.
B After if, we use the present.
What will he do if he is late for the orientation?
A Sit in the back.
B Walk to the front and apologize to the professor.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

A plan for every surprise

If + present (the situation), will + verb (the result or your plan).

positive

If I arrive early, I will sleep.
If she calls me, I will answer.

negative

If I don’t read it, I will wing it.
If I read it, I won’t need to wing it.

question

What will you do if you are late?
Will you come if you are tired?

order

If I am late, I will sit in the back.
I will sit in the back if I am late.

× "If I will arrive early" — after if, the present: if I arrive
× "If the professor call me" — he / she / it takes -s: if the professor calls me

With if first, use a comma. With if in the middle, no comma.

Stage 4: In the room

Quiz
Which sentence is correct?
A "If I will finish the pre-reading on Sunday, I will be ready."
B "If I finish the pre-reading, I will be ready."
The professor cold calls you. What is the best answer?
A "No, I would not sell. The market will recover next year."
B "Sorry, my English is not good and I did not read everything, so maybe I am wrong."
You are tired on the first night. What will you do about the welcome dinner?
A "I will stay in the hotel, because nobody will notice."
B "I will go and leave early."

Stage 5: Your three answers

Reflection
Record one minute in English. Give your three answers for the first day: who you are, what your bank does, and what you want to take home from the program. Then finish with one plan: "If the professor cold calls me, I will ...". No apologies, and no silence longer than three seconds.
