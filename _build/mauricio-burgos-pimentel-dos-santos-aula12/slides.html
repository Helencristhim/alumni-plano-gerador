Grammar Discovery

A plan for every surprise

If you arrive early, you will have time to sleep.
If the professor cold calls you, you will have a few seconds.
If you lose your name tag, the front desk will print a new one.
You will freeze if you try to give a perfect answer.

If + present, will + verb.

The if part is a real situation that can happen. The will part is the result, or your plan. After if, use the present — never will. The two parts can change places: with if first, add a comma.

The Pattern

Situation, then plan

If + present
will / won’t + verb
If I arrive on Saturday,
the jet lag will be gone by Monday.
If I finish the pre-reading,
I won’t need to wing it.
What will you do
if the session runs late?

After if: present. "If he cold calls me" — with the -s, and never "if he will call".

if I am late, I will if she asks me, I will I won’t ... if what will you do if
Practice

Present or will?

If I ___ (arrive) on Saturday, I ___ (have) a day to rest.
If I arrive on Saturday, I will have a day to rest.
If the professor ___ (cold call) me, I ___ (answer) in one sentence.
If the professor cold calls me, I will answer in one sentence.
I ___ (not / need) to wing it if I ___ (finish) the pre-reading.
I won’t need to wing it if I finish the pre-reading.
What ___ you ___ (do) if somebody ___ (put) you on the spot?
What will you do if somebody puts you on the spot?
