Two of these are false friends waiting to happen. A grievance is not a general complaint about the coffee: it is a formal, written procedure with a deadline attached. And guidelines are exactly what the word says: they guide, they do not oblige. The moment a guideline becomes an obligation, the verb in the sentence has to change, and that is what this lesson is about.

Chapter 3: Read the Policy

The Word That Decides

Read for the Main Idea

Two Registers, One Document

The Word That Decides

A wellbeing policy is not a piece of writing about wellbeing. It is a list of obligations, and every line of it is read twice: once by an employee who wants to know what to expect, and once by a lawyer who wants to know what the company promised. That is why the verb matters more than the paragraph around it. Managers must complete the training. Grievances have to be answered in writing within five days. Nobody may investigate a complaint about their own team. Three sentences, three obligations, and no room for interpretation.

Then the same document changes register, and the change is deliberate. Managers should document the conversation. Employees may use the anonymous channel. Teams are encouraged to review workload together. Nothing there is optional by accident: a company can be held to what it wrote, so a duty of care is written with must, and everything the company recommends but cannot guarantee is written with should. Get the two mixed up and the policy either promises what it cannot deliver, or obliges people to do something nobody can be obliged to do.

Chapter 4: The Code

Obligation, Recommendation, Permission

Three strengths, three verbs

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Have in Common?

Every manager must complete the training this quarter.
A written grievance has to be answered within five days.
You should document the conversation afterwards.
Employees may use the anonymous channel.
Click to reveal the rule
Obligation: must (the rule itself speaks) and have to (an outside rule obliges you). In a policy they are interchangeable; in speech have to sounds less formal. Recommendation: should — strong advice, no consequence if you skip it. Permission: may (formal) or can (neutral). And the trap that costs money: must not is a prohibition (it is forbidden), while do not have to is the absence of obligation (it is optional). They are opposites, not variations. All of them are followed by the base verb: must complete, never must to complete.

Extract from the Wellbeing Policy

PEOPLE & CULTURE · POLICY 04.2

WELLBEING AND PSYCHOSOCIAL RISK

4.2.1 All managers must complete the psychosocial risk training each year.

4.2.2 Written grievances have to be answered within five working days.

4.2.3 Managers should review the distribution of workload every quarter.

4.2.4 Employees must report any concern through the anonymous channel.

TWO OF THESE FOUR LINES ARE WRONG

Reviewing the draft with the business

One Word per Paragraph

M
I read the draft. Honestly, it feels heavy. Everything is must this, must that.
A
Only three lines are must. Everything else is should or may. Which one felt heavy to you?
M
The training. Can we say managers should complete it, and see who does?
A
We can write should. But then, if something happens in a team, the company has no answer for why the manager was never trained.
M
And the workload paragraph? That one is a should, and it is the one I actually care about.
A
Then it has to change. If it is part of the duty of care, should is too weak. And in the same document there is the opposite problem: we wrote that employees must report a concern. Nobody can be obliged to report. That one has to become may.
M
So you are making one line stronger and one line weaker, in the same review.
A
Exactly. The strength of the verb has to match the strength of the obligation. Anything else is a promise the company cannot keep.
