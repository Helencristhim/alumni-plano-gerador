Chapter 3: The Code

As Soon As, Until, By the Time

Time clauses and sequence

Grammar Discovery

First, Then, Until When?

"As soon as the engineers finish, we will call the regulator."

"Once the report is ready, traffic will resume."

"The bridge will stay closed until they sign the report."

"By the time the regulator called, we had already closed the bridge."

Sentences 1 to 3 talk about the future. Look at the verb right after each blue word: finish, is, sign. Where is will, and where is it NOT? In sentence 4, which action happened first: the call or the closing?

JobFormExample
Immediately after a future eventas soon as + present · will in the other halfAs soon as they finish, we will call.
After something is completeonce + present (or present perfect)Once the report is ready, traffic will resume.
Up to a moment, and not beforeuntil + presentIt will stay closed until they sign the report.
One action finished before anotherby the time + past · had + past participleBy the time he called, we had already closed it.
Two things at the same timewhile + sentence · In the meantime, / Meanwhile,In the meantime, trucks use the old road.
The trapnever will right after as soon as, once, until or whenNot As soon as they will finish.

In one line: after as soon as, once, until and when, the future goes in the present tense; will goes in the other half.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Crisis, at Real Speed

One conversation, three recordings, one timeline

Dialogue

The Call at Two Thirty

C
Aguinaldo, we have a problem. The engineers found a crack in the Rio Claro bridge half an hour ago.
A
Okay. First question: is the bridge closed?
C
Yes. We closed it at two twenty. The regulator will want a full timeline before the end of the day.
A
Then let's prioritize. What happens first, and what happens after that?
C
The engineers are inspecting it now. As soon as they finish, they will send a report. In the meantime, trucks are using the old road.
A
Good. And we have a contingency plan for this, don't we?
C
We do. Traffic can resume once the engineers sign the report, but not before.
A
Perfect. I will call the regulator promptly, before they call us, and send an interim update until the report arrives.

Listening 1 · The Crisis Consultant

The First Hour

A crisis communications consultant explains what to do in the first hour of a crisis. Sound first — no text.

1. When should you inform the people who need to know?
As soon as you have the basic facts.
2. What should you do if there is no news at the time of the update?
Send the update on time anyway.
3. What do people not forgive, according to her?
Silence.
Listening 2 · The Former Regulator

How an Agency Reads a Timeline

A man who worked at a transport agency for twelve years explains what regulators look for. Sound first — no text.

1. What has a good company already done by the time the agency calls?
It has already acted.
2. Why is "in the afternoon" a problem in a timeline?
Because it is not an exact time.
3. Which companies almost never get a penalty?
Companies that escalate problems early.
Morning Radio

The Bridge Reopens

A short radio news report the morning after the bridge reopened. Sound first — no text.

1. How long was the bridge closed?
About thirty-six hours.
2. How much time did the old road add to each trip?
Roughly forty minutes.
3. What will happen until the permanent repairs are finished?
Heavy trucks will only be allowed to cross the bridge at night.

Seven Lines, No Wrong Minute

INCIDENT TIMELINE — RIO CLARO BRIDGE, KM 214
Sent to the national transport agency · prepared by the legal and operations teams
Tuesday 14:05Engineers find a crack during a routine inspection
14:20Bridge closed in both directions · contingency plan activated
14:45The agency calls · the bridge is already closed
15:30Interim route open: trucks use the old regional road
18:00First update sent to the agency, as promised
Thursday 02:10Engineers sign the report · traffic resumes
Next monthPermanent repairs start · heavy trucks cross only at night until they finish
The lawyers checked every time on this page twice. In a timeline for a regulator, one wrong hour can look like one hour of silence.
1. Was the bridge already closed by the time the agency called?
Yes. It was closed at 14:20, and the agency called at 14:45.
2. What happened in the meantime, while the bridge was closed?
Trucks used the old regional road.
3. When did traffic resume?
On Thursday at 02:10, once the engineers signed the report.
