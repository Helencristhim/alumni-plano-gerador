Lesson 19 -- Pre-class

Precision Under Pressure -- Time Clauses

A bridge on a concession highway closes, and the regulator wants a timeline. Key words: a timeline, to escalate, a contingency plan, promptly, beforehand, in the meantime, to resume, interim, a time frame, to prioritize. Structure: as soon as, once, until and by the time.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A timeline -- a list of events in the order in which they happened or will happen
"The regulator wants a full timeline by six o'clock."
To escalate -- to send a problem to a person with more authority
"If the agency does not answer, we will escalate it to the minister."
A contingency plan -- a plan for what to do if something goes wrong
"Every concession needs a contingency plan for a bridge closure."
Promptly -- quickly and without delay
"The company informed the agency promptly."
Beforehand -- before a particular event or time
"Read the report beforehand, not during the meeting."
In the meantime -- during the time between two events
"The engineers arrive at four. In the meantime, trucks use the old road."
To resume -- to start again after a pause
"Traffic resumed on Thursday morning."
Interim -- temporary, until something permanent is ready
"The old road is an interim solution."
A time frame -- the period of time in which something must happen
"What is the time frame for the repairs?"
To prioritize -- to decide what is most important and do it first
"In a crisis, you have to prioritize."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At five past two, engineers find a crack in a bridge on a concession highway. The company closes the bridge at two twenty. By the time the regulator calls, at two forty-five, the bridge has already been closed for twenty-five minutes.

Aguinaldo prepares the timeline. The engineers are inspecting the bridge. As soon as they finish, they will send a report. In the meantime, trucks use the old road, an interim route.

The bridge will stay closed until the engineers sign the report. Once they sign it, traffic will resume. Notice: after as soon as, until and once, the verb is in the present, even for the future.

1. Was the bridge open when the regulator called?
A Yes, because the company waited for the regulator to call before it did anything at all.
B No, it was already closed.
C Yes, but only for trucks, because the old regional road was closed for repairs that afternoon.
2. Why does the text say "as soon as they finish" and not "as soon as they will finish"?
A Because after as soon as we use the present, even for the future.
B Because "will finish" is only for formal texts, and this text is an informal story about a bridge.
C Because the engineers already finished the inspection, so the sentence is about the past, not the future.
3. When will traffic resume?
A As soon as the regulator calls the company at two forty-five and asks for the full timeline.
B Once the engineers sign the report.
C Before the engineers finish the inspection, because the old road is too slow for the trucks.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Time Clauses: As Soon As, Once, Until, By the Time

GRAMMAR

After as soon as, once, until and when, use the present for the future. With by the time, use had + past participle for the action that happened first.

JobFormExample
Immediately after a future eventas soon as + present · will in the other halfAs soon as they finish, we will call.
After something is completeonce + present (or present perfect)Once the report is ready, traffic will resume.
Up to a moment, and not beforeuntil + presentIt will stay closed until they sign the report.
One action finished before anotherby the time + past · had + past participleBy the time he called, we had already closed it.
Two things at the same timewhile + sentence · In the meantime, / Meanwhile,In the meantime, trucks use the old road.
The trapnever will right after as soon as, once, until or whenNot As soon as they will finish.

Never: As soon as they will finish · until the report will be ready · By the time he called, we already closed it

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The engineers will finish soon, and you will call the regulator immediately after. You say:
A "As soon as the engineers will finish the inspection, I will call the regulator to explain."
B "As soon as they finish, I will call."
C "Until the engineers finish the inspection, I will call the regulator to explain everything."
The agency called at 14:45. You closed the bridge at 14:20. You say:
A "By the time they called, we had closed it."
B "By the time the agency called us this afternoon, we already close the bridge in both directions."
C "Until the agency called us this afternoon, we have closed the bridge in both directions."
The bridge cannot open before the report is signed. You say:
A "The bridge will stay closed as soon as the engineers sign the report on Thursday."
B "It will stay closed until they sign the report."
C "The bridge will stay closed until the engineers will sign the report on Thursday morning."
The engineers are working on the bridge. During that time, trucks use another road. You say:
A "Beforehand, the trucks will use the old regional road while the engineers are working."
B "In the meantime, trucks will use the old road."
C "Promptly, the trucks will use the old regional road until the engineers will finish."
