Chapter 3: The Call Before the Coffee

A Manager Who Cannot Wait

A conversation about a request that is on hold

Dialogue

The Manager Who Calls First

D
Camila, good morning. Sorry to call so early. Did you see my request?
C
Good morning, Diego. I am going through my inbox right now. Which request is it?
D
The one from Friday, about the analyst position. It is urgent for us.
C
I see it here. It is on hold, because the budget for the quarter is not confirmed yet.
D
And when do you think you can approve it? My team is waiting.
C
I need the confirmation from finance first. I get back to you by end of day.
D
By end of day works for me. Can you also check the contract issue from last week?
C
That one is with shared services, not with me. I follow up on it this morning and I copy you.
D
Perfect. And for your information, we have two people leaving in November.
C
Thank you for telling me. That changes my priority for next week, not for today.
Comprehension

About the Call

1. Why is the analyst request on hold?
Because the budget for the quarter is not confirmed yet.
2. What does Camila promise Diego, and when?
To get back to him by end of day, after the confirmation from finance.
3. Who is dealing with the contract issue, and what will Camila do about it?
Shared services. She will follow up on it in the morning and copy Diego.
Chapter 4: The Code

Every Morning, Not Yesterday

The present simple for what you always do

Grammar Discovery

Routine or One Day?

"Every morning I go through my inbox before the first meeting."

"I need to approve three requests today."

"Last Friday I approved the request from Chile."

"She sends me the headcount file every Monday."

Two of these sentences are about the routine. One is about one day in the past. What changes in the verb?

FormWhat it doesExample
present simplewhat happens every day, every week, alwaysI go through my inbox. I need to approve this.
present simple, third personhe, she and it take an -s at the endShe sends the file. The system blocks the request.
past simpleone finished moment in the past, with a time wordLast Friday I approved the request. Yesterday he called me.
the mistake to avoidpast for something that is still true nowNot "I needed to approve" when you mean I need to approve.
Watch outusing the past for the routine. "I needed to approve" tells your manager the approval is finished and in the past — which is not what you mean.

Sound Check

Say It Clearly

No extra vowel (point 5 of 14) — the word ends on the consonant: no "i" at the end and none before s + consonant

Why this one is hard for a Brazilian speaker

In Portuguese: Almost every word ends in a vowel, so an "i" appears after a final consonant (clube, Facebook).

In English: The word stops on the consonant. Nothing comes after it, and nothing goes between s and the next consonant.

If it slips: "Request" becomes three syllables and stops sounding like the word you meant.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Two Messages, One Morning

Your manager and a manager from Chile, before nine o'clock

Listening 1

A Message from Your Manager

Claire leaves a message before the first meeting of the day. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. What does Claire need by end of day, and what can wait?
The answer on the Chile position by end of day; the policy update can wait until next week.
2. What does she say about the headcount file?
It is on hold because finance has not confirmed the budget yet.
3. What does Claire ask Camila to do with the message about the works council?
Only to read it: it is for information, and nobody has to answer.
Listening 2

A Follow-Up from Chile

A local manager leaves a second message about an old request. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. How many times has the manager written about this request?
Twice: the first message and this follow-up.
2. What does he need, and by when?
The approval for the replacement, before the end of the week, because the person leaves on the thirtieth.
3. What does he offer to do to help?
To send the information again in a shorter form, with the cost in one line.
