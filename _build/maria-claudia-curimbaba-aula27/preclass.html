Lesson 27 -- Pre-class

The Container Must Be Cleared -- Logistics and Shipping

Move goods from the plant to the port and say what has to be done without naming who does it: the passive with modals ("The container must be cleared", "It should have been sent"). New vocabulary: freight forwarder, carrier, bill of lading, lead time, customs clearance, demurrage, consignment, pallet, reroute, expedite.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Freight forwarder -- a company that books the transport and handles the documents for your goods, from door to door
"Our freight forwarder booked the space and handled every document."
Carrier -- the shipping line, airline or trucking company that actually moves the goods
"The carrier changed the vessel without telling anyone."
Bill of lading -- the document the carrier issues, listing the goods and showing who has the right to collect them
"The goods cannot be released without the original bill of lading."
Lead time -- the time between placing an order and receiving the goods
"The lead time on that part is now nine weeks."
Customs clearance -- official approval that lets imported goods enter the country, after checks and duties
"Customs clearance in Santos usually takes three to five days."
Demurrage -- a fee charged for every day a container stays at the port after the free days end
"Every extra day in the terminal costs us demurrage."
Consignment -- a batch of goods sent together to one buyer in one delivery
"The June consignment will travel in two containers."
Pallet -- a flat wooden base that goods are stacked on, so a forklift can move them
"Two pallets were damaged when the truck was loaded."
Reroute -- to send goods by a different route or through a different port than planned
"We could reroute the second container through another port."
Expedite -- to make a process happen faster than usual, often by paying extra
"Can we expedite the release if we pay the priority fee?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Ten days after Greg's voicemail, the May container is at sea and expected in Santos on the twenty-eighth. Maria Claudia's freight forwarder calls with a list. Before the container can be released, three things must be done. The original bill of lading must be sent by courier, because customs will not accept a copy. The invoice has to be corrected, because its value does not match the packing list. And the import license should be approved before the vessel arrives. If everything is ready by Friday, customs clearance takes three to five days; if not, demurrage will be charged from the sixth day. On the call with Pittsburgh, Maria Claudia does not ask who made the mistakes. She says what must be sent today, and Greg admits that the bill of lading should have been sent on the day the vessel sailed. Then they talk about June: the next consignment will be shipped in two containers, but the carrier has changed the route and the lead time is now five weeks. Maria Claudia asks whether the second container could be rerouted through another port, or whether it can be expedited. She also asks Greg to confirm that every pallet will be checked against the packing list before the container is sealed.

1. Why does the text say "the bill of lading must be sent" and not who sends it?
A Because nobody knows how to send it.
B Because what matters is the task and the deadline, not the person doing it.
C Because the bill of lading sends itself.
2. What does "should have been sent" tell you?
A It was sent at the right time.
B It will be sent tomorrow.
C It was not sent at the right time -- this is a past regret.
3. What will happen if the documents are not ready by Friday?
A Demurrage will be charged from the sixth day.
B The container will be rerouted to another port.
C The vessel will return to Pittsburgh.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Passive with Modals

GRAMMAR

Use the passive when the action matters more than the person who does it -- the container, the document, the deadline. Put a modal in front to add the meaning: must (obligation), can (possible), should (advice), will (plan). For the present or future it is modal + be + past participle. For the past -- usually something that did NOT happen -- it is modal + have been + past participle.

MeaningFormExample
obligationmust / has to + be + participleThe container must be cleared by Friday.
possibilitycan / could + be + participleThe release can be expedited for a fee.
adviceshould + be + participleThe license should be approved before arrival.
planwill + be + participleThe consignment will be shipped in two containers.
negativemodal + not + be + participleThe goods cannot be released without the original.
questionmodal + subject + be + participleCan the container be rerouted?
past (it did not happen)modal + have been + participleIt should have been sent on Monday.

Key point: three traps. First, "be" is never optional: "must cleared" → "must be cleared". Second, after "be" comes the past participle: "can be release" → "can be released". Third, in the past you need all three words: "should have checked" (the pallets did the checking) → "should have been checked".
