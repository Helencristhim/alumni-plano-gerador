Chapter 3 -- The Code

What Has to Be Done

must be cleared... should have been sent

Discovery

Four Sentences, One Container

All four are about the same shipment. What do the highlighted words have in common -- and who does the action?

"The container must be cleared before it can be released."

"The original bill of lading should be sent by courier today."

"The documents should have been checked before the vessel sailed."

"Could the release be expedited if we paid the fee?"

PartFormExample
now or latermodal + be + past participle"The container must be cleared."
in the past (it did not happen)modal + have been + past participle"The documents should have been checked."
negativemodal + not + be + participle"The goods cannot be released."
questionmodal + subject + be + participle"Could the release be expedited?"
who did it (only if it matters)... + by + person"It must be signed by the consignee."

Key idea: the modal carries the meaning (must = obligation, can = possible, should = advice, will = plan) and be carries the passive. The subject is the container or the document, not the person. Two traps: "be" is never optional ("must cleared" → "must be cleared"), and in the past it is "should have been checked" -- "the documents should have checked" says the documents did the checking.

The Structure

Three Jobs on the Route

The same form, doing three very different things.

1 · Say what has to happen, without naming who

"The original bill of lading must be sent by courier today."

The task is clear. Whose desk it lands on is a separate conversation.

2 · Ask what is possible, without asking a favor

"Can the release be expedited if we pay the priority fee?"

You ask about the process, so nobody has to say no to you personally.

3 · Record what went wrong, without accusing anyone

"The invoice should have been checked before the goods were shipped."

The lesson is written down. The person is not put on trial.

Notice what the passive lets you leave out: the name. On a call with a partner, that is often the whole point.

The Call with Pittsburgh

M

"Greg, the forwarder called. The container is on schedule, but the original bill of lading still has not arrived in Santos. Where is it?"

G

"It was sent by courier on Monday. It should have been sent on the day the vessel sailed, and that one is on us. The tracking number is in my email from this morning."

M

"Thank you. There is a second problem: the value on the invoice does not match the packing list. It must be corrected before Friday, or the goods cannot be released."

G

"That was our mistake too. A corrected invoice can be issued today, and I will have it signed by our finance director before noon."

M

"Good. Now the June run. The volume is confirmed, all of it, so the other half of the space can be booked today, before the rate goes up on the twentieth."

G

"Then the June consignment will be shipped in two containers. But the lead time is longer now: five weeks, not four, because the carrier changed the route."

M

"Five weeks is too long for the plant. Could the second container be rerouted through Paranaguá, or can it be expedited if we pay the difference?"

G

"Paranaguá is possible. I will ask the freight forwarder to price both options, and you will have the numbers in writing before your family meeting on Friday."

Dialogue Review

About Greg

1. When was the bill of lading sent, and when does Greg say it should have been sent?
By courier on Monday. It should have been sent on the day the vessel sailed -- and he admits that one is on them.
2. What does Greg promise to do about the invoice?
A corrected invoice will be issued the same day and signed by their finance director before noon.
3. Why does Greg say the lead time is longer now?
Because the carrier changed the route: five weeks instead of four.
4. What will Greg send before Friday?
The prices of both options, rerouting and expediting, in writing from the freight forwarder.
