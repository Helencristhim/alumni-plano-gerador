Lesson 40 -- Pre-class

Negotiating with a Global Client -- Already, Yet and the Tenses

Ten to twenty minutes before class. How to give a negotiation update with the right tense for each time. Key words: a proposal, a deal, to sign, procurement, a counteroffer, a deadline, to agree on.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A proposal -- a document that offers a plan and a price to a client
"The proposal is ready for review."
A deal -- an agreement between two companies
"We closed the deal in December."
To sign -- to write your name to accept a contract
"The client is going to sign next week."
Procurement -- the team that buys services for a company
"Procurement always asks for a discount."
A counteroffer -- a new offer after the other side says no
"They sent a counteroffer with a lower price."
A deadline -- the last day to do something
"The deadline for the answer is Friday."
To agree on -- to accept the same decision as the other side
"We agreed on the main points."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Laura works with clients in Europe. In her update, she uses the present perfect with already and yet: “We have already agreed on the price, but the client hasn’t signed yet.”

For a finished time, she uses the past simple: “Two days ago they asked for a new deadline.” And for the plan: “We are going to answer tomorrow.”

"The client hasn’t signed yet." What does it mean?
A The client will never sign.
B The client has not signed, but we expect it.
C The client signed a long time ago.
Why does Laura use the past simple in "Two days ago they asked"?
A Because two days ago is a finished time.
B Because it is still happening.
C Because it is a plan.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Already, Yet and the Time

GRAMMAR

Already: done, sooner than expected. Yet: not done, but expected. Finished time: past simple.

JobFormExample
Done (positive)have already + third formWe have already sent it.
Not done, expectedhaven’t + third form + yetThey haven’t answered yet.
Finished timepast simpleLast week they asked for a discount.
Plangoing toWe are going to reply on Monday.

Never: They didn’t answer yet (in an update) · We already have sent it last week · They asked already

A "We already send it."
