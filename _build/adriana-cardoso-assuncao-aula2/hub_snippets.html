Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These eight words are on every HR dashboard in English.

Turnover -- the percentage of people who leave a company and have to be replaced
"Turnover is at fourteen point two percent this quarter."
Absenteeism -- days lost when people are away from work and should be there
"Absenteeism has been steady for four quarters."
Benchmark -- a standard number you compare your own result with
"The market benchmark for our sector is eleven point five."
Rate -- a number, usually a percentage, that shows how often something happens
"The rate went up by one point six points."
Gap -- the difference between two numbers or two situations
"The gap between operations and the offices is enormous."
Steady -- staying at the same level, with no big change
"Absenteeism is steady at three point eight percent."
To break down -- to divide a total number into its smaller parts
"If you break the number down by area, the story changes."
Overtime -- the hours somebody works beyond the normal working day
"We paid one point four million dollars in overtime."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

The report opens with one rate for the whole company: fourteen point two percent. That is higher than last quarter and higher than the market benchmark of eleven point five. It is also the highest figure in three years. But when you break the number down, the story changes. Operations is at nineteen percent and the corporate offices are at six, so the gap inside the company is much wider than the gap between the company and the market. Absenteeism, meanwhile, is as steady as it was four quarters ago. And overtime is the most expensive line on the page: one point four million dollars, with forty percent of it coming from two sites.
"The gap inside the company is much wider than the gap with the market." How many things are being compared?
A Two — and "much" makes the difference sound big
B Three, because there are three numbers in the text
C Only one, because a gap is a single number
Why does the text say "the highest figure in three years" and not "a higher figure"?
A Because it is being compared with one other quarter
B Because it is being compared with every quarter in a period of three years
C Because the number is an estimate
"Absenteeism is as steady as it was four quarters ago." What does this mean?
A It is worse than it was
B It is at the same level — there is no difference
C It has never been measured before

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Comparatives and Superlatives

GRAMMAR

A comparative puts two things side by side. A superlative puts one thing against all the others, and it always needs the.

short word-er than / the -esthigh → higher than → the highest. wide → wider than → the widest.
long wordmore ... than / the mostexpensive → more expensive than → the most expensive.
irregulargood / badgood → better → the best. bad → worse → the worst.
equalas ... asAbsenteeism is as steady as it was four quarters ago.
make it biggermuch / far + comparativeOperations is much worse than the offices. The gap is far wider.

Two traps in a meeting: never say "more higher" — one form is enough. And the decimal point is said as point, never as comma: fourteen point two percent, one point four million.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
You want to say the rate moved from 12.6 to 14.2. Which sentence is correct?
A "The rate went up by one point six percent."
B "The rate went up by one point six points, to fourteen point two percent."
C "The rate went up one comma six percents."
The vice president says your number is bad because it is above the benchmark. What is the professional answer?
A "The benchmark is wrong."
B "The number is right. Which companies are inside that benchmark?"
C "I do not have that information."
Operations is at 19% and the offices are at 6%. Which sentence sounds like an executive?
A "Operations is more bad than the offices."
B "Operations is much worse than the corporate offices."
C "Operations has the most worst rate that the offices."
You need to say this is the worst quarter since 2023. Which one is correct?
A "This is the worst quarter we have had since 2023."
B "This is worst quarter we have had since 2023."
C "This is the more bad quarter since 2023."
