Podcast
BBC 6 Minute English — "Is it OK to disagree?"

Six minutes on what happens when two people hold opposite positions and neither of them shouts. It is this lesson from the other side: not how to be right, but how to stay in the conversation long enough to find out.

Tip: listen once, then find three modal verbs in the transcript. For each one, ask yourself whether the speaker is guessing, predicting or concluding.

Talk
TED — Kathryn Schulz, "On being wrong" (17 min)

A writer on what it actually feels like to be wrong, which she says feels exactly like being right until the moment it does not. It is the argument behind every modal verb in this lesson: certainty is a feeling, and a feeling is not evidence.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Then say one thing out loud that you were completely sure about last year and are not sure about now, and choose the modal that fits how sure you are today.

Video
TED on YouTube — Sal Khan, "How AI could save (not destroy) education" (15 min)

A strong, clearly argued position on the exact question your course keeps arguing about, from somebody who has built the thing he is describing. Watch how he separates what he has seen from what he is predicting.

Tip: pause every time he makes a claim and ask yourself: would he defend this in front of a hostile room, or is it a guess? Then check which words he used.
