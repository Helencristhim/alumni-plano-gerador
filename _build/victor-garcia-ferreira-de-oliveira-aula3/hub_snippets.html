Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

Defending Your Point -- How Sure You Sound

In an argument, the difference between "it is" and "it might be" is not politeness. It is how much you are willing to defend. Key words: reliable, a shortcut, bias, to rely on something, overrated, to replace, hands-on, to keep up with something, in practice, to have a point. Structure: modal verbs of opinion and probability.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Reliable -- working the same way every time, so people can plan around it
"The tool is fast, but it is not reliable yet. It gives me a different answer twice."
A shortcut -- a faster way that gets there by skipping part of the work
"Copying the answer is a shortcut. The problem is the day the answer is wrong."
Bias -- the way a person or a system leans one way without noticing it
"A model trained on old data carries the bias of the people who wrote it."
To rely on something -- to need something so much that you could not work without it
"I use the tool every day, but I do not want to rely on it before I understand the code."
Overrated -- talked about as better than it really is
"Half the tools on that list are overrated, and everybody in the course knows which half."
To replace -- to put one thing where another was, so the first one is no longer used
"The tool did not replace the developer. It replaced the boring half of the afternoon."
Hands-on -- learned by doing the thing yourself, not by watching somebody do it
"The course is hands-on. You break something in week one and you fix it in week two."
To keep up with something -- to move as fast as something that keeps changing
"Nobody keeps up with everything. You keep up with the part you actually use."
In practice -- what actually happens, as opposed to what should happen in theory
"In theory the tool writes the tests. In practice somebody still reads every line."
To have a point -- to be right about part of what you are saying, even if the other person disagrees overall
"You have a point about the speed. I still would not put it near the payment code."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Two people can look at the same fact and be equally certain about opposite things, which is why English puts the certainty in one small word and leaves the rest of the sentence alone. The tool might replace part of the job. That is a possibility and nothing more, and may and could do exactly the same work. Move one step up and you get probability: junior developers are likely to use it every day, or they will probably use it every day. Nothing has been proved, but you are now standing behind the claim.

The top of the dial is not about the future at all. It is about a conclusion you are drawing right now from something you can see. The course spends six weeks on the fundamentals, so that must be deliberate. He has written software for ten years, so he cannot be a beginner. Must and cannot are a pair: one says you are sure it is true, the other says you are sure it is not. And here is the trap that catches every Portuguese speaker: the opposite of must be is cannot be, never must not be.

The second trap is smaller and just as common. Can is not a way of saying maybe about one specific event. The interview can happen next week is wrong; the sentence you want is the interview might happen next week. The third trap is stacking two openers: pick in my opinion or I think, never both. Get those three right, add in practice when the theory and the reality disagree, and concede with you have a point when the other person is partly right. That last one costs you nothing and buys you the rest of the conversation.

1. "That must be deliberate." According to the text, what is the speaker doing?
A Making a prediction about the future.
B Drawing a conclusion right now from something they can see.
C Saying that something is only possible.
2. The text calls "must not be" a trap. What is the correct opposite of "must be"?
A "cannot be"
B "might not be"
C "should not be"
3. Why is "The interview can happen next week" wrong, according to the text?
A Because "can" is never used about the future.
B Because an interview is not something you can plan.
C Because "can" is not a way of saying maybe about one specific event. The word for that is "might".

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Modal Verbs of Opinion and Probability

GRAMMAR

The modal is the promise. Choose it on purpose, because that is what the other person will hold you to.

How certainFormExample
Possible, no more than thatmay / might / could + base formIt might replace part of the job.
Probablewill probably + base · is likely to + baseJuniors are likely to use it every day.
A conclusion from evidencemust + base formSix weeks on the basics must be deliberate.
A conclusion that it is NOT socannot + base formHe cannot be a beginner.
Opening an opinionI would argue that... · It seems to me that... · In my opinion...I would argue that the fundamentals matter more now.
Softening a claimtends to + base · in most cases · up to a pointA tool like that tends to help experienced people more.
Conceding one partYou have a point about... , and...You have a point about the speed, and I still disagree about the rest.

Never: maybe the interview can happen next week · he must not be a developer · in my opinion I think it is overrated · it is probably that the course will change.

Stage 2: Put the Disagreement in Order

Order

Somebody has just told you that in five years nobody will write code. Put your answer in an order that keeps the conversation alive.

?"You have a point about the speed, and I still disagree about the rest."
?"I would argue that the fundamentals matter more now, not less."
?"Six weeks on the basics must be deliberate, and in practice it works."
?"It cannot be true that nobody reads the code any more."
?"It might change the way teams hire, but I would not go further than that."
?"So how do you keep up with it without relying on it completely?"

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

An interviewer asks where you think your field is going, and what that means for somebody starting now.
