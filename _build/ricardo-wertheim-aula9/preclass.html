Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

The Art of the Story -- Telling a Travel Story People Remember

Building a travel story with a shape: set the scene, keep the ordinary short, pause, reveal. Key words: turning point, to set the scene, to linger, layer, to reveal, reflection, connection, to pause. Structure: verb + ing versus verb + to with keep, decide, start, remember and stop.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Turning point -- the moment when a situation changes direction
"That was the turning point of the whole year, not just the evening."
To set the scene -- to say where and when something happened, before the story starts
"First you set the scene, and only then you start the story."
To linger -- to stay somewhere longer than you need to, because you want to
"I lingered there for an hour and nearly missed the last bus."
Layer -- one level of something built on top of another, like history under a floor
"He talked for ten minutes about the layers under the church floor."
To reveal -- to show or tell something that was hidden until then
"You reveal what changed, and then you stop talking."
Reflection -- the act of looking back and thinking carefully about what happened
"On reflection, that half hour was the best part of the whole trip."
Connection -- a feeling that ties you to a place or to a person
"I felt a connection with that street before I understood why."
To pause -- to stop for a short moment and then continue
"Pause right before the turning point, and everybody leans in."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

It was the last morning, and I had one hour before the airport. I remember standing in the square with a coffee, deciding to do absolutely nothing. Then an old man sat down beside me and started to talk about a small church at the end of the street.

He kept talking for ten minutes: the layers under the floor, the door that came from a ship. After a while I stopped listening politely and started listening properly. Eventually I stood up and ran to see it, and I nearly missed my flight. I only stopped to take one photograph, and then I lingered inside for twenty minutes. Looking back, that half hour is the only part of the whole trip I can still see with my eyes closed.

1. The text says "I remember standing in the square". Why not "I remember to stand"?
A Because it is a memory of something she did. "Remember to do" means do not forget a task.
B Because "stand" is an irregular verb and cannot take "to".
C Because the sentence is in the past, and "to" is only for the future.
2. The text has "I stopped listening politely" and "I stopped to take one photograph". What is the difference?
A There is no difference. Both shapes mean exactly the same thing.
B "Stopped listening" ended the listening. "Stopped to take" means she stopped in order to take the photo.
C "Stopped to take" is more polite, and "stopped listening" is informal.
3. The text says "he kept talking" and "she decided to do nothing". What decides the shape after these two verbs?
A The person: "he" takes -ing and "she" takes "to".
B The length of the second verb decides it.
C The first verb decides it. "Keep" always takes -ing and "decide" always takes "to".

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Verb + ing or Verb + to

GRAMMAR

When two verbs meet, the first one decides the shape of the second. Five verbs cover almost every story you will tell.

VerbShapeExample
keepalways + ingI kept walking in the rain.
decidealways + toI decided to walk home.
startboth, same meaningHe started talking = he started to talk.
stopboth, different meaningstopped to look = stopped in order to look · stopped looking = ended the looking
rememberboth, different meaningremember standing = a picture in your head · remember to book = a task you must not forget

In a story you almost always want the memory one: "I remember standing there", "I could not stop looking", "I kept walking". The "to" shape is for decisions and for purpose: "I decided to walk", "I stopped to look".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are telling a story at a dinner table. Choose the best thing to say.

You want to say that you continued walking in the rain. You say:
A "I kept to walk in the rain."
B "I kept walking in the rain."
C "I kept walk in the rain."
You interrupted your walk in order to photograph a doorway. You say:
A "I stopped to photograph the doorway, and then I walked on."
B "I stopped photographing the doorway, and then I walked on."
C "I stopped for photograph the doorway, and then I walked on."
You want to describe a picture that is still in your head from twenty years ago. You say:
A "I remember to stand in that square with a coffee."
B "I remember standing in that square with a coffee."
C "I remember stand in that square with a coffee."
You want to mark the exact moment the story changed direction. You say:
A "So, after this, the situation was different in some ways."
B "At that point, I realized it was my grandmother's name."
C "And then the thing happened that I said before."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Tell one story you have told many times in Portuguese and never once in English. Take about ninety seconds. Set the scene in one sentence, keep the ordinary part short, pause right before the moment everything changed, then reveal what changed and stop. Use at least four verb patterns from this lesson and three words from the vocabulary. Do not explain the ending, and do not stop to correct yourself.
