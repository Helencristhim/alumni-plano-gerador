Chapter 3: The Code

Walking or To Walk?

kept walking · decided to walk · stopped to look

Grammar Discovery

Same Verb, Two Meanings

"I kept walking until I found the square."

"I decided to take the longer route."

"I stopped to look at the doorway, and then I walked on."

"I could not stop looking at the name in the stone."

Two carry -ing. Two carry to. Look at the last two: the same verb, and two completely different afternoons.

VerbShapeExample
keepalways + ingI kept walking in the rain.
decidealways + toI decided to walk home.
startboth, same meaningHe started talking = he started to talk.
stopboth, different meaningstopped to look = stopped in order to look · stopped looking = ended the looking
rememberboth, different meaningremember standing = the memory is in your head · remember to book = do not forget to do it

In a story you almost always want the memory one: I remember standing there, I could not stop looking, I kept walking.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I kept to walk in the rain for an hour."
"I kept walking in the rain for an hour."
"I decided walking home instead of taking the bus."
"I decided to walk home instead of taking the bus."
"I remember to stand in that square twenty years ago."
"I remember standing in that square twenty years ago."

Keep never takes to. Decide never takes -ing. And remember to do is a task you must not forget, while remember doing is a picture already in your head.

Useful Language

Three Phrases for Any Story

"I was just... when suddenly..."

Puts the listener inside the moment before the turn

"What struck me most was..."

Delivers a judgement without sounding like a lecture

"At that point, I realized..."

Marks the turning point out loud, so nobody misses it

Chapter 4: One Story, Line by Line

The Name in the Stone

A story Elena has told a hundred times

Dialogue

Elena Tells the Story

E
I have told this story a hundred times, so stop me if it gets long. Twenty years ago I decided to walk home from the harbor instead of taking the bus.
R
You decided to walk. At night?
E
At night, in November. And I remember thinking it was a terrible idea about four minutes later, when it started raining.
R
So it was raining and you were alone. Did you keep walking?
E
I kept walking, and then I stopped to look at a doorway I had passed a thousand times. There was a name carved into the stone.
R
A name you knew. Out of nowhere.
E
My grandmother's name. That was the turning point of the whole year, not just the evening. I lingered there for an hour, and I could not stop looking at it.
Comprehension

About Elena

1. What did Elena decide to do instead of taking the bus, and when?
She decided to walk home from the harbor, at night, in November, twenty years ago.
2. What made her stop on the way home?
She stopped to look at a doorway she had passed a thousand times, because there was a name carved into the stone.
3. Why does she call it the turning point of the whole year?
Because the name in the stone was her grandmother's. She lingered there for an hour and could not stop looking at it.
Listening

The Four Moves of a Good Story

A storyteller explains what most people get wrong. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does he say the first move is, and what does it need?
To set the scene: where you were, what time of day it was, and one detail that puts the listener beside you.
2. Which move does he say most people skip?
The pause right before the turning point. He says one second of silence makes everybody lean in.
3. What does he tell you to do at the very end of a story?
Reveal what changed, then stop talking. He says not to explain the meaning, because a story that explains itself is a story nobody repeats.
Listening 2

The Church at the End of the Street

The same four moves, this time in a real story. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What was she planning to do with her last hour before the airport?
Absolutely nothing. She remembers standing in the square with a coffee, deciding to do nothing at all.
2. What did the old man ask her about, and what did he tell her?
He asked whether she had seen the small church at the end of the street, not the famous one. He kept talking about the layers under the floor and a door that came from a ship.
3. How does the story end, and what does she say twenty years later?
She ran to see the church and nearly missed her flight. Twenty years later it is the only thing from the whole trip she can still see with her eyes closed.
