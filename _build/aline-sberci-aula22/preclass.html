Lesson 22 -- Pre-class

Comparisons: The Best Choice

Compare three or more options and choose the best one with superlatives: the cheapest, the most convenient, the best. New vocabulary: crowded, affordable, convenient, reliable, spacious, cozy, impressive, lively, overrated, worth it.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Aline travels to Basel three or four times a year, and she knows the hotels near the office very well. The Rhine Hotel is the most convenient: it is five minutes from the office. But it is also the most expensive, and the rooms are the smallest. The Old Town Inn is the coziest hotel, with a small garden and the best breakfast in the city. The Station Hotel is the most affordable, and it has the most spacious rooms, but the street is the noisiest of the three. For Aline, the Old Town Inn is the best choice: it is not the cheapest, but it is worth it. Notice: short adjectives take the + -est; long adjectives take the most; good becomes the best.

A The Rhine Hotel.
B The Station Hotel, because it is the most affordable and has the most spacious rooms.
C The Old Town Inn.

A It is the cheapest hotel, and it is five minutes from the office.
B It has the best breakfast in the city.

3. Which sentence is correct?
A The Station Hotel is the more affordable of the three hotels near the office.

C The Station Hotel is the most affordable.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Superlatives

GRAMMAR

Use the superlative to say which one is number one in a group of three or more.

AdjectiveFormExample
Short (cheap, big)the + adjective + -estIt's the cheapest option.
Ends in -y (cozy, busy)the + -iestIt's the coziest hotel.
Long (convenient, expensive)the most + adjectiveIt's the most convenient hotel.
Irregular (good, bad)the best / the worstIt was the best trip of my life.

Key point: two options = comparative ("Basel is cheaper than Zurich"); three or more = superlative ("Basel is the cheapest"). Don't forget the, and never say "the most cheap" or "the most good". After the superlative, use in + a place ("the biggest city in Brazil") or of + a group ("the best day of the week").
