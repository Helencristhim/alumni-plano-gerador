Chapter 3: The Code

I Always Leave Early

Small words that say how often

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Show?

"I always listen to a podcast in the car."

"She usually takes the subway."

"The traffic is often terrible on Mondays."

"How often do you work from home?"

Look at the blue words: they say how often. They go before the main verb (listen, takes), but after the verb be (is often). The red words ask the question.

Chapter 4: Stuck in Traffic

Stuck in Traffic

One phone call, two drivers, one app

Dialogue

Monday, 8:10 a.m. — A Phone Call

P
Hi, Andrea, it’s Peter. Are you in the office? The meeting starts in twenty minutes.
A
Hi, Peter. Sorry, I’m running late. I’m stuck in a traffic jam.
P
Oh no. Is the traffic always this bad on Mondays?
A
Usually, yes. Monday at rush hour is the worst. I commute by car, and it often takes an hour.
P
An hour! I live near the office, so I usually walk. It takes ten minutes.
A
Lucky you! But I always listen to a podcast on the way, so the time is not lost.
P
Good idea. Do you know a shortcut?
A
The app sometimes finds one. I think I can be there at eight forty.
P
No problem. I’ll start with the numbers, and you can talk about the new hires at the end.
Comprehension

About Peter

1. Why does Peter call Andrea?
Because the meeting starts in twenty minutes.
2. How does Peter usually go to the office?
He walks. It takes ten minutes.
3. What does Peter decide to do in the meeting?
He starts with the numbers, and Andrea talks at the end.
