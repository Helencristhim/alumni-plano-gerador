Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

On the Way to Work -- The Commute

You spend a lot of time in traffic: today that time becomes English. Key words: to commute, rush hour, a traffic jam, to get stuck, to run late, a shortcut, on the way, an episode. Structure: adverbs of frequency with the present simple.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To commute -- to travel from home to work and back every day
"I commute by car. It takes about an hour."
Rush hour -- the time when everybody goes to work or goes home
"At rush hour, the traffic is terrible."
A traffic jam -- a long line of cars that cannot move
"There was a traffic jam on the main avenue."
To get stuck -- to be in a place and not be able to move
"I usually get stuck in traffic on Mondays."
To run late -- to be later than the right time
"Sorry, I’m running late. I’m in traffic."
A shortcut -- a faster or shorter way to go somewhere
"The app shows me a shortcut when there is traffic."
On the way -- during the trip from one place to another
"I listen to podcasts on the way to work."
An episode -- one part of a podcast or a series
"One episode is about thirty minutes, the same as my commute."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Jack lives outside Sydney. His commute is long: he usually takes the train at six thirty. He never drives, because at rush hour the traffic jams are terrible.

On the train he always listens to a podcast. One episode is about forty minutes, so he finishes one on the way to work. He sometimes reads a book, and he works from home twice a week.

Priya, in London, goes to work by bike. She knows a shortcut through a park, so she never gets stuck. She is usually at the office before her boss, and she is never late.

1. Why does Jack never drive to work?
A Because the traffic jams at rush hour are terrible.
B Because he does not have a car.
C Because the train has free podcasts.
2. How does Priya avoid the traffic?
A She leaves home very early, at five in the morning, every day.
B She takes a shortcut through a park.
C She works from home.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “Jack listens always to a podcast.”
B “Jack always listens to a podcast.”
C “Jack is always listen to a podcast on the train.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Adverbs of Frequency -- Always, Usually, Sometimes, Never

GRAMMAR

Adverbs of frequency say how often something happens. They go before the main verb, but after the verb be. To ask, use How often...?

JobFormExample
100% to 0%always › usually › often › sometimes › neverI always leave at seven.
With a normal verbbefore the verbShe usually takes the subway.
With beafter beThe traffic is often terrible.
QuestionHow often do / does + subject + verb?How often do you work from home?
Answer with a timeonce / twice / three times a weekI work from home twice a week.
The trapnever is already negativeNot I don’t never drive. Say I never drive.

Never: I listen always · The traffic usually is bad · I don’t never drive · How often you work from home?
