Chapter 3: The Code

Turn the Verb into a Noun

we reduced costs → the cost reduction of 12%

Grammar Discovery

Read and Notice

"We reduced costs." → "The cost reduction of twelve percent."

"Margins improved." → "The margin improvement in the third quarter."

"We implemented the model." → "The implementation of the model."

"The team performed well." → "The performance of the team."

Each pair says the same fact twice. What happens to the verb on the right?

EndingVerb → nounWhy it helps
-tionreduce → reduction, implement → implementationThe noun can carry a number: a reduction of 12%.
-mentimprove → improvement, align → alignmentIt becomes the subject of the sentence, so the fact leads.
-ance / -enceperform → performance, comply → complianceYou describe the thing, not who did it.
WarningDo not overdo it. A sentence made only of nouns is heavy and hard to follow. Alternate: one noun phrase, then a normal verb.

The noun phrase is what lets you say a fact and a number in the same breath.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"We delivered a cost reduction in twelve percent."
"We delivered a cost reduction of twelve percent."
"The margin improved four comma two percent."
"The margin improved by four point two percent."
"The category price model implementation performance evaluation."
"We evaluated how the new pricing model performed by category."

A number after a noun takes of; after a verb it takes by. English says point, not comma. And more than three nouns in a row stops being precise and starts being unreadable.

Detail

Give a Number Without Committing to a Decimal

Precision when you have it, honesty when you do not

Exact: "Four point two percent." → Careful: "___Approximately four percent."
Exact: "Two hundred and forty million." → Careful: "___In the region of two hundred and forty million."
Exact: "Between 3.1 and 4.9." → Careful: "___Somewhere between three and five percent."
No number yet: "I do not know." → Professional: "___I would rather give you an exact figure tomorrow."
Chapter 4: The Board Presentation

Compared to What?

The question every board asks about every percentage

Dialogue

In Front of the Board

S
You said four point two percent. Four point two percent compared to what, exactly?
M
To the twelve months before the rollout. It is a like for like figure, not a calendar comparison.
S
Good. And is that number the whole business, or a few categories carrying it?
M
It is aggregate. Two categories carried most of it and three moved backwards. I can disaggregate it now if that is useful.
S
Not now. But tell me this: is it a trend?
M
Not yet. One quarter is a data point. The trajectory over four quarters is what would make it a trend.
S
And if we extend the model to the remaining categories, what do we get?
M
A ballpark figure would be somewhere between three and five percent. I would rather give you an exact figure next month.
Comprehension

About Sylvia

1. What is the first thing Sylvia wants to know about the number?
What it is compared to.
2. Does she want the number disaggregated during the meeting?
No. She says "not now".
3. What is her last question about?
What they would get if the model were extended to the remaining categories.

Listening 2

Do Not Open With the Number

Advice the night before a board presentation. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What happens when you open with the number?
Half the room starts checking whether they believe it, and nobody listens to the next three sentences.
2. What order does he recommend?
The question first, then the baseline, then the result.
3. What should you do with the granularity?
Keep it. If they ask, disaggregate it in front of them.
