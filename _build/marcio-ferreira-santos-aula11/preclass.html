Presenting a result so the room believes it. Key words: baseline, uplift, trajectory, aggregate, to disaggregate, granularity, like for like, ballpark figure. Structure: nominalization and noun phrases, plus hedging a figure without sounding unsure.

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Baseline -- the starting point you measure everything against
"The baseline is the twelve months before the rollout."
Uplift -- the gain you get above the starting point
"The uplift in the second column is a like for like figure."
Trajectory -- the direction a number is moving over several periods
"Look at the trajectory, not at the single number."
Aggregate -- a single total that hides many different numbers inside it
"The margin improvement of four point two percent is aggregate."
To disaggregate -- to open a single total and show the parts inside it
"If they ask, you can disaggregate it in front of them."
Granularity -- how much detail a number is broken into
"If they want the granularity, they will ask for it."
Like for like -- comparing two periods that are genuinely comparable
"The uplift is a like for like figure, not a calendar comparison."
Ballpark figure -- a rough figure, given on purpose, when precision is not needed yet
"A ballpark figure would be somewhere between three and five percent."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

One comment before we open the numbers. The baseline for this comparison is the twelve months before the rollout, so the uplift in the second column is a like for like figure. The margin improvement of four point two percent is aggregate: at category level, two categories carried almost all of it. The implementation of the model at SKU level is what produced the change, and the performance of the southern region explains most of the difference. My advice is to look at the trajectory rather than the single number. A four percent improvement in one quarter is not a trend. It is a data point with a good story attached to it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Noun Phrases and Hedging

GRAMMAR

Turning the verb into a noun is the shortest path from student to director.

EndingVerb becomes nounWhy it helps
-tionreduce → reduction, implement → implementationThe noun carries the number: a reduction of 12%.
-mentimprove → improvement, align → alignmentThe fact becomes the subject, so it leads the sentence.
-ance / -enceperform → performance, comply → complianceYou describe the thing, not who did it.
Prepositionsof after a noun, by after a verba reduction of 12% / it improved by 12%. English says point, not comma.
Hedging a figureapproximately, in the region of, somewhere between X and Y, a ballpark figure would be. Used on purpose, these show control, not doubt. But do not build a whole sentence out of nouns: alternate.
