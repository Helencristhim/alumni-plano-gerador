Talk
"The best stats you've ever seen" -- Hans Rosling, TED

Twenty minutes of a statistician turning tables into a story. Watch what he does BEFORE each number: he always says what it is compared to, and only then gives the figure.

Tip: turn on the English transcript. Collect five sentences where he names the baseline before the number, and rewrite each one about your own results.

Video
"6 Minute English -- Business & Work 2" box set, BBC Learning English

Thirty minutes of workplace English in short episodes, with the vocabulary explained as it appears. Good background listening for the week when you only have the commute.

Tip: do not try to watch it all at once. One episode a day, and after each one say two sentences out loud using a word you just met.

Watch on YouTube ↗
Podcast
"The Indicator from Planet Money" -- NPR

Ten minutes a day, and each episode is built around exactly one number. It is the format you are training for: one figure, its baseline, and what it means.

Tip: pick any episode. After listening, say the whole story in four sentences: the question, the baseline, the number, the caveat.
