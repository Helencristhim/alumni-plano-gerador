YouTube · BBC Learning English
How to use reported speech -- 6 Minute Grammar

Six minutes on exactly what you practiced tonight: how the verb moves one step back when you report what somebody said, and when you keep it where it is. Two BBC presenters build the shift live, at natural speed, with the same say and tell contrast you drilled in class.

Tip: watch it twice. The second time, pause after each example and report one real sentence from your own week: "My director told me that..."

YouTube · BBC Learning English
Reported Speech -- The Grammar Gameshow, Episode 25

Ten minutes in quiz format: contestants report statements and questions under pressure while the host corrects every mistake out loud. It covers the two traps from class -- "said me" instead of "told me", and keeping the question word order in a reported question.

Tip: answer out loud before the contestants do. If you beat them to "asked whether", the form is already yours.

TED Talk · Work & Leadership
David Grady: How to save the world (or at least yourself) from bad meetings

A short, funny talk about the meetings that produce nothing, and what a professional does instead: ask what the agenda is, ask what decision is expected, and leave with something to report. It is the natural context for tonight's language -- brief, consensus, alignment, takeaway and minutes all appear in the working world he describes.

Tip: turn on English subtitles, then record two minutes reporting his argument back: "He said that most meetings...", "He asked whether...".
