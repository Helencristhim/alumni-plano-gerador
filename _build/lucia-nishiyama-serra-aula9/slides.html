"He Said That..."

Chapter 1: The Room You Just Left

From What Was Decided to Who Decided It

Last time, the passive voice let you put the decision first: "The rollout was approved." Tonight you do the opposite. You name the source and quote it: "The head of engineering told me that the rollout would be delayed." A leader who reports precisely is a leader people trust.

The Document

The Minutes

AI Safety Council
Q3 Review · 9:00 AM
Present: L. Serra (Chair) · Head of Research · Legal · Engineering

1. The head of research said that the new model had passed every internal test, and reiterated that the external audit was still open.

2. Legal told the council that they would need two more weeks for the review.

3. The Chair asked whether the review could run in parallel with testing. Legal confirmed that it could.

4. Decision: nothing ships before sign-off. Next review: October 4.

1. What did Legal tell the council?
That they would need two more weeks for the review.
2. What did the Chair ask, and what was the answer?
She asked whether the review could run in parallel with testing; Legal confirmed that it could.
The Big Idea

One Step Back

In the room

the words as they were spoken: "We need two more weeks."

Outside the room

the words as you report them: "Legal said they needed two more weeks."

The meeting is over, so the sentence moves one step back in time. Get that step right and people trust your version of the room.

Chapter 3: The Shift Code

"She Told Me That..."

The grammar of carrying someone else's words

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The launch is under review." → "He said that the launch was under review."

"I will send the minutes." → "She told me that she would send the minutes."

"Did you test the model?" → "They asked whether we had tested the model."

"What is the biggest risk?" → "The director asked what the biggest risk was."

The blue verbs moved one step back in time. The orange ones did something else too. What happened to the word order?

say vs telltell needs a person: "He told me that..." · say does not: "He said that..." (never "He said me")
Reported questionsNo inversion and no do/does/did. Wh- question: "She asked what the risk was." Yes/no question: "She asked if / whether we had tested it."
Person, time, placeI → she/he · today → that day · tomorrow → the next day · here → there

"That" is optional after said and told. The step back is the eco of the report, not a claim that the fact changed.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"He said me that the model was ready."
"He told me that the model was ready."
"She asked what did the team decide."
"She asked what the team had decided."
"In the meeting he said he will review the risks."
"In the meeting he said he would review the risks."

Use tell with a person and say without one. A reported question is a statement: no inversion, no did. And when the meeting is over, will becomes would.

Chapter 4: The Debrief

Mark Asks the Hard One

A senior recruiter for a VP of AI Safety role

Dialogue

Carrying a Decision You Did Not Make

M
Lucia, let me go to a hard one. Tell me about a moment when leadership changed direction and you had to carry that message to your own team.
L
Last year, the head of engineering told me that the release would be delayed by a quarter. He said that the safety review had found two open risks.
M
How did you convey that to a team that had worked on it for months?
L
I briefed them the same afternoon. I reiterated that the decision was about safety, not about their work, and I said that finding the risks early was good news.
M
Did anyone push back?
L
Two engineers did. They asked why the review had not started sooner. That was a fair question, so I took it back to the leadership meeting.
M
And what came out of that room?
L
We reached consensus in twenty minutes. The minutes said that reviews would start at design, not at launch. The takeaway was simple: alignment costs one meeting, and a bad release costs a year.

Listening 2

Reporting Without Distorting

An executive on the hardest part of the job. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she say is the hardest part of an executive role?
Not deciding, but reporting what was decided.
2. What are the three things she does before she speaks to anyone?
She writes the takeaway in one sentence, separates what was said from what she thinks about it, and names who said it.
3. Why does she say "Finance told us" instead of "someone thinks"?
Because naming the source is what lets you convey a decision without distorting it, and it keeps alignment across the organization.
