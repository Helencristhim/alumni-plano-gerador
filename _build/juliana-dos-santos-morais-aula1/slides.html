Chapter 3: The Code

Who You Are, or What You Are Doing?

Two present tenses for one working life

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Show?

"I coordinate partnership meetings every month."

"This week I am preparing a call with a company from Toronto."

"We usually meet our member companies on Thursdays."

"At the moment we are discussing a new partnership."

Look at the time words in red. Some mean always, in general: every month, on Thursdays. Others mean now, not forever: this week, at the moment. The verb in blue changes with them.

JobFormExample
Your job, always truepresent simple: I / we + verbI coordinate partnership meetings.
A routine, how oftenpresent simple + every month / usuallyWe meet our members every month.
Something happening nowam / is / are + verb-ingAt the moment I am preparing a call.
A task for this week or montham / is / are + verb-ing + this weekThis week we are organizing an event.
The two questionsWhat do you do? / What are you working on?What do you do? — What are you working on now?
The trapstate verbs stay simpleNot I am knowing her. Say I know her well.

In one line: the simple form says who you are; the -ing form says what you are doing now.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Call, at Real Speed

One call, three recordings, one contact card

Dialogue

The Round of Introductions

D
Good morning, everyone. I am Daniel Ross, and I coordinate international programs at a trade association in Toronto. Juliana, would you like to start?
J
Sure. Good morning, Daniel. I am Juliana, and I am a coordinator at the Brazil-Canada Chamber of Commerce.
D
Nice to meet you. And what do you do there, exactly?
J
I work with partnerships and commercial contacts. I am the point of contact for many member companies.
D
Great. And what are you working on at the moment?
J
At the moment we are preparing a partnership with associations in Canada. That is why I reached out to you.
D
Perfect. I am in charge of our Brazil program, and my colleague Emma is based in Vancouver. She joins our calls every two weeks.
J
Wonderful. So you are my counterpart here. I will send you a short summary after the call.
Comprehension

About Daniel

1. Where does Daniel work, and what does he coordinate?
At a trade association in Toronto. He coordinates international programs.
2. What is Daniel in charge of?
The Brazil program of his association.
3. Who is Emma, and how often does she join the calls?
A colleague of Daniel, based in Vancouver. She joins every two weeks.
Listening 1 · The Host

How I Start an International Call

A meeting host explains what she wants from the first round of a call. Sound first — no text.

1. How long does she give each person?
About thirty seconds.
2. What is the problem with saying only a job title?
People from other countries do not know what the title means.
3. What three things does she ask for?
Your name, what you do, and what you are working on now.
Listening 2 · The Trade Officer

What I Do, and What I Am Doing Now

A trade officer in Ottawa describes his job and his current project. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he do in general?
He helps Canadian companies find partners in Latin America.
2. What is he working on this month?
A visit of five Canadian companies to São Paulo.
3. Why does he say both things?
Because his job explains why he is on the call, and his current project explains what people can ask him about.
Listening 3 · The Welcome Message

How a Chamber Works

A recorded welcome message for new member companies. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the chamber do for its members?
It connects companies from the two countries, organizes events and shares market information.
2. Who is the first person a new member talks to?
A point of contact on the chamber team.
3. What does the message ask new members to do?
To reach out to their point of contact and say what they are working on.
