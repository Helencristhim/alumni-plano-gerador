Live from the Beach

Stage 1.1: Competition Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Heat — a short competition between two or three surfers
"The heat is thirty minutes long."
Drop in — to take a wave that another surfer is already riding
"Never drop in on a local."
Barrel — the tube of water inside a breaking wave
"He is riding inside the barrel."
Judge — the person who gives the score
"The judges are watching from the tower."
Priority — the right to take the next wave before anybody else
"She has priority, so she is choosing the wave."
Cheer — to shout to support somebody
"The whole beach is cheering."
Final — the last heat of a competition
"They are surfing the final right now."
Commentator — the person who describes the action live on TV
"The commentator is talking very fast."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the live commentary. Then answer the questions.

Four minutes left in the final. The surfer in yellow has priority and she is waiting for a bigger set. The other one is paddling to the north end. The judges are watching from the tower and the commentator is talking very fast. Here it comes. She is going! She is riding inside the barrel and the whole beach is cheering. The judges watch every wave twice before they give a score — but this one is easy.
Why is the surfer in yellow not taking the first wave?
A She has priority and she is waiting for a bigger set.
B She is paddling to the north end.
What is the beach doing at the end?
A It is going home.
B It is cheering.
Why is it "the judges watch every wave twice" and not "are watching"?
A Because that is what they always do, not what is happening now.
B Because "judges" is more than one person.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Present continuous — happening right now

Two parts, never one: am / is / are + verb-ing. The verb to be is back — do not lose it.

The form

I am watching
He is paddling
They are cheering
He isn't winning
Are you watching?

Which one, when

every day, always, never
→ he surfs
now, right now, at the moment
→ he is surfing

Spelling: ride → riding · win → winning · sit → sitting · watch → watching.
In fast speech: he's paddling, they're cheering, what's happening? (the verb is small, but it is there)

Stage 4: On the sand

Quiz
A friend arrives late and asks about the heat. What do you say?
A She riding the barrel now.
B She's riding the barrel right now.
You are describing what the judges normally do, in every heat. Which sentence is correct?
A The judges watch every wave twice.
B The judges are watching every wave twice.
You want to know what is going on in the water. What do you ask?
A What happens out there?
B What's happening out there?
Somebody asks: "Are you competing today?" You are not. What do you answer?
A No, I'm not. I'm just watching.
B No, I don't. I just watch.

Stage 5: You on the microphone

Reflection
Open a surf video, turn the sound off, and be the commentator for 30 seconds in English. Who is paddling? Who has priority? What is the crowd doing? Check one thing before you send it — every sentence has am, is or are AND an -ing.
