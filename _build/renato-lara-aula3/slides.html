Grammar Discovery

Same verb, two times

Gabriel surfs every day.
Gabriel is surfing the final right now.
The judges watch every heat.
The judges are watching this wave.

Happening now → present continuous

Two parts, never one: am / is / are + verb-ing. The verb to be comes back — and without it the sentence is broken.

The Code

Two parts, always

The form

I am watching.
He is paddling.
They are cheering.
He isn't winning.
Are you watching?

Which one, when

every day, always, never
→ present simple: he surfs
now, right now, at the moment
→ continuous: he is surfing

Spelling: ride → riding · win → winning · sit → sitting

Did You Catch It?

What is happening?

1. Why is the girl in yellow not taking the first wave?
She has priority and she is waiting for a bigger one.
2. According to Jack, why can't the other surfer take that wave?
Because he would be dropping in on her.
3. What are the judges doing while everybody waits?
They are watching the wave again before they give the score.
Listening 1

Live commentary

1. How much time is left in the heat?
Four minutes.
2. What is the other surfer doing while the one in yellow waits?
She is paddling north, looking for something on the inside.
3. What does the commentator say about the crowd?
Everybody is standing up and cheering.
Listening 2

A voice note before the heat

1. Where is she, and what is she doing?
She is sitting on the sand, waiting for her heat.
2. What is her coach doing?
He is standing at the water, watching the waves and counting the sets.
3. How does she feel, and why?
Nervous, because the swell is getting bigger.
