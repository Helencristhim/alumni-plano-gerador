How Long Have You Been Surfing?

Eight career words (sponsor, injury, progress, goal, grommet, comp, lately, improve) and the present perfect continuous: have or has been plus -ing, with for and since.

Stage 1.1: Career Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Sponsor — a company that pays for your gear or your trips
"He has had the same sponsor since he was twelve."
Injury — damage to your body that stops you training
"I have been out with a knee injury for a month."
Progress — getting better little by little
"I have been making progress since January."
Goal — the thing you want to achieve
"My goal has been the same since I was ten."
Grommet — a young surfer, in Australian English
"He has been surfing here since he was a grommet."
Comp — short for competition -- nobody says the long word
"I have been doing comps for three years."
Lately — in the last few days or weeks
"The waves have been terrible lately."
Improve — to get better at something
"My English has been improving since March."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the profile. Then answer the questions.

I have been surfing since I was six, so that is nine years. I have been doing comps for three years, but I haven't been doing many lately, because I had a knee injury in May. I have been training with the same coach since January and honestly I have been making more progress than before. My goal has been the same since I was ten, and I have been studying English since August, three times a week, because of it.
Is he still surfing today?
A Yes — "have been surfing" means it started before and it is still going.
B No — he stopped after nine years.
Why hasn't he been doing many comps lately?
A Because he changed his goal.
B Because he had a knee injury in May.
Why is it "for three years" but "since January"?
A "For" says how long; "since" says when it started.
B They are the same and you can swap them.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Present perfect continuous — started before, still going

Three pieces: have / has + been + verb-ing. It is the tense that answers How long...?

The form

I have been surfing
He has been training
I haven't been surfing
How long have you been surfing?

for or since

for + how long
for six years, for a month
since + when it started
since 2019, since I was six

Compare: "I surfed for six years" → finished. "I have been surfing for six years" → still going.
Never "since three years". If it is a duration, it is for.
In speech: I've been, he's been. The "s" in "he's been" is HAS, and the -ing tells you so.

Stage 4: Meeting new people

Quiz
You started surfing at six and you still surf. What do you say?
A I surf since six years old.
B I've been surfing since I was six.
Three years of competitions, still going. Which one is correct?
A I've been doing comps for three years.
B I've been doing comps since three years.
You want to ask a new classmate about his time in that school.
A How long you have been in this school?
B How long have you been in this school?
You surfed for six years as a kid and then you stopped. How do you say it?
A I surfed for six years when I was a kid.
B I have been surfing for six years when I was a kid.

Stage 5: Nine years in sixty seconds

Reflection
Tell your surf story out loud for 30 seconds. How long have you been surfing? How long have you been competing? What have you been working on lately? What is the goal? Check one thing before you send it — "for" is how long, "since" is when it started.
