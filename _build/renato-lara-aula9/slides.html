Grammar Discovery

In which one do I still do it?

I surfed for six years. (and then?)
I have been surfing for six years. (and now?)
She trained with that coach last year.
She has been training with that coach since January.

Started before, and it is still going

The past simple closes the door: it happened, it finished. have / has been + verb-ing keeps it open: it started in the past and it is still true today. That is why you use it to answer How long...?

The Code

Three pieces, and two little words

The form

I have been surfing.
He has been training.
I haven't been surfing.
How long have you been surfing?

for or since

for + how long
for six years, for two months, for an hour
since + when it started
since 2019, since January, since I was six

In fast speech: I've been, he's been — and yes, that small verb is the same one from Lesson 1.

Did You Catch It?

About Zoe

1. How long has Zoe been doing comps, and why not many lately?
Three years. She hasn't been doing many lately because of a shoulder injury in May.
2. What has she been doing since August?
Training every morning, and making more progress than before the injury.
3. What is her goal, and how long has it been the same?
The junior tour, and it has been the same since she was ten.
Listening 1

Post-heat interview

1. How long has he been working on his backhand?
Since the start of the season, about eight months.
2. What has he not been doing lately, and why?
He hasn't been travelling much, because of a back injury.
3. What does he say about his sponsor?
The same one since he was thirteen, and they stayed with him during the injury.
Listening 2

What the coach has been seeing

1. How long has the student been training with her?
For two years.
2. What has changed in the last three months?
He has been arriving earlier and watching the water before he paddles out.
3. What has she NOT been seeing from him lately?
She hasn't been seeing him give up when a session goes badly.
