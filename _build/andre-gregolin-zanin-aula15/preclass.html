Talking About the World -- What Goes in the Spotlight

Key words: inequality, access, infrastructure, reform, controversial, perspective, mainstream, the way I see it, to bring something to light, to spark a debate. Structure: cleft sentences (It was... that / What... is).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Inequality -- an unfair difference between groups of people, in money, chances or rights
"Two streets apart, and the inequality is impossible to miss."
Access -- the chance or the right to use something, or to get to it
"In some towns, access to a specialist means a four-hour bus ride."
Infrastructure -- the basic systems a place needs to work: roads, hospitals, schools, internet
"The interior often has the talent. What it lacks is the infrastructure."
Reform -- a change to a system or a law, made to improve it
"Everybody agrees that the system needs reform. Nobody agrees on which one."
Controversial -- causing strong disagreement between people
"Any change to the vestibular is controversial. Somebody always loses."
Perspective -- a particular way of seeing something, shaped by your own experience
"Two years in Franca gave me a different perspective on São Paulo."
Mainstream -- what most people think, watch or accept; the normal, popular view
"Abroad, the mainstream picture of Brazil is beaches and football."
The way I see it -- a spoken way to start giving your personal opinion
"The way I see it, sport is one of the few ladders that still works."
To bring something to light -- to make a fact or a problem known to many people
"One small report brought the problem to light."
To spark a debate -- to make people start discussing something, often strongly
"One post about the vestibular sparked a debate in my class group."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Every sentence carries information, but not every piece of information is equally important. English has two sentence shapes that put one part in the spotlight. The first starts with It is or It was: It was basketball that taught me discipline. The information is the same as in Basketball taught me discipline, but now the listener knows that basketball, and not something else, is the point.

The second shape starts with What: What I miss most is the team. Here the spotlight falls at the end, on the team. Speakers use this shape all the time to introduce an idea slowly, and it is very common in interviews: What surprised me was the size of the city. Notice that be follows the time of the story, so a past experience takes was.

Two more openers work the same way: The reason I chose medicine is that I want to work with people, and All I want is to pass the exam. In speech, the part in the spotlight gets the strongest stress. Without that stress, a cleft just sounds long.

1. What does a cleft sentence change, according to the text?
A The information in the sentence, because it always adds a new fact that the listener did not know before.
B Which part of the sentence is the point.
C The tense of the sentence.
2. In “What I miss most is the team”, where is the spotlight?
A On “what”, because the first word is always the most important one in English.
B On “I miss”.
C At the end, on “the team”.
3. Why does the text use “was” in “What surprised me was the size of the city”?
A Because it is a past experience.
B Because a what-cleft must always use was, even when you are talking about the present.
C Because the city is singular.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Cleft Sentences

GRAMMAR

Choose the part the listener should remember, and put it in the spotlight.

ShapeFormExample
It-cleftIt + be + FOCUS + that / who + the restIt was my coach who taught me.
What-cleftWhat + clause + be + FOCUSWhat I need is more sleep.
Other openersThe reason... is that · The thing that... is · All I want is toThe reason I left is that I wanted medicine.
Timebe follows the time of the storyWhat surprised me was the size of the city.
Stressthe FOCUS gets the strongest stressIt was BASKETBALL that taught me discipline.

Never: What I miss it is the team · The reason is because... · What I want is study medicine

Stage 2: Put the Answer in Order

Order

A student answers the question “What does the world misunderstand about Brazil?” Put the answer in a logical order: the mainstream picture first, the conclusion last.

?"Abroad, the mainstream picture of Brazil is beaches and football."
?"But what people rarely see is how big the country really is."
?"I grew up in São Paulo, but it was two years in Franca that changed my perspective."
?"There, the talent was everywhere. What was missing was the infrastructure."
?"The way I see it, inequality in Brazil is not only about money."
?"It is about access, and that is the story I would like to bring to light."
