Chapter 3: Must, Have To, Should

Where Is the Rule Coming From?

Inside you, outside you, or nowhere at all

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Do?

"We have to confirm it in writing before anything moves."

"You should give her a heads-up before the meeting."

"You do not have to reply tonight, and nobody expects you to."

"You must not send it before they sign."

The red one comes from outside: a rule, a contract, a client. The blue one comes from you and the other person can still say no. Now look at the last two carefully: the orange one says there is no obligation, and the dark red one says it is forbidden. Portuguese uses one expression for both.

In one line: before you choose the word, ask where the rule is coming from — a contract, you, or nobody.

Comprehension

About Claire

1. What does Claire say happens when somebody answers an email late at night?
The person reading it starts to feel that they should answer at that hour too.
2. Does she forbid him from replying in the evening, or say something else?
Something else. She says he does not have to, and that nobody expects it — that is the absence of an obligation, not a prohibition.
3. What does she say at the end about the same behaviour in two places?
That it is the same behaviour and it means two different things.

The Rules Nobody Wrote Down

1. Why does she say nobody teaches the second set of rules?
Because the people who know them have stopped noticing that they exist.
2. What three signs does she describe?
A small silence, a slightly too quick change of subject, and an email answered in one line when the others get five.

Listening 2 · A Voice Note

The First Four Words

A voice note on two things that caught somebody out in a first year abroad. Sound first — no text.

1. What was the first thing, and how did he describe the reaction to it?
Answering email in the evening. He says it was not wrong exactly, just not done, and one or two people found it uncomfortable.
2. What was the second thing, and why was it harder?
Saying plainly what he thought in a meeting. The same sentence carried more weight in that room than in his own.
3. What did he change, and what did he not change?
He changed the first four words of the sentence. He did not change what he thought and he did not start hiding his opinion.
Real Document

How We Work Here

How we work here — one page
Given to everybody who works with us · page 1 of 1

Email. You do not have to reply outside working hours, and we would rather you did not. If something cannot wait, call.

Meetings. You should send anything you want read at least a day before. You must not bring a decision to the meeting that the group has not seen in writing.

Dates. We have to confirm every date in writing. As a rule of thumb, if a date is at risk you tell us the day you know, not the day it is due.

Disagreement. You should say so, and early. It might feel blunter than you are used to. It is not personal here.

Sample onboarding page written for this lesson. The company is invented.
1. Find one thing that is forbidden and one thing that is simply not necessary. Which words carry the difference?
Forbidden: you must not bring a decision the group has not seen in writing. Not necessary: you do not have to reply outside working hours. The words are must not and do not have to.
2. Which line is advice rather than a rule, and how do you know?
“You should send anything you want read at least a day before” and “you should say so, and early”. Should is advice: the reader can still decide otherwise.
3. The last line uses might. What is the writer doing by choosing that word?
Warning without accusing. Might marks it as a possibility for some readers, not a fact about all of them, so nobody has to defend themselves.
