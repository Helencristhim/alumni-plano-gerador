Chapter 4: The Code

What Is Closed, and What Is Still Open

Two tenses, one experience

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"I played in the championship last November."

"I have played competitive soccer since I was nine."

"I lived in Canada for five months, and then I came home."

"I have lived abroad, so I know what the first week feels like."

Two of the red verbs carry have and two do not. Now look at the blue part of each sentence: one of the four has no blue part at all, and that is not an accident.

JobFormExample
A finished event, in a closed timepast simple + last / in 2025 / two years agoI played in the championship last November.
Life experience, no time givenhave / has + past participleI have lived abroad.
Started in the past, still truehave / has + participle + for / sinceI have played soccer since I was nine.
Just now, and it still mattershave just / have already / have not yetI have just sent the application.
Asking about experienceHave you ever...? · I have never...Have you ever lived abroad?
for and sincefor + a length of time · since + a starting pointfor five months / since March
The two trapsnever present tense with for / since · never present perfect with a closed timeNot I am here since March: say I have been here since March. Not I have arrived last year: say I arrived last year.

In one line: if the time is closed, use the past simple; if the time is open or absent, use the present perfect.

Your File, and Two People Who Read Files

Real Document

File statusincomplete — one item outstanding
Reviewer note: a file marked incomplete is not rejected. It waits. Reviewers open it again on the day the last item arrives, and by then the rest of the file has usually been read by somebody else.
1. Three lines on this record are closed and finished, and three are still open. Which is which, and how do you know?
Closed: the exchange, which is completed, and the two letters, which have been received. Open: the sport, which has run since age nine, the development course, which is in progress, and the certificate, which has not been submitted yet.
2. What is the difference between "completed" and "in progress" on a record like this, for the person reading it?
Completed means the reviewer can count it today. In progress means it is a promise, so it only counts if the applicant explains what has already been done inside it.
3. According to the reviewer note, what actually happens to a file marked incomplete?
It is not rejected. It waits, and it is opened again on the day the last item arrives, by which time the rest has usually been read by somebody else.
Listening 1 · The Reviewer

Two Thousand Files, One Line Each

An admissions reviewer speaking to a room of school counsellors. Sound first — no text.

1. How many files does she say she reads, and how long does she say she spends on each one first time round?
About two thousand a year, and roughly seven minutes each on the first pass.
2. What does she say is the difference between the files she remembers and the rest?
The ones she remembers say what has changed about the person. The rest give her a list of things that happened, which she says every file already has.
3. What advice does she give about the word "significant", and why?
She says applicants should stop calling everything significant and instead show what came after it, because a reader can judge a consequence and cannot judge an adjective.
Listening 2 · Second Year

What Nobody Tells You About the First Month

A Brazilian student in his second year in Canada. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say he did wrong in his first month, and what did he do about it?
He spent the first month with other Brazilians because it was easier, and his English did not move. He then joined a student club where nobody spoke Portuguese.
2. What has changed for him since then, in his own words?
He says he has stopped preparing sentences in his head before he speaks, and that he now answers a question while he is still thinking about it.
3. What does he say about the language certificate, and what does he tell students who ask him about it?
He got his before he applied and he says it opened the door, but he tells students that the certificate proves the level, it does not build it, and that the two things have to happen in that order.
