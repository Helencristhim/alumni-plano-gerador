Podcast
BBC 6 Minute English — "Why take a gap year?"

Six minutes on why people spend a year outside a classroom before university, and what universities actually think about it. It is your own decision, argued by two people who are not you. Free, with the full transcript and vocabulary on the same page.

Tip: listen once without the transcript. Then write down two sentences from the episode in the present perfect and two in the past simple, and say why each one is in the tense it is in.

Talk
TED — Carol Dweck, "The power of believing that you can improve" (10 min)

A psychologist on one small word that changes what a result means: yet. It is the same word this lesson uses to keep a sentence open, and here it is the whole argument — not failed, failed yet.

Tip: count how many times she says "yet" and notice the tense around it every time. Then say one true sentence about your own file using "I have not... yet".

Video
TED-Ed on YouTube — Mia Nacamulli, "The benefits of a bilingual brain" (5 min)

Five animated minutes on what actually happens inside the head of somebody who lives in two languages, and why the age at which you start changes the result. It is the evidence behind a claim you will make about your own five months.

Tip: it is short and fast. Watch it twice, and pick one fact you could use to back up an argument about learning a language by living in it rather than studying it.
