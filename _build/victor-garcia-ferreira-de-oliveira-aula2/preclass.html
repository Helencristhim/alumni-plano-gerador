Lesson 02 -- Pre-class

From Living It to Telling It -- What Happened and What Has Changed

Five months in Canada are already yours. Saying them in a way that lands is a different skill, and English keeps two tenses apart to do it. Key words: an achievement, a recommendation, to adapt, significant, to qualify for something, competitive, to stand out from, to gain experience, to build confidence, to rise to the challenge. Structure: present perfect contrasted with past simple.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An achievement -- something difficult that you finished, and that you can point to
"Reaching the final in my first season is the achievement I would put first."
A recommendation -- a letter or a word from somebody who says you are worth taking
"I came back with two recommendations from teachers who had watched me for five months."
To adapt -- to change the way you do things so that a new place starts working for you
"It took me about three weeks to adapt to a school where nobody explained anything twice."
Significant -- big enough to change the picture, not simply big
"Five months is short, but the difference in my listening was significant."
To qualify for something -- to reach the level that lets you enter something or take part in it
"The team qualified for the final round in November, which nobody expected."
Competitive -- at a level where other people are actively trying to beat you
"It was a competitive league, not a school tournament. That changes everything."
To stand out from -- to be clearly different from everybody else in the same group
"Everybody has good grades. What made my file stand out from the rest was the season."
To gain experience -- to get the practice that only doing the thing can give you
"I did not go there to learn grammar. I went there to gain experience I could not get here."
To build confidence -- to slowly become sure of yourself by doing something again and again
"Nobody builds confidence in a week. I built mine on a bus, over nine months of away games."
To rise to the challenge -- to do well exactly at the moment the situation gets hard
"The first month was the worst month. After that I rose to the challenge and stopped counting the days."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Somebody reads an application file in about seven minutes, and in those seven minutes two kinds of sentence do two different jobs. The first kind closes a box. He studied in Canada for five months. The team qualified for the final round last November. He received two recommendations before he came home. Every one of those sentences carries a time that is over, and English answers a closed time with the past simple. Note the trap: last November, in 2025 and two months ago all close the box, so none of them can take the present perfect.

The second kind of sentence points at today, and that is where the present perfect lives. He has played competitive soccer since he was nine, and he still plays. He has not submitted the language certificate yet, so the file is still open. His listening has changed in a way he calls significant, and it is still changing. Nothing here is finished, so the verb takes have or has plus the past participle.

There is a third use, and it is the one that opens interviews: experience with no date at all. I have lived abroad. Have you ever studied in another country? Nobody is asking when; they are asking whether it is in you. One warning for a Portuguese speaker, and it is the most common mistake of all: never use the present tense with for or since. Not I am back in Brazil since June, but I have been back in Brazil since June. Get that one right and half the file writes itself.

1. "The team qualified for the final round last November." Why the past simple and not the present perfect?
A Because qualify is a verb that never takes the present perfect.
B Because "last November" is a closed time, and a closed time takes the past simple.
C Because the sentence is about a team and not about a person.
2. According to the text, what do "has played since he was nine" and "has not submitted it yet" have in common?
A Neither of them is finished, so both point at today.
B Both of them describe events that are completely over.
C Both of them need a date to be correct.
3. The text calls one mistake the most common of all. Which is it?
A Asking a question with "ever" instead of a date.
B Using the past simple for an experience with no date.
C Using the present tense with "for" or "since", as in "I am back in Brazil since June".

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect and Past Simple

GRAMMAR

If the time is closed, use the past simple. If the time is open or absent, use the present perfect.

JobFormExample
A finished event, in a closed timepast simple + last / in 2025 / two months agoI played in the championship last November.
Life experience, no time givenhave / has + past participleI have lived abroad.
Started in the past, still truehave / has + participle + for / sinceI have played soccer since I was nine.
Just now, and it still mattershave just / have already / have not yetI have not submitted the certificate yet.
Question about experienceHave you ever + past participle?Have you ever studied in another country?
Question about durationHow long have you + past participle?How long have you played competitively?
Negativedid not + base form / have not + participleI did not go in 2024. I have not been back since.

Never: I have played in the championship last November · I am back in Brazil since June · Did you ever study abroad? · I have received the letters two months ago.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Somebody who has read your record asks the open question: tell me about the five months. Give them what happened, in the past simple, and what has changed and is still true, in the present perfect. Keep the two apart, and finish with the one thing about you that is still going on today. Speak for two minutes without stopping.
