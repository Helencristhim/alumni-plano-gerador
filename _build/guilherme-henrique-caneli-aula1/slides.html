Chapter 3: The Code

Hold the Key Word for Last

Four ways to frame an idea so the room hears the part that matters

Grammar Discovery

Why Do These Sentences Start So Far Away?

"What investors want is a framework that outlives the government."

"What I have come to realize is that the pipeline is rarely the problem."

"The way I see it, a concession is a thirty-year promise."

"All we need from the regulator is a date."

Each idea could be said in fewer words: Investors want a stable framework. So why do the speakers make the room wait? Where does the most important word land, and what does the speaker gain?

JobFormExample
Move the key idea to the endWhat + clause + is + key ideaWhat investors want is stability.
Signal a considered viewWhat I have come to realize is that + clauseWhat I have come to realize is that the rules matter more.
Give your lens, not a verdictThe way I see it, + clauseThe way I see it, greenfield is still early.
Narrow the requestAll (that) + subject + need is + nounAll we need is a date.
Put an action in focusWhat + subject + did was + base verbWhat the ministry did was delay the auction.
A softer frameMy sense is that + clauseMy sense is that the funds are waiting.
The trapnever the way how; never is that isNot The way how I see it. Say The way I see it.

In one line: Open with what or the way, hold the key idea for the end, and let your voice fall on it.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Dinner Before the Panel

One conversation, two recordings, one run of show

Dialogue

A Seat Next to Toronto

C
Guilherme, right? Claire Tremblay. I run the infrastructure book at a pension fund in Toronto. So, what exactly do you do at GRI?
G
The short version: I run the infrastructure vertical for Latin America. What we do is put investors, operators and regulators in the same room, before the money moves.
C
And do they actually talk in those rooms? In my experience, a regulator and a pension fund mostly exchange business cards.
G
They talk when somebody makes them. That is my job at the table: I bring in the ministry, I unpack what the operator just said, and I connect the dots for the funds.
C
So where is the opportunity right now? Everyone in Toronto is asking me about Brazilian toll roads.
G
The way I see it, the pipeline is strong, but greenfield is still where the risk sits. The brownfield concessions are the ones that are ready now.
C
And the rules? We were in a public-private partnership in another market, and the government rewrote the contract after two years.
G
That is the right question. What I have come to realize is that project finance is rarely the problem. What moves the needle is whether the contract survives an election.
C
Then I want that conversation on stage. Are you moderating the Thursday panel?
G
I am opening it. And if you are willing, I would like to bring you in on the second question.
Comprehension

About Claire

1. Where does Claire work, and what does she run there?
At a pension fund in Toronto. She runs the infrastructure book.
2. According to Claire, what do a regulator and a pension fund usually do in those rooms?
They mostly exchange business cards.
3. What does she say everyone in Toronto is asking her about?
Brazilian toll roads.
4. What happened in the public-private partnership she mentions, and what does she ask Guilherme at the end?
The government rewrote the contract after two years. At the end she asks whether he is moderating the Thursday panel.
