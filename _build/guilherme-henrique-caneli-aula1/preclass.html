Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

The Executive Voice -- Putting the Key Idea Last

At a dinner in New York, a pension fund director asks what you do, and you have two sentences to make them count. Key words: Project finance, A concession, Greenfield, Brownfield, A toll road, A public-private partnership, A sovereign wealth fund, A pension fund, A regulatory framework, The pipeline, To bring in, To unpack, To connect the dots, To move the needle, A takeaway. Structure: wh-clefts and opinion frames to put the key idea last.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Project finance -- paying for a large project with the money the project itself will earn, not with the owner’s balance sheet
"The toll road was built with project finance, so the lenders look only at the traffic."
A concession -- a contract that lets a private company build and run a public asset for a fixed number of years
"The concession runs for thirty years, and then the airport goes back to the state."
Greenfield -- describes a project built from nothing, on a site where there was nothing before
"A greenfield port carries construction risk that a pension fund rarely wants."
Brownfield -- describes a project that expands or upgrades an asset that already exists and already earns money
"Brownfield assets pay from day one, and that is why the funds like them."
A toll road -- a highway where drivers pay every time they use it
"The toll road carries forty thousand cars a day."
A public-private partnership -- a long-term deal in which the government and a private company share the risks of a public project
"The hospital was delivered as a public-private partnership, and the state pays for its availability."
A sovereign wealth fund -- an investment fund owned by the government of a country
"A sovereign wealth fund from Abu Dhabi took twenty percent of the port."
A pension fund -- a fund that invests workers’ savings so that it can pay their retirement
"A pension fund needs income for decades, not a quick profit."
A regulatory framework -- the whole set of rules and agencies that control how a sector works
"Investors will wait until the regulatory framework is stable."
The pipeline -- the list of projects that are planned and expected to come to market
"The pipeline for rail is thin, but the pipeline for roads is full."
To bring in -- to invite another person to join a discussion that is already happening
"I would like to bring in the regulator on this point."
To unpack -- to explain a complex idea by taking it apart, one piece at a time
"Let me unpack that before we move on."
To connect the dots -- to show how facts that look separate are actually related
"Nobody had connected the dots between the election and the delay."
To move the needle -- to make a real, visible difference to a result
"Another roadshow will not move the needle; a stable contract will."
A takeaway -- the main point that people remember after a meeting or a talk
"The key takeaway from tonight is that the rules matter more than the rates."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Most executives believe the problem at a dinner table is vocabulary. It rarely is. The problem is where the important word lands. In a room where English is everyone’s second language, people listen for the end of the sentence, because that is where the voice falls. If the key idea is buried in the middle, it disappears.

English has a family of structures that solve this. The first is the wh-cleft: instead of “Investors want stability,” you say “What investors want is stability.” The sentence takes longer, and that is the point: the room waits for the last word. A variation signals that you have thought about it: “What I have come to realize is that the pipeline is rarely the problem.” Notice the word that. It cannot disappear.

When you want to give a personal view without sounding weak, use “The way I see it, greenfield is still where the risk sits.” Never “the way how I see it” — that is Portuguese in English clothes. When you want to make a request smaller and harder to refuse, use All: “All we need from the regulatory framework is a date.”

Finally, when the action is the news, put it in focus with do: “What the ministry did was postpone the auction for the concession.” None of these structures is decoration. Each one decides which word the investor from Abu Dhabi will remember when the dinner is over.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Clefts and Opinion Frames

GRAMMAR

Frame the idea first, hold the key word for the end, and let your voice fall on it.

JobFormExample
Move the key idea to the endWhat + clause + is + key ideaWhat investors want is stability.
Signal a considered viewWhat I have come to realize is that + clauseWhat I have come to realize is that the rules matter more.
Give your lensThe way I see it, + clauseThe way I see it, greenfield is still early.
Narrow a requestAll + subject + need is + nounAll we need is a date.
Put an action in focusWhat + subject + did was + base verbWhat the ministry did was delay the auction.
A softer frameMy sense is that + clauseMy sense is that the funds are waiting.

Never: the way how I see it · what I have come to realize is the pipeline it is strong · I would like to bring the minister · a project of greenfield
