One Question

Two Lawyers

The client: “Can we end the contract with our distributor next month?”

Lawyer A: “Yes. Clause 12 says thirty days’ notice. I’ll draft the letter today.”

Lawyer B: “Probably. But before I answer: what happened? Why now?”

Lawyer A answered faster. Which lawyer will find the real problem? Why?

Chapter 3: The Code

Short Questions, Exact Form

Who told you? Who did you tell? Not the same question.

Grammar Discovery

With Did or Without?

1. Who told you about the new CEO?

2. Who did you tell about the new CEO?

3. What happened after the board meeting?

4. Didn’t they sign an exclusivity clause?

In 1 and 2, who did the telling? Which questions have did, and which don’t? And in 4: do you think the answer is yes or no?

QuestionFormExample
Who / What is the subjectwho / what + verb (no do, does, did)Who told you? What happened?
Who / What is the objectwho / what + aux + subject + verbWho did you tell? What did she say?
Negative questionDidn’t / Isn’t / Haven’t + subject ... ? (you think yes, and you check)Didn’t they sign an exclusivity clause?
Preposition at the endquestion word ... verb + prepositionWho did you sign it with? What is it for?
The shortest follow-upsquestion word + prepositionWhat for? Since when? Like what?
The trapsdid in a subject question · no auxiliary in an object questionNot Who did tell you?, not Who you told?

In one line: if who or what did the action, no did; if somebody did it to who or what, did comes back.

Chapter 4: Real Voices

People Who Ask for a Living

One call, two recordings, one email

Dialogue

A Simple Question

C
Rodrigo, thanks for taking the call. I'll get straight to the point. Can we end our contract with our distributor in Brazil next month?
R
Possibly. But let me probe a little before I answer. What happened? Did they break the contract?
C
No, not at all. Their sales are fine. It's just that our new CEO wants a change.
R
I see. Who made that decision? And who else knows about it?
C
He made it last week, and only the board knows. Well, the board and an online retailer in São Paulo. He's been talking to them for months.
R
Then I won't beat around the bush. Is the real question how to end this contract, or how to start selling online without a fight?
C
Honestly? The second one. I didn't want to put you on the spot with the whole story in the first five minutes.
R
That's fine. Can you elaborate on one thing? Didn't the distributor sign an exclusivity clause with you?
C
They did, in 2022. And that's our underlying concern. If they're exclusive, can we even talk to the retailer?
R
That's exactly what we need to get to the bottom of. Send me the contract today, and I'll call you on Thursday with an answer.
Comprehension

About Claire

1. Is Claire's company unhappy with the distributor's sales?
No. Their sales are fine. The new CEO just wants a change.
2. Who knows about the CEO's decision, according to Claire?
Only the board, and an online retailer in São Paulo that the CEO has been talking to for months.
3. What is Claire's underlying concern?
The exclusivity clause the distributor signed in 2022: it may stop them from talking to the retailer.
Listening 1 · The Doctor

One More Thing

A family doctor on the question patients really came to ask. Sound first — no text.

1. When do many patients mention the real reason for their visit?
At the very end, with a hand on the door handle, as they are leaving.
2. Why doesn't she ask "Is that all?" anymore?
Because people always say yes.
3. What does she ask instead, early in the visit?
"What else is worrying you today?" — an open-ended question.
Listening 2 · The Radio Journalist

The Second Question

A radio journalist on how he interviews politicians. Sound first — no text.

1. Which question does he prepare before an interview?
Only the first one. The interview happens in the second question.
2. What does he ask when a politician says "Mistakes were made"?
"Who made them?"
3. Why does he never ask two questions at once?
Because people always answer the easy one.
Real Document

The First Email

Subject: Quick question about our distributor
From: Claire Dunn, Operations Director, Hartwell Kitchens (Chicago) · To: Rodrigo Marques de França

Hi Rodrigo,

Our new CEO has asked me to check one thing with you before Friday: can we end our agreement with our Brazilian distributor with thirty days’ notice?

Their numbers are fine, so this is not about performance. We would simply like more flexibility in the Brazilian market from next year.

Could you also let me know if there is anything that might stop us from speaking to other partners in the meantime?

Thanks so much · Claire
1. What does Claire ask directly?
If they can end the agreement with their Brazilian distributor with thirty days' notice.
2. Which sentence hides a second question?
The last one: "anything that might stop us from speaking to other partners in the meantime" — they are already talking to somebody else.
3. What is the first follow-up question you would ask Claire?
For example: "Who would you like to speak to, and have you spoken to them already?"

Chapter 6: Your Turn

You Ask the Questions

Guided > Semi-free > Free

Role-Play 1 of 3 — Guided

The Intake Call

Scenario: A new client from a hotel group calls with a simple question about the lease of a hotel in Rio. Do not give advice yet. Ask at least six short follow-up questions until you find the question behind the question.

Keyword chips:

What happened...? Who did you...? Didn't they...? an underlying concern
Role-Play 2 of 3 — Semi-free

The Renovation

Scenario: A contractor is going to renovate your kitchen. Every answer is friendly, and every answer is vague. Before you sign anything, find out how long it will take, how much it will cost, who will do the work, and what happens if it is late.

Keywords:

Who will...? Didn't you say...? What for? elaborate
Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

Now You Interview

Scenario: Two lessons ago, you were the guest. Today you interview your teacher about their career, for five minutes. Every main question needs at least two follow-up questions before you change the subject.

No keywords, no notes.
