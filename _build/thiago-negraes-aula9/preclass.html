Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

A commentator uses two tenses all afternoon, and switches between them in the same breath. For what is happening while he speaks, the verb takes am, is or are plus -ing: "he is overtaking on the outside", "they are changing the tyres". For a habit, a rule or a fact that does not depend on this second, the verb stays simple: "he usually starts well", "a pit stop takes about two seconds", "qualifying decides the grid". Portuguese uses the same simple form for both ideas, which is why he overtakes right now keeps coming out. And a small group of verbs never takes -ing at all, because they describe a state and not an action: know, want, mean, need, believe.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present continuous vs present simple

GRAMMAR

One question decides everything: is it happening while I speak?

TenseSignal wordsExample
Present continuousnow, right now, at the moment, todayHe is overtaking right now.
Present simplealways, usually, normally, every raceHe usually starts well.
General truthno time word at allA pit stop takes two seconds.
Never continuousknow, want, mean, need, believeI know this circuit.

Two details that decide the sentence: in the present simple the third person takes an -s ("qualifying decides the grid"), and in the continuous the negative and the question move the be, not the main verb: "he is not overtaking", "is he overtaking?".
