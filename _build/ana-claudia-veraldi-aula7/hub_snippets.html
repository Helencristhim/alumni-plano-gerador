The House I'm Restoring -- What Was Done, and What Is Still Waiting

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To strip back -- to take the old paint or paper off until only the bare surface is left
"Every wall in the back room had to be stripped back to the brick."
To gut -- to empty a room completely, until only the walls and the floor are left
"The kitchen was gutted in a single weekend."
To rewire -- to replace all the old electrical wiring in a building
"The ground floor is being rewired at the moment."
Damp -- the wet that gets into old walls and slowly ruins them from inside
"There was damp coming up through the kitchen wall."
Beam -- a long, thick piece of wood that holds a roof or a ceiling up
"One beam under the roof had to be replaced completely."
Plaster -- the smooth grey layer that goes on a wall before any paint does
"The old plaster came off the wall in sheets."
Sagging -- bending down in the middle because it is old or badly supported
"The ceiling in the back room is sagging in the middle."
Sturdy -- strong, solid and unlikely to break or move
"The beams are a hundred and forty years old and still completely sturdy."
A quote -- the written price a builder gives you before any work starts
"The first quote we got was almost twice the second one."
To take something on -- to accept a difficult job knowing how much it is going to demand
"Everyone asked why I took on a house nobody else wanted."
To cut corners -- to do a job faster and cheaper by skipping the steps that matter
"The previous owner cut corners everywhere, and we are still paying for it."
A money pit -- a building that keeps swallowing money long after you expected it to stop
"Everybody warned me the place would turn into a money pit."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The house was built in 1912 and almost nothing had been done to it since the seventies. The plaster was falling off the walls, there was damp in the kitchen, and one ceiling was visibly sagging.

Two quotes were sent to Ana before she chose a builder. The cheaper one was rejected, because the man who wrote it wanted to paint straight over the damp. That is exactly how the previous owner cut corners, and the same wall had to be stripped back twice.

Today the back room is being replastered and the ground floor is being rewired. The beams were inspected before anything else started: one was replaced, the other six were declared completely sturdy. The windows have not been touched. Ana knew what she was taking on, and she still calls the place a money pit, affectionately.

1. Why does the text say "the house was built in 1912" and not "somebody built the house in 1912"?
A Because the builder is unknown, irrelevant, or both — the house is the subject.
B Because the passive is always more polite than the active.
C Because English avoids dates in active sentences.
2. "The back room is being replastered." What does this form tell you?
A That the work is planned but has not started.
B That the work is in progress right now, unfinished.
C That the work was finished a long time ago.
3. "The windows have not been touched." Why the present perfect passive here?
A Because the result is still true today: they are untouched as you read this.
B Because the windows do not exist yet.
C Because the sentence is about a specific date in the past.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive Voice

GRAMMAR

The work goes first. The worker goes last, or nowhere at all.

FormWhat it doesExample
be + past participleThe general shape of every passiveThe roof was replaced.
present simple passiveHow it is normally doneOld floors are sanded before they are sealed.
present continuous passiveHappening right nowThe kitchen is being rewired.
present perfect passiveFinished, result still visibleThat wall has been plastered twice.
past simple passiveFinished, at a known momentThe beams were cut by hand.
by + personOnly when who did it is the newsIt was built by a Portuguese carpenter.
get + past participleThe spoken, everyday versionA window got broken in the storm.

Never: The house was build · the kitchen is rewiring · the floors have sanded · the fire was happened. Two of those lose the verb be, one loses the participle, and the last one makes a passive out of a verb that cannot have one.

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

Somebody asks Ana about the house. Put the exchange in a logical order.

?"So what state was it in when you got the keys?"
?"Terrible. Nothing had been done to it since the seventies."
?"And what is happening now?"
?"The back room is being replastered and the ground floor is being rewired."
?"Is there anything you have decided to leave alone?"
?"The windows. They have not been touched, and I am in no hurry."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

A visitor asks what has been done to the back room. You answer:
A "The plaster has been stripped back and the ceiling is being repaired."
B "Somebody has stripped the plaster and somebody repairs the ceiling."
C "The plaster has stripped back and the ceiling is repairing."
You want to say the wiring work is happening at this moment. The natural version is:
A "The ground floor is rewiring this week."
B "The ground floor is being rewired this week."
C "The ground floor was rewiring this week."
A builder offers to paint over the damp. You refuse politely:
A "No. You are trying to cheat me like the last one."
B "I would rather not. That wall was painted over once already, and it had to be stripped back."
C "The wall was happened before, so no."
Somebody asks who replastered the walls. You have no idea. The natural answer is:
A "It was done before I bought the place."
B "It was done by somebody, I think by a man."
C "Nobody was plastered the walls."
