The House I’m Restoring -- How Things Get Done

Talking about a renovation: what was done, by whom, and what is still being fixed. Key words: renovation, beam, plumbing, wiring, leak, damp, to sand down, to strip, to salvage, to knock down, rewarding, to be worth it. Structure: the passive voice (be + past participle) in the past, the present, and for work happening right now.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Renovation -- the work of repairing and improving an old building
"The renovation was supposed to take eight weeks."
Beam -- a long, thick piece of wood that holds up a roof or a floor
"Two of the beams in the ceiling had to be replaced."
Plumbing -- the system of pipes that carries water through a building
"The plumbing was replaced by a professional."
Wiring -- the system of electric cables inside the walls
"The old wiring was dangerous, so it was all redone."
Leak -- a crack or a hole that lets water escape
"A leak was found behind the kitchen wall."
Damp -- slightly wet, in a way that damages walls and wood
"Every wall was damp when we bought the place."
To sand down -- to rub a surface with rough paper until it is smooth
"The floors were sanded down by hand."
To strip -- to remove the old paint or covering from a surface
"The doors were stripped and painted again."
To salvage -- to save something old and useful before it is thrown away
"Two doors were salvaged from a house down the road."
To knock down -- to destroy a wall or a building on purpose
"That wall was knocked down by a builder."
Rewarding -- giving a good feeling because the effort produced something real
"Slow work on an old house is rewarding."
To be worth it -- good enough to justify the time, money and effort
"It took months, but it was worth it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Ana’s house was built in the nineteen forties, and it had been empty for years. The roof was leaking and every wall was damp. The wiring and the plumbing were replaced by professionals, because that work is not for an amateur. One wall was knocked down by a builder, so the kitchen and the living room became one room. Notice the pattern: the sentence starts with the THING, not the person, and the verb is always be + past participle. The floors were stripped and sanded down by hand — here the agent stays, because who did it is the interesting part: she did. Right now the back room is being painted, and the damp treatment on the north wall has not been started. It has taken years, but it was worth it.

1. Why does the text say "the wiring was replaced" instead of "an electrician replaced the wiring"?
A Because the important information is the wiring, not the person who did the work.
B Because the passive is more polite than the active.
C Because we never name people in written English.
2. Why does "by hand" stay in "the floors were stripped and sanded down by hand"?
A Because every passive sentence needs an agent after "by".
B Because how it was done is surprising, and that is the point of the sentence.
C Because the verb "sand down" cannot be used without "by".
3. What is the difference between "was painted" and "is being painted"?
A There is no difference; they are two ways of saying the same thing.
B "Was painted" is informal and "is being painted" is formal.
C "Was painted" is finished; "is being painted" is happening right now.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive Voice

GRAMMAR

The structure for when the thing matters more than the person.

FormUseExample
Past passive
was / were + past participle		Something that happened to the thing, and is finished.The house was built in 1946.
Present passive
is / are + past participle		A general fact, a rule, or a current state.Old beams are treated against damp.
Happening now
is / are being + past participle		The work is going on at this moment, unfinished.The kitchen is being painted.
by + agentAdd it ONLY when who or how is interesting or surprising.The floors were sanded by hand.
QuestionQuestion word + was/were + thing + past participleWhen was it built?
In one lineThe thing first, then be + past participle. The person only after "by", and only when it matters.
