Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

The House I Am Restoring -- What Was Done, and What Is Being Done

Beams, plaster and damp, and the structure everybody uses to talk about an old building. Key words: a beam, plaster, to strip, damp, to rewire, to sand down, sturdy, shoddy, off-the-shelf, to cut corners, a money pit, built to last. Structure: the passive voice, in every tense you actually need.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A beam -- a long heavy piece of wood or metal that holds up a roof or a floor
"The beams were cut from one single tree."
Plaster -- the soft material spread on a wall to make it smooth
"The plaster was removed by hand, one square meter at a time."
To strip -- to take off old paint or covering until you reach the original surface
"We stripped the floorboards ourselves."
Damp -- wet inside the wall, in a way that slowly damages a building
"Everything downstairs was damp when we bought it."
To rewire -- to replace all the electrical wiring in a building
"The whole house was rewired in eleven days."
To sand down -- to rub a surface again and again until it is completely smooth
"The floorboards were sanded down over six weekends."
Sturdy -- strong, firm and not likely to break or move
"Those beams are sturdier than anything you can buy today."
Shoddy -- made badly, quickly and without any care
"The roof was shoddy work and it all had to come off."
Off-the-shelf -- ready-made and bought exactly as it is, not made for your house
"An off-the-shelf window would not fit an opening from 1932."
To cut corners -- to do a job badly in order to save time or money
"Do not cut corners on the roof. You pay for it twice."
A money pit -- something that keeps costing more and more, with no end
"People call these houses a money pit and they are not wrong."
Built to last -- made so well that it will still be standing in a hundred years
"It was built to last. Once it is done properly, it is done."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The house was built in 1932 and it has been repaired badly at least three times since then. When Ana bought it, everything downstairs was damp and nobody could say who had put the steel bar in the front wall.

The plaster was removed by hand, one square meter at a time, because a machine would have taken the brick with it. The beams have been treated twice; they were cut from one single tree and they are sturdier than anything you can buy today. The floorboards were stripped and sanded down over six weekends.

The kitchen is being rewired at the moment. The roof has not been started yet, and the whole thing will have to be replaced, because it was done cheaply and shoddily by the previous owner. Ana calls the house a money pit, and then she says it was built to last, and that she is not going to cut corners on it.

1. Why "the plaster was removed by hand" and not "somebody removed the plaster by hand"?
A Because the passive is a more formal way of saying exactly the same thing.
B Because the plaster is what matters here, and who did it is unknown or unimportant.
C Because remove is a verb that has no active form.
2. "The kitchen is being rewired at the moment." Why is being there?
A Because the work is happening right now, and the continuous passive needs is or are plus being.
B Because the kitchen is a place and places take being.
C Because it is a polite way of saying it is finished.
3. "It was done cheaply by the previous owner." Why does the person come back into this sentence?
A Because every passive sentence needs a by phrase to be complete.
B Because the sentence is in the past.
C Because here the doer is the point: it explains why the work was bad.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Be Plus the Past Participle

GRAMMAR

One machine, every tense, and the one moment the person comes back.

TenseFormExample
Pastwas / were + participleThe house was built in 1932.
Presentis / are + participleThese tiles are made by hand.
Right nowis / are being + participleThe kitchen is being rewired.
Up to nowhas / have been + participleThe beams have been treated twice.
With a modalmodal + be + participleThe roof will have to be replaced.
by + personOnly when the doer is the pointIt was done cheaply by the previous owner.

Never: The tiles was made · The kitchen is rewired at the moment · The beams have been treat twice · The roof was replaced by somebody last year.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

A colleague asks how old the house is. You answer:
A "The house was build in 1932."
B "The house was built in 1932."
C "The house is built in 1932 by somebody."
The electricians are in the kitchen right now. You say:
A "The kitchen is rewired at the moment."
B "The kitchen is being rewired at the moment."
C "The kitchen has been rewiring at the moment."
The work on the beams is finished, and it happened twice. You say:
A "The beams have been treated twice."
B "The beams have been treat twice."
