Chapter 3: The Code

Who Did It? Nobody Says.

be + past participle — and when the name comes back

Grammar Discovery

Where Did the Person Go?

"The house was built in 1932."

"The kitchen is being rewired at the moment."

"The beams have been treated twice."

"The floor was laid by my grandfather."

In all four, the orange part is the same machine: a form of be plus a past participle. The blue part appears only once, and only because there it is the whole point.

TenseFormExample
Pastwas / were + participleThe house was built in 1932.
Presentis / are + participleThese tiles are made by hand.
Right nowis / are being + participleThe kitchen is being rewired.
Up to nowhas / have been + participleThe beams have been treated twice.
With a modalmodal + be + participleThe roof had to be replaced.
Why use itThe doer is unknown, obvious or unimportant.Nobody knows who laid it. Say it anyway.
by + personOnly when the doer is the point.... by my grandfather.

In one line: be + participle, and the name only comes back when it matters.

Chapter 4: Many Englishes

Same Damp, Different Accents

A conversation, and two houses that were nearly lost

Dialogue

What Was Wrong With It?

M
So this place of yours. How old is it, and what was wrong with it when you got it?
A
It was built in 1932. Everything downstairs was damp, and the plaster had to be taken off by hand.
M
By hand? That is weeks of work. Why not a machine?
A
A machine would have taken the brick with it. And the beams turned out to be perfect — sturdier than anything you can buy today.
M
Ours were the same. Ours had been empty twelve years, mind you, and somebody had painted every window shut.
A
What did you do with the floor?
M
We stripped it ourselves and it was sanded down over six weekends. The roof was the problem. It had been done shoddy and cheap, so the whole thing came off.
A
People call these houses a money pit. They are not wrong. But mine was built to last, and I am not the one who is going to cut corners on it.
Comprehension

About Mick

1. How long had Mick’s house been empty, and what had somebody done to the windows?
Twelve years. Somebody had painted every window shut.
2. What did he and his family do to the floor, and how long did it take?
They stripped it themselves, and it was sanded down over about six weekends.
3. What was the real problem in his house, and why?
The roof. It had been done shoddy and cheap, so the whole thing had to come off.
Listening 1 · The Netherlands

Sixteen Seventy-One

A house repaired badly four times, and two steel bars nobody can explain. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What was holding the front wall up, and who put it there?
Two steel bars that were put there in the nineteen sixties. Nobody could tell them who had put them there.
2. Why was the plaster removed by hand?
Because a machine would have taken the brick with it.
3. What are his two pieces of advice?
Do not cut corners on the roof, and do not believe anybody who gives you a number in the first week.
Listening 2 · Australia

Two Meters Off the Ground

A house on posts, empty for twelve years, and a roof that had to come off. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Why is the house built on posts?
It was designed that way so the air could move underneath. He says that is not decoration.
2. What had been done to the floor before they arrived?
It had been covered with three layers of vinyl.
3. Does he regret it? What is his last word on the house?
No. He agrees it is a money pit, but it was built to last, and once it is done properly it is done for another hundred years.
