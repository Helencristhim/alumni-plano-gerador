Comprehension

About Maddie

1. What has already been done to Maddie’s house, and what is being done right now?
The kitchen was gutted in January, and the whole back of the house is being rewired at this moment. Everything else has not been touched.
2. What is stopping Maddie from dealing with the damp?
The price. Two quotes have been sent to her and, in her words, both of them are terrifying.
3. What does Ana warn her about, and what happened to Ana because of it?
She warns her not to take the cheap option. Ana’s own damp work was done by somebody who cut corners, and the wall had to be stripped back again eighteen months later.

Nothing Had Been Done Since 1974

A Dutch man on the house he bought in Rotterdam. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What condition was the house in when he bought it, and why was the price so low?
Nothing had been done to it since about nineteen seventy four, and it showed from the street. The price was low because everybody else had walked away.
2. What was the one thing that made him go ahead with the purchase?
The beams. They were inspected before he made the offer and declared completely sound. The roof was fine too, which surprised him.
3. What does he say nobody warns you about, and what did it cost him?
The damp. Two walls were stripped back to the brick and left open for a month, and he lived with plastic sheeting for a whole winter.
Chapter 4: The Code

The Work Comes First

was replaced · is being rewired · has been repaired — and no builder anywhere

Grammar Discovery

Where Is the Person?

"The roof was replaced two years before I bought the house."

"The whole ground floor is being rewired at the moment."

"That wall has been plastered three times, and it is still not flat."

"These beams were cut by hand, about a hundred and forty years ago."

Somebody replaced the roof. Somebody is rewiring the floor. In all four sentences, where is that somebody? And ask yourself the better question: does the house care?

FormWhat it doesExample
be + past participleThe general shape. The thing done matters more than who did it.The roof was replaced.
present simple passiveHow it normally is, or how it is normally done.Old floors are sanded before they are sealed.
present continuous passiveHappening right now.The kitchen is being rewired this week.
present perfect passiveFinished, with a result you can still see.That wall has been plastered twice.
past simple passiveFinished, at a known moment in the past.The beams were cut by hand.
by + personOnly when who did it is the news. Usually you leave it out.It was built by a Portuguese carpenter.
get + past participleThe spoken, everyday version. Common in speech, rare in writing.A window got broken in the storm.

In one line: the passive puts the work first and the worker last — or nowhere at all.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"The house was build in 1948."
"The house was built in 1948."
"The kitchen is rewiring this week."
"The kitchen is being rewired this week."
"The floors have sanded already."
"The floors have already been sanded."
"The fire was happened in the old kitchen."
"The fire happened in the old kitchen."

Three of these four are the same habit: the little verb be goes missing, or the participle turns back into an infinitive. The fourth is the opposite problem — happen has no passive at all, because nobody happens anything.

Listening 2

Everything I Was Told to Skip

An Australian builder on the corners people ask him to cut. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What question is he asked every week, and what is his honest answer?
People ask him if a step can be skipped. He says almost any of it can be skipped, and almost all of it will come back.
2. Why does he refuse to paint over damp?
Because in eight months the wall will be exactly where it was, only now with a coat of paint on it that has to be removed as well.
3. What will he never let anybody skip, and what does he say about a house that has been done properly?
Anything structural — a sagging beam gets replaced and he will walk off a job over it. A house done properly does not look better on the day it is finished; it only looks better in ten years.
Real Document

The Builder’s Quote

QUOTE — A. VERALDI
Ref 0714 · valid for 30 days
Page 1 of 1
Not a contract
Item 01Existing plaster stripped back to the brick, all four walls of the back room
Item 02Roof beams inspected; one sagging beam replaced, the rest treated and left
Item 03Ground floor rewired; the original fuse box removed and taken away
Item 04Damp treated along the kitchen wall, then left open for three weeks
Item 05Walls replastered and left to dry for 21 days before any paint is applied
Not includedFloors, windows and chimney. A separate quote will be sent next week.
1. Say item 02 as one full sentence. Which two passives does it need?
"The roof beams were inspected, one sagging beam was replaced, and the rest were treated and left." Past simple passive throughout — the work is finished and the date is known.
2. Say item 05 in the present, as the builder would describe his method.
"The walls are replastered and left to dry for twenty one days before any paint is applied." Present simple passive — this is how it is always done, not one particular wall.
3. Read the last line. Why is a separate quote will be sent better here than we will send you a separate quote?
Because the company is not the point and everybody already knows who is sending it. The passive keeps the quote itself in the position of importance — which is also why it sounds more formal and more careful.
