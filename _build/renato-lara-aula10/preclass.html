Stage 1.1: Profile Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Profile — a short text about who somebody is and what they have done
"Every athlete on the site has a profile."
Achievement — something important that you did
"His biggest achievement was the junior title."
So far — until now, and it is not finished
"He has won two events so far this season."
Debut — the first time you do something in public
"She made her debut at fourteen."
Title — the position of champion
"He has won the junior title twice."
Retire — to stop doing your sport professionally
"She retired in 2023 and became a coach."
Background — where somebody comes from and what they did before
"Her background is skateboarding, not surfing."
Highlight — the best moment of a game or a season
"That wave was the highlight of the event."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the profile. Then answer the questions.

Her background is skateboarding. She started surfing at eleven and she made her debut in 2022, when she was fourteen. Since then she has entered nineteen events and she has won two titles. This season she has made three finals so far. The highlight? Everybody says the same wave: she scored a nine point eight last April. And there is one thing she has never done: she has never surfed a reef in a competition.
Why is it "she made her debut in 2022" and not "she has made"?
A Because the sentence has a finished date.
B Because "make" is irregular.
How many finals has she made this season?
A Nineteen.
B Three so far, and the season is not over.
What has she never done?
A She has never surfed a reef in a competition.
B She has never won a title.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Present perfect or past simple?

The shortcut that solves most cases: if the sentence has a finished date, use the past simple.

Present perfect — no date

He has won two titles.
I have never surfed a reef.
Have you ever been there?
Two wins so far.

Past simple — finished time

He won in 2024.
I surfed a reef last summer.
Did you go last year?
She retired in 2023.

Words that call the present perfect: ever, never, so far, already, yet, this season.
Words that call the past simple: in 2024, last April, yesterday, when I was twelve, two years ago.
Never mix them: "I have won in 2024" is broken. Either the date goes, or the "have" does.

Stage 4: The interview

Quiz
You won something in 2024. How do you say it?
A I have won my first comp in 2024.
B I won my first comp in 2024.
Two wins, and the season is not over. Which one is correct?
A I've won two events so far this season.
B I won two events so far this season.
You want to ask about somebody's experience, with no date in mind.
A Did you ever been to a reef break?
B Have you ever surfed a reef break?
She stopped competing in 2023. How do you say it?
A She retired in 2023.
B She has retired in 2023.

Stage 5: Your own profile

Reflection

What comes next. Check one thing before you send it — no sentence has "have" AND a finished date at the same time.
