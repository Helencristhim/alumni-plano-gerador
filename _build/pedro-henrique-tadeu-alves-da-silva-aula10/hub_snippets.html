Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A go-to person -- the person you always ask when you need something done
"Marcos is the go-to person who knows every cable in the studio."
Behind the scenes -- out of sight of the audience, where the real work happens
"Most of the work that makes a show feel easy happens behind the scenes."
A point of contact -- the one person you talk to about a specific thing
"Dara is the point of contact for the backing tracks, so ask her first."
To go unnoticed -- to happen or be done without anybody seeing it
"When a handoff works, it goes unnoticed. When it fails, everybody sees it."
Unsung -- important, but never praised or thanked in public
"The runner who found the cable is the unsung hero of the night."
To delegate -- to give part of your work to somebody you trust
"You cannot mix, edit and book the band yourself. You have to delegate."
A handoff -- the moment a piece of work passes from one person to the next
"The arrangement was perfect. The handoff to the band was the problem."
To keep somebody in the loop -- to keep somebody informed about what is happening
"Please keep the video team in the loop when the click track changes."
To step in -- to take over a job when the usual person cannot do it
"Marcos was sick on Thursday, so somebody else had to step in."
A stakeholder -- a person who is affected by a decision and has a say in it
"Before we change the schedule, let us talk to every stakeholder."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Stay in your seat after any live show and watch the credits. The host is the person who everybody remembers. The credits are a list of the people who made the host possible: the stage manager who counted the seconds, the engineer who kept the vocals clean. Almost all of that work happens behind the scenes, and almost all of it goes unnoticed. Look at the small word after each person, because it is the grammar of the whole lesson: it points back to the noun and says which one you mean.

The word changes with what it points back to. For a person, use who: every show has a go-to person who knows which door is locked. For a thing, use which: the arrangement which was perfect on Monday arrived without notes. In everyday speech, that works for both: the file that goes to the video team, the runner that I trust. For a place, use where plus a full sentence: the control room is the place where every handoff happens.

Two warnings. First: never repeat the pronoun. Not the person who she answers questions — say the person who answers questions. Second: after where, nothing is missing, so do not add in it at the end. And one freedom: when the word is the object of the clause, you can leave it out. The runner I trust most is perfect English.

Now the useful part. The best teams give every task a point of contact who answers questions about it and keeps everybody in the loop. When the go-to person is sick, somebody can step in, because it is written down. If you want to run a project one day, you will have to delegate, talk to every stakeholder before a big decision, and thank the unsung people by name.

1. "The host is the person who everybody remembers." What does "who" point back to?
A "Everybody", because it is the next word.
B "The person", the noun just before it.
C Nothing. It only asks a question.
2. Which word do you use for a place, like a control room?
A "Where", followed by a full sentence, with nothing added at the end.
B "Who", because people work there.
C "Which", followed by "in it" at the end.
3. According to the text, what does a point of contact do?
A Takes over every job when somebody is sick.
B Reads the credits at the end of the show.
C Answers questions about one task and keeps everybody in the loop.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Relative Clauses

GRAMMAR

One question: is it a person, a thing or a place?

Points back toWordExample
A personwhothe engineer who kept the vocals clean
A thingwhichthe arrangement which was perfect on Monday
A person or a thing, everyday speechthatthe runner that I trust · the file that goes to video
A placewhere + a full sentencethe studio where we record the live week
Negativewho / which + does notthe singer who does not need a click track
QuestionWho is the person who...?Who is the person who sends the backing tracks?
The objectyou can leave the word outthe runner (that) I trust most
The trapnever a second pronounNot the person who she mixes. Say the person who mixes.

Never: the assistant which checks the microphones · the person who she sends the tracks · the studio where we record in it · the arrangement who arrived

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A new colleague asks who checks the microphones. You say:
A "Marcos is the assistant which checks every microphone."
B "Marcos is the assistant who checks every microphone."
C "Marcos is the assistant who he checks every microphone."
Somebody asks where the handoffs happen. You say:
A "Control room B is the place where all the handoffs happen."
B "Control room B is the place where all the handoffs happen in it."
C "Control room B is the place who all the handoffs happen."
Your go-to person is sick on Thursday. The producer asks what the plan is. You say:
A "Nobody knows what he does, so we cancel the rehearsal."
B "Dara can step in, and I will keep the video team in the loop."
C "Dara can step on, and I keep the video team in the circle."
The season is over and you want to thank the team. You say:
A "Thanks to everybody, in general, for everything."
B "I thank the engineer which he kept the vocals clean."
C "I want to thank Marcos, who checked every microphone all season."
