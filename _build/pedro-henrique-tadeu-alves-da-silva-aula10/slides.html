Chapter 4: The Code

One Small Word That Points Back

A title says what somebody is called. The clause says which one.

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Words Do?

"The host is the person who everybody remembers."

"The arrangement which was perfect on Monday arrived without notes."

"They look for the one person that they trust to fix it."

"The credits are the only place where the whole machine is visible."

Each red word points back to the noun just before it. The blue part tells you which one you mean.

Points back toWordExample
A personwhothe engineer who kept the vocals clean
A thingwhichthe arrangement which was perfect on Monday
A person or a thing, everyday speechthatthe runner that I trust · the file that goes to video
A placewhere + a full sentencethe studio where we record the live week
When the word is the objectyou can leave it outthe runner (that) I trust most · the song (which) we chose
The trapnever a second pronounNot the person who she mixes. Say the person who mixes.

In one line: the clause replaces a job title with what the person actually does.

Listening 1 · The Person Behind the Curtain

The Go-To Person Nobody Planned For

Eleven years behind the curtain, and one Thursday that changed how she works. Sound first — no text.

1. Who was the go-to person on her show, and what was his official job?
Marcos, a sound assistant. He was not on the call sheet as anything special.
2. Name two things that Marcos knew.
Which microphone had a weak cable, which singer needed water before every take, and which door to the stage got stuck in the rain.
3. What happened when he was sick, and what did the team do the next week?
Somebody else stepped in and the show still happened, but everybody discovered how much depended on one person. The next week they wrote down everything he knew, and it took four pages.
Listening 2 · One File, One Person

Before the Rule, People Guessed. After It, They Asked.

A music director on the part of the job that was harder than the music. Sound first — no text.

1. What did he think the hard part would be, and what was it really?
He thought it would be the music. It was really the handoffs, the places where work passes from one team to another.
2. Name two of the handoffs he describes.
The arrangement that leaves his desk on Monday and arrives at the band on Wednesday, the click track that goes to the video team, and the lyric sheet that goes to the captions.
3. What was the rule, and what did it teach him about delegating?
Every file has a point of contact who answers questions about it and keeps the others in the loop. It taught him to delegate properly, because he finally knew who was doing what, not only what needed to be done.
