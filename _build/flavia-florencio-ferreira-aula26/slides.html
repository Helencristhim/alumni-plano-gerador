Chapter 4: The Partnership Code

Offer, Expect, Benefit

This partnership would allow us to... · In return, we would expect...

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"What we are offering is access to producers who have a geographic indication."

"In return, we would expect your expertise in export rules."

"This partnership would allow us to test the idea with low risk."

"One of the key benefits is that our producers can reach new markets."

What do the orange words do? Which one says what we give, which one says what we want back, and which ones say why the partnership is good?

FunctionPhraseWhat comes next
What you giveWhat we are offering is...a noun: access to producers
What you want backIn return, we would expect...a noun: your expertise
The resultThis partnership would allow us to...to + verb: to test the idea
A benefitOne of the key benefits is that...a full sentence: our producers can...
A future doorThis agreement could open doors to...a noun: new fairs in Italy

Add details with who (people) or that / which (things): "producers who have a GI". Add a condition with if: "If we sign in May, the pilot will start in July."

Dialogue

The Partnership Talk

G
Flavia, thank you for the draft. What exactly are you offering?
F
What we are offering is access to producers who have a geographic indication.
G
And what would you expect from us in return?
F
In return, we would expect your expertise in export rules, which is something our producers need.
G
That sounds fair. Is there a clause about the pilot?
F
Yes. If we sign in May, the pilot will start in July. This partnership would allow us to test the idea with low risk.
G
Good. And what are our obligations?
F
Your team sends two experts who can visit the farms, and we organize the visits. It is a win-win situation.
