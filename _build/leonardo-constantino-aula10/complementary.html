Documentary
Skandal! Bringing Down Wirecard

Journalists, short sellers and regulators explain how a German payments company reported cash that never existed, and how everybody around it kept recommending, deciding and refusing to act. Connection to Lesson 10: the whole film is people saying what they suggested doing, what the board decided to do, and what nobody managed to stop in time.

Tip: watch with English subtitles and write down five sentences where one verb is followed by another. Mark whether the second verb is an -ing form or an infinitive with to, then rewrite two of them about a live file of yours.

Planet Money -- "My Favorite Tax Loophole"

The NPR team walks through the loopholes they find most interesting in the tax code and where the line sits between avoiding a charge and evading one. Connection to Lesson 10: your own subject matter, argued at native speed, and full of verbs that decide who has to act -- avoid, consider, manage, fail, decide.

Tip: the full transcript is on the same page. Listen once without it, then read it and highlight every verb followed by an -ing form or by to. Choose three and write your own version about a client structure.

British Council -- "Verbs followed by other verbs (infinitives and gerunds)"

A short lesson on exactly the pattern you discovered in class: which verbs take the infinitive with to, which take the -ing form, and which take either one with a change of meaning. Connection to Lesson 10: a second explanation in a different voice, right before you use the pattern in a client memo.
