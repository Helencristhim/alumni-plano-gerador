Lesson 10 -- Pre-class

Verbs That Commit -- Gerunds and Infinitives in Legal Recommendations, Risk Communication and Professional Decision-Making

Writing advice that says who has to act. Key words: restructuring, exposure ceiling, tax optimization, legal standing, interpose, consolidate, defend a position, challenge a ruling, file for an appeal. Expressions: consider restructuring, avoid triggering, manage to secure, at stake, make the case for, opt out of. Structures: gerunds and infinitives -- verbs that always take -ing, verbs that always take to, verbs that take both, and the pairs where the form changes the fact.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Restructuring -- the process of changing the legal or financial shape of a group
"The board has agreed to fund the restructuring this year."
Exposure ceiling -- the highest level of risk a board agrees to carry
"This route keeps the group inside the exposure ceiling agreed in March."
Tax optimization -- arranging a structure lawfully so that the tax cost is as low as it can be
"The second route offers better tax optimization on paper."
Legal standing -- the right to bring or defend a case before a court or an authority
"Without legal standing, the group cannot challenge the assessment at all."
Interpose -- to place a new entity between two others in a corporate structure
"We suggest interposing a holding entity in a treaty jurisdiction."
Consolidate -- to bring separate entities or results together into one
"We recommend consolidating the three insurance vehicles into the new holding."
Defend a position -- to argue and support a tax or legal view when somebody attacks it
"No client should try defending a position it cannot document."
Challenge a ruling -- to formally dispute an official decision of the authority
"The client has decided to challenge the ruling before the filing deadline."
File for an appeal -- to submit a formal request for a decision to be reviewed
"The only remaining route would be to file for an appeal after the assessment."
Consider restructuring -- to think seriously about changing the shape of a group
"The board should consider restructuring before the end of the quarter."
Avoid triggering -- to keep something from starting a tax obligation
"This route avoids triggering the new withholding obligation."
Manage to secure -- to succeed in obtaining something that was difficult
"We managed to secure an extension two days before the deadline."
At stake -- what will be lost if a decision goes the wrong way
"What is at stake here is not the rate, it is eighteen months of delay."
Make the case for -- to give the reasons why something should be done
"I can make the case for the treaty route in three minutes."
Opt out of -- to formally leave a regime or a scheme you were part of
"If the group opts out of the regime, it cannot come back for five years."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Vantara Seguros wants an answer by Friday, and the file has three possible routes. The board has already decided to move the reinsurance arm out of the current holding, and it has agreed to fund the operation this year, so nobody is reopening that. What is still open is how. Leonardo's team recommends interposing a holding entity in a treaty jurisdiction and consolidating the three insurance vehicles into it. That route avoids triggering the new withholding obligation and keeps the group inside the exposure ceiling the board approved in March. The second route promises better tax optimization, but the group would risk losing its legal standing if the structure were ever challenged, and no client should try defending a position it cannot document. There is one more problem. Somebody in finance keeps pushing to opt out of the special regime this year. If the group opts out first, it will not manage to secure the ruling in time, and the only route left would be to file for an appeal after the assessment. What is at stake is not the rate: it is eighteen months. Leonardo writes one last line to the tax director. Please remember to confirm that the group stopped filing under the old regime in June, because the December return cannot be amended twice.

1. "Leonardo's team recommends interposing a holding entity." Why "interposing" and not "to interpose"?
A Because the action has not happened yet.
B Because recommend is one of the verbs that is always followed by the -ing form.
C Because there are two verbs after it in the same sentence.
2. "The board has decided to move the reinsurance arm." Why "to move" and not "moving"?
A Because decide belongs to the group of verbs that are always followed by the infinitive with to.
B Because the sentence is in the present perfect.
C Because the subject is a group of people and not one person.
3. "The group stopped filing under the old regime in June." What does this tell us?
A The group paused something else in order to file in June.
B The filing ended in June: the group does not file under that regime any more.
C The group has not started filing under that regime yet.
4. Why does the desk advise against opting out of the regime first?
A Because the rate would be higher for the reinsurance arm.
B Because the board approved the exposure ceiling only in March.
C Because the group would not secure the ruling in time and would lose eighteen months.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Gerunds and Infinitives

GRAMMAR

When one verb is followed by another, English does not let you choose. The first verb decides the form of the second one, and the decision belongs to the verb, not to the sentence. Four groups cover almost everything you write in a memo, and one of them changes the meaning, not only the shape.

GroupVerbsExample
Always + -ingavoid, consider, recommend, suggest, risk, involve, deny"We recommend restructuring the entity." / "They risk losing the benefit."
Always + toagree, decide, offer, refuse, manage, tend, afford, fail"The client decided to challenge the ruling." / "We managed to secure an extension."
Both, same meaningbegin, start, continue, prefer"They began filing" = "They began to file".
rememberremember doing = you have the memory of a past act ("He remembers signing the return"). remember to do = do not forget it, it is still ahead ("He must remember to sign the return").
stopstop doing = the action ends ("The group stopped filing under the old regime"). stop to do = you interrupt one thing in order to do another ("On his way out he stopped to file the appeal").
trytry doing = an experiment, to see what happens ("Try consolidating the vehicles first"). try to do = an effort that may fail ("Try to consolidate them before December").
The mistake that costs yourecommend and suggest never take a person followed by to. Not "we recommend the client to restructure". Write "we recommend restructuring", or "we recommend that the client restructure". Advise is the verb that does take a person: "we advise the client to restructure". Same idea, and the verb decides who has to act.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each real conversation.

You are writing the recommendation paragraph of a memo to a foreign bank. You write:
A "We recommend the client to restructure the holding before December."
B "We recommend restructuring the holding before December."
C "We recommend to restructure the holding before December."
You need to name the person who has to act, in the same memo. The correct sentence is:
A "We advise the board to confirm the exposure ceiling in writing."
B "We suggest the board to confirm the exposure ceiling in writing."
C "We recommend the board confirming the exposure ceiling in writing."
The tax director has not signed the return, and the deadline is on Friday. You write:
A "Please remember signing the return before Friday."
B "Please remember to sign the return before Friday."
C "Please remember that you sign the return before Friday."
A partner asks whether the group still files under the old regime. You answer:
A "No, they stopped to file under that regime in June."
B "No, they stopped filing under that regime in June."
C "No, they stopped for filing under that regime in June."
