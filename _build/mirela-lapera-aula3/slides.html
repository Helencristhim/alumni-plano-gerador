Chapter 3: The Code

If I Lived... I Would...

How English talks about a dream

Grammar Discovery

Is This Real or Imagined?

"If I lived in New York, I would rent a studio in Brooklyn."

"If I had more time, I’d go to a concert every week."

"Would you live there if you could?"

"If I were you, I’d visit in the fall."

Does Mirela live in New York now? No. Does she have more time? No. The red part is in the past form, but it talks about NOW: an imagined present. The blue part has would. And look at sentence 4: if I were, not if I was.

JobFormExample
The imagined situationIf + simple pastIf I lived in New York...
The imagined resultwould / ’d + verb...I would rent a studio.
The questionWould you + verb ... if ...?Would you live there if you could?
AdviceIf I were you, I’d + verbIf I were you, I’d visit in the fall.
Orderthe if part can come first or secondI’d be happy if I lived there.
The trapnever would in the if partNot If I would live there. Say If I lived there.

In one line: if + past for the dream, would for the result: if I lived there, I would...

Chapter 4: Real Speed

New York at Real Speed

One video call, three recordings, one apartment

Dialogue

A Call to Brooklyn

M
Hi, Mirela! So, you’re thinking about living in New York one day?
M
Well, it’s a dream for now. But if I lived there, I’d want a quiet neighborhood close to the city.
M
Then I’d look at Brooklyn. It’s lively, but some streets are really calm.
M
And how much is the rent there? Could I afford a two-bedroom?
M
A two-bedroom is about four thousand a month. A studio apartment is cheaper, around twenty-eight hundred.
M
Wow. The cost of living is really high. What about the commute to Manhattan?
M
By subway, twenty-five minutes. Honestly, if I were you, I’d come for a month first and see how it feels.
M
That’s a good idea. If I did that, I’d know if I could really settle in.
Comprehension

About Mike

1. Which part of the city does Mike suggest, and why?
Brooklyn, because it is lively but some streets are calm.
2. How much is a studio apartment, according to Mike?
About twenty-eight hundred dollars a month.
3. What is Mike’s advice?
To come for a month first and see how it feels.
Listening 1 · A New Yorker

The Real Price of New York

A man who was born in New York talks about the cost of living in the city. Sound first — no text.

1. How much of his salary goes to the rent?
Almost half of his salary.
2. Why does he not have a car?
Parking is very expensive and the subway goes everywhere.
3. Why does he stay in New York?
Because there is always something to do, and he would miss the energy of the city.
Listening 2 · A Londoner Abroad

My First Year in Manhattan

A British woman remembers her first year after she moved to New York for work. Sound first — no text.

1. How did she feel in the first months?
She was homesick: she missed her family and the quiet streets of her town.
2. What helped her to settle in?
She joined a running group in her neighborhood and made friends there.
3. What is her advice for people who want to move?
Find one activity you love and do it in the new city from the first week.
Listening 3 · A Commuter

An Hour Each Way

A man who lives outside the city explains his daily commute and how he uses the time. Sound first — no text.

1. Where does he live, and how long is his commute?
In New Jersey; about an hour each way.
2. Why does he not live in Manhattan?
He could not afford a big apartment there for his family.
3. How does he use the time on the train?
He reads, listens to podcasts and sometimes he just rests.
