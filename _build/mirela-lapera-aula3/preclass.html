Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

If I Lived in New York -- If I Lived..., I Would...

Imagining a life in New York and talking about rent, neighborhoods and the cost of living. Key words: a neighborhood, the cost of living, to afford, the rent, a commute, lively, to settle in, homesick, a studio apartment, a borough. Structure: the second conditional.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A neighborhood -- a part of a city where people live, with its own character
"Brooklyn has very different neighborhoods."
The cost of living -- how much money you need for rent, food and transport in a place
"The cost of living in New York is very high."
To afford -- to have enough money to pay for something
"I couldn’t afford an apartment in Manhattan."
The rent -- the money you pay every month to live in a place that is not yours
"The rent is three thousand dollars a month."
A commute -- the trip you make every day between your home and your work
"My commute to São Paulo takes more than an hour."
Lively -- full of people, energy and things happening
"The neighborhood is lively at night."
To settle in -- to start to feel comfortable in a new home or a new place
"It took me a few months to settle in."
Homesick -- sad because you are far from your home and your family
"I was homesick during my first winter abroad."
A studio apartment -- a small apartment with one main room for living and sleeping
"She rents a studio apartment near the park."
A borough -- one of the five main parts of New York City
"Brooklyn and Queens are two of the five boroughs."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Mirela has been to New York many times, and she always says the same thing: “If I lived here, I would be happy.” But what would that life look like? She would probably choose a calm neighborhood in Brooklyn, one of the five boroughs, close to a park.

The problem is money. The cost of living is very high, and the rent for a studio apartment is almost three thousand dollars. If she had a job there, maybe she could afford it. Her commute would be short: twenty-five minutes by subway.

“Would you be homesick?” a friend asks. “Maybe at first,” she answers. “But the city is so lively that I think I would settle in fast.”

1. Does Mirela live in New York?
A Yes, she moved there last year and she lives in a studio apartment in Brooklyn.
B No. The text imagines what her life would be like if she lived there.
C Yes, but only during the summer vacation.
2. Why does the text say “If she had a job there” and not “If she has a job there”?
A Because it is an imagined situation in the present, not a real plan.
B Because the job was in the past, many years ago, before she went to New York for the first time.
C Because had is more polite than has.
3. What does she think about being homesick?
A She would never be homesick, because she does not like her city very much.
B She would be homesick for many years.
C Maybe at first, but she would settle in fast.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Second Conditional

GRAMMAR

To talk about an imagined situation in the present, use if + simple past, and would + verb for the result. For advice, use if I were you, I’d...

JobFormExample
The imagined situationIf + simple pastIf I lived in New York...
The imagined resultwould / ’d + verb...I would rent a studio.
The questionWould you + verb ... if ...?Would you live there if you could?
AdviceIf I were you, I’d + verbIf I were you, I’d visit in the fall.
Orderthe if part can come first or secondI’d be happy if I lived there.
The trapnever would in the if partNot If I would live there. Say If I lived there.

Never: If I would live there · If I had time, I will go · If I was you (for advice) · Would you go if you can?

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A friend asks where you would live if you could choose. You say:
A "If I would choose, I would live in New York, near a big park."
B "If I could choose, I would live in New York."
C "If I can choose, I live in New York."
Your cousin wants advice about the best season to visit. You say:
A "If I am you, I visit in the fall."
B "If I were you, I’d visit in the fall."
C "If I was you, I will visit in the fall, because it is the best season of the year."
You think an apartment is too expensive for you. You say:
A "I can’t afford it."
B "I can’t commute it, because the rent is much higher than my salary every month."
C "I can’t settle in it."
You describe a street with music, people and restaurants. You say:
A "It’s a very homesick street, especially on Friday and Saturday nights."
B "It’s a very lively street."
C "It’s a very borough street."
