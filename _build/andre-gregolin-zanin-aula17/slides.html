Chapter 3: Which Way the Arrow Points

Cause First, or Effect First?

The same story, told from both ends

Grammar Discovery

Where Does the Arrow Start?

"Two years without practice led to a big drop in my fluency."

"The drop in my fluency resulted from two years without practice."

"I stopped my classes because of the training schedule."

"The schedule caused me to stop, which in turn made me lose my rhythm."

Same story, four sentences. Which ones start with the cause? Which one starts with the effect? What comes after because of: a noun or a full clause?

DirectionFormExample
Cause → effectCAUSE + lead to / result in / bring about / cause + EFFECTThe move led to a new routine.
Effect ← causeEFFECT + result from / stem from / be caused by / be due to + CAUSEThe new routine resulted from the move.
Cause + personcause / make + PERSON + verbIt caused me to stop. · It made me stop.
+ nounbecause of / due to / owing to + NOUNbecause of the schedule
+ clausebecause / since / as + SUBJECT + VERBbecause I moved
Chain..., which led to... · ..., which in turn... · As a result, ...I slept less, which in turn hurt my focus.
Notno “caused that” · no “due to” + clause · affect = verb, effect = nounNot It caused that I stopped · Not due to I moved

Draw the arrow before you speak. Lead to and result in point forward. Result from and stem from point back.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Where the Rust Came From

One conversation, two recordings, one study log

Dialogue

So, What Happened?

H
Can I ask you something? You told me once that your English used to be better. What happened?
A
It was a chain, really. At sixteen I moved to Franca to play basketball, and the move led to a completely new routine, with training twice a day.
H
And English didn't fit in that routine.
A
Exactly. I stopped my classes because of the schedule, and that had a knock-on effect I didn't see coming. With no classes, I stopped watching series in English, too.
H
That happened to me with Spanish. I studied it for six years back in Chicago, then I got my first job and stopped. Now I can read a menu, and that's about it.
A
So you know the vicious circle. I speak less because I feel rusty, and I feel rusty because I speak less. Honestly, most of my frustration stems from that.
H
But you're breaking it now. Twice a week, every week. What made you come back?
A
The cursinho, actually. I had to read texts in English for the exams, and that brought about a small surprise: I still understood a lot. That, in turn, made me want the rest back.
Comprehension

About Hannah

1. How long did Hannah study Spanish, and where?
For six years, back in Chicago.
2. Why did Hannah stop studying Spanish?
She got her first job.
3. What can Hannah still do in Spanish today?
Read a menu, and that is about it.
Listening 1 · A City Planner

More Lanes, More Traffic

A planner on why the obvious solution to traffic often backfires. Sound first — no text.

1. What does a new lane do in the first few months?
It makes driving faster.
2. What do some people who used to take the bus do?
They go back to their cars.
3. According to the planner, what was the root cause of the traffic?
The number of people with no other good way to get to work, not the size of the road.
Listening 2 · A Debate Coach

Every Link Has to Hold

A coach on the three mistakes students make when they explain causes. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the coach tell students to do before they speak?
Draw the arrow in their head: check the direction.
2. What follows because of and due to?
A noun, for example: because of the move.
3. Why should you say “one factor” or “the main factor”?
Because real life is rarely that simple, and the listener will trust you more.
