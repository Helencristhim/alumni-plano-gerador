Lesson 15 -- Telling the Story of a Case

Talk
"The clues to a great story" -- Andrew Stanton, TED

Nineteen minutes from the writer of Toy Story and WALL-E on what makes people keep listening: start with a promise that the story is going somewhere, and make the listener work to put it together. Connection to Lesson 15: it is the reason you start a case at the turning point instead of at the first date.

Tip: every time he tells a story inside the talk, notice where he starts it. Then retell your own case from the lesson the same way, out loud, in under a minute.

Podcast
"Love Wins" -- Jim Obergefell, The Moth

Twenty minutes, told live and without notes, by the plaintiff of the Supreme Court case on marriage equality: how the case began, what was happening in his life when it did, and the morning the decision came. Connection to Lesson 15: it is a real case told as a story, and the narrative tenses carry the whole thing.

Tip: listen once for the story. On the second listen, stop every time he jumps back in time and say which tense took him there. You will find more past perfect than you expect.

Reading
"Telling a story: narrative tenses" -- British Council English Online

A short lesson on the four tenses of a story -- past simple, past continuous, past perfect and past perfect continuous -- with a model story and the job each tense does in it. Connection to Lesson 15: it is the grammar tip of the lesson with a second worked example.

Tip: rewrite the model story so that it starts at its most interesting moment. If the tenses still make the order clear, you have understood the lesson.
