FIFA Program Overview -- The Road to Zurich

Get ready for Block 3: look at the whole FIFA program, from the orientation to the certificate, and talk about what will be finished at a point in the future with the future perfect (will have + past participle). Key words: module, cohort, itinerary, orientation, mentor, assignment, workload, peer, certificate, graduate.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Module -- a separate part of a course or a program
"The first module of the program is in Miami."
Cohort -- a group of people who start and finish a program together
"Our cohort has participants from more than twenty countries."
Itinerary -- a detailed plan of a trip, with places and times
"The itinerary for Miami arrived by email this morning."
Orientation -- a first session that explains how a program works
"The orientation call explains the rules of the program."
Mentor -- an experienced person who gives advice to someone with less experience
"My mentor was a participant in the program three years ago."
Assignment -- a piece of work that you must do as part of a course
"We have one written assignment between each module."
Workload -- the amount of work that a person has to do
"The workload is heavy, so I plan two hours a week for the program."
Peer -- a person at the same level as you, or in the same group
"I learn a lot from my peers in other federations."
Certificate -- an official document that shows you completed a course
"Every participant receives a certificate at the end of the program."
Graduate -- to finish a course or a program successfully
"By the time we graduate, we will have visited three continents."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Block 3 of Nilo's English program starts with the big picture: the FIFA Leadership in Football program. After the orientation call on Monday, Nilo joins a cohort of participants from many countries. The program has three modules: Miami, Rabat, and Zurich. His mentor, Daniel, is a former participant. By the end of the Miami module, Nilo will have met all his peers. By the time he arrives in Rabat, he will have finished his first assignment. The workload is about two hours a week, so he won't have finished the readings in one weekend. If everything goes well, he will graduate in Zurich. By then, he will have visited three continents, and he will take his certificate home to Brazil.

1. What will Nilo have done by the end of the Miami module?
A "He will have met all his peers in the cohort."
B "He will have finished all three modules and received his certificate in Miami."
C "He will have graduated."
2. Why won't Nilo finish the readings in one weekend?
A Because the orientation call on Monday is very long and he has to prepare a big presentation for it.
B Because the workload is about two hours a week.
C Because he has no mentor.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "By the time he will arrive in Rabat, he will have finished his first assignment for the program."
B "By Rabat, he will has finish it."
C "By the time he arrives in Rabat, he will have finished his first assignment."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Future Perfect

GRAMMAR

How to talk about something that will be finished before a point in the future.

FormStructureExample
Affirmativewill have + past participleBy May, I will have finished the module.
Negativewon't have + past participleI won't have read it by Friday.
QuestionWill + subject + have + past participle?Will you have sent it by Monday?
Time wordsby + time / by the time + present simpleBy the time we arrive, we will have met the cohort.
RuleThe action is finished before the point in the future. "By" means "not later than". After "by the time", use the present simple, not "will". Irregular participles: meet - met, write - written, read - read, send - sent.
