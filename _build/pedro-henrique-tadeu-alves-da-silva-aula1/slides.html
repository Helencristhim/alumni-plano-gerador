Chapter 3: The Code

What You Do, and What You Are Doing

Two jobs, two forms, one decision

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"I work on music shows."

"My colleague mixes the audio for the live shows."

"Right now I am building the backing tracks."

"This week we are rehearsing every afternoon."

Two of the red verbs describe the shape of the job. Two describe something you are in the middle of. Look at the blue part: it appears on one side only, and that is the clue.

JobFormExample
True every seasonthe base verbI work on music shows. We book the studio in advance.
True every season, he / she / itbase verb + -sMy colleague mixes the audio. The singer performs over a backing track.
In the middle of it nowam / is / are + verb-ingRight now I am building the backing tracks. This week we are rehearsing.
Negative, every seasondo not / does not + base verbI do not work with the camera team.
Negative, right nowam not / is not / are not + verb-ingI am not mixing this week.
QuestionDo / Does ... ? · Am / Is / Are ... ?Do you mix your own tracks? · What are you working on at the moment?
The trapstate verbs stay in the base formNot I am knowing that song. Say I know that song.

In one line: the base form says what the job is, the -ing form says what the week is.

Dialogue

Ten Minutes Before Anyone Talks Money

N
Nina Whitfield. I am a music supervisor, based in London. This is my first time working with a Brazilian team, so tell me what you actually do, and please skip the job title.
P
Pedro. I work between audiovisual production and music production, on music shows. The title is useless, so I usually say I make sure the music and the pictures arrive at the same second.
N
That is the best answer I have had all month. So you are the person who finds out, at eleven at night, that the cue is four seconds too long.
P
That is exactly it. And right now I am building the backing tracks for a new season, so this week I am in rehearsals every afternoon with a notebook.
N
Good. Then you already know the question I care about. On your show, who decides when a key is wrong for a singer? The musical director, the artist, or you?
P
Officially the musical director. In practice it happens in the rehearsal, and whoever hears it first says it. Half of the crew has been on this show since the first season, so nobody waits for permission.
N
I spent eleven years as a session singer before I moved to this side of the glass, so I know that room very well. The best show I worked on was the one where the runner was allowed to say something.
P
Then you understand why I keep the third take and not the first. The first one is always the one where everybody is being polite.
N
Say that again in the meeting on Thursday and you will have the room. One last thing: do you do anything outside the show? I ask everybody, and the answer is usually the interesting one.
P
On the side, I produce my own projects, and I am studying songwriting. And you, what are you working on at the moment?
Comprehension

About Nina

1. Who is Nina, and what is new for her here?
She is a music supervisor, based in London, and it is her first time working with a Brazilian team.
2. Nina does not repeat his job title. How does she describe his job in her own words?
She says he is the person who finds out, at eleven at night, that the cue is four seconds too long.
3. What did Nina do before this job, and what does she say made the best show she worked on?
She spent eleven years as a session singer. She says the best show was the one where the runner was allowed to say something.
Listening 1 · The Course Welcome

Every Week, You Bring Something

A welcome message recorded before the first class of an online songwriting course. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she ask the students to bring every week, and what happens to it before the live session?
Something of their own: eight bars, a single verse, or a rough voice memo recorded on a phone. They post it, and the rest of the group listens to it before the class meets.
2. What exactly are the students asked to do in the live session, and why does she say it frightens people?
They have to say what the song is trying to do, in their own words, out loud, in front of eleven strangers.
3. She says nobody is grading one thing, and that she is listening for another. What are they?
Nobody is grading the accent. She is listening for whether the student can say why they made a choice. She adds that the person who says their English is not good enough usually turns out to have the most interesting reason.
Listening 2 · The Same Job, in English

Two Departments, One Second

A producer on a podcast, answering the question he has never answered cleanly. Sound first — no text.

1. He says he sits between two sides. What is on each side?
On one side the picture: the cameras, the edit, and the show going out on a Sunday night. On the other side the music: the band, the arrangements, and the backing tracks the singers perform over.
2. He names three things he is doing at the same time this week. What are they?
He is building the backing tracks for the live shows, he is sitting in rehearsals with a notebook, and he is on the phone with the picture team about a cue that runs four seconds long.
3. Why does he sit in the rehearsals, and what does he say at a party instead of all this?
Because the rehearsal is where you find out that a key is wrong for somebody. At a party he says he makes sure the music and the pictures arrive at the same second.

The Sheet Everyone Reads The Night Before

Note for visitors: the cue for song 7 still runs four seconds long. Music and picture will agree a cut in the rehearsal, not in the edit. Nothing is locked until the third take.
1. Two lines on this sheet talk about time in opposite ways. Which are they, and what is the difference?
"Every show: backing tracks, cues, music and picture sync" is the shape of the job, true every time. "Today: building tracks for songs 3 and 7, sitting in rehearsal" is only true now.
2. The note says the cut will be agreed in the rehearsal and not in the edit. Why would a music department prefer that?
Because in the rehearsal the band and the singer are in the room, so a four-second problem can be solved by playing it differently. In the edit, the only option left is to cut the picture.
3. According to the sheet, what happens to the shortlist, and when?
It has forty names, and the final twelve are announced on Friday.
