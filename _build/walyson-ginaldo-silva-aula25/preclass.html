Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Futures market -- where coffee is bought and sold for a future date, at a price agreed today
"Arabica is traded on the futures market in New York."
Differential -- the amount added to or taken from the futures price, for quality and origin
"This lot has a differential of twenty-five cents."
Spot price -- the price for coffee that is delivered now
"The spot price went up after the frost."
Hedge -- to protect yourself against a big change in price
"Roasters hedge part of their coffee for next year."
Broker -- a person or company that connects buyers and sellers
"A broker in London found us a new buyer."
Fix the price -- to decide the final price of a contract
"The buyer wants to fix the price before February."
Green coffee -- coffee beans that are not roasted yet
"We export green coffee, and the roasters roast it in their countries."
Lot -- a group of bags of the same coffee, sold together
"The buyer chose the lot from the Cerrado farm."
Cupping -- a professional tasting that gives a coffee a score
"The cupping is at ten, in the lab on the second floor."
Defect -- a problem in a bean, like a black or broken bean
"The sample had too many defects, so the score was low."
Moisture content -- the amount of water in the beans
"The moisture content must be below twelve percent."
Specialty coffee -- high-quality coffee with a score of eighty or more
"Specialty coffee gets a much better price."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A new trainee asked Walyson to explain his job. He said: "A broker is a person who connects buyers and sellers. Cupping is a tasting which gives a coffee a score. The differential is the amount that we add to the futures price. And the lab is the room where we do the cupping."

The words in bold start a defining relative clause: it tells you exactly which person, thing or place we mean. Use who for people, which or that for things, and where for places. There is no comma before the clause.

Brazilians often use what after a noun: "the coffee what we sell". The correct word is that or which. And do not repeat the object: say "the lot that we bought", not "the lot that we bought it".

1. Which sentence is correct?
A A broker is a person which connects buyers and sellers in the coffee market.
B A broker is a person who connects buyers and sellers.
C A broker is a person what connects buyers.
2. Which word do we use for a place?
A where
B which, because a place is a thing and not a person
C who
3. What is wrong with "This is the lot that we bought it"?
A It needs who, because the lot is very important for the company and for the buyer.
B It needs a comma before that.
C The word it repeats the object, so we remove it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Defining Relative Clauses

GRAMMAR

Say exactly which person, thing or place you mean: who, which, that, where.

ForStructureExample
Peoplenoun + who (or that) + verba buyer who pays on time
Thingsnoun + which / that + verba lot that has no defects
Placesnoun + where + subject + verbthe port where our coffee leaves Brazil
Objectnoun + that + subject + verb (no it)the coffee that we sold
QuestionWhich + noun + ...?Which lot did the buyer choose?

Remember: never use what after a noun. Say the price that we agreed, not the price what we agreed.
