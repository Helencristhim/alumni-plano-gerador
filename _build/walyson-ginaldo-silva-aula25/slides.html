Chapter 3: The Code

A Person Who, a Price That

Defining relative clauses: how to explain exactly what a word means

Grammar Discovery

Explain Exactly

"A broker is a person who connects buyers and sellers."

"The differential is the amount that we add to the futures price."

"Cupping is a tasting which checks the quality of a coffee."

"Santos is the port where most Brazilian coffee leaves the country."

Look at the blue words. Which one is for a person? A thing? A place?

ForUseExample
Peoplewho (or that)a buyer who pays on time
Thingswhich or thata lot that has no defects
Placeswherethe lab where we do the cupping
No commathe clause says WHICH onethe coffee that we sold to Tokyo
Don’t repeatwho / which / that replaces it, him, herthe lot that we bought (not bought it)

In one line: who for people, which or that for things, where for places -- and never what in the middle of a sentence.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

How Does the Price Work?

One conversation, two recordings, one contract

Dialogue

Ms. Sato Asks About the Price

S
Walyson, thank you for the farm visit. Can I ask you about prices? This system is new for me.
W
Of course. Most arabica is priced on the futures market, which is in New York.
S
So the price that I see on the screen is the final price?
W
Not exactly. That's the base. Then we add a differential, which depends on the quality and the origin.
S
I see. And who decides the differential?
W
We negotiate it with the buyer. Sometimes a broker, who connects buyers and sellers, helps us.
S
And how do you check the quality of a lot?
W
First we look at the green coffee for defects and check the moisture content. Then we do a cupping.
S
That's the tasting that we did this morning, isn't it? I loved the coffee from the Cerrado.
W
Exactly. That lot scored eighty-four, so it's specialty coffee. When you're ready, we can fix the price.
Comprehension

About Ms. Sato

1. Why does Ms. Sato ask about prices?
Because the pricing system is new for her.
2. What did she think about the price on the screen?
She thought it was the final price.
3. What did she do this morning, and which coffee did she like?
She did a cupping, and she loved the coffee from the Cerrado.
Listening 1 · A Market Analyst

The Price of a Cup

A coffee market analyst explains how prices work. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the base price for arabica coffee around the world?
The futures price in New York.
2. What is the spot price, according to her?
The price for coffee that you can deliver now.
3. Why do companies hedge?
Because prices can change very fast -- for example after a frost in Brazil -- and a hedge protects their budget.
Listening 2 · A Coffee Taster

A Day in the Lab

A coffee taster in Santos describes his working day. Sound first — no text.

1. How long has he been working as a coffee taster?
For twelve years.
2. Why does he check the moisture content?
Because coffee that is too wet can get mold during the trip.
3. Which lots are specialty coffee?
The lots that get eighty points or more.
Real Document

The Sales Confirmation

Seller: Sucafina Brazil · Santos office · Trader: Walyson Ginaldo Silva
Buyer: Sato Coffee Roasters · Tokyo
Sales Confirmation · Lot SAN-2417

Coffee: Brazil Cerrado, green coffee, specialty grade

Quantity: 120 bags of 60 kg

Cupping score: 84 points

Price: New York futures (March) + 25 cents per pound differential

Price fixing: by the buyer, before February 20

Quality limits: moisture content max. 12% · max. 8 defects per 300 g

Document written for this lesson. The buyer and the lot are fictional.
1. Which lot is this?
It is the lot that scored eighty-four points, from the Cerrado.
2. How is the price calculated?
It is the New York futures price for March plus a differential of twenty-five cents per pound.
3. Who fixes the price, and when?
The buyer, who must fix it before February 20.
