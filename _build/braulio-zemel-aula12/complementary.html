Twenty minutes from the economist who put inequality back on the front page, laying out one formula and the data behind it. Connection to Lesson 12: it is a whole argument about money in the third person, with not one sentence about the speaker's own finances. Tip: watch once with English subtitles and once without. Then say out loud, in two sentences, where you agree and where you would push back -- using the concession move from Lesson 8.

Podcast
"What's a bubble? (Nobel Edition)" -- Planet Money, NPR

Twenty minutes of American English with two Nobel economists who disagree about whether bubbles exist at all. Connection to Lesson 12: it is today's vocabulary in its natural habitat, and it is also a masterclass in disagreeing without anybody losing the room. The full transcript sits under the player.

Tip: listen once without the text and write down every time one of them concedes a point before pushing back. Then decide which of the two you agree with, and say why in three sentences.

Grammar Video
"Prefer, would prefer, or would rather?" -- Ask BBC Learning English

A short, direct answer on the form you used most today, and on the two structures that get confused with it. Connection to Lesson 12: it is the first line of your survival card, and the video makes very clear where the to goes and where it does not.

Tip: pause after each example and say the same preference three ways -- with prefer, with would prefer and with would rather. Only the last one takes a bare verb, and your ear needs to hear that.
