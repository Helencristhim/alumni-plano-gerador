Podcast
NPR Shots -- "One doctor takes the fight for health literacy to the street"

A physician who left the clinic to explain medicine on the pavement, in the words people actually use. The whole story is about the gap this lesson closes: what the doctor said, and what the patient walked out understanding. Clear American English, audio and full transcript on the same page.

Tip: stop every time he defines something. Write down the sentence and mark the relative clause. You will find one in almost all of them.

Audiobook
Atul Gawande -- "Being Mortal" (audiobook preview)

The book behind the interview, read by the author. The opening chapter is one long exercise in saying a complicated thing in plain words, and it is the closest written model there is to the ninety seconds you practised today.

Tip: after the preview, take one paragraph and say it out loud in your own words, keeping every relative clause. Then say the same paragraph to somebody who is not a doctor.

Music
Simon & Garfunkel -- "Bridge Over Troubled Water"

Five minutes of the plainest reassurance in English, and a lyric that runs on relative clauses and on the structure this lesson trains: when you're weary, when tears are in your eyes, I will comfort you. Slow enough to hear every word, and every word is a common one.

Tip: read the lyrics and circle the clauses that define or explain. Then rewrite two of your own clinical sentences with the same shape: plain word first, clause after.
