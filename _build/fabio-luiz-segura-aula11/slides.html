Chapter 3: What the Verb Decides

Two Verbs, One Sentence

And the first one decides what the second one looks like

Grammar Discovery

Why -ing Here and to There?

"We decided to shortlist three of the forty."

"I recommend starting with one corridor."

"They agreed to send the reference client this week."

"We postponed making a decision until the next budget cycle."

Two of the red verbs end in -ing and two start with to. The meaning does not explain it, and neither does the tense. Look at the blue word in front of each one.

First verbWhat follows itExample
decide · agree · plan · offer · promise · refuse · manage · expect · hope · fail · arrangeto + verbWe decided to shortlist three vendors.
recommend · suggest · consider · avoid · postpone · involve · risk · keep · finish · mindverb + -ingI recommend starting with one corridor.
ask · want · advise · expect · allow — with a PERSONperson + to + verbShe asked me to write the business case.
after a preposition (to, in, about, for, of)always -ingI am looking forward to hearing from you · interested in running a pilot.
The meaning changesstop · rememberWe stopped funding it (we ended it) · We stopped to fund it (we paused in order to fund it).
The trapsuggest, recommend and avoid NEVER take toNot I suggest to start. Say I suggest starting.
The other trapin look forward to, that to is a prepositionNot I look forward to receive. Say to receiving.
Why it matters herea debrief is made almost entirely of these verbsdecided, agreed, recommend, postponed, considering — five sentences, five patterns.

In one line: the first verb decides. Learn the verb, not a rule about meaning, because there is no rule about meaning.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Eight Minutes with Your Director

One conversation, three recordings, and the memo that follows

Dialogue

The Debrief, Standing Up

R
You have eight minutes and I did not go. Tell me what came back with you.
F
Forty conversations. We narrowed them down to a shortlist of three, using one criterion: does it solve a gap we have already measured.
R
Three is a good number. What do you recommend doing with them?
F
I recommend starting with one corridor and one vendor. They agreed to send a reference client this week, and I have arranged to see their architecture team in November.
R
And what are you asking me for? Because I am not approving anything today.
F
Not money. Two things. I need you as the internal sponsor when this reaches the committee, and I need to know who the decision-maker is on the technology side, because I have three names and no order.
R
I will give you both. But I would avoid promising anything before we see the numbers. When does your business case exist?
F
Two deliverables, both before the budget cycle closes: the corridor numbers on the fifteenth, the vendor comparison a week later. The momentum is worth about three weeks, so I would rather move now than explain later.
Comprehension

About Renata

1. What does she say about her own time, and about the conference?
She gives him eight minutes, and she says she did not go to the conference herself.
2. What does she refuse to do today, and what does she agree to give him?
She refuses to approve anything today, and she agrees to be his internal sponsor and to tell him who the decision-maker is on the technology side.
3. What does she advise him to avoid, and until when?
She would avoid promising anything until they see the numbers, which is why she asks when the business case will exist.
Listening 1 · The Decay Curve

Three Weeks, and Then Nothing

Somebody who has done this for twenty years. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say happens to a conference project after about three weeks?
The momentum is gone. Nothing was refused and nobody said no, but the meeting is no longer in anybody's head, so the project quietly stops.
2. What does she recommend doing in the first week, and what does she recommend avoiding?
She recommends narrowing the list down to three and putting two dates in the calendar. She recommends avoiding a long report that nobody asked for.
3. What does she say is the real test of a conference?
Not how many people you met, but how many of them are still in your calendar in March.
Listening 2 · From the Other Side

How the Vendor Decides Who to Keep

A partnerships lead who met ninety people that week. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she do with her own list in the first days after the event?
She narrows ninety names down to about eight, and she does it in the first three days, before she forgets who was who.
2. What makes her keep a bank on the list, and what makes her drop one?
She keeps the ones who name a gap they have already measured and give her a date. She drops the ones who only say they are interested in exploring a partnership.
3. What does she say about people who apologise for being direct?
She says they should stop apologising, because being direct is what makes her able to plan her own quarter.
Listening 3 · How It Actually Gets Approved

The Room You Are Not In

A transformation lead on what happens to your business case. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say happens to your business case in the room you are not in?
Somebody else reads it out, badly, in about ninety seconds. So it has to survive being summarised by a person who does not care about it.
2. What are the three roles he says you have to identify, and why?
The sponsor, who carries it; the decision-maker, who signs; and the one stakeholder who can block it. He says people map the first two and forget the third.
3. What does he say about the budget cycle?
That the calendar beats the argument. A good case that arrives after the cycle closes waits a year, and by then the vendor has moved on.
Real Document

The Memo That Follows the Debrief

Post-conference debrief — three of forty
From: Fabio Luiz Segura · To: Renata · Cc: the trade finance team

What came back. Forty conversations. We narrowed them down to a shortlist of three, using one criterion: each one addresses a gap we have already measured.

What I recommend. I recommend starting with one corridor and one vendor. I would avoid running three pilots at once, because we cannot staff them and we would learn nothing from any of them.

What they agreed to do. They agreed to send a reference client this week. I have arranged to meet their architecture team in November.

What I postponed. We postponed making a commercial decision until the business case exists.

What I need from you. Not money. Your name as internal sponsor at the committee, and the order of the three technology stakeholders. Deliverables: corridor numbers on the fifteenth, vendor comparison a week later — both before the budget cycle closes.

Fabio

Illustrative memo, written for this lesson. Count the verb patterns in it.
1. Find the four verbs that decide the form of the verb after them, and say which form each one takes.
Recommend + starting, avoid + running, agreed + to send, postponed + making. Also arranged + to meet, which makes five.
2. Which sentence gives the reason for the recommendation, and what is the reason?
"I would avoid running three pilots at once, because we cannot staff them and we would learn nothing from any of them."
3. The memo asks for two things. What are they, and what do they cost?
Her name as internal sponsor at the committee, and the order of the three technology stakeholders. Both cost nothing, which is why they are easy to say yes to.
