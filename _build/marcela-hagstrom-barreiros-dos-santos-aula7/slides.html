Chapter 3: The Code

Going To, Will

Two futures, two jobs

Grammar Discovery

Two Futures, Two Jobs

"We’re going to launch the new page in November."

"First, I’m going to look at your data."

"Good question. I’ll send you the numbers today."

"Don’t worry. We won’t go over the budget."

Look at the blue words: going to is a plan, decided before the call. Now the red words: will (’ll) and won’t are decisions made right now, on the call, and promises to the client.

JobFormExample
Plan (decided before)am / is / are + going to + verbWe’re going to launch in November.
Decision made nowwill (’ll) + verbGood question. I’ll check and tell you.
Promisewill / won’t + verbWe won’t go over the budget.
QuestionAre you going to...? · Will you...?Are you going to use our data?
Negativenot going to · won’tWe’re not going to change the brand.
The trapno to after willNot I will to send. Say I’ll send.

In one line: going to for the plan you bring to the call; will for what you decide and promise on the call.

Chapter 4: The Proposal Call

The Proposal Call

One call, two voices, one proposal summary

Dialogue

The Proposal Call

R
Hi, Marcela. I read your diagnosis. It was very clear. So, what’s the plan?
M
Thanks, Ryan. The plan has three phases. First, we’re going to fix the form on your website. That’s our quick win.
R
OK. And after that?
M
Then we’re going to organize your leads in a CRM, and in month three we’re going to launch a new campaign. If it works, we’ll scale it.
R
Sounds good. But my budget is not very big this year.
M
I understand. Don’t worry: we won’t spend more on paid media in the first two months.
R
Great. Can you send me the timeline and the list of deliverables?
M
Sure. I’ll send the proposal today, and we can have the kickoff meeting next Tuesday.
Comprehension

1. What did Ryan think of the diagnosis?

2. What is Ryan’s worry?

3. What does Ryan ask Marcela to send?
The timeline and the list of deliverables.
