Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

The Client Proposal -- The Plan and the Promise

Ryan wants to know what happens next. Key words: a proposal, a budget, a timeline, a quick win, to launch, a deliverable, to scale, a kickoff meeting. Structure: going to for plans, will for decisions and promises.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A proposal -- a document that explains a plan and its price to a client
"I’m going to send the proposal on Friday."
A budget -- the money that you can spend on something
"The client’s budget for the project is ten thousand dollars."
A timeline -- a plan that shows what happens and when
"The timeline has three phases in three months."
A quick win -- a small, fast result that shows the plan works
"The new form is going to be our quick win."
To launch -- to start a new product, campaign or website
"We’re going to launch the new page in November."
A deliverable -- something that you promise to give the client, like a report
"The first deliverable is a data diagnosis."
To scale -- to make something bigger when it works
"When the campaign works, we’re going to scale it."
A kickoff meeting -- the first meeting of a new project
"The kickoff meeting is next Tuesday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Marcela presents her proposal to Ryan. “First, we’re going to fix the form. That’s our quick win. Then we’re going to organize your leads, and in month three we’re going to launch a new campaign.”

Ryan says his budget is small. Marcela answers on the spot: “Don’t worry. We won’t spend more on paid media in the first two months.”

Ryan asks for the timeline. “Sure. I’ll send it today, and we can have the kickoff meeting next Tuesday.”

1. What is the quick win of the plan?
A A new campaign.
B Fixing the form.
C A CRM.
2. Why does Marcela say “we won’t spend more”?
A Because Ryan’s budget is small.
B Because the campaign is ready.
C Because Ryan wants to scale.
3. Ryan asks for the timeline. Which answer is correct?
A “Sure. I going to send it today.”
B “Sure. I’ll send it today.”
C “Sure. I will to send it today.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Future -- Going To and Will

GRAMMAR

Use going to for a plan you decided before. Use will (’ll) for a decision you make now, and for promises. The negative of will is won’t.

JobFormExample
Plan (decided before)am / is / are + going to + verbWe’re going to launch in November.
Decision made nowwill (’ll) + verbGood question. I’ll check and tell you.
Promisewill / won’t + verbWe won’t go over the budget.
QuestionAre you going to...? · Will you...?Are you going to use our data?
Negativenot going to · won’tWe’re not going to change the brand.
The trapno to after willNot I will to send. Say I’ll send.

Never: We going to launch · I will to send · We don’t will go over · She is go to scale
