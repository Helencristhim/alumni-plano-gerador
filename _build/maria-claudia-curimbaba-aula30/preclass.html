Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Go-ahead -- official permission to start or continue with something
"The board gave us the go-ahead on Friday."
Fallback -- a second plan that you use if the first one does not work
"Expediting is our fallback if the rate is not confirmed."
At stake -- at risk; what can be won or lost if a decision goes one way or the other
"With the June run, two months of production are at stake."
Mandate -- the official authority given to a person to decide or act for a group
"The partners gave me a mandate to negotiate up to that number."
Leeway -- the freedom or margin to act as you choose within limits
"We have some leeway on price, but none on the date."
Sound out -- to find out informally what someone thinks before you make a decision
"Sound out Tom before the call so there are no surprises."
Spell out -- to explain something clearly and in detail, leaving nothing unsaid
"Spell out the caveat so nobody reads it as a promise."
Walk through -- to explain something to someone step by step
"I will walk through the two options first."
Weigh in -- to give your opinion in a discussion or decision
"I would like the whole family to weigh in before we vote."
Follow through -- to complete an action or a promise until the end
"The partners trust people who follow through."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Before the Houston call, Maria Claudia sounds out Tom. He tells her the partners are worried about quality, not price, and he asks whether she has a backup supplier. On the call, she opens with a hook and walks the partners through the update. To begin with, the May container has been cleared. Having said that, June is still at stake: the second container must be rerouted, and the documents should have been sent earlier. "If we had booked the space in May, we would have paid the old rate," she says, without blaming anyone. In the questions, she spells out every caveat. As far as she knows, the rate is likely to go up in October. The partners weigh in, give her the go-ahead to reroute and a mandate to negotiate up to 2,600 dollars, with a little leeway. Expediting stays as the fallback. Now she has to follow through.

1. Why does she say "If we had booked the space in May, we would have paid the old rate"?
A To explain a past that did not happen, without blaming anyone.
B To promise a lower rate in the future.
C To report what Tom said.
2. What does "the second container must be rerouted" focus on?
A The person who reroutes it.
B What has to happen to the container.
C What happened in May.
3. What does the board give her at the end?
A A new supplier.
B A guarantee that the rate will not go up.
C The go-ahead to reroute and a mandate to negotiate up to 2,600 dollars.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Block 3 Toolkit

GRAMMAR

This lesson reviews the five structures of Lessons 25 to 29. In a real meeting they work in sequence: discourse markers carry the update, the passive with modals says what must happen, the third conditional explains a past that did not happen, reported speech carries what other people said, and hedging protects every forecast.

ToolFormExample
third conditionalif + had + participle, would have + participleIf we had booked in May, we would have paid less.
reported speechsaid / told / asked whether + one step backTom asked whether we could guarantee June.
passive with modalsmust / should + be / have been + participleThe container must be cleared by the tenth.
discourse markersto begin with, having said that, as a resultHaving said that, June is still at risk.
hedgingit seems that, is likely to, as far as I knowThe rate is likely to go up.

Key point: one typical mistake per tool. "If we would have booked" → "If we had booked". "He asked can we" → "He asked whether we could". "Should have sent" (the documents) → "should have been sent". "Is likely go" → "is likely to go".

Stage 2: One Meeting in Order

Order

Listen first, then put the moments of a well-run meeting in the correct order.

?You give the update: a hook, three signposted points and a call to action.
?You follow through: you do what you promised, and you report back by the date you gave.
?Before the meeting, you sound out one person to learn what the others are worried about.
?You answer the questions, conceding what is true and hedging what you cannot promise.
?You write the outline around the one decision you need from the room.
