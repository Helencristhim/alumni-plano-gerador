Chapter 3 -- The Toolkit

Five Tools, One Meeting

Lessons 25 to 29, side by side

Toolkit

Five Sentences, Five Tools

All five come from the same meeting. Which tool is each one using, and what job does it do?

"If we had booked the space in May, we would have paid the old rate."

"Greg told me the goods had been ready on time."

"The second container must be cleared before the tenth."

"Having said that, the June run is still at risk."

"As far as I know, the rate is likely to go up."

ToolFormJob in the meeting
third conditional (L25)if + had + participle, would have + participleexplain a past that did not happen, without blame
reported speech (L26)said / told / asked whether + backshiftpass on what the other side really said
passive with modals (L27)must / can / should + be + participlesay what has to happen, not who does it
discourse markers (L28)to begin with · having said that · that brings me toshow the listener where the talk is going
hedging (L29)it seems that · is likely to · as far as I knowsay exactly how sure you are

Key idea: in a real meeting the tools come in a sequence: markers carry the update, the passive with modals says what must happen, the third conditional explains what went wrong, reported speech carries what the partners said, and hedging protects every forecast in the Q and A.

The Structure

Three Moments of One Meeting

The block, in the order a real meeting uses it.

1 · Before -- sound them out

"Tom told me the partners were worried about quality, not price."

Reported speech carries what you learned before you walk in.

2 · During -- the update

"To begin with, the May container has been cleared. Having said that, June must be rerouted."

Markers and the passive with modals carry the talk.

3 · After -- the questions

"If we had booked in May, we would have paid less. As far as I know, the rate is likely to rise."

The third conditional explains; the hedge protects the forecast.

Notice that no tool works alone. The skill of the block is choosing the next one quickly.

Spot the Error

Find the Mistake

0 / 4 errors found

"If we would have booked in May, we would have paid less."
"If we had booked in May, we would have paid less." (third conditional: never "would" after "if")
"Tom asked me can we guarantee June."
"Tom asked me whether we could guarantee June." (reported question: whether + normal order)
"The documents should have sent earlier."
"The documents should have been sent earlier." (passive with a modal needs "been")
"The rate is likely go up in October."
"The rate is likely to go up in October." (hedge: likely TO + verb)
