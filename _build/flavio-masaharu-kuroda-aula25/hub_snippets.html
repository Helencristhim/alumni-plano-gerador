Lesson 25 -- Pre-class

The Difficult Stakeholder -- Disagreeing Without the Fight

How to talk to the person inside a customer who doubts you: show you heard, disagree politely, and offer a way forward. Key words: a skeptic, a concern, to stall, buy-in, to win over, risk-averse, to overrule, a hidden agenda, to back, a veto. Structure: polite disagreement (I see your point, but; I'm not sure I agree; with respect; I take your point).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A skeptic -- a person who doubts that something is true or good
"The CFO is a skeptic about new suppliers."
A concern -- a worry about a possible problem
"Her main concern is support in Italy."
To stall -- to stop something from moving forward, often on purpose
"If she is not convinced, she will stall the decision."
Buy-in -- the agreement and support of the people involved
"Without the CFO's buy-in, nobody signs."
To win over -- to make someone agree with you or support you
"You need to win her over, not beat her."
Risk-averse -- not wanting to take any risks
"Finance teams are often risk-averse."
To overrule -- to change a decision because you have more power
"The CEO could overrule her, but he never does."
A hidden agenda -- a secret reason behind what someone does
"I don't think she has a hidden agenda. She is only careful."
To back -- to support a person or an idea
"Giulia backs us, but she cannot sign alone."
A veto -- the power to say no and stop a decision
"In this deal, the CFO has a veto."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

When a stakeholder disagrees with you, the first thing to do is show that you heard. I see your point, but our support team works in Italian. That's a fair concern. These phrases do not say the other person is right. They say you listened.

Then you can disagree, but softly. I'm not sure I agree that distance is the main risk. In a more formal situation, you start with with respect: with respect, our last three go-lives were done remotely.

I take your point about + a noun accepts one part of what the person said: I take your point about the fee. After that, you offer something: would it help if we linked half of the fee to the go-live? The verb after we is in the past simple, but the idea is about the future.

Brazilian speakers often say I am not agree, or you are wrong, which sounds very aggressive in English. Another common mistake is I take your point of the fee: the preposition is about.

1. Which sentence is correct?
A "I am not agree with you about the setup fee and the support."
B "I take your point of the fee."
C "I'm not sure I agree with you about the fee."
2. Why does Flávio say "I see your point" before he disagrees?
A To show that he heard the concern, before he gives his answer.
B To say that the CFO is completely right and that he has nothing more to add to the conversation.
C Because it is a rule of grammar.
3. In "Would it help if we linked the fee to the go-live?", what is the time of the idea?
A The past: it already happened last year, before the call with the CFO.
B The future: it is an offer, even with the verb in the past.
C The present: it is happening now.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Polite Disagreement

GRAMMAR

First show you heard. Then disagree softly. Then offer something.

JobPhraseExample
show you heardI see your point, but...I see your point, but our support is in Italian.
accept partI take your point about + nounI take your point about the fee.
disagree softlyI'm not sure I agree that...I'm not sure I agree that it is risky.
disagree formallyWith respect, ...With respect, the data says otherwise.
offerWould it help if we + past?Would it help if we started with one company?

Never: you are wrong · I am not agree · I take your point of · would it help if we link

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The CFO says your price is too high. The best first answer is:
A "I take your point about the price. Can I show you the cost over three years?"
B "You are wrong."
C "No, our price is not high, because our product is much better than all the others on the market."
You do not agree with the CFO about the risk. You say:
A "I am not agree, and I think that you do not understand how our support team works in Europe."
B "With respect, I'm not sure I agree that distance is the main risk."
C "Distance is not a risk, okay?"
The CFO is blocking the deal. The best idea is:
A "Call the CEO and ask him to overrule her."
B "Send her a long presentation with forty slides about the history of your company and all your awards."
C "Ask her what she would need to see to feel comfortable."
You want to offer something to the skeptic. You say:
A "Would it help if we started with one company instead of four?"
B "Would it help if we start with one company, and then after that, maybe all the other companies?"
C "We give you one company."
