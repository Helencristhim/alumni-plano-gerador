Complementary Activities

Optional extras to enjoy outside class. Mark each one as done after you watch or listen to it.

Film
The Secret -- the 2006 documentary

You have already seen this one, and you liked it. That is exactly why it works now: you know the story, so your ear is free to follow the English instead of the plot. Almost every sentence in it is present simple in the first person -- I am, I do, I believe, I think -- which is the grammar of this lesson, spoken slowly for ninety minutes.

Tip: watch it again in English, with English subtitles. Stop at three sentences that start with "I" and say each one out loud, replacing the words with your own life.

Talk
Kio Stark -- "Why you should talk to strangers" (TED)

Twelve minutes on the exact situation of today’s dialogue: a person you have never met, a park, a dog, and the first sentence. She argues that a short exchange with a stranger costs almost nothing and changes the whole day, and she shows how the smallest question keeps it going.

Tip: turn the English subtitles on. Write down every question she uses, then use one of them this week -- in English or in Portuguese, it does not matter. The habit is the point.

Watch on TED ↗
Video
BBC Learning English -- "What does 'small talk' mean?"

Under three minutes, and it names the thing this lesson is about. Small talk is the short, easy conversation you have with somebody you do not know well -- the weather, the weekend, the dog. It is not empty: it is how two strangers agree to keep talking.

Tip: it is short, so watch it twice. The second time, pause after every example and say it out loud before the speaker does.
