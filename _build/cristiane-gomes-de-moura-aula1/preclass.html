Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

Who Is Cristiane? -- Saying Who You Are, and Asking Back

Somebody you have just met asks who you are and what you do, and the answer stops after four words. Key words: a neighborhood, free time, a hobby, to walk the dog, to go out with friends, a problem solver, a team, a teenager, a play, to travel. Structure: present simple with I and you — am for who you are, the bare verb for what you do, and do for the negative and the question.

A hobby -- something you do because you enjoy it, and never for money

"On Saturdays I go out with friends, usually for dinner."
A problem solver -- a person who finds the answer when something goes wrong
"From the moment I arrive until I leave, I am a problem solver."
A team -- a group of people who work together on the same thing
"I lead a team of nine people."
A teenager -- a person between thirteen and nineteen years old
"I have a son of sixteen, so I live with a teenager."
A play -- a story that real actors perform live, on a stage, in front of you
"I saw a very good play last month, and I want to go back."
To travel -- to go to another city or another country, for work or for pleasure
"I like to travel, and I just came back from a holiday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Somebody you have just met asks who you are. The whole answer fits into four shapes, and you already have all four. The first one says who you are: "I am Cristiane, and I am forty-eight." Look at that little word. It is am, and it appears because the sentence is about your name and your age, not about an action. English never says I have forty-eight years. Age is something you are.

The second shape says what you do, and here the am disappears completely: "I live in a neighborhood called Tatuape. I lead a team of nine people. I walk the dog every morning." The verb stands alone, with nothing in front of it. This is the shape people get wrong most often, because in Portuguese you say that you are responsible for something. In English, the verb does the work by itself. Never I am work. Say I work.

The third shape says no, and English does not touch the verb. It puts do not in front of it: "I do not have much free time from Monday to Friday, and I do not work on Sundays." The fourth shape is the question, and it is the one that keeps a conversation alive: "Do you have a dog? What do you do in your free time?" And when you have finished answering, hand the question back with three words: what about you? That is how the other person keeps talking, and how you get a few free seconds to think.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Am, the Bare Verb, and Do

GRAMMAR

Am says who you are. The bare verb says what you do. The two never stand together.

JobFormExample
Who I am, how old I amI am + name, number or wordI am Cristiane. I am forty-eight.
What I do every weekI / you + bare verb, and no amI walk my dog every morning.
Negativedo not + bare verbI do not work on Sunday.
QuestionDo + you + bare verbDo you like to travel?
Question word in frontWhat / Where / How often + do + you + bare verbWhat do you do in your free time?
Short answerYes, I do. / No, I do not."Do you have a dog?" — "Yes, I do."
Hand the question backAnd you? / What about you?I go to the theater. What about you?
In speechdo not becomes don’tI don’t work on Sundays.

Never: I have forty-eight years · I am work with technology · I am live in Tatuape · I not have free time · You like to travel?
