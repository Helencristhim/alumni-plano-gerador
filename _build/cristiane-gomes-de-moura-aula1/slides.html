Who Is Cristiane?

Warm-up

In Three Words: Who Are You?

The Target

One Minute, Three Moves

1. Your name, your city, your age. Three short sentences.

2. What you do, and what you do when you are not working.

3. One question back, so the other person keeps talking.

Diagnostic

What Do You Do When You Are Not Working?

Somebody you have just met asks: “And what do you do in your free time?”

Answer with two or three things. Short sentences are fine. Then ask the same question back.

Chapter 3: The Code

What I Am, and What I Do

Two little words that never appear together

Grammar Discovery

Why Do Two of These Have Am, and Two Do Not?

"I am Cristiane, and I am forty-eight."

"I live in Sao Paulo, and I walk my dog every morning."

"I do not work on Sunday."

"Do you like to travel?"

The red word is always the important one. In the first sentence it is am, because the sentence says who you are. In the others there is no am at all, because the sentence says what you do. And the blue word only turns up to say no, or to ask.

JobFormExample
Who I am, how old I amI am + name, number or wordI am Cristiane. I am forty-eight.
What I do every weekI / you + bare verb, and no amI walk my dog every morning.
Negativedo not + bare verbI do not work on Sunday.
QuestionDo + you + bare verbDo you like to travel?
Question word in frontWhat / Where / How often + do + you + bare verbWhat do you do in your free time?
Short answerYes, I do. / No, I do not."Do you have a dog?" — "Yes, I do."
Hand the question backAnd you? / What about you?I go to the theater. What about you?
The two trapsno am in front of another verb; no have for ageNot I am work → I work. Not I have 48 years → I am 48.

In one line: am says who you are, the bare verb says what you do, and the two never stand together.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

A Sunday Morning, at Full Speed

One conversation, three recordings, one profile

Dialogue

Sunday Morning at the Park

D
Good morning. Your dog is beautiful. Is she friendly?
C
Good morning. Yes, she is very sweet. I walk the dog here every Sunday.
D
I am new here. I am Daniel. I moved to this neighborhood last month.
C
Nice to meet you, Daniel. I am Cristiane. I live two streets from the park.
D
And what do you do, Cristiane?
C
I work with technology. I lead a team of nine people, and I am a problem solver all day. And you? What do you do?
D
I am a teacher. I do not work on Sundays, so I come here with my dog. Do you have free time on Sundays?
C
Yes, I do. I go out with friends, or I go to the theater. What about you?
Comprehension

About Daniel

1. Why is Daniel new in the neighborhood?
He moved there last month, so he does not know the people in the park yet.
2. What is his job, and on which day does he not work?
He is a teacher, and he does not work on Sundays.
3. What does he do with his free Sunday, and what does he ask at the end?
He comes to the park with his dog, and he asks Cristiane if she has free time on Sundays.

She is from Lisbon, but she lives in Dublin now.

On the weekend, because she does not work then.

Where you live, what you do, and what you like doing when you are not working.
Listening 2 · A Podcast

Three Words That Keep a Conversation Alive

A teacher on why people go quiet in a second language. Sound first — no text.

1. Which three words does he say change everything?
What about you.
2. What does he say is the real reason people feel stuck?
Not grammar. They answer the question and stop, and then there is a silence that feels like their fault.
3. What does he say you get when the other person keeps talking?
Thirty free seconds to think.
Listening 3 · A Sunday

The Only Day That Is Completely Mine

A woman describing her Sunday, from morning to night. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the first thing she does after she gets up, and for how long?
She takes the dog out, and they walk for about an hour, always in the same park.
2. Why does she cook something slow on a Sunday?
Because during the week she never has time for that.
3. What is the one thing she does not do on a Sunday, and why does that rule matter to her?
She does not open her work computer. She says that rule is the reason Monday is possible.

Chapter 6: Your Turn

The Same Minute, Three Times

Guided > Semi-free > Free

Role-Play 1 of 3 — Guided

Daniel Asks Again

Scenario: Daniel asks the same three questions he asked at the park: who you are, what you do, and what you do on a free Sunday. Answer all three, and finish with a question back to him.

Keyword chips:

a neighborhood a team a problem solver free time
Role-Play 2 of 3 — Semi-free

Ask Before You Tell

Scenario: A new person sits on the bench next to you in the park. Ask two questions about who they are and what they do, before you say one word about yourself — then connect their answer to yours in a single sentence.

Keywords:

What do you do? Where do you live? Do you have a dog? What about you?
Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

One Minute, No Notes

Scenario: A group of people who have never met you. Give them your name, your city, what you do in one line, one thing you do not do, two things you do in your free time, and one question for the group.

No keywords, no notes, one minute. Nobody will interrupt you.
