Chapter 3: The Code

Condition, Consequence

The sentence every proposal in English is built on

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Have in Common?

If the pilot works, we will scale it to the whole region.
We will not get the budget unless finance is in the room.
As long as the managers release the time, this will work.
If you ask for everything in the first meeting, they will say no.
Click to reveal the rule
The first conditional: a real, likely future. Two halves — if + present simple (the condition) and will + verb (the consequence). The future never goes after if: it is if the pilot works, never if the pilot will work. Portuguese pushes you the other way, so this one has to be chosen on purpose. Three words replace if and change the meaning: unless (= if not), as long as / provided that (= only on this condition), in case (= as a precaution, before the problem happens). Either half can come first; when the if half opens the sentence, it takes a comma.

Chapter 4: The Pitch

The Proposal Meets the Room

One page, one condition, one way out

Listening 1

Advice Before the Meeting

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What does Claire say will happen if you ask for the whole budget at once?
They will say no in the first meeting. She tells you to ask for a pilot in one site instead.
2. What should move from page nine to page one, and why?
The paragraph about the root cause. If they read it first, they will understand why the initiative exists.
3. When should the finance director be brought in?
Before the meeting, not during it. With his buy-in beforehand, nobody will argue with the numbers.
Artefact

The One-Page Proposal

PEOPLE & CULTURE · PROPOSAL

ONBOARDING PILOT — SITE 04

Root cause: new employees are on the line in three days, with no structured first month.

Proposal: pilot a 30-day onboarding program in one site, from March.

Condition: if early attrition falls by five points in ninety days, we scale it to the region.

Exit: if it does not, we stop. No second pilot.

OWNER: A. CARDOSO · SPONSOR: FINANCE

Fifteen minutes before the budget meeting

Selling the Pilot

M
I have your one-pager. Before we go in, give me the whole thing in two sentences.
A
People leave in the first year because there is no first month. If we pilot a structured onboarding program in one site, early attrition will fall.
M
How much is this going to cost me?
A
Almost nothing in the pilot. If it works and we scale it, the investment comes back in the overtime line, not in the training line.
M
The site managers will tell you their teams are too busy for this.
A
They will. That is why I need your buy-in first. As long as it comes from you, the conversation with them is short.
M
And if it does not work?
A
Then we stop in ninety days and I bring you the reason. Unless attrition falls by five points, there is no second pilot. That is written on the page.

Chapter 5: Practice

The Questions a Board Always Asks

Cost, risk, owner, and what happens if it fails

Listening 2

Announcing the Pilot

Read the questions, then listen.

1. Where and when does the pilot start, and when is it reviewed?
In one site, starting in March, and it is reviewed in ninety days.
2. What is the exact condition for scaling it to the region?
Early attrition in that site has to fall by at least five points. Nothing else counts.
3. What should you do if a manager says his team is too busy?
Send the manager to him — that conversation is his, not yours.
