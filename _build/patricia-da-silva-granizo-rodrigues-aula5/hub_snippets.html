Lesson 05 -- Pre-class

Saying It With Precision

The ten words of argument rather than information, and the four sentence shapes that put your point where English puts its weight -- at the end. Key words: to underscore, to reframe, trade-off, caveat, bottom line, to hedge, to double down, to spell out, pushback, in a nutshell. Structure: cleft sentences and fronting for emphasis.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read it inside a real sentence. These ten belong to the part of the meeting that happens after the numbers.

To underscore -- to give extra weight to a point you have already made
"Let me underscore one number before we move on."
To reframe -- to describe the same fact in a different way so it is understood differently
"She reframed the cost as an investment in the first ninety days."
Trade-off -- a gain you accept in exchange for a loss somewhere else
"The trade-off is speed against cost, and I would take speed."
Caveat -- a warning or a condition attached to what you have just said
"One caveat: the September figure is provisional."
Bottom line -- the essential point, once everything else is taken away
"The bottom line is that we cannot open two sites with one recruiter."
To hedge -- to protect yourself by not committing fully to a statement
"Stop hedging and tell me what you would do."
To double down -- to commit harder to a position instead of softening it
"He doubled down on the December date even after the pushback."
To spell out -- to explain something in full detail, leaving nothing implied
"Let me spell out what happens if we do nothing."
Pushback -- resistance or disagreement from the people you are speaking to
"I expected pushback on the budget and got none."
In a nutshell -- in the shortest possible summary
"In a nutshell, the problem is onboarding, not pay."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

What the data says is something narrower than the usual story. It is the people under a year of service that we are losing, and their pay is at the market rate. The one thing that separates them from everybody else is their first ninety days.

The benchmark objection is a fair caveat, and the answer to it is one fact rather than five. What I am asking for is one owner for onboarding at each site -- not a new process and not more budget. All I am saying is that money is not the driver here. In a nutshell: one quarter and one person.

1. It is the people under a year of service that we are losing. What does this shape do that we are losing people under a year of service does not?
A It makes the sentence longer and therefore more formal.
B It isolates one group and quietly denies all the others.
C It shows that the writer is not sure which group it is.
2. Why does the writer answer the benchmark objection with one fact rather than five?
A Because five facts would take too long to say.
B Because one checkable fact is harder to argue with than a list, which invites the listener to pick the weakest item.
C Because the other four facts are confidential.
3. Not a new process and not more budget. What is this doing?
A Denying the two alternatives the reader was about to imagine.
B Apologising in advance for the size of the request.
C Listing the two things that were tried before.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Cleft Sentences and Emphasis

GRAMMAR

English puts its weight at the end of the sentence. These structures exist to move the important part there and make the room wait for it. It is not more words: it is the same words, reordered.

FormWhat it doesExample
What ... is / was ...puts your point at the END, where the room is listening hardestWhat we need is one owner for each site.
It is ... that ...isolates the one element that matters and denies the othersIt is the first ninety days that we are losing people in.
The one thing ... is ...announces that only one item is coming, so nobody waits for a listThe one thing I would change is the shift pattern.
All I am saying is ...reduces a long argument to its claim, which is what pushback needsAll I am saying is that money is not the driver.
why this reads as presenceit is not more words. It is the same words, ordered so that the point lands LAST -- the structure tells the room where to put its attention before the content arrives.

The mirror image is worth practising too. A nervous speaker fronts every condition and arrives at the point after forty seconds. Say the point, then the conditions.

Stage 2: Put the Argument in Order

Order

The order that survives pushback, from the claim to the ask.

?The trade-off is that Ana comes off the engagement survey until December.
?It is the first ninety days that we are losing people in, not the pay.
?What I am asking for is one owner for onboarding at each site.
?And if attrition has not moved by December, then I was wrong and we look at pay.
?The leavers with three years or more are on the same benchmark and they are staying.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

One right answer each. Read the situation before you look at the options.

You have forty seconds and one thing to ask for. How do you start?
A Given the constraints and the timeline, we might possibly consider looking at onboarding.
B What I need is one owner for onboarding at each site.
C I do not know if this is the right moment, but there is something about onboarding.
D There are several things we could do about onboarding.
Ray raises an objection that is partly right. What is the strongest move?
A Ignore it and repeat your claim more firmly.
B Agree with the part that is right, then give one checkable fact.
C Concede the whole point and offer to look again next quarter.
D Say that you will send the data by email afterwards.
You want to signal that only one item is coming, so nobody waits for a list.
A There are a few things I would change.
B The one thing I would change is the shift pattern.
C Firstly, I would change the shift pattern.
D Among other things, the shift pattern.
You have finished your point and the room is silent. What do you do?
A Add a summary so that the silence is not awkward.
B Stop talking and let the silence sit.
C Ask whether that was clear.
D Say so, yeah, and hand over.
