Chapter 3: The Pushback

Ray Does Not Agree

A VP who has read your note and thinks the money argument is the real one

Dialogue

When Somebody Pushes Back

R
I read your note. I am not convinced. Everywhere I have worked, people leave for money and then tell you it was something else.
P
That is often true. What the data says here is something narrower. It is the people under a year of service we are losing, and their pay is at the market rate.
R
At the rate you benchmarked. Benchmarks lag. A competitor can move in March and the survey shows it in September.
P
Agreed, and that is a fair caveat. Let me spell out why I still think it is not pay. The leavers with three years or more are on the same benchmark and they are not going anywhere. Same pay, different behaviour.
R
Hm. So what are you actually asking for?
P
What I am asking for is one owner for onboarding at each site. Not a new process and not more budget. The trade-off is that Ana comes off the engagement survey until December.
R
That I can live with. And if attrition has not moved by December?
P
Then I was wrong and we look at pay properly, with a survey we commission ourselves. In a nutshell, give me one quarter and one person.
Comprehension

About Ray

1. What is Ray's opening position, and what reason does he give for it?
He is not convinced. He says that everywhere he has worked, people leave for money and then tell you it was something else.
2. What technical objection does Ray raise about the benchmark?
That benchmarks lag: a competitor can move in March and the survey only shows it in September.
3. What does Ray accept, and what does he ask at the end?
He accepts the trade-off of taking Ana off the engagement survey until December, and asks what happens if attrition has not moved by then.

Chapter 4: The Code

The Shapes That Put Your Point at the End

Four sentence structures that carry more authority than any adjective

Grammar Discovery

What We Need Is...

"What we need is one owner for each site, not a new process."

"It is the first ninety days that we are losing people in, not the pay."

"The one thing I would change is the shift pattern."

"All I am saying is that money is not the driver here."

Rewrite any of these as a plain sentence in your head. What do you lose when you do?

FormWhat it doesExample
What ... is / was ...puts your point at the END, where the room is listening hardestWhat we need is one owner for each site.
It is ... that ...isolates the one element that matters and denies the othersIt is the first ninety days that we are losing people in.
The one thing ... is ...announces that only one item is coming, so nobody waits for a listThe one thing I would change is the shift pattern.
All I am saying is ...reduces a long argument to its claim, which is what pushback needsAll I am saying is that money is not the driver.
why this reads as presenceit is not more words. It is the same words, ordered so that the point lands LAST -- the structure tells the room where to put its attention before the content arrives.

English puts its weight at the END of the sentence. These four structures exist to move the important part there and to make the room wait for it. That waiting is what gets called presence.

Chapter 5: The Ear

Why Some People Sound Decisive

A communications coach and an executive who changed his mind

Listening 1

It Is Not the Voice, It Is the Order

A communications coach, on what she actually fixes. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she say the problem usually is not, and what is it instead?
It is not the voice. It is the order of the sentence.
2. What does a nervous speaker do, in her description?
They put every condition first, so that forty seconds go by before the point arrives and nobody knows what they are being asked.
3. What is the second thing she fixes?
The ending: not trailing off and not adding so, yeah, but saying the last word and stopping. She says the silence is the point landing.
Listening 2

The Meeting Where I Changed My Mind

An executive remembers being argued out of a position. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What is the first thing the person did, and what effect did it have on him?
She agreed with his objection first, and said which part of it was right. He stopped preparing his next sentence and started listening, because he was no longer being resisted.
2. How many facts did she give him, and why did that matter?
One fact, not five, and it was checkable -- so he could not argue with it without going to look, which he was not going to do in front of eight people.
3. What does he say winning an argument is really about?
Not pressure, but removing every reason the other person has to keep defending themselves.
