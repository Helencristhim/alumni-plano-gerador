Lesson 02 — Pre-class

Twenty-Three Years

Eight words for a career (promotion, milestone, achievement, turning point, challenge, assignment, take over, eventually), the past simple for finished moments, and for/since for what is still true. Around 30 minutes, in one sitting.

Stage 1.1: Words for a Career

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say each word out loud with your own numbers. Eight words, three minutes.

Promotion — a move to a higher position at work
"I got my first promotion after four years."
Milestone — an important moment in a long story
"Two thousand and fifteen was a milestone for me."
Achievement — something good that you did and can be proud of
"My biggest achievement was building this team."
Turning point — the moment when things changed direction
"New Zealand was a turning point for me."
Challenge — a difficult thing that you have to deal with
"The first year was a real challenge."
Assignment — a task or a job that someone gives you
"They gave me an assignment in another city."
Take over — to start doing a job that another person did before
"I took over the team in 2015."
Eventually — after a long time, in the end
"Eventually, I became head of operations."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the text. Then answer the questions.

I joined this bank in two thousand and one, when I was twenty-six. For the first four years I worked in the back office and I did not speak to a single client. Then I got my first promotion. They gave me an assignment in another city for a year, and that was the real turning point — I came back with clients of my own. In two thousand and fifteen I took over the commercial team. Eventually the team grew from three people to eight. I have been here for twenty-three years now, and I have led this team since two thousand and fifteen.
How long did he work in the back office?
A Four years.
B One year.
Why was the assignment a turning point?
A Because it was in another city.
B Because he came back with clients of his own.
Why is it "I have been here for twenty-three years" and not "I am here since twenty-three years"?
A Because it started before and it has not stopped — and for goes with how long.
B Because twenty-three is a big number.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Two shapes for one career

A moment that is over → past simple. Something that started before and is still true → have + verb.

Over

join → joined
work → worked
take → took
become → became
spend → spent

Still true

I have been here for 23 years.
I have worked here since 2001.
I have led the team since 2015.

for + how long — for 23 years, for six months, for a long time
since + when it started — since 2001, since I was 26, since March
× "I work there since 2001" — this is the one sentence to fix before November

Stage 4: In the room

Quiz
Someone asks how long you have been at the bank. Which answer is correct?
A "I work there since 2001."
B "I’ve worked there since 2001."
You want to say the number of years, not the starting date. Which word do you need?
A for — "for twenty-three years"
B since — "since twenty-three years"
Which sentence tells a story instead of listing titles?
A "Analyst, then manager, then head of operations."
B "I started in the back office, and in 2015 I took over the team."

Stage 5: Your two minutes

Reflection
Record two minutes in English. Tell your career story: where you started, one moment that changed your direction, and where you are now. No list of job titles — one story. Use at least three past simple verbs and one sentence with for or since. Finish with a question for your teacher.
