"Keep the receipt, because the desk will ask for it."

"The cancellation policy is at the bottom of the page."
To check in -- to arrive and give your name at a hotel or an airport
"We check in at three, so we have the whole morning free."
Free of charge -- with no extra money to pay
"You can leave your bags here free of charge until three."

"They asked for proof of the reservation, so I showed the e-mail."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Every policy page in the world does only four things, and English uses four little structures to do them. The first is obligation. Guests must show a photo ID when they check in, and they have to leave a deposit on a card. Must and have to mean the same thing here; must belongs to the written rule, and have to is what a person says out loud. After both of them the verb is bare: must show, never must to show.

The second is permission. Children under twelve are allowed to stay in the room free of charge, and every guest is allowed to leave the bags at the desk before three. The little word to is not optional: allowed to leave, never allowed leave. And the question is built the same way: Am I allowed to leave my bags here?

The third is prohibition, and the fourth is the trap. You must not smoke in the room means the hotel forbids it. You do not have to print the receipt means something completely different: nobody is forcing you, and you can print it if you want. In Portuguese the two sound close. In English they are opposites, and the difference on a non-refundable booking is money.

One last thing: in the past, all of this becomes had to. Last October we had to pay a fee to change the date, and the hotel never gave us a refund. There is no such word as musted, and the desk will always ask you for proof of what you say. Bring it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Obligation and Permission

GRAMMAR

Must not forbids. Do not have to frees. They are opposites.

JobFormExample
A written rulemust + bare verbGuests must show a photo ID.
An obligation, in speechhave to / has to + bare verbYou have to leave a deposit. She has to sign it.
Permissionam / is / are allowed to + bare verbYou are allowed to leave your bags.
Prohibitionmust not / are not allowed toYou must not smoke in the room.
No obligationdo not have to + bare verbYou do not have to print it — but you can.
In the pasthad to (there is no musted)Last year we had to pay a fee.
In speechmust not becomes mustn’t · do not have to becomes don’t have toYou mustn’t smoke here.

Never: I must to pay · are we allowed take the towels · you do not have to smoke (when you mean it is forbidden) · last October we must pay a fee
