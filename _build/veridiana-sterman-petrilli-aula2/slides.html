Was It Worth It?

The Target

Three Things a Rule Can Do

1. It obliges you. You have no choice.

2. It allows you. Somebody is giving you permission you did not know you had.

3. It frees you. There is no obligation at all — and that is not the same as forbidding.

Diagnostic

Think of the last hotel you stayed in, anywhere.

Name one thing they obliged you to do, one thing they let you do, and one thing you were not allowed to do.

Chapter 4: The Code

Obliged, Allowed, or Free?

Four words, and one of them is a trap

Grammar Discovery

Which One Is Not Like the Others?

"Guests must show a photo ID at check-in."

"You have to leave a deposit on a card."

"You are allowed to leave your bags free of charge."

"You do not have to print the confirmation."

The red words take away your choice. The blue words give it back — but in two very different ways. And look at what comes straight after each one.

JobFormExample
A written rulemust + bare verbGuests must show a photo ID.
An obligation, in speechhave to / has to + bare verbYou have to leave a deposit. She has to sign it.
Permissionam / is / are allowed to + bare verbYou are allowed to leave your bags.
Prohibitionmust not / are not allowed toYou must not smoke in the room.
No obligationdo not have to + bare verbYou do not have to print it — but you can.
In the pasthad to (there is no musted)Last year we had to pay a fee.
QuestionDo I have to...? / Am I allowed to...?Am I allowed to leave my bags here?
The trapnever must to, never allowed takeNot I must to pay. Say I must pay.

In one line: must not forbids, and do not have to frees. They are opposites, and Portuguese hides that.

Listening 1 · The Front Desk

Three Lines Nobody Reads

A hotel receptionist on the three lines guests always get wrong. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say the deposit is, and when do guests get it back?
It is not a payment, it is a hold. Guests get it back in ten working days, not on the day they leave.
2. Guests tell her they cancelled and should get everything back. When is that true, and when is it not?
It is only true on a flexible rate. On the cheap rate the room is non-refundable, and the word means what it says.
3. What does she say guests are allowed to do when they land early, and why does it matter?
They are allowed to leave their bags at the hotel free of charge, and she says most people do not know that.
Listening 2 · Consumer Radio

Cheap Rate or Flexible Rate?

A consumer programme answering the question it gets every week. Sound first — no text.

1. If the flexible rate costs fifteen per cent more, what does he say you are actually buying?
Insurance. And the question is simply whether the trip is likely to change.
2. What does he say the word refund sometimes means in practice?
Some companies give money, but a lot of them give a voucher, and a voucher is not a refund.
3. What is the third thing he says almost nobody does, and why does it matter?
Save the confirmation as a file, not as an e-mail, because proof you can show on a screen in five seconds is worth more than proof you have to look for.

Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

Your Own Rules, No Notes

Scenario: A new patient from abroad has just booked an appointment with you. In sixty seconds, tell them what they have to bring, what they are allowed to do in the waiting room, what they must not do before the appointment, and one thing they do not have to worry about at all.

No keywords, no notes, sixty seconds. Every rule needs a reason after it.
