Lesson 16 -- Pre-class

Timelines and Deadlines

A schedule update for the east side pumping stations, and how to talk about plans and deadlines. Key words: milestone, deadline, phase, kick off, on track, behind schedule, push back, handover. Structure: present continuous for future arrangements, and by for deadlines.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Milestone -- an important point or stage in a project
"Finishing the foundations is the first milestone."
Deadline -- the latest time or date to finish something
"The deadline for the electrical work is June 30."
Phase -- one stage of a project
"Phase two starts after the design is approved."
Kick off -- to start a project or a meeting
"We're kicking off the excavation on Monday."
On track -- happening as planned, with no delay
"Station 1 is on track for the first milestone."
Behind schedule -- later than planned
"The supplier is two weeks behind schedule."
Push back -- to move an event to a later date
"We're pushing back the installation to May 19."
Handover -- the moment when the finished work is given to the client
"The handover is happening at the end of August."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

It is Friday afternoon, and Thiago is planning next week. Phase one is finished, so the team is kicking off phase two on Monday. The crew is arriving on site at seven, and an inspector is visiting on Thursday.

Not everything is on track. The supplier is two weeks behind schedule, so Thiago is pushing back the pump installation to May 19. The first milestone, the foundations, must be finished by April 11, and the deadline for the handover does not change.

Rachel needs the new schedule by Wednesday, because she is meeting the client on Thursday. Notice the plans: they are already in the calendar, so Thiago uses am, is, are plus -ing. And by Wednesday means on Wednesday or before, never after.

1. Why is the installation moving to May 19?
A Because the inspector is visiting the site on Thursday and the crew cannot work that week.
B Because the supplier is two weeks behind schedule.
C Because phase one is not finished.
2. Which sentence talks about a plan that is already booked?
A The crew is arriving on site at seven.
B The crew arrive on site at seven next Monday.
C The crew arriving on site at seven.
3. Rachel needs the schedule by Wednesday. When can Thiago send it?
A On Thursday morning, before the meeting with the client.
B Only on Wednesday, not before.
C On Wednesday or before.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Plans and Deadlines

GRAMMAR

When a plan is already in the calendar, use am, is or are plus -ing, and say the time. For a deadline, use by.

Not naturalNaturalWhy
We kick off on Monday.We are kicking off phase two on Monday.a fixed plan: am / is / are + -ing
Does the inspector visit on Thursday?Is the inspector visiting on Thursday?question: be before the subject
The crew doesn't work on Friday.The crew isn't working on Friday.negative: isn't / aren't + -ing
Send it until Friday.Send it by Friday.a deadline: by = on this day or before
The road is closed by Friday.The road is closed until Friday.until = all the time up to this day

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are talking about a project schedule with the office in the United States. Choose the best thing to say.

Rachel asks when phase two starts. It is booked for Monday:
A We're kicking off phase two on Monday.
B We will maybe kick off the phase two on the next Monday, if everything is fine.
C We kick off phase two in Monday.
Your manager needs the report not later than Friday:
A I am sending you the report until Friday, so you can read it during the weekend.
B I'll send you the report by Friday.
C I send the report since Friday.
The pumps are late, so the installation moves to a later date:
A We are pushing up the installation to May 19 because the pumps are coming early.
B We are pulling the installation to May 19.
C We're pushing back the installation to May 19.
The client asks if Station 1 has any delay. It does not:
A Station 1 is on track for the first milestone.
B Station 1 is behind schedule, but it is not late at all for the first milestone.
C Station 1 is on time track.
You want to know about Rachel's plans for next week:
A Do you visit the site in the next week with the team from the office in Houston?
