Chapter 4: Booked and Due

What Is Booked, What Is Due

The language of every schedule

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"We are kicking off phase two on Monday."

"The crew is arriving on site at seven."

"The foundations must be finished by April 11."

"I'm sending the new schedule by Friday."

FormUseExample
am / is / are + -ing + future timea fixed plan: the date, the people and the place are already decidedWe are kicking off phase two on Monday.
Are you / Is she + -ing ...?question about a planAre you visiting the site next week?
by + time or datea deadline: not later than this momentSend me the slides by Wednesday.
until + time or dateall the time up to this moment, then it stopsThe road is closed until Friday.

One question decides it: is it already in the calendar? Then use am / is / are plus -ing. And for a deadline, by means on this day or before, never after.

Dialogue

The New Dates

R
I'm preparing the update for the client. Can we go over the new dates?
T
Sure. We're kicking off phase two on Monday. The crew is arriving at seven.
R
Great. And when is the first milestone?
T
The foundations of Station 1 must be finished by April 11.
R
Is Station 2 on track too?
T
Not really. The supplier is two weeks behind schedule with the pumps.
R
So are we pushing back the installation?
T
Yes, we're installing the pumps on May 19. But the deadline doesn't change.
R
Good. The client asks me about the handover every week.
T
The handover is still happening on August 29. I'm sending the new schedule by Friday.
R
Perfect. I'm meeting the client on Thursday, so could I have it by Wednesday?
