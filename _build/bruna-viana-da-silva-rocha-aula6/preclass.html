Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

You Should Have Packed That -- Advice, Rules and Regret

Everything that goes wrong on a trip, and the four verbs that talk about it. Key words: travel insurance, a baggage allowance, a boarding pass, a terminal, currency exchange, a travel adapter, a hostel, to overpack, to pack light, to run out of. Structure: should, must, have to, do not have to — and should have plus the third form, for the thing you did not do.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Travel insurance -- the coverage you buy before a trip, so somebody else pays when it goes wrong
"The travel insurance cost forty dollars and it paid for a hospital in Anchorage."
A baggage allowance -- how much luggage your ticket lets you take before they charge you
"Check the baggage allowance before you pack, not at the counter."
A boarding pass -- the paper or the screen that lets you onto the plane
"Screenshot your boarding pass -- airport wifi always dies at the worst moment."
A terminal -- one of the separate buildings at an airport, each with its own gates
"We were in the right airport and the wrong terminal, forty minutes apart."
Currency exchange -- the place or the act of turning one country's money into another's
"Never do the currency exchange at the airport. That is where the worst rate lives."
A travel adapter -- the small piece that makes your plug fit somebody else's wall
"One travel adapter for two people is a mistake you only make once."
A hostel -- cheap shared lodging where you can have a bed in a room with strangers
"The hostel was louder than I wanted and better company than any hotel."
To overpack -- to take far more than you will ever use
"I overpack every single time, and every single time I wear four things."
To pack light -- to take only what you will actually need
"If you pack light, you can move between three cities without thinking about it."
To run out of -- to use the last of something and have none left
"We ran out of cash in a town with one machine, and it was broken."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

There are four ways to talk about what somebody did wrong on a trip, and they are not interchangeable. "You must show your passport at customs" is not a suggestion — it is the law of the building. "You should always carry a charger" is advice: a good idea, and nobody will stop you if you ignore it.

"You have to be at the gate thirty minutes early" sounds like must, and in speech Americans use it far more often. The difference people actually feel: must comes from the speaker, have to comes from a rule out there in the world. And the one that catches everybody: "You do not have to check in at the counter anymore" does not mean it is forbidden. It means you are free not to.

Then there is the one this whole lesson is named after. "I should have bought travel insurance." It did not happen, it is over, and I am not happy about it. Shape: should have + the third form of the verb — bought, taken, packed, checked. The negative is the same regret from the other side: "I shouldn't have overpacked."

One warning about your ears. Nobody says should have out loud. Americans say shoulda, coulda, woulda — "I shoulda packed light", "we coulda taken the earlier one". You will never write it that way, and you will hear it that way every single time.

1. What does "You do not have to check in at the counter" mean?
A That checking in at the counter is forbidden.
B That you are free not to — there is no obligation, but you still may.
C That you must check in somewhere else instead.
2. "I should have bought travel insurance." What actually happened?
A She bought it and regrets buying it.
B She is going to buy it before the next trip.
C She did not buy it, and she wishes she had.
3. What is the difference the text gives between "must" and "have to"?
A Must comes from the speaker; have to comes from a rule out in the world.
B Must is for the past and have to is for the future.
C They are identical and Americans use must more in speech.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Should, Must, Have To -- and Should Have

GRAMMAR

Advice, a rule, a ban and a free choice are four different verbs. And regret about the past has a shape of its own.

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

A friend is debriefing you about a trip that went wrong. Put their side of the conversation in a logical order.

?"So, how bad was it? Start at the beginning."
?"Wait -- you were in the right airport and the wrong terminal?"
?"You should have screenshotted the boarding pass. The wifi always dies."
?"And you did not buy travel insurance, did you."
?"Okay, but you do not have to beat yourself up about it. Everybody does this once."
?"Next time, pack light and take two adapters. That is the whole lesson."
