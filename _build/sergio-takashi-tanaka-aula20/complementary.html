"The secret structure of great talks" -- Nancy Duarte (TEDxEast)

A free talk of about eighteen minutes on the shape underneath a presentation that works: where the speaker puts the problem, where the request goes, and why the last sentence is the one the room remembers. Connection to Lesson 20: it is the argument for choosing your closing remark before you write anything else.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Stop at the end and write, in one sentence, the closing remark of the next thing you have to present.

"7 common issues people face when speaking in public" -- Life Kit, NPR

A free episode of about twenty minutes in clear American English, with the full text on the same page. The guest is a speechwriter, and her first question is the one you asked yourself today: if the room could only take one thing out of the door, what would it be? Connection to Lesson 20: it is the work you do before you decide which three sentences carry the weight.

Tip: listen once without reading, then read the transcript and mark every place where a pause is doing the work of a filler.

Video
"Cleft sentences: advanced complex sentences in English + test"

A short free grammar class on exactly the two structures you practiced today: "What the committee needs is one number" and "It was the manual step that failed". There is a short test at the end, so you find out immediately whether the singular verb and the front "It" are automatic yet. Connection to Lesson 20: it drills the form so that you can spend the ten minutes thinking about the room instead of the grammar.

Tip: pause after each example and rebuild it with something from your own quarter. Say it out loud -- these sentences only work when you hear where the weight falls.
