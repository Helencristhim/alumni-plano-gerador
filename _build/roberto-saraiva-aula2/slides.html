Chapter 3: The Code

The Tense of a Closed Quarter

Finished, measured, and never coming back

Discovery

What Do These Four Verbs Share?

"Revenue grew eleven percent last quarter."

"The cost of packaging increased in April."

"We missed the target by two million."

"The bottom line did not move at all."

Two of the red verbs end in -ed and two do not. And in the last one, the -ed disappears completely.

UseFormExample
Regular verbverb + -ed, for every subjectThe cost increased. We missed it.
Irregular verba different word, learned one by onegrow → grew · fall → fell · make → made
Negativedid not + naked verbIt did not move. We did not reach it.
QuestionDid + subject + naked verb?Did revenue grow in the second quarter?
Time wordsa finished periodlast quarter, in April, two years ago, in 2025
The trapthe -ed lives in did, never twiceNot did not moved. Not the margin is fell.

In one line: -ed for regular verbs, a new word for irregular ones, and after DID the verb goes back to naked.

Listening 1 · The Message to the Team

Good Quarter, One Problem

The finance director recorded this for the whole sales team. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What were the two good numbers, and what was the bad one?
Revenue grew eleven percent and volume grew eight percent. The margin fell two points.
2. What reason does he give for the bad number?
Packaging costs increased in April and nobody changed the price list after that.
3. What does he ask the sales team to bring to the next meeting?
The three accounts where they gave the biggest discounts last quarter, and the reason for each one.
Listening 2 · The Analyst

The Same Numbers, From Outside

Somebody who does not work there, reading the same page. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Which number does she say the market looked at, and which one did she look at?
The market looked at the revenue growth. She looked at the margin.
2. What is her explanation for what happened to the margin?
The company bought growth with discounts: it sold more units at a lower price instead of raising the price list.
3. What will she watch in the next quarter, and why?
Whether the margin comes back without losing the volume — because that is the only way to know if the discounts were a decision or an accident.

1. Revenue went up and the bottom line did not. Which two lines explain that?
The cost of packaging, up fourteen percent since April, and the gross margin, down two points.
2. How far was the company from its target, and did it reach it?
It did not reach it. Revenue was 41.2 million against a target of 43.0, so it missed by 1.8 million.
3. What does the forecast at the bottom promise for the third quarter?
44.5 million of revenue, with the margin back at twenty-nine percent.
