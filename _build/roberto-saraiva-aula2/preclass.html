Lesson 02 -- Pre-class

Reading the Quarter -- The Numbers, and What Happened to Them

Three months, one page of numbers, and somebody asking what happened. Key words: revenue, a cost, a profit, a margin, growth, a target, a forecast, to break even, the bottom line. Structure: the past simple, regular and irregular.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Revenue -- all the money a company receives from its sales, before any cost
"Revenue grew eleven percent in the second quarter."
A cost -- the money a company spends to make or to sell something
"The cost of packaging rose again in April."
A profit -- what is left when you take the costs off the revenue
"We made a small profit, and nobody expected it."
A margin -- the part of the price that is profit, written as a percentage
"Our margin fell two points, and the reason was the dollar."
Growth -- the increase in a number, compared with the same period before
"Volume growth came from two regions only."
A target -- the number a company decides it wants to reach in a period
"We missed the target by two million."
A forecast -- the number you expect for a period that has not happened yet
"The forecast for the third quarter was too optimistic."
To break even -- to earn exactly what you spend: no profit and no loss
"The new line broke even in nine months."
The bottom line -- the final profit, on the last line of the report
"Sales looked great, and the bottom line did not move."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The second quarter closed on the thirtieth of June. On the first page it looked like the best three months in three years. Revenue grew eleven percent and volume grew eight. The new line, which the company launched in January, broke even in month five.

The second page told a slower story. The company set a target of forty-three million and did not reach it. The cost of packaging increased fourteen percent in April, and nobody changed the price list. The division absorbed all of it.

So the margin fell two points, and the bottom line did not move at all. The company sold more products to more customers and finished the quarter with the same profit it started with. The forecast for the third quarter is a different conversation.

1. "Revenue grew eleven percent." Why is it grew and not growed?
A Because the subject is singular.
B Because grow is an irregular verb: its past is a different word, not verb plus -ed.
C Because the sentence is a question.
2. "The company did not reach the target." Why is it reach and not reached?
A Because did already carries the past. After did, the verb goes back to its naked form.
B Because reach is an irregular verb.
C Because the sentence is negative, and negatives never use -ed anywhere.
3. The whole text is in the past. What tells you the period is finished?
A Nothing. The quarter could still be open.
B The word forecast, at the end.
C The closed time reference: the quarter closed on the thirtieth of June, and April is a month inside it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Past Simple

GRAMMAR

One tense for everything that is finished, measured and closed. It is the tense every results report is written in.

UseFormExample
Regular verbverb + -ed, for every subjectThe cost increased. We missed it.
Irregular verba different word, learned one by onegrow → grew · fall → fell · break → broke · sell → sold
Negativedid not + naked verbIt did not move. We did not reach it.
QuestionDid + subject + naked verb?Did revenue grow in the second quarter?
Time wordsa finished periodlast quarter, in April, two years ago, in 2025
Short answerYes, it did. / No, it did not.Did you reach the target? No, we did not.

Never: Revenue growed eleven percent · We did not reached the target · Did the line broke even? · The margin is fell · We have missed the target last quarter.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

The finance director asks what revenue did in a quarter that is already closed. You answer:
A "Revenue is growing eleven percent last quarter."
B "Revenue grew eleven percent last quarter."
C "Revenue growed eleven percent last quarter."
You did not hit the number, and you want to say so before he asks. You say:
A "We did not reach the target, and I can tell you why."
B "We did not reached the target."
C "We not reach the target."
You want to ask whether the new product paid for itself last year. You ask:
A "Did the new line broke even last year?"
B "Did the new line break even last year?"
C "The new line broke even last year?"
Sales went up and the final profit stayed the same. You explain:
A "The revenue was good, so the profit was good."
B "The bottom line is moved by the forecast."
C "We sold more and we kept less: the margin fell, so the bottom line did not move."
