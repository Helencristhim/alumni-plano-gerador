Key words: constructive criticism, praise, feedback sandwich, point out, room for improvement, blunt, tactful, defensive, come across, pat on the back, keep up the good work, take it personally. Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Constructive criticism -- comments about a problem that help someone do better
"Good managers give constructive criticism, not just complaints."
Praise -- words that say someone did a good job
"The director’s praise made Ana’s week."
Feedback sandwich -- praise, then the problem, then praise again
"I used the feedback sandwich to talk about the late report."
Point out -- to tell someone about something they did not notice
"Can I point out one small mistake on page two?"
Room for improvement -- the possibility for something to be better
"The report is good, but there is room for improvement in the summary."
Blunt -- saying what you think very directly, without being gentle
"His comment was so blunt that Ana felt bad."
Tactful -- careful not to upset people when you say something difficult
"She was tactful and started with what went well."
Defensive -- reacting as if you are attacked when someone criticizes you
"Try not to get defensive when your manager gives you feedback."
Come across -- to seem to other people in a certain way
"You come across as very confident on calls."
Pat on the back -- praise or thanks for doing something well
"The director gave the team a pat on the back after the deal."
Keep up the good work -- what you say to encourage someone to continue doing well
"Your reports are excellent. Keep up the good work!"
Take it personally -- to feel hurt because you think criticism is about you as a person
"The comment was about the email, so don’t take it personally."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Tuesday, Ana had her first call with Mr. Becker, a coffee buyer in Hamburg. After the call, Mr. Becker phoned Walyson. Later, Walyson told Ana what the buyer had said: “He said that your prices were very clear. But he told me that he hadn’t received the samples.”

This is reported speech. When we tell someone what another person said, the verb usually goes one step back in time: are becomes were, need becomes needed, will becomes would, can becomes could, and haven’t received becomes hadn’t received. The people change too: Mr. Becker said “I”, but Walyson says “he”.

Be careful with say and tell. We say something: He said that the coffee was excellent. We tell someone: He told me that the coffee was excellent. With a person after the verb, use told: never “he said me”.

1. Mr. Becker said: “Your prices are very clear.” How does Walyson report it?
A He said that your prices were very clear.
B He said that your prices are very clear, and then he talked about the samples and the order.
C He said me that your prices were very clear.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A He told that he will confirm the order after the tasting on the 14th.
B He said me that he would confirm the order.
C He told me that he would confirm the order.
3. Why does Walyson say “he hadn’t received the samples”?
A Because the samples arrived in Hamburg on Friday morning, before the call with Ana.
B Because the verb goes one step back when we report what someone said.
C Because every sentence in English needs the word had.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

The verb goes one step back. You say something, but you tell someone.

Direct speechReported speech
“The samples are late.”She said that the samples were late.
“I need them next week.”He told me that he needed them the next week.
“We are working on it.”They said that they were working on it.
“I have sent the report.” / “I sent it.”She said that she had sent the report.
“I will call.” / “I can call.”He said that he would call. / He said that he could call.
“I don’t like the price.”He told us that he didn’t like the price.

Remember: said + (that) + sentence · told + person + (that) + sentence. Never “said me”.

Stage 2: Put the Feedback in Order

Order

You give feedback to a new colleague with the feedback sandwich. Put the sentences in a natural order: ask, praise, point out the problem, suggest, reassure and close.

?"Next time, please tell me early if you need more time."
?"Don’t take it personally. It’s a small thing."
?"Overall, it was a great first report. Keep up the good work!"
?"First, you did a great job with the numbers. They were very clear."
?"I want to point out one thing: the report was two days late."
?"Do you have a minute? I’d like to give you some feedback on the report."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A new analyst sent a report with good numbers but two days late. You say:
A Your report was late. Again.
B It's fine.
C The numbers were great. One thing: it was two days late. Can you tell me early next time?
Your director says your slides had too much text. You say:
A Thanks for pointing that out. Which slide should I change first?
B That is not true, because I worked on those slides all weekend and everybody liked them in the meeting.
C You are wrong.
The buyer told you on the phone: “I can’t visit Santos in May.” You tell your manager:
A He said me that he can't visit Santos in May, so maybe we need to plan a video call with him and the team.
B He told me that he couldn't visit Santos in May.
C He told that he couldn't visit Santos.
Ana asks: “Was my presentation OK?” It was good, but she spoke very fast. You say:
A It was terrible. You spoke too fast and nobody understood anything.
B Yes.
C You come across as very confident. There's some room for improvement in your speed, but keep up the good work!
