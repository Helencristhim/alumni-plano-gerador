Chapter 3: Who Said What?

He Said the Samples Were Late

Reported speech: how to tell someone what another person said

Grammar Discovery

Look at the Verbs

Mr. Becker: “Your prices are very clear.”

→ He said that my prices were very clear.

Mr. Becker: “I need the samples by next week.”

→ He told me that he needed the samples by the next week.

Mr. Becker: “I will confirm the order.”

→ He said that he would confirm the order.

Mr. Becker: “We haven’t received the samples.”

→ He told me that they hadn’t received the samples.

Look at the blue words. What happens to the verb when you report it? And when do we use told instead of said?

He saysYou reportExample
am / is / arewas / were“It is late.” → He said it was late.
present simplepast simple“I need it.” → He said he needed it.
present continuouspast continuous“I’m working.” → She said she was working.
present perfect / past simplepast perfect“I sent it.” → She said she had sent it.
will / canwould / could“I can call.” → He said he could call.
say or tell?said (that) · told + person (that)He said that... · He told me that...

In one line: the verb goes one step back in time; I and you change to he, she or they; and you say something, but you tell someone.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Ana Asks for Feedback

One conversation, two recordings, one email from Hamburg

Dialogue

Ana’s First Client Call

A
Walyson, do you have a minute? I'd like some feedback on my call with Mr. Becker.
W
Sure, Ana. First, you did a great job with the numbers. Mr. Becker said that your prices were very clear.
A
Really? That's a nice pat on the back. And what didn't go well?
W
Well, there's some room for improvement in the follow-up. He told me that he hadn't received the samples yet.
A
Oh no. I sent them on Friday, I think. Did he say anything else?
W
He said that he was happy with our service, but he needed the samples by next week. It's a small thing, so please don't take it personally.
A
I won't. Honestly, I prefer constructive criticism. My last manager was very blunt, and I always got defensive.
W
I understand. Let's check the tracking number together after lunch. And you come across as very confident on calls.
A
Thanks, Walyson. You were really tactful. I'll call Mr. Becker this afternoon.
W
Great. Keep up the good work, Ana.
Comprehension

Feedback on her first call with Mr. Becker, the buyer in Hamburg.

On Friday.
3. What was Ana’s last manager like, and how did she react?
He was very blunt, and she always got defensive.
Listening 1 · A Coach

The Secret of a Good Sandwich

A leadership coach on giving feedback that people really hear. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the biggest mistake people make with the feedback sandwich?
They make the middle too small, so the problem is hidden between two compliments.
2. What should feedback talk about: the work or the person?
The work. Then people do not take it personally or get defensive.
3. What is the difference between tactful and vague?
Tactful feedback is kind and clear at the same time; vague feedback does not help anybody.
Listening 2 · A Trader

The Feedback That Changed Me

An experienced trader remembers the first feedback of his career. Sound first — no text.

1. What did Mrs. Lima say about his slides and the client?
She said that his slides were very clear, and that the client had liked his numbers.
2. Which two problems did she point out?
She told him that he spoke too fast and that he never looked at the audience.
3. What did she say she would do?
She said that she would watch his next presentation and give him one more tip.
