Lesson 18 -- Pre-class

Numbers That Say Something -- One Figure, One Comparison

The panel will ask how big your company is. What it remembers is not how many numbers you gave, but what one number meant. Key words: revenue, a backlog, a cost overrun, a headline figure, a breakdown, a ballpark figure, to soar, to plummet, to level off, to peak, steady, to put something into perspective. Structure: describing numbers and change -- rise by and rise to, an increase of and an increase in, and approximators like just under, well over and in the region of.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Revenue -- the total money a company receives from its sales, before costs are taken out
"Revenue rose last year, but so did the cost of steel."
A backlog -- work that has been signed or ordered but not done yet
"Our backlog is about two years of work at the current pace."
A cost overrun -- the amount by which the final cost goes above the budget
"The average cost overrun on our projects fell to under five percent."
A headline figure -- the main number that people see first and remember
"The headline figure was revenue, but it was not the interesting one."
A breakdown -- a list that shows how a total is divided into its parts
"If the panel wants the breakdown, they will ask for it."
A ballpark figure -- a number that is not exact but is close enough to be useful
"Give me a ballpark figure: are we talking about ten million or a hundred?"
To soar -- to rise very fast to a high level
"Steel prices soared in 2021, and every budget on the site moved with them."
To plummet -- to fall very fast and very far
"Margins plummeted during the downturn and took three years to recover."
To level off -- to stop rising or falling and stay at about the same level
"After two good years, our margins have leveled off at around nine percent."
To peak -- to reach the highest point before starting to fall
"Overruns on our projects peaked three years ago."
Steady -- changing slowly and regularly, without sudden jumps
"Growth has been steady: around eight percent a year for five years."
To put something into perspective -- to compare something with something else so that its real size is clear
"To put it into perspective, the industry average is three times higher."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

When the panel asks how big your company is, the grammar of numbers matters as much as the numbers. The first thing to get right is the little word after the verb. Revenue rose by 18 percent tells the listener the size of the change. Revenue rose to 485 million tells them where it arrived. Brazilian speakers often say "rose of", because Portuguese says subiu de. In English it is always by for the change and to for the new level, and from ... to, never from ... until, for the start and the end.

The noun works the same way, with two different prepositions: an increase of 18 percent (the size) in revenue (the thing that changed). And after a number, million never takes an -s: 485 million reais, not "485 millions". You only say millions of when there is no number in front of it.

The second thing to get right is precision. An interview is not an audit. Round the number and tell the listener that you rounded it: just under 485 million, well over 900 million, in the region of 15 percent. Then add one comparison, so the panel knows whether the number is big or small. One figure, one comparison, and stop.

1. According to the text, what is the difference between "rose by" and "rose to"?
A There is no difference in meaning.
B "By" gives the size of the change; "to" gives the new level.
C "To" is only used with percentages.
2. Which sentence is correct, according to the text?
A "We have 485 millions in contracts."
B "There was an increase of 18 percent in revenue."
C "Revenue rose of 18 percent."
3. What does the last paragraph recommend?
A Give every number with its decimals, so the panel trusts you.
B Round the number, say that you rounded it, and add one comparison.
C Avoid numbers completely in an interview.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Numbers and Change

GRAMMAR

By is the change, to is where you arrive. Of is the size, in is the thing. Round it, and say that you rounded it.

ToolWhat it meansExample
rise / fall bythe size of the changeRevenue rose by 18 percent.
rise / fall to; from ... tothe new level; the start and the endOverruns fell from 11 to 4 percent.
an increase ofthe size, as a nounan increase of 18 percent
an increase inthe thing that changedan increase in revenue
just under / almosta little less than the numberjust under 485 million
well over / in the region ofmuch more than / close to the numberwell over 900 million

Never: rose of 18 percent · 485 millions · an increase of revenue · from 11 until 4 percent · 484.6 million in an interview

Stage 2: Put the Answer in Order

Order

This is a numbers answer in the order a panel wants it: the headline figure, the comparison, then the number underneath.

?"Revenue rose by 18 percent last year, to just under 485 million."
?"To put that into perspective, the market grew by about the same."
?"The number I watch is the backlog, and it is well over 900 million."
?"That is about two years of work at our current pace."
?"Cost overruns fell from 11 percent to 4 in the same period."
?"The industry average is in the region of 15 percent."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The panel asks how big your company is. Which answer is the strongest?
A "Revenue was 484.6 million reais, headcount 611, and we had 23 active projects."
B "Revenue is just under 500 million reais, which puts us among the larger builders in the state."
C "It is quite big, and it grew a lot."
Revenue went from 410 million to 485 million. You say:
A "Revenue rose of 410 to 485 millions."
B "Revenue rose from 410 million to 485 million."
C "Revenue rose from 410 million until 485 million."
The interviewer asks about a bad year. Which answer puts the number into perspective?
A "Our margins plummeted in 2016, like everybody else’s. It was a very difficult year."
B "Our margins fell to 3 percent in 2016, when the industry average was below zero."
C "I would prefer not to talk about 2016."
You want to say the backlog is 912 million without giving the exact figure. You say:
A "The backlog is well over 900 million."
B "The backlog is well under 900 million."
C "The backlog is 900 millions, more or less."

Reflection
