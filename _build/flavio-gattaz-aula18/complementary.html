Video
Jeff Su -- "7 Effective Tips for Presenting Data at Work"

A former Google product marketer on how to show numbers so that people remember them: fewer figures, one clear comparison, and the headline first. American English at a natural pace, very practical.

Tip: pause after each tip and apply it to one number from your company. Say it out loud with a comparison.

Podcast
No Stupid Questions (Freakonomics) -- "Why Are Stories Stickier Than Statistics?"

A psychologist and an economist discuss why people remember a story and forget a number — and what you can do to make a number stick. It is the reason behind today’s rule: one figure, one comparison.

Tip: every time you hear a number, write down how it is compared with something. Then do the same with your headline figure.

Talk
TED -- Hans Rosling, "The best stats you’ve ever seen"

A famous statistician turns decades of data into a story that anybody can follow. Listen to how he describes numbers rising, falling and leveling off — it is today’s grammar at full speed.

Tip: write down every verb he uses for change (rise, fall, level off, peak...). Then describe your company’s last five years with three of them.
