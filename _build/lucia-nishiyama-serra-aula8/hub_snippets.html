Passive Voice for Executive Precision -- Framing Decisions and Impact

Putting the decision and the result first, the way an executive memo does. Key words: to attribute, credit, to streamline, oversight, to delegate, to escalate, rationale, deliverable. Structure: the passive voice (be + past participle) across present, past, present perfect, modal, and future -- plus when to add "by + agent" and when to switch back to the active voice.

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To attribute -- to say who or what caused or created something
"The delay was attributed to a change in the compliance rules."
Credit -- public recognition for good work that someone has done
"The credit should be shared with the whole team."
To streamline -- to make a process simpler and faster
"The approval process was streamlined last quarter."
Oversight -- the work of watching a project so the rules are followed
"Nothing is shipped without oversight."
To delegate -- to give a task or a decision to someone else on your team
"The daily checks are delegated to the team leads."
To escalate -- to pass a problem to a more senior level for a decision
"The issue was escalated to the vice president the same day."
Rationale -- the reasons and the thinking behind a decision
"The rationale is explained in the first paragraph."
Deliverable -- a specific result that a team must produce and hand over
"Two deliverables have been completed ahead of schedule."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Lucia is writing the quarterly memo. She does not start with herself; she starts with the result. "The approval process was streamlined last quarter," she writes. "Two deliverables have been completed ahead of schedule, and the rationale is explained on page two." Notice the pattern. The passive voice is always a form of be plus a past participle, and only be changes with the tense. When one issue appeared in March, it was escalated to the vice president the same day; the delay was attributed to a change in the rules. The daily checks are delegated to the team leads, and every new model must be reviewed before release, so nothing is shipped without oversight. Then she names people on purpose: the framework was designed by four engineers, so the credit is theirs -- and the final call was made by her.

1. Why does Lucia write "The approval process was streamlined" instead of "My team streamlined the approval process"?
A In a memo, the result matters more than the name of the person who did the work.
B Because the passive voice is more polite than the active voice.
C Because her team did not really do the work.
2. In "Two deliverables have been completed", which word carries the tense?
A "completed" -- the participle changes with the tense.
B "have been" -- the tense always lives on the verb "be".
C "deliverables" -- the subject sets the tense.
3. Why does she add "by four engineers" at the end?
A Because "by" is required in every passive sentence.
B Because the reader would not understand the sentence without it.
C Because there the doer is the point -- she wants the credit to go to them.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive Voice

GRAMMAR

How to put the result first in every tense: be + past participle.

FormUseExample
Present
is / are + participle		A routine or a rule.The rationale is explained on page two.
Past
was / were + participle		A finished decision or action.The issue was escalated the same day.
Present perfect
has / have been + participle		A past result that matters now.Two deliverables have been completed.
Modal / future
must / should / will be + participle		A rule, advice, or a plan.Every new model must be reviewed. / It will be escalated.
by + agent
(optional)		Add it only when the doer is the point: "The framework was designed by four engineers." Leave it out when the doer is obvious, unknown, or not the point. Negative: "The report was not reviewed." Question: "Was the issue escalated?" A verb with no object (happen, arrive, rise) has no passive.
