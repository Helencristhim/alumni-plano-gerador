Chapter 4: The Passive Code

Be + Past Participle

Present, past, present perfect, modal, future

The Passive Map

One Pattern, Five Tenses

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The approval process was streamlined last quarter."

"The rationale is explained in the first paragraph."

"Two deliverables have been completed ahead of schedule."

"Every new model must be reviewed, and the framework was designed by four engineers."

What do all the highlighted groups have in common?

PartFormExample
The subjectthe thing that receives the actionThe approval process ...
be (the tense lives here)is/are · was/were · has/have been · must be · will be... was ... / ... have been ...
The past participlenever changes: streamlined, explained, completed, reviewed, made... streamlined last quarter.
by + agent (optional)Add it only when the doer is the point: "The framework was designed by four engineers." Leave it out when the doer is obvious, unknown, or simply not the point.

Question: Was the issue escalated? · Negative: The report was not reviewed.

Detail

Active vs Passive

ACTIVE = the person matters

Credit and responsibility.

"My team streamlined the approval process."

"I made that final call."

PASSIVE = the result matters

Impact, process, and rules.

"The approval process was streamlined."

"Every new model must be reviewed."

In a memo, lead with the result. In an interview, lead with you.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"The decision made last week by the board."
"The decision was made last week by the board."
"The memo was wrote by my team."
"The memo was written by my team."
"The delay was happened in March."
"The delay happened in March."

The passive always needs be + the past participle. And a verb with no object -- happen, arrive, rise -- has no passive at all.

Dialogue

The Quarterly Memo

M
Lucia, your quarterly memo landed this morning. Walk me through what actually changed.
L
The approval process was streamlined last quarter, and two deliverables have been completed ahead of schedule.
M
And the review that was flagged in March?
L
It was escalated to the vice president the same day, and the rationale is explained on page two.
M
Who owns that decision now?
L
The daily checks are delegated to the team leads, but the final call was made by me.
M
Good. And what is the rule going forward?
L
Every new model must be reviewed before release, so nothing is shipped without oversight. And the credit should be shared with the team.
M
That is the clearest update I have read all quarter.
Did You Catch It?

About the Review

1. What did Mark read this morning?
The quarterly memo, and he asks what actually changed.
2. What does Mark ask about after the two deliverables?
The review that was flagged in March, and then who owns that decision now.
3. How does Mark end the conversation?
He says it is the clearest update he has read all quarter.
Listening 1

The Sixty-Second Update

An executive reports the quarter. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What was streamlined, and what did that save?
The approval process, last quarter -- two full weeks were cut from the cycle.
2. What happened to the issue that was flagged in March?
It was escalated to the vice president the same day it appeared.
3. Who does the speaker say designed the framework?
Four engineers -- not one manager, so the credit should be shared with the whole team.
Listening 2

A Coach's One Test

A writing coach shares one test. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What question does she tell you to ask before writing?
What the reader should notice first: the result, or the person who produced it.
2. What is wrong with "Mistakes were made"?
Nobody is named, so it sounds like cover, not precision.
3. What is her rule when responsibility is yours?
Switch back to the active voice and say it: "That decision was made by me."
