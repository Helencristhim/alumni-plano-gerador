Chapter 4: The Code

The Clause That Narrows, and the One That Only Adds

Two commas, two different groups of people

Grammar Discovery

How Many People Does Each Sentence Cover?

"The photographers who signed before 2020 will be paid a fee."

"The photographers, who signed before 2020, will be paid a fee."

"The clause that everything turns on is number four."

"Clause four, which nobody read, is where the whole case lives."

Two of these sentences narrow the group they are talking about. Two do not narrow anything at all: they name one thing and then add a fact about it. The only visible difference is the blue punctuation.

In one line: no commas narrows the group; two commas keeps the whole group and adds a fact. In a contract that is not punctuation, it is money.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

The Clause, and Two Voices

Two drafts side by side, two recordings

Real Document

The Same Clause, Twice

CLAUSE 4 — TWO DRAFTS
annotated by Ana Ladeia Tavares

Draft A. "The contributors who delivered their work before 1 March shall be paid the full fee."

Draft B. "The contributors, who delivered their work before 1 March, shall be paid the full fee."

Both drafts came from the same firm, three days apart, and nobody flagged the difference.

Margin note: Draft A pays only the contributors who delivered before 1 March. Draft B pays all of them and states, as a fact, that they all delivered before 1 March, which is not true. Eleven of the nineteen delivered in April. On Draft B we owe eleven fees we never agreed to. This is not a typographical question.
1. Under Draft A, who gets paid, and under Draft B?
Under Draft A, only the contributors who delivered before 1 March. Under Draft B, all nineteen of them, because the clause between the commas does not narrow the group.
2. The note says Draft B also states something that is not true. What is it?
That all of them delivered before 1 March. A non-defining clause presents its content as a fact about the whole group, and eleven of the nineteen delivered in April.
3. Draft a third version out loud that removes the ambiguity completely.
Something like: the full fee shall be paid only to those contributors who delivered their work on or before 1 March. Naming the date twice and adding "only" leaves nothing for a comma to decide.
Listening 1 · The Drafter

A Comma Is Not Punctuation

A lawyer who drafts contracts, on the mark that costs the most. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say happens when a junior asks him about punctuation?
He says he stops the conversation, because in a clause the comma is not punctuation. It decides who is inside the group and who is outside it.
2. What test does he give for checking a relative clause?
Cover the clause with your hand and read what is left. If the sentence still means what you want, the clause is only adding a fact and takes commas. If the sentence goes too wide, the clause is doing work and must not have them.
3. What does he say about the drafts that cause litigation?
That they are almost never the badly written ones. They are the ones where two competent people read the same correct sentence in two different ways and neither of them was being unreasonable.
Listening 2 · The Plain-Language Lawyer

Long Is Not the Same as Safe

A lawyer who rewrites contracts in plain English, on why length is not protection. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say most people believe about long contracts, and what does she say instead?
People believe that a longer contract is a safer one. She says length adds places for two readings to hide, and that most of the length is boilerplate that nobody has read since it was first copied.
2. What happened when her team rewrote a standard agreement in plain English?
It came out at less than half the length, and the disputes about what it meant stopped almost entirely. The clients also started reading it, which nobody had expected.
3. What does she say to students who are afraid of writing simply?
That simple and vague are opposite things, and that the fear is really about sounding like a lawyer rather than about being understood. She says a watertight clause is usually a short one.
