Taking the Floor

You understand the discussion. You have a point. But the conversation is fast, and the moment passes. This lesson gives you the short, polite phrases that open the door.

Chapter 1: First Words

The Moment That Passes

In the last two lessons you presented yourself and planned a meeting. Now the meeting is happening, people are speaking fast, and you want to say something. What do you say first?

Think of your last meeting in English. Was there a moment when you wanted to speak and did not? What happened?

Today’s Goal

Three Missions

1

Ten words and expressions for joining a conversation.

2

Ask, interrupt and ask for permission in a polite way.

3

Take your turn in a fast discussion, and get it back when somebody interrupts you.

The target of the day: Could I just add something? opens the door; Would you mind repeating that? gets you the information again; Can I finish my point? keeps your turn. Short, polite, and ready before the call.

One Question

Direct, or Polite?

Two sentences, out loud, right now.

Ask a colleague to send you a file. First say it the way you say it to a close friend. Then say it the way you say it to a partner you met last week. Do not think about grammar yet.

Chapter 3: The Code

Can, Could, Would, May

The small words that make a request polite

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Questions Show?

"Can I ask a question?"

"Could you send me the list after the call?"

"Would you mind repeating the date?"

"May I add something?"

Look at the blue words: they make the question polite. Now look at the red verbs. After can, could and may, the verb is simple. After would you mind, the verb takes -ing.

JobFormExample
Permission, informalCan I + verb?Can I ask a question?
Permission, more politeCould I / May I + verb?Could I add something?
Asking someone to do somethingCould you / Would you + verb?Could you send me the list?
Very polite requestWould you mind + verb-ing?Would you mind repeating the date?
Keeping your turnCan I just + verb?Can I just finish my point?
The trapno to after can, could, would, mayNot Could you to send. Say Could you send.

In one line: can, could, would and may + simple verb; would you mind + -ing.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Discussion, at Real Speed

One call, three recordings, one quick guide

Dialogue

The Moment to Speak

D
So the plan is to invite twenty companies from Ontario, and the first visit is in March. Any questions?
J
Yes. Sorry, could I just chip in here?
D
Of course, go ahead.
J
Thank you. I did not catch the number. Would you mind repeating it?
D
Not at all. Twenty companies. And they are mostly from the food sector.
J
Great. Could you clarify one thing? Is March the first visit, or the only visit?
D
Good question. March is the first visit. We are planning a second one in the fall. Sorry, I was not very clear.
J
No problem. Then I would like to bring up the dates of our events. May I share my screen?
Comprehension

About Daniel

1. How many companies does Daniel want to invite, and from where?
Twenty companies, from Ontario.
2. Which sector are most of the companies from?
The food sector.
3. How many visits is Daniel planning?
Two: the first in March and a second one in the fall.
Listening 1 · The Facilitator

How to Get Into a Fast Discussion

A meeting facilitator explains how people from other countries can join a fast discussion. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say most people wait for?
The perfect moment, and it never comes.
2. What short phrase does she recommend to start?
Could I just add something?
3. What does she say about raising your hand online?
It is a good idea: the person who runs the call sees it and gives you the floor.
Listening 2 · The Partner

When I Do Not Catch Something

A business partner in Mexico City talks about asking people to repeat. Sound first — no text.

1. Why did he stop asking people to repeat, some years ago?
He thought it was not professional.
2. What happened because of that?
He sent a proposal with the wrong date.
3. What does he say now when he does not understand?
Sorry, I did not catch that. Would you mind repeating it?
Listening 3 · The Call Rules

The Rules of Our Calls

A recorded message that opens the calls of an international business network. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the message ask people to do when they are not speaking?
To be on mute.
2. How do people ask for the floor in these calls?
They raise their hand or write in the chat.
3. What does the message say about asking someone to repeat?
It is always welcome, because many members speak English as a second language.
