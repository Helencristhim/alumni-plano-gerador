Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

Taking the Floor -- Joining the Conversation Politely

You understand the discussion and you have a point: now you need the phrase that opens the door. Key words: to chip in, to go ahead, to bring up, to clarify, to rephrase, to hold on, to speak up, to be on mute, to catch, to take turns. Structure: polite requests with can, could, would, may and would you mind + -ing.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To chip in -- to add a comment to a conversation that other people started
"Can I chip in here? I have some information about that company."
To go ahead -- to start or continue speaking, often after someone gives permission
"Sure, go ahead, Juliana. We are listening."
To bring up -- to start talking about a subject in a meeting
"I would like to bring up the date of the event."
To clarify -- to make something easier to understand by explaining it again
"Could you clarify the second point, please?"
To rephrase -- to say the same idea again with different words
"Let me rephrase that. I mean the partners from Montreal."
To hold on -- to wait for a short time
"Hold on a second. I am opening the document."
To speak up -- to speak louder, or to give your opinion when others are quiet
"Could you speak up a little? The sound is low."
To be on mute -- to have your microphone off in an online call
"Sorry, I was on mute. Can you hear me now?"
To catch -- to hear or understand what someone says
"Sorry, I did not catch the last part."
To take turns -- to speak one after the other, in order
"Let us take turns, so everybody can speak."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The call is fast. Daniel is talking about a visit from Canada, and Juliana has a question. She does not wait for a silence. “Could I just chip in here?” she says. Daniel stops. “Sure, go ahead.”

Juliana did not catch the number of companies. “Would you mind repeating it?” she asks. Daniel repeats it, and she asks one more thing: “Could you clarify the date of the first visit?”

Notice the pattern. After can, could, would and may, the verb is simple, with no to. After would you mind, the verb takes -ing.

1. Why does Juliana say “Could I just chip in here?”
A To end the call.
B To join the conversation politely, without waiting for a silence.
C To ask Daniel to speak louder.
2. What did Juliana not catch?
A The number of companies.
B The name of the city.
C The time of the call.
3. Which question is correct?
A “Would you mind to repeat it?”
B “Would you mind repeating it?”
C “Would you mind repeat it?”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Polite Requests and Permission

GRAMMAR

Can, could, would and may make a question polite. After them the verb is simple; after would you mind, the verb takes -ing.

JobFormExample
Permission, informalCan I + verb?Can I ask a question?
Permission, more politeCould I / May I + verb?Could I add something?
Asking someone to do somethingCould you / Would you + verb?Could you send me the list?
Very polite requestWould you mind + verb-ing?Would you mind repeating the date?
Keeping your turnCan I just + verb?Can I just finish my point?
The trapno to after can, could, would, mayNot Could you to send. Say Could you send.

Never: Could you to send me the list · Would you mind to repeat · May I to add something · Repeat, please (to a new partner: say Could you repeat that, please?)

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The discussion is fast and you want to speak. You say:
A "Stop, I want to talk."
B "Could I just add something here?"
C "Could I to add something?"
You did not hear a number. You say:
A "Would you mind repeating the number?"
B "Would you mind to repeat the number?"
C "Repeat."
Someone interrupts you before you finish. You say:
A "Can I just finish my point?"
B "Can I just to finish my point?"
C "You interrupt me."
Your microphone was off when you started speaking. You say:
A "Sorry, I was on mute. Can you hear me now?"
B "Sorry, I was in mute. You hear me?"
C "Sorry, I am mute."
