Documentary
The Last Dance — Episode 1

Ten episodes about a team that kept winning and a player who walked away twice, at the top, and came back. Every interview in it is somebody choosing between a finished story and one that is still running — which is the whole grammar of this lesson, spoken by people who lived it.

Tip: watch episode one with English subtitles. Write down three sentences with since or for, and three with a year in them. Then say which is which and why.

Talk
Angela Duckworth — “Grit: The power of passion and perseverance” (TED)

Six minutes on why staying with something for years predicts more than talent does. She left a demanding job to study this, so the talk is itself a turning point being described — and she narrates it in exactly the two shapes you worked on today.

Tip: watch it once for the argument. The second time, count how many times she uses I have and how many times she uses I did, and notice what changes.

Podcast
BBC Learning English — “Present perfect and past simple” (6 Minute Grammar)

Six minutes, two native speakers, on the exact choice you practised today. Short enough for the bus, and the examples are all spoken, never written — which is where you need the choice to be automatic.

Tip: pause after every example they give and say the other version out loud, then say why it would be wrong.
