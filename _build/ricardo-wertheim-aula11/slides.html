Chapter 3: The Code

What Is Always True

If you arrive early, you avoid the crowds

Grammar Discovery

Future, or Every Time?

"If you arrive early, you avoid the crowds."

"If the sun hits the facade, the stone turns almost orange."

"If you stand under the archway, the noise disappears."

"We always eat outside if the weather is good."

None of these is about tomorrow. They are about every time. And look at the tense in the if part and in the other part — what do you notice?

PartFormExample
The if partif + present simpleIf you arrive early...
The other partpresent simple again — no will...you avoid the crowds.
What it meansA general truth: every time this happens, that happens. Not a plan, not a prediction.
The orderEither half can come first. With if first you need a comma: If the sun hits it, the stone turns orange · The stone turns orange if the sun hits it.
Against what you knowIf you arrive early tomorrow, you will avoid the crowds is a prediction about one day. Take the will out and it becomes a law of the place. That one word is the whole difference.

This is how a guide talks about a building, and how you explain a city to somebody who has never been there.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"If you will arrive early, you avoid the crowds."
"If you arrive early, you avoid the crowds."
"If the sun hit the facade, the stone turn orange."
"If the sun hits the facade, the stone turns orange."
"If you are standing under the archway, the noise is disappearing."
"If you stand under the archway, the noise disappears."

One tense on both sides, and it is the simple one: no will after if, the s stays for he, she and it, and no -ing for something that is always true.

Chapter 4: One Phone Call

She Is Standing in the Square

No photograph, no map, and a bad connection

Dialogue

Elena Describes the Square

E
I am in the square now, and I am not going to send you a photograph, because it never works. Close your eyes for a second.
R
All right. Where are you standing exactly?
E
Under an archway on the north side, in the shade. If you stand here, the noise of the street stops and you hear water instead.
R
Water. So there is a fountain. What is in front of you?
E
Four columns, and they do not match. Somebody took them from an older building. The facade behind them is completely plain, and the stone is weathered on the left side only.
R
Weathered on one side. Because of the wind from the sea?
E
Exactly. And then, high above the door, there is one small mosaic, gold and blue and completely ornate. It is the kind of place where nobody looks up, so nobody has ever photographed it.
Comprehension

About the Square

1. Where exactly is Elena standing, and what happens to the sound there?
She is under an archway on the north side, in the shade. If you stand there, the noise of the street stops and you hear water instead.
2. What does she say about the four columns and the facade behind them?
The columns do not match, because somebody took them from an older building. The facade behind them is completely plain, and the stone is weathered on the left side only.
3. Why has nobody ever photographed the mosaic?
Because it is high above the door, and it is the kind of place where nobody looks up.
Chapter 5: Practice

Two People Who Do This for a Living

A guide inside a cathedral, and a local who refuses to recommend a photograph

Listening

With Your Back to the Door

A guide who has started every tour in the same spot for eleven years. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is different about the light at ten in the morning and at four in the afternoon?
At ten the light lands on the mosaic above the altar. At four it lands on the floor instead, and nobody looks up at all.
2. Where do the columns come from, and what can you still see on the third one?
They are Roman, taken from an older temple. On the third one you can still see the marks of the sea on the stone.
3. Why is the plain ceiling not an accident?
Because everything else inside is ornate. When the eye has nowhere else to go, it goes up.
Listening 2

There Is No Good Photograph of This Street

A local answers the question she is asked every day. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Why does she say there is no good photograph of the street?
Because if you stand at the end of it, the facades look flat and grey. The phone cannot see the texture of the stone.
2. What happens if you stand under the archway and wait?
The shade is cold, the noise changes, and you start hearing the water.
3. What does she recommend doing instead of taking a photograph first?
Walking the street once with the phone in your pocket and describing it out loud to somebody who is not there. If you can make them see it, you have understood the place.
