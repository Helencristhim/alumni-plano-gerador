Lesson 3 -- I See It Differently

Talk
Julia Dhar -- "How to disagree productively and find common ground" (TED, 12 min)

A world debate champion explaining the exact move you practiced today: separate the person from the claim, say what you accept, and only then attack the part you reject. Twelve minutes, very clear delivery, and every expression from this lesson appears in it.

Tip: watch with the transcript open and copy out the three sentences she uses to concede. Then rewrite each one about a claim in your own field.

Podcast
Aesthetics Bites -- Elisabeth Schellekens Dammann on Disagreement About Taste

Fifteen minutes on the question that sits under every argument you have at a fair: when two people disagree about a work, is one of them simply wrong? Philosophy in spoken English, at conversational speed, with two people who interrupt each other politely.

Tip: pause every time the interviewer pushes back and predict how she will concede before you hear it. You will start to recognize the pattern in two or three tries.

Interview
Philosophy Bites -- Teresa Bejan on Civility

A historian of political thought on what civility actually is, and on the uncomfortable idea that it is a way of disagreeing rather than a way of avoiding disagreement. Directly useful for the register you need in a panel, and short enough for one walk.

Tip: after listening, write two sentences: one where you agree with her and one where you do not. Bring the second one to class.
