BBC 6 Minute English — "Why are we all so stressed?"

Six minutes on the difference between something being hard and there being too much of it at once, which is the exact distinction Priya makes about the first term. Free, with the full transcript on the same page.

Tip: listen once, then find three relative clauses in the transcript. Say for each one whether it tells you which thing, or whether it is only extra detail.

TED on YouTube — Sir Ken Robinson, "Do schools kill creativity?" (19 min)

The most watched talk in TED’s history, and a masterclass in the thing this lesson is about: almost every sentence carries two ideas, and none of them is hard to follow.

Tip: pick any ninety seconds and count his sentences. Then count yours from the last recording over the same time. That difference is the whole lesson.

Talk
TED — Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie, "The danger of a single story" (18 min)

A novelist on arriving somewhere new and being read before you speak, which is worth hearing before your first month on a campus where nobody knows you. Her sentences are long and effortless, and that is not an accident.

Tip: this one is harder than the others. Watch it with subtitles, and write down one sentence of hers that carries three ideas without becoming confusing.
