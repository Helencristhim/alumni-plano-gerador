Lesson 18 — Pre-class

Any Questions?

About ten minutes. Eight presentation words — audience, rehearse, eye contact, stage fright, handout, topic, mumble, nod — and the polite way to ask anything: Could you tell me where it is?, Do you know if it starts at ten?

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud.

Audience -- the people who watch and listen to a presentation
"Could you tell me how big the audience was?"
Rehearse -- to practice a presentation before the real day
"Do you know if we can rehearse in the classroom?"
Eye contact -- looking at people's eyes while you talk to them
"I wonder whether eye contact is really that important."
Stage fright -- the fear you feel before you speak in front of people
"Can you tell me what you do about stage fright?"
Handout -- a paper with information that you give to everybody
"Do you know who printed the handouts?"
Topic -- the subject that you talk or write about
"Could you tell me what your topic is?"
Mumble -- to speak too quietly, so people can't understand you
"I don't know why I mumble when I'm nervous."
Nod -- to move your head up and down to say yes
"I wonder if she nodded because she liked it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read Sophia's notes before and after her presentation. The words in bold are polite questions.

Before — I have two questions for Mrs. Lee. Could you tell me how long each group has? And do you know if the projector is working?

I also wonder whether we need handouts for everybody. I hope not!

After — Lucas asked me, “Can you tell me where you found the photos?” I knew the answer, and I didn't mumble once.

"Could you tell me how long each group has?" Why is it "each group has" and not "does each group have"?
A After Could you tell me, the question uses normal sentence order.
B Because the verb have always changes to has when the question is about groups of students.
C Because it is about the past.
"Do you know if the projector is working?" What kind of question is inside it?
A A wh- question, like where or why, about the time of the presentation.
B A yes or no question.
C It is not a question.
Which sentence is correct?
A Do you know when does it start?
B Do you know when starts it, and if the room is free?
C Do you know when it starts?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Polite questions: the question becomes a sentence

  • Could you tell me / Do you know + where / what / why + subject + verb: Could you tell me where the projector is?
  • Do you know / Can you tell me + if / whether + subject + verb (yes or no questions): Do you know if we can rehearse today?
  • I wonder + if / whether / why... = a question you ask yourself: I wonder why she chose that topic.

Watch out

  • No do / does / did inside: Do you know when it starts? (not when does it start). I wonder if she liked it. (not if did she like)

Remember: your life up to now is still have: Have you ever given a presentation in English?
