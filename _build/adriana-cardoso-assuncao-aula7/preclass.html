Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These words decide what happens after everybody leaves the room.

Minutes -- the written record of what was decided in a meeting
"I want the minutes written by somebody who was not involved."
Agenda -- the list of points a meeting is going to cover
"Your case study is on the agenda for October."
Action item -- one task, with one owner and one date, agreed in a meeting
"He told me to send you the list of action items today."
To escalate -- to take a problem to somebody with more authority
"I am escalating this, and I want it on record."
Debrief -- a short meeting after an event to say what happened
"I want a debrief with the three of us in the room this week."
Off the record -- said in confidence, not to be written down or repeated
"He told me, off the record, that he had already decided to leave."
To recap -- to say again, in short, what has been agreed
"Let me recap what we agreed before we close."
Deadlock -- the point where two sides cannot agree and nothing moves
"We were in a deadlock until somebody agreed to own it."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

When you report what somebody said, the verb moves one tense back, and so do the time words.

present → pastis → was"The budget is approved." → He said the budget was approved.
perfect → past perfecthave finished → had finished"We have finished." → She said they had finished.
modalswill → would · can → could"I will present it." → She said she would present it.
questionswhether / if, no inversion"Have you considered April?" → He asked whether we had considered April.
instructionstold + person + to"Send the list." → He told me to send the list.

Two traps: say never takes a person (he said me does not exist — it is he told me or he said to me), and time words move as well: today becomes that day, next week becomes the following week.

Speaking
