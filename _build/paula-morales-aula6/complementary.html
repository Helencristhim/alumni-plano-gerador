Lesson 6 -- What Time Is It? My Week, My Schedule

Film
The Devil Wears Prada (2006) · Disney+ — included in the subscription

A woman joins a new company and the first thing she is taught to do is look after somebody else's schedule — hour by hour, appointment by appointment, deadline by deadline. The whole film is today's lesson: “you have a meeting at nine thirty”, “the deadline is tonight”, “she's busy”. We chose it for one thing that runs from start to finish: nobody there apologizes for having a full calendar. Andy learns to say the time in English before she learns anything else — and that is what keeps her job.

Tip: watch it with English subtitles — the goal here is not to understand everything. Do ONE thing only: every time someone says a time or a day (“at nine”, “on Monday”, “in November”), hit pause and repeat it out loud. Then say your own version: “My meeting is at nine thirty.” You do not have to finish the film — if you watch only the first 30 minutes, the task is done.

Podcast
Espresso English Podcast — “Don't say 'today morning'!” (~4 min, free)

Four minutes, American English, and the subject is the mistake you were about to make: today morning. That does not exist in English — the right form is this morning. Shayna is the same teacher as the lesson 1 and lesson 5 episodes, and that is on purpose: you already know her voice and her pace, so your ear is free for the content. She also shows the in the morning and in the afternoon from today's lesson, in sentences from real life.

Tip: listen the first time without stopping, just to let your ear catch the rhythm — you understand more than you think, listening is your strength. The second time, pause after each example and say YOUR version out loud, with your own week: “I have a meeting this morning.” “I'm busy in the afternoon.” The episode is short on purpose: you can listen to it twice on the way to work. Open and free, with no sign-up to listen.

YouTube
Telling Time + Practice — American English for Adult Beginners · English Explained Vids (~5 min, free)

Five minutes and the whole clock is solved — in American English, which is yours. The video has four short parts: how to say the time, a speaking practice, a.m. versus p.m. (the part that matters most for Zurich: nobody there says “17 hours”, they say five p.m.) and a final listening practice. It is the exact complement to the lesson: here you practice the HOUR, and in class you drop the hour into the sentence with at.

Tip: watch it once all the way through, without taking notes. The second time, pause at each practice and answer out loud BEFORE the video answers — it is the same game as the Pre-class. Then take your real calendar and say three of its times in English: 8:30 → “eight thirty a.m.”, 14:00 → “two p.m.”, 17:00 → “five p.m.” The channel is open, with no sign-up to watch.
