Lesson 11 · What He Said in the Rehearsal

What He Said in the Rehearsal

A note travels from one person to another. Your job is to make sure it arrives in one piece.

Chapter 1: First Words

The People Behind It, Again

One episode, sixty seconds, the people nobody sees. Use at least three of these: a go-to person, behind the scenes, a point of contact, to go unnoticed, unsung, to delegate, a handoff, to keep somebody in the loop, to step in, a stakeholder.

Then today’s question: the last time somebody passed a message to you, did it arrive exactly as it was said?

Today’s Goal

One Note, Three Moves

1. The Words

a run-through, to rush, to drag, pitchy, a key change, to clarify...

2. One Shift

what somebody said, asked and asked you to do, told by you later

3. Your Report

pass a real piece of feedback on, without changing it

When you repeat somebody else’s words later, English moves them: “You are rushing” becomes he said you were rushing.

One Question

What Did the Director Actually Say?

Think of the last note a director gave during a rehearsal or a recording. What did he say, and who did you pass it on to?

Then the harder one: did the note change on the way?

Chapter 3: The Code

The Words Move One Step Back

What he said yesterday, told by you today

Grammar Discovery

What Happens to His Words?

Marcus: “The verse is great.” → He said that the verse was great.

Marcus: “She is rushing the chorus.” → He told me that you were rushing the chorus.

Marcus: “Can she sing it lower?” → He asked whether you could sing it lower.

Marcus: “Tell her to sit back.” → He asked me to tell you to sit back on the beat.

Compare the words in quotation marks with the report. What happened to the verbs? And where did the question go in the third one?

JobFormExample
Statementssaid (that) / told somebody (that) + one step backis → was · are rushing → were rushing · can → could · will → would · has done / did → had done
Said or told?tell always needs a person · say does notHe told me that... · He said that... · He said to me that...
Yes / no questionsasked (me) if / whether + normal sentence order“Can you come?” → He asked if I could come.
Wh- questionsasked + where / what / why + normal sentence order, no do / did“Where is the singer?” → He asked where the singer was.
Requests and ordersasked / told somebody (not) to + verb“Please slow down.” → He asked him to slow down. “Do not change it.” → He told us not to change it.
Still true, or right nowthe verb can stay the same“The key is too high.” → He says the key is too high (he is saying it now).
The trapthe report is a statement, not a questionNot he said me, not he asked where was the singer, not he told to me to slow down.

In one line: when you report it later, the verb takes one step back, a question becomes a normal sentence, and a request becomes to + verb.

She thought that she was dragging, not rushing.

Listening 1 · Same Note, Different Temperature

You Carry the Message, in One Piece

A vocal coach who stands between the director and the singers. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the director say during the run-through, and what did she tell the singer instead?
He said that the singer was rushing the chorus again. She told the singer that the band wanted a little more space in the chorus, and she asked her if she could sit back on the beat.
2. What does she do when the director asks whether a singer is ready?
She never just says yes. She asks the singer what she needs, and then she tells the director exactly what the singer said, word for word.
3. What did a contestant once tell her, and what happened next?
The contestant said that she was scared of the key change. The director moved the key down a step, and she nailed it on the night.
Listening 2 · Tell Him to Slow Down

Nobody Asked Who Him Was

Four words, the wrong musician, and a song that dragged. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the director ask him to do, and what did he actually do?
The director said: tell him to slow down. He went to the drummer and told him to slow down, and the whole song dragged.
2. What did the director ask twenty minutes later, and what did he say he had meant?
He asked why the drummer had changed the tempo. He said that he had meant the singer, not the drummer, because the singer was the one who was rushing.
3. What is his rule now?
When somebody gives him a note for another person, he repeats it back before he walks away, and he asks who it is for and what exactly they want to hear differently.

Chapter 6: Your Turn

From Guided to Free, Three Times

Guided > Semi-free > Free

Role-Play 1 of 3 — Guided

Pass the Notes On

Scenario: Dara missed the run-through again. Use the notes sheet from slide 18. Tell her what Marcus said, what he asked her, and what he told everybody not to do. Do not add your opinion. Finish by offering to clarify one note with him.

Keywords:

He told me that He asked whether you could He told us not to I can clarify it with him
Role-Play 2 of 3 — Semi-free

Repeat It Back Before You Walk Away

Scenario: Now you are the producer. The director gives you three fast notes for three different people. Repeat each one back to him to check, and ask who it is for. Then turn to the teacher, who is now the band, and pass the notes on.

Keywords:

Just to clarify, is this for So you want me to tell her He said that He asked you to
Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

A Real Note, No Notes

Scenario: Think of real feedback somebody gave you or your team on a production: a director, a client, a contestant. Report it: what they said, what they asked, and what they told you to do or not to do. Then say whether the message changed on the way, and what you would do differently.

No keywords, no notes, two minutes.
