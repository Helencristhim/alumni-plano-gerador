Lesson 11 -- Pre-class

What He Said in the Rehearsal

A note travels from the director to the singer, and it can change on the way. Key words: a run-through, to give somebody notes, to rush, to drag, pitchy, to nail something, a key change, to run something by somebody, to bring something up, to come across as something, to clarify, to be on the same page. Structure: reported speech -- the verb takes one step back, a question becomes a normal sentence, and a request becomes to + verb.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A run-through -- a complete practice of a song or a show from start to finish, before the real thing
"We did one run-through at six and the real show at eight."
To give somebody notes -- to tell somebody what to change or improve after you watch their work
"The director gave me notes on every chorus, one by one."
To rush -- to play or sing a little ahead of the beat, faster than you should
"You are rushing the second chorus again."
To drag -- to play or sing a little behind the beat, so the song feels slow
"When the drummer slowed down, the whole song dragged."
Pitchy -- a little out of tune, not exactly on the right note
"The high note came out a bit pitchy in the run-through."
To nail something -- to do it perfectly, exactly as it should be
"She was nervous all week, and then she nailed it on the night."
A key change -- the moment a song moves up or down to a new key
"The key change in the last chorus is where the audience starts clapping."
To run something by somebody -- to tell somebody an idea to hear their opinion before you act on it
"Can you run the octave idea by Marcus before Thursday?"
To bring something up -- to start talking about a subject, often one that nobody mentioned yet
"Nobody brought up the bridge until the very end of the rehearsal."
To come across as something -- to give a particular impression to other people, even if you did not mean it
"His note came across as an accusation, and she stopped smiling."
To clarify -- to make something clear by explaining it or by asking about it
"I asked him to clarify who the note was for."
To be on the same page -- to understand a plan in the same way as the other people involved
"Before Thursday, I want all of us to be on the same page."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Dara missed the run-through on Tuesday, so the next morning Pedro passed the notes on. Marcus, the director, had stood up after the second chorus and said, “The verse is great, but she’s rushing the chorus.” Pedro did not repeat those words exactly. He told her that the verse was great, but that she was rushing the chorus. Look at the verbs: is became was, is rushing became was rushing. When you report something later, the verb usually takes one step back.

Marcus also had a question: “Can she take the bridge an octave lower?” Pedro asked her whether she could take it lower. The question became a normal sentence — no question mark, and could after she, not before it. The same happens with where and why: Marcus asked where the drummer was, never where was the drummer.

The last note was an order: “Don’t change the arrangement before Thursday.” Pedro told everybody not to change it. Requests and orders become to + verb, or not to + verb. And one small rule that Brazilians often forget: tell always needs a person after it. He told me, he said that, he said to me — but never he said me.

The words of the rehearsal room complete the picture. A singer who is ahead of the beat rushes; a band that is behind it drags. A note that is almost right is pitchy, and the key change is where it usually happens. Before he passed anything on, Pedro ran the octave idea by Marcus and asked him to clarify who each note was for. A note that comes across as an accusation does not help anybody, and the goal is simple: everybody on the same page, and the singer free to nail it on the night.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

One question: was it something he said, something he asked, or something he wanted?

JobFormExample
Statementssaid (that) / told somebody (that) + one step backis → was · can → could · will → would · did → had done
Said or told?tell needs a person · say does notHe told me that... · He said that...
Yes / no questionsasked if / whether + normal orderHe asked if I could come on Thursday.
Wh- questionsasked where / what / why + normal orderHe asked where the singer was.
Requests and ordersasked / told somebody (not) to + verbHe told us not to change the arrangement.
Still true, or right nowthe verb can stay the sameHe says the key is too high.

Never: he said me that · he asked where was the singer · he told to me to slow down · he asked me if I can come yesterday

Quiz
