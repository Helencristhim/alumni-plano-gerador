Chapter 4: The Code

A 20-Centimeter Slab, 20 Centimeters Thick

Numbers before the noun, and after the verb

Grammar Discovery

Before the Noun, or After the Verb?

"It is a twelve-story building on a two-level basement."

"The typical floors have 20-centimeter slabs."

"The transfer slab is 40 centimeters thick."

"The walls in red are load-bearing walls."

Look at the words in red. Is there an s on story and centimeter? Now look at the words in green. Where is the number, and what comes after it?

PatternFormExample
number + noun, before a nounhyphen, and the noun is singulara twelve-story building · a 20-centimeter slab
number + plural noun + adjective, after the verbno hyphen, the noun is pluralThe slab is 20 centimeters thick.
noun / adjective + -ing or past participleone adjective, usually with a hyphena load-bearing wall · reinforced-concrete frame
noun + nounthe first noun works as an adjective, always singulara steel beam · a drawing set · the title block
two dimensionsX by Y (length first)The room is six meters by four.
Portuguese trapa slab does not have 20 centimetersThe slab has 20 centimeters — wrong

In one line: before the noun, a number is an adjective — hyphen, no s; after the verb, it is a measurement — plural, plus thick, wide, long or high.

Chapter 5: Real Voices

The Drawing, Two Voices

A title block, an architect and a site engineer

Real Document

The Title Block

TITLE BLOCK — Sheet A-104
Bottom right corner of every drawing in the set

Project: Residential Tower — Vila Mariana

Drawing: A-104 · Fourth Floor Plan

Scale: 1:50 (unless marked N.T.S.)

Revision: C — stair moved; new 2-meter opening, axis 4

Drawn by / Checked by: J. Moreira / F. Gattaz

Notes: All dimensions in millimeters unless noted otherwise. Do not scale this drawing. TYP. = typical · N.T.S. = not to scale · EL. = level above ground floor · dim. = dimension. Walls in red are load-bearing walls.
1. What changed in Revision C?
The stair was moved, and there is a new two-meter opening on axis 4.
2. Can you measure this drawing with a ruler?
No. The note says “Do not scale this drawing”: use the written dimensions, in millimeters.
3. What does “TYP.” mean next to a detail?
Typical: the same detail applies to every similar case on the drawing.
Listening 1 · A Project Architect

The Most Expensive Mistake

A project architect on what goes wrong most often on site. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the most expensive mistake she sees on site?
An old drawing: a crew building from a revision that has already been replaced.
2. What did Revision C change in her example?
The stair and a two-meter opening on the fourth floor.
3. What is her rule when a new revision arrives?
The old drawings leave the site office the same day, and the foreman checks the revision letter in the title block before any crew starts.
Listening 2 · A Site Engineer

The Wall Nobody Cut

A site engineer remembers his second project. Sound first — no text.

1. What discrepancy did he find?
The architectural plan showed a large opening in a wall that was a load-bearing wall on the structural drawings.
2. What did he do instead of letting the foreman cut the wall?
He flagged the discrepancy and sent an RFI to the structural engineer, who answered in two days.
3. What had caused the problem?
The architect had moved the opening in the last revision, and nobody had told the structure team.
