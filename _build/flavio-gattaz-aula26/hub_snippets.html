Lesson 26 -- Pre-class

Reading a Set of Drawings -- The Plan, the Section and the Numbers

On site, every instruction starts on paper. Today you read a set of drawings and the outline specification that goes with it, and you describe a building in English. Key words: a floor plan, an elevation, a cross-section, a slab, a load-bearing wall, a specification, a revision, a discrepancy, to cross-reference, an RFI, as-built drawings, to flag something. Structure: numbers before a noun (a twelve-story building, a 20-centimeter slab) and after the verb (20 centimeters thick, six meters by four).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A floor plan -- a drawing of one level of a building, seen from above
"Check the floor plan of the fourth floor before you mark the walls."
An elevation -- a drawing of one side of a building, seen straight from the front
"The north elevation shows all the windows on that side."
A cross-section -- a drawing of a building as if it were cut from top to bottom
"The cross-section shows the height of every floor."
A slab -- a flat, thick layer of concrete that forms a floor or a roof
"We pour the fifth-floor slab on Friday."
A load-bearing wall -- a wall that carries the weight of the floors above it
"You cannot open a door in a load-bearing wall without the engineer."
A specification -- a document that describes the materials and the standards for the work
"The specification says which concrete we use on every floor."
A revision -- a new, corrected version of a drawing
"Revision C changed the stair on the fourth floor."
A discrepancy -- a difference between two things that should be the same
"There is a discrepancy between the plan and the section."
To cross-reference -- to check information in one document against another
"Always cross-reference the plan with the structural drawings."
An RFI -- a request for information: a formal question to the designer when a drawing is not clear
"Send an RFI to the architect before the crew starts."
As-built drawings -- drawings that show what was actually built, with every change
"The client receives the as-built drawings at the end of the job."
To flag something -- to point out a problem so that other people notice it
"If you see a problem in the drawings, flag it right away."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

An engineer who describes a building in English uses numbers all the time, and the numbers change form with their position. Before a noun, a number and a unit become one adjective, with a hyphen and with no s: It is a twelve-story building. The typical floors have 20-centimeter slabs. Many Brazilian speakers say "a twelve-stories building", which sounds wrong to a native speaker.

After the verb, the unit is plural again, and an adjective of measurement comes after it: The transfer slab is 40 centimeters thick. The opening is two meters wide. In Portuguese a slab has twenty centimeters, but in English it is twenty centimeters thick. For two dimensions, say the length first: The room is six meters by four.

The same logic explains a load-bearing wall, a steel beam and the title block: the first word works as an adjective, so it never takes a plural.

1. According to the text, what happens to a number and a unit before a noun?
A They become one adjective, with a hyphen and with no s.
B They stay plural, as in Portuguese, because there is more than one unit in the measurement.
C They move after the noun.
2. How does the text say you should describe the thickness of a slab?
A The slab has 20 centimeters.
B The slab is a 20 centimeters, in thickness, on the typical floors of the building.
C The slab is 20 centimeters thick.
3. Why is it "a steel beam" and not "a steels beam"?
A Because steel is a material, and materials never take a plural in any situation in English.
B Because the first word works as an adjective.
C Because the beam is singular.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Numbers Before and After

GRAMMAR

Before the noun, a number is an adjective. After the verb, it is a measurement.

PatternFormExample
number + unit before a nounhyphen, singulara twelve-story building · a 20-centimeter slab
number + unit after the verbplural + thick / wide / long / high / deepThe slab is 20 centimeters thick.
two dimensionslength by widthThe room is six meters by four.
noun + -ing / past participleone adjective, usually with a hyphena load-bearing wall · reinforced-concrete frame
noun + nounthe first noun is singulara steel beam · the title block

Never: a twelve-stories building · a 20-centimeters slab · the slab has 20 centimeters · a wall load-bearing
