Lesson 1 · Who Is Bruna?

A Homebody Who Travels

Somebody at an RV park in Alaska turns around and asks you what your story is. Everything you answer is either who you are now, or something that already finished. In English those are two different verbs.

Chapter 3: The Code

Every Week, and That One Time

One life, two verb shapes

Grammar Discovery

Two Happen Every Week. Two Are Over.

"Every morning I wake up at eight and I take the dogs out."

"I work at the office three days a week, and I work from home two days."

"Last month we flew to Anchorage and we drove an RV for seventeen days."

"We did not stay in hotels, and honestly it was worth it."

The blue verbs are bare: they describe a week that repeats. The red verbs changed shape: they describe one block of time that started, ended, and will not come back.

JobFormExample
A week that repeatsI / you / we + bare verbI work from home two days a week.
One finished block of timethe past form of the verbLast month we drove to Alaska.
Negative, repeatingdo not + bare verbI do not go out much.
Negative, finisheddid not + bare verbWe did not stay in hotels.
Question, repeatingDo + subject + bare verbDo you travel a lot?
Question, finishedDid + subject + bare verbDid you like Alaska?
The trap in questionsafter did, the verb goes back to bareNot did you went. Say did you go.
The trap in storiesa normal day is not finished timeNot every day I woke up at eight. Say every day I wake up at eight.

In one line: if the time is still around you, the verb stays bare. If the time closed, the verb changes.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Conversation You Did Not Have

One stranger, three recordings, one piece of paper

Dialogue

Seven in the Morning at Riverbend

D
Morning. You two are in site fourteen, right? I am Dave, I run the place. Where are you folks in from?
B
São Paulo, Brazil. We landed in Anchorage on Sunday and we picked up the RV the same day.
D
Brazil. That is a long way to come for a road trip. First time up here?
B
First time. And honestly, the scenery does not look real to me yet.
D
It does that to people. So what do you do back home, when you are not driving a rental around my parking lot?
B
I am an operations manager at a tech company. I studied nutrition, though — I made a career change four years ago.
D
Huh. Good for you. Well, the store opens at seven if you want to grab a coffee before you drive out to the park.
B
We will. And Dave — what do you do here in the winter, when nobody comes?
Comprehension

About Dave

1. What does Dave tell her about himself in his very first line?
He gives his name and says that he runs the place, and then he asks where they are from.
2. What does Dave say about Brazil, and what does he say happens to people in Alaska?
He says Brazil is a long way to come for a road trip, and that Alaska does that to people, meaning everybody says the scenery does not look real.
3. What useful piece of information does Dave give her without being asked?
That the store opens at seven, so she can grab a coffee before she drives out to the park.
Listening 1 · A Travel Podcast

A Homebody Who Travels

A podcast host answering the question her listeners kept asking. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say about a normal week of hers?
She works from home four days a week, she walks her two dogs every morning before anything else, and most evenings she winds down with a book.
2. Why does she say people find it strange that she calls herself a homebody?
Because she hosts a travel show. She says it is true anyway, and that the difference is that twice a year she closes the laptop and goes somewhere far.
3. What did she do last spring, and what does she say about hotels?
She and her husband rented an RV and drove up through Alaska for two weeks. They did not stay in a single hotel.
Listening 2 · A Recorded Message

Nobody Is at the Front Desk

The message you get when you call an RV park at nine at night. Sound first — no text.

1. What time is check-in, and where do you find your site number?
Check-in is any time after two in the afternoon, and the site number is on the board by the gate.
2. What advice does the message give to first-time guests, and why?
To fill the water tank that night and not the next morning, because the line gets long.
3. What does the message say about the sky at that time of year?
That it stays light until almost midnight, so you should not trust the sun to tell you when to sleep.
Listening 3 · A Pronunciation Coach

Why the Book Does Not Sound Like the Street

A coach on the gap between written English and spoken American. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say about the English in a book?
That it is real English, but it is not the English that anybody says out loud.
2. She compares two versions of the same question. What does she say was done to the words?
Nothing was taken away. The words were only pushed together, because we speak in beats and not in letters.
3. What exactly is the method she gives for the week?
Take one minute of any American show, play it four times, and speak along with the speaker until your mouth moves at their speed.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I can describe a normal week of mine and a finished trip, one after the other, without mixing the two.
I use the bare verb for the week that repeats, and the past form for one block of time that closed.
I say "Did you go", never "Did you went", and "We did not stay", never "We did not stayed".
I can ask a stranger two questions about their life and their last trip, and keep the conversation going.
I know the words: a homebody, a couch potato, to be into something, to wind down, a career change, an RV, a road trip, scenery, to grab something, worth it.
