Lesson 29 -- Pre-class

Airport, Hotel and the Unexpected -- Been To or Went To?

Ten to twenty minutes before class. How to talk about your business trips: the places you have been to, and the moment you went. Key words: a boarding pass, a delay, to cancel, a booking, a layover, a voucher, to check in.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A boarding pass -- the document that you need to get on a plane
"I have my boarding pass on my phone."
A delay -- a period of time when something is late
"There was a two-hour delay in Atlanta."
To cancel -- to say that something planned will not happen
"The airline cancelled the last flight of the day."
A booking -- an arrangement to have a room or a seat
"I made the booking through the company app."
A layover -- a short stop between two flights
"I had a long layover in Miami."
A voucher -- a paper that you can use instead of money
"They gave us a voucher for dinner."
To check in -- to arrive and give your details at a hotel or an airport
"We checked in at eleven at night."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Paula works in sales for a data company. She has been to the United States eleven times. She has been to New York, Atlanta and Chicago, but she has never been to California.

Her worst trip was in March. She went to Chicago for a client meeting, and a snowstorm closed the airport. She slept at a hotel near the airport and flew home two days later.

"She has been to the United States eleven times." What does it tell you?
A Her experience, with no date.
B A trip that she is planning for next year, with her team.
C One trip, in March.
Why does the text say "She went to Chicago" and not "She has been to Chicago" in March?
A Because Chicago is far.
B Because she did not like the trip, and she never wants to go back.
C Because it is one trip with a moment: March.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Been To or Went To?

GRAMMAR

Your travel experience, with no date: have been to. One trip, with a moment: went to.

JobFormExample
Experience, no datehave / has been toI’ve been to Chicago twice.
One trip, with a momentwent toI went to Chicago last year.
Never in my lifehave never been toI’ve never been to California.

Never: I have been to Chicago last year · Did you ever been to Atlanta? · When have you been to London?

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

Listen first, then put the lines in order. Two colleagues talk at an airport. Put their conversation in a natural order.

Step 1: listen to the whole conversation. Step 2: put the lines in the order you heard.
?"Great, let's go. I need a coffee before the call."
?"Is there a good place to wait?"
?"Our flight is delayed again. Have you ever been to this airport before?"
?"There is a quiet cafe near gate twelve. I went there last time."
?"Yes, many times. I came here in May for the client visit."

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen, then record yourself. Been is short in American English: /bin/.

I've been to Atlanta three times.
We went to Chicago in March.
Our flight was cancelled last night.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A client asks: "Have you ever been to Atlanta?" You went in 2024. You say:
A "Yes, I've been there in 2024."
B "Yes, I have. I went there in 2024."
C "Yes, I go there in 2024."
You want to know a colleague's travel experience. You ask:
A "Are you ever been to Chicago?"
B "Did you ever been to Chicago?"
C "Have you ever been to Chicago?"
The hotel cannot find your booking. You say:
A "I made the booking last week. Could you check again, please?"
B "You lost my booking, this is terrible, and I want to speak to the manager."
C "I want my room now."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Talk about your business trips in about one minute: which cities you have been to, when you went to the last one, and one problem you had at an airport or a hotel.
