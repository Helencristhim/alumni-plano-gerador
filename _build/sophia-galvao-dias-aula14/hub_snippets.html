Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud.

Line-up -- the list of all the artists who play at a festival
"Have you seen the line-up for next year yet?"
Setlist -- the list of songs an artist plays at one show
"He had changed the setlist, so the first song was a surprise."
Encore -- the extra songs an artist plays after pretending the show has finished
"Everybody was shouting for an encore."
Crowd -- all the people watching a show together
"The crowd had started singing before the lights went off."
Opening act -- the artist who plays first, before the main artist
"By the time we arrived, the opening act had already finished."
Sold out -- with no tickets left to buy
"The tickets had sold out in ten minutes."
Wristband -- a band you wear on your arm to get into a show
"I have kept every wristband from every show."
Sing along -- to sing a song together with the artist or the crowd
"The whole stadium sang along to the last song."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. The words in bold are your life up to now (have) or the past before another past (had).

I have been to four shows in my life, but last Saturday was different. When we got to the gate, the tickets had sold out, and my friend had left ours at home!

Her mum drove back to get them. By the time we got in, the opening act had finished and the crowd had started the first chant. I had never heard a stadium that loud.

I have not stopped listening to the setlist since.

"When we got to the gate, my friend had left our tickets at home." What happened first?
A She left the tickets at home.
B Both at the same time.
C They got to the gate.
"I have been to four shows in my life." Why have and not had?
A Because it happened last Saturday.
B Because be never takes had.
C Because it is her life up to now, and it is not finished.
Which sentence is correct?
A When we arrived, the show has already started.
B When we arrived, the show already started before.
C When we arrived, the show had already started.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Have or had?

  • have / has + third form = your life up to now: I have been to three concerts.
  • past simple = the story, at a finished time: We arrived at nine.
  • had + third form = inside the story, what happened BEFORE: The show had already started.

The test: is there another past moment in the sentence (when we arrived, before that night, by the time)? Then had can go there. No other past moment? Your life up to now is have.
