The British Client in Real Time -- Thinking Out Loud Without Freezing

Hesitation is normal. Silence is the problem. Key words: to think on your feet, to buy time, off the top of my head, to freeze, to hold the floor, to rephrase, to pick up on, a clarification. Focus: five jobs for the moment before you answer -- buy time, hold the floor, ask for clarification, recover, close.

Vocabulary

To think on your feet -- to think and answer quickly, without time to prepare
"In a live call, you have to think on your feet."
To buy time -- to say something that gives you a few more seconds to think
"Saying 'that is a good question' helps you buy time."
Off the top of my head -- from memory, right now, without checking
"Off the top of my head, I'd say about forty percent."
To freeze -- to suddenly stop, unable to speak or think
"I sometimes freeze when a question is unexpected."
To hold the floor -- to keep speaking until you finish, without being interrupted
"Phrases like 'what I mean is' help you hold the floor."
To rephrase -- to say the same idea again with different words
"Sorry, let me rephrase that."
To pick up on -- to notice something somebody said and talk about it
"I want to pick up on something you said earlier."
A clarification -- an explanation that makes something easier to understand
"Could I ask for a quick clarification?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Most people who freeze in a second language are not missing words. They are missing time. When a native speaker needs three seconds to think, they fill those seconds with something: That's a really good question, Let me think about that for a moment, Off the top of my head, I'd say... A learner usually fills them with silence, and the silence feels much longer to the speaker than to the listener.

These phrases do five jobs. You buy time before you answer. You hold the floor with What I mean is or The reason I say that is, so nobody starts talking before you finish. You ask for a clarification when you are not sure what the client means: Just to make sure I've understood, you're asking about the cost? You recover when a sentence goes wrong: Sorry, let me rephrase that. And you close and pass the turn: I hope that answers your question.

In a British meeting, a pause of three or four seconds is normal, and nobody will think less of you. What causes real problems is a fast, confident answer to a question you did not understand. So the goal is not to stop hesitating. It is to hesitate on purpose, with words, and to think on your feet instead of going silent.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Five Jobs for the Moment Before You Answer

GRAMMAR

There is no new grammar in this lesson. You use what you already know, in real time, with a few fixed phrases that give you time to think.

JobWhat you sayExample
1. Buy timeLet me think about that for a moment. / Off the top of my head, I'd say...That's a really good question. Let me think about that.
2. Hold the floorWhat I mean is... / The reason I say that is...What I mean is, the information is old by the time you get it.
3. Ask for clarification (question)Could you say a little more about that? / Would you mind clarifying what you mean by...?Just to make sure I've understood, you're asking about the cost?
4. RecoverLet me rephrase that. / What I meant to say was...What I meant to say was Thursday, not Tuesday.
5. CloseI hope that answers your question. / Does that work from your side?That is our plan. I'd be curious to know what you think.
NegativeI don't want to give you the wrong number, so let me check and confirm by email.

Never: let me to think · can you explain me...? · silence while you look for the perfect word

Stage 2: Put the Call in Order

Order

The client raises a concern with no warning. Put the six lines of the call in a logical order.

?"Off the top of my head, I'd say a short call every Tuesday would help."
?"That's a fair point. Let me think about that for a moment."
?"Could I ask for a quick clarification? Is it the calls or the updates?"
?"Before we finish, I would like to raise a concern about coordination."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give on a call.

The client asks a question you did not expect, and you need a few seconds. You say:
A Wait.
B Sorry, I am not understanding nothing about this question, so I need to check with my team first.
C That's a really good question. Let me think about that.
You are not sure what the client means by “the second phase”. You say:
A Can you explain me what is the second phase, because I am not sure and I do not want to give you a wrong answer?
B Would you mind clarifying what you mean by the second phase?
C Yes, of course.
In the middle of your answer you say “June”, but you meant “July”. You say:
A What I meant to say was July, not June.
B June. No. Sorry.
C Sorry, let me to rephrase that sentence, because the month that I said before was not the correct month.
You have finished your answer and want the client to speak. You say:
A Finished.
B I hope that answers your question. I would be curious to know what you think about the plan.
C So this is all.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Ninety seconds, no notes. Imagine your British client asks you, with no warning: “What is the biggest risk in this project right now, and what would you do about it?” Answer as if you were on the call. Buy time at the start, hold the floor once, rephrase once if you need to, and close by passing the turn back to the client.
