Chapter 3: Five Jobs, Five Phrases

None of These Sentences Answers the Question

And every one of them is doing an important job

Language Discovery

What Is Each Sentence Doing?

“That's a really good question. Let me think about that for a moment.”

“What I mean is, the information is old by the time you get it.”

“Just to make sure I've understood, are you asking about the cost or the timing?”

“We sent it on Friday. Sorry, let me rephrase that. We sent it on Thursday night.”

“I hope that answers your question.”

None of the colored words gives the client new information. What do they have in common, and what is each one doing?

JobWhat you sayWhen
1. Buy timeThat's a really good question. / Let me think about that for a moment. / Off the top of my head, I'd say... / I want to make sure I'm being accurate here.You need three seconds and you do not want silence.
2. Hold the floorWhat I mean is... / To put it another way... / The reason I say that is... / And the point I'm making is...You have not finished, and you do not want to be interrupted.
3. Ask for clarificationCould you say a little more about that? / Just to make sure I've understood, you're saying that...? / Would you mind clarifying what you mean by...?You are not sure what the client means.
4. RecoverWhat I meant to say was... / Let me rephrase that. / Actually, to be more precise...Your sentence went wrong in the middle.
5. CloseI hope that answers your question. / Does that work from your side? / I'd be curious to know what you think.You have finished, and it is the client's turn.
The trapSilence while you look for the perfect word, or a fast answer to a question you did not understand.

In one line: you do not have to stop hesitating. Hesitate with words, on purpose.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Dialogue

A Concern Nobody Expected

C
Before we finish the review, Gustavo, I would like to raise a concern. My team feels the coordination between our two sides has not been great this quarter.
G
Thanks for bringing that up. Could I ask for a quick clarification? Is it the weekly calls, or the way we share updates?
C
Mostly the updates. Twice last month, we heard about a delay from our own client before we heard it from you.
G
That's a fair point. Let me think about that for a moment, because I want to make sure I'm being accurate here.
C
Of course. Take your time.
G
Off the top of my head, both delays started on a Monday, and our updates go out on Fridays. What I mean is, the information is old by the time you get it.
C
That makes sense. So what would you suggest?
G
A short call every Tuesday, fifteen minutes, no slides. And the point I am making is that you would hear about a problem on day one, not day five. Does that work from your side?
C
It does. But I picked up on something you said earlier. You said our updates. Who actually sends them now?
G
Good question. Daniel used to, but since Priya's team took over, it has been less clear. What I meant to say was, nobody owns it right now. I'll sort that out this week.
Comprehension

About Claire

1. What concern does Claire raise at the end of the review?
Her team feels the coordination between the two sides has not been great this quarter.
2. According to Claire, what happened twice last month?
They heard about a delay from their own client before they heard it from Gustavo.
3. What did Claire pick up on, and what does she ask?

Listening 1 · A Communication Coach

Not Missing Words, Missing Time

A coach who works with professionals who speak English as a second language. Sound first — no text.

1. According to her, what are most professionals who freeze really missing?
Time, not words.
2. How does the silence feel to the speaker, compared with the listener?
It feels much longer to the speaker than it does to the listener.
3. What should you say when a sentence goes wrong, and how does it sound?
“Let me rephrase that.” It sounds confident, not weak.
Listening 2 · A Voice Note

A Pause Is Not a Problem

A voice note from a British project director who works with suppliers from many countries. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say happens if you stop for three or four seconds in a British meeting?
Nothing bad: nobody jumps in, and nobody thinks less of you.
2. What makes British people uncomfortable, according to him?
When somebody answers a question they have not understood, quickly and with confidence, and later it turns out they were answering a different question.
3. What does “I hope that answers your question” tell the client?
That you have finished, and it is their turn to speak.
