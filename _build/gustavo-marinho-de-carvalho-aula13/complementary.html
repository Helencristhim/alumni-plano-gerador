Complementary Activities

Optional extras to enjoy outside class. Mark each one as done after you watch or listen to it.

Film
The King’s Speech (2010)

A British king who freezes every time he has to speak in public, and the teacher who helps him find the words. It is the whole lesson in one film: the fear of silence, and the patience to get through it. The English is British, slow and very clear.

Tip: English subtitles on. Watch the first speech and the last one, and notice how the pauses change: at the start they are frozen silence, at the end they are pauses on purpose.

Podcast
BBC The English We Speak — “Think on your feet”

Three minutes, two British speakers, one expression from this lesson: what it means to think on your feet, with examples from work and daily life. Free transcript on the same page.

Tip: listen once with no text. Then answer out loud: when was the last time you had to think on your feet at work, and what did you say?

YouTube
Matt Abrahams — “Think Fast, Talk Smart: Communication Techniques” (Stanford GSB)

A Stanford lecturer on speaking when you did not prepare: answering an unexpected question, calming the nerves, and giving your answer a simple structure. Exactly the situation of the role-plays in this lesson.

Tip: watch the first fifteen minutes with English subtitles. Write down one technique, and use it in your next call with the client.
