Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Tactful -- careful not to upset people when you say something difficult
"She was tactful: she gave the bad news without upsetting anyone."
Blunt -- saying what you think very directly, without trying to be polite
"His question was too blunt for a first meeting."
Sensitive -- likely to upset people, so it needs care
"Job cuts are a sensitive topic."
Tension -- a feeling of stress or conflict between people
"There is some tension between the two finance teams."
Put someone on the spot -- to ask someone a difficult question in front of other people
"Do not put the CFO on the spot in front of the committee."
Tread carefully -- to act or speak with great care in a difficult situation
"We need to tread carefully with the unions."
Candid -- honest and open, even about difficult things
"Thank you for being so candid about the numbers."
Cost overrun -- when a project costs more money than planned
"The cost overrun on the bridge is about two hundred thousand dollars."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The payroll bridge has a cost overrun, and Carlos needs answers from Brightline finance. But the topic is sensitive, so he does not ask, “When will it be ready?” He asks, “Could you tell me when it will be ready?”

He also asks, “Do you know if the board has approved the budget?” And because he does not want to put the CFO on the spot, he writes, “I was wondering if we could talk privately on Thursday.”

Notice the pattern. After could you tell me, do you know and I was wondering, the question becomes a normal sentence: subject + verb. Do, does and did disappear. After would you mind, use -ing.

1. Why does Carlos change the way he asks his questions?
A Because the topic is sensitive and he wants to be tactful.

C Because he does not need the answers.
2. Where does Carlos want to talk to the CFO first?
A In front of the whole committee.
B Privately, on Thursday.
C By email to the whole company.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “Could you tell me what does it cost?”
B “Could you tell me what it costs?”
C “Could you tell me what it does cost?”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Indirect Questions: Could You Tell Me...?

GRAMMAR

An indirect question opens the door gently. After the opener, use normal word order: subject + verb.

JobFormExample
Ask for informationCould you tell me + wh- word + subject + verbCould you tell me when it will be ready?
Ask a yes / no questionDo you know + if / whether + subject + verbDo you know if the board has approved it?
Suggest softlyI was wondering if + subject + couldI was wondering if we could talk privately.
Ask a very polite favorWould you mind + verb-ingWould you mind telling me why the cost went up?
No do / does / didwh- word + subject + verbCould you tell me what it costs?

Never: Could you tell me when will it be ready · Do you know what does it cost · I was wondering if could we · Would you mind to tell me
