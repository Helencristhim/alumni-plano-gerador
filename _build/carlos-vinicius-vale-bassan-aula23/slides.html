Chapter 3: The Code

Could You Tell Me...?

Hard questions with a softer door

Grammar Discovery

Same Question, Softer Door

When will the bridge be ready? → "Could you tell me when the bridge will be ready?"

Has the board approved it? → "Do you know if the board has approved it?"

Can we talk privately? → "I was wondering if we could talk privately."

Why did the cost go up? → "Would you mind telling me why the cost went up?"

The blue words open the door gently. After them, the question becomes a normal sentence: subject + verb. Do, does and did disappear. A yes / no question uses if. And after would you mind, the verb takes -ing.

JobFormExample
Ask for informationCould you tell me + wh- word + subject + verbCould you tell me when the bridge will be ready?
Ask a yes / no questionDo you know + if / whether + subject + verbDo you know if the board has approved it?
Suggest or ask very softlyI was wondering if + subject + couldI was wondering if we could talk privately.
Ask a very polite favorWould you mind + verb-ingWould you mind telling me why the cost went up?
No do / does / didwh- word + subject + verbCould you tell me what it costs?
The trapno question order after the openerNot Could you tell me when will it be ready?

In one line: Could you tell me / Do you know / I was wondering + (if / wh- word) + subject + verb; Would you mind + -ing.

Chapter 4: At the Table

A Sensitive Budget

One call, two recordings, one question list

Dialogue

The Sensitive Budget

C
Diana, thanks for making time. I have a few sensitive questions about the payroll bridge.
D
Go ahead. I'd rather you were candid.
C
Could you tell me whether the board has approved the budget for the bridge?
D
Not yet. We're worried about a cost overrun. The first estimate from IT was too low.
C
I understand. Do you know when the board will decide? Priya needs an answer before the town hall.
D
By Friday, I hope. But I don't want to put the CFO on the spot in front of the whole committee.
C
That makes sense. I was wondering if we could talk to him privately first, on Thursday.
D
Good idea. Let's tread carefully. There is already some tension between the two finance teams.
Comprehension

About Diana

1. How does Diana want Carlos to speak to her?
Candidly: openly and honestly.
2. Why has the board not approved the budget yet?
It is worried about a cost overrun: the first estimate from IT was too low.
3. What does Diana not want to do in front of the committee?
Put the CFO on the spot.

Same information, softer door. After could you tell me, do you know and I was wondering, the question becomes a normal sentence.
1. Make it tactful: “Where is the report?”
Could you tell me where the report is?
2. Make it tactful: “Is the data ready?”
Do you know if the data is ready?
3. Why does “Who made this mistake?” become “what caused the delay”?
It focuses on the problem, not the person, so nobody is put on the spot.
