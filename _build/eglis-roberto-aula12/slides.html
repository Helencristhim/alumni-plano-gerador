The Next Trip

The Question

Where Are You Going Next?

A friend at the sailing club says: “I’ve got two free months next year. Let’s go somewhere together!” Where would you go? What are you going to see, and what are you not going to miss?

Real Speed

I’ll, We’re, Going To

Listen for the short forms, then say it back at the same speed.

I'm going to book it tonight.
We're leaving on the second of June.
I'll call the marina tomorrow.
This time next year, I'll be sailing.
The Conversation · The World

The Future of Travel

1. In twenty years, will people travel more or less than today? Why?
2. Is the off-season the best time to travel, or will you miss the best part of a place?
3. Some people say that tourists are ruining the most beautiful places. What will happen to them?

Language you can use

The Plan

Four Cards, One Trip

You and a friend from the sailing club plan one trip together, card by card.

Card 1: The Dream
Where are you going to go, and why this place?
Card 2: The Route
Day by day: what are you going to do first, and then?
Card 3: The Bookings
What is already booked: flights, the boat, the first night?
Card 4: The Picture
This time next year, what will you be doing there?

Language you can use

We’re going to…We’re flying on…This time next year, we’ll be…OK, I’ll…
an itineraryto charter a boata layoverto book in advance
Under Pressure

Explain the Plan, Then Question It

Round 1: someone at home calls you and wants to know every detail of your next trip. Round 2: a friend tells you about his big trip, and you are not sure the plan is a good one.

  • Round 1: you explain the plan, the bookings and what will happen if something goes wrong.
  • Round 2: you ask the hard questions and check what is really booked.

Closing

One Minute, One Trip

A friend at the bar asks you: “So, what’s next for you?” Tell him where you are going to go, one thing that is already booked, and what you will be doing this time next year. One minute. Go.

I can talk about my plans with going to and about bookings with the present continuous.
I can make a decision or a prediction on the spot with will and won’t.
I can describe a future scene with will be + -ing: This time next year, I’ll be sailing.
