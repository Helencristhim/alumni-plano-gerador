Lesson 12 -- Pre-class

The Next Trip -- The Plan

How to plan a trip with a friend: the plan, the bookings, quick decisions and a picture of the future. Key words: an itinerary, a bucket list, to book in advance, a layover, to look forward to, to charter a boat, the off-season, to pack light, a day trip, to get away. Structure: the future (I’m going to visit Greece. We’re leaving on Monday. I’ll call the marina. This time next year, I’ll be sailing.).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example.

An itinerary -- a plan of the places you will visit on a trip, day by day
"Our itinerary has five islands in ten days."
A bucket list -- a list of things you want to do before you die
"Sailing around Cape Horn is on my bucket list."
To book in advance -- to reserve a ticket or a room a long time before the trip
"In August, you have to book in advance."
A layover -- a stop between two flights on the same trip
"We have a six-hour layover in Madrid."
To look forward to -- to feel happy about something that is going to happen
"I'm really looking forward to the trip."
To charter a boat -- to pay to use a boat for some days, with or without a crew
"We're chartering a boat in Croatia next summer."
The off-season -- the time of year when few tourists visit a place
"Hotels are much cheaper in the off-season."
To pack light -- to travel with very few things in your bag
"On a small boat, you have to pack light."
A day trip -- a short trip when you go and come back on the same day
"We took a day trip to the island."
To get away -- to leave your normal life for a while to rest
"We need to get away for a few days."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Paulo retired last year, and next spring he is going to sail around the Greek islands with his old friend Rui. The tickets are booked: they are flying to Athens on the third of May, and they are chartering a small boat for two weeks. Rui is worried about the wind. “Don’t worry,” Paulo says. “The weather will be fine in May.”

Then the phone rings. The marina has a problem with the boat. Paulo thinks for a second: “OK, I’ll call them right now.” He is already looking forward to the trip. “This time next month,” he tells Rui, “we’ll be sailing to Santorini.”

Four futures, four jobs: going to is the plan, the present continuous is what is already booked, will is a decision now or a prediction, and will be + -ing is a picture of a moment in the future.

"They are flying to Athens on the third of May." Why does the text use the present continuous here?
A The tickets are already booked.
B Paulo decides it at that moment on the phone, without thinking about it first and without asking Rui.
C It is a prediction about the weather.
"OK, I’ll call them right now." Why does Paulo use will?
A It is an arrangement that is already in the calendar of both men, with a date and a time.
B He decides at that moment.
C It is something he does every day.
Which sentence is a picture of a moment in the future?
A "We sail to Santorini next month, after we arrive in Athens and pick up the boat at the marina."
B "We would sail to Santorini."
C "This time next month, we’ll be sailing to Santorini."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Plans, Bookings, Decisions and Pictures

GRAMMAR

English has no single future tense. Choose the form for the job.

JobFormExample
A planbe going to + verbI’m going to visit Greece next year.
A bookingpresent continuous + timeWe’re flying to Athens on May 3.
A decision nowwill + verbThe flight is full? I’ll take the train.
A predictionwill / won’t (+ probably)It will probably be windy. It won’t be cold.
A picture of the futurewill be + verb-ingThis time next year, I’ll be sailing.

Never: I will fly on Monday (the ticket is booked) · Tomorrow I go to the marina · This time next year I will sail · I look forward to see you

Order

Two friends plan a sailing trip. Put the story in a natural order.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

Your flight to Lisbon is booked for Friday. A friend at the club asks about your plans. You say:
A "I’m flying to Lisbon on Friday."
B "I fly to Lisbon on Friday if I want, because the ticket it is booked already since last month."
C "I would fly to Lisbon."
At the marina, a man tells you: "Your boat has a problem with the engine." You decide at once:
A "OK, I call the mechanic."
B "OK, I’ll call the mechanic."
C "OK, I’m calling the mechanic next Tuesday at ten, because it is in my calendar since last month."
A friend asks: "What will you be doing this time next year?" You answer:
A "I sail around the Greek islands every year in the summer, when the weather is good and the sea is calm."
