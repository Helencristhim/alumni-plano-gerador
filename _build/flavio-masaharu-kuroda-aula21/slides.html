Grammar Discovery

Now, or Always?

"Right now I'm opening the invoices."

"The system is matching each invoice with its order."

"As you can see, the column is turning green."

"While I'm doing this, tell me who signs first."

What do the purple verbs have in common? Are they about every day, or about this moment on the screen?

Always contractI'm, it's, we'reNobody says I am clicking on a live demo.
Neverpresent simple for a live actionNot now I click · not I opening

In one line: what is happening on the screen is be + -ing; what the product always does is present simple.

Listening 1 · The Presales Engineer

Never Leave a Silence

A presales engineer on her rules for a live demo. Sound first — no text.

1. Why is silence dangerous in a demo?
Because the customer is looking at the screen and does not know what to look at.
2. Where should a demo start, and where should it not start?
With the customer's problem, not with the menu of the product.
3. What should you do if the screen freezes?
Don't panic: say it's loading, and use the time to ask the customer a question.
Listening 2 · The Buyer

The Worst Demo and the Best

A finance director on two demos he will never forget. Sound first — no text.

1. What was wrong with the worst demo?
It lasted ninety minutes, showed every menu and setting, and the salesperson never asked a question.
2. What did the best salesperson ask for before the call?
Three real invoices, so she could process their own invoices during the demo.
3. What did she do after fifteen minutes?
She stopped and asked if that was what his team does today.
