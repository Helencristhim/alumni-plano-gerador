The Demo Without Slides -- Showing the Product Live

A live demo with no slides: the customer looks at your screen and you tell them what is happening while it happens. Key words: to share your screen, a walkthrough, to zoom in, to scroll down, to hover over, a drop-down menu, a tab, a sandbox, a glitch, to freeze. Structure: present continuous for live narration, and present simple for what the product always does.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To share your screen -- to let the other people in a call see your computer screen
"Give me one second, I'm sharing my screen now."
A walkthrough -- a step-by-step explanation of how something works
"I can give you a short walkthrough of the approval screen."
To zoom in -- to make part of the screen bigger so it is easier to see
"Let me zoom in on the total so you can read it."
To scroll down -- to move down a page on the screen
"I'm scrolling down to the invoices from last week."
To hover over -- to put the mouse on something without clicking it
"When you hover over the icon, you see the supplier's name."
A drop-down menu -- a list of options that opens when you click on it
"You choose the currency from a drop-down menu."
A tab -- one of the pages you can open inside a program or a browser
"The approval rules are on the second tab."
A sandbox -- a safe copy of a system where you can test without risk
"We're working in a sandbox, so nothing here is real data."
A glitch -- a small, sudden problem with a computer or a system
"Sorry, that was a small glitch. Let me try again."
To freeze -- to stop moving or working on the screen
"If the screen freezes, I refresh the page."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

In a demo without slides, the customer is looking at your screen, and your job is to tell them what is happening at that moment. For that, English uses the present continuous: I'm opening the dashboard, the system is matching the invoice, the column is turning green.

Short phrases help the customer follow you. Let me show you starts a new step. As you can see tells them where to look. While I'm loading this fills the waiting time with a question. And I'm going to says what comes next.

The present simple has a different job. It describes what the product does every time, not what is on the screen now: the system matches every invoice, it sends a reminder every Monday. Narrating a live demo in the present simple, like now I click or now I open, sounds like a list of instructions.

One last detail: in a live demo, people always use contractions. Nobody says I am clicking; they say I'm clicking, it's loading, we're waiting.

1. Which sentence describes what is on the screen right now?
A "The system matches every invoice with its order, every day of the month."
B "The system matched the invoice."
C "The system is matching this invoice with its order."
2. Which sentence describes something the product does every time?
A "It is sending a reminder right now, while we are talking about the approval rules."
B "It sends a reminder every Monday."
C "It's sending a reminder now."
3. Why should you say As you can see in a demo?
A Because it tells the customer where to look on the screen.
B Because it is the polite way to start a sales call with a new customer you do not know.
C Because it shows that the screen has frozen.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Narrating a Screen

GRAMMAR
