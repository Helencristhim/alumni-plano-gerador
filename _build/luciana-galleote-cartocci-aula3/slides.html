Chapter 3: The Code

The Story, in the Past

Regular verbs, irregular verbs, and the words that give the order

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Show?

"First, we visited the hospital and studied the old network."

"Then we ran into a problem: the walls were very old."

"After that, the team found a workaround and worked at night."

"In the end, the system went live on schedule. We didn’t have any downtime."

Look at the blue verbs. Some end in -ed: visited, studied, worked. Others change completely: ran, found, went. And the red words give the order of the story.

JobFormExample
Regular verbsverb + -edWe installed the cameras.
Irregular verbsa new form: go → went, find → found, run → ranThe system went live.
Negativedidn’t + verb (no -ed)We didn’t have any downtime.
QuestionDid + subject + verb?Did you finish on schedule?
The orderfirst, then, after that, in the endFirst we visited; in the end it went live.
The trapafter did, the simple verbNot did you finished. Say did you finish?

In one line: regular verbs take -ed, irregular verbs change, and after did the verb goes back to its simple form.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Three Stories, at Real Speed

One call, three recordings, one project summary

Dialogue

Can You Give Me an Example?

R
Hi Luciana, thanks for joining. As you know, we have three clients in Brazil, and one of them is a hospital chain. Can you give me an example of a hospital project?
L
Sure, Ryan. Last year a big hospital needed to upgrade its network. First, we did a site survey of the whole building.
R
And what was the biggest challenge?
L
A hospital never closes, so we could not have downtime. And in the second week we ran into a problem: the walls were much older than the plans showed.
R
That happens a lot here in California too. How did you solve it?
L
We found a workaround. The team worked at night, one floor at a time, and we finished on schedule.
R
Great. My client wants a reference like that. Could you send me a short summary by Friday? I am presenting you to their board next Tuesday.
L
Of course. I will send it by Friday, with the dates of the handover and the day the system went live.
Comprehension

About Ryan

1. How many clients does Ryan’s company have in Brazil, and what is one of them?
Three clients. One of them is a hospital chain.
2. What does Ryan say about old walls?
It happens a lot in California too.
3. What does Ryan need, by when, and why?
A short project summary by Friday, because he is presenting Teleinfo to his client’s board next Tuesday.
Listening 1 · The Project Manager

The Data Center Move

A project manager in Bangalore tells the story of moving a hospital data center. Sound first — no text.

1. How long did the hospital give the team for the move?
One weekend: from Saturday night to Monday morning.
2. What problem did they run into?
One of the new servers did not start.
3. How did the story end?
The data center went live at six on Monday morning, one hour before the deadline.
Listening 2 · The Facilities Director

The Cameras That Arrived Late

The facilities director of a hospital in Lyon remembers a camera project with a setback. Sound first — no text.

1. How many cameras did the hospital buy?
Four hundred cameras.
2. What was the setback?
The cameras arrived five weeks late, because of a problem at the factory.
3. What did the partner do while they waited?
They installed all the cabling first, so the installation of the cameras took only one week.
Listening 3 · The Engineer

The Night It Went Live

An engineer from an American integrator describes the night a new access control system went live. Sound first — no text.

1. Why did they choose a Sunday night?
Because the hospital had the fewest visitors on Sunday nights.
2. What did the first nurse do in the morning?
She used her new badge, and the door opened.
3. What did the team do before they went home?
They wrote a short report of the night for the hospital.
