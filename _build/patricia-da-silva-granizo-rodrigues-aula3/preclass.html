Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

The Hard Conversation

The ten words a feedback conversation is made of, the conditional that shows a consequence without accusing anybody, and a real twenty-minute conversation with a manager whose reports are late. Key words: underperformance, improvement plan, to address, accountability, to set expectations, blind spot, candid, to document, turnaround, to follow through. Structure: second conditional in feedback.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read it inside a real sentence. These are the words the conversation is made of, in the register American HR uses.

Underperformance -- work that is consistently below the standard that was agreed
"Nobody had named the underperformance out loud in eight months."
Improvement plan -- a written plan with goals and dates for somebody whose work is below standard
"An improvement plan is a chance to recover, not a step towards the exit."
To address -- to deal with a problem instead of leaving it alone
"We are going to address the reporting delay today, not in December."
Accountability -- being answerable for a result, not only for the effort you put in
"Accountability starts the moment a date has a name next to it."
To set expectations -- to say clearly what good work looks like, before it is judged
"Half of our performance problems are expectations nobody set."
Blind spot -- something about your own behaviour that everybody sees except you
"His blind spot is that he answers a question with a longer question."
Candid -- direct and honest, even when the message is uncomfortable
"I would rather be candid in March than sympathetic in December."
To document -- to write down what was said and agreed, so that it exists later
"If it is not documented, the conversation did not happen."
Turnaround -- a change from poor results to good ones
"That team is the clearest turnaround we have had in three years."
To follow through -- to do the thing you said you would do, all the way to the end
"He agrees in the room every time and then does not follow through."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

The report had been late seven months out of eight, and nobody had said it out loud. If somebody had named it in March, the conversation in November would have been much smaller. That is what silence costs: not the problem itself, but the size the problem reaches while nobody addresses it.

Her opening was four sentences long. If the report arrived on the Friday, she said, corporate would have the numbers before the call. If she were in his position, she added, she would ask for help earlier. And then the question that did the real work: what would you do if the same thing happened next month?

1. If somebody had named it in March, the conversation would have been smaller. What does this sentence tell you?
A That somebody did name it in March.
B That nobody named it in March, and the speaker is imagining the result if they had.
C That the conversation in November was small.
2. Why does she say if the report arrived on the Friday instead of your report is always late?
A Because it is more formal.
B Because it shows the consequence without asking the person to defend themselves.
C Because the present simple would be grammatically wrong there.
3. What would you do if the same thing happened next month? Why does the writer call this the question that does the real work?
A Because it moves the responsibility for the solution to the other person.
B Because it is the only question in the passage.
C Because it repeats what she has already said.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Second Conditional in Feedback

GRAMMAR

A past form used for something that has not happened. In a difficult conversation it does one job better than anything else: it turns an accusation into a picture that both of you can look at.

FormWhat it doesExample
If + past simple, would + verban imagined situation and its result: the message becomes a picture instead of an accusationIf you sent the numbers on Friday, I would have time to check them.
could / might instead of wouldsoftens the result even further, so nobody has to defend itIf we changed the deadline, that might solve half of it.
the past form is not past timeit marks distance from reality, not something that happenedIf I were in your position, I would ask earlier.
were, not wasthe careful form after if with I, he and sheIf I were you, I would document it today.
why it works in feedbackthe person can picture the behaviour without being accused of it. Instead of you never send it on time, say if you sent it on Friday, I would have time to check it.

Compare the two openings out loud: you are always late and if it arrived on Friday, I would have time to check it. The first asks for a defence. The second asks for a solution.

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

The shape of a feedback conversation that works, from the first sentence to the last.

?Here is what I would like: Friday at noon, from next month.
?I want to be candid with you about the monthly report.
?I am going to document what we agreed, so that neither of us remembers it differently.
?It has been late seven months out of eight, and if it arrived on the Friday I would have time to check it.
?What would you do if the same thing happened next month?

Speaking

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

One right answer each. Read the situation before you look at the options.

A manager's reports have been late for eight months and nobody has mentioned it. What is the first sentence?
A Your reports are always late and it is becoming a problem.
B I want to be candid with you about the monthly report.
C I do not want to make this awkward, but there is something small.
D Are you aware that your reports are late?
He answers that nobody ever told him the real deadline. What is the professional move?
A Say that the deadline was obvious.
B Take that share of it, then restate the standard clearly.
C Change the subject to his other results.
D End the meeting and send the deadline by email.
Which sentence shows the consequence without accusing him?
A You never send it on time.
B If it arrived on the Friday, corporate would have the numbers before the call.
C Everybody else manages to send it on Friday.
D This is the third time we have discussed this.
The conversation ends in agreement. What still has to happen?
A Nothing -- an agreement between two adults is enough.
B Document what was agreed and send it to him to confirm.
C Tell his line manager what was said.
D Wait a month and see whether it improves.
