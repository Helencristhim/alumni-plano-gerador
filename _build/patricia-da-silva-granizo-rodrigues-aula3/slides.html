Chapter 3: The Conversation

Twenty Minutes With Doug

A plant manager whose reports have been late for eight months

Dialogue

Naming It Out Loud

P
Doug, thanks for making the time. I want to be candid with you about the monthly report.
D
Sure. I know it has been tight. It has been a hard quarter down here.
P
It has. And the report has been late seven months out of eight. If it arrived on the Friday, corporate would have the numbers before the call. As it is, I am reading them out from memory.
D
That is fair. Honestly, nobody has ever told me that Friday was the real deadline. I was working to the Tuesday.
P
Then that is on me. Nobody set expectations properly, and that is half of this. Here is what I would like. Friday at noon, from next month, and you send it even if two numbers are missing.
D
Even if it is incomplete? That is the part I would push back on. I do not like sending something half done.
P
I know. But a report with two gaps on Friday is worth more to me than a perfect one on Tuesday. And I am going to document what we agreed today, so that neither of us is remembering it differently in March.
D
Understood. Friday at noon, gaps and all. Send me what you write down and I will confirm it.
Comprehension

About Doug

1. What reason does Doug give for the late reports, and what does he say about the deadline?
He says it has been a hard quarter, and that nobody had ever told him Friday was the real deadline -- he was working to the Tuesday.
2. What is the one thing Doug pushes back on, and why?
Sending the report even when two numbers are missing. He says he does not like sending something half done.
3. How does Doug close the conversation?
He accepts Friday at noon with gaps, and asks her to send him what she writes down so that he can confirm it.

Chapter 4: The Code

The Sentence That Shows the Consequence

How to make somebody see the result without accusing them of causing it

Grammar Discovery

If You Sent It on Friday...

"If you sent me the numbers on Friday, I would have time to check them."

"If we changed the deadline, that might solve half of the problem."

"If I were in your position, I would ask for help earlier."

"What would you do if the same thing happened next month?"

Every one of these is about the future, and every verb is in a past form. What is the past form actually doing here?

FormWhat it doesExample
If + past simple, would + verban imagined situation and its result: the message becomes a picture instead of an accusationIf you sent the numbers on Friday, I would have time to check them.
could / might instead of wouldsoftens the result even further, so nobody has to defend itIf we changed the deadline, that might solve half of it.
the past form is not past timeit marks distance from reality, not something that happenedIf I were in your position, I would ask earlier.
were, not wasthe careful form after if with I, he and sheIf I were you, I would document it today.
why it works in feedbackthe person can picture the behaviour without being accused of it. Instead of you never send it on time, say if you sent it on Friday, I would have time to check it.

This is the difference between you are always late and if it arrived on Friday, I would have time. The first asks the person to defend themselves. The second asks them to look at the same picture you are looking at.

Chapter 5: The Ear

Two Voices on the Same Problem

What an improvement plan really is, and what feedback felt like from the other side

Listening 1

What an Improvement Plan Actually Is

A US HR director, talking to a group of new managers. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What does he say an improvement plan is not, and what does that use of it tell the employees?
It is not the paperwork you do before letting somebody go. If that is what it is, the company is using it as a legal shield and the people know it.
2. What are the three things a real plan contains?
A behaviour described precisely enough that a stranger could say whether it happened, a date -- usually thirty, sixty or ninety days -- and the support the company is putting in.
3. What is his final point about the person hearing it for the first time?
If it is the first they are hearing of it, the plan is not the problem: the eleven months of silence before it are.
Listening 2

The Feedback That Actually Landed

A manager remembers one conversation. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What exactly did the director say the manager does?
That she answers every question with a longer question, so people leave her meetings without a decision and think it is their fault.
2. What did the director say would happen if she gave one answer first?
Half of her meetings would be shorter.
3. Why does the manager say the feedback landed?
Because the director described something she does, not something she is -- she said she answers questions with questions rather than that she was indecisive, and that was something the manager could check.

Chapter 6: Your Turn

The Part Where You Talk

Guided

Open the Conversation

Task: Open a feedback conversation in four sentences: name the behaviour with a number and a date, say what it costs, use one second conditional, and ask a question. Use the frames below. Then do it again with the frames covered.

Support:

I want to be candid with you about...It has been ... out of ...If it arrived on..., I would...What would you do if...Here is what I would like.
Semi-free

When They Push Back

Task: Your teacher plays a manager who first blames the systems, then blames another department, and finally says nobody ever told him. Hold the line on the behaviour without raising your voice and without agreeing that it is somebody else's problem. You may not use the words wrong, fault or must.

Support:

That is fair, and...Let me separate two things.If that changed, would...What I need from you is...I am going to document...
Free

Your Own Person, No Script

Task: Think of a real conversation you have been postponing. Hold it now, out loud, for three minutes. Your teacher plays the other person and will not make it easy. Nobody needs to know who the person is.

No support. Target: name one behaviour precisely, use two conditionals, and close with an agreement that has a date in it.
