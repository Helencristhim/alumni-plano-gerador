It Was Launched Last Year -- The Language of the Announcement

The passive voice, and what it hides when a company writes about itself. Key expressions: to implement, rollout, to be staffed, statement, to revise, to issue, to be affected, pending, as of, to notify, in stages, mandatory. Structure: be plus the third form -- present, past, present perfect and future -- and when the passive is the right choice.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. These twelve stay here in your material -- they do not disappear after the class.

To implement -- to put a decision or a plan into practice
"The new structure will be implemented in stages."
Rollout -- the gradual introduction of a new system across a company
"The rollout of the booking platform starts in March."
To be staffed -- to have all the positions in a team filled
"The Bogota office has been fully staffed since last week."
Statement -- an official written position on something
"A statement was issued to all four countries on Monday."
To revise -- to change a document or a price after reviewing it
"The corporate fare structure has been revised."
To issue -- to send out an official document or instruction
"The revised guidelines were issued on Monday morning."
To be affected -- to be changed by a decision that somebody else took
"Existing contracts are not affected by the revision."
Pending -- waiting for a decision that has not been taken yet
"The waiver request is still pending."
As of -- starting from that exact date
"As of the first of March, the old fare codes are no longer valid."
To notify -- to tell somebody officially that something has happened
"Clients will be notified by their account manager, not by a system."
In stages -- done in separate steps, one after another
"The platform will be introduced in stages, country by country."
Mandatory -- something that is required, not optional
"Attendance at the training session is mandatory."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The quarterly announcement arrives on a Monday and it is written the way these documents are always written. Three points, no names, and a great many sentences in which something happens and nobody appears to have done it.

Point one. The regional office in Bogota has been fully staffed as of last week, and the two remaining positions were filled internally. Point two. The new booking platform will be rolled out in March, in stages, and training sessions are going to be scheduled for every account manager beforehand. Attendance is mandatory. Point three. The corporate fare structure has been revised. Existing contracts are not affected, and clients will be notified by their account manager.

Most of that is written well. When a process matters more than the person behind it, the passive is exactly the right tool: nobody needs to know who schedules the training. But one sentence in point three is different. The corporate fare structure has been revised tells a client that his price may change and refuses to say who decided it or when. The answer, if you ask, is simple: pricing revised it in March and the regional director approved it two weeks ago.

So there is a habit worth building, on both sides. When you read a company email, ask every sentence a single question: who did this? And when you write one, remember that the passive describes a process beautifully and delivers bad news badly. A statement that says a request is pending is fine. A statement that says a request has been refused, with no name attached, is not.

1. Why does the text defend the passive in point two?
A Because it is more formal and formal is always better.
B Because the process matters and nobody needs to know who schedules the training.
C Because the writer did not know who was responsible.
2. What is the problem with "the corporate fare structure has been revised"?
A It tells a client his price may change and refuses to say who decided.
B It is grammatically incorrect.
C It uses the wrong tense.
3. What single question does the text tell you to ask of every corporate sentence?
A When did this happen?
B Who did this?
C How much will it cost?
4. What does pending mean?
A Already decided, but not announced.
B Refused, but not yet communicated.
C Still waiting for a decision that has not been taken.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive Voice

GRAMMAR

The sentence that puts the action first and the person last -- or nowhere at all. It is the default style of every corporate announcement you receive, and knowing how it is built is half of understanding those emails.

FormWhat it saysExample
be + third formThe thing that receives the action becomes the subject. Only be changes tense; the third form never moves.is operated · was opened · has been approved · will be notified
by + the agentThe person or company appears only if you choose to show them. Most corporate sentences leave it out."approved by the director"
"operated by three airlines"
When it is the right choiceWhen the process matters more than the person, when the agent is obvious, or when naming somebody would be unfair."The platform will be rolled out in March."
When it is the wrong choiceBad news, and anything where the reader is waiting to know who decided. Then it sounds like hiding.NOT "Your request has been refused."
Say who refused it, and why.
The trapAfter be comes the third form, not the past: take, took, taken · write, wrote, written.NOT "The decision was took."
"The decision was taken."

A habit worth building when you read a company email: ask every sentence who did this? Half the time the answer is missing, and noticing that is already half of understanding the message.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are reading the quarterly announcement and then calling a worried client. Choose the best thing to say.

1. You want to describe the launch of the platform. You say:
A The platform will be roll out in March.
B The platform will be rolled out in March, in stages.
C The platform will roll out in March by the company.
2. You want to say the decision came from headquarters. You say:
A The decision was took at headquarters.
B The decision was taken at headquarters, not here.
C The decision taken at headquarters, not here.
3. A client asks whether his contract changes. You say:
A Existing contracts are not affected by the revision.
B Existing contracts do not affect by the revision.
C Existing contracts are not affect by the revision.
4. You have to tell a colleague that compliance refused his waiver. The better sentence is:
A Your waiver request has been refused.
B Compliance refused the waiver, because the contract was signed in March.
C The waiver was refused, unfortunately.
5. You want to say the change starts exactly on the first of March. You say:
A Since the first of March, the old fare codes will not be valid.
B As of the first of March, the old fare codes are no longer valid.
C From the first of March, the old fare codes were no longer valid.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer, and nobody is scoring it.

Take one real change your company announced this year and record the announcement out loud, in about ninety seconds and in three points. Use the passive for the parts that describe a process, and use one active sentence, with a name and a date, for the part where hiding the person would be unfair. Then say the one line you would add if you were calling a client instead of writing to everybody.
