Fluency Under Pressure -- When the Conversation Goes Off-Script

Fluent people are not the ones who never get lost -- they are the ones who have a sentence ready for the moment they do. Key phrases: bear with me, off the top of my head, let me rephrase that, to be clear, in other words, hold that thought, if I follow you correctly, long story short, let me double-check, just to confirm, I will get back to you, give me a second. Structure: indirect questions for clarification -- could you tell me what the deadline is, do you know when they signed it -- and the narrowing check, do you mean X, or Y?

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each phrase and read what it does. These are not decorations -- each one buys you a second, narrows a question, or postpones an answer without guessing.

Bear with me -- please wait a moment while I finish or find something
"Bear with me for a second, I want to get this right."
Off the top of my head -- without checking, from memory only
"Off the top of my head, the notice period there is thirty days."
Let me rephrase that -- I am going to say the same thing in better words
"Let me rephrase that. The risk is in the renewal, not in the price."
To be clear -- I am about to remove any doubt about what I mean
"To be clear, that clause is not signed yet."
In other words -- here is the same idea said more simply
"In other words, we cannot leave the contract before March."
Hold that thought -- keep your question, I will come back to it
"Hold that thought -- let me finish the first point first."
If I follow you correctly -- I think you mean this, and I am checking
"If I follow you correctly, you want the cap in the local annex."
Long story short -- here is the conclusion without the details
"Long story short, we can sign, but not this week."
Let me double-check -- I will confirm it before I answer
"Let me double-check that clause before I give you a number."
Just to confirm -- I am repeating it back so we both agree
"Just to confirm, do you mean the master agreement, or the local one?"
I will get back to you -- I do not have the answer now, and I will send it later
"I will get back to you before the end of the day."
Give me a second -- I need a short pause before I answer
"Give me a second, I am opening the file now."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

The call is almost over when the manager changes the subject and asks about a contract Ana has never opened. She does not go silent, and she does not guess. First she buys time: bear with me for a second, give me a second to open it. Then she narrows the question. He has used three letters she does not know, so she asks an indirect question: just to confirm, could you tell me what MFN stands for in this contract? Notice the shape. The direct question is "what does MFN stand for?", but after could you tell me the word order goes back to subject and verb, and the does disappears. She uses the same shape twice more: do you know when they signed it, and I was wondering whether the cap applies here. When he answers, she reports what she has: off the top of my head, the wording there is wider than ours. And then she parks it. Let me double-check that, and I will get back to you before the end of the day. To be clear, she is not saying she does not know. She is saying she will not invent.

1. Why does the text say "could you tell me what MFN stands for" and not "what does MFN stand for"?
A Because "stands" is the only correct form of the verb.
B Because inside an indirect question the word order goes back to subject and verb, and "does" disappears.
C Because the question is about the past.
2. In "do you know when they signed it", where did "did" go?
A It disappeared, and the main verb "signed" carries the past.
B It moved to the end of the sentence.
C It is optional, so both forms are correct.
3. Why does Ana say "off the top of my head" before giving the information?
A To sound more formal in front of a manager.
B To say that the information is confidential.
C To warn him that she is answering from memory and has not checked it.
4. What does the last sentence of the text mean?
A That saying "I do not know" is unprofessional.
B That postponing an answer with a time is professional, and inventing one is not.
C That she should have answered immediately with a number.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Indirect Questions for Clarification

GRAMMAR

The opener carries the politeness. Everything after it behaves like a statement.

FormUseExample
Direct questionVerb before subject. Fine with a colleague, sharp with a client on a first call.What is the deadline?
Could you tell me / Do you know + question word + subject + verbThe polite check. After the opener, normal statement order comes back.Could you tell me what the deadline is?
do / does / didThey disappear inside the indirect question. The main verb carries the tense.Do you know when they signed it?
I was wondering + if / whetherFor a yes / no question, when there is no question word.I was wondering whether the cap applies here.
Do you mean X, or Y?The narrowing check. It asks the other person to choose instead of repeating.Do you mean the cap, or the penalty?
Four trapsNever keep the inversion ("could you tell me what is the deadline"). Never keep the auxiliary ("do you know when did they sign"). "What?" and "Repeat, please" are correct English and socially expensive. And "sorry, my English is not very good" is not a question at all -- it does not bring you the information you need.

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

The manager asks about a contract you have never opened. You need twenty seconds to find it. You say:
A Nothing. You open the file in silence and answer when you are ready.
B "Bear with me for a second, that one is not in front of me."
C "Sorry, my English is not very good today."

A "Could you tell me what does that mean?"
B "Just to confirm, could you tell me what that stands for in this contract?"

You caught half the question. It is either the cap or the penalty. You say:
A "Sorry, do you mean the cap, or the penalty?"

C "Yes, yes."

Which of these is a correct indirect question?
