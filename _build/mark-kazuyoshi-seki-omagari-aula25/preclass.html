The Game, a Game, Games -- Articles in Real Use

Explain something the other person has never seen. Introduce it with a or an, refer back to it with the, use the when there is only one of something, and drop the article completely when you mean the whole category. Key words: genre, learning curve, grind, sandbox, replay value, cutscene, soundtrack, franchise, niche, mainstream, word of mouth, get into.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. These are the words people use when they describe something they love to somebody who has never tried it.

Genre -- a type of film, game or book with its own rules and its own audience
"Survival is a genre people either love or never touch."
Learning curve -- how hard something is to learn in the first hours
"The learning curve is brutal, and then it stops being brutal."
Grind -- a long, repetitive stretch you go through only to make progress
"There is a grind in the middle, and I am not going to pretend there is not."
Sandbox -- a world that hands you the tools and no fixed path
"It is a sandbox: the game gives you the tools and then leaves."
Replay value -- the reason to go back and do the whole thing a second time
"I have finished it three times, so the replay value is real."
Cutscene -- a short film inside a game that you watch instead of play
"There are almost no cutscenes, so nothing stops to explain the plot."
Soundtrack -- the music written for one film, series or game
"The soundtrack is the part nobody expects."
Franchise -- a series of games or films that share one name and one world
"It is not a mainstream franchise, and it never tried to be."
Niche -- loved by a small, specific audience rather than by everybody
"It is niche, and the people who play it never stop talking about it."
Mainstream -- known and accepted by most people, not just by fans
"The franchise went mainstream the year the film came out."
Word of mouth -- people telling each other about something, with no advertising
"It grew by word of mouth, which is why you have never heard of it."
Get into -- to start enjoying something enough to keep going with it
"If you want to get into it, skip the tutorial."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the post and answer the questions. Watch what happens to the nouns every time the reader learns something new.

Somebody in the comments asked me to explain this one to people who have never played it, so here it is from zero. It is a survival game. You land on an island with nothing, and the island is different every time you start, so nobody can hand you a route to follow.

There is a crafting system, and the crafting system is where most people give up in the first hour. That is the learning curve everybody complains about, and honestly they are right. There is a grind too, and I am not going to pretend there is not.

After that it becomes something else. It is a sandbox, which means the game hands you the tools and then leaves. There are almost no cutscenes, so nothing ever stops to explain the plot to you. Games are usually louder than this one, and that is exactly the point.

One warning about how you find it. It is not a mainstream franchise. It is niche, it grew by word of mouth, and I read about it on the internet three years after it came out. If you want to get into it, skip the tutorial and give it three hours.

1. Why does the writer say "a survival game" and not "the survival game"?
A Because it is the first mention and the reader cannot identify which game yet.
B Because survival games always take "a" in English.
C Because he is not sure which game he means.
2. Why does "an island" become "the island" in the very next line?
A Because the second sentence is more formal than the first.
B Because it has already been introduced, so the reader now knows which island is meant.
C Because there is only one island in the whole world.
3. Why is there no article at all before "Games are usually louder than this one"?
A Because the sentence is about the whole category, not about particular games.
B Because the noun is plural, and plural nouns never take an article.
C Because the writer forgot it, which happens in informal writing.
4. Why does "the internet" take "the"?
A Because it was mentioned earlier in the post.
B Because every technology word takes "the" in English.
C Because there is only one of it, so the reader can identify it without any introduction.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- A, The, and No Article

GRAMMAR

The rule is not about the noun. It is about how much the person listening already knows.

FormUse it whenExample
a / anYou are introducing one member of a group, and the listener cannot identify it yet."I found a game you would like." / "She is a journalist."
the (second mention)You already introduced it, so the listener can identify which one you mean."There is a crafting system. The crafting system is where people give up."
the (only one)There is only one in the conversation, or the words after it point to one."the internet" / "the news" / "the first game in the series"
no articleYou mean the whole category: a plural or an uncountable noun in general."Games are expensive." / "Music helps me concentrate."
a or an?Decide by sound, never by spelling: an hour, an honest answer, a university, a European studio.
The first trap"The games are expensive" is correct English, but it means some specific games you have both been talking about. For a general statement, remove the article: "Games are expensive."
The second trapWith school, bed, work and home, English drops the article when it means the activity: "I go to school", never "I go to the school" (unless you mean the building itself).

Stage 2: Put the Five Questions in Order

Order

Listen first, then put the five questions in the order that gets you to the right article.

?Can the person listening identify which one I mean? Then it is the.
?Is it a or an? Check with your ear, never with your eyes.
?Do I mean the whole category? Then there is no article at all.
?Is there only one possible thing, like the internet or the sun? Then it is also the.
?Is this the first time it comes up in the conversation? Then it is a or an.
