Lesson 3 · Small Talk, Wine and Food

The Art of Small Talk

A company dinner, a glass in your hand, and a person you have never met. Nobody is testing your English. They are deciding whether talking to you is interesting.

Chapter 1: First Words

The Last Bottle You Actually Enjoyed

First, one sentence from last week: how long have you done your job, and how much of the portfolio is still open?

Now the real question. Tell me about a bottle you have opened recently, or a region you have visited, or a meal you still remember. No wine vocabulary yet, just tell it.

Today’s Goal

Three Questions, Two Minutes

1. The Words

dry, bold, sparkling, a vintage, a grape variety, a pairing, the finish...

2. One Structure

have you ever ... ? · I have never ... · I have just ... · not yet

3. The Move

answer, add one concrete thing, hand the question back

Nobody at that table wants a lecture about grapes. They want a question they can answer. By the end of the hour you will have three of them, and they all use the same structure.

One Question

Ask Me Something

We are at the same table and we have just been introduced. Ask me two questions about food or wine. Any two.

Then a harder one: what do you say when somebody asks about a wine and you have no idea what to say about it?

Chapter 3: The Code

Have You Ever?

The tense that opens conversations

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"Have you ever tried a wine from this region?"

"I have never been to Portugal."

"I have just tried it, and it is drier than I expected."

"They have already opened the red, but the food has not arrived yet."

Every red verb has two parts. Now look at the blue words and at what is missing from all four sentences: none of them says when.

WordWhat it doesExample
everat any time in your life, in a questionHave you ever tried a wine from this region?
nevernot once, in your whole life — already negative, so no notI have never been to Portugal.
justa very short time agoI have just tried it.
alreadysooner than expected, in a positive sentenceThey have already opened the red.
yetnot until now, in negatives and questions — and it goes at the endThe food has not arrived yet. Have you ordered yet?
Where they sitever, never, just and already go between have and the verbI have just tried · I have never been
The trapthe moment you say WHEN, you leave this tenseNot I have tried it last year. Say I tried it last year.

In one line: this tense is about experience, not about dates. Say when, and the tense changes.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Table, at Full Speed

One conversation, two recordings, one menu

Dialogue

The Year-End Dinner, Before the Food Arrives

N
They have already opened the red, and the food has not arrived yet. I am Nora, by the way. I flew in this morning.
L
Luiz. Good to meet you. Have you ever been to this part of the country before?
N
Never. And I know nothing about the wine here. Somebody put a glass in my hand and I have been nodding politely for ten minutes.
L
That is the safest way to survive a table. I have just tried it, and it is drier than I expected. Quite bold, actually. It would go well with the beef.
N
You clearly know what you are talking about. Is this something you do properly, or just at dinners like this one?
L
I am really into it, but not the labels. More the history, the regions, and which grape variety came from where. And the food, because everything about wine ends up being about food.
N
Now that is a conversation I can have. I grew up on a farm in Iowa, so food is the only part of this I understand. What would you have chosen tonight?
L
Honestly, something lighter. Very heavy reds are not really my thing with a long dinner. But I have never refused a good glass in my life.
N
I have been to about nine of these dinners this year and that is the first honest answer anybody has given me about a wine.
L
Then I will ask an honest question. A farm in Iowa — what did they grow, and are you still in touch with the place?
Comprehension

About Nora

1. Has Nora been to this part of the country before, and when did she arrive?
She has never been before. She flew in this morning.
2. How long has Nora been nodding politely, and why?
For ten minutes. Somebody put a glass in her hand and she knows nothing about the wine here.
3. Where did Nora grow up, and what does she say is the only part of the evening she understands?
On a farm in Iowa. She says food is the only part of it she understands.
4. How many of these dinners has Nora been to this year, and what does she say about Luiz's answer?
About nine. She says it is the first honest answer anybody has given her about a wine.
Listening 1 · The Morning After

Two Follow-Ups From Last Night

Diane, from the events team, leaves Luiz a voicemail. Sound first — no text.

1. What has Nora already asked for, and what has Diane done about it?
She has already asked for his email, and Diane has sent it.
2. What has Nora never done before, and what does she want to talk about?
She has never worked with a Brazilian supplier before. She wants to talk about the harvest calendar.
3. Who has just joined the board, and who has he not met yet?
Tom Baker. He has not met the credit team yet.
4. When and where has Diane put Luiz next to him, and what does she tell him not to do?
On Thursday, in the same room, at seven o'clock. She tells him not to prepare slides, because Tom hates them.
Listening 2 · The New Board Member

Bring Me One Customer

Tom Baker calls Luiz before Thursday. Sound first — no text.

1. How did Tom get Luiz's number?
Diane has already given it to him.
2. Where has he just come from, and which region has he not seen yet?
He has just come from the plant in Rio Verde. He has not seen the south region yet.
3. What has he already read, and what does he want from Luiz instead?
He has read the aging report and does not want it again. He wants one customer, with a name.
4. What has he never done, and what does he ask Luiz?
He has never eaten dinner at seven in Brazil. He asks Luiz to tell him if that is too early.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I can ask a stranger a question about wine or food that is easy for them to answer.
I can describe a glass in one sentence without pretending to be an expert.
I use have you ever, I have never and I have just, and I know that a date sends me to the past tense.
I can say politely that something is not really my thing, and keep the conversation going.
I know the words: dry, bold, sparkling, a vintage, a grape variety, a pairing, the finish, tasting notes.
