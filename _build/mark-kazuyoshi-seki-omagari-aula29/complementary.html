Lesson 29 -- Although It Seems Easy...

Documentary
The Social Dilemma

Former engineers from the biggest tech companies explain how the apps on your phone are built to keep you there. It is a powerful film and it is also a one-sided one, which is exactly why it belongs in this lesson: it never concedes anything to the other side.

Tip: watch it in English with English subtitles, then write the three sentences the film never says, each one starting with "Although", "Despite" or "Even though". That is the concession it is missing.

Podcast
BBC 6 Minute English -- How advertisers make us spend money

Six minutes on the difference between something that informs you and something that is built to sell to you. It is the same line this lesson draws between a review and an advertisement, and the two presenters disagree without either of them shouting.

Tip: the transcript is on the same page. Listen once, then read it and find every sentence with "although", "though", "despite" or "while". Copy two of them and rewrite each one about a game you have played.

engVid -- though, although, even though, despite, in spite of

The same five connectors you worked with in class, on a whiteboard, with the difference between the ones that take a clause and the ones that take a noun written out side by side. Watch it after the lesson, not before: it will confirm what you already worked out.

Tip: pause at every example and say out loud what comes after the connector -- a clause, a noun or an -ing form -- before he explains it.
