Four Verdicts, One Game

Which One Would You Trust?

"Best game of the year. Buy it now."

An advertisement. Nothing here can be checked.

"Forty hours wasted. Do not buy it."

A rant. It tells you about the writer, not the game.

"The world is beautiful but the missions repeat."

Two facts side by side. Nothing says which one wins.

"Although the missions repeat, the world is worth the money."

A verdict. The first half admits, the second half decides.

The word at the front of the sentence tells the reader which half you are standing behind. That is the whole tool, and it is what turns two opinions into one judgement.

Chapter 3: The Concession

Admit First, Then Decide

Although, despite, whereas, no matter how

Grammar Discovery

Four Sentences With Two Sides

Although the missions are repetitive, the world is worth the money.

I complained for an hour even though I played it again on Sunday.

Despite the clunky menus, nobody else builds a coastline like that.

No matter how many patches they release, the ending falls flat.

Read only the coloured half of each sentence, then only the other half. One half is what the writer admits. The other half is what the writer decided. Now look at what comes immediately after each coloured word.

ConnectorWhat comes after itExample
although / even though / thougha full clause: subject + verb"Although the missions are repetitive, the world is worth it."
despite / in spite ofa noun, an -ing form, or the fact that — never a bare clause"Despite the clunky menus..." / "Despite being clunky..." / "Despite the fact that it is clunky..."
while / whereasa full clause, with the two halves side by side"The world is enormous, whereas the story is thin."
no matter how / howeveradjective or adverb, then subject + verb"However beautiful it is, the ending falls flat."
though, at the very endnothing — it closes the sentence, and this is the spoken one"It is worth the money, though."
One connector per sentenceEnglish takes the concession OR the contrast word, never both: "Although it is clunky, it is worth it", never "Although it is clunky, but it is worth it".

The Tool

Three Places It Goes Wrong

Trap 1 · nothing follows despite except a noun

Portuguese lets you carry on with a whole sentence and English does not. Say "Despite the clunky menus", "Despite being clunky" or "Despite the fact that the menus are clunky" — never "despite the menus are clunky".

Trap 2 · one connector, not two

"Embora... mas..." becomes one word in English. "Although it is clunky, it is worth it." The moment you add but in the second half, the sentence has two bosses and neither of them is in charge.

Trap 3 · even though is a fact, even if is a guess

"Even though the ending falls flat, I finished it twice" — it does fall flat, and I did finish. "Even if they patch the ending, I will not play it again" — they may never patch anything.

A reader believes the half of the sentence that has no connector in front of it. Put the part you want believed there, and put everything you are conceding in the other half.

Comprehension

About Nina

1. Why does she start the conversation?
Because he gave the game a seven while everybody else in the group gave it a nine, and she wants to know why.
2. What does she say about the clunky menus?
That despite them, nobody else builds a coastline like that one — so the menus do not decide it for her.
3. Which part of the game does she refuse to defend?
The ending. She says it falls flat no matter how you read it.

What Goes After the Connector

Your teacher on the three families and the trap that costs the most. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she call a review that only praises, and one that only complains?
An advertisement, and a rant.
2. What are the three things that can follow "despite"?
A noun ("despite the clunky menus"), an -ing form ("despite being clunky"), or "the fact that" ("despite the fact that the menus are clunky").
3. Which connector does she call the most spoken one of all, and where does it go?
"Though", on its own, at the very end of the sentence: "It is worth the money, though."
4. What is the trap that costs the most, and where does it come from?
Using two connectors in one sentence ("although... but..."), which comes straight from the Portuguese "embora... mas...".

Chapter 5: Practice

Writing the Verdict

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "Despite the game is beautiful, the missions are repetitive."
"Despite the beauty of the game, the missions are repetitive." or "Although the game is beautiful, the missions are repetitive." (despite never takes a bare clause)
2. "Although the menus are clunky, but the world is stunning."
"Although the menus are clunky, the world is stunning." (one connector per sentence, never although plus but)
3. "Even if the ending falls flat, I finished the game twice."
"Even though the ending falls flat, I finished the game twice." (it does fall flat and he did finish, so this is a fact and not a guess)
4. "In spite of be repetitive, the missions look wonderful."
"In spite of being repetitive, the missions look wonderful." (in spite of takes an -ing form, never a bare verb)
5. "However it is beautiful, the story is shallow."
"However beautiful it is, the story is shallow." (the adjective comes immediately after however, and only then the subject and the verb)

A Verdict in Four Lines

Player review — four lines

The praiseDespite the clunky menus, this is the most stunning world I have played in this year.
The drawbackAlthough the first ten hours are wonderful, the side missions become repetitive after that.

Your cardFour lines on a game, a film or a series you actually finished this year. A different connector in every line, and the admission is not optional.
1. In line one, why is it "Despite the clunky menus" and not "Despite the menus are clunky"?
Because "despite" takes a noun phrase, an -ing form or "the fact that" — never a bare clause with a subject and a verb.
2. What does the admission line do for the review?
It tells the reader where the reviewer might be unfair, so the complaint that follows can be weighed instead of simply believed or ignored.
