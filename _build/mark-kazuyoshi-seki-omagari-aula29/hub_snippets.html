Lesson 29 -- Pre-class

Although It Seems Easy... -- Concessive Clauses

A review that only praises is an advertisement and a review that only complains is a rant. The sentence that does both at once is the concessive clause: although the missions are repetitive, the world is worth the money. Three families of connector, three traps, and one verdict that admits the other side and still decides. Key words: drawback, outweigh, merit, clunky, repetitive, shallow, stunning, make up for, fall flat, admittedly, on balance, nitpick.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. These are the words you need to weigh two sides against each other instead of only cheering or only complaining.

Drawback -- a bad part inside something that is otherwise good
"The main drawback is the price, although the game itself is excellent."
Outweigh -- to matter more than the other side, when you put both on a scale
"For me the world design outweighs every complaint on this list."
Merit -- a real good quality, whatever else is wrong with the thing
"Even though I stopped playing, the game has genuine merit."
Clunky -- badly designed, so that using it feels heavy and slow
"The crafting menus are clunky, though you do get used to them."
Repetitive -- doing the same thing again and again until it stops being interesting
"Although the missions are repetitive, the world never is."
Shallow -- with nothing underneath the surface, so it gives you little to think about
"Despite the beautiful writing, the characters are shallow."
Stunning -- so good to look at that it stops you for a second
"The harbor at sunset is stunning, even on an old machine."
Make up for -- to be good enough to cancel out something bad
"The soundtrack almost makes up for the story."
Fall flat -- to fail to have the effect it was built to have
"The ending falls flat, no matter how well the rest is built."
Admittedly -- used to accept a point first, before you argue with it
"Admittedly, I played forty hours before I complained about anything."
On balance -- after putting both sides on the scale and deciding
"On balance I would still recommend it, with two warnings."
Nitpick -- to complain about very small details that hardly matter
"I am not going to nitpick about loading times."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the review and answer the questions. Notice what comes immediately after each connector in bold -- a full clause, a noun, or an -ing form.

Forty hours in, and here is where I have landed. Although the world is stunning, the side missions are repetitive, and I do not think those two sentences cancel each other out. Let me start with what is not arguable. The light on the water at six in the morning is worth the price on its own, and I say that as somebody who normally nitpicks about frame rates.

Now the other side. Admittedly I waited two years for this game, so read my disappointment with that in mind. The crafting menus are clunky, and despite the size of the patch that arrived last week, they are clunky in a way the code cannot fix, because the problem is the design. The world is enormous, whereas the story is thin: the characters are shallow and the ending falls flat, no matter how carefully the rest is built.

So what is the verdict. On balance I would still tell you to buy it, and I would tell you to buy it on sale. The world almost makes up for the menus, even though it does not make up for the ending, and the drawbacks are real even though none of them stopped me playing.

If you want a story that pays you back at the end, this is not your game. If you want a place you actually miss when you close the laptop, it is. That merit is not a small one, though, and it is the reason the score is a seven and not a four.

1. In "Although the world is stunning, the side missions are repetitive", which half is the writer standing behind?
A The first half, because it comes first.
B The second half, because it is the one with no connector in front of it.
C Neither -- "although" means both halves weigh the same.
2. Why is it "despite the size of the patch" and not "despite the patch was big"?
A Because "despite" can only be used about technology.
B Because a sentence cannot start with "despite".
C Because "despite" takes a noun, an -ing form or "the fact that" -- never a bare clause.
3. "The world is enormous, whereas the story is thin." What is "whereas" doing here?
A Putting two halves side by side, so the reader compares them directly.
B Giving the reason why the story is thin.
C Introducing a hypothesis that may never happen.
4. Why does the writer put "Admittedly I waited two years for this game" before the complaints?
A To prove that he knows the game better than anybody else.
B To tell the reader where he might be unfair, so the complaint can be weighed instead of simply believed.
C To make the review longer before the verdict arrives.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Concessive Clauses

GRAMMAR

A concessive clause admits one thing and decides another inside a single sentence. What changes from connector to connector is only what is allowed to follow it.

ConnectorWhat comes after itExample
although / even though / thougha full clause: subject + verb"Although the missions are repetitive, the world is worth the money."
despite / in spite ofa noun, an -ing form, or the fact that — never a bare clause"Despite the clunky menus..." / "Despite being clunky..." / "Despite the fact that it is clunky..."
while / whereasa full clause, with the two halves side by side"The world is enormous, whereas the story is thin."
no matter how / howeveran adjective or adverb, then subject + verb"However beautiful it is, the ending falls flat."
though, at the very endnothing — it closes the sentence, and this is the spoken one"It is worth the money, though."
One connector per sentenceEnglish takes the concession OR the contrast word, never both: "Although it is clunky, it is worth it", never "Although it is clunky, but it is worth it".
even though vs even if"Even though the ending falls flat, I finished it twice" is a fact. "Even if they patch the ending, I will not play it again" is a guess about something that may never happen.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Take a game, a film or a series you actually finished this year and give it a verdict in four sentences: the strongest praise, the drawback, the part where you might be unfair, and what you would tell somebody who is about to spend their own money on it. Use a different connector in every sentence -- although, despite, whereas and though -- and never two connectors in the same sentence.
