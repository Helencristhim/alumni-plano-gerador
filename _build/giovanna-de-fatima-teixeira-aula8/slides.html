Chapter 3: The Verbs That Break the Rule

Where Is the -ed?

eight verbs · zero -ed · all of them finished

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The water came down the ramp."
"One truck got stuck at the entrance."
"Nobody saw the sun until Saturday."
"They went through the old reports."

Every sentence is finished: it was last Tuesday. So where is the -ed?

TodayFinishedExample
gowentThey went through the old reports.
comecameThe water came down the ramp.
getgotOne truck got stuck at the entrance.
seesawNobody saw the sun until Saturday.
taketookSix people took shovels.
makemadeRay made a decision.
saysaidHe said one sentence to the crew.
havehadThey had two long meetings.
The one ruleThere is no rule. These eight are not spelling problems — they are eight words you learn one by one. The good part: the past form never changes with the person. I went, she went, they went.
Grammar Discovery

And in the Negative?

"We went underground on Monday."
"We did not go underground on Wednesday."
"The rain did not stop for five days."
"Nothing was broken."

Two of these are negative. Look at the verb after "did not". What happened to it?

PositiveNegativeNever
We went.We did not go.We did not went.
He made a decision.He did not make a decision.He did not made a decision.
They took the sheets.They did not take the sheets.They did not took the sheets.
Whydid is already the past. One past per sentence is enough, so the verb goes back to the dictionary form.
Except beThe verb be needs no help: I was not happy. Nothing was broken. They were not there.

Monday Morning, After the Rain

Ray Collins opens the week with two minutes about the week before. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What happened to the truck on Tuesday, and who solved it?
It got stuck at the entrance. Six people took shovels and spent the whole morning on it.
2. Why does Ray talk about eleven o'clock on Wednesday?
Because the lower area was under water at eleven, and he had closed it that morning. Nobody was down there.
3. What is the news from the office?
The delay is three days, not seven, and nothing is broken.
Listening 2

The Same Week, in London

A voice note from Karen, with two things she learned. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What did Karen's supervisor say, and what did the team do?
He said one thing only: everybody out. So they went up, they made tea, and they waited.
2. What did Karen take during the flood, and why does she like it now?
She took photographs of the water for the report, and she says they are the best photographs she has.
3. What do they do every Friday afternoon in London, even in July?
They cover the machines, because a storm is never a surprise there.

Chapter 6: Your Turn

Tell Somebody What Happened

from guided to free — the same eight verbs, three times

Role-Play — Guided

You Missed Tuesday

Scenario: A colleague was away last week and came back this morning. Tell him about Tuesday only: the water, the mud, the truck, and the two hours with the shovels. Four sentences, all finished.

Keywords:

the water came there was mud a truck got stuck six people took
Role-Play — Semi-free

The Call from London

Scenario: Karen calls from London and asks about the week. Take her through the five days: what happened, what the crew did instead, and the news at the end. She will interrupt you once with a question.

Keywords:

Ray made the decision we did not go we covered three days, not seven
Free Practice

Sixty Seconds About a Bad Week

Sixty seconds: a week when something went wrong — at your site, at university, or at home. What happened, what you did about it, and how it ended. Day by day, from the first problem to the last day.

No keywords. No stopping. If a verb feels irregular and you are not sure, say it anyway and keep going.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do

I know eight verbs that never take -ed: went, made, took, saw, had, said, got, came.
I can say what did not happen: "We did not go underground on Wednesday."
I know nine words for a bad week: rain, storm, mud, flood, stuck, delay, cover, decision, news.
I can tell a colleague what happened on a difficult day at my site.
I can tell a whole week as a story, day by day, from the problem to the result.
