Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Three of them are about the weather, three about the problem, and three about the result.

Rain -- water that falls from the sky
"The rain started at five in the morning."
Storm -- very bad weather, with strong wind and heavy rain
"A storm never asks permission."
Mud -- soft wet earth, after a lot of rain
"By nine o'clock there was mud everywhere."
Flood -- when water arrives and covers a place that is normally dry -- it sounds like "blood"
"There was a small flood in the lower area."
Stuck -- not able to move, because something is holding you
"One of the trucks got stuck at the entrance."
Delay -- when something happens later than the plan
"The delay was three days, not seven."
Cover -- to put something over a thing, to protect it
"They took plastic sheets and covered the machines."
Decision -- what you choose after you think about a problem -- you MAKE a decision, you never take one
"On Wednesday Ray made a decision."
News -- new information about something that happened -- it looks plural and it is singular
"The news was good: only three days."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the text out loud, then answer the questions below. Read slowly -- nobody is timing you.

Last week the rain started on Monday at five in the morning, and nobody saw the sun again until Saturday. On Tuesday the water came down the ramp. There was mud everywhere, and one truck got stuck at the entrance. Six people took shovels and worked for two hours. On Wednesday Ray made a decision. He said one sentence: nobody goes underground today. Some people were not happy, because a delay costs money. Then, at eleven o'clock, there was a small flood in the lower area, and nobody said anything about the decision again. On Thursday and Friday the crew did the work that a storm cannot stop: they covered the machines, they went through the old reports and they had two long meetings. On Saturday the sun came back and the news was good. The delay was three days, not seven. Look at the verbs in this text: almost none of them ends in -ed.

1. The text says "one truck got stuck" and "six people took shovels". Why is there no -ed on got and took?
A Because they are not in the past; they are happening now.
B Because get and take have their own past form, and it is not made with -ed.
C Because the subject is singular in one and plural in the other.
2. In the text, Ray "made a decision". What is wrong with "Ray took a decision"?
A In English you make a decision. "Take a decision" is a translation from Portuguese.
B Nothing is wrong; both are correct in American English.
C "Took" cannot be used in the past with a person.
3. The last sentence says "the news was good". Why is it was and not were?
A Because news is always about one single day.
B Because the sentence is positive; in the negative it would be were.
C Because news looks plural but it is a singular word: the news is, the news was.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Verbs That Refuse -ed

Grammar

Most verbs take -ed in the past. The most common ones do not. There is no rule to learn here: there are eight words to know, and they are the eight you use every day.

TodayFinishedExample
gowentThey went through the old reports.
comecameThe water came down the ramp.
getgotOne truck got stuck at the entrance.
seesawNobody saw the sun until Saturday.
taketookSix people took shovels.
makemadeRay made a decision.
saysaidHe said one sentence to the crew.
havehadThey had two long meetings.
Good newsThe past form never changes with the person: I went, she went, they went. No -s, ever.
PositiveNegativeNever say
We went underground.We did not go underground.We did not went.
He made a decision.He did not make a decision.He did not made.
They took the sheets.They did not take the sheets.They did not took.
Whydid is already the past. One past per sentence is enough, so the verb goes back to the dictionary form.
Except beThe verb be needs no help: I was not happy · Nothing was broken · They were not there.
