Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A forecast -- a statement about what is likely to happen in the future
"Our forecast for next year is cautious."
Demand -- the amount of a product that people or companies want to buy
"Demand for heavy machinery is growing again."
Growth -- an increase in size, amount or importance
"We expect steady growth in the second half."
A slowdown -- a period when economic activity becomes slower
"Many analysts are talking about a slowdown."
Interest rates -- the percentage that a bank charges when you borrow money
"Interest rates are expected to fall slowly."
The exchange rate -- the value of one currency compared with another
"The exchange rate is our biggest risk this year."
Investment -- money that is put into a business or a project to make a profit
"Investment in new plants is coming back."
To recover -- to return to a normal or healthy situation after a bad period
"The automotive market is starting to recover."
A trend -- a general direction in which something is changing
"The long-term trend is positive."
Uncertainty -- a situation in which nothing is sure
"There is still a lot of uncertainty about costs."
Infrastructure -- the basic systems of a country, like roads, ports, railways and energy
"Infrastructure projects need a lot of steel."
Steady -- developing in a regular way, without sudden changes
"Growth will be steady, not spectacular."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Next month, Julius has to present the Brazilian market to the board. He is preparing his forecast carefully. He thinks demand for heavy machinery will grow, and he has evidence: the orders for the first quarter are already high, so it is going to be a strong start.

But he also wants to be honest about the risks. Some analysts are talking about a slowdown, and there is uncertainty about the exchange rate. Interest rates are likely to fall slowly, which usually helps investment, and infrastructure projects are expected to continue.

His main message is simple: growth will be steady, the market will slowly recover, and the long-term trend is positive. This time next year, his plants will be working at full capacity.

1. Why does the text say “it is going to be a strong start”?
A Because going to is the only future form that we can use in a presentation to the board of a company.
B Because it is an opinion without evidence.
C Because there is evidence now: the orders for the first quarter are already high.
2. What are some analysts talking about?
A A new plant in Brazil, with a lot of investment from the headquarters and from local partners.
B A slowdown.
C A very fast growth.
3. What will his plants be doing this time next year?
A They will be working at full capacity.
B They will be closing for a long time, because the slowdown will be stronger than his forecast.
C They will be recovering.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Future Forms for Prediction

GRAMMAR

Use will for an opinion, going to for a prediction based on evidence now, be likely to / be expected to for careful, formal predictions, and will be + -ing for an action in progress at a future time. After likely and expected, always use to + verb.

JobFormExample
Opinion, predictionwill (+ probably / definitely) + verbDemand will probably grow.
Prediction from evidence nowam / is / are going to + verbLook at the orders: it is going to be a strong quarter.
Probability, formalbe likely / unlikely to + verbRates are likely to fall.
What experts predictbe expected to + verbInvestment is expected to continue.
In progress at a future timewill be + verb-ingThis time next year, we will be producing in three shifts.
The trapafter likely and expected, use to + verbNot “Demand is likely grow.” Say Demand is likely to grow.

Never: Demand is likely grow · The economy is expected grow · I think the market is recovering next year · We will producing

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The board asks how sure you are about growth. You are careful. You say:
A "Growth is likely be steady, but maybe not, because nobody knows what will happen next year."
B "Growth is likely to be steady."
C "Growth likely steady."
You can see that orders are already high for the first quarter. You say:
A "The first quarter is going to be strong."
B "I think that probably the first quarter will maybe be strong, but I am not sure about it."
C "The first quarter is being strong."
A director asks what your plants will be doing at this time next year. You say:
A "We produce at full capacity next year, in this time, with all the lines and three shifts."
B "We will producing at full capacity."
C "We’ll be producing at full capacity."
You want to say that the market is starting to get better after a bad year. You say:
A "The market is having a slowdown, so the exchange rate is going to be our main opportunity."
