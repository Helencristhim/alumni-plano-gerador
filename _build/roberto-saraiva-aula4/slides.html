Chapter 3: The Code

The Language That Hides the Hand

What was done matters. Who did it, often, does not

Discovery

Who Did It, in Each One?

"The shipment was held at customs for six days."

"Two containers were loaded in Santos on Friday."

"Nothing is shipped without the invoice."

"The transport is organized by our freight forwarder."

Every red part is the same machine: a form of be plus a past participle. In three of the four, the person who did it is simply not there. In the fourth, one blue word puts him back.

UseFormExample
Now, or alwaysam / is / are + past participleNothing is shipped without the invoice.
Finished, in the pastwas / were + past participleThe shipment was held at customs.
Naming who did it+ by + person or companyThe transport is organized by our forwarder.
Negativewas not / is not + participleTwo pallets were not loaded.
QuestionWas / Were + subject + participle?Was the order delivered on time?
The trapthe participle, never the past formNot was hold (hold → held). Not the goods delivered late.

In one line: be plus participle. Add BY only when the person matters.

Listening 1 · The Forwarder Calls

The Voicemail You Did Not Want

Somebody left this on your phone at seven in the morning. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is the situation, and what does he need from you today?
The container is at the port and cannot clear customs. He needs a corrected invoice, today, with the product code on every line.
2. What will happen if the document does not arrive today?
The container loses its place and is moved to storage, and storage is charged by the day.
3. What does he say about the other two shipments?
They were cleared without any problem, because the invoices were prepared with the codes from the beginning.
Listening 2 · The Root Cause

It Was Not the Border

The logistics manager, explaining the same nine days to the team. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. How does she divide the nine days?
Six days at customs, two days waiting for a truck, and one day because the goods were delivered to the wrong warehouse.
2. What does she say the real cause was, and where did it start?
A template. The invoice template was changed in March, when the freight forwarder was changed, and the product code was left out of it.
3. What is her point about blaming people?
Nobody made a mistake twice, so it is not a people problem. If the document is fixed, the same nine days cannot happen again.
Real Document

The Tracking Sheet

SHIPMENT BS-4471
Santos, Brazil → Columbus, Ohio · 40 pallets
Pallets prepared at the plantMay 3 · 40 of 40
Containers loaded, SantosMay 5 · 2 containers
Arrival at the port, SavannahMay 23 · on schedule
Held at customsMay 23 to May 29 · 6 days
Reason on the customs formproduct code missing on the invoice
Truck assignedMay 31 · 2 days after release
DeliveredJune 2 · promised May 24
First delivery attempt was made to warehouse 4, which is not the address on the order.
1. The order was promised for May 24 and delivered on June 2. Where did each part of the delay happen?
Six days at customs, two days waiting for a truck after release, and one more day because the first delivery was made to the wrong warehouse.
2. Read the reason on the customs form. Was the ship late?
No. The ship arrived in Savannah on schedule. The shipment was held because the product code was missing on the invoice.
3. Which single line on this sheet, if it had been different, would have saved six days?
The invoice. If the product code had been on it, the shipment would have cleared customs on May 23.
