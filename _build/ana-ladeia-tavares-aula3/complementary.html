Video
Miss Krystle -- "Music Attorney Explains Record Label Contracts"

An American entertainment lawyer, who is also a musician, going clause by clause through a record deal: the term, the options, the renewal, and the parts nobody at nineteen asks about. It is the contract behind today's case, explained by somebody who signs them for a living.

Tip: stop the video every time she names a clause and say out loud what would happen to an artist who had not read it. Write down the two best sentences you produce.

Podcast
Freakonomics Radio -- "Get Your Share of the Pie"

Barry Nalebuff, of Yale, arguing that almost everybody negotiates from the wrong starting point: they argue about who deserves more instead of asking what the two of them can only create together. It is the theory behind the concession you offered in the corridor today. Full transcript on the page.

Tip: listen with the transcript open the second time and mark every conditional sentence. Then rewrite two of them so that one half is about right now.

Talk
TED -- Adam Galinsky, "How to speak up for yourself"

A social psychologist on the narrow space between saying too little and saying too much, and on how that space grows or shrinks depending on how much power you have in the room at that moment. Fifteen minutes, and it is the academic version of what the radio report called leverage changing sides.

Tip: watch it once with no subtitles. Then answer one question out loud, in two sentences: what would be different in the corridor conversation if the artist had less leverage today?
