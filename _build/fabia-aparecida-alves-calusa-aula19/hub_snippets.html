Lesson 19 — Pre-class

The Tax That Replaces Two

Four new words for the tax reform — replace, gradual, credit, purchase. And one thing only: saying which one with "that" — the tax that replaces PIS and Cofins. Around ten minutes.

Stage 1: Four new words

Vocabulary

You know the tax reform better than almost anyone. What is new is only the English word and the sound. Read each one and say it out loud.

Replace — to take the place of something
"Two new taxes replace five old ones."
Gradual — happening slowly, step by step
"The change is gradual, from 2026 to 2033."
Credit — money you can take off the tax you pay
"You get a credit for the tax you already paid."
Purchase — something a company buys
"Every purchase has tax in the price."

Stage 3: Which one? The one that...

Grammar

One structure this week. To say which tax or which companies, put that + a verb after the noun: the CBS is the tax that replaces PIS and Cofins; companies that pay tax on their purchases get a credit. For a person you can also use who: the client who called. Never "the tax what replaces", and no extra "it": not "the tax that it replaces".

"The CBS is the tax ______ replaces PIS and Cofins."
A what
B that
"Sarah is the client ______ asked about the reform."
A who
B which she
Which sentence is correct?
A The IBS is the tax that it replaces two local taxes, in the states and cities.
B The IBS is the tax that replaces two local taxes.
A foreign client asks about the reform. Which answer can she plan with?
A It is a reform that replaces five taxes, from 2026 to 2033.
B It is a big change, some old taxes go away and there are new ones, I think, with new names.

Stage 5: Say it out loud

Speaking

Say "the tax that replaces" as one smooth block. That small chunk is what makes the explanation sound clear.

It is a reform that replaces five taxes.
The IBS is the tax that replaces two local taxes.

Stage 6: Before class

Speaking
Imagine a foreign client asks you about the tax reform. Record three sentences: what the reform does (it is a reform that...), one new tax (the CBS is the tax that...), and who gets a credit (companies that...). One sentence each — no more.
