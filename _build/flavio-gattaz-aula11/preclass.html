Lesson 11 -- Pre-class

The Interview Warm-Up -- The First Ten Minutes

Before the hard questions there is easy conversation, and somewhere in it a sentence you do not catch. Key words: small talk, to break the ice, to put somebody at ease, rapport, to ease into something, nerves, to catch something, to lose the thread, to rephrase, to buy time, a stumble, to talk over somebody. Structure: indirect questions and clarification requests.

Vocabulary

Small talk -- the short, easy conversation that comes before the real business
"The small talk is not wasted time. It is where they decide how hard to push you."
To break the ice -- to take the stiffness out of the first minutes
"She broke the ice by asking about the trip, and after that it was a conversation."
To put somebody at ease -- to make a nervous person comfortable enough to speak normally
"A good interviewer puts you at ease in the first minute, and it is not kindness, it is method."
Rapport -- the quick, easy understanding between two people who have just met
"We had rapport in four minutes, and the rest of the hour was easier because of it."
To ease into something -- to start something slowly instead of going straight into the hardest part
"They ease you into it. Nobody opens with the failure question."
Nerves -- the physical part of being anxious, which shows in the voice before anywhere else
"My nerves were in my hands and in the speed of my first two answers."
To catch something -- to hear and understand something the first time it is said
"I caught the question but I did not catch the name of the professor."
To lose the thread -- to stop following what somebody is saying halfway through
"I lost the thread around the second clause and I answered a question she had not asked."
To rephrase -- to say the same thing again in different words
"Could you rephrase that? I understood the words and not the question."
To buy time -- to give yourself a few seconds to think before you answer
"That is a fair question, and I want to answer it properly. He was buying time, and it worked."
A stumble -- a small break in your own speech when the word does not come
"There were two stumbles in twenty minutes, and neither of them cost him anything."
To talk over somebody -- to start speaking before the other person has finished
"On a video call you talk over people without meaning to, and the half second of delay is why."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

An interview begins with small talk, and the questions you ask in those first minutes are the polite kind. A polite question in English is longer than a direct one, and the extra length is the whole point: it starts with an opener. Could you tell me..., Do you know..., I was wondering.... What happens after the opener is where Portuguese speakers lose it. "Where is the interview?" becomes Could you tell me where the interview is? The subject comes back in front of the verb, because everything after the opener behaves like a statement and not like a question.

The second half of the rule is shorter and just as easy to forget: do, does and did disappear. "How long does the residency last?" becomes Do you know how long the residency lasts? The auxiliary goes and the ending moves onto the main verb. For a yes-or-no question you need whether or if: I was wondering whether you interview by panel. Notice that nothing is inverted after whether either. Portuguese keeps the same shape for a direct and an indirect question, which is exactly why this one has to be practised out loud rather than understood once.

Then there is the other half of the lesson, which is what you say when you did not catch something. There are three different tools and they are not interchangeable. If you missed the sound, ask for repetition: I am sorry, I did not catch the last part. If you heard every word and still lost the thread, ask the speaker to rephrase: Would you mind rephrasing that? If one word is the problem, check that word: When you say scope, do you mean the size or the list of tasks? And if you understood perfectly and simply need five seconds, buy time with a full sentence rather than a noise: That is a fair question, and I want to answer it properly.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Indirect Questions and Clarification

GRAMMAR

The opener is polite, the order inside is a statement, and do, does and did never survive the trip.

The openerCould you tell me... / Do you know... / I was wondering...Could you tell me how the interviews are structured?
