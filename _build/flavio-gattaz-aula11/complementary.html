Video
Vanderbilt Owen -- "How to Ace Your B-School Interview" (4 min)

An American business school explaining, in four minutes, how its own admissions conversation runs: how it opens, how long it lasts, and what the interviewer is doing while you talk. Short enough to watch twice, and it is the format of your October call.

Tip: watch it once, then write down three things about the format that the video does not tell you. Turn each one into an indirect question starting with "Could you tell me".

Talk
TED -- Julian Treasure, "How to speak so that people want to listen" (10 min)

A sound expert on what a voice does to the person listening to it: pace, pitch, silence, and the habits that make people stop paying attention. Ten minutes, slow and very clearly spoken, and it is about the first minutes of any conversation with somebody you have just met.

Tip: record yourself saying the five survival phrases from this lesson, then listen back with his six tools in mind. Pace is the one to watch when you are nervous.

Podcast
NPR Life Kit -- "How to work with your social anxiety"

Specialists on surviving small talk and on the nerves that show up before an important conversation. Everyday spoken American English at normal speed, with two voices and no music underneath, which makes it good listening practice as well as good advice.

Tip: listen without the transcript first. Every time you lose the thread, note the minute, then go back and read only those parts. That list is your listening homework.
