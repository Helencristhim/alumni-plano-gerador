Chapter 3: The Code

Which One Do You Mean?

Five small words that do all the pointing

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The bakery which opens at six is on the corner."

"The woman whose name is on the sign makes the coffee."

"It is a restaurant where nobody takes a reservation."

"The team that did the Adidas activation already knows the building."

Each pink word joins two ideas. What kind of thing comes just before it?

WordPoints atExample
whoA personThe client who approved the brief is in London.
whichA thingA venue which has a story.
thatA person or a thing, when the information says WHICH oneThe team that did the activation.
whereA placeA restaurant where nobody takes a reservation.
whoseSomething belonging to somebodyThe woman whose name is on the sign.
No commasThe information says WHICH one, so you cannot remove it: "The venue that we booked is in Barra Funda."
Two commasExtra information you could remove: "Renata, who signs everything off, is on the call." Never use that here.
In speechWhen the next word is a subject, you can drop the pronoun: "The concept (that) we pitched was approved."

One move: name the thing, then say which one, inside the same sentence.

The Real Reason

Two Commas, Two Meanings

NO COMMAS — ONLY SOME OF THEM

"My colleagues who speak English joined the call."

The others stayed out. The clause tells me which colleagues.

TWO COMMAS — ALL OF THEM

"My colleagues, who speak English, joined the call."

Everybody joined. The English part is just extra news.

Same words, two commas, and a different number of people in the room.

In speech the commas are two small pauses. If you do not pause, people hear the first version.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"The client which approved the brief is in London."
"The client who approved the brief is in London."
"The restaurant where I go there every Friday is packed."
"The restaurant where I go every Friday is packed."
"The woman that her name is on the sign makes the coffee."
"The woman whose name is on the sign makes the coffee."

People take who, things take which, and once you have used where or that you never say the place or the object again.

The Visitor from London

N
I found a place this morning which was completely empty at seven and packed by nine. It is two streets from the hotel.
A
That is the bakery everybody here goes to. Was there a woman behind the counter whose name is on the sign?
N
There was, and she is the reason I stayed. She talked to me for twenty minutes in Portuguese, which I do not speak.
A
She does that to everyone. She is very down-to-earth, and the regulars who stand at that counter have been going there for years.
N
I am going back tomorrow. Is there anywhere for dinner that is not full of tourists?
A
There is a restaurant round the corner where nobody takes a reservation. It is the kind of place people walk past for years, and it is my go-to when I cannot decide.
N
One more thing. The team in London asked me to find a venue for something in November. Somewhere with character, not a hotel ballroom.
A
Then you want the old factory in Barra Funda, which they have just turned into an event space. We did an activation there, and the insight was that people stay much longer in a building that has a story.
Comprehension

About Nathan

1. What surprised Nathan about the place he found?
It was completely empty at seven and packed by nine.
2. Why did Nathan stay at the bakery?
Because of the woman whose name is on the sign. She talked to him for twenty minutes in Portuguese, which he does not speak.
3. What has the team in London asked Nathan to do?
To find a venue for something in November, somewhere with character and not a hotel ballroom.
Listening

You Asked for One, You Get Three

A voice message from a friend who cannot recommend just one thing. Sound first — no text.

1. What time should you go to the bakery, and why?
Before eight, because after that it is packed and the regulars at the counter are not moving for anybody.
2. What is unusual about the restaurant two streets down?
Nobody takes a reservation, and the owner cooks one dish a day, so there is no menu.
3. How does she describe the bar on the corner?
As a hidden gem that everybody in her building swears by.
Listening 2

The One Person Who Signs It Off

Daniel, with a venue briefing before the call. Sound first — no text.

1. Which venue was booked for November?
The old factory in Barra Funda, which is the one Ana visited in March.
2. Who is Renata, and what is the only thing she can do?
She signs everything off, and she is the only one who can approve a change to the layout.
3. What was the insight from the last event?
People stay much longer in a space that has a story, so the factory walls stay as they are.

Free Practice

The Two-Minute Portfolio

Scenario: A creative recruiter in London asks you to talk her through your work. Take two minutes. Describe two activations from your career: the brand, the venue where each one happened, the people who made the decisions, and the insight that came out of it. Every project needs one sentence that says which one you mean.

No keywords, no interruptions. If a sentence breaks, start it again and keep going.
