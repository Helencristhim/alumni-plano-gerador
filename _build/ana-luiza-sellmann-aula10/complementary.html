Grammar Video
"Defining relative clauses" -- BBC Learning English, 6 Minute Grammar

Six minutes on exactly what you practiced today: who, which, that and where, and how the clause tells your listener which one you mean. It also covers when you can drop the word altogether in speech.

Tip: after each example, say the same idea as two short sentences first, then join them into one. Notice how much shorter the joined version feels.

Podcast
"The Chinatown Punk Wars" -- 99% Invisible, episode 525

Two small venues on the same two streets of Los Angeles Chinatown, the owners who ran them and the bands who had to choose between them. It is a whole story built on saying which club, which owner and which night — and it is about the culture side of live events, which is your world. The full transcript is on the page.

Tip: listen once for the story. Then describe each venue in one sentence that contains "which", "where" or "who" — three sentences in total, said out loud.

Talk
"How public spaces make cities work" -- Amanda Burden, TED (18 minutes)

The woman who planned New York's public spaces explains why one square is packed all day and the one next to it is empty. Clear American English, and almost every sentence describes a place by saying which place it is. Connection to Lesson 10: this is the language you need for talking about a venue and why it works.

Tip: watch with English subtitles and write down five sentences she uses that contain who, which, that or where. Then say the same five about places in your own city.
