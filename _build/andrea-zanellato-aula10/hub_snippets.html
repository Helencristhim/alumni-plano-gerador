My Profile in English -- Your Career in a Few Lines

Your career, the way a recruiter reads it. Key words: a profile, a headline, a summary, an achievement, to lead, a degree, to be based in, a network. Structure: present perfect with for, since and How long...?

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A profile -- a page with information about you and your career
"A recruiter looked at my profile yesterday."
A headline -- the short line under your name that says who you are
"My headline says: Head of HR, people and culture."
A summary -- a short text that tells the most important things
"The summary is the first thing recruiters read."
An achievement -- something good and important you did with effort
"My biggest achievement was the new benefits program."
To lead -- to be in charge of a group of people and show the way
"I have led teams of up to twenty people."
A degree -- what you get when you finish university
"She has a degree in psychology."
To be based in -- to live and work in a place
"I am based in Brazil, and I work with teams in other countries."
A network -- the group of professional people you know
"My network has many HR managers."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Tom is a recruiter. He has worked as a recruiter for twelve years, and he has read thousands of profiles. First he reads the headline, then the first lines of the summary.

Rachel is an HR director. She has worked in HR for twenty years, and she has been a director since 2018. She is based in Edinburgh, and she has a degree in psychology.

She has led teams in three countries. Her biggest achievement: the time to hire is now thirty days. She likes to connect with people in her network.

1. What does Tom read first in a profile?
A The headline.
B The list of all the jobs and the dates of the person, from the first to the last.
C The degree.
2. Since when has Rachel been an HR director?
A For twenty years, since she started in payroll in Edinburgh.
B Since 2008.
C Since 2018.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “Rachel works in HR for twenty years.”
B “Rachel has worked in HR for twenty years.”
C “Rachel has worked in HR since twenty years in Scotland.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect -- For, Since and How Long

GRAMMAR

Use have / has + past participle for something that started in the past and continues now. Use for + a period of time, and since + the starting point. Ask with How long have you...?

JobFormExample
Past until nowhave / has + past participleI have worked in HR for years.
for+ a period of timefor eighteen years · for six months · for a long time
since+ the starting pointsince 2019 · since March · since I was 25
QuestionHow long have / has + person + past participle?How long have you worked here?
behave / has beenShe has been the Head of HR since 2019.
The trapnot the present simpleNot I work here for ten years. Say I have worked here for ten years.

Never: I work here for ten years · since five years · for 2019 · How long do you work here?
