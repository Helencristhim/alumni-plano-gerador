Stage 1.1: Words for due diligence

Vocabulary

You answer all eight of these every year, in Portuguese. Tap Listen and say each one out loud.

Audit trail — the record of who changed what, and when
"Every entry has an audit trail. Nothing is edited silently."
Liability — legal responsibility if something goes wrong
"The contract states where our liability begins and ends."
Confidentiality — the duty not to share what you know about a client
"Every analyst signs a confidentiality agreement on day one."
Data integrity — the numbers being complete, correct and unchanged
"Data integrity is checked before every payroll run."
Scalable — able to grow a lot without breaking or costing much more
"Forty employees or four hundred, it is the same process."
Regulatory — required by the rules of the authorities, not by the client
"That is a regulatory deadline. We cannot move it."
Due diligence — the checking a company does before it signs with you
"Their due diligence took three weeks. We passed."
Breach — when protected information gets out, or a rule is broken
"In a breach, we must notify you within 24 hours."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

An answer sent to a foreign head office. Read it once, then answer.

Dear Ralph,

Four people have access to your payroll data: three analysts and one partner. Every analyst must sign a confidentiality agreement before their first day, and every change leaves an audit trail which we must keep for five years. That retention period is regulatory, not our own policy.

In the event of a breach, we must notify you within 24 hours, in writing.

We can give your auditors read-only access whenever they need it. You should also keep a copy of the register on your side — that is not obligatory, but every client who does it sleeps better.

On volume: the process is scalable, so two hundred employees change nothing structurally. Onboarding may take one extra week at that size.

Could you send me the remaining questions so I answer them in writing?

Fábia
Which of these is NOT an obligation?
A Keeping a copy of the register on the client’s side.
B Notifying the client within 24 hours of a breach.
Why does she write "that retention period is regulatory, not our own policy"?
A Because a rule imposed by law reassures a client more than a promise from a supplier.
B Because she wants to avoid responsibility for it.
She asks for something at the end. How?
A With "must" — because she needs the list.
B With "could" — the obligation is hers, the request is polite.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Four strengths, four different messages

On a compliance call, the modal you choose is the answer.

must — no choice. Law, regulation, or firm policy.
"We must notify you within 24 hours."
should — we recommend it. Good practice, not obligation.
"You should keep a copy on your side."
can — we are able to. The door is open if they want it.
"We can give your auditors read-only access."
may / might — it is possible. Honest about uncertainty.
"Onboarding may take an extra week at that volume."

Two mistakes to avoid: never must to (the verb after a modal stays bare: must keep), and modals never take an s: musts, cans.

Softening without weakening: could and would are the polite versions of can and will. "Could you send the data by Friday?" is more senior than "Can you send the data?". But never soften an obligation: "we should notify you in a breach" turns a law into a suggestion, and a client hears that as risk.

Stage 4: On the compliance call

Quiz
Head office asks what happens if their data leaks. Which answer wins the contract?
A "We would try to let you know as quickly as we could."
B "We must notify you within 24 hours, in writing. That is regulatory."
You are recommending something that is genuinely optional.
A "You should keep a copy on your side — not obligatory, just sensible."
B "You must keep a copy on your side."
Which sentence is grammatically correct?
A "Every analyst must to sign the agreement."
B "Every analyst must sign the agreement."

Stage 5: The due diligence question

Reflection
Record ninety seconds in English. A foreign head office asks three things: who can see their data, what happens in a breach, and whether their auditors can have access. Answer all three — and make it audible which parts are obligations and which are choices. Use must, should and can at least once each, and finish by asking for the rest of the list with could.
