The Pattern

Order them, strongest first

In a breach, we must notify you within 24 hours.
You should keep a copy of the register on your side too.
We can give your auditors direct read-only access.
That may take an extra week, depending on the volume.

must = no choice · should = we recommend · can = we are able to · may = it is possible

The verb after a modal never changes: must notify, not "must to notify" and not "must notifies".

The Pattern

Same message, two temperatures

Direct
Softer — and more senior
"Can you send the data?"
"Could you send the data by Friday?"
"We will need a new scope."
"We would need to look at the scope again."
"You must send it by the tenth."
"We would need it by the tenth to be safe."

must vs have to: use must when the rule comes from the law or from your own policy. Use have to when the circumstances force it. "We must keep the audit trail" — that is regulatory. "We have to wait for their data" — that is life.

Softening never means being vague. It means the obligation stays and the tone drops.

Did You Catch It?

Obligation or choice?

1. How many people can see the payroll data?
Four — three analysts and one partner.
2. Is the 24-hour notification the firm’s own policy?
No — Karen says it is regulatory, so there is no choice about it.
3. What does Karen recommend that is not obligatory?
That the client keeps a copy of the register on their side too.
4. What does Karen ask for at the end, and how?
The list of questions — and she asks with "could", not "send me".
Listening 1

The list from head office

1. Which two things does head office say are non-negotiable?
The audit trail, and notification of a breach within 24 hours.
2. What do they recommend rather than require?
A quarterly call with the provider — they say it should happen, not that it must.
3. What might delay the decision?
The legal review, which may take another two weeks.
Listening 2

The answer that lost the deal

1. What was he asked about?
Who has access to the data, and what happens in a breach.
2. Why did the answer sound weak?
He used "we try to", "we usually" and "we could probably" for things that should have been "we must".
3. What did the client do next?
Asked for the same answers in writing — and then stopped replying.
Your Card

The questionnaire, answered

Vendor due diligence

Aurius Corporate — data and compliance

Who has access to client payroll data?

Three analysts and one partner. Every analyst ______ sign a confidentiality agreement before day one.

Is there an audit trail?

Yes. Every change is logged. We ______ keep the trail for five years — that is regulatory.

What happens in a breach?

We ______ notify you within 24 hours, in writing.

Can our auditors have access?

Yes, we ______ give them read-only access. You ______ also keep a copy of the register on your side.

Does the service scale?

Yes. The process is the same at forty or four hundred employees. Onboarding ______ take an extra week at that volume.

Fill the five gaps out loud. Which ones are obligations?
must sign · must keep · must notify — those three are obligations. Then: we can give them access, you should keep a copy, onboarding may take an extra week.
Which single answer here would most reassure a foreign head office?
"We must notify you within 24 hours — that is not our policy, it is regulatory." Because it is not a promise, it is a rule you are already under.
