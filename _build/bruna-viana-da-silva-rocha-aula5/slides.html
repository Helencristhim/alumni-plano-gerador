One Question

Chapter 3: The Code

Two Could Happen. Two Never Will.

And the verbs know the difference

Grammar Discovery

Real Tonight, or Imagined?

"If the person next to me starts talking, I will answer."

"If she asks me where I am from, I will tell her."

"If I were braver, I would start the conversation myself."

"If somebody sat down next to me in a cafe, I would love it."

The blue sentences could happen to you before you go to sleep. The red ones are imagined — and they use a past form that has nothing to do with yesterday.

JobFormExample
A real possibility (last lesson)if + present, ... will + bare verbIf she asks, I will answer.
An imaginary situation (today)if + past form, ... would + bare verbIf I were braver, I would start it.
The if half steps back one formpresent → past formIf somebody sat down next to me…
With be, use were for everybodyif I were · if he were · if it wereIf I were you…
Advice in disguiseIf I were you, I would + bare verbIf I were you, I would just say hello.
Softer than wouldcould / might + bare verbIf I had more time, I might stay.
The trapnever would inside the if halfNot if I would be braver. Say if I were braver.
Not about yesterdaythe past form here means “not real”If I lived in New York… (I do not)

In one line: step the if half back one form, and the answer takes would. Never both.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

One Table, Two Strangers

A cafe, a coach, and eleven years behind a bar

Dialogue

Four in the Afternoon in Brooklyn

T
Is anybody sitting here? Everything else is taken.
B
Go ahead. I was going to move anyway — this table gets the sun at four.
T
Good to know. You come here a lot?
B
First time. I am five days into a three-week trip and I have been picking cafes at random.
T
That is a better system than mine. So where are you five days into a trip from?
B
São Paulo. I have been saying that a lot this week.
T
My sister lived there for two years. She says nobody sits alone in a cafe in Brazil.
B
She is right, and it is the thing I like most about being here. If I did this at home, three people would ask me if I was okay.
Comprehension

About Theo

1. What does Theo say first, and what is he actually asking about?
He asks if anybody is sitting there, because everything else is taken. He is asking about the chair, not about her.
2. What does he say about his own way of choosing cafes?
That picking them at random is a better system than his.
3. What does he say about his sister, and what did she tell him about Brazil?
His sister lived in São Paulo for two years, and she told him that nobody sits alone in a cafe in Brazil.
Listening 1 · A Conversation Coach

“It Is Not a Question. It Is a Doorbell.”

An American on what small talk is actually for. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say “how are you doing” really is?
A doorbell, not a question. You answer with good, how are you, and then the real conversation can start, or not.
2. Why does she disagree with people who say small talk is fake?
Because it is not the conversation — it is what makes the conversation possible. Without it you would be the person who walks into a room and starts talking about interest rates.
3. What is her advice about the easiest opener in the United States?
Not a question about the person, but a comment about the room: the line, the weather, the thing on the table. Say something about the gate, and then wait.
Listening 2 · Eleven Years Behind a Bar

“Nobody Starts Because They Are Brave.”

A bartender in Anchorage on who actually talks to whom. Sound first — no text.

1. Who does he say actually starts conversations?
Never the loud ones. The person who takes their coat off slowly and looks around first — and only when somebody has given them a reason.
2. What does he do to make two strangers talk, and why does he walk away?
He puts a bowl of something on the bar between two people who came in alone, and then he leaves. If he stood there and introduced them, it would die in thirty seconds, because everybody hates being introduced.
3. What is his one piece of advice for traveling alone?
Sit at the bar, not at a table. He says a table is a door with a lock on it.
