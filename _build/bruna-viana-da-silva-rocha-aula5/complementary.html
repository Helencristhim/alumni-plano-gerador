Film
Before Sunrise (1995) — Richard Linklater

Two strangers start talking on a train and do not stop for a whole film. There is no plot to hide behind: the entire movie is the thing you are practicing — how you open, how you keep it going, how you say the slightly too honest thing and the other person stays.

Tip: watch the first twenty minutes with English subtitles and write down the exact sentence he uses to approach her. Then write the version you would use, in your words.

Podcast
NPR Life Kit — "How to be a supercommunicator"

An American journalist on what actually happens in the first two minutes with a stranger: which questions open a person up, which ones close them, and why "how are you doing" is not a question at all. Normal conversational speed, full transcript on the same page.

Tip: listen once with the transcript closed. Then find three questions he says work on anybody, and ask two of them out loud, with the voice going up at the end.

Video
Rachel's English — "Questions and Intonation: Up or Down?"

Why the same five words sound friendly or sound like an interrogation. She shows which questions rise at the end and which ones fall — and the rise is what makes an American sound curious instead of suspicious.

Tip: after the video, say "Is anybody sitting here?" twice, once flat and once rising, and listen to how different a person you sound like.
