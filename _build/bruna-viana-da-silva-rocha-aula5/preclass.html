Lesson 05 -- Pre-class

Small Talk, Big Moves -- Starting a Conversation With a Stranger

How Americans open a conversation, and the sentence shape that carries half of it. Key words: small talk, a stranger, to approach somebody, awkward, genuine, a vibe, curious, the locals, to break the ice, to hit it off. Structure: the second conditional — if plus the past form, then would plus the bare verb, for everything that is imagined and not planned.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Yesterday you learned the sentence for a real plan: if I get the offer, I will take it. Today is the other one, and it is the one that runs the whole of small talk: the sentence for something that is not true.

"If I were braver, I would talk to more people." You are not braver. That is the point. The if half steps back into the past form, and the other half takes would instead of will. Nothing there is about yesterday: the past form is English's way of saying this is imaginary.

It is everywhere once you hear it. "If somebody sat down next to me in a cafe, I would love it." "If I knew the locals, I would eat better." And the most useful of all is a piece of advice wearing a disguise: "If I were you, I would just say hello." Americans give half their advice in that shape, because it sounds like an idea instead of an order.

Two traps. The would never goes inside the if half — "if I would be braver" is not English, and everybody hears it. And with be, the careful form is were for everybody: if I were, if he were, if it were. You will hear Americans say if I was in the street; say were and you will never be wrong.

1. "If I were braver, I would talk to more people." What does the past form tell you here?
A That it happened a long time ago.
B That the speaker used to be braver.
C That it is imaginary — the speaker is not braver.
2. Why do Americans say "If I were you, I would just say hello" instead of "Say hello"?
A Because it sounds like an idea instead of an order.
B Because it is grammatically impossible to give a direct order in English.
C Because it means they are not sure who they are.
3. Which sentence is wrong, and why?
A If somebody sat next to me, I would love it.
B If I would be braver, I would talk to more people.
C If I knew the locals, I would eat better.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- If + Past Form, Then Would

GRAMMAR

The past form here does not mean yesterday. It means this is not real. And would never goes inside the if half.

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

You have just sat down in a cafe in Brooklyn. Put your side of the exchange in a logical order.

?"Is anybody sitting here? Everything else is taken."
?"Thanks. First time in this place -- do the locals actually come here, or is it all visitors?"
?"That is good to know. I am five days into a three-week trip and I am choosing cafes at random."
?"Sao Paulo. I have been saying that a lot this week."
?"If I did this at home, three people would ask me if I was okay."
?"Anyway -- I will let you get back to your book. It was good to meet you."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to give somebody a suggestion without sounding like an order. You say:
A "You have to try the place on the corner."
B "If I were you, I would try the place on the corner."
C "If I would be you, I will try the place on the corner."
You are imagining something that is not true. You say:
A "If I were braver, I would talk to more people."
B "If I would be braver, I would talk to more people."
C "If I am braver, I would talk to more people."
The person next to you might ask where you are from — a real possibility, tonight. You say:
A "If she asked me, I would tell her."
B "If she will ask me, I will tell her."
C "If she asks me, I will tell her."
An American asks "How are you doing?" as you walk in. The natural answer is:
A "Good, how are you?"
B A short summary of your week.
C "I am fine, thank you, and you? I am from Brazil and I am here for three weeks."
