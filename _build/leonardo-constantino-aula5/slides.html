Chapter 3: The Code

May · Might · Could · Ought to

Possibility, option, and the art of not promising

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"You should consider restructuring before the fourth quarter."

"The client ought to seek an advance ruling first."

"There might be a fifteen percent exposure in the base case."

"We could explore a tax treaty route instead."

Which one is your advice, which one is a possibility, and which one is an option you are offering?

ModalMeaningExample
may / might + base verbA possibility, not a promise. Might feels a little less certain than may.There might be an exposure of fifteen percent.
could + base verbAn option you put on the table, or something that is possible in the right conditionsWe could explore a treaty route instead.
should / ought to + base verbYour recommendation. Ought to is the formal, slightly stronger version of should.The client ought to seek an advance ruling.
must / have to + base verbThe obligation, from the law or from the deadline (lesson 3)Each legal entity must comply with the reporting rules.
Negativemight not = perhaps not · could not = it was not possible · must not = it is prohibited
Only ought keeps the to"might to be" and "could to explore" do not exist. No -s either: "he might", never "he mights".

This is hedging: giving a number without promising it. In an advisory meeting it is not weakness, it is precision.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"There might to be an exposure of fifteen percent."
"There might be an exposure of fifteen percent."
"The rate maybe lower under the tax treaty."
"The rate may be lower under the tax treaty."
"He oughts to seek an advance ruling."
"He ought to seek an advance ruling."

Modals never take -s, and only ought keeps the to. And watch the space: maybe is one word that means perhaps, while may be is a modal plus a verb.

Chapter 4: The Advisory Meeting

Meet Margaret

Partner at a New York fund · twenty minutes, one committee

Dialogue

Twenty Minutes With a Fund Partner

M
Leonardo, we have twenty minutes. I need to understand our exposure in Brazil before the committee meets on Thursday.
L
Let me take you through the agenda. Three points: your structure today, the treaty position, and my recommendation.
M
Go ahead.
L
The fund holds two legal entities in Brazil. Each one carries its own withholding obligation on the dividend.
M
And the tax treaty? My previous advisor told me we were fully protected.
L
Partly. The treaty reduces the rate, but only if we identify the beneficial owner clearly. Before I move on, that is the open point.
M
And if the tax authority disagrees with us?
L
Then there might be an exposure of around fifteen percent. I would not present that number as final today.
M
So what is the bottom line for the committee?
L
You ought to seek an advance ruling before the next distribution. We could also structure the transaction differently, but the ruling is faster.
M
How long does that take?
L
Two months, and the exposure drops to almost nothing. To wrap up: I will send a follow-up with the details by Thursday morning.
M
That is the clearest answer I have had on Brazil. Send it, and I will take it to the committee.
Comprehension

About Margaret

1. How much time does she have, and why?
Twenty minutes, because her committee meets on Thursday.
2. What did her previous advisor tell her about the treaty?
That the fund was fully protected by the tax treaty.
3. What does she ask about the tax authority?
What happens if the tax authority disagrees with them.
4. What does she decide at the end?
She asks him to send the follow-up and she will take it to the committee.

Take Me Through the Agenda

Advisory Meeting Agenda
Leonardo Constantino · ALMA
Northbridge Capital
Thursday · 20 minutes
1.Where the structure stands today: two legal entities, one withholding obligation on each dividend.
