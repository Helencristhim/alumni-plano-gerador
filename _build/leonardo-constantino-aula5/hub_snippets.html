Running the Room -- Leading a Full Advisory Meeting in English From Opening to Close

Opening the meeting, setting the agenda, giving a number without promising it, and closing with clear next steps. Key words: fiscal consolidation, tax authority, transfer pricing, tax treaty, withholding obligation, legal entity, beneficial owner, advance ruling, tax efficiency. Expressions: comply with regulations, seek an advance ruling, structure a transaction, the bottom line, read the fine print, look into. Structures: expanded modals -- may, might, could, ought to for possibility, options and hedging.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Tax authority -- the government body that collects taxes and checks the returns
"The tax authority asked for the full file in ten days."
Transfer pricing -- the price one company of a group charges another company of the same group
"Transfer pricing is the first thing an auditor opens."
Tax treaty -- an agreement between two countries so that the same income is not taxed twice
"The tax treaty reduces the rate on the dividend."
Withholding obligation -- the duty to hold back part of a payment and send it to the government
"The withholding obligation sits with the company that pays."
Legal entity -- a company that exists on its own in the eyes of the law
"Each legal entity files its own return."
Beneficial owner -- the person who really owns or profits from a company, even if another name appears
"The bank asked us to identify the beneficial owner."
Advance ruling -- an official answer from the tax authority before you do the transaction
"An advance ruling removes the doubt before we sign."
Tax efficiency -- paying the right amount of tax legally, with nothing wasted
"Tax efficiency comes from structure, not from luck."
Fiscal consolidation -- a government plan to reduce its deficit by raising taxes or cutting spending
"Fiscal consolidation is the reason behind the new rate."
Comply with regulations -- to follow the rules your sector has to obey
"Every legal entity has to comply with the new regulations."
Seek an advance ruling -- to ask the tax authority for an official position before acting
"We ought to seek an advance ruling before the distribution."
Structure a transaction -- to organize a deal legally, before anyone signs it
"We could structure the transaction through the holding instead."
The bottom line -- the final result that really matters
"The bottom line is two months of work and almost no exposure."
Read the fine print -- to check the small details of a contract before signing
"Read the fine print before the committee approves it."
Look into -- to investigate something and come back with an answer
"Let me look into that and come back to you on Thursday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Leonardo is running a twenty-minute meeting with a fund partner in New York. "Let me take you through the agenda," he opens. "Three points, and then your questions." First, the structure: the fund holds two legal entities in Brazil, and each one carries its own withholding obligation on the dividend. Every entity must comply with the new reporting rules, because that part is the law. Second, the tax treaty. It reduces the rate, but only if the beneficial owner is clearly identified. "Before I move on, that is the open point," he says. Then the number. "If the tax authority disagrees with us, there might be an exposure of around fifteen percent. I would not present that as final today." Finally, the advice. "You ought to seek an advance ruling before the next distribution. We could also structure the transaction differently, but the ruling is faster, and it protects your tax efficiency. The authority may ask for the transfer pricing file as well, so we should read the fine print of the service contract this week." The partner asks one more question, and Leonardo does not invent an answer: "Let me look into that and come back to you. To wrap up, I will send a follow-up with the details by Thursday."

1. Why does he say "there might be an exposure of around fifteen percent"?
A Because he is not sure how to calculate it.
B Because it is a possibility, not a promise, and the ruling has not arrived.
C Because the exposure already happened last year.
2. "Every entity must comply with the new reporting rules." Why "must" and not "ought to"?
A Because "must" is the polite form in a meeting.
B Because it is an obligation in the law, not his personal recommendation.
C Because the rules are optional for foreign funds.
3. "We could also structure the transaction differently." What does "could" express here?
A One option among others, offered to the client.
B A legal obligation with a deadline.
C Something the firm did in the past.
4. What does he do when he cannot answer the last question?
A He gives a number anyway, to sound confident.
B He changes the subject and closes the meeting.
C He says he will look into it and come back with an answer.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- May, Might, Could, Ought to

GRAMMAR

In lesson 3 the modals told the client what is obligatory. Here they do something harder: they let you give a number, an option or a recommendation without promising anything. That is called hedging, and it is how advisory English sounds precise instead of vague.

FormUseExample
may + base verbA real possibility, quite neutral and formalThe authority may ask for the transfer pricing file.
might + base verbA possibility that feels a little less certain than mayThere might be an exposure of fifteen percent.
could + base verbAn option you put on the table, or what is possible in the right conditionsWe could structure the transaction differently.
should / ought to + base verbYour recommendation. Ought to is the formal, slightly stronger version.The fund ought to seek an advance ruling.
must / have to + base verbThe obligation, from the law or from the deadlineEach legal entity must comply with the rules.
Negativemight not = perhaps not · could not = it was not possible · must not = it is prohibited
No "to", no "-s""might to be" and "he oughts" do not exist. Only ought keeps the to.
One word or twomaybe is one word and means perhaps · may be is a modal plus a verb: "The rate may be lower."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each real conversation.

The partner asks for the exposure number before the ruling arrives. You answer:
A "The exposure is fifteen percent, final."
B "There might be an exposure of around fifteen percent, but I would not present that as final today."
C "The exposure maybe fifteen percent, more or less, I think."
You want to open the meeting and take control of the twenty minutes. You say:
A "Let me take you through the agenda: three points, and then your questions."
B "So, what do you want to talk about today?"
C "I will explain about the agenda of the meeting of today."
A question comes up that you cannot answer in the meeting. The professional answer is:
A "I do not know that. Sorry."
B "Let me look into that and come back to you on Thursday, with the follow-up."
C "It is probably not important for the committee."
You are recommending the ruling, but the client is free to refuse. You say:
A "You must to seek an advance ruling before the distribution."
B "You oughts to seek an advance ruling before the distribution."
C "You ought to seek an advance ruling before the distribution."
