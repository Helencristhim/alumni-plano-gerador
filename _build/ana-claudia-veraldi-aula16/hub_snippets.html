It Must Be... -- Guessing From the Clues

A moving truck, a waiter who does not smile and a shop closed on a Tuesday: must, might and can’t for guesses, now and in the past. Key words: a clue, evidence, a hunch, to assume, to figure something out, to give something away, obvious, puzzled, suspicious, to jump to conclusions, to put two and two together, to read between the lines. Structure: modals of deduction must, might and cannot, present and past.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A clue -- a piece of information that helps you understand something
"The surfboards were a clue: they must like the sea."
Evidence -- facts that show something is true
"There is no evidence that he is angry. He is just quiet."
A hunch -- a feeling that something is true, without real proof
"I had a hunch that the shop was closed, so I called first."
To assume -- to believe something is true without checking
"I assumed he was French, but he was from Belgium."
To figure something out -- to understand or solve something by thinking about it
"It took me a week to figure out why the neighbors never used the front door."
To give something away -- to show something that somebody wanted to hide, often by accident
"She said she was fine, but her face gave it away."
Obvious -- very easy to see or understand
"It was obvious that they had a baby. There was a crib in the truck."
Puzzled -- confused because you cannot understand something
"I was puzzled when the waiter did not smile back."
Suspicious -- feeling that something is wrong, or making people feel that
"A car parked outside all night looked suspicious."
To jump to conclusions -- to decide something too quickly, before you know the facts
"Do not jump to conclusions. He might just be tired."
To put two and two together -- to understand something by connecting the things you know
"I saw the crib and the tiny shoes and put two and two together."
To read between the lines -- to understand what somebody means but does not say directly
"If you read between the lines, her message says no."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Saturday, a moving truck stopped at number fourteen, next to Ana’s house. Out came a piano, three surfboards and a crib. "They must have a baby," Ana said. It was obvious. Her French neighbor, Julien, was more careful. "They might be surfers, or they might just like the beach. Let's not jump to conclusions."

The man carrying the piano did not say hello. "He can’t be very friendly," said Julien. Ana laughed. "He might not have seen you. He was carrying a piano!" Then Julien put two and two together: in France, people do not say hello to strangers in the street. The new neighbor might be French too.

A delivery note gave the rest away. They came from Florianopolis, and forty boxes were full of books. "They must love reading," Ana said. This time she had real evidence, not just a hunch.

1. "They must have a baby," Ana said. Why is she almost sure?
A Because she saw the baby.
B Because there was a crib in the truck.
C Because Julien told her.
2. "He can’t be very friendly." What does can’t mean here?
A Julien is almost sure the man is NOT friendly.
B The man is not allowed to be friendly.
C The man cannot speak.
3. Why did the man not say hello, according to Julien in the end?
A Because he was angry.
B Because he was carrying a piano.
C Because in France people do not say hello to strangers in the street.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Modals of Deduction

GRAMMAR

Three small words for how sure you are — and have + past participle for the past.

FormWhat it doesExample
must + verbAlmost sure it is trueThey must be home.
might / may / could + verbPossibleThey might be at the beach.
can’t + verbAlmost sure it is not trueThey can’t be asleep.
must have + past participleAlmost sure about the pastIt must have rained.
might have / can’t havePossible / impossible in the pastShe might not have seen us.

Never: He can’t to be French · they mustn’t be home (for a guess) · it must rained last night · it might is closed. After a modal, always the base form, and have + past participle for the past.
