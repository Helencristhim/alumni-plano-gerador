Lesson 12 -- Can You Say That Again? Clarification and Survival Strategies

Film · scene (YouTube, free)
The Terminal · 2004 · the “goat scene” — Tom Hanks interpreting at customs (~4 min)

One scene, and it is the whole of today's lesson. A man is at immigration, desperate, and nobody understands what he is saying — the officers speak fast, he catches nothing, and it almost turns into a disaster. Then Viktor (Tom Hanks), who barely speaks English, steps in and does something simple: he asks, confirms, repeats — slowly, one word at a time — until everybody understands each other. Nobody there speaks perfect English. And the conversation still reaches its end, because somebody had the courage to say “wait, what do you mean?” instead of pretending to understand. It is exactly what you practiced today.

Tip: watch it with English subtitles — the goal is not to understand everything. Do ONE thing: every time someone in the scene asks for a repeat, confirms, or explains a word, hit pause and say out loud the sentence from your kit that would fit there (“What does that mean?”, “Could you say that again?”). It is not about understanding the film — it is about seeing that nobody understands everything, and the conversation carries on anyway.

Podcast / audio (VOA Learning English, free)
How to Ask for Clarification — VOA Learning English (slow English audio + text, ~4 min)

This is the most useful episode of the week, and it is today's lesson in four minutes. VOA is an American station that speaks slowly on purpose, for learners — and this episode takes exactly your kit: how to ask someone to say it another way, how to ask for a repeat, how to confirm that you understood. You will hear “Could you repeat that?” and “What do you mean?” said by an American, at a pace you can follow, and the text is on the screen to read along.

Tip: listen twice. The first time, just listen. The second time, read the text along with it and stop the audio at every clarification sentence and repeat it out loud. If you can say three sentences from your Survival Kit along with the audio, without thinking, you arrive in class with half of it done. Open and free, no sign-up — the player and the text are on the page itself.

YouTube (free)
A1-A2 English: Essential Beginner Phrases — JForrest English (American teacher, ~12 min)

Look at the start of the title: A1-A2. This video was made for exactly this level — American English, a teacher who speaks slowly, and ready-made everyday sentences. Among the essential phrases are several from today's kit: asking for a repeat, saying you did not understand, asking someone to slow down. You will hear each one said calmly and see how the mouth moves — which is the physically hard part (the could you stuck together, the didn't with the T that almost disappears).

Tip: you do not have to watch it all in one go — it is twelve minutes. Do this: every time she says a clarification sentence (repeat, slowly, understand), hit pause and repeat it out loud three times, tripping along with her. The goal is not to understand (you already do), it is to get your mouth through the movement until it becomes automatic. Open channel, no sign-up.
