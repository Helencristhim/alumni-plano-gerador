Chapter 4: The Code

We Used To, We Would

Habits that belong to the past

Grammar Discovery

Habits That Belong to the Past

"Millions of families used to wait for Thursday night."

"They would sit on the sofa and turn on the TV."

"I used to watch it with my sister every week."

"Now I’m used to watching it alone, after work."

Blue and red: habits in the past that do not happen anymore. Used to works for actions and states; would works only for repeated actions. Green is different: I’m used to + -ing means it is normal for me now.

JobFormExample
A past habit or stateused to + verbWe used to watch it every Thursday.
A repeated past actionwould + verbWe would laugh for hours.
The questionDid you use to + verb?Did you use to watch it?
The negativedidn’t use to + verbI didn’t use to like sitcoms.
Normal for me nowbe used to + verb-ingI’m used to watching it alone.
The trapwould is not for statesNot I would have a big TV. Say I used to have.

In one line: past habits: used to or would; normal now: be used to + -ing.

Listening 1 · A TV Critic

When TV Had a Schedule

An American TV critic remembers how people used to watch series before streaming. Sound first — no text.

1. How did people use to watch a new episode, according to him?
They had to be at home at a certain time, in front of the TV.
2. What would people do the next day?
They would talk about the episode at work or at school.
3. What does he miss about that time?
Watching the same thing at the same time as everybody else.
Listening 2 · A Psychologist

The Science of Nostalgia

A British psychologist explains why we enjoy old songs and old series. Sound first — no text.

1. What is nostalgia, according to her?
A mix of happiness and a little sadness when we think about the past.
2. When do people feel more nostalgia?
When they are stressed, lonely or going through a big change.
3. Is nostalgia good or bad for us, in her opinion?
Mostly good: it makes people feel more connected and less alone.
Listening 3 · A Fan

How a Sitcom Taught Me English

An Australian man talks about the sitcom that helped him learn American English. Sound first — no text.

1. Where did he grow up, and what did he use to watch?
In a small town in Australia; he used to watch an American sitcom every afternoon.
2. How would he learn new expressions?
He would repeat the lines with the characters and write the jokes in a notebook.
3. What surprised him when he visited New York?
People really talked like the characters in the show.
