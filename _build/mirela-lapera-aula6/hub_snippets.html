Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Nostalgia -- a happy and slightly sad feeling when you think about the past
"Old songs fill me with nostalgia."
A comfort show -- a series you watch again because it makes you feel safe and relaxed
"Friends is my comfort show after a long day."
A catchphrase -- a short sentence that a famous character says again and again
"‘How you doin’?’ is Joey’s catchphrase."
A laugh track -- recorded laughter that you hear in a sitcom
"Some people hate the laugh track in old sitcoms."
A rerun -- an old episode of a series that is shown on TV again
"They show reruns of the series every afternoon."
A spin-off -- a new series about a character from an older series
"Young Sheldon is a spin-off of The Big Bang Theory."
A fan -- a person who likes a series, a singer or a team very much
"I’m a big fan of the show."
Timeless -- not old-fashioned; good in any period of time
"Some jokes are timeless."
Outdated -- old-fashioned; not right for the present time
"Some jokes in old sitcoms feel outdated today."
To look back on -- to think about something that happened in the past
"I look back on those years with a smile."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Mirela never watches a series twice. “I watch it once, and that’s it,” she says. But there are two exceptions: Friends and The Big Bang Theory. She used to watch them years ago, and she still watches an episode from time to time. They are her comfort shows.

Why? Because any episode makes sense. She can start anywhere, and she knows every catchphrase. Some people say the jokes are outdated and the laugh track sounds strange today, but for her the shows are timeless.

When she looks back on those years, she feels a little nostalgia. Today she is used to watching everything in Portuguese. A good next step: watching her old favorites in English, and maybe the spin-off of The Big Bang Theory.

1. Why does the text say “She used to watch them years ago”?
A Because it is a habit from the past.
B Because she is watching them right now, at this moment, in the living room with her family.
C Because she will watch them next year.
2. What does “she is used to watching everything in Portuguese” mean?
A She watched everything in Portuguese in the past, but she does not do it anymore.
B She has never watched anything in Portuguese, only in English, because she prefers the original voices.
C It is normal for her now to watch everything in Portuguese.
3. Why are Friends and The Big Bang Theory her comfort shows?
A Because every episode has a big plot twist and a cliffhanger, so she can never stop watching.
B Because any episode makes sense and she knows every catchphrase.
C Because they are new.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used To / Would x Be Used To

GRAMMAR

Use used to + verb for past habits and states that are not true anymore. Use would + verb for repeated past actions (not states). Be used to + verb-ing means that something is normal for you now.

JobFormExample
A past habit or stateused to + verbWe used to watch it every Thursday.
A repeated past actionwould + verbWe would laugh for hours.
The questionDid you use to + verb?Did you use to watch it?
The negativedidn’t use to + verbI didn’t use to like sitcoms.
Normal for me nowbe used to + verb-ingI’m used to watching it alone.
The trapwould is not for statesNot I would have a big TV. Say I used to have.

Never: I use to watch (past) · Did you used to? · I would have a TV (state) · I’m used to watch

Stage 2: Put the Memory in Order

Order

A fan remembers how she used to watch her favorite sitcom. Put the memory in a logical order.

?"At eight o’clock, my sister and I would sit on the sofa."
?"Now I’m used to watching it alone, after work."
?"In the nineties, I used to wait for Thursday night."
?"Years later, I bought all the seasons on DVD."
?"The next day, we would talk about the episode at school."
?"We would laugh at every joke, even the silly ones."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A friend asks about your habits twenty years ago. You say:
A "I use to watch a lot of TV when I was young, every day after school."
B "I was used to watch a lot of TV."
C "I used to watch a lot of TV."
You want to ask your colleague about a past habit. You ask:
A "Did you use to watch sitcoms?"
B "Did you used to watch sitcoms when you were a teenager, with your family?"
C "Would you watch sitcoms?"
Watching series alone is normal for you now. You say:
A "I used to watch series alone."
B "I’m used to watching series alone."
C "I’m used to watch series alone, it is normal for me now after work."
You describe a series that is good in any period of time. You say:
A "It’s timeless."
B "It’s outdated, so people of any age can enjoy it in any period of time."
C "It’s a rerun."
