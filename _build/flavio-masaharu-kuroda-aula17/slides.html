Chapter 3: The Code

Ask Before You Talk

Permission, and a past tense that is not about the past

Grammar Discovery

Pushy or Polite?

"Is it OK if I take two minutes of your time?"

"Do you mind if I ask how you choose your vendors?"

"I was wondering if you had fifteen minutes next week."

"I was calling because you work with mid-sized companies."

Compare with: I want two minutes. Give me fifteen minutes next week. What do the colored words do that these do not? And why are was and had in the past when the call is now?

A Call to Milan

G
Giulia Romano speaking.
F
Hi Giulia, this is Flavio Kuroda from Invent, in Brazil. Marta at Harbrook gave me your name. Is this a bad time?
G
Marta? OK, I have two minutes. What is it about?
F
I was calling because you sell to mid-sized companies in Italy, and many of them delay a purchase because of the SAP connection. We have a pre-built one.
G
That is true, it is a real problem. But honestly, just send me an email.
F
Happy to. Would it be OK if I sent you one page today, and we spoke for fifteen minutes next week?
G
Fifteen minutes is fine. Tuesday at three, Milan time?
F
Perfect. Do you mind if I invite your technical lead too? The hook is the integration, so it helps to have him there.
Comprehension

About Giulia

1. What makes Giulia agree to listen for two minutes?
The name of Marta at Harbrook, someone she knows: it is a referral.
2. What does Giulia say about the SAP problem, and what does she ask for?
She says it is a real problem, and she asks for an email instead of continuing the call.
3. What does Giulia agree to at the end?
Fifteen minutes on Tuesday at three, Milan time.
Listening 1 · The Trainer

The First Ten Seconds

A sales trainer on how to open a cold call. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say most cold calls die in the first ten seconds?
Because the caller talks about the product before the other person agrees to listen.
2. What should the reason for the call be about?
About the other person, not about you, and in one sentence.
3. What should you ask for at the end, and why?
Something small, like one page and fifteen minutes next week, because a small yes today is worth more than a big no.
Listening 2 · The Receiver

One Call in Ten

A partner manager on which cold calls he stays on. Sound first — no text.

1. What makes the difference for him, if not the accent or the product?
Respect for his time: asking if it is a good moment, and calling back at the time agreed.
2. When does he hang up?
When it is clear that the caller is calling everybody on a list.
3. What did the Brazilian vendor do last month, and what happened?
He used a referral from a shared customer in his first sentence. It became a warm call, and they have a meeting on Thursday.

Five lines, thirty seconds, and the product is not on the card. Line 2 is the one people skip because they are nervous, and it is the one that buys the other four.
1. Why is the product not on the card?
Because the cold call is not for selling the product; it is for earning a small next step.
2. Which line does the note say people skip, and why does it matter?
Line 2, permission. It matters because asking first is what makes the other person agree to listen.
3. What do you say if the person is busy?
Ask when is a good time to call back, and then really call back at that time.
