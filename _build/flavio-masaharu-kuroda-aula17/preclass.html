Lesson 17 -- Pre-class

The Cold Call -- Reaching a Partner Who Does Not Know You

A stranger, a phone and ten seconds: ask permission, give a reason about them, and leave with a small yes. Key words: a cold call, a gatekeeper, to reach out, a referral, a warm introduction, a hook, a voicemail, a brush-off, to get through, a callback. Structure: asking permission and the polite past (is it OK if, do you mind if, I was wondering if).

Vocabulary

A cold call -- a call to a person who does not know you and did not expect it
"Most cold calls die in the first ten seconds."
A gatekeeper -- a person who decides who can talk to the boss
"The assistant was a polite but very good gatekeeper."
To reach out -- to contact somebody, often for the first time
"I am reaching out because you work with mid-sized companies."
A referral -- the name of a new contact that somebody gives you
"A referral from a shared customer opens almost any door."
A warm introduction -- when somebody you both know presents you to a new contact
"Marta gave me a warm introduction to her colleague in Milan."
A hook -- one short fact that makes the other person curious
"My hook was that half of their customers delay because of SAP."
A voicemail -- a recorded message you leave when nobody answers
"Keep a voicemail under thirty seconds and say your number twice."
A brush-off -- a polite answer that is really a no
"Just send me an email is often a brush-off."
To get through -- to manage to speak to the person you want on the phone
"I called three times before I got through to her."
A callback -- a call you make back to somebody at a time you agreed
"She asked for a callback on Tuesday at three."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On a cold call, the other person did not ask you to call, so the first thing you say is a question about permission: Is this a bad time? or Is it OK if I take two minutes of your time? After is it OK if and do you mind if, the verb is in the present: do you mind if I ask a question?

Be careful with the answer to do you mind. If she says No, not at all, it means you can continue. If she says yes, it means she does mind, so you stop. Many Brazilian speakers answer yes when they want to say that something is fine.

English also uses the past to sound polite, even when the call is happening now. I was calling because you work with mid-sized companies. I was wondering if you had fifteen minutes next week. Nothing here is about yesterday: the past just makes the request softer and gives the other person the choice.

Finish with something small. Not a one-hour meeting, but Would it be OK if I sent you one page today? If the person gives you a brush-off like just send me an email, accept it and add a date. A small yes is the goal of the call.

1. After "Do you mind if I...", which form do you use?
A Do you mind if I ask a question?
B Do you mind if I to ask a question?
C Do you mind if I asking a question?
2. A partner asks "Do you mind if I call you back?" and you are happy with that. You say:

3. Why does "I was wondering if you had fifteen minutes" use the past?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Permission and the Polite Past

GRAMMAR

Ask before you talk. Use the past to make a request softer, not older.

Check the momentIs this a good / bad time?Hi, this is Flavio. Is this a bad time?

A soft requestI was wondering if + pastI was wondering if you had a minute.
