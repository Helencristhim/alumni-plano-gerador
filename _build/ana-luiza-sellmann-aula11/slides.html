Chapter 3: The Code

The First Verb Decides

Two shapes, and no logic to work out — only pairs to hear

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I finally decided to do it."

"My boss agreed to keep my job open."

"I kept planning it and then finding a reason to wait."

"Have you considered asking for a month instead?"

Look at the second verb in each sentence. There are two shapes. What decides which one?

ShapeVerbsExample
verb + -ingenjoy, avoid, keep, consider, finish, suggest, recommend, practice, imagine, mind"I avoid looking at the website until March."
verb + towant, need, plan, decide, hope, expect, agree, manage, offer, refuse"She agreed to keep my job open."
The list IS the ruleThere is no logic behind it. The first verb carries the shape, the way a noun carries its gender in Portuguese. You learn it by hearing pairs, not by working it out.
suggest / recommendThese two never take to + verb: "I suggest offering one more revision." This is the single most common mistake in a client email.
stop / remember / tryThese three take BOTH shapes, and the meaning changes completely. Next slide.

One move: say the first verb, and let it choose. Do not stop to think in the middle.

The Real Difference

Three Verbs, Two Meanings

"I stopped waiting."

I do not wait any more. The waiting ended.

"I stopped to wait."

I paused what I was doing in order to wait.

"I remember sending it."

I sent it, and I have the memory of doing it.

"I remembered to send it."

I did not forget. The memory came first, then the action.

"I tried talking to her."

I did it, as an experiment, to see what would happen.

"I tried to talk to her."

I attempted it, and it was difficult. Maybe I failed.

Same verb, two shapes, and a different story about what actually happened.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I suggest to offer one more revision."
"I suggest offering one more revision."
"I am considering to ask for a month instead."
"I am considering asking for a month instead."
"She refused listening to my excuses."
"She refused to listen to my excuses."

In Portuguese the second verb never changes, so the ear has nothing to copy. The fix is not a rule: it is hearing suggest offering and refused to listen enough times that anything else sounds odd.

C

Comprehension

About Caio

1. What did Caio ask for, and what did his boss agree to do?
He asked for three months off, and his boss agreed to keep his job open.
2. Why does Caio say it was not on a whim?
Because he kept planning the trip for two years and then finding a reason to wait.
3. What did Caio's sister do?
She refused to listen to his excuses and offered to look after his flat.
Listening

She Paid So She Could Not Quit

A voice message about finally booking the thing. Sound first — no text.

1. What did she finally do, and how did she pay for it?
She booked the ceramics course and paid for the whole term on a Tuesday afternoon, on a whim.
2. What does she usually do every January, and what happens next?
She decides to start, and then avoids looking at the website until March.
3. Why is she reluctant to tell her mother?
Because her mother will want to come with her, and she is even more stubborn.
Listening 2

Do Not Promise Anything Today

Daniel, thirty seconds before you answer the client. Sound first — no text.

1. What has changed about the project?
The client has asked to add two more cities, so the scope has changed, and they seem to expect the agency to absorb it.
2. What does he suggest doing about the revisions?
Offering one more revision and asking the client to pay for the extra cities.
3. What should Ana not promise, and what should she say instead?
She should not promise to deliver in March. She should say the scope needs reviewing and that she will come back to them on Thursday.

Chapter 6: Your Turn

Role-Play

The Thing You Finally Did

Scenario: Tell a friend about something you postponed for a long time and finally did. Say what you kept doing instead, what made you stop, and what you decided in the end.

Keyword chips:

I kept I decided to I stopped eventually
Role-Play

Talk Him Out of Half of It

Scenario: A friend wants to quit his job tomorrow, on a whim, with no plan. You think part of the idea is good and part of it is reckless. Give him real advice: agree with something, suggest something else, and say what you would avoid doing in his place. Then ask him one question.

Keywords:

I suggest I would avoid have you considered
Free Practice

Say Yes to Half, No to the Rest

Scenario: The client wants a third revision of the film and two more cities, at the same price and by March. Answer them out loud, for two minutes. Agree to one thing, refuse one thing, suggest an alternative, and say what you need before you can confirm the timeline. Do not promise a date.

No keywords, no interruptions. If a sentence breaks, start it again and keep going.
