Review & Consolidation -- The Industry Debate

A formal debate at the Jewelers' Forum in New York: should a luxury jeweler sell lab-grown diamonds? Milton speaks against the motion. Key words: stance, rebuttal, counterargument, concede, compelling, drawback, outweigh, consensus. Review of Lessons 17-23: third and mixed conditionals, provided that and unless, what-clefts, used to, gerunds and infinitives, what if.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Stance -- a public opinion or position on an issue
"My stance is clear: our brand sells natural stones."
Rebuttal -- an answer that tries to show that the other side's argument is wrong
"Each speaker has one minute for a rebuttal."
Counterargument -- a reason that goes against another argument
"Her strongest counterargument was the price."
Concede -- to admit that the other person is right about something
"I concede that lab-grown stones are cheaper."
Compelling -- so strong or convincing that people pay attention
"Rarity is a compelling reason to buy a natural stone."
Drawback -- a disadvantage or a problem that makes something less attractive
"The main drawback is that they lose value fast."
Outweigh -- to be bigger or more important than something else
"For me, the risks outweigh the benefits."
Consensus -- an agreement that all or most people in a group accept
"There is no consensus in the industry yet."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Twenty years ago, buyers used to ask only two questions: how big is it, and how much is it? Today the industry is divided. At the Jewelers' Forum, Claire Bennett argues that a luxury jeweler should sell lab-grown diamonds. Milton disagrees. "What our clients pay for is rarity," he says. He concedes that the new stones are cheaper, but he warns that they lose value fast.

Claire asks, "What if a customer only wanted the look?" Milton answers that he would consider selling them, provided that every stone had a clear label. He refuses to confuse his clients. In his opinion, if the industry had been more honest in the past, customers would trust jewelers more today.

1. "Buyers used to ask only two questions." Do buyers ask only two questions today?
A Yes, because nothing has changed in the industry in the last twenty years.
B No. That was true in the past, but not now.
C We don't know.
2. When would Milton consider selling lab-grown stones?
A Only if Claire agrees to buy his natural stones for her chain of department stores first.
B Never, under any condition.
C Provided that every stone had a clear label.
3. "If the industry had been more honest, customers would trust jewelers more today." What is true now?
A Customers don't trust jewelers very much.
B Customers trust jewelers completely, because the industry was always honest with them.
C The industry is honest now.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Your Toolkit from Lessons 17-23

GRAMMAR

Each structure has a job in an argument. Choose the tool for the job.

StructureJob in a debateExample
What-cleft (L18)put the main idea in the spotlightWhat our clients pay for is rarity.
provided that / unless (L19)accept something with a conditionWe'll sell them, provided that every stone has a clear label.
used to + verb (L20)compare the past with todayCustomers used to ask only about price.
verb + -ing / to (L21)recommend and warnavoid confusing · refuse to sell
What if / suppose (L22)test the other side with a scenarioWhat if a client asked for a cheaper stone?
Third conditional (L17)a past that did not happenIf we had known, we would have changed.
Mixed conditional (L23)a past decision with a result todayIf the industry had been honest, customers would trust us today.

Remember: no will after provided that or unless, and no would in the if-part.
