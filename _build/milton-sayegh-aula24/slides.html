Chapter 3: The Full Toolkit

Seven Lessons, One Toolbox

had known · what we need · provided that · used to · avoid selling · what if · would be today

Review Discovery

What Does Each Orange Part Do?

"What our clients pay for is rarity."

"We'll sell them provided that every stone has a clear label."

"Customers used to ask only about size and price."

"We should avoid confusing our clients."

"What if a client asked for a cheaper stone?"

"If the industry had been more honest, customers would trust us more today."

Look at the orange words. Which lesson does each one come from? What job does it do in an argument?

StructureIts job in a debateExample
What-cleft (L18)Put the main idea in the spotlightWhat our clients pay for is rarity.
provided that / unless (L19)Accept something, with a conditionprovided that every stone has a clear label
used to + verb (L20)Compare the past with todayCustomers used to ask only about price.
verb + -ing / to (L21)Recommend and warnavoid confusing · refuse to sell
What if / suppose (L22)Test the other side with a scenarioWhat if a client asked for a cheaper stone?
Third conditional (L17)Talk about a past that did not happenIf we had known, we would have changed.
Mixed conditional (L23)Connect a past decision to todayIf the industry had been honest, customers would trust us today.

A strong argument uses different tools for different jobs: one to be clear, one to set a condition, one to compare, one to challenge.

Chapter 4: The Stage

Debate Night at the Forum

New York, 8:15 p.m. The rebuttal round.

Dialogue

Milton Answers Claire

C
So that's my stance. Young couples want a big, beautiful stone, and lab-grown diamonds cost seventy percent less.
M
Thank you, Claire. Let me make my position clear. What our clients pay for is not the size. It's rarity.
C
But they're real diamonds, Milton. Same chemistry, same fire. Isn't that a compelling argument?
M
I concede that point. But in my business, a ring used to be something you kept for fifty years and gave to your children.
C
And what if the customer doesn't care about that? What if she only wants the look?
M
Then the drawback is the value. If she had bought a natural stone ten years ago, it would still be worth something today.
C
So your rebuttal is about investment. Would you never sell them, then?
M
I'd consider it, provided that every stone had a clear label. I refuse to confuse my clients.
C
Then maybe there is a consensus here. Unless we're honest with customers, we all lose.
M
Agreed. For a luxury brand, trust outweighs everything else.
Comprehension

About Claire

1. According to Claire, what do young couples want?
A big, beautiful stone, and lab-grown diamonds cost seventy percent less.
2. What compelling argument does Claire give for lab-grown diamonds?
They are real diamonds, with the same chemistry and the same fire.
3. At the end, what does Claire say everybody needs to do?
Be honest with customers. Unless they are honest, they all lose.

0:000:00
