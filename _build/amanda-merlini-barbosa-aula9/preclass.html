Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

What They Said in the Meeting

The meeting you attended, told to somebody who was not there. Key words: to state, to confirm, to note, to acknowledge, to warn, to argue, to point out, to advise. Structure: reported speech -- every verb one step back, and a reporting verb that says more than said.

Stage 1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Four verbs for facts, four for positions. Listen to each one and read it in context.

to state -- to say something clearly and formally, as a fact
"Their lawyer stated that they wanted two hundred thousand credits."
to confirm -- to say that something is true or will happen, after checking
"The verifier confirmed that the annex had arrived."
to note -- to mention something briefly, so that it is on the record
"He noted that the discrepancy was still under review."
to acknowledge -- to accept that something is true, even when you do not like it
"She acknowledged that the market was moving, but she kept her position."
to warn -- to tell somebody about a risk before it happens
"Our finance director warned that the price might fall before the spring."
to argue -- to give reasons to show that your position is right
"He argued that we should not accept a fixed price."
to point out -- to draw attention to a fact that the others may have missed
"I pointed out that we could not control the verification date."
to advise -- to tell somebody what you think they should do
"I advised them to link the payment to the issuance, not to a date."

Stage 3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about what it says.

Every week somebody asks you what happened in a meeting they missed, and every week you make two decisions without noticing: which tense to use, and which verb to put in front of what was said.

The tense moves one step back. The buyer's lawyer said, "We want two hundred thousand credits." In your report: she stated that they wanted two hundred thousand credits. The verifier said, "The annex has arrived." In your report: he confirmed that the annex had arrived. Will becomes would, may becomes might, can becomes could.

The verb carries a judgment. To state is to present something as a fact. To confirm is to say it is true after checking. To note is to put it on the record. To acknowledge is to accept a point you do not like. To warn is to talk about a risk, to argue is to give reasons, and to point out is to show the others a fact they may have missed.

Two patterns are worth remembering. You advise somebody to do something: "I advised them to link the payment to the issuance." And you tell somebody, but you say something: "She told me that the price was fixed", never "she said me".

Stage 4: Grammar Tip -- reported speech

GRAMMAR

The verb moves one step back, and the reporting verb says what kind of statement it was.

They saidYou reportExample
"We want..." (present)past simpleShe stated that they wanted more credits.
"It has arrived." (present perfect)past perfectHe confirmed that it had arrived.
will / may / canwould / might / couldThey said they would send a draft.
advise / warn + somebodyto + infinitiveI advised them to link the payment to the issuance.
say or tell?say something, tell somebody: "She told me that the price was fixed."
Still true now?the step back is optional: "She said the price is fixed" is correct if it is still fixed today.

Quick test before you speak: what did they actually say, and what kind of statement was it?
