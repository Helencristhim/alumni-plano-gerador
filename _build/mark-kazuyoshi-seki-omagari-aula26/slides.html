Chapter 1: The Statement

The Last Time a Company Told You Bad News

A delay, a price change, a server going down. Tell me what they said, and what they carefully did not say.

Same News, Four Reports

The Verb Is the Opinion

"She said the game was late."

Neutral. Nothing about the room she was standing in.

"She admitted the game was late."

She did not want to say it. Somebody made her.

"She insisted the game was late."

People were arguing with her, and she did not move.

"She denied the game was late."

Somebody had already accused her of it.

A journalist never writes "said" twice in a row. The verb carries the pressure in the room, and choosing it is the whole job.

Chapter 3: The Pattern

Every Verb Comes With Its Own Rules

What goes straight after it is not a choice

Grammar Discovery

Four Reports, Four Shapes

The director admitted changing the ending twice.

The studio refused to comment on the budget.

Fans urged the studio to delay the release.

The magazine accused the studio of hiding the delay.

All four report what somebody said. Look only at what comes straight after the reporting verb, and try to swap the four shapes around.

PatternVerbsExample
verb + that + clauseadmit, insist, confirm, deny, warn, reply"She insisted that nobody had worked weekends."
verb + -ingadmit, deny, suggest, recommend"She admitted changing the ending."
verb + to + infinitiverefuse, promise, threaten, offer, agree"The studio refused to comment."
verb + object + to + infinitiveurge, persuade, advise, warn, ask, remind"Fans urged the studio to delay it."
verb + object + preposition + -ingaccuse somebody of, apologize for, blame somebody for"They accused the studio of hiding it." / "She apologized for the silence."
suggest & recommendNever an object plus to. Use that + subject + base form, or the -ing form: "He suggested that I wait." / "He suggested waiting." Not "he suggested me to wait".

The Tool

Three Places It Goes Wrong

Trap 1 · suggest and recommend

Portuguese lets you suggest something to somebody to do. English does not. Say "he suggested that I wait" or "he suggested waiting", and never put an object plus to after either verb.

Trap 2 · the preposition is part of the verb

You accuse somebody of doing it, you apologize for doing it, you insist on doing it. Learn the preposition with the verb, the way you learned the verb itself.

Trap 3 · the verb is a judgement

"He claimed the servers were fine" tells the reader you do not believe him. Choose said when you have no opinion, and choose anything else on purpose.

Three verbs used well beat ten used at random. Pick the one that matches what actually happened in the room.

Comprehension

About Olivia

1. How much of the story did she already know?
Only a headline about the game being late — she asked whether that was the whole story.
2. What was her reaction to the apology?
She admitted that it was more than she had expected from the studio.
3. What does she ask him to do in November?
To tell her whether the producer was right, because she wants to know how the story ends.
Listening

What Each Verb Demands

Your teacher on the patterns, and on the one that catches everybody. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does the choice of reporting verb tell the listener?
Whether the person was defending themselves, giving in, or attacking somebody — the attitude that "said" leaves out.
2. Which verbs take "to" plus the infinitive?
Refuse, promise and threaten — for example, "the studio refused to comment".
3. What comes after "accuse" and after "apologize"?
A preposition and then the -ing form: accuse somebody of doing it, apologize for doing it.
4. What is the one mistake she says catches almost everybody?
Saying "he suggested me to wait". English only allows "he suggested that I wait" or "he suggested waiting".

Writing the Report

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "He suggested me to wait for the reviews."
"He suggested that I wait for the reviews." or "He suggested waiting for the reviews." (suggest never takes an object plus to)
2. "The studio refused commenting on the budget."
"The studio refused to comment on the budget." (refuse only takes to plus the infinitive)
3. "She admitted to change the ending twice."
"She admitted changing the ending twice." (admit takes the -ing form)
4. "They accused the studio to hide the delay."
"They accused the studio of hiding the delay." (accuse takes an object, then of, then the -ing form)
5. "The producer pretends to release it in November."
"The producer intends to release it in November." (in English "pretend" means to act as if something false were true)
The Real Thing

A Bulletin in Five Lines

The Frame — news desk
Mark · the press conference, reported
5 lines
5 different verbs
The factThe studio confirmed that the sequel had moved to November.
The admissionThe director admitted that the last six months had been hard, and apologized for the silence.
The defenceShe insisted that nobody had been asked to work weekends, and refused to discuss the budget.
The accusationA fan account had accused the studio of hiding a bigger problem, and the studio threatened to sue.
Your bulletinFive lines about a real story you followed this year. Five different reporting verbs, and no "said".
1. Why does the bulletin open with "confirmed" and not with "said"?
Because "confirmed" tells the reader the news was already suspected and is now official. "Said" would throw that information away.
2. What would change if line four used "reported" instead of "accused"?
The conflict would disappear. "Accused of" says somebody was blamed; "reported" would make it sound like neutral information nobody objected to.
