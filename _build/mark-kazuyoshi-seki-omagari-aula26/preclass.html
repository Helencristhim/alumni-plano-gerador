Lesson 26 -- Pre-class

The Director Insisted That... -- Reporting Verbs in the News

Stop reporting everything with "said". One verb carries the attitude: she admitted it, she insisted on it, she refused to discuss it, they accused the studio of hiding it. Each verb also demands its own shape after it -- to plus infinitive, the -ing form, an object first, or a preposition. Key words: insist, admit, refuse, urge, warn, threaten, persuade, accuse, reassure, apologize, confirm, recommend.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each verb and read the example. Each one reports what somebody said and tells you how they said it.

Insist -- to say something firmly again, even when people are arguing against you
"She insisted that nobody had been asked to work weekends."
Admit -- to accept that something difficult or embarrassing is true
"The director admitted that the last six months had been hard."
Refuse -- to say clearly that you will not do something
"The studio refused to answer a single question about the budget."
Urge -- to try hard to get somebody to do something, because it matters
"Two writers urged the studio to delay the release."
Warn -- to tell somebody about a danger or a bad result before it happens
"He warned that November leaves almost no time for testing."
Threaten -- to say you will do something bad unless you get what you want
"The studio threatened to take legal action over that post."
Persuade -- to make somebody agree with you by giving reasons
"Nobody managed to persuade her to name the date earlier."
Accuse -- to say publicly that somebody did something wrong
"The account accused the studio of hiding a much bigger problem."
Reassure -- to say something that stops another person worrying
"Nothing said at that press conference reassured the producer."
Apologize -- to say you are sorry for something you did
"She apologized for the silence, which is more than most studios manage."
Confirm -- to say officially that something people were unsure about is true
"The studio finally confirmed that the sequel had moved to November."
Recommend -- to say that something is good and that somebody should do it
"The producer recommends that fans stop pre-ordering until the reviews are out."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the news story and answer the questions. Count how many times the writer avoids the word "said".

At a press conference on Tuesday the studio finally confirmed that the sequel has been pushed to November. The director admitted that the last six months had been difficult, and apologized for the silence. She insisted that nobody had been asked to work weekends, and she refused to answer a single question about the budget.

The delay was first reported three weeks ago by a fan account, which accused the studio of hiding a far bigger problem. The studio threatened to take legal action over the post, then dropped the threat a week later. On Tuesday the director denied reading the account at all, and suggested that whoever writes it apply for a job instead.

Two of the writers had already urged the studio to delay the release rather than ship the game half finished. A producer in the room, who asked us not to use his name, recommends that fans stop pre-ordering until the reviews are out, and warned that November leaves almost no time for testing. Nothing at the press conference reassured him.

The studio has promised to show forty minutes of the game in September, and has advised players to ignore anything that does not come from the official channel. Nobody managed to persuade the director to name an exact day.

1. Why does the writer use "admitted" instead of "said" in the first paragraph?
A Because it shows she accepted something she would rather not have accepted.
B Because "admit" is the formal version of "say" in written English.
C Because the sentence is in the past and "said" cannot be used there.
2. In "accused the studio of hiding a far bigger problem", what comes after the verb?
A An object and then "to" plus the infinitive.
B An object, then the preposition "of", then the -ing form.
C A "that" clause with a subject and a full verb.
3. What does "denied reading the account" tell you that "said she did not read it" would not?
A That she read it more than once before the conference.
B That somebody had already suggested she had read it, so she was answering pressure.
C That the journalist is certain she is telling the truth.
4. Why is it "urged the studio to delay" and not "urged to delay"?
A Because "urge" always needs the person being urged before "to".
B Because "delay" cannot follow "to" without an object in front of it.
C Because the sentence would otherwise be in the passive voice.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reporting Verbs and Their Patterns

GRAMMAR

The verb you choose carries the attitude. What comes straight after it is fixed by the verb, not by you.

PatternVerbsExample
verb + that + clauseadmit, insist, confirm, deny, warn, reply"She insisted that nobody had worked weekends."
verb + -ingadmit, deny, suggest, recommend"She admitted changing the ending."
verb + to + infinitiverefuse, promise, threaten, offer, agree"The studio refused to comment."
verb + object + to + infinitiveurge, persuade, advise, warn, ask, remind"Fans urged the studio to delay it."
verb + object + preposition + -ingaccuse somebody of, apologize for, blame somebody for, insist on"They accused the studio of hiding it."
suggest & recommendNever an object plus to. Say "he suggested that I wait" or "he suggested waiting" — never "he suggested me to wait".
Choosing the verb"Said" is neutral. Everything else is a judgement: admitted means it was hard to say, insisted means somebody was arguing, claimed means the writer is not convinced.

Stage 2: Put the Bulletin in Order

Order

Listen first, then put the five parts of a news bulletin in order.

?Report what the person defended, using "insist".
?Close with what somebody else wants to happen, using "urge" or "recommend".
?Open with the fact that is now official, because it is the only line most people read.
?Report what the person would not do, using "refuse".
?Report what the person accepted, using "admit".
