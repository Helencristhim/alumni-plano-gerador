Two Hours from Oslo

A Norwegian woman on the town she chose. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. How does she describe the town compared to Oslo?
Nowhere near as busy, and considerably cheaper. She says it is far quieter than anywhere she had lived before.
2. What did people warn her about, and was it true?
That she would be bored and would miss the amenities. She says the opposite happened — the town has everything except the crowds.
3. What is the trade-off she accepted?
Fewer concerts and a longer trip to the airport — and she says the quiet more than makes up for it.
Chapter 4: The Code

How Much Quieter?

far · nowhere near · by far — the words that give a comparison its size

Grammar Discovery

What Do the Orange Words Do?

"The town is far quieter than the capital."

"It is nowhere near as busy as Sao Paulo."

"Moving here was by far the best decision of my life."

"Houses here are considerably more affordable."

Take the orange away and every sentence still works. Put it back and the listener knows how big the gap is. That is the whole difference between B1 and B2 here.

BoosterWhat it doesExample
far / much + comparativeA big gap.It is far quieter here.
considerably / significantlySame job, more formal.considerably more affordable
slightly / a littleA small gap.slightly colder in winter
not nearly as ... asMuch LESS. The gap is huge.not nearly as expensive as
nowhere near as ... asStronger, and more spoken.nowhere near as busy as
by far + the superlativeFirst place, with nobody close.by far the best decision
the -er, the -erTwo things moving together.The smaller the town, the faster you know everyone.

In one line: the comparative says WHICH way; the booster says HOW FAR.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"This town is more quieter than the capital."
"This town is far quieter than the capital."
"The house was very cheaper here."
"The house was much cheaper here."
"It is not as busy than Sao Paulo."
"It is not as busy as Sao Paulo."
"It was the most best decision I ever made."
"It was by far the best decision I ever made."

Three of these four are the same error in different clothes: the comparison is marked twice. English marks it once — and then you add a booster to say how far apart the two things are.

Comprehension

About Tom

1. What was Tom expecting before he arrived?
Something bigger. He says the town is much smaller than he pictured, and asks whether she feels cut off.
2. What is the one thing Tom says he could not give up?
The amenities. In London everything is ten minutes away.
3. How does Tom disagree with Ana? Find his exact words.
Indirectly: "I am not sure I could give up the amenities, though" and "That is a fair point, actually." He never says she is wrong.
Listening 2

The Man Who Went the Other Way

He left the small town for the city, and he does not regret it. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Why did he leave the small town?
He grew up there and everybody knew everything about him. He wanted to be one face among millions.
2. What does he say about the quiet everybody praises?
That it is overrated. For him the town was not laid-back, it was slow, and there was nothing to do after eight in the evening.
3. What does he admit the city costs him?
A cramped apartment he can barely afford, and a commute he puts up with. He calls it a trade-off he would make again.
