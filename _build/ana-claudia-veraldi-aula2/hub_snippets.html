Lesson 02 -- Pre-class

A Town of 40,000 -- The Place You Chose

Comparing the town you live in with the city you left. Key words: sprawling, cramped, affordable, a trade-off, to put up with, bustling, overrated, amenities, laid-back, to make up for, the middle of nowhere, the best of both worlds. Structure: comparatives and superlatives with boosters -- far quieter, nowhere near as busy as, by far the best.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Sprawling -- spreading out over a very large area, with no clear end
"Sao Paulo is a sprawling city of twenty million."
Cramped -- so small that there is no room to move comfortably
"We lived in a cramped apartment for eleven years."
Affordable -- cheap enough for an ordinary person to pay without difficulty
"Houses here are far more affordable than in the capital."
A trade-off -- a good thing you give up in order to get another good thing
"Fewer flights is the trade-off, and I accepted it."
To put up with -- to accept something annoying without complaining about it
"I put up with that traffic for far too long."
Bustling -- full of noise, movement and busy people
"The market square is bustling on Saturday mornings."
Overrated -- praised more than it really deserves
"Honestly, city nightlife is overrated after forty."
Amenities -- the useful services a place offers, like shops, clinics and transport
"A town of forty thousand still has every amenity I need."
Laid-back -- relaxed, calm and not easily worried
"People here are much more laid-back than I expected."
To make up for -- to be good enough to balance something that is missing
"The quiet makes up for the shorter list of restaurants."
The middle of nowhere -- a place very far from any town, with nothing around it
"People assume I live in the middle of nowhere. I do not."
The best of both worlds -- a situation where you get the advantages of two different choices
"Birds in the garden and an airport two hours away: the best of both worlds."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Ana lives in a town of forty thousand people. It is far quieter than Sao Paulo, and the house she bought is considerably more affordable than her old apartment. The apartment was cramped; the house is three times the size and has a garden.

She is honest about the cost. The airport is two hours away, so the town is nowhere near as convenient as the capital for travelling. That is the trade-off. But for her, the quiet makes up for it, and she says the move was by far the best decision she has made.

People assume she lives in the middle of nowhere. In fact the town has every amenity she needs, and it is only slightly colder in winter than the city she left.

1. Why does the text say "far quieter" and not "more quieter"?
A Because "quiet" is an irregular adjective.
B Because the comparison is marked once, by -er, and "far" only says how big the gap is.
C Because "far quieter" is informal and "more quieter" is formal.
2. "Nowhere near as convenient as the capital" means the town is:
A A little more convenient than the capital.
B Exactly as convenient as the capital.
C Much less convenient, and the gap is big.
3. The text says winters are "only slightly colder". What does "slightly" tell you?
A That the difference is small.
B That the difference is enormous.
C That there is no difference at all.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Comparatives with Boosters

GRAMMAR

The comparative says WHICH way. The booster says HOW FAR.

BoosterSize of the gapExample
far / much + comparativeBigIt is far quieter here.
considerably / significantlyBig, more formalconsiderably more affordable
slightly / a littleSmallslightly colder in winter
not nearly as ... asMuch lessnot nearly as expensive as
nowhere near as ... asMuch less, spokennowhere near as busy as
by far + superlativeFirst, nobody closeby far the best decision
the -er, the -erTwo things togetherThe smaller the town, the faster you know everyone.

Never: more quieter · very cheaper · as busy than · the most best. English marks the comparison only ONCE.

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

Somebody asks Ana about her town. Put the exchange in a logical order.

?"So where exactly do you live these days?"
?"In a town of forty thousand, about two hours from the capital."
?"That sounds like the middle of nowhere, honestly."
?"It is nowhere near as remote as people assume. We have every amenity."
?"And you do not miss the city at all?"
?"The airport is the one trade-off. The quiet makes up for it."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

An American colleague asks how your town compares with Sao Paulo. You answer:
A "It is more quieter and more cheaper than there."
B "It is far quieter and considerably more affordable."
C "It is very quieter than Sao Paulo."
You want to say the town is much less busy than the capital. The most natural spoken version is:
A "It is nowhere near as busy as the capital."
B "It is not as busy than the capital."
C "It is nowhere near busier as the capital."
Somebody says small towns are boring. You disagree politely with a British colleague:
A "No. You are wrong about that."
B "I can see why you would think that, but for me the quiet makes up for it."
C "That is not true, everybody knows it."
You want to name what you gave up, without sounding negative:
A "The airport is two hours away. That is the trade-off, and I accepted it."
B "There is nothing bad here, everything is perfect."
C "I put up with the airport because I have no choice."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Somebody who has never left a big city asks you to be honest about your town. In one minute, say what it does far better, what the capital still does better, and what the trade-off costs you. Use at least three boosters.
