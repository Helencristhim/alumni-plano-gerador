Chapter 3: The Code

If You..., We Will...

One structure for every offer

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Offers Show?

"If you sign for three years, we will include maintenance."

"If we start in March, we will finish before the deadline."

"We can offer a discount if you pay in advance."

"Unless you confirm by Friday, the price will change."

Each sentence has two parts: a condition and a result. Look at the blue verbs. After if and unless, the verb is in the present, even when you talk about the future. In the result, you use will or can.

JobFormExample
The conditionif + presentIf you sign for three years...
The resultwill / can + verb...we will include maintenance.
The order can changeresult + if + conditionWe can offer a discount if you pay in advance.
Negative conditionunless = if notUnless you confirm by Friday, the price will change.
A counterofferIf you..., we could...If you pay in advance, we could give five percent.
The trapno will after ifNot if you will sign. Say if you sign.

In one line: if + present for the condition, will or can for the result; never will after if.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Negotiation, at Real Speed

One call, three recordings, one offer

Dialogue

The Price Call

R
Luciana, we have your quote for the four hospitals. The work is excellent, but the price is fifteen percent above our budget.
L
I understand, Ryan. What exactly does your budget cover? Installation only, or also maintenance?
R
Installation and one year of maintenance. And our clients want a service-level agreement with a response in four hours.
L
Four hours is fine. If you sign for three years, we will include the first year of maintenance at no extra cost.
R
That helps, but three years is a big commitment for us. Can you give us a discount on the installation?
L
Here is a counteroffer. If you change the payment terms to thirty days, we can reduce the installation by five percent.
R
So five percent, plus the maintenance. That is close. What is your bottom line?
L
That is our best offer. If you meet us halfway on the payment terms, we will send the new quote today.
Comprehension

About Ryan

1. How far is the quote from Ryan’s budget?
Fifteen percent above the budget.
2. What does Ryan’s budget cover, and what do his clients want?
Installation and one year of maintenance. His clients want a service-level agreement with a response in four hours.
3. Why is Ryan not sure about three years?
Because it is a big commitment for his company.
Listening 1 · The Negotiation Coach

Never Give for Free

A negotiation coach in London explains his most important rule. Sound first — no text.

1. What is his most important rule?
Never give anything for free: every concession has a condition.
2. What is the problem with saying "OK, five percent"?
The client learns that your price was not real, and asks for more.
3. What sentence does he recommend?
If you do this, we will do that.
Listening 2 · Chen, the Supplier

The Price Depends on the Volume

Chen, the camera supplier from Shenzhen, explains his prices to a Brazilian partner. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the price per camera for five hundred cameras?
Ninety dollars per camera.
2. What happens if the partner orders more than one thousand?
The price will go down to seventy-five dollars.
3. What are his payment terms?
Thirty percent in advance and the rest on delivery.
Listening 3 · The Procurement Director

What a Hospital Really Wants

The procurement director of a group of hospitals in France explains how he negotiates with suppliers. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the first thing he looks at in a quote?
Not the price: the service-level agreement.
2. Why does he prefer long contracts?
Because changing suppliers in a hospital is expensive and risky.
3. What did he accept in his last negotiation?
A higher price, because the supplier offered a response in two hours.
