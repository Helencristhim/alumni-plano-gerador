Hiring the Next Designer -- Interviews and Teams

You interview candidates for your team, and you prepare to be interviewed. Key words: a candidate, a portfolio, a role, a background, a skill set, a strength, a weakness, to mentor, to onboard, a red flag. Structure: verbs + -ing and verbs + to.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A candidate -- a person who is trying to get a job
"We interviewed three candidates this week."
A portfolio -- a collection of your best work, to show to other people
"Her portfolio has three strong case studies."
A role -- a job or a position in a team or a company
"The role is for a senior product designer."
A background -- the experience and education that a person has
"He has a background in fintech."
A skill set -- all the abilities that a person has for a job
"Her skill set is perfect for our team."
A strength -- something you are very good at
"Her biggest strength is research."
A weakness -- something you are not very good at, and want to improve
"My weakness is presenting in public."
To mentor -- to help a less experienced person to learn and grow
"I enjoy mentoring junior designers."
To onboard -- to help a new person start in a job or a team
"We onboard new designers in two weeks."
A red flag -- a sign that something may be a problem
"No questions at the end of the interview is a red flag."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Juliane needs to hire a senior designer for her team. She enjoys interviewing people, but she avoids judging a candidate only by the screens in the portfolio.

This week she talked to Alex. His background is in health and payments. He enjoys talking to users, and his biggest weakness is presenting. He decided to practice every month.

Juliane saw no red flag. If Alex gets the role, she plans to mentor him and onboard him in two weeks.

1. What does Juliane avoid doing?
A Judging a candidate only by the screens in the portfolio.
B Interviewing people.
C Talking to users.
2. Why does the text say “she plans to mentor him” and not “she plans mentoring him”?
A Because mentor is a noun.
B Because plan is in the to family.
C Because it is in the past.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “Alex enjoys to talk to users.”
B “Alex enjoys talk to users.”
C “Alex enjoys talking to users.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Verb + -ing or Verb + to

GRAMMAR

Some verbs take -ing after them (enjoy, avoid, finish, keep). Others take to + verb (want, need, decide, plan). After in, about, of and at, always use -ing.

JobFormExample
The -ing familyenjoy, avoid, finish, keep, consider, recommendI enjoy mentoring people.
The to familywant, need, decide, plan, hope, would likeWe need to hire a designer.
After in, about, of, atverb + -ingShe is good at presenting.
A tendency, not a lawto often points to the futureI want to grow. I plan to mentor her.
Like, love, startboth forms are fineI like working / I like to work with data.
The trapenjoy to, want -ing do not existNot I enjoy to mentor. Say I enjoy mentoring.

Never: I enjoy to mentor · We need hiring · Avoid to ask · Interested in work
