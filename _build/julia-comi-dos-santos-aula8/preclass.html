Lesson 08 — Pre-class

What Was Happening?

Eight words for the scene (wave, sunset, text message, interrupt, argue, notice, background, suddenly) and the two tenses in one sentence: the long thing that was happening, and the short thing that cut it.

Stage 1.1: Words for the Scene

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Wave — the water that rises and breaks on the beach
"I was watching the waves when my phone rang."
Sunset — when the sun goes down at the end of the day
"We were waiting for the sunset."
Text message — a short written message on your phone
"She sent me a text message at midnight."
Interrupt — to stop somebody while they are speaking or doing something
"Sorry to interrupt, but we have to go."
Argue — to disagree with somebody, loudly
"They were arguing about the bill when I arrived."
Notice — to see something without looking for it
"I did not notice the time."
Background — everything that is behind the main thing
"In the background, people were singing."
Suddenly — quickly, and with no warning
"Suddenly the music stopped."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. Then answer the questions.

Last Saturday I was at the beach house. At four I was swimming, and the waves were bigger than usual. At six we were watching the sunset from the sand — the whole family, twelve almost identical photos. I did not notice my phone at all. While I was taking pictures, my mother sent me nine text messages, because the deadline for my assignment had moved. Suddenly, on a Saturday. When I finally saw them, my cousins were arguing in the background about who was driving home, so I could not even think. I hate to interrupt a good day with an assignment, but that is what happened.
What was she doing when her mother sent the messages?
A She was taking pictures of the sunset.
B She was swimming.
Why is it "my mother sent" and not "my mother was sending"?
A Because sending nine messages takes a long time.
B Because that is the short action that cut the long one.
What was happening in the background when she saw the messages?
A Her cousins were arguing about who was driving home.
B The waves were getting bigger.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Two tenses in one sentence

The long thing that was already happening → was / were + -ing. The short thing that cut it → past simple.

Background

I was watching the waves.
We were waiting for the sunset.
They were arguing about the bill.

Interruption

my phone rang.
she sent a text message.
the music stopped.

+ I was watching the waves when my phone rang.
= While I was watching the waves, my phone rang. (same thing, other order)
? What were you doing at eight? (not "what did you doing")

while comes before the long one. when comes before the short one. That is the only thing to remember.

Stage 3: Say it out loud

Speaking

Tap Listen, then Record. Say "was" and "were" fast and quiet — the stress falls on the -ing verb, not on them.

Stage 4: In the conversation

Quiz
Somebody asks why you missed their call. Which answer works best?
A I was on the bus with my headphones in, and I did not notice the phone.
B I did not answer.
Which sentence is correct?
A What did you doing at eight?
B What were you doing at eight?
You want to interrupt politely in a meeting. What do you say?
A Stop, I want to speak.
B Sorry to interrupt — can I say one thing?

Stage 5: A story with a background

Reflection
Write four sentences first: where you were and what you were doing last Saturday evening, something that interrupted you this week, what was happening in the background at that moment, and one sentence joining both tenses with "while". Then turn the paper over and record 30 seconds telling it as one story — without reading.
