Chapter 3: The Code

The -ed and the Helper

I organized · I did not finish · Did you go?

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I organized four school visits."

"I wrote the report last week."

"I did not finish the data."

"Did you go to all the schools?"

SentenceFormExample
affirmative, regularverb + -edI organized. I finished. I decided.
affirmative, irregulara different wordgo → went · write → wrote · say → said · meet → met
negativedid not / didn't + verbI did not finish the data.
questionDid + person + verb?Did you go to all the schools?
Key ideaAfter did and didn't, the verb goes back to its clean form: did you go, never did you went.

Short answers: Yes, I did. / No, I didn't.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"Did you finished the report?"
"Did you finish the report?"
"I writed the report last week."
"I wrote the report last week."
"Last month I organize four visits."
"Last month I organized four visits."

The word did already carries the past. After it, the verb is clean: did you finish, I didn't go.

Chapter 4: The Report Back

The Report Back

what you did, and what you did not

Dialogue

What Did You Do Last Month?

H
Hi, Joice. What did you do last month?
J
I organized four school visits, and I wrote the report.
H
Four visits! Did you go to all of them?
J
Yes, I did. I travelled to two of them by bus.
H
And did you finish the report about the platform?
J
I finished it on Wednesday. But I did not finish the data about small schools.
H
No problem. What did the teachers say in the visits?
J
They said the platform is useful, but the report page is difficult.
H
That is important. Did you write that in the report?
J
Yes. And I decided to put it on the first page, so nobody forgets it.
Comprehension

About Hugo

1. What does Hugo ask first?
What she did last month.
2. What does Hugo want to know about the visits?
If she went to all of them.
3. What does Hugo say about the teachers' opinion?
That it is important, and he asks if she wrote it in the report.

What I Can Do
