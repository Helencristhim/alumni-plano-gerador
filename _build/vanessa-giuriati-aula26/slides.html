Chapter 3

Three Futures

The form carries the meaning

Grammar Discovery

Which One Is Booked?

"I am meeting the client on Friday at ten."

"I am going to call the supplier before Friday."

"That is a good point — I will send you the notes today."

"It will probably rain on the day of the event."

FormUseExample
am / is / are + verb-ingAn arrangement: agreed with someone, in the calendarI am meeting the client on Friday at ten.
be going to + base verbAn intention you decided BEFORE nowI am going to call the supplier this week.
will + base verbA decision made right now, a promise, or a predictionI will send you the notes today.
will probably / I intend toA plan that is real but not set in stoneI will probably travel in October — nothing is set in stone.
⚠ Careful'Going to' keeps its to: 'I am going to call', never 'I am going call'. Use the present continuous only for things already arranged with someone. And once the decision was made before now, 'going to' is more natural than 'will'.

Comprehension

Did You Catch It?

1. What does Daniel have arranged for the upcoming quarter?
He is presenting to the board on the fourth, and he has two client visits lined up.
2. What has he decided but not booked?
He is going to start the certification course, and he intends to finish it before June — but nothing is set in stone.
3. What does he say about the trip?
They are saving up for it, and he will get around to booking it — in the long run, that one matters more to him.
Listening

The Calendar Test

Listen first. No text. Sound only.

1. What is the test the speaker uses?
She asks whether the plan is already in the calendar and agreed with someone else.
2. Which form does she use for something she has decided but not booked?
"Going to" — because nobody has agreed to it except her.
3. What does she try never to do, and why?
Never use "will" for a plan she made last week — it sounds like she just thought of it, and people plan around what you say.
Listening

When Plans Fall Through

A different voice, a different message. Listen carefully.

1. What did the speaker change about his plans?
Not the number of plans, but how he describes them — he marks out loud which ones are booked and which are not.
2. What happens when he says "nothing is set in stone"?
Nobody is disappointed if the plan moves, and people still take the booked plans seriously.
3. Why does he say it is not pessimism?
Because it is accuracy — in the long run, being the person whose firm plans are actually firm is worth more than having the most plans.
