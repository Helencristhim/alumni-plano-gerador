Lesson 26 -- Pre-class

Future Plans and Intentions

Three futures, three meanings: present continuous for what is arranged, "going to" for what you decided, "will" for what you decide right now. Key words: intend, upcoming, in the long run, get around to, save up, line up, fall through, set in stone.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Intend -- To have the plan or the purpose to do something
"I intend to finish the report this week."
Upcoming -- Happening soon, in the near future
"The upcoming quarter will be busy."
In the long run -- Over a long period of time, not immediately
"In the long run, it will save us money."
Get around to -- To finally do something you have been postponing
"I never get around to reading those reports."
Save up -- To keep money aside for something in the future
"We are saving up for the trip."
Line up -- To arrange or organize something in advance
"I have three interviews lined up for next week."
Fall through -- When a plan fails and does not happen
"The Lisbon project fell through in March."
Set in stone -- Fixed and impossible to change
"Nothing is set in stone yet."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Here is my upcoming quarter, sorted honestly. I am flying to Lisbon on the twelfth and I am presenting to the board on the fourth of November: both are in the calendar, and both were agreed with other people. I am going to start the certification course after the presentation, and I intend to finish it before June — nobody has agreed to that except me. I have two client visits lined up, and if the board moves the date, the course simply falls through and I start in March. We are also saving up for a longer trip, but the dates are not set in stone. And the reading list from March? One day I will get around to it. In the long run, that one matters more than it looks.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Three futures, three jobs

GRAMMAR

The form you choose tells the other person how firm the plan is.

StructureUseExample
am / is / are + verb-ingAn arrangement: agreed with someone, in the calendarI am meeting the client on Friday at ten.
be going to + base verbAn intention you decided BEFORE nowI am going to call the supplier this week.
will + base verbA decision made right now, a promise, or a predictionI will send you the notes today.
I intend to / will probablyA real plan that is not set in stoneI intend to travel in October — nothing is set in stone.

'Going to' keeps its to: 'I am going to call', never 'I am going call'. Use the present continuous only for things already arranged with someone. And a decision made before now takes 'going to', not 'will'.
