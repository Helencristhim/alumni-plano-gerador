Lesson 14 -- Your Research in Ninety Seconds

Conference Talk
"Talk nerdy to me" -- Melissa Marshall, TED

Four minutes on exactly today's problem: how scientists can explain their work to people outside their field. Her advice -- tell people why it matters, and drop the technical words -- is the middle part of your pitch.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Write down her two main recommendations, then check your own pitch: which technical word can you simplify?

Conference Talk
"The secret structure of great talks" -- Nancy Duarte, TED

A presentation designer shows that the talks people remember share the same shape: they move between what is now and what could be, and they end with a clear call to action -- the invitation at the end of your pitch.

Tip: listen for how she opens and how she closes. Then say the first and the last sentence of your own pitch out loud, and check: is there a question or an invitation at the end?

Video
"How to speak so that people want to listen" -- Julian Treasure, TED

A sound expert on the voice itself: speed, pauses, volume and tone. Useful for a pitch in a noisy corridor, and for a listener who is working hard to follow a second language.

Tip: try his warm-up exercises, then record your pitch again slower, with one clear pause before the takeaway. Compare the two recordings.
