Your Research in Ninety Seconds -- The Pitch

One pitch for a specialist, one for everybody else. Key words: audience, pitch, to tailor, to simplify, to highlight, to expand on, to engage, takeaway. Structure: the five parts of a pitch and the verb form of each part, plus Let me put it this way.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These are the words people use when they talk about a presentation -- and when they give you feedback on yours.

Audience -- the people who listen to a talk or a presentation
"A pitch for a specialist audience is not the same as a pitch for a journalist."
Pitch -- a short talk that explains your work and makes people interested
"My pitch is ninety seconds long, not ten minutes."
To tailor -- to change something so it is right for a specific person or group
"I tailor my pitch to the person in front of me."
To simplify -- to make something easier to understand
"Let me simplify: the body attacks its own nerves."
To highlight -- to show that one thing is more important than the others
"In ninety seconds I highlight one result, not ten."
To expand on -- to give more details about something you said before
"If they ask about the method, I expand on it later."
To engage -- to get somebody's attention and keep them interested
"A question at the start engages the listener."
Takeaway -- the main idea that people remember after a talk
"The takeaway of my pitch is simple: look at the feet early."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Five Parts, Five Verb Forms

GRAMMAR

There is no new grammar in a pitch: you already know every form it needs. Each part talks about a different time, so each part has its own verb form.

1. who you arepresent simple (a fact)In a nutshell, I study nerve pain in leprosy.
2. why it matterscause and effect linksNerve damage starts early, so patients live with pain for years.
3. what you foundpast simple (finished)Last year we found that early treatment works better.
4. what you are doing nowpresent continuous (in progress)At the moment we are testing a new dose schedule.
5. what comes nextgoing to (a plan)Next year we are going to start a study in two states.
negative and questionthe same formsWe did not find a difference in age. / Are you testing it in children?

When the listener looks lost, you get a second chance: Let me put it this way -- and then one simpler sentence.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Record your ninety-second pitch for a researcher you just met at the poster session. Use the five parts: who you are and your research in a nutshell, why it matters, what you found, what you are doing now or next, and a question at the end. Use "Let me put it this way" once, to simplify the hardest idea. No script -- just speak.
