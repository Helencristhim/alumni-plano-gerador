Chapter 3: The Code

Five Parts, Five Verb Forms

Everything you know, in the right place

Grammar Discovery

Why a Different Form in Each Part?

1. In a nutshell, I study nerve pain in leprosy.
2. Nerve damage starts early, so patients live with pain for years.
3. Last year we found that early treatment leads to a better outcome.
4. At the moment we are testing a new dose schedule.
5. Next year we are going to start a study in two states.
Click to reveal the rule
Each part of a pitch talks about a different time, so each part has its own verb form. Who you are and what you study are facts: present simple (I study). Why it matters is a chain: cause and effect links (so, because of, as a result). What you found is finished: past simple (we found). What you are doing now is in progress: present continuous (we are testing). What comes next is a plan: going to (we are going to start). And when the listener looks lost, one chunk gives you a second chance: Let me put it this way + a simpler sentence.

Chapter 5: Practice

Two Pitches, Two Accents

The opening, the result, the invitation

Listening 1

Wounds That Do Not Heal

Read the questions, then listen.

1. How does he open his pitch?
With his name, where he works, and his research in a nutshell: why small wounds on the feet of diabetic patients do not heal.
2. What was the main result last year?
In their first clinic, the number of amputations went down by almost half.
3. How does he close his pitch?
With a question: do you see the same problem in your country? He invites the listener to compare data.
Listening 2

An Alarm That Is Too Sensitive

Read the questions, then listen.

1. How does she open her pitch?
With a number: one in five adults lives with pain every day.
2. How does she simplify the idea of pain?
"Let me put it this way": pain is like an alarm, and sometimes the alarm is too sensitive.
3. How does she close her pitch?
With an invitation: come and see her poster, number forty-two, this afternoon.
