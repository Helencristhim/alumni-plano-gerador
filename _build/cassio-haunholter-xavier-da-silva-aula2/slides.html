Lesson 2 · Who Does What, and By When

Splitting the Scope

Two offices, one industrial plant. Somebody has to say out loud which buildings are yours, which equipment is theirs, and where the line between the two runs. That conversation is almost entirely in the future.

Chapter 1: First Words

Building A, Block B, Equipment C

Last week you said who you are and what you are working on. This week the project is real and the work has to be divided: your team takes some packages, the other office takes the rest, and the two halves have to meet cleanly.

Think of a project shared with another office. Which part did your team take, and which part did they take?

English uses three different forms, and each one tells the other office something different.

Three sentences, out loud, right now.

One about something your team decided last month and will do next year. One about something you are promising right now. One about a meeting that is already in your calendar. Do not think about the grammar yet.

Chapter 4: The Code

Will, Going To and -ing

Three futures, three different messages

Grammar Discovery

Three Futures in One Memo

"We are going to take the two industrial buildings." (agreed three weeks ago)

"I will send you the equipment list tonight." (decided in this second)

"We are meeting the client on Thursday at ten." (already in the diary)

"The handover is going to be late." (we can see it coming)

All four sentences are about the future. Ask yourself, for each one: when was this decided? Three weeks ago, one second ago, or is it a fixed appointment with a time and a place?

JobFormExample
A decision already made, a plangoing to + verbWe are going to take the two buildings.
A prediction based on what you can see nowgoing to + verbThe handover is going to be late.
A decision made while you speak, an offer or a promisewill + verbI will send you the list tonight.
A fixed arrangement with a time and a placeam / is / are + verb-ingWe are meeting the client on Thursday at ten.
A timetable that does not depend on youpresent simpleThe design freeze is on March 14.
The trapafter if, when and before, no willNot If it will rain. Say If it rains, we will stop the survey.

In one line: going to = already decided or clearly coming; will = decided now; -ing = already in the diary.

Every line of this sheet is a sentence about the future, and the three forms are all there: what was agreed weeks ago, what somebody promised in the meeting, and what is already fixed in a calendar with a date.
1. Which package is not in the Brazilian scope, and why?
Package 04, electrical and instrumentation, because Brazil has no capacity this year.
2. Turn the Critical interface line into a full sentence.
The critical interface is between the building slab and the equipment base, and we are reviewing it every two weeks.
3. Which line is an arrangement with a fixed date, and how would you say it?
The handover: we are handing over to construction on June 3.
Listening 1 · The Project Manager

How I Run a Scope Meeting

A project manager explains the meeting where two offices divide the work. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she put on one page before anybody talks about people?
The list of work packages, sometimes twelve or fifteen, sometimes thirty.
2. Which column is the slow part of her meeting?
The third column, the interfaces.
3. How did the time divide in the meeting with the Brazilian team?
Twenty minutes on the packages and an hour and a half on the interfaces.
Listening 2 · The Lead Engineer

Where a Joint Project Usually Breaks

A lead engineer describes the mistake he has seen most often. Sound first — no text.

1. Where does he say a joint project almost never fails?
Inside a package. It fails at the joint, at the interface.
2. What happened in the example he gives?
The building team designed a foundation for a machine that the equipment team had changed in March, and nobody told them.
3. What does he do now on every interface?
He puts one person on each side, and they talk every two weeks whether there is news or not.
