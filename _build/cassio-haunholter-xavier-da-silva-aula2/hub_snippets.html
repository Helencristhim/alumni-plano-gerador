Splitting the Scope -- Dividing a Joint Project

Two offices, one plant, and a line that has to be drawn. Key words: the scope of work, a deliverable, a work package, to allocate, an interface, a milestone, a handover, a partnership, capacity, to take on. Structure: will vs going to vs present continuous.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

The scope of work -- everything a team has agreed to deliver on a project, and nothing more
"The scope of work for our office is the two industrial buildings."
A deliverable -- a specific document or product a team has to hand over
"The first deliverable is the general arrangement drawing."
A work package -- one defined block of work inside a bigger project
"Each work package has one responsible engineer."
To allocate -- to decide officially who gets a task, a person or a budget
"We will allocate the piping design to the Houston team."
An interface -- the point where the work of two teams meets and has to fit
"The interface between the building and the equipment is the critical one."
A milestone -- an important date on a project that marks the end of a stage
"The next milestone is the design freeze in March."
A handover -- the moment when one team passes finished work to another
"The handover to the construction team is in June."
A partnership -- a formal agreement between two companies or offices to work together
"This plant is a partnership between our office and Houston."
Capacity -- how much work a team can actually take, with the people it has
"We do not have the capacity for a third project this quarter."
To take on -- to accept a new piece of work or responsibility
"We can take on the structural package, but not the electrical one."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The two offices meet to divide the plant. Lena reads the list. “We are going to take the process equipment,” she says. “That was agreed three weeks ago.” Cassio confirms his scope of work: the two industrial buildings and the steel structure.

Then she asks about the electrical work package. Cassio does not have the capacity this year, so he says no. “But I will send you a list of the buildings tonight,” he adds. That decision is made in the room, while he speaks.

At the end they fix the critical interface, and Lena closes the call: “We are meeting the client on Thursday at ten.” Nobody decides that during the call — it is already in everybody’s calendar.

1. Why does Lena say “we are going to take the process equipment”?
A Because she is deciding it at that moment.
B Because the decision was already made three weeks ago.
C Because it is a fixed appointment in her diary.
2. Why does Cassio say “I will send you a list tonight” and not “I am going to send”?
A Because he decides it in the room, while he is speaking.
B Because it is written in the contract.
C Because it is an appointment with a time and a place.
3. Which sentence describes a fixed arrangement?
A “We will meet the client on Thursday.”
B “We are meeting the client on Thursday at ten.”
C “We meet the client on Thursday, I think.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Will, Going To and Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

Three ways to talk about the future. The difference is when the decision was made, and whether there is a time and a place.

JobFormExample
A decision already made, a plangoing to + verbWe are going to take the two buildings.
A prediction based on what you can see nowgoing to + verbThe handover is going to be late.
A decision made while you speak, an offer or a promisewill + verbI will send you the list tonight.
A fixed arrangement with a time and a placeam / is / are + verb-ingWe are meeting the client on Thursday at ten.
A timetable that does not depend on youpresent simpleThe design freeze is on March 14.
The trapafter if, when and before, no willNot If it will rain. Say If it rains, we will stop the survey.

Never: If the client will approve, we start · We will take the buildings (decided last month) · I am going to send it tonight (decided this second) · We meet the client Thursday at ten

Stage 2: Put the Scope Proposal in Order

Order

You are answering the other office on a shared plant. Put your proposal in a logical order.

?"Thanks for the package list. Here is what we can do."
?"We are going to take package one, the two industrial buildings."
?"We can also take on the steel structure, if the design freeze stays in March."
?"We do not have the capacity for the electrical package this year."
?"The critical interface is the building slab and your equipment base."
?"I will send you the names of the lead engineers tonight."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

Your office agreed last month to take the buildings. You say:
A "We will take the buildings."
B "We are going to take the two industrial buildings."
C "We take the buildings, probably."
A colleague asks for the equipment list and you decide to send it now. You say:
A "I am going to send it tonight."
B "I will send it to you tonight."
C "I send it tonight."
The client meeting is in your calendar for Thursday at ten. You say:
A "We are meeting the client on Thursday at ten."
B "We will meet the client on Thursday at ten, maybe."
C "We are going to meet the client sometime Thursday."
You want to say that the handover looks late, from what you can see now. You say:
A "The handover will be late, I decide now."
B "The handover is going to be late."
C "The handover is late on Thursday."
