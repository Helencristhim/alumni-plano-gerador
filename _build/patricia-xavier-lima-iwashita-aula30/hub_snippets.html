Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Account plan -- a document with the goals and steps for one customer
"The account plan covers the next twelve months."
Forecast -- what you expect to happen in the future, usually with numbers
"The forecast is sixty stores by December."
Expansion -- making a business, or the use of a product, bigger
"The main goal this year is expansion."
Kickoff -- the first meeting that officially starts a project
"The kickoff is on the second Monday of January."
Go-live -- the day when a new system starts to be used for real
"The go-live of the mobile app is in May."
Phase -- one part or stage of a longer plan
"The plan has three phases, one for each part of the year."
Checkpoint -- a planned moment to stop and check the progress
"We have a checkpoint call at the end of each phase."
Ahead of schedule -- earlier than the date in the plan
"If the stores open ahead of schedule, we will add a checkpoint."
Stretch goal -- a goal that is difficult to reach, but possible
"Twenty new stores is our stretch goal, not a promise."
Fiscal year -- the twelve months that a company uses for its budget and results
"Their fiscal year starts in January."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Patrícia is writing the account plan for Brightwave's next fiscal year. The main goal is expansion, and the forecast is sixty stores. The plan has three parts, and each phase ends with a checkpoint. After the kickoff in January, the team will have moved twelve stores by the end of February. In March, the team will be training the store managers. The go-live of the mobile app is in May, and by then every manager will have received a login. The twenty new stores are a stretch goal: if they open ahead of schedule, there will be an extra checkpoint. This time next year, Patrícia will be preparing the renewal with real numbers.

1. "By the end of February, the team will have moved twelve stores." On March 1st, is the move finished?
A Yes, it is finished.
B No, it is still happening.
C It has not started.
2. "In March, the team will be training the managers." In March, is the training finished?
A Yes, it is finished.
B No, it is in progress.
C It is cancelled.
3. What is the stretch goal of the plan?
A The go-live of the mobile app.
B The twenty new stores.
C The kickoff in January.
4. Which sentence is correct?
A "By May, every manager will have receive a login."
B "By May, every manager will have received a login."
C "By May, every manager will received a login."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Future Perfect and Future Continuous

GRAMMAR

Two future forms for every plan: what will be FINISHED by a date, and what will be HAPPENING at a moment.

StructureUseExample
will have + past participleFinished before a date.By February, we will have moved the stores.
by + date / by thenThe deadline: not later than.By the end of the year, sixty stores will have joined.
will be + -ingIn progress at a future moment.In March, we will be training the managers.
this time next yearA moment in the future.This time next year, we will be preparing the renewal.
won't have + past participleNot finished by that date.By May, we won't have finished phase three.
AvoidNever the base verb after will have: not "will have move" → "will have moved". Never forget be: not "we will training" → "we will be training". And never "will has": it is always will have, also with he, she and it.

Reflection
