Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

The Person in the Room -- Saying What You Actually Do

A room of strangers asks what you do, and the title in your head does not translate. Key words: a law firm, a partner, to advise a client, a contract, a grain trader, a shipment, to import, a master’s degree, a deadline, a hearing. Structure: present simple with I, you and we — positive, negative and question.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A law firm -- a company of lawyers who work together and share the same clients
"Our law firm has an office in Sao Paulo and a small one in Miami."
A partner -- a lawyer who owns part of the firm and answers for its decisions
"I am a partner, so I answer for the work of the whole team."
To advise a client -- to tell a client what the law allows and what it does not
"I advise companies that buy and sell grain."
A contract -- a written agreement that both sides have to keep
"The contract is in English, and the client wants somebody who reads both."
A grain trader -- a company that buys and sells corn, soya or sugar in very large quantities
"Most of our clients are grain traders and the banks behind them."
A shipment -- goods that travel together from a seller to a buyer, usually by ship
"One shipment of sugar can be worth more than a year of office rent."
To import -- to bring goods into a country from another one
"Some of my clients import diesel, and every cargo has a contract behind it."
A master’s degree -- a university course you take after your first degree
"I am applying for a master’s degree, and the classes are all in English."
A deadline -- the day and the hour when something has to be finished
"The deadline for the application is the end of this month."
A hearing -- a meeting in front of a judge, where each side explains its case
"I do not go to a hearing every week. Most of my work is on paper."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A room of strangers asks what you do, and the title in your head does not travel well. Here is the whole answer, in three shapes. The first one is the easy one: "I advise companies that buy and sell grain. Most of them are grain traders, and a few of them import diesel." Look at the verb. It is bare. In this tense, with I, you and we, the verb never changes shape at all.

The second shape is the one people get wrong. To say no, English does not touch the verb. It puts do not in front of it: "We do not go to a hearing every week. We write contracts, and we read the ones the other side sends." Never we not go, and never I am advise. In Portuguese you say that you are a lawyer; in English the verb does the work by itself.

The third shape is the question, and it is the one that keeps a conversation alive. English puts do in front again: "Do you work with contracts as well?" A question word simply goes before it: "Who do you work for?" And the short answer is short: "Yes, I do." One last use of this tense: it says how often something happens. "I travel to Miami twice a year, and I read every shipment file before the deadline."

1. "I advise companies that buy and sell grain." Why is there nothing in front of the verb?
A Because the sentence is short.
B Because a positive sentence with I, you or we needs only the bare verb.
C Because advise is a verb that never takes anything in front of it.
2. Why is it "we do not go to a hearing" and not "we not go to a hearing"?
A Because a negative sentence in this tense needs do not in front of the bare verb.
B Because go is an irregular verb.
C Because a hearing happens in front of a judge.
3. The text warns against "I am advise companies". What is the problem?
A Companies needs the word the in front of it.
B The verb does the work alone. There is no am, is or are in front of it.
C The sentence has to start with a question word.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple with I, You and We

GRAMMAR

The verb never changes shape. Do carries the no and the question for it.

JobFormExample
Something true every weekI / you / we + bare verbI advise grain traders.
Negativedo not + bare verbWe do not go to a hearing every week.
QuestionDo + subject + bare verbDo you work with contracts?
Question word in frontWhat / Who / How often + do + subject + bare verbWho do you work for?
Short answerYes, I do. / No, we do not."Do you read the contracts yourself?" — "Yes, I do."
How oftenevery week, twice a year, usually, neverI travel to Miami twice a year.
In speechdo not becomes don’tWe don’t go to a hearing every week.

Never: I am advise companies · I am a lawyer of agribusiness · we not go to a hearing · you work with contracts? · how often you travel to Miami?

Quiz

You want to say that you own part of the firm. You say:
