Lesson 9 -- What They Told Me

Podcast
NPR Life Kit -- "Want to listen better? Turn down your thoughts and tune in to others"

Before you can report what somebody said, you have to have heard it. The episode argues that most of us listen while composing our own answer, which is exactly how a meeting turns into a vague impression instead of a record.

Tip: the page carries the full transcript. Listen first without reading, then reconstruct three sentences in reported speech before you check the text.

Talk
Elizabeth Loftus -- "How reliable is your memory?" (TED, 17 min)

A psychologist who spent forty years showing juries how confidently people report things that never happened. For anybody who takes a witness statement or writes a file note, this is the argument for writing down what was said on the day it was said.

Tip: after watching, reopen the last meeting note you wrote in Portuguese and mark every sentence that is a record and every sentence that is your reading. Then say the two halves out loud in English.

Series
The Crown (Netflix) -- Season 1

An entire series built on people reporting conversations they were not part of. Private secretaries walk into a room and say what somebody else said, and the whole plot turns on how much of it is the record and how much is their reading of it.

Tip: pick one scene where a courtier reports a meeting. Pause and write what was actually said, then write what the courtier added. The gap between the two is this lesson.
