Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

What They Told Me -- Reporting the Meeting to Your Client

The meeting is over and somebody has to be told what happened. The hard part is keeping what they said apart from what you concluded. Key words: to relay, to convey, to be adamant, to hint at, to walk something back, an aside, to reiterate, to flag, a takeaway, to downplay, guarded, off the record. Structure: reported speech.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To relay -- to pass a message on exactly as it was given to you
"I am relaying their position, not endorsing it."
To convey -- to communicate an idea or a feeling, not only the words
"He conveyed, without saying it, that the deadline was not real."
To be adamant -- completely firm about something, and unwilling to change
"She was adamant that fraud had to stay outside the cap."
To hint at -- to suggest something without saying it directly
"He hinted at a second bidder, and never mentioned one again."
To walk something back -- to soften or partly withdraw something you said earlier
"By the end of the call he had walked most of it back."
An aside -- a remark made in passing, outside the main discussion
"The most useful thing he said was an aside, at the door."
To reiterate -- to say again something you have already said, for emphasis
"She reiterated that the deadline was not going to move."
To flag -- to draw attention to something so that it is not missed
"I flagged the notice period and nobody picked it up."
A takeaway -- the single thing worth remembering from a meeting
"The takeaway is that they will move, but not this week."
To downplay -- to make something sound less important than it actually is
"He downplayed the delay, which told me it mattered."
Guarded -- careful about how much you reveal, without being unfriendly
"She was guarded about the timetable and open about everything else."
Off the record -- said on the understanding that it will not be repeated or attributed
"He said, off the record, that the board had already decided."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The client has four minutes before her next call, so the report has to be three sentences long. They said that they could accept a cap of twelve months. She was adamant that fraud stays outside it. They asked whether we had spoken to the board. That is the record, and it is all he relays in the first thirty seconds.

Then the client asks the question that separates a good report from a useless one: did they say twelve, or did they say they could live with twelve? Her exact words, he answers, were that twelve was a number she could take to her client. So nothing was agreed. He flagged the notice period and nobody picked it up, and there was one remark off the record that will not go into the note. The takeaway is that they will move, but not this week.

Only now does he cross the seam, and he says so out loud. She was guarded about the timetable and open about everything else. She downplayed the delay twice, which is what people do when a date matters. She hinted at a board meeting and walked it back both times. His reading — and he calls it his reading — is that their board meets before ours. She never said it. What she actually conveyed, in an aside at the end, was that nobody on her side wants this to fail, and he reiterated the same thing back to her before hanging up.

1. "They asked whether we had spoken to the board." Why had spoken and not have spoken?
A Because the board meeting happened a long time ago.
B Because in reported speech the tense steps back one: have spoken becomes had spoken.
C Because questions are always reported in the past perfect.
2. "She was adamant that fraud stays outside it." What does the writer gain by reporting it this way?
A It records both the content and how firmly it was said, which is information the client needs before Friday.
B It hides who said it.
C It turns an opinion into a fact.
3. Why does he say his reading and he calls it his reading?
A Because he is not sure whether the meeting took place.
B Because clients prefer lawyers who sound uncertain.
C Because the seam has to stay visible. If the conclusion turns out to be wrong, the record underneath it is still usable.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

Everything moves one step away from the room: the tense, the person, and the day.

JobFormExample
The tense steps backcannot → could not · will → wouldShe said they could not accept it.
Present perfect toohave spoken → had spokenShe asked whether I had spoken to them.
Say and tellsay that · tell somebody thatHe said that... / He told me that...
A question insideasked whether / if · normal word orderShe asked whether I had spoken.
An instructionasked / told somebody + to + verbHe asked me to send it.
Time and place movetomorrow → the next day · here → there...he would send it the next day.
Keeping the seam visiblethey told me X, my reading is YTwo clauses, and the join is on purpose.

Never: he said me · he told that · she asked whether had I spoken · they said they are still reviewing it (of a past meeting) · he said to me that... as your only form

Stage 2: Put the Report in Order

Order

Your client has four minutes. Put the report in the order she asked for.

?"Three things, and then my reading."
?"They said that they could accept twelve months on the cap."
?"She was adamant that fraud stays outside it."
?"Nothing was agreed, though: twelve is only a number she can take to her client."
?"My reading is that their board meets before ours. She did not say this."
?"She undertook to revert by Friday. Shall I hold you to that date?"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You are reporting what somebody said to you personally. You say:
A "She said me that they could not accept the cap."
B "She told me that they could not accept the cap."
C "She told that they could not accept the cap."
You are reporting a question they asked. You say:
A "She asked whether we had spoken to the board."
B "She asked whether had we spoken to the board."
C "She asked us have we spoken to the board?"
The client asks whether the twelve-month cap is agreed. It is not. You say:
A "Yes, they accepted twelve months."
B "No. Her words were that twelve was a number she could take to her client."
C "Probably. She seemed comfortable with it."
You are about to give your interpretation rather than the record. You say:
A "They are clearly not going to sign."
B "That part is my reading, not theirs. She never said it."
C "She was guarded, so the board has decided."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Report a meeting you were actually in, with no names. In one minute: what was agreed, what was not, what you read into it, and what you would do next. Every time you cross from record into interpretation, say so out loud.

Elizabeth Loftus -- "How reliable is your memory?" (TED, 17 min)

A psychologist who spent forty years showing juries how confidently people report things that never happened. For anybody who takes a witness statement or writes a file note, this is the argument for writing down what was said on the day it was said.
