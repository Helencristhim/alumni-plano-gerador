Chapter 4: The Code

One Small Word, a Whole Position

Modals as a way of saying how sure you are

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"We may have to revisit the delivery window."

"That might well be the sticking point."

"They are bound to ask about the tariff."

"I would rather park the price and agree the volume first."

Take the red words out of each sentence and read what is left. Nothing about the facts changes. What disappears is the speaker — how sure they are, and where they stand.

JobFormExample
A polite warning of a changemay have to / might have toWe may have to revisit the window.
Likely, and you are saying so carefullymight well / may wellThat might well be the sticking point.
Certain, in your judgementbe bound to / be sure toThey are bound to ask about the tariff.
A preference, softer than I wantwould rather + plain verbI would rather park the price.
A guess offered as a guessI would imagine / I would sayI would imagine they have a second quote.
A general pattern, not a promisetend toBuyers tend to ask about volume first.
The trapno to after would rather, and no would in the if halfNot I would rather to park. Say I would rather park.

In one line: the modal is where the speaker is standing; take it out and you only have the facts.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

The Thread, and Two Voices

One document, two recordings

Real Document

The Note You Write on the Way Home

MEETING NOTE — CHICAGO
internal · written on the plane, before anything is forgotten
MeetingCategory buyer, midwest convenience chain
ToneWarm throughout
Asked us forNothing
Their words“Really interesting — we will circle back”
Next step agreedNone — no date, no owner
ReadLukewarm. Treat as a decline unless they contact us.
Second meetingDifferent buyer — asked for a sample and a reference
Two meetings on the same day and the useful one is the second, although the first felt better. The buyer in the first meeting was warm and non-committal and asked us for nothing, which is bound to mean no. The second was harder work and ended with a request, which is the only signal that counts. We may have to revisit the delivery window for them, and I would rather find that out now than after a quote.
1. Why does the note record what the buyer asked for, on its own line?
Because a request is the only reliable signal of interest. Warmth is constant, so it carries no information.
2. Which meeting does the note treat as the useful one, and why is that counter-intuitive?
The second, which was harder work, because it ended with a request for a sample and a reference. The first felt better and produced nothing.
3. Find two hedges in the paragraph and say what each one does.
"is bound to mean no" states a certainty as a judgement rather than a fact; "we may have to revisit" warns of a change without committing to it.
Listening 1 · The Buyer

What I Mean When I Say We Will Circle Back

A retail buyer translating her own vocabulary, on the record. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she admit that her colleagues will not thank her for?
That when she says at the end of a meeting that it is really interesting and they will circle back, she has already decided, and the decision is no.
2. Why does she say the phrase exists, if it is not dishonesty?
Because saying no in the room to somebody who has flown a long way feels brutal, and the phrase gives everybody a soft exit.
3. What does a yes sound like, and what is the test she gives?
A yes is specific: she asks you for something before you leave — a sample, a reference, a date. The test is not whether she was warm, but whether she asked you for something.
Listening 2 · The Negotiator

The Layer Foreign Speakers Never Learn

A negotiation trainer on what a missing hedge does to a sentence. Sound first — no text.

1. What word do his clients use about foreign counterparts, and what does he say it is not about?
Blunt. He says it is never about rudeness — nobody is being rude.
2. What is the difference between "we cannot deliver in June" and "we may have to revisit June"?
The first is a wall; the second is the same fact with a door in it, so the other side can move without anybody losing anything.
3. What does he say the fix is?
Not more vocabulary, but four or five modal patterns used until they are automatic: I would rather, it might well be, we may have to, they are bound to.
