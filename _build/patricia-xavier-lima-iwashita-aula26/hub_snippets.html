Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Handover -- passing your work and responsibility to another person
"The handover of the Northwind account happens on Friday."
Portfolio -- the group of accounts that one person manages
"My new portfolio has twelve premium accounts."
Quirk -- a small, unusual habit or detail that you need to know
"One quirk of this account: they never answer on Mondays."
Pain point -- a problem that keeps causing trouble for a customer
"Their biggest pain point is the monthly report for finance."
Warm introduction -- introducing two people personally, not with a cold email
"I will give you a warm introduction to David on Tuesday."
Continuity -- keeping things going without a break or a change
"The customer only cares about one thing: continuity."
Shadow -- to follow a colleague and watch how they do the job
"You can shadow me on the next two calls."
Ramp up -- to become fully productive in a new role, step by step
"Most new managers need a month to ramp up."
Touchpoint -- any planned moment of contact with the customer
"The monthly call is the most important touchpoint."
Heads-up -- a short, early warning about something
"Just a heads-up: the CFO is joining the next call."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

On Friday Patrícia does the handover of the Northwind account, because she is moving to a new portfolio. Her note is full of advice. She recommends keeping the monthly call on the first Thursday, because the customer wants continuity. She tells Tom to avoid promising new features, and to remember to send the report before the fifth. She also writes down two quirks: David prefers talking on the phone, and finance hates PDFs. The biggest pain point is the monthly report. Patrícia has agreed to join Tom's first two calls, and she suggests shadowing her in July so that Tom can ramp up faster. Before any of that, she will give him a warm introduction to David — and a heads-up about every touchpoint in the calendar.

1. "She recommends keeping the monthly call." Which form comes after recommend?
A to + verb (to keep)
B verb + -ing (keeping)
C the base verb (keep)
2. "Remember to send the report before the fifth." Is the report already sent?
A Yes, it is a memory of the past.
B No, it is a task for the future.
C We do not know.
3. Why does Patrícia suggest shadowing her in July?
A So that Tom can ramp up faster.
B Because she wants to stay on the account.
C Because David asked for it.
4. Which sentence is correct?
A "I have agreed joining your first two calls."
B "I have agreed to join your first two calls."
C "I have agreed join your first two calls."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Verb Patterns: verb + -ing / verb + to

GRAMMAR

After some verbs English wants -ing; after others it wants to. Learn them in groups.

StructureUseExample
recommend / suggest + -ingGiving advice.I recommend keeping the call on Thursdays.
avoid / keep / finish + -ingWhat not to do, what to continue, what to complete.Avoid promising new features.
agree / need / plan / decide + toDecisions and plans.I have agreed to join your first two calls.
remember / forget + toA task in the future.Remember to send the report before the fifth.
remember + -ingA memory of the past.I remember sending it in May.
prefer + -ing / toBoth are correct.David prefers talking on the phone.
AvoidNever "recommend to" or "suggest to" + verb: not "I recommend to keep" → "I recommend keeping". Never "avoid to": not "avoid to send" → "avoid sending". And never -ing after agree: not "agreed joining" → "agreed to join".

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Imagine you are moving to a new portfolio tomorrow and one of your real accounts goes to a new colleague. Record your handover in five or six sentences: what you recommend keeping, what to avoid doing, one quirk of the customer, the biggest pain point, and what you have agreed to do to help. Use at least four verb patterns -- recommend, suggest, avoid + -ing, and remember, agree, need + to. Take your time.
