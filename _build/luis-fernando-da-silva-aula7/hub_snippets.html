How the Work Changed

Your own career, and an industry that used to run on paper. Key words: to evolve, a shift, to expand, to replace, to automate, a decade, a transition, paperwork, a career path, back in the day, to look back on, it was not always like this. Structure: used to for habits and states, would for repeated actions, against the past simple for one finished day.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To evolve -- to change slowly, in stages, into something different
"The job evolved. Nobody announced it, and one day it was a different job."
A shift -- a change of direction in a whole industry or a whole way of working
"The real shift was not the software. It was who ends up carrying the risk."
To expand -- to grow into more territory, more customers or more products
"The company expanded, some of the middle disappeared, and the risk moved."
To replace -- to take the place of something that was there before
"A screen replaced the folder, and something was lost as well as gained."
To automate -- to make a machine or a system do work that a person used to do
"We automated the paperwork. A decision that used to take eleven days now takes forty minutes."
A decade -- ten years, used when you are describing a long change
"Over the past decade the whole chain compressed."
A transition -- the uncomfortable period between the old way and the new one
"The transition took about four years, and nobody enjoyed any of them."
Paperwork -- all the printed forms and signatures a job used to run on
"We used to do everything on paper. The paperwork was the job."
A career path -- the sequence of jobs a person has held, seen as one line
"Nobody plans a career path in credit. You look back and there is one anyway."
Back in the day -- in the old days, said warmly rather than formally
"Back in the day I would drive two hours for one signature."
To look back on -- to think about a period of your life from where you are now
"When I look back on those years, the folder is not what I remember."
It was not always like this -- a way of introducing how different something used to be
"It was not always like this. Ten years ago half of this office was filing cabinets."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

It was not always like this. When this analyst started, half the office was filing cabinets and the paperwork was the job. We used to do everything on paper: every order, every invoice, every credit decision. That form — used to plus the base verb — does one job and does it perfectly: it says that something was the normal state of things then, and that it is not now. Portuguese does this with one tense and no extra word, which is why English speakers hear a Brazilian say I worked on paper and miss the whole point of the sentence.

There is a second form, and it is the one that turns a description into a story. Back in the day I would drive out to a farm every Tuesday, sit at somebody’s kitchen table, and drive back with a signature. Would is for actions you repeated, again and again, and it needs a story around it to make sense. But it has a hard limit: would does not work with states. Not I would be an analyst, and not there would be a filing cabinet there. For those you need used to: there used to be a filing cabinet where that desk is, and I used to be in customer service.

And then there is the ordinary past simple, for one day with a date on it. In 2013 I moved into credit. One event, finished, no repetition, so no used to and no would. Put the three together and a career path stops being a list: the industry evolved, the company expanded, a screen replaced the folder, the firm automated the forms, and over a decade the whole shift happened without anybody announcing it. One trap to memorise: the -d only survives in the positive. I used to drive, but I did not use to drive and did you use to drive? When you look back on a transition, that small letter is the difference between sounding fluent and sounding translated.

1. Why is "we used to do everything on paper" better than "we worked on paper"?
A Because "work" is not a strong enough verb for this context.
B Because "used to" says it was the normal state then and is not now, which is the whole point of the sentence.
C Because the past simple cannot be used with the word "everything".
2. Why can you not say "there would be a filing cabinet where that desk is"?
A Because "would" only works for repeated actions, not for states. A filing cabinet standing somewhere is a state.
B Because "would" is only used in conditional sentences.
C Because the sentence needs a date.
3. What happens to the -d in the negative and the question?
A Nothing. It stays in every form.
B It moves onto the main verb instead.
C It disappears. "I did not use to drive", "did you use to drive?"

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used To and Would

GRAMMAR

Used to covers habits and states. Would covers habits only, and it needs a story around it.

JobFormExample
A habit that stoppedused to + base verbWe used to do everything on paper.
A state that is no longer trueused to + be / have / knowThere used to be a filing cabinet there.
A repeated action, inside a storywould + base verbI would drive out to a farm every Tuesday.
The limit of wouldwould does NOT work with statesNot I would be an analyst. Say I used to be an analyst.
One finished event, with a datethe past simpleIn 2013 I moved into credit.
Negative and questiondid not use to · did you use to ... ?I did not use to handle such large accounts.
The trapthe -d only survives in the positiveI used to drive · but did you use to drive?

Never: we use to do everything on paper · there would be a filing cabinet there · in 2013 I used to move into credit · I did not used to handle it · I would be an analyst.

Quiz

Somebody asks what the work was like when you started. You say:
A "We use to do everything on paper."
B "It was not always like this. We used to do everything on paper."
C "We are doing everything on paper before."

A "There would be a filing cabinet where that desk is."
B "There used to be a filing cabinet where that desk is."

A "In 2013 I moved into credit."
