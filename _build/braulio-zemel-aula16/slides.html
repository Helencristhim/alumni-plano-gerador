Lesson 16 · The Body You Have Now

Health, Aging and Well-Being

Sooner or later the conversation turns to the body. In English it is not a complaint and it is not a confession — it is a subject like any other.

Chapter 1: The Body You Have Now

What Has Changed in Ten Years?

One thing your body does better than it did at fifty-six, and one thing it does worse. Say both. The first one is harder to find and it is the one people forget to look for.

Where You Start

Three Ways This Conversation Goes Wrong

Too little

I am fine, thank you. True, polite, and it closes the subject in four words.

Too much

Fourteen minutes of symptoms. Everybody at the table is now trapped.

Too vague

I have a problem in my back. Nobody can help and nobody can answer.

There is a middle setting, and English has a whole register for it: precise about the fact, light about the weight of it, and finished in two sentences. Today is that register — useful at a dinner, and useful in a consulting room in a country where you do not speak the language.

Chapter 3: The Code

I Stopped Smoking

Two words after the verb, and the sentence means something else entirely

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I stopped smoking twenty years ago." — "I stopped to think about what I was doing."

"I remember reading that exercise protects the brain." — "I must remember to book the appointment."

"I tried walking before breakfast for a month." — "I tried to walk and my knee said no."

"He went on talking for an hour." — "He went on to talk about prevention."

Each pair uses the same verb twice, and the two halves do not mean anything like the same thing. What is the difference doing?

FormWhat it doesExample
stop + verb-ingYou ended the activity. It is over."I stopped smoking twenty years ago."
stop + to + verbYou interrupted something else in order to do this."I stopped to think about it."
remember + verb-ingA memory of something already done."I remember reading that somewhere."
remember / forget + to + verbAn obligation ahead of you."Remember to book the appointment."
try + verb-ing / try + to + verbTry doing is an experiment you can afford to abandon. Try to do is an attempt that may fail."Try walking before breakfast."
"I tried to walk and could not."

The pattern behind all of them. The -ing form points backwards, at something real that happened or is happening. The to form points forwards, at something intended. Portuguese carries the same distinction, but it does it with different verbs instead of different endings, which is why the trap is invisible until somebody laughs.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I stopped to smoke twenty years ago."
"I stopped smoking twenty years ago."
"I remember to read that exercise protects the brain."
"I remember reading that exercise protects the brain."
"Do not forget booking the screening for next month."
"Do not forget to book the screening for next month."

The first one is the famous one: I stopped to smoke means you interrupted your walk in order to have a cigarette, which is the opposite of what the speaker meant, and it is the sentence that gets a laugh at a dinner table. The second turns a memory into an instruction. The third turns an obligation into a memory. One test covers all three: is this thing behind you or ahead of you? Behind takes -ing, ahead takes to. Read the correct versions aloud twice.

Nobody Wants the Whole File

R
Forty years as a physician and the question I was worst at answering was how are you.
B
Because you knew too much to give the short answer?
R
Exactly that. And the short answer is still the right one socially. The skill is the sentence after it.
B
I remember reading somewhere that people either say nothing or say everything, with nothing in between.
R
There is a middle, and it has a shape. One fact, one measure of how much it costs you, and then you hand the conversation back.
B
I stopped taking one of my tablets in March, and I have never once said that out loud in English.
R
In a consulting room you would have to. And there the rule is the opposite: be boring, be exact, and never round a number to make it sound better.
B
Try saying that to a Brazilian man of sixty-six. We round everything, especially downwards.
Comprehension

About Ruth

1. What does she say she was worst at, after forty years?
Answering the question how are you -- because she knew too much to give the short answer.
2. What does she say is the right social answer, and where is the real skill?
The short answer is still the right one socially -- the skill is in the sentence that comes after it.
3. What shape does she give for the middle register?
One fact, one measure of how much it costs you, and then you hand the conversation back.
4. How does she say the rule changes in a consulting room?
It reverses: be boring, be exact, and never round a number to make it sound better.
Listening

Four Minutes with the Doctor

A physician explaining a result. Sound first -- no text.

1. What does he say the numbers actually show, and what does he say they do not show?
They show inflammation that has been there for a while -- they do not show anything that has deteriorated since the last screening.
2. What does he recommend, and what does he say about the routine?
Walking before breakfast rather than in the evening -- and that the routine matters far more than the intensity of it.
3. What is his instruction about the tablets, and what must the patient remember to do?
Do not stop taking them without telling him -- and remember to book the next screening before leaving the building.
Listening 2

Fine, Thank You, and Then What?

A short method clip. Sound first -- no text.

1. What does she say is wrong with the two usual answers?
One says nothing and ends the subject; the other gives the whole file and traps everybody at the table.
2. What is the three-part shape she gives for the social version?
One fact, one measure of what it costs you, and then a question that hands the conversation back.
3. What does she say changes completely in a consulting room?
There you should be boring and exact, give dates and numbers, and never round anything to sound better.
