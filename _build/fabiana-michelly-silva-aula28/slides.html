Chapter 3: The Polite No

The Polite No

A past verb that talks about now

Grammar Discovery

Past Form, Present Meaning

"I'd rather we didn't promise the fourteenth."

"It's time we agreed on a date we can keep."

"I'd rather you sent me the request in writing."

"It's high time we stopped blaming Penang."

What tense are the purple verbs? Are these sentences about the past, or about now?

StructureWhat it saysExample
would rather + someone + pastwhat you prefer another person to do (a polite request or a no)I'd rather you sent it in writing.
would rather + someone + didn'ta polite noI'd rather we didn't promise the fourteenth.
would rather + base verbyour own preference (same person)I'd rather wait until Friday.
it's time + someone + pastsomething should already be happeningIt's time we agreed on a date.
it's high time + someone + pastthe same, but stronger and more urgentIt's high time we stopped blaming Penang.
The trapthe verb is past, but the meaning is nowNot I'd rather you send, not It's time we agree, not I'd rather to wait.

In one line: the past form adds distance, and distance makes a firm request sound polite.

Chapter 4: The Calls

The Calls

One angry message, one coaching conversation, one feedback call

Dialogue

Before You Call Greg

F
Greg from Sales just called. He wants the Chicago order on the fourteenth. That's impossible.
M
Was it heated?
F
Very. He was venting, and honestly, I got a little rattled.
M
Hear him out first. Then validate the pressure he's under, and stand your ground on the date.
F
So something like: I understand the customer is pushing you, but I'd rather we didn't promise the fourteenth.
M
Good. And don't take it personally. He's angry with the customer, not with you.
F
And if he doesn't back down?
M
Then say it calmly: it's time we agreed on a date we can keep. The twenty-first is non-negotiable.
Comprehension

About Marcus

1. What three steps does Marcus give Fabiana for the call with Greg?
Hear him out first, then validate the pressure he is under, and stand her ground on the date.
2. Why does Marcus tell her not to take it personally?
Because Greg is angry with the customer, not with her.
3. What does Marcus suggest she say if Greg doesn't back down?
To say calmly that it's time they agreed on a date they can keep, and that the twenty-first is non-negotiable.
Listening 1 · An Angry Message

Greg from Sales

Greg Walsh, VP of Sales, leaves a voicemail. Sound first — no text.

1. What does Greg want, and why?
The Chicago order on the fourteenth, not the twenty-first, because he already promised that date to their biggest customer.
2. What solutions does Greg suggest?
Put it on a plane, run extra shifts in Monterrey, or whatever it takes.
3. Who does Greg say will be responsible if they lose the account, and what is he tired of?
Supply chain, not sales. He is tired of hearing about Penang.
Listening 2 · Feedback from Priya

After the CFO Meeting

Priya calls after the meeting with the CFO. Sound first — no text.

1. What did Fabiana do well in the meeting with the CFO?
She kept a cool head, did not take it personally, and reframed the budget as insurance for twelve days of lost supply, not a cost.
2. What should she do differently next time?
Not apologize when he interrupts. She can say, "I'd rather finish the point, and then I'll take your question."
3. What was the result, and what does Priya ask about Greg?
The CFO wants a revised number by Friday: not a yes, but a real door. About Greg, Priya would rather Fabiana didn't solve it alone and asks to be copied on the reply.
