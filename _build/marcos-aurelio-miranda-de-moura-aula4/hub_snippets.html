Lesson 04 -- Pre-class

What Is Happening Right Now -- The Live Update

Somebody interrupts and asks where things stand. Not last quarter, not next quarter: this week. Key words: currently, temporary, ongoing, an update, a status, an initiative, a pilot, a rollout, a bottleneck, to fall behind. Structure: present continuous, and when to use it instead of the simple form.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Currently -- in this period, and not for ever — the word that goes with what is happening now
"I currently oversee five areas, and I am currently rebuilding one of them."
Temporary -- lasting only for a period, and then ending
"The extra discount is temporary. It ends when the new line arrives."
Ongoing -- started and still running, with no end date yet
"The conversation with the distributor is ongoing. Nothing is signed."
An update -- a short message that tells people where something stands now
"I send a two-line update every Monday, and nobody has to ask."
A status -- the condition of a project at this moment: on plan, late, or stopped
"The status is amber. We are running one week behind and we know why."
An initiative -- a new piece of work a company decides to start, with a name and an owner
"We are running three initiatives this quarter, and two of them are mine."
A pilot -- a small first version of a project, tested in one place before the rest
"The pilot is running in twelve workshops in one state."
A rollout -- the process of taking something new to every place, step by step
"The rollout starts in the north and reaches the south in November."
A bottleneck -- the one step that slows everything else down, however fast the rest goes
"The bottleneck is the label supplier, not the plant."
To fall behind -- to be later than the plan, and to know it
"We are falling behind on the rollout, and I am not hiding it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Two sentences about the same person, and they are not the same sentence. "I oversee pricing." "I am rebuilding the price list." The first one is the job: it was true last year and it will be true next year. The second one is temporary: it started, it has not finished, and one day it will be over. English marks that difference with am, is or are plus a verb with -ing.

Certain words almost always travel with it, and they are the words a status round is made of: currently, right now, this month, at the moment. "Right now the team is running a pilot in twelve workshops." "At the moment we are falling behind on the rollout." The negative and the question use the same helper: "We are not waiting for the supplier." "Is the rollout falling behind?" Watch the spelling of the -ing: make becomes making, run becomes running, get becomes getting.

There is one group of verbs that refuses the -ing, however present the moment is: know, have, own, need, want, mean. "I know the number", never I am knowing the number. And the trap that costs a Portuguese speaker most: the simple form never means right now. Right now we work on the campaign sounds, to the person listening, like something you do every year. Say we are working on the campaign. That one letter difference is why a room understands that an initiative is ongoing and not finished, and why it knows that a bottleneck is a problem today and not a description of your company.

1. What is the difference between "I oversee pricing" and "I am rebuilding the price list"?
A The second one is more polite.
B The first is the job, always true. The second is in progress now and will end.
C The second one is about the past.
2. Why is "I am knowing the number" wrong?
A Because know is a state verb, and state verbs stay in the simple form.
B Because the sentence has no time expression.
C Because know cannot be used about a number.
3. The text says "right now we work on the campaign" sounds wrong to the listener. Why?
A Because right now cannot start a sentence.
B Because the simple form reads as something you do every year, not something in progress.
C Because campaign needs the word the in front of it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

The simple form is the job. Am, is or are plus -ing is the week.

JobFormExample
The job, every yearthe simple formI oversee pricing.
In progress, this periodam / is / are + verb-ingI am rebuilding the price list.
The words that go with itright now, currently, this month, at the momentAt the moment we are running a pilot.
Spelling of the -ingdrop the final -e · short verbs double the last lettermaking · running · getting
Negativeam / is / are not + -ingWe are not waiting for the supplier.
QuestionAm / Is / Are + subject + -ingIs the rollout falling behind?
State verbs stay simpleknow, have, own, need, want, meanI know the number. Not I am knowing.

Never: right now we work on the campaign · I am knowing the number · the team running a pilot · the rollout is falling behind? · we are having two plants.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

Somebody asks where the rollout stands today. You answer:
A "Right now we run one week behind on the rollout."
B "We are running one week behind, and the bottleneck is the supplier."
C "The rollout is with one week of delay in this moment."
You want to say the job is permanent and one piece of it is temporary. You say:
A "I oversee five areas, and this month I am rebuilding one of them."
B "I am overseeing five areas, and this month I rebuild one of them."
C "I am oversee five areas and I rebuilding one."
You already have the number in front of you. You say:
A "I am knowing the number since this morning."
B "I know the number. It came in this morning."
C "I am having the number of this morning."
The other person has finished. You want to keep the conversation going. You say:
A "Fine. Everything is fine on our side."
B "And you, are you falling behind on anything this week?"
C "You are falling behind in something this week?"

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Give the status of three real things you have running right now. For each one: what is happening, whether it is on plan, and what you are doing about it. Put the late one first, and end with the decision you need from the room. Ninety seconds.
