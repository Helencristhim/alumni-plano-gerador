Chapter 3: The Code

Always, and Right Now

Two presents, two jobs

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"I oversee pricing and trade marketing."

"This month I am rebuilding the price list."

"The team launches a campaign four times a year."

"Right now the team is running a pilot in twelve workshops."

Two of the red verbs stand alone and two picked up a helper and an ending. Now look at the blue parts: they only appear on one of the two sides, and that is the clue.

JobFormExample
The job, every yearthe simple formI oversee pricing.
In progress, this periodam / is / are + verb-ingI am rebuilding the price list.
The words that go with itright now, currently, this month, at the momentAt the moment we are running a pilot.
Spelling of the -ingdrop the final -e · short verbs double the last lettermaking · running · getting
Negative and questionam / is / are not + -ing · Am / Is / Are + subject + -ingWe are not waiting. Is the pilot working?
State verbs stay simpleknow, have, own, need, want, meanI know the number. Not I am knowing.
The trapthe simple form never means right nowNot now we work on the campaign. Say we are working on the campaign.

In one line: the simple form is the job, and am, is or are plus -ing is the week.

Listening 1 · A Manager

Ninety Seconds on Where We Are

A manager giving a live status in a weekly meeting. Sound first — no text.

1. How many things does he report on, and in what order does he put them?
Three. He puts the one that is late first, then the one that is working, then the one that has not started.
2. What is slowing the rollout down, and what is he doing about it?
The label supplier. He is splitting the order between two suppliers this week.
3. What does he say about the pilot, and what is he waiting for?
The pilot is running in twelve workshops and the early numbers look good. He is waiting for four more weeks before he calls it a success.
Listening 2 · A Podcast

Why Most Status Updates Say Nothing

A business podcast on weekly reporting. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say most status updates are really doing?
They are listing activity, which proves that people are busy and tells nobody anything useful.
2. What two questions does she say a good update answers?
What is different since last week, and what decision is somebody waiting for.
3. What does she say about the word fine?
That fine is the most expensive word in a status meeting, because a project is never fine right up to the week it is not.

1. What is the difference between amber and red on this board?
Amber means late and understood. Red means late and not understood, so the problem is the explanation, not the delay.
2. Which line is a question and not a report, and who has to answer it?
The last one, about splitting the label order between two suppliers. It is the decision the meeting is waiting for.
3. Why does the note say nobody is allowed to write fine?
Because fine says nothing. The board only accepts a colour and a reason.

The Same Ninety Seconds, Three Times

Role-Play 1 of 3 — Guided

The Monday Round

Scenario: It is the Monday round. You are asked three things: where the rollout stands, whether the pilot is working, and what is different since last week. Ninety seconds for all three, and finish with the decision you need.

Keyword chips:

one week behind the bottleneck is twelve workshops nothing is signed
Role-Play 2 of 3 — Semi-free

Ask, Do Not Assume

Scenario: Somebody on your team says a project is fine, and you do not believe it. Ask two questions to find out what is really happening and what decision they are avoiding — then say back, in one sentence, what you understood.

Keywords:

What is different since last week? Are we falling behind? a bottleneck ongoing
Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

Ninety Seconds, No Notes

Scenario: Give the status of three real things you have running right now. For each one: what is happening, whether it is on plan, and what you are doing about it. Put the late one first. End with the decision you need from the room.

No keywords, no notes, ninety seconds. Expect one question you did not prepare for.
