Chapter 3: The Formal Code

Polite, Formal, Clear

How lawyers ask for things

Grammar Discovery

Ask Like a Lawyer

"Could you provide the tax documents for the last five years?"

"Would you be able to send us the list by Friday?"

"Would you mind explaining this clause?"

"We would appreciate it if you could answer in writing."

Look at the orange words. They are requests, not orders. What makes them formal?

RequestFormExample
Polite and directCould you + base verb?Could you provide the contracts?
Very politeWould you be able to + base verb?Would you be able to send the list?
Polite, a small favorWould you mind + verb-ing?Would you mind explaining this clause?
Formal, in writingWe would appreciate it if you could + base verbWe would appreciate it if you could reply by Friday.
AnsweringCertainly. / Of course. / I am afraid...I am afraid we cannot share that yet.

In one line: could / would make the request formal. After mind, use -ing.

Chapter 4: The Meeting

Questions From Chicago

One meeting, two recordings, one document

Dialogue

The Due Diligence Meeting

C
Thank you for the meeting, Rafael. We have started the due diligence, and we have a few questions.
R
Of course. Everything we have is in the data room. What do you need?
C
Could you provide the tax documents for the last five years? We only found three.
R
Certainly. I will upload the other two today. Would you mind checking the folder again tomorrow?
C
Not at all. Also, would you be able to send us a list of active lawsuits?
R
Yes. I want to disclose one now: there is a labor case with a former employee. It is small, but it is in the list.
C
Thank you for telling us early. That is not a red flag for us.
R
Good. We would appreciate it if you could send any new questions in writing, so our compliance team can answer quickly.
Comprehension

About Claire

1. What has Claire's team started?
The due diligence.
2. What does she ask for first, and why?
The tax documents for the last five years, because they only found three.
3. How does she react to the lawsuit?
She thanks Rafael for telling them early and says it is not a red flag.

Listening 2 · Advice

No Surprises

An M&A lawyer gives advice to sellers. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. According to him, what is the buyer really looking for?
Surprises.
2. What is the difference between a problem disclosed early and one found later?
Early, it is a detail. Found later in the data room, it is a red flag.
3. What happens if you hide a liability?
The buyer will find it, and then the price will change, or the deal will end.
