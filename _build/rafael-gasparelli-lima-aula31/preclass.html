Lesson 31 -- Pre-class

Legal Meetings -- Due Diligence

How to ask for documents and answer questions in a legal meeting, in formal and polite English. Key words: due diligence, data room, liability, lawsuit, compliance, red flag, to disclose, confidential. Structure: formal requests with could you, would you be able to, would you mind + -ing, and we would appreciate it if you could.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example.

Due diligence -- a careful check of a company before you buy it
"The due diligence will take six weeks."
Data room -- a secure place, usually online, with all the documents of a company
"We opened the data room yesterday."
Liability -- a debt or a legal risk that a company has
"The buyer found a tax liability."
Lawsuit -- a case that one person or company takes to court against another
"There are two active lawsuits."
Compliance -- following the laws and rules that apply to a company
"Our compliance team checks every contract."
Red flag -- a warning sign that something may be wrong
"An unsigned contract is a red flag."
To disclose -- to give information that was not known before
"We disclosed the lawsuit in the first meeting."
Confidential -- secret, for only a few people to see
"All documents in the data room are confidential."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

An American company wants to buy a Brazilian business, and its lawyer, Claire, starts the due diligence. She does not say “Send me the documents.” She says: “Could you provide the tax documents?”

Then she asks: “Would you be able to send us the list of lawsuits by Friday?” After would you be able to, the verb is in the base form. Rafael answers: “Certainly. Would you mind checking the folder again tomorrow?” After would you mind, the verb has -ing.

In her email, Claire writes: “We would appreciate it if you could explain the unsigned contract.” This is the most formal request, and it is very common in writing.

Why does Claire say "Could you provide" and not "Send me"?
A Because it is a formal, polite request.
B Because she is not sure that the documents exist, and she thinks the company is hiding them in another place.
C Because could is the past of can.
Which sentence is correct?
A "Would you mind to check the folder again tomorrow morning, before the meeting with the buyer?"
B "Would you mind checking the folder again?"
C "Would you mind check the folder?"
Where do we usually see "We would appreciate it if you could..."?
A Only in informal messages to friends.
B In formal requests, very often in writing.
C Only when we are angry with the other side and want to show it clearly in the email.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Ask Like a Lawyer

GRAMMAR

Could / would make the request formal. After mind, use -ing.

RequestFormExample
Polite and directCould you + base verb?Could you provide the contracts?
Very politeWould you be able to + base verb?Would you be able to send the list?
A small favorWould you mind + verb-ing?Would you mind explaining this clause?
Formal, in writingWe would appreciate it if you could + base verbWe would appreciate it if you could reply by Friday.
AnsweringCertainly. / I am afraid...Certainly. I will upload them today.

Never: Would you mind to send · Would you be able sending · We would appreciate if you could

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

You need the bank documents from the other lawyer. You say:
A "Send me the bank documents now, because we need them for our report this week."
B "Could you provide the bank documents, please?"
C "You can give me the documents?"
You want the other side to explain a clause. You say:
A "Would you mind explaining clause seven?"
B "Would you mind to explain clause seven of the contract that we received last Monday?"
C "Explain clause seven."
You write a formal email asking for answers in writing. You write:
A "We would appreciate if you could answer in writing, because our team is very busy."
B "Answer in writing."
C "We would appreciate it if you could answer in writing."
There is a small lawsuit. The buyer has not found it yet. You say:
A "Nothing. Everything is perfect in the company, and there are no problems at all with employees."
B "I want to disclose one small lawsuit now."
C "It is confidential, so I cannot say."
