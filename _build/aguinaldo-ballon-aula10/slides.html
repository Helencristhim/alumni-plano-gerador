Chapter 4: The Code

Could You Tell Me What...?

Indirect questions that save a meeting

Grammar Discovery

What Happened to the Word Order?

"Could you tell me what you said about the tariff?"

"Do you mind explaining what that term means?"

"Could you remind me when the hearing is?"

"Do you know if the fund approved it?"

The direct questions would be: What did you say? What does that term mean? When is the hearing? Did the fund approve it? Look at the blue part of each polite question. Where is the verb now? What happened to did and does? And where does if come from?

JobFormExample
Start politelyCould you tell me / Do you know / Do you mind explaining ...Could you tell me ...?
Then normal word orderquestion word + subject + verb... what you said? (not what did you say)
No do / does / didthe main verb takes the tenseWhat does it mean? → ... what it means?
With besubject before beWhen is the hearing? → ... when the hearing is?
Yes / no questionsif (or whether) + subject + verbDid they approve it? → ... if they approved it?
Check understandingSo, what you are saying is that ...So, what you are saying is that the date will change?

In one line: polite start + question word or if + normal word order, with no do, does or did.

Listening 1 · The Project Manager

Three Offices, Three Accents

A project manager explains how her team works across Singapore, London and Brazil. Sound first — no text.

1. Where are the three offices of her team?
In Singapore, London and Brazil.
2. What was the problem with the people in Singapore?
They were too polite to ask questions.
3. What rule did the team create?
After every important decision, one person paraphrases it.
Listening 2 · The Investor

A Fast Update, Full of Jargon

An American investor gives a quick update on a call. Listen for the gist first. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the gist of his update?
The deal was approved with conditions, the lenders want a bigger reserve, and they plan to close by the end of the first quarter.
2. Why do the lenders want six months instead of three?
Because one of the financial numbers came in a bit tight.
3. Write one polite indirect question you would ask him about the jargon.
For example: "Do you mind explaining what DSRA means?" or "Could you tell me what conditions the committee set?"
