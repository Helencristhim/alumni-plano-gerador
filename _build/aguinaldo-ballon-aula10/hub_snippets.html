When You Do Not Catch It -- Asking to Clarify

Accents, fast speech and jargon in international meetings, and the questions that prevent a misunderstanding. Key words: an accent, to catch, to clarify, to paraphrase, to mumble, jargon, to slow down, a misunderstanding, the gist, a follow-up question. Structure: indirect questions.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An accent -- the way people from a particular place pronounce a language
"Her accent is from Singapore, and it is very clear."
To catch -- informal: to hear or understand something that somebody says
"Sorry, I did not catch the last number."
To clarify -- to make something clearer or easier to understand
"Could you clarify what you mean by risk?"
To paraphrase -- to say what somebody said using different words
"Let me paraphrase to check that I understood."
To mumble -- to speak quietly and not clearly
"He mumbled the answer, and nobody understood it."
Jargon -- special words used by a group that other people find hard to understand
"Please avoid jargon when you speak to the users."
To slow down -- to speak or move at a lower speed
"Could you slow down a little, please?"
A misunderstanding -- a situation where people understand something in different ways
"The problem was a misunderstanding about the date."
The gist -- the main idea of something, without the details
"I did not understand every word, but I got the gist."
A follow-up question -- a question you ask after an answer, to get more information
"She asked a good follow-up question about the costs."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On a call with a fund in New York, the investor speaks very fast and uses a lot of jargon. Aguinaldo gets the gist, but he does not catch the date of the closing.

He does not say "What did you say?". He asks: "Could you tell me what the closing date is?" Then, when the investor uses an abbreviation, he asks: "Do you mind explaining what that term means?"

At the end, he checks with a follow-up question: "Do you know if the lenders have approved the new reserve?" and he paraphrases the main decision. There is no misunderstanding. Notice that after "Could you tell me" and "Do you know", the word order is normal, with no do, does or did.

1. What did Aguinaldo not catch?
A The name of the fund.
B The date of the closing.
C The price of the project.
2. Why does he say "what the closing date is" and not "what is the closing date"?
A Because after "Could you tell me", the word order is normal.
B Because "is" is always at the end of a sentence.
C Because the question is about the past.
3. Which question is correct?
A "Do you know did the lenders approve it?"
B "Do you know if the lenders approved it?"
C "Do you know if did the lenders approve it?"

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Indirect Questions

GRAMMAR

A polite start + question word or if + normal word order. No do, does or did in the second part.

JobFormExample
Start politelyCould you tell me / Do you know / Do you mind explaining ...Could you tell me ...?
Then normal word orderquestion word + subject + verb... what you said? (not what did you say)
No do / does / didthe main verb takes the tenseWhat does it mean? → ... what it means?
With besubject before beWhen is the hearing? → ... when the hearing is?
Yes / no questionsif (or whether) + subject + verbDid they approve it? → ... if they approved it?
Check understandingSo, what you are saying is that ...So, what you are saying is that the date will change?

Never: Could you tell me what did you say? · Do you mind explaining what means that term? · Could you remind me when is the hearing? · Do you know did the fund approve it?

Stage 2: Put the Call in Order

Order

An investor gives you a fast update on a call. Put your part of the conversation in a logical order.

?"Sorry, could you slow down a little? The connection is not very good."
?"Thank you. Could you tell me what the closing date is?"
?"Do you mind explaining what that abbreviation means?"
?"Do you know if the lenders have approved the new reserve?"
?"So, what you are saying is that we close at the end of March?"
?"Perfect. Could you tell me what the next steps are?"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You did not catch what the investor said about the tariff. You say:
A "Could you tell me what did you say about the tariff?"
B "Could you tell me what you said about the tariff?"
C "What you said about the tariff?"
Somebody uses a term you do not know. You say:
A "Do you mind explaining what that term means?"
B "Do you mind explaining what means that term?"
C "Do you mind to explain what does that term mean?"
You want to know if the fund approved the budget. You say:
A "Do you know did the fund approve the budget?"
B "Do you know if the fund approved the budget?"
C "Do you know the fund approved the budget if?"
At the end of an important point, the best way to avoid a misunderstanding is:
A "Yes, yes, perfect." (even if you did not understand)
B "So, what you are saying is that the deadline will change?"
C "Sorry, my English is very bad."
