Lesson 21 -- Pre-class

Positioning Your Brand

Positioning a luxury brand for American buyers: Milton meets brand strategist Olivia Grant in New York. Key words: positioning, niche, heritage, craftsmanship, exclusivity, differentiate, perception, tagline. Structure: gerunds and infinitives -- we avoid competing on price, we plan to open a showroom.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Positioning -- the place a brand takes in the mind of its buyers, compared with others
"Our positioning is handmade gold, not cheap gold."
Niche -- a small, specific part of a market with its own type of buyer
"We sell to a small niche of private collectors."
Heritage -- the history and traditions that a family or company passes on
"Three generations of jewelers is real heritage."
Craftsmanship -- the skill and care used to make something well by hand
"Buyers pay for the craftsmanship, not just the gold."
Exclusivity -- the quality of being available only to a few people
"Limited editions protect the exclusivity of the brand."
Differentiate -- to show clearly how you are different from others
"Our story is how we differentiate the brand."
Perception -- the way people see and understand something
"The perception in the US is still a good price."
Tagline -- a short, memorable phrase that represents a brand
"Our new tagline is only five words long."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

For years, American buyers saw Milton's jewelry as a good product at a good price. Now Milton wants to change that perception. Last week he met Olivia Grant, a brand strategist in New York. Olivia recommends telling the story of the family workshop: three generations of craftsmanship.

"Competing on price is a trap," Olivia said. She suggests stopping the online discounts and focusing on a small niche of private collectors. Milton agreed. He plans to open a showroom in New York, and he refuses to sell to outlet stores. He is looking forward to seeing the new tagline.

1. "Olivia recommends telling the story." Why is it telling and not to tell?
A Because the story happened in the past, so the verb needs a past form like -ing.
B Because recommend is followed by the -ing form.
C Because Olivia is speaking to a client.
2. Which verb in the text is followed by to + verb?
A plans
B suggests, because it is an idea that Olivia has for the future of the brand
C recommends
3. "Competing on price is a trap." What is Competing in this sentence?
A A verb in the past continuous that describes a long action in the background.
B An adjective.
C The subject of the sentence.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Gerunds and Infinitives

GRAMMAR

The verb in front decides the form that follows.

FormRuleExample
verb + -ingavoid, enjoy, consider, recommend, suggest, keep, riskWe avoid competing on price.
verb + to + verbwant, plan, decide, aim, expect, refuse, needWe plan to open a showroom.
-ing as subjectthe action is the topic of the sentenceTelling our story is the key.
preposition + -ingafter in, at, about, before, by and look forward toI look forward to working with you.
stop + -ing / stop + tostop selling = no more selling · stop to call = pause in order to callWe stopped selling to outlet stores.

Learn the verbs in pairs: avoid competing, plan to open, refuse to sell, recommend telling.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.
