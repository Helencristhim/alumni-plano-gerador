Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

This Time Next Week -- Arranging Plans

The words of a free Saturday: kick-off, to pick up, long weekend, to be off, to make it, get-together, to run late, layover. Structure: present continuous for a fixed arrangement, and will be doing for a moment you can picture. Mission: arrange a whole day with somebody who does not know your city.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Kick-off -- the moment a football match starts
"Kick-off is at nine sharp, so the girls are arriving at a quarter past eight."
To pick up -- to collect somebody or something from a place
"I am picking up two of her friends on the way."
Long weekend -- a weekend with an extra day off because of a holiday
"It is a long weekend, so nobody is answering emails on Monday."
To be off -- to not be at work, because of a holiday or a day off
"I am off on Friday, so call my mobile if anything breaks."
To make it -- to manage to arrive or to be somewhere as planned
"If the traffic is bad I am not going to make it before kick-off."
Get-together -- an informal meeting of friends or family, usually at somebody home
"We are having a get-together at my sister house after the match."
To run late -- to be later than planned
"If I am running late, I will send you a message."
Layover -- the time you spend in an airport between two flights
"He has a nine hour layover in Lisbon on the way home."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Saturday I am taking my daughter to her match. Kick-off is at nine, so I am picking up two of her friends at a quarter to eight. At one we are having a get-together at my sister house, and I am not free until four. In the evening I am meeting two friends at seven. None of that was decided just now. All of it is in the diary, with an hour and a place, and that is why every verb is in the -ing form of the present.

Two sentences in that day are different. At ten on Saturday morning I will be watching the match is not an appointment. It is a photograph: pick a moment in the future and look at what is happening inside it. Same with Lars: this time next week he will be sitting in an airport in Lisbon. And when Lars asks for a ticket and I answer I will get you one tomorrow, that is neither. That is a decision, taken at the second I speak it, and it goes back to plain will.

1. Why is it I am picking up two of her friends and not I will pick up?
A Because picking up is a physical action and physical actions take -ing.
B Because it is already arranged: there is an hour, a place and other people involved.
C Because it happens before nine in the morning.
2. What does At ten I will be watching the match describe?
A A decision he is taking right now about ten o clock.
B A moment in the future, seen from inside, while it is happening.
C A promise he made to his daughter.
3. Why does I will get you a ticket tomorrow use plain will?
A Because it is a decision taken at the second he speaks it.
B Because tickets are always in the simple future.
C Because tomorrow is too close for the continuous.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Continuous for the Future and Future Continuous

GRAMMAR

Two futures that have nothing to do with will. One is your diary. The other is a photograph of a moment that has not happened yet.

FormUse it forExample
am / is / are + -ingA fixed arrangement: a time, a place, and usually another person who also knows.I am taking her to the match at nine.
will be + -ingA moment in the future, seen from inside it while it is in progress.This time next week I will be flying home.
The time expressionThe future continuous nearly always needs one: at ten, this time next week, all Sunday morning.
Still plain willA decision taken at the second you speak, a promise or an offer: I will get you a ticket.
Never the present simple for a planWhat are you doing this weekend?, never What do you do this weekend?
Negatives and questionsI am not coming on Friday. Are you going to the game? Will you be working on Monday?

A quick test: if somebody else already knows about the plan, it is an arrangement and it takes am doing. If you are describing what a moment will look like, it takes will be doing.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are arranging a weekend with somebody. Choose the best thing to say.

A colleague wants to know your weekend plans. The natural question is:
A What do you do this weekend?
B What are you doing this weekend?
C What will you do this weekend, normally?
The match is already arranged for nine on Saturday. You say:
A I will take my daughter to her match at nine.
B I am taking my daughter to her match at nine.
C I take my daughter to her match at nine on Saturday.
You want to describe your Sunday morning as a picture. You say:
A At ten on Sunday I will be driving to the airport.
B At ten on Sunday I will drive to the airport, I think.
C At ten on Sunday I will driving to the airport.
Your friend asks for a ticket and you decide, right now, to get one. You say:
A I am getting you a ticket tomorrow.
B I will get you a ticket tomorrow.
C I will be get you a ticket tomorrow.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think about your real next weekend. Record about ninety seconds. Go hour by hour: say what you are doing and at what time, and who else knows about each plan. Use the present continuous for everything that is already arranged. Then pick two moments, one on Saturday and one on Sunday, and describe them from inside, with will be doing. Finish with one thing you are deliberately not doing this weekend, and why. Use at least four words from this lesson. Do not stop to correct yourself.
