What Is in the Room -- What There Is, How Much, and What Is Missing

Describe a room: what there is, how much there is, and what is missing. Key words: a waiting room, cozy, cramped, spotless, a shelf, a drawer, supplies, to run out of, plenty of, a couple of. Structure: there is and there are with quantifiers.

"All my supplies are in these drawers."

"We always run out of gloves on a Friday."

"There is plenty of coffee, so please help yourself."

"There are a couple of magazines in English."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

In your first language, one small verb probably describes almost any room. In English, that job belongs to two expressions, there is and there are, and the choice depends on what comes after them.

For one thing, or for something you cannot count, English uses there is: "There is a small sofa in the waiting room." "There is a lot of light." For two or more things, it uses there are: "There are four chairs by the window." In a question, the words change places: "Is there a hair dryer?" "Are there any towels?"

Small words tell you how much. For things you can count, like chairs or magazines, use many, a few or a couple of: "There aren't many magazines, but there are a few books." For things you cannot count, like space, coffee or water, use much or a little: "There isn't much space." Some words work with both: some, any, a lot of, plenty of and no.

The first trap is very Brazilian. Because the same verb also means has, it slips into English as have: in my office have a window. In English, a room does not have things in this sentence: there is a window in my office. The second trap is there is with two things: there is two chairs. Two chairs, so there are.

1. How do you say that your office has one big window?
A "There is a big window in my office."
B "In my office have a big window, and a lot of light comes in all day."
C "There are a big window in my office."
2. Which word goes with space, coffee and water?
A Many, because there is a lot of each one in a normal room.
B Much, because you cannot count them.
C A few, because they are small things that you can see and touch.
3. Why is "There is two chairs" wrong?
A Because chairs are things you cannot count, so they need much.
B Because English never uses there is when you describe furniture in a room.
C Because two chairs are more than one, so it is there are.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- One Thing, or Many?

GRAMMAR

There is for one thing, there are for many. Many for things you count, much for things you don't.

JobFormExample
One thingthere isThere is a sofa in the waiting room.
Two or more thingsthere areThere are four chairs.
Negativethere isn't / there aren'tThere aren't any towels.
Questionis there...? / are there...?Is there a hair dryer in the room?
Things you countmany · a few · a couple ofThere are a few foreign patients.
Things you don't countmuch · a littleThere isn't much space.
Both kindssome · any · a lot of · plenty of · noThere is plenty of coffee.

Never: in my office have a window · there is two chairs · there isn't many space · there are some towels?
