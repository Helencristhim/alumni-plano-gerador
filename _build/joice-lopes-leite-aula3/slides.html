Chapter 3: The Code

Two Little Words: Do and Does

to say no · to ask a question

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I don't work on the weekend."

"She doesn't read in the morning."

"Do you walk in the park?"

"Does he work at home?"

WhoTo say noTo ask
I / you / we / theydon't + verbDo you read?
he / she / itdoesn't + verbDoes he read?
Short answerYes, I do. / No, I don't. — Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.

The s moves to the helper word. After does and doesn't, the verb is clean: she doesn't read, not doesn't reads.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"She doesn't reads in the morning."
"She doesn't read in the morning."
"I not work on the weekend."
"I don't work on the weekend."
"You walk in the park?"
"Do you walk in the park?"

In Portuguese the question is the same sentence with a different voice. In English the question needs Do or Does at the front.

Chapter 4: A Real Conversation

The Coffee Break

where the real English happens

Dialogue

What Do You Do on the Weekend?

M
Hi, Joice. Do you work on Saturday too?
J
No, I don't. I don't work on the weekend.
M
Good. So what do you do on Saturday morning?
J
I walk in Ibirapuera. It is a very big park.
M
Nice. Does your family walk with you?
J
Sometimes. My son doesn't like the morning, so he stays at home.
M
And in the week? Do you study English in the morning?
J
Yes, I do. I read one page in English every day.
M
One page every day is a lot in one year. And you? Do you ask me something?
J
Yes. Do you work from home, or do you go to the university?
Comprehension

About Mark

1. What does Mark ask about Saturday?
If she works on Saturday too.
2. What does Mark ask about her family?
If her family walks with her.
3. What does Mark say about one page every day?
That it is a lot in one year.
