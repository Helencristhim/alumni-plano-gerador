Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

The Spec Sheet -- What Gets Written Before Anything Is Made

Everything is approved and nobody can give a date. Key words: a specification, a requirement, a prototype, shelf life, a batch, to approve, to scale up, a trial run. Structure: the present perfect with already, just and yet -- how to give a status without giving a date.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A specification -- the written document that says exactly what a product has to be
"The specification has already been approved by two departments."
A requirement -- something the product must have, not something it could have
"There are nine requirements and one of them is impossible."
A prototype -- the first version of something, made only to test the idea
"The prototype has just arrived from the plant."
Shelf life -- how long a product stays good, from production until it must be thrown away
"We have not tested the shelf life yet, and that is the whole risk."
A batch -- a quantity made at the same time, under the same conditions
"The whole batch has the same problem, because it has the same origin."
To approve -- to give the official agreement that lets something go ahead
"Quality has approved it. Finance has not signed yet."
To scale up -- to produce much more than in the test, in a real factory
"Everything works in the lab. Nothing survives the day you scale up."
A trial run -- a first real production, made to test the process and not the idea
"The trial run is on the eighth, and nothing moves before that."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The specification has already been approved, in version four, by quality and by the plant. Nine requirements are listed and all nine are mandatory. The prototype has just arrived from the lab, two kilos of it, and it does what the document says.

Then the line that decides everything. The product needs a shelf life of twelve months, and the test has not started yet. It takes twelve weeks and nothing makes it faster. Has anybody scheduled the trial run in the real line yet? No. Nobody has scheduled it.

The plant has approved the document and has also written a note under the signature: this specification describes a two-kilo batch, and the line produces two tons. When you scale up, the time and the temperature change. So the memo asks for one thing: no launch date to any customer until both tests exist.

1. "We have already approved the specification." Why not "We already approved it"?
A Because the point is the situation now, not the day it happened. That is what have plus participle does.
B Because approve is an irregular verb.
C Because already can only be used with have.
2. Where do already and just go, and where does yet go?
A All three go at the end of the sentence.
B Already and just go in the middle, before the participle. Yet goes at the end, in negatives and questions.
C All three go in the middle, before the participle.
3. Which sentence is wrong: "We have approved it last week" or "We approved it last week"?
A The first one. With a finished date, English uses the past simple, not have plus participle.
B The second one. Approve always takes have.
C Neither. Both are correct and mean the same.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect With Already, Just and Yet

GRAMMAR

The structure for giving a status. It says where something is now, and it never carries a date.

UseFormExample
Done, and it still matters nowhave / has + past participleQuality has approved it.
alreadyin the middle, before the participleWe have already approved the spec.
just (a moment ago)in the middle, before the participleThe prototype has just arrived.
yet (not until now)at the END, in negatives and questionsWe have not tested it yet. Have you finished yet?
Useful participlesirregular, learned one by onedo → done · send → sent · write → written · run → run
The trapno finished date allowedNot we have approved it last week. Use we approved it last week.

Never: We already approved it (for a status) · We have yet not tested it · The plant has wrote a note · We have approved it last week · Have you already finished yet?

Stage 2: Put the Status in Order

Order

You are giving the status of the project in a meeting. Put your sentences in a logical order.

?"I will give you the whole picture in two minutes."
?"We have already approved the specification, version four."
?"The prototype has just arrived from the plant."
?"But we have not tested the shelf life yet."
?"That test takes twelve weeks and nothing makes it faster."
?"So I will not give a date before that test is finished."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

Somebody asks where the specification is. It is signed and that is the situation now. You say:
A "We already approved it."
B "We have already approved it."
C "We have approved it already last week."
The test has not been done. You want to say it is missing, not refused. You say:
A "We have yet not tested the shelf life."
B "We have not tested the shelf life yet."
C "We do not have tested the shelf life."
You are asking a supplier for the status of a test. You ask:
A "Have you finished the trial run yet?"
B "You have finished the trial run yet?"
C "Have you yet finish the trial run?"
The approval happened on a specific day and you are naming the day. You say:
A "We have approved the specification last Tuesday."
B "We approved the specification last Tuesday."
C "We have already approved the specification last Tuesday."
