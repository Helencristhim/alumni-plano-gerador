Chapter 3: The Code

Three Words That Give the Status

Already, just, and the one everybody puts in the wrong place

Discovery

Where Does the Small Word Go?

"We have already approved the specification."

"The prototype has just arrived from the plant."

"We have not tested the shelf life yet."

"Have you finished the trial run yet?"

The red machine never changes: have or has plus a past participle. What moves is the blue word — two of them sit in the middle and one always goes to the end.

UseFormExample
Done, and it still matters nowhave / has + past participleQuality has approved it.
alreadyin the middle, before the participleWe have already approved the spec.
just (a moment ago)in the middle, before the participleThe prototype has just arrived.
yet (not until now)at the END, in negatives and questionsWe have not tested it yet. Have you finished yet?
Participles you will needirregular, learned one by onedo → done · send → sent · write → written · run → run
The trapno date allowed hereNot we have approved it last week. With a date, use the past simple.

In one line: have plus participle for the status, already and just in the middle, yet at the end — and never a date.

Listening 1 · The Status Update

Two Minutes, Everything

The person who runs the project, giving the room the whole picture. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What has already been done, according to her?
The specification has been approved by quality and by the plant, and the prototype has just arrived.
2. What has not happened yet, and why is it the only thing that matters?
The shelf life test. It takes twelve weeks and it cannot be made faster, so nothing after it can move until it is done.
3. What does she ask the room not to do?
Not to give a launch date to any customer before the shelf life result is in.
Listening 2 · From the Plant

What Breaks When You Scale Up

The plant manager, on the difference between two kilos and two tons. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is his complaint about the way products arrive at the plant?
They arrive with a specification written for two kilos, and he has to produce two tons with it.
2. Give two things he says change when the quantity changes.
The time the mixture needs, and the temperature in the middle of the batch. He also mentions that the machine cannot stop when the lab could.
3. What does he want from R&D, and what does he say it would save?
One trial run in the real line before the specification is closed. He says it would save three months of arguing afterwards.
Real Document

The Spec Sheet, Page One

SPECIFICATION SP-207
New reference · private label · version 4
Requirements listed9 · all mandatory
Approved by qualityyes · version 4
Approved by the plantyes · with one note
Prototypereceived · 2 kg, lab scale
Trial run in the real linenot scheduled
Shelf life required12 months
Shelf life testnot started · takes 12 weeks
Note from the plant: this specification describes a two-kilo batch. The line produces two tons.
1. Say the status of the four lines at the bottom, in full sentences, with already, just or yet.
The prototype has already arrived. The trial run has not been scheduled yet. The shelf life test has not started yet. And the requirement is twelve months, which nothing has confirmed yet.
2. Everything on this page is approved. Why can nobody give a launch date?
Because the shelf life test has not started and it takes twelve weeks. Twelve weeks is the earliest possible answer, and the answer might be no.
3. Read the note from the plant. What is it really saying about the approval above it?
That the plant approved a document written for the lab. What has been approved is not what the factory will actually have to produce.
