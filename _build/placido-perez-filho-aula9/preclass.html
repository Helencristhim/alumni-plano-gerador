Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

Advice for a Young Manager -- If I Had Known

A young manager wants to quit. Key words: to quit, to be laid off, a career path, a turning point, to take a risk, to regret, hindsight, a mentor, to weigh up, a safety net, to step down, to burn out. Structure: third and mixed conditionals.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To quit -- to leave a job or stop an activity because you decide to
"He quit his job to start a business."
To be laid off -- to lose your job because the company does not need you anymore
"She was laid off when the factory closed."
A career path -- the series of jobs and steps in a person’s working life
"His career path started in sales."
A turning point -- a moment when an important change happens
"Losing that job was the turning point of my life."
To take a risk -- to do something that could go wrong, hoping it will go well
"At thirty, I took a risk and opened my own company."
To regret -- to feel sorry about something you did or did not do
"I don’t regret leaving; I regret waiting so long."
Hindsight -- understanding a situation only after it has happened
"With hindsight, I should have asked for help earlier."
A mentor -- an experienced person who advises someone younger
"Every young manager needs a mentor."
To weigh up -- to think carefully about the good and bad sides of something
"Weigh up the pros and cons before you decide."
A safety net -- something, like savings, that protects you if things go wrong
"Don’t quit without a safety net."
To step down -- to leave an important position, often by your own choice
"The founder stepped down at seventy."
To burn out -- to become extremely tired and stressed because of too much work
"If you never rest, you will burn out."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At thirty-two, Tom was laid off from a bank. It felt terrible, but with hindsight it was a turning point. “If I hadn’t been laid off,” he says, “I would never have started my own company.”

He took a risk, but he had a safety net: six months of savings. He also found a mentor. “If I hadn’t had a mentor, I would have made many more mistakes.”

Now seventy, Tom has stepped down. His only regret? Working too much. “If I had rested more, I wouldn’t have burned out at forty, and I would be healthier now.”

1. “If I hadn’t been laid off, I would never have started my company.” Was Tom laid off?
A Yes, and then he started his company.
B No, he quit.
C No, he stayed at the bank.
2. What was Tom’s safety net?
A A mentor.
B Six months of savings.
C His job at the bank.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “If I would have rested more, I wouldn’t have burned out.”
B “If I had rested more, I wouldn’t have burned out.”
C “If I had rested more, I wouldn’t burned out.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Third and Mixed Conditionals -- If I Had Known

GRAMMAR

Use if + had + past participle to imagine a past that did not happen. The result can be in the past (would have + p.p.) or in the present (would + verb).

JobFormExample
Imagined past, past resultIf + had + p.p., would have + p.p.If I had stayed, I would have stayed poor.
Imagined past, result now (mixed)If + had + p.p., would + verbIf I had taken the risk, I would be elsewhere now.
Present situation, past result (mixed)If + past / were, would have + p.p.If I were more patient, I wouldn’t have quit.
Less certainmight have / could have + p.p.She might not have burned out.
In fast speechI’d have / If I’d knownIf I’d known, I’d have called.
The trapno would have in the if-partNot if I would have known. Say if I had known.

Never: If I would have known · I would never started · I would have be there now · laid out

Stage 2: Put the Advice in Order

Order

A young manager asks if he should quit. Put your advice in a logical order.

?"I understand. Let me tell you something."
?"When I was your age, I almost stayed in a job I hated."
?"If I had stayed, I would never have started my company."
?"But I didn’t quit on a bad day."
