Chapter 3: The Code

If I Had Known

A past that did not happen, and the present it would have made

Grammar Discovery

A Past That Did Not Happen

"If I had stayed in my first job, I would never have started my company."

"If he hadn’t been laid off, he wouldn’t have found his real career."

"If I had taken that risk at thirty, I would be somewhere else now."

"If she had had a mentor, she might not have burned out."

All four sentences imagine a past that did not happen. Red: the imagined past (had + past participle). Blue: the imagined result. In sentences 1, 2 and 4 the result is in the past too (would have + participle). In sentence 3, look at the time word: now.

JobFormExample
Imagined past, past resultIf + had + p.p., would have + p.p.If I had stayed, I would have stayed poor.
Imagined past, result now (mixed)If + had + p.p., would + verbIf I had taken the risk, I would be elsewhere now.
Present situation, past result (mixed)If + past / were, would have + p.p.If I were more patient, I wouldn’t have quit.
Less certainmight have / could have + p.p.She might not have burned out.
In fast speechI’d have / If I’d knownIf I’d known, I’d have called.
The trapno would have in the if-partNot if I would have known. Say if I had known.

In one line: if + had + past participle imagines a different past; would have + p.p. for a past result, would + verb for a result now.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Choices, at Real Speed

One conversation, three recordings, one decision

Dialogue

Coffee at the Trade Fair

A
Plácido, can I ask you something personal? I’m thinking about leaving my job.
P
Of course, Arjun. What’s happening?
A
I’ve been working seventy hours a week for two years. I think I’m starting to burn out. But if I quit, I lose a good salary.
P
I understand. When I was your age, I almost stayed in a job I hated. If I had stayed, I would never have started my own company.
A
So you think I should take a risk?
P
Not blindly. Weigh up the options first. Do you have a safety net? Six months of savings, for example?
A
About four months. And my manager has offered me a different role, with fewer hours.
P
Then talk to him before you decide. With hindsight, the people I see regret are the ones who left angry, not the ones who left prepared.
Comprehension

About Arjun

1. How many hours a week has Arjun been working?
Seventy hours a week, for two years.
2. What is Arjun afraid of losing if he quits?
A good salary.
3. What has Arjun’s manager offered him?
A different role, with fewer hours.
Listening 1 · A Founder Looks Back

The Day I Was Laid Off

A British businessman who stepped down from his company at seventy looks back on his career. Sound first — no text.

1. What happened to him at thirty-two?
He was laid off from a bank.
2. What would his life be like now if that had not happened?
He would probably still be at a desk in a bank, or retired from one.
3. What does he regret?
Not taking more time for his family when his children were young.
Listening 2 · A Career Coach

Before You Quit

An American career coach explains what to do before you leave a job. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say most people do wrong?
They quit when they are angry or burned out, without a plan.
2. What are her three questions before quitting?
Am I leaving something, or going to something? Do I have a safety net? Have I talked to my manager?
3. What does she say about regret?
People regret the risks they did not take more than the ones they took.
Listening 3 · A Second Career

Starting Again at Fifty

A Frenchman who changed careers at fifty talks about what he would have done differently. Sound first — no text.

1. What was his first career?
He was an engineer in the car industry for twenty-five years.
2. What does he do now?
He has a small bakery in Lyon.
3. What would he have done differently?
He would have found a mentor before he started.

Notes like these are full of conditionals: some real (if I stay, I will...), some imagined (if I quit, I would...). Months from now, they will become third conditionals: if I hadn’t talked to my manager...
1. Which option did Plácido suggest Arjun look at first?
Talking to his manager about the new role.
2. Why is quitting now risky for Arjun?
He only has four months of safety net.
3. Imagine it is one year later and Arjun took the new role. Make a third conditional.
For example: if I hadn’t talked to my manager, I would have quit without a plan.
