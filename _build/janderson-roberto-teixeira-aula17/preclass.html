Lesson 17 -- Pre-class

How Things Used to Be -- The City, the Career, You

How to talk about the habits and situations of your past that are not true now, and join them with the present. Key expressions: to grow up, a hometown, a neighborhood, back then, nowadays, to commute, to miss, to move away, an old friend, to recognize. Structure: used to and didn't use to.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. These ten stay here in your material -- they do not disappear after the class.

To grow up -- to change from a child into an adult
"I grew up in a small town near the coast."
A hometown -- the town or city where you were born or grew up
"I go back to my hometown about once a year."
A neighborhood -- an area of a city where people live
"We used to live in a quiet neighborhood near the park."
Back then -- at that time in the past
"Back then, every report was printed on paper."
Nowadays -- at the present time, compared with the past
"Nowadays most clients read everything on their phones."
To commute -- to travel regularly between home and work
"I used to commute for an hour every morning."
To miss -- to feel sad because something is not in your life anymore
"I miss the Friday lunches with the old team."
To move away -- to go and live in a different place
"She moved away from the city ten years ago."
An old friend -- a friend you have known for a long time
"I ran into an old friend at the airport."
To recognize -- to know who somebody is because you have seen them before
"I almost didn't recognize you with short hair."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At a conference coffee break last month, somebody said Janderson's name, and for a second he did not recognize her. It was Lauren, an old friend from the New York office. Ten years ago they used to sit on the same floor, and she used to laugh at the coffee he brought every morning.

Back then, Lauren used to live in a small apartment in Brooklyn and commute by subway, which she hated. She moved away to the suburbs a few years later. She didn't use to like quiet places, but nowadays she says she would never go back.

Janderson's story was different. He grew up far from any financial center, and his first job was in research. He used to write reports all day and he didn't use to talk to clients at all. Nowadays he spends most of his time with them.

They talked for twenty minutes. Neither of them misses the long hours of those years, but they both miss the Friday lunches with the team, and they agreed to have one again the next time she is in town.

1. "Lauren used to live in Brooklyn." Does she live there now?
A Yes, she still lives there, in the same small apartment she had ten years ago.
B No. It was true for a long time, and it is not true now.
C We do not know.
2. Why is it "she didn't use to like" and not "she didn't used to like"?
A Because after did, the verb loses the d.
B Because she is a woman.
C Because the sentence is in the present and talks about the suburbs today.
3. How was Janderson's first job different from his job now?
A He used to travel a lot, and nowadays he works from home every day of the week.
B He used to write reports and did not talk to clients; now he spends his time with them.
C He used to work in New York.
4. What do they both miss?
A The long hours and the small apartments they had in the city.
B The Friday lunches with the team.
C The subway.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used To

GRAMMAR

Used to talks about habits and situations in the past that are not true anymore. It always goes with the base form of the verb.

FormWhat it saysExample
used to + verbA past habit or situation, not true now."I used to live near the office."
didn't use to + verbNegative: after did, no d."She didn't use to like the suburbs."
Did ... use to + verb?Question: again, no d."Did you use to work in research?"
There used to beSomething that existed and does not exist now."There used to be a cinema on the main street."
Past simple for one timeIf it happened once, use the past simple."I moved in 2015."
PronunciationUsed to and use to sound the same."YOOS-tuh"

The quick test: was it true for a long time, and is it over? Then use used to. Did it happen once? Then use the past simple.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are talking about your past with people at work. Choose the best thing to say.

You want to say where you lived for years, before you moved:
A I used to live near the office.
B I use to live near the office, many years ago, before the move.
C I was used to live near the office.
You want to ask an old colleague about her first job:
A Did you used to work in research when we first met?
B Did you use to work in research?
C You used to working in research?
You want to say a habit you did not have in the past:
A I didn't used to drink coffee in the morning.
B I not used to drink coffee.
C I didn't use to drink coffee.
You want to say something happened one time:
A I used to move to New Jersey in 2015 with my family.
B I moved to New Jersey in 2015.
C I use to move to New Jersey in 2015.
You want to tell a client how banking has changed:
A Clients used to wait a week, but nowadays they want an answer the same day.
B Clients were used to waiting.
C Clients use to wait a week, but nowadays they want an answer.
