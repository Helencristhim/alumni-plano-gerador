The city, the career, and you. Some things changed and some did not, and English has two small words for the difference.

Chapter 1: The Old Days

Ten Years Ago

Think of your life ten years ago: where you lived, how you got to work, what you did every Friday. What is different now?

Where You Start

Once, or for Years

One time

"I moved in 2015." -- it happened once

For years, and not now

"I used to live near the office." -- a long situation that is over

The difference

Two small words tell the listener it is over

Every life story needs this structure: the city you grew up in, the job you had first, the habits you do not have anymore. Without it, every sentence sounds like one single day.

Chapter 3: The Code

Used To, Didn't Use To

Two small words for a whole life

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I used to live in Brooklyn."

"She didn't use to like the suburbs."

"We used to print every report."

"Did you use to work in research?"

All four sentences are about the past, and in all four it is not true anymore. Look at the blue words: when is it used, and when is it use?

FormWhat it saysExample
used to + verbA habit or a situation in the past that is not true now."I used to live in Brooklyn."
didn't use to + verbNegative. After did, the d disappears."She didn't use to like it."
Did ... use to + verb?Question. Again, no d after did."Did you use to work there?"
There used to beSomething that existed and does not exist now."There used to be a cinema here."
Past simple, not used toFor one single time, use the past simple."I moved in 2015." -- not "I used to move in 2015."

In speech, used to and use to sound the same: YOOS-tuh. The d only matters when you write.

Practice

Choose the Form

Say it first, then click to check

"When I was a child, I ___used to live (live) near the beach."
"She ___didn't use to like (not / like) the suburbs."
"___Did you use to work (you / work) in research?"
"We ___used to print (print) every report."
"He ___didn't use to commute (not / commute) by car back then."
"There ___used to be (be) a cinema on the main street."
Detail

Then, and Now

Two short sentences become one

"I lived in Brooklyn. Now I live in New Jersey. → "___I used to live in Brooklyn, but nowadays I live in New Jersey."
"He walked to work. Now he drives. → "___He used to walk to work, but now he drives."
"I did not like coffee. Now I love it. → "___I didn't use to like coffee, but now I love it."
"Clients waited a week. Now they wait a day. → "___Clients used to wait a week, but now they wait a day."
"Was there a cinema here in the past? → "___Did there use to be a cinema here?"
Chapter 4: The Reunion

Ten Years Later

A coffee break, and a face you almost do not recognize

Dialogue

An Old Friend at the Coffee Break

L
Janderson? I almost didn't recognize you! It's Lauren, from the New York office.
J
Lauren! Of course. You used to sit next to the window on the fourth floor.
L
That's right. And you used to bring that terrible coffee every morning.
J
I still do, honestly. Do you still live in Brooklyn?
L
No, I moved away years ago. I didn't use to like the suburbs, but nowadays I love them.
J
And the commute? You used to hate the subway.
L
I work from home three days a week now, so I only commute twice. Did you use to work in research?
J
I did. Back then I used to write reports all day. Nowadays I spend my time with clients.
Comprehension

About Lauren

1. Where did Lauren use to sit?
Next to the window on the fourth floor.
2. Where did she use to live?
In Brooklyn.
3. How has her opinion of the suburbs changed?
She didn't use to like them, but nowadays she loves them.
4. How often does she commute now?
Twice a week, because she works from home three days.
Listening

A Small Town in Ohio

A short radio segment. Sound first -- no text.

1. What used to happen on Friday nights?
The whole town used to go to the cinema.
2. What is the cinema nowadays?
A pharmacy.
3. Why does nobody walk to work anymore?
The offices moved to a business park outside town, so everybody commutes by car.
Listening 2

Twenty Years in Banking

A banker remembers how it used to be. Sound first -- no text.

1. How long did a client use to wait for an answer?
A week.
2. What did his clients use to ask about?
Returns, not fees.
3. How has his idea of his job changed?
He used to think it was knowing more than the client. Now he thinks it is explaining things better than the internet does.

The Habit

Then and Now, Out Loud

Before the next class, say one sentence out loud each day that joins the past and the present: I used to..., but nowadays... About work, the city, food, sports, anything.

One sentence a day, and in two weeks your story has twenty chapters.
