Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Etiquette -- the rules of polite behavior in a group or a place
"Business etiquette in Miami is a little formal at first."
Custom -- something people in a culture usually do
"Shaking hands is a common custom in American meetings."
Punctual -- arriving or doing something exactly on time
"American partners expect you to be punctual."
Small talk -- short, friendly conversation about easy topics
"We made small talk about the weather before the meeting."
Gesture -- a movement of the hand or head that shows a meaning
"A thumbs-up is a friendly gesture in the United States."
Personal space -- the distance people like to keep from others
"Americans usually need more personal space than Brazilians."
Stereotype -- a fixed, simple idea about a group of people
"Not every Brazilian is late; that is a stereotype."
Appropriate -- right or suitable for a situation
"A handshake is appropriate in a first meeting."
Offend -- to make someone feel upset or disrespected
"He did not want to offend his American partner."
Misunderstanding -- a situation where people do not understand each other correctly
"The delay was a cultural misunderstanding, not bad will."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

At the start of his career, Nilo used to arrive at meetings ten minutes late. In Brazil, it was a normal custom, and nobody worried. He also used to spend twenty minutes on small talk. Then he started working with American sponsors. They were used to starting exactly on time, and they kept the small talk short. At first, Nilo was not used to this style. He thought his partners were cold, but that was a stereotype. It was a cultural misunderstanding. Slowly, he got used to being punctual and respecting personal space. Today, he is used to American business etiquette, and he knows that a handshake is appropriate in a first meeting.

1. What did Nilo use to do at the start of his career?
A "He used to arrive early and keep every meeting very short with his Brazilian partners."
B "He used to arrive ten minutes late and spend a long time on small talk."
C "He is used to arriving late now."
2. Why does the text say "cold" was a stereotype?
A Because his American partners were really cold and unfriendly people at every meeting.
B Because Nilo was cold.
C Because it was a cultural misunderstanding: they only had a different style.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "Today, he is used to American business etiquette."
B "Today, he used to American business etiquette in his meetings with the sponsors."
C "Today, he is used to follow American etiquette."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used To, Be Used To, Get Used To

GRAMMAR

How to talk about old habits, normal habits, and change.

FormMeaningExample
used to + verban old habit, not true nowI used to arrive late.
didn't use to + verbthe negativeI didn't use to read the agenda.
be used to + noun / verb-ingit is normal for meI am used to arriving early.
get used to + noun / verb-ingthe change, the processI got used to the short meetings.
RuleAfter "used to", use the base verb. After "be used to" and "get used to", use a noun or verb-ing. For a habit you have now, use the present simple: "I arrive early now", not "I used to arrive early now".
