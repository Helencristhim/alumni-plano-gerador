Chapter 3: The Habit Code

Used To, Be Used To, Get Used To

old habits · normal habits · adapting

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"In Brazil, I used to arrive ten minutes late."

"Now I am used to arriving early for international meetings."

"Americans are used to short small talk."

"At first it felt cold, but I got used to it in a few days."

What do the orange words mean? Which one is an old habit? Which one is normal now? Which one is a change?

FormMeaningExample
used to + verban old habit or situation that is not true nowI used to arrive late.
be used to + noun / verb-ingit is normal for me, not strangeI am used to arriving early.
get used to + noun / verb-ingthe process of becoming used to somethingI got used to the short meetings.
didn't use to + verbthe negative of used toI didn't use to check the agenda.

After used to, use the base verb: "used to arrive". After be used to and get used to, use a noun or verb-ing: "used to arriving", "used to the culture". For the present habit, use the present simple, not used to: "I arrive early now".

Reading

Doing Business in Miami

Doing Business in Miami: A Short Guide

Many international participants say the same thing after their first week in Miami: the business culture felt cold at first, but they got used to it quickly. Most of the differences are small, and almost none of them are personal.

Time is the first difference. American partners are used to starting meetings exactly on time, and many people arrive five minutes early. Being punctual is a basic part of business etiquette. If you are going to be late, a short message before the meeting is appropriate and expected.

Small talk is the second difference. It is friendly, but it is short: two or three minutes about the weather, the trip, or sports. Then the conversation goes straight to the agenda. This is a custom, not a lack of interest in you.

The third difference is personal space. People usually stand about an arm's length apart, and they do not touch much in a first meeting. A firm handshake is the normal greeting; a hug or a kiss on the cheek can surprise a new partner, although it will rarely offend anyone.

Finally, avoid the stereotype in both directions. Not every American is cold, and not every Brazilian is late. A cultural misunderstanding is easy to fix when you name it with a smile.
