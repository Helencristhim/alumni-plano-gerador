Lesson 28 -- Terms of Reference

Series
Grand Designs (UK, Channel 4)

Every episode follows one family building or renovating a house, and almost every project grows bigger, later and more expensive than planned. It is scope creep in real life, explained in clear British English by the presenter, Kevin McCloud.

Tip: in each episode, listen for the moment when the plan changes. What was included at the start, and what was added later? How would you have defined the scope?

Podcast
Freakonomics Radio -- "Why Your Projects Are Always Late -- and What to Do About It"

Stephen Dubner talks to the Oxford professor Bent Flyvbjerg, who has studied thousands of big projects, from bridges to software, and explains why most of them go over budget and over time. The general side of the same problem as Lumen v. Orbis.

Tip: note how they describe what a project includes and excludes, and every time they talk about a plan that "grew". Which words would you use to fence it?

Podcast
Clifford Chance -- The Arbitration Lifecycle, Act II: "Navigating the Process"

Three arbitration lawyers discuss the procedural framework of an international arbitration: party autonomy, the timetable, memorials and pleadings, and how to control delay and costs. It is the stage where the Terms of Reference live.

Tip: write down every phrase they use to set limits on the process (for example, "in relation to", "limited to", "to the extent that") and compare them with the ones in this lesson.
