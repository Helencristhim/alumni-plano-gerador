Lesson 28 -- Pre-class

Terms of Reference -- Scope and Definition

How to read the document that opens an arbitration, or a builder's quote, and see what each word means and where the scope ends. Key words: terms of reference, a remit, to fall within, exhaustive, inter alia, to stipulate, scope creep, to narrow down, a catch-all, clear-cut, off-limits, to encompass. Structure: the language of scope and definition -- means, refers to, is defined as, for the purposes of, including but not limited to, limited to, excluding, to the extent that, falls outside the scope.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Terms of reference -- the document that says what a tribunal or a committee will decide, and how
"The parties signed the terms of reference in June."
A remit -- the area of activity or authority that a person or group is responsible for
"Tax questions are outside my remit."
To fall within -- to be included inside a limit, a category or an area
"Does this request fall within the original budget?"
Exhaustive -- complete, including every possible item
"This is not an exhaustive list, just the main examples."
Inter alia -- among other things (a Latin phrase used in formal documents)
"The claim concerns, inter alia, late payment."
To stipulate -- to say clearly, usually in writing, that something must be done
"The lease stipulates that pets are not allowed."
Scope creep -- the slow growth of a project beyond what was first agreed
"Scope creep turned a three-month project into a year."
To narrow down -- to reduce the number of possibilities or the size of something
"We narrowed down the list to three hotels."
A catch-all -- a general word or rule that covers anything that is not mentioned specifically
"'Other services' is just a catch-all at the end of the list."
Clear-cut -- easy to understand or decide, with no doubt
"It isn't a clear-cut case: both sides have a point."
Off-limits -- not allowed to be used, visited or discussed
"Work emails are off-limits on our vacation."
To encompass -- to include a wide range of things
"The new role encompasses legal, tax and compliance."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The first draft of the terms of reference arrived on Monday. Rodrigo went straight to the definitions. “The Go-Live Date” was defined as the date on which the System is first used in any store. One word, and fourteen months of delay became four. He changed it to all 140 stores. Then he read the tribunal’s remit: all disputes in relation to the Agreement, including but not limited to delay and payment. Good: the list was not exhaustive.

Orbis wanted more. Its lawyer asked to add the 2023 maintenance contract, but that contract had its own dispute clause and fell outside the scope of the case. Rodrigo agreed to one new issue only to the extent that it related to the Agreement. “It isn’t clear-cut,” he told the client, “but this is how we stop scope creep before the hearing.”

1. Why did Rodrigo change the definition of the Go-Live Date?
A Because the tribunal had asked both parties to rewrite every definition in the document before the first hearing.
B Because "any store" made the delay look much shorter.
C Because the client wanted a later date.
2. "Including but not limited to delay and payment." What does this tell you about the list?
A It is open: other disputes can also be decided.
B It is closed, so the tribunal can only decide disputes about delay and payment, and nothing else.
C It is not important.
3. "That contract ___ the scope of the case." Which form is correct?
A fell out of
B fell outside
C was fallen outside of

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Scope and Definition

GRAMMAR

First say what a word means. Then say exactly how far the scope goes: open the list, close it, or limit it.

JobPatternExample
Define a word"X" means + noun · refers to + noun · is defined as + noun"Completion" is defined as the day the work ends.
Set the frameFor the purposes of + nounFor the purposes of this quote, the kitchen includes the pantry.
Open the listincluding (but not limited to) · inter aliaCosts, including but not limited to travel.
Close the listlimited to · excludingThe price is limited to Section 2, excluding taxes.
Only as far asto the extent that + clauseWe agree to the extent that it relates to the contract.
In or outfalls within / falls outside the scope of · in relation to + nounThe old contract falls outside the scope.

Never: is defined like · includes but not limited to · the date which · falls out of the scope

Stage 2: Put the Terms in Order

Order

Good Terms of Reference go from the definitions, to the remit, to the parties' positions, to the issues. Put the sentences in that order.

?"The Agreement means the contract signed on 3 March 2025."
?"The Go-Live Date is defined as the first day the System is used in all stores."
?"The Tribunal shall decide all disputes in relation to the Agreement."
?"The maintenance contract falls outside the scope of this arbitration."
?"The Claimant contends that the System was fourteen months late."
?"The issues include, inter alia, whether the cap applies."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose what a careful drafter would say.

A builder's quote ends with "any other work that may be necessary." What do you say before you sign?
A "That's a catch-all. Extra work only if I approve it in writing."
B "Perfect, it's good that everything is included, so I'm sure the final price will stay exactly the same."
C "The work is defined like everything."
The other side wants to add a dispute about a different contract. You say:
A "That contract falls out of the scope."
B "That contract falls outside the tribunal's remit."
C "Fine, let's add it, because the more issues we put in the terms, the stronger our position will be."
You want to keep a list of costs open. You write:
A "All costs, including but not limited to travel."
B "All costs, limited to travel."
C "All costs, includes but not limited to travel, hotels, meals and any other expenses of the team."
A client asks you about a tax issue that is not your area. You say:
A "Tax is within my remit."
B "That's outside my remit, but I can loop in our tax team."
C "I'll answer anyway, because a good lawyer should know a little about every area of the law."
