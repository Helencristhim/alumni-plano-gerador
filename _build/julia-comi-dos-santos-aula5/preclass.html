Plans and Intentions

Eight words for the internship hunt (resume, recruiter, opportunity, department, company, offer, hire, position) and the two futures: going to for the plan you already had, will for the decision you make while speaking.

Stage 1.1: Words for the Hunt

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Resume — the one-page document with your studies and experience
"I am going to send my resume tomorrow."
Recruiter — the person who chooses who gets the job
"The recruiter is going to call me on Friday."
Opportunity — a chance to do something good
"This is a real opportunity and I am going to take it."
Department — one part of a company, like finance or marketing
"I would like to work in the marketing department."
Company — a business that people work for
"The company is going to hire six interns."
Offer — when a company says yes and invites you to work there
"If I get the offer, I am going to accept it."
Hire — to give somebody a job
"They are going to hire two people this semester."
Position — the exact job you are applying for
"The position starts in March."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. Then answer the questions.

Next semester I am going to apply for an internship. I am not going to wait until December like last year, because by then every position is gone. This week I am going to update my resume and send it to three companies. One of them is going to hire six interns, so the opportunity is real. If I get an offer, I am going to accept it, even if the department is not my first choice — experience is experience. The recruiter from the second company writes on Fridays, so I will know soon. And in January I am going to travel for a month.
Why is she not going to wait until December?
A Because by then every position is gone.
B Because she is going to travel.
Why is it "I am going to accept" and not "I will accept"?
A Because it is already decided, not decided while speaking.
B Because "accept" cannot be used with will.
What is she going to do in January?
A Start the internship.
B Travel for a month.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Two futures, one question: did you decide before, or now?

The plan you already had → going to. The decision you make while speaking → will.

going to

I am going to apply in March.
She is going to send her resume.
They are not going to hire.

will

Okay, I’ll send it today.
I’ll help you with that.
I’ll text you when I know.

+ I am going to apply next semester.
− I am not going to wait until December.
? Are you going to apply? (the to is part of it — never "going apply")

In real speech "going to" sounds like gonna. You will hear it everywhere. Do not write it.

Stage 1.5: Complete the sentence

Practice

Write going to or will. Ask yourself first: was it decided before, or right now?

"Next semester I apply for an internship."

"— Nobody has the file. — Fine, send it now."

Stage 3: Say it out loud

Speaking

Tap Listen, then Record. In real speech "going to" sounds like "gonna" — say it fast, write it long.

I'm going to apply for an internship next semester.
The recruiter is going to call me on Friday.
Can I ask you something about the position?

Stage 4: In the conversation

Quiz
A recruiter asks about your plans. Which answer works best?
A Internship. Next semester. Marketing.
B I'm going to apply for an internship next semester, ideally in the marketing department.
Your friend says nobody sent the file. You decide to do it now. What do you say?
A I'm going to send it now.
B I'll send it now.
Which question is correct?
A Are you going to accept the offer?
B Do you going to accept the offer?

Stage 5: Your six-month plan

Reflection
Write four sentences first, all with going to: something for this month, something for next semester, something you are NOT going to do and why, and one question you are going to ask a recruiter. Then turn the paper over and record 30 seconds saying them — without reading.
