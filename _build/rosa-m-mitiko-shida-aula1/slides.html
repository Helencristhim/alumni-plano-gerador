Chapter 3: The Code

What Stays and What Is Changing

I manage · I am rolling out

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I work for an American airline."

"I manage thirty corporate accounts."

"This year the company is rolling out a regional structure."

"At the moment I am spending more time with clients than in the office."

Two of the colored verbs describe what is always true. Two describe only this year. Which is which?

FormWhat it saysExample
Present simple
work · manage · report		Your role, your routine, what is generally true: every week, usually, twice a day."I work for an American airline."
"I manage thirty corporate accounts."
Present continuous
am rolling out · am spending		In progress now, temporary, still changing: this year, at the moment, this quarter."The company is rolling out a regional structure."
"I am spending more time with clients."
The third person
he · she · it		In the present simple, the third person adds -s. It disappears the moment you use the continuous."She manages the Santiago office."
"She is managing two teams this quarter."
State verbs
be · have · know · belong		Some verbs describe a state, not an action, and stay in the simple form even when you mean right now."I belong to the commercial area."
NOT "I am belonging to the commercial area."

Your whole professional description fits in these two. One says who you are; the other says what is moving around you this year.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"Every week I am visiting three corporate clients."
"Every week I visit three corporate clients."
"At the moment we roll out the new regional structure."
"At the moment we are rolling out the new regional structure."
"I am belonging to the commercial area."
"I belong to the commercial area."

A routine takes the present simple, and something in progress takes the present continuous. And a few verbs — be, have, know, belong, understand — describe a state, not an action, so they stay simple even when you mean this exact moment. If you can put "every week" in front of it, it is simple.

Chapter 4: The Call from Bogotá

Thursday, Eleven O'Clock

A colleague you have never met asks what you do

Dialogue

The Call from Bogotá

D
Rosa? Diego Restrepo, from the Bogotá office. We are on the same call on Thursday, so I thought I would say hello first.
R
Hello, Diego. Thank you for calling. I am Rosa, and I am in charge of the corporate accounts in São Paulo.
D
Corporate only? How big is your client portfolio?
R
About thirty companies. Most of them are banks and technology companies, and I visit two or three of them every week.
D
That is a serious workload. Mine is smaller, but I also cover Ecuador, so I am on a plane most weeks.
R
Then we have the same life. And this year my team is rolling out the new structure, so nothing is quiet.
D
Nothing is quiet anywhere. Are you coming to the convention in November? Everybody from the four countries is going.
R
I am. It will be good to put faces to the names. And you — how long have people in Bogotá worked with one regional office?
Comprehension

About Diego

1. Which office does Diego call from?
The Bogotá office.
2. Why is he on a plane most weeks?
Because he also covers Ecuador.
3. Why did he call before Thursday?
Because they are on the same call, and he wanted to say hello first.
4. What does he say about the convention in November?
That everybody from the four countries is going.
Listening

The Voicemail About Thursday

A voice message from the regional program office. Sound first — no text.

1. What time does the call start now, and why did it move?
Eleven in the morning, São Paulo time, because the Santiago team asked for it.
2. How many people are on the call, and how long does it run?
Eleven people, and it runs for fifty minutes.
3. What does every account manager have to do at the start?
Give one minute: their name, their city, and what their portfolio looks like today.
Listening 2

The First Commercial Update

The opening of a global training session. Sound first — no text.

1. What exactly is changing about the commercial teams?
Four countries now report into one regional office instead of four separate ones.
2. What is not changing this quarter?
The client portfolios — nobody is moving accounts.
3. What reason does she give for English as the working language?
Because eleven people from four countries need one language to share a call.
