Complementary Activities

Extra material to keep English in your week between classes. Nothing here is an exercise and nothing here is graded -- read, watch or listen when it fits your day, and mark it as done. All three are free and open: one click, no account, no payment. Every lesson includes one text you can send to your Kindle before a trip.

Lesson 1 -- Who Is Rosa?

Podcast & Reading
"How to make a good first impression at a new job" -- Life Kit, NPR

Eighteen minutes of clear American English about walking into a room where nobody knows you yet, and saying who you are without over-explaining. Connection to Lesson 1: this is the same task you practiced today -- introducing yourself to people you have never met. The page carries the full transcript beside the audio, so it is also the reading of the week.

Tip: read the transcript first and mark three sentences you could reuse on the regional call. Then listen without reading and see how many of them you catch by ear. Send the page to your Kindle if you want it for the plane.

Talk
"Your body language may shape who you are" -- Amy Cuddy, TED

Twenty minutes from a social psychologist about the two minutes before a high-pressure meeting, and what they do to the way you speak in it. Connection to Lesson 1: the words are only half of the sixty seconds you rehearsed today -- the other half is walking into the call believing you belong there.

Tip: watch it once with English subtitles on, then a second time with them off. If you catch about half the second time, that is already a win -- the ear is what we are training, and the transcript is on the page when you want to check.

Grammar Video
"Present Simple and Present Continuous" -- BBC Learning English, 6 Minute Grammar

Six minutes on exactly the line you drew in class today: the present simple for your role and your routine, the present continuous for what is in progress right now. Connection to Lesson 1: this is the difference between "I manage thirty accounts" and "this year we are rolling out a new structure".

Tip: pause after each example and say one sentence about your own work -- one routine, then one thing that is changing this quarter. If both come out in the same tense, say them again.
