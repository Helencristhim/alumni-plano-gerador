Chapter 4: The Code

How Sure Is the Report?

Must have, may have, could not have -- three degrees of certainty

Grammar Discovery

Fact, Guess or Ruled Out?

"The figures must have been produced from a plan that was never followed."

"The ballast pumps may have been running against a closed valve."

"A free surface effect could have made the list worse."

"A difference of that size could not have appeared without a change of sequence."

Three of these are the writer's own conclusions, not things anybody saw. Rank them: which is the surest, which is only possible, and which one is being ruled out?

FormWhat it doesExample
must have + past participlenear certainty: it is the only explanation left standingThe figures must have been produced from another plan.
may / might have + past participleone possible explanation among others, not chosenThe pumps may have been running against a closed valve.
could have + past participleit was possible; the report is not saying it happenedFree surface could have made the list worse.
could not have + past participleruled out: the report says it is impossibleIt could not have started the list.
The reading trapmust have is the writer's deduction, not a witnessed fact. An option that says 'the text states that the crane operators changed the sequence' is claiming more than the page does -- the page says the writer concluded it.

A modal perfect is how a report declares its own confidence. In the options, the swap is nearly always between the text STATES and the text SUGGESTS -- and the modal is the only thing on the page that tells you which of the two you are looking at.

Chapter 6: Say It Back

Say It Back

Two recordings, then the findings in your own words

Listening 1

The Chief Officer Explains

The officer who stopped the loading tells it his way. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. How fast did the list develop, and how big was it?
It was not gradual. Inside twenty minutes the vessel had seven degrees to starboard and the gangway was scraping the quay.
2. What did he do, and how long did it take?
He stopped the loading straight away and transferred ballast from the starboard tanks to the port side. That took four hours.
3. What is the part he says he cannot explain?
The plan. The manifest called for the two holds nearest the bow first, and the cranes began amidships. He says somebody must have changed the sequence and never told the bridge.
Listening 2

Before the Ship Moves

A pilot describes the passage plan she checks on the way in. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What does the pilot say her job is, and what is it not?
She is not there to take the ship over. The master keeps command. What she brings is knowledge of the water: the bank, the current and how the channel behaves on a falling tide.
2. What are the two things she asks for first?
The passage plan, with every waypoint from the pilot station to the berth and a time against each one, and the draft the ship is leaving with.
3. What does she do if a waypoint on the plan does not match what she would do?
They discuss it on the bridge before the ship moves, not halfway down the channel. And if there is no waypoint for the turn into the basin, they make one.
