Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read it inside a real sentence. These are the words the exam text will use.

Starboard -- the right-hand side of a ship, looking forward
"She took a list of seven degrees to starboard."
Port side -- the left-hand side of a ship, looking forward
"The gangway was rigged on the port side."
Bow -- the front part of a ship, the end that cuts the water
"The two holds nearest the bow were to be filled first."
Stern -- the rear part of a ship, behind the accommodation
"The tug made fast at the stern before the vessel moved."
Keel -- the structural spine that runs along the bottom of a ship
"The keel-to-deck stress figures had to be recalculated."
Ballast -- water carried in tanks to keep a ship stable and correctly trimmed
"The list was corrected by transferring ballast."
Manifest -- the official document listing everything a ship is carrying
"No stage was verified against the manifest."
Pilot -- a local navigation expert who boards a ship to guide her in and out of port
"The pilot had left the vessel before loading began."
Berth -- the place at a quay where a ship is tied up, or a crew member's bunk
"The vessel lay alongside her berth for two more days."
Waypoint -- a fixed position on a planned route, used to check progress
"Every waypoint of the passage plan is logged as the ship passes it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

The investigators concluded that the stability figures must have been produced from a plan that was never followed. A difference of seven degrees could not have appeared without a change of sequence.

Two other explanations were entertained and discounted. The ballast pumps may have been running against a closed valve, which would have produced a slow list rather than a sudden one. A free surface effect in a partly filled tank could have made the list worse, although it could not have started it. Somebody must have changed the loading sequence, and whoever changed it may not have told the bridge.

1. The figures must have been produced from a plan that was never followed. Did anybody watch that happen?
A Yes. Must have reports something a witness saw.
B No. Must have is the writer's conclusion: it is the only explanation left standing.
C No, and it also means the writer thinks it is unlikely.
2. Which sentence is the writer LEAST sure about?
A A difference of that size could not have appeared without a change of sequence.
B The ballast pumps may have been running against a closed valve.
C The figures must have been produced from another plan.
3. It could have made the list worse, although it could not have started it. Is free surface effect ruled out?
A Yes, completely. The report discounts it.
B Only as the cause. It is still allowed as something that made the list worse.
C No. The report says it started the list.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Modal Perfects -- How Sure the Report Is

GRAMMAR

A modal perfect is a verb that carries a percentage. It tells you how much of what follows is fact and how much is the writer's own deduction -- and that difference is what the wrong options are built on.

FormWhat it doesExample
must have + past participlenear certainty: it is the only explanation left standingThe figures must have been produced from another plan.
may / might have + past participleone possible explanation among others, not chosenThe pumps may have been running against a closed valve.
could have + past participleit was possible; the report is not saying it happenedFree surface could have made the list worse.
could not have + past participleruled out: the report says it is impossibleIt could not have started the list.
The reading trapmust have is the writer's deduction, not a witnessed fact. An option that says 'the text states that the crane operators changed the sequence' is claiming more than the page does -- the page says the writer concluded it.

A modal perfect is how a report declares its own confidence. In the options, the swap is nearly always between the text STATES and the text SUGGESTS -- and the modal is the only thing on the page that tells you which of the two you are looking at.
