Lesson 04 -- Pre-class

Your First Week of School -- Timetable, Locker, Homeroom

Reading the student handbook and building a plan B for everything that could go wrong. Key words: timetable, homeroom, attendance, locker, cafeteria, assignment, to hand in, quiz, field trip, to catch up on. Structure: first conditional -- if this happens, I will do that.

"The history assignment is due on Friday the twentieth."
To hand in -- to give finished work to a teacher
"Hand it in through the portal, not on paper."
Quiz -- a short test, usually at the start or end of a class
"There is a vocabulary quiz every Friday and it lasts ten minutes."
Field trip -- a school trip to learn something outside the classroom
"The field trip form has to come back signed by Wednesday."
To catch up on -- to do work that you missed and get back to where the class is
"I was ill for two days, so I have to catch up on chemistry."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The day starts in homeroom at eight forty, and that is where attendance is taken. If you are not in the room when the teacher says your name, it will go down as late, even if you are standing in the corridor with your bag. My timetable is printed on the back of my student card, so if I lose the card, I will lose the timetable with it. I took a photo of it on the first afternoon.

Assignments go through the online portal. If you hand something in late, you will lose ten percent for every day, which is arithmetic and not a threat. But if you email the teacher before the deadline, most of them give you the weekend. Ask early and they are flexible. Ask after and they are not.

The rest is geography. The lockers are assigned in the first week, and if you leave the combination inside the locker you will spend two days without your coat. Lunch is short, so if you queue at the main counter of the cafeteria you will stand for the whole break. And there is a quiz every Friday and a field trip in October. If I miss a class, I will catch up on it in the free block on Wednesday.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- If This Happens, I Will Do That

GRAMMAR

Two halves, one rule. The half with if never carries will.

FormUseExample
The if halfPresent simple. Never will, even though the meaning is future.If I miss the bus...
The result halfwill + base verb. This is where the future lives....I'll walk to school.
OrderEither half can come first. Comma only when if starts the sentence.If I miss it, I'll walk.
I'll walk if I miss it.
NegativeUse don't or doesn't in the if half, and won't in the result half.If I don't finish, I'll email her.
QuestionThe question word and will go at the front, and the if half stays the same.What will you do if the bus is late?
if or whenUse if for something possible, when for something certain. "When the bell rings" — the bell always rings.

This is the first conditional, and it is for things that really can happen. Only one thing goes wrong with it: putting will after if.
