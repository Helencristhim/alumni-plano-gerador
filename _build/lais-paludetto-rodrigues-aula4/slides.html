Chapter 3: The Code

Every Problem Has a Plan B

If this happens, I will do that. That is the whole shape.

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"If I miss the bus, I'll walk to school."

"If you hand it in late, you'll lose ten percent."

"I'll email the teacher if I don't finish tonight."

"If you use the north stairs, you'll be late."

Each sentence has two halves. Which half carries will — the one with if, or the other one?

HalfWhat goes in itExample
The if halfPresent simple. Never will, even though the meaning is future.If I miss the bus...
The result halfwill + base verb. This is where the future lives....I'll walk to school.
OrderEither half can come first. Comma only when if starts the sentence: "If I miss it, I'll walk" / "I'll walk if I miss it".
Negative and question"If I don't finish, I'll email her." — "What will you do if the bus is late?"
Its nameThis is the first conditional, and you have just made five of them. It is for things that are really possible. Later we meet the one for imaginary things.

Two halves, one rule: no will after if. That is the only thing that goes wrong.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"If I will miss the bus, I walk to school."
"If I miss the bus, I'll walk to school."
"If you will hand it in late, you lose ten percent."
"If you hand it in late, you'll lose ten percent."
"If the bell rings at three, I'll go home." (it always rings at three)
"When the bell rings at three, I'll go home."

The word will never sits in the if half, even though the meaning is clearly future. And when something is certain, not possible, English uses when, not if.

Lost in the Corridor

L
Sorry, do you know where room 214 is? I'm new and the bell went two minutes ago.
R
Second floor, but not through those stairs. If you use the north ones you'll come out by the gym. I'm going that way, come on.
L
Thank you. Does it count as late if I walk in now? They took attendance in homeroom already.
R
Nobody marks you late in the first week. If Mr Dawson says anything, just tell him you're the exchange student. It works every time.
L
Good to know. Is there a quiz today? Somebody said Fridays, but I've lost my timetable already.
R
Fridays, ten minutes, vocabulary. If you photograph the back of your student card now, you'll never lose it again.
L
Doing it right now. And if I don't finish the history assignment by Friday, what happens?
R
Ten percent a day. But if you email him before Friday, he usually gives you the weekend. Ask early, not after.
Comprehension

About Ryan

1. Why does he say not to use the north stairs?
Because they come out by the gym, so she would be even later.
2. What does he suggest doing with the student card?
Photographing the back of it, so she never loses the timetable again.
3. What does he say about handing the assignment in late?
Ten percent a day, but if you email the teacher before Friday he usually gives you the weekend.
Listening

Homeroom, Eight Forty

Mr Dawson gets through the announcements before the bell. Sound first — no text.

1. Why should new students see him at the end?
To get their locker number, because the list on the wall is out of date.
2. What counts as late, according to him?
Not being in the room when he calls your name — even standing in the corridor.
3. What happens if the field trip form is not signed by Wednesday?
You spend that day in the library with him instead of going.
Listening 2

The Map, in Words

Emma explains the school before your first Monday. Sound first — no text.

1. What will happen if you take the north stairs?
You will end up outside the gym and be late. She says to use the middle stairs.
2. Where does she say to eat, and why?
At the smaller counter behind the library, because the main queue eats the whole break.
3. What happened to Emma in grade nine?
She locked her own coat in her locker for two days, because she had not written the combination down.
