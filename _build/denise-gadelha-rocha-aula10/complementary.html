Lesson 10 -- Speaking Under Pressure

Series
The Crown -- Season 2, Episode 5, "Marionettes"

A head of state is told in public that her delivery is the problem: the words are correct, the pace is wrong, and the room has stopped listening. Everything that follows is about how somebody senior learns to sound the way they already are. Watch the pauses rather than the script — where she lets a silence stand, and where she fills it.

Tip: watch the final address twice. The first time, listen for content. The second time, count the deliberate pauses and note what each one buys her.

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart -- the episodes on speaking off the cuff and on anxiety

This is the show that treats spontaneous speaking as a skill with a method, not as a personality trait. Look for the episodes on answering without preparation and on managing the physical side of nerves. Their central claim is the one from today: the goal is not to be quicker, it is to have somewhere to stand while you think.

Tip: steal one structure per episode and write it on a card. Before your next call in English, put the card where you can see it and use it once, on purpose.

YouTube
Amy Cuddy -- Your Body Language May Shape Who You Are (TED)

Her subject is what a room reads before you have finished a sentence, which is exactly the gap this lesson works on. The part worth your time is her own story: an articulate person who lost access to her words under pressure and rebuilt the presence first, not the vocabulary. Twenty minutes, and the argument is uncomfortable in a useful way.

Tip: record ninety seconds of yourself on a work topic before you watch and ninety seconds after. Do not judge the English. Watch the shoulders, the pace and where your eyes go when a word is late.
