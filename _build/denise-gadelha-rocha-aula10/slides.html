Chapter 3: The Code

Used To, and Used To

The same three words, three different meanings

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I used to translate every sentence from Spanish in my head."

"I am not used to holding a room in silence."

"I am getting used to leaving the pause there."

"It used to take me four seconds. Now it takes one."

Two of these take an infinitive and two take -ing. Same three words. So what decides it?

FormWhat it meansExample
used to + verbA habit or state in the PAST that is over now. Past only — there is no present form.I used to translate everything in my head.
be used to + -ingYou are ACCUSTOMED to it. It is normal for you now. Any tense.I am used to presenting in English.
get used to + -ingYou are BECOMING accustomed. The process, not the result.I am getting used to the silence.
The testCan you say "I am used to it"? Then the "to" is a PREPOSITION, and a verb after it takes -ing. Cannot? Then it is past habit, and the verb is bare.
Negative and questionThe "d" moves to the auxiliary: I did not use to prepare them · Did you use to dread that call? Never did not used to.
The one to watchI am used to work means nothing at all. Either I used to work (past habit) or I am used to working (it is normal for me). Two different sentences, one letter apart.

The Recovery Kit

Four Moves, in This Order

Cheapest first. Most of the time you never reach the fourth.

1 · Rephrase — you have the idea, not the word

"Let me put that a different way."

2 · Answer at low resolution, then zoom in

"The short answer is yes, and I will come back to the detail."

3 · Stall openly — ask for the pause instead of taking it

"That is a fair question. Give me a second on it."

4 · Concede the detail and close it with a date

"I do not have that number in front of me. I will send it today."

One move is missing from this list on purpose: the apology. It buys nothing and it costs the room's expectations of you.

About Bruno

He started timing the pause. He used to call it a vocabulary problem, until he measured how late the word actually arrived.
2. What does he say he would have said in her place, and why?
"Let me put that a different way", then the same point again more slowly — because a rephrase reads as care, not as a rescue.

Chapter 5: Practice

Under Load

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "I am used to work in Spanish with the region."
"I am used to working in Spanish with the region." (in "be used to" the "to" is a preposition, so the verb takes -ing)
2. "I am used to be nervous in those calls, but now I am fine."
"I used to be nervous in those calls, but now I am fine." (a habit that is over needs the past form, not the accustomed one)
3. "Sorry, I forgot my English."
"Sorry, the word has gone for a second." Better still: "Let me put that a different way." (you cannot forget a language mid-sentence, and the apology is optional)
4. "Can I make a question about the reserving page?"
"Can I ask a question about the reserving page?" (in English you ask a question; only the answer can be made)
