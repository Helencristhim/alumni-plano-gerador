Lesson 08 — Pre-class

Sorry, The Tenth?

Eight words for checking — clarify, rephrase, confirm, note down, catch, talk over, summarize, minutes — and the shortest way to check a fact without stopping the call. Around fifteen minutes.

Stage 1.1: Words for checking

Vocabulary

These eight are what you say when you did not catch something. Tap Listen and say each one out loud.

Clarify — to make something clear that was not clear
"Could you clarify who signs on your side?"
Rephrase — to say the same thing again, with different words
"Could you rephrase that? I want to be sure."
Confirm — to check that what you understood is right
"Just to confirm: the tenth, not the twentieth."
Note down — to write something quickly, while it is being said
"Let me note down the reference number."
Catch — to hear and understand something as it goes past
"Sorry, I did not catch the last part."
Talk over — to speak while another person is still speaking
"Ten people, and everyone talking over everyone."
Summarize — to say the long thing again, short
"Let me summarize what we agreed, in three lines."
Minutes — the written record of what a meeting decided
"I will circulate the minutes this afternoon."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

A short stretch of a call, with the checks left in. Read it once, then answer.

David: We are looking at forty-eight employees, live on the first of March.
You: Forty-eight? The file we have says forty-two.
David: Forty-eight. The old spreadsheet is out of date.
You: Let me note that down. And Ralph approves the budget, doesn’t he?
David: He does, from London. Nothing moves until he has seen it.
You: Sorry, I didn’t catch the date for the file.
David: The tenth. Not the twentieth — the tenth.
You: Just to confirm: the tenth, and it goes to you, is that right?
David: To me. And copy Ralph.
You: Let me summarize: forty-eight people, file to you by the tenth, Ralph in copy, go live on the first of March.
Why "Forty-eight?" instead of "Sorry, could you repeat everything?"
A It is shorter, and short is always more polite.
B It asks about one thing only, so the call keeps moving instead of starting again.
C It hides the fact that you did not understand.
What is the job of the last line, the summary?
A To show David that you were paying attention.
B To catch anything still wrong while he is still on the call to correct it.
C To fill the last minute politely before saying goodbye.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Checking has three sizes. Pick the smallest one that works.

1. Echo the fact. Repeat the word you are unsure about, with your voice going up. "The tenth?" · "Ralph, in London?" · "Forty-eight?" This is the cheapest check there is: it asks about one thing and costs the other person two seconds.

2. Add a tag when you want a clear yes or no. Positive sentence, negative tag: "You said Tuesday, didn’t you?" · "Ralph approves it, doesn’t he?" If the tag is hard in the moment, "is that right?" works after anything and never changes shape.

3. Ask for the whole thing again only when you really lost the sentence: "Sorry, I didn’t catch that last part." · "Could you rephrase that?"

Most people jump straight to 3, or say nothing at all. The echo is what fluent professionals actually use, and it is the easiest of the three to say.

Speaking

Stage 4: On the call

Quiz
He says a number and you are 70% sure you heard it. What do you do?
A Write down what you think you heard and check the minutes later.
B Echo it: "Forty-two?" It costs two seconds now and a week later.
C Ask him to repeat the whole explanation from the beginning.
Someone joined the call ten minutes ago and you still do not know who he is.
A "Sorry — I don’t think we’ve met. Could you tell me your role?"
B Say nothing and work it out from the email thread afterwards.
C Address every answer to David and hope the other one leaves.
Two people are talking over each other and you have lost both.
A Wait until they stop and answer whoever spoke last.
B "Sorry, I can’t understand anything."
C "Sorry — David first, then Karen?" Order the room instead of apologising for it.

Stage 5: One listen, four facts

Reflection
Think of the last call where you understood about half. What were the four things you actually needed — the numbers, the dates, the names, who does what? Say which one you missed, and record the sentence you could have used to check it. One take, out loud.
