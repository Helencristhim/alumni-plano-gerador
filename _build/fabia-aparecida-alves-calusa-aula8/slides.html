The Pattern

Check without stopping the call

"We need it by the tenth." — "The tenth?"
"Ralph signs it off." — "Ralph, in London?"
"You said Tuesday, didn’t you?"
"So that’s forty-two people, is that right?"

Repeat the fact, with your voice going up

"Sorry?" makes them repeat everything. Echoing just the number, the name or the date asks about ONE thing and keeps the call moving. Add didn't you? or is that right? when you want a clear yes or no.

The Pattern

One phrase per situation

What happened
What you say
You missed a number
"Sorry, forty-two or forty-eight?"
You missed a whole sentence
"Sorry, I didn’t catch that last part."
You heard it but it made no sense
"Could you rephrase that? I want to be sure."
Two people talking at once
"Sorry — David first, then Karen?"
The call is ending
"Let me summarize what we agreed, in three lines."

None of these admits weakness. On an international call, everybody uses them — including native speakers.

Listening 2

The minutes are wrong

1. Why does she say the minutes are wrong?
David said the tenth, someone else said the twentieth, and nobody corrected it — so the document says the twentieth.
2. What does she still not know about two of the people on that call?
Who said the twentieth, and the name of the person who joined halfway through.
3. Where exactly would you have interrupted, and with which phrase?
At the second date — "Sorry, the tenth or the twentieth?" — and at the new voice: "Sorry, who has just joined us?"
