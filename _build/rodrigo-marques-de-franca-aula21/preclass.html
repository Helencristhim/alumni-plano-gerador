Lesson 21 -- Pre-class

The Interview -- Emphasis and Information Order

How to tell the story of your career in an interview so that people remember it: one moment, not a list, with the weight at the start of the sentence. Key words: to be thrown in at the deep end, to learn the ropes, a mentor, to pay off, to land, a breakthrough, to pivot, a niche, to thrive, to lay the groundwork, rewarding, to make a name for yourself. Structure: inversion after a negative or limiting word at the start (not only did I, never have I, only after... did I, little did I know) and emphatic do (I do enjoy it).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To be thrown in at the deep end -- to start a difficult job with no help or preparation
"In my first week, I was thrown in at the deep end."
To learn the ropes -- to learn how a job or a place works
"It took me a year to learn the ropes at the firm."
A mentor -- an experienced person who advises and guides you over a long time
"My mentor never gave me the answer. He made me find it."
To pay off -- to bring good results after a long period of effort
"All those late nights finally paid off."
To land -- to succeed in getting something that many people want, like a client or a job
"In 2007, I landed my first international client."
A breakthrough -- an important success that makes more success possible
"That deal was my breakthrough."
To pivot -- to change the direction of your work or your career
"After two years in litigation, I pivoted back to corporate work."
A niche -- a specialized area of work where you have few competitors
"Cross-border distribution deals became my niche."
To thrive -- to grow and do very well
"Some lawyers thrive under pressure."
To lay the groundwork -- to do the early work that makes later success possible
"Those first years in Salvador laid the groundwork for everything."
Rewarding -- giving you satisfaction, even when it is hard
"Teaching young lawyers is the most rewarding part of my job."
To make a name for yourself -- to become well known and respected in your field
"She made a name for herself in arbitration before she was forty."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Megan Hale hosts Counsel Across Borders, a podcast for lawyers who work with foreign clients. This week, her guest was Rodrigo. He started his career in Salvador, almost by accident, and in his first week he was thrown in at the deep end: a client meeting, alone. Megan laughed. On her first day as a reporter, her editor had sent her to a murder trial on her own.

Rodrigo learned the ropes with a mentor, an older partner. Not only did he make Rodrigo write every contract twice, he made him explain every clause out loud. The hard work paid off. Only after he moved to São Paulo did Rodrigo really see it: two years later, he landed his first international client, and that was his breakthrough.

He tried litigation for a while and then pivoted back to corporate work. "I do enjoy a good argument," he said, "but rarely have I felt as useful as when a deal closes." His advice to young lawyers who want to make a name for themselves: lay the groundwork early and find a niche where you can thrive. "Little did I know that those first years would be the most rewarding."

1. "Not only did he make Rodrigo write every contract twice..." Why does the sentence say did he make, and not he made?
A Because "not only" is at the start, so the auxiliary comes before the subject.
B Because it is a question that Megan asks Rodrigo during the interview, and questions always need did before the subject.
C Because the story happened in the future.
2. What happened to Megan on her first day as a reporter?
A She interviewed sixty lawyers for her new podcast and only three of them agreed to come on the show.
B Her editor sent her to a murder trial on her own.
C She had a client meeting alone.
3. In "I do enjoy a good argument", what does do add?
A It turns the sentence into a question.
B It shows that he used to enjoy arguments in the past but stopped when he left litigation for good.
C Emphasis: he really enjoys it, even if the listener doubts it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Emphasis and Information Order

GRAMMAR

A negative or limiting word at the start of the sentence pulls the auxiliary in front of the subject. It is still a statement, not a question.

Start withFormExample
Not onlyaux + subject + verb ..., (but) alsoNot only did I learn the law, I also learned the business.
Never / Rarely / Seldomaux + subject + verbRarely have I felt so useful.
Only later / Only after... / Only when...aux + subject + verbOnly after I moved did I see it.
Littledid + subject + know / realizeLittle did I know how hard it would be.
Emphatic dodo / does / did + base verbI do enjoy it. She did try.
Where to use itinterviews, speeches, presentations, formal writingIn a relaxed chat with friends, it can sound too dramatic.

Never: Not only I learned... · Never I have seen... · Only later did I realized... · I did enjoyed...

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A podcast host asks: "What did your first job teach you?" You want to stress that it taught you two things. You say:
A "Not only I learned the law there, but I also learned how a business works from the inside."
B "Not only did I learn the law, I also learned the business."
C "Not only learned I the law."
An interviewer says: "Lawyers don't really enjoy long contracts, do they?" You disagree, with emphasis. You say:
A "Some of us do enjoy them, believe me."
B "Some of us did enjoyed them a lot, especially the long ones with many clauses and annexes."
C "Do some of us enjoy them?"
You want to say that you understood the risk only years later, with emphasis. You say:
A "Only years later I understood the risk."
B "Only years later did I understand the risk."
C "Only years later did I understood the risk that the client was taking with that contract."
At a barbecue, a friend asks what you did last weekend. Which answer sounds natural there?
A "Never have I worked so hard on a weekend as I did on this one, my friend."
B "Little did I know that I would work."
C "I worked the whole weekend, unfortunately."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

A podcast host asks you: "Tell us about one moment that changed your career." Choose one real moment, not a list of dates. In about sixty seconds, tell it as a scene, and use at least two forms of emphasis: not only did I, never have I, only after... did I, little did I know, or I do / I did.
