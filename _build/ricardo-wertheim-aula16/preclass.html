Lesson 16 -- Pre-class

How Long Have You...?

How to say how long something has lasted. Key words: century, decade, generation, resident, last, survive, ruins, restore. Structure: present perfect with for and since -- I have lived here since 1968, for fifty years.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Century -- a period of one hundred years
"The palace is seventeen centuries old."
Decade -- a period of ten years
"I have lived in this apartment for two decades."
Generation -- all the people in a family born at about the same time
"Four generations of my family have lived here."
Resident -- a person who lives in a place
"About three thousand residents live inside the walls."
To last -- to continue to exist or to work for a long time
"Good stone buildings last for centuries."
To survive -- to continue to exist after something difficult or dangerous
"The old walls survived two earthquakes."
Ruins -- what is left of a very old building after most of it has fallen down
"You can see Roman ruins near the harbor."
To restore -- to repair an old building so it looks the way it did before
"They restored the facade in 2015."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Luka has been a ferry captain for twenty-two years. He has worked on the line between Split and the island of Hvar since 2010, and he has crossed the same sea thousands of times.

His father was a captain too. He worked on the ferries for thirty years and stopped in 1998. "I have known some passengers since they were children," Luka says. "Now they travel with their own children. Three generations on my boat!"

1. "He has worked on the line since 2010." Does Luka still work on this line?
A No, because 2010 was many years ago and the sentence talks about a finished time in his life.
B Yes. It started in 2010 and it is still true.
C We do not know.
2. Why does the text say for twenty-two years, and not since twenty-two years?
A Because twenty-two years is a period, a length of time.
B Because twenty-two is a big number, and since is only for small numbers like two or three.
C Because Luka is a captain.
3. "His father worked on the ferries for thirty years." Is his father still a captain?
A Yes, because the sentence uses for thirty years.
B Yes, he works with Luka on the same boat between Split and Hvar every summer.
C No. It is finished: he stopped in 1998, so the text uses the past simple.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- For and Since

GRAMMAR

How to say how long something has lasted, when it is still true today.

FormRuleExample
have / has + past participlestarted in the past, still true nowI have lived here since 1968.
for + a periodhow longfor ten years · for a decade · for four generations
since + a starting pointwhen it startedsince 1968 · since Monday · since I was a child
QuestionHow long + have / has + person + participleHow long have you known Elena?
Finished or still true?Finished: He lived there for two years (past simple). Still true: He has lived there for two years.
The trapNever I live here since 1968. When it is still true and you say how long, use have or has: I have lived here since 1968.

Ask yourself two questions. Did it start in the past? Is it still true? Two yeses mean have or has, and then for or since.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are talking about how long things have lasted. Choose the best thing to say.

A new friend asks about your city. You say:
A "I have lived in Sao Paulo for most of my life."
B "I live in Sao Paulo since I was a child, in the same neighborhood near the park."
C "I am living in Sao Paulo for many years."
You want to know how long Elena has had the guesthouse. You ask:
A "How long do you have the guesthouse, Elena, and who helped you at the beginning?"
B "How long have you had the guesthouse?"
C "When have you had the guesthouse?"
The cathedral became a church in the seventh century. You say:
A "It has been a church for the seventh century, like many old buildings in Split."
B "It is a church since the seventh century."
C "It has been a church since the seventh century."
You met your best friend at university in 1972. You say:
A "We know each other since 1972."
B "We have known each other since 1972."
C "We have known each other for 1972, from the first week at university."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think of something in your life that has lasted: a building you love, a friendship, a cafe, a football club. Take about ninety seconds. Say what it is, how long it has been part of your life, and why it has lasted. Use for and since at least two times each, one question with How long, and four words from this lesson.
