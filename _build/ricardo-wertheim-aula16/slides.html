Chapter 4: The Code

For Fifty Years, Since 1968

have / has + participle · for + a period · since + a starting point

Grammar Discovery

For or Since?

"People have lived inside the walls since the seventh century."

"Marko has lived near the Peristyle since 1968."

"My family has been here for four generations."

"I have known my neighbors for fifty years."

Did these things start in the past? Are they still true today? Now look at what comes after for and after since: a length of time, or the moment it started?

FormRuleExample
have / has + past participleit started in the past and it is still true nowI have lived here since 1968.
for + a periodhow long: ten years, a decade, four generationsfor fifty years
since + a starting pointwhen it started: 1968, Monday, I was a childsince 1968 · since I was a child
How long ...?How long + have / has + person + participleHow long have you lived in Sao Paulo?
Still true or finished?He lived there for a few years -- finished, past simple. People have lived there for 1,300 years -- still true.
The trapNever I live here since 1968 and never I am here for ten years when you mean how long. Say I have lived here since 1968 and I have been here for ten years.

One question decides it: did it start in the past, and is it still true? Then it is have or has.
