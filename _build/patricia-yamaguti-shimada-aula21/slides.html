Chapter 3: Do You Know...?

Do You Know...?

Two words that make any question polite

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Where is the restroom? → Do you know where the restroom is?"

"Where is the station? → Do you know where the station is?"

"Is there a pharmacy nearby? → Do you know if there is a pharmacy nearby?"

"Where can I buy a charger? → Do you know where I can buy a charger?"

Direct questionPolite questionWhat moves
Where is the restroom?Do you know where the restroom is?is goes to the END
Where are the elevators?Do you know where the elevators are?are goes to the END
Is there a pharmacy nearby?Do you know if there is a pharmacy nearby?yes/no question: add if
Where can I buy a charger?Do you know where I can buy a charger?I can, not can I

After Do you know, the rest of the sentence is NOT a question any more.

Practice

The Four for Any Stranger

Open, Ask, Ask Again, Thank

"Excuse me, sorry to bother you."

the door: one sentence, never three apologies

"Do you know where the restroom is?"

the polite question: the verb goes to the end

"Do you know if there is a pharmacy nearby?"

yes/no? add if

"Thank you so much. You are very kind."

the close: the stranger leaves happy

Listening

A Tip from a Local

A man who lives in the city talks about tourists who ask for help. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is the first thing tourists should say?
Excuse me.
2. How many times should they say sorry?
Once. One sorry is enough.
3. What is the best question, in his opinion?
Do you know where... is? It is short, and people can say yes or no.
