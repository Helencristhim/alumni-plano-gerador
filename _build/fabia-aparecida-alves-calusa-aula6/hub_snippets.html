Lesson 06 — Pre-class

We Used To Do It By Hand

Eight words for change — transition, expansion, shift, legacy, restructure, incorporate, evolve, outgrow — and the form that says "and we do not do that anymore" without you having to add it: used to. Around fifteen minutes.

Stage 1.1: Words for change

Vocabulary

You have lived through all eight of these. Tap Listen and say each one out loud.

Transition — the period of moving from one way of working to another
"The transition to digital filing took us two years."
Expansion — growth into new areas, new clients or new offices
"The expansion into BPO changed the firm completely."
Shift — a change of direction, usually gradual
"There has been a shift from bookkeeping to advisory work."
Legacy — old, still running, and expensive to replace
"Their legacy system still ran payroll in 2019."
Restructure — to change how the teams and the work are organised
"We restructured the firm around three service lines."
Incorporate — to register a company formally, or to bring something inside
"They incorporated the Brazilian entity in 2021."
Evolve — to change slowly into something better suited
"The role evolved. I started in bookkeeping and ended in HR."
Outgrow — to become too big for something that used to fit
"We outgrew the old system long before we replaced it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

A partner telling a prospect how the firm got here. Read it once, then answer.

When I joined, we used to do everything on paper. Filing cabinets, physical delivery, all of it. We used to work only with Brazilian companies, and honestly we did not use to think about anything else.

Then in 2015 we took on our first foreign client, almost by accident. They needed every report in English, every month. That one client changed the direction of the firm.

We had outgrown the legacy system without noticing. The transition took about three years, and we restructured the whole back office around it.

Today we serve a hundred and forty companies. I tell you this because you are about to hand us something important, and I think you want to know that we have changed before — and survived it.
Does the firm still work only with Brazilian companies?
A No — "used to" already tells you that ended.
B Yes — nothing in the text says it changed.
Why "in 2015 we took on" and not "we used to take on"?
A Because it happened more than once.
B Because it is one single event, with a date — not a habit.
Why does she tell this story to a prospect at all?
A To show the firm has been through a change before and came out of it.
B To explain why the fees went up.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

A habit that ended, or one moment with a date

A good company story alternates the two. That is the whole technique.

used to — and it ended

We used to do it by hand.
Clients used to come to the office.
I used to work only in bookkeeping.

past simple — one moment

We replaced the system in 2018.
The pandemic ended that in a week.
I moved to back office in 2016.

− We did not use to work with foreign clients. (no d after did not)
? Did you use to handle payroll internally? (one of the best discovery questions you have)

The shape of the story: "We used to do it by hand. We replaced the system in 2018. Now we run payroll for two hundred companies." Then → turning point with a date → now.

used to only talks about the past. For a habit today, use the present simple.

Stage 4: Telling the story

Quiz
You want to say the firm no longer works only with Brazilian companies. Which is best?
A "We used to work only with Brazilian companies."
B "We worked only with Brazilian companies before, but now not anymore."
The system was replaced once, in 2018. How do you say it?
A "We used to replace the system in 2018."
B "We replaced the system in 2018."
Which question would tell you most about a prospect?
A "Did you use to handle payroll internally?"
B "Do you like your current provider?"
