Chapter 4: The Code

What Must Be True, What Can’t

Four sentences about the library notice, and how sure each speaker is

Grammar Discovery

How Sure Are You?

"The library closes at nine, so students can’t be there at ten."

"The group rooms are booked online, so there must be a booking website."

"She left the last three questions blank. She must have run out of time."

"He changed his answer. He might have had a hunch, or he may have found proof."

Nobody in these sentences knows for sure. How certain is each speaker: almost certain yes, almost certain no, or only possible? And which two sentences are about the past?

JobFormExample
Almost certain it is true (now)must + base verbThere must be a booking website.
Almost certain it is not true (now)can’t / couldn’t + base verbThey can’t be there at ten.
Possible (now)might / may / could + base verbIt might be a distractor.
Almost certain (past)must have + past participleShe must have run out of time.
Almost certain it did not happen (past)can’t have / couldn’t have + past participleHe can’t have read the whole passage.
Possible (past)might have / may have / could have + past participleHe might have had a hunch.
The trapnever mustn’t for deduction; never must toNot He mustn’t be there. Say He can’t be there.

In one line: Must for almost yes, can’t for almost no, might for maybe, and add have + participle for the past.

Listening 1 · A Tutor

Stop Looking for the Sentence

A TOEFL tutor on inference questions. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does the tutor think the students who read the most are often the slowest?
They were trained to find the sentence that contains the answer, and on inference questions that sentence does not exist.
2. What is an inference, according to her?
What must be true if two facts in the text are both true.
3. What exercise does she give everyone?
Answer the question in your own words before looking at the options, and then choose the option that says it in different words.
Listening 2 · A Campus Announcement

The Library, This Week

A short announcement, like a Read in Daily Life text, but spoken. Sound first — no text.

1. Why is the second floor now reserved for silent study?
Because of the exam period.
2. A student booked a room by email last week. What must be true, and what should they do?
Their request was not saved, so they must book it again on the website.
3. On Friday, can a student study in the library at eight in the evening? Where can they print?
No, the library closes at six on Friday. They can print on the first floor.
