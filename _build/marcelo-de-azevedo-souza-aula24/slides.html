Each sentence comes from one of your last six lessons. Which lesson, and what does each one do?

LessonStructureExample
18causative: have / get + object + past participleI had my bike serviced.
19future forms: arrangement, plan, predictionI’m meeting Fiona on Thursday.
20participle clausesHaving learned the basics, I relaxed.
21future in the pastI was going to quit, but I didn’t.
22comparatives that measureThe more I read, the less I translate.

Dialogue

Talking with Fiona

F
Thanks for doing this, Marcelo. So, halfway through. Has it got easier?
M
I think so, but not in the way I expected. The more I speak, the less I notice my own progress.
F
Interesting. Was there a moment when you nearly gave up?
M
Yes. I was going to stop in July. Then I had my calls recorded and listened to them, and I could pinpoint three habits.
F
What is your sticking point now?
M
Under pressure, I fall back on simple sentences. Having seen that, I now practice the hard ones on purpose.
F
And what would you tell someone who is about to quit?
M
Do less, but do it every week. Consistency matters more than hours, and then you raise the bar.

Fiona’s New Idea

0:000:00

Listening 2

The Plateau

A podcast about learning things the slow way. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. According to the host, what does the plateau really mean?
Not that you have stopped, but that the easy gains are over.
2. What are the two traps?
Some people quit, and others go back to their comfort zone and use the same twenty sentences.
3. What do the learners who break through do?
They record themselves, pinpoint one problem, and work only on that problem for a month.
