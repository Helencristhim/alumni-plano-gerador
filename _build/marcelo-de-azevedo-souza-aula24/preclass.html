Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example.

A comfort zone -- the situation where you feel safe and nothing is difficult
"On long rides I stay in my comfort zone, and I never get faster."
To brush up on -- to practice something again to improve a skill you once had
"I need to brush up on my phone English."
A breakthrough -- a sudden, important step forward after a long effort
"The breakthrough came when I stopped translating."
To backslide -- to return to old, worse habits after making progress
"After the holidays, it is easy to backslide."
Consistency -- doing something in the same way, regularly, over time
"Consistency beats a weekend course."
To fine-tune -- to make small changes so that something works perfectly
"Now I am fine-tuning the details."
To pinpoint -- to find or describe something exactly
"Can you pinpoint the moment it got easier?"
Deliberate practice -- practice that targets one weakness on purpose, with feedback
"Ten minutes of deliberate practice is worth an hour of chatting."
A sticking point -- the one problem that keeps stopping progress
"My sticking point is the present perfect."
To fall back on -- to use something familiar when the new thing is too hard
"Under pressure, I fall back on simple sentences."
Self-aware -- knowing your own strengths, habits and limits
"A self-aware learner knows which mistakes he repeats."
To raise the bar -- to set a higher standard for yourself
"For the second half, I want to raise the bar."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Marcelo is halfway through his course. Having finished twenty-three lessons, he decides to look back. Last month he had two of his calls recorded, and he listened to them with his teacher.

He noticed something. The more he spoke, the less he hesitated, but under pressure he still fell back on simple sentences. He was going to ignore it, but he did not. Now he practices the hard sentences on purpose.

“Will the second half be easier?” his teacher asked. “I hope not,” Marcelo said. “I’m meeting a new client in English next month, and I want to raise the bar.”

"He had two of his calls recorded." Who recorded the calls?
A Marcelo recorded them himself, using his own phone, in his office at the end of each call.
B Somebody else recorded them for him.
C Nobody recorded them.
"He was going to ignore it, but he did not." Did he ignore it?
A No. It was a plan that did not happen.
B Yes, he ignored it and continued with the simple sentences that he already used at work.
C We do not know.
Why does Marcelo say "I hope not"?
A Because he does not want to continue the course in the second half and plans to stop soon.
B Because he did not understand the question.
C Because he wants the second half to be harder, so he can raise the bar.

Quiz
