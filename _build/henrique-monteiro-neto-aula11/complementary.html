Lesson 11 -- The Thing Is

Podcast
"The 15 Minute City" — 99% Invisible

An episode on the idea that everything you need should be a quarter of an hour from your door, and on what happens to a city that tries. Connection to Lesson 11: it is today's vocabulary in use — walkable, sprawling, gentrify — in unhurried American English. The full transcript is on the page beside the audio.

Tip: listen for fifteen minutes and stop. Then say out loud, with emphasis, whether you would want to live in the city they describe — and use the very fact that at least once.

Talk
"Four Ways to Make a City More Walkable" — Jeff Speck, TED

A city planner making a strong argument, quickly, with numbers. Connection to Lesson 11: he uses emphatic structures constantly, because he is arguing against an orthodoxy — watch for do, precisely because and far and away. English transcript on the page.

Tip: watch once, then pick the claim of his you least agree with and argue against it out loud for sixty seconds, with emphasis. Disagreeing is the exercise.

Grammar Video
"Using DO and DID to Make a Strong Point in English"

A short lesson on the emphatic auxiliary: when it is used, why the verb after it stays in the base form, and how the stress falls in speech. Connection to Lesson 11: the grammar kept outside the class, as you asked.

Tip: this one is about SOUND as much as form. Say each example aloud twice, once flat and once with the stress on do, until the difference is obvious in your own mouth.
