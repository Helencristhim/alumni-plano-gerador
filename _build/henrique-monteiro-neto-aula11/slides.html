Chapter 3: The Code

Small Words That Carry Weight

How spoken English puts its foot down

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I do think it is overrated, and I say that with affection."

"It is precisely because it is run-down that it still has character."

"The very fact that nobody photographs it is the point."

"The old town is every bit as good as the guidebooks say."

Remove the highlighted words and every sentence stays correct. What exactly is lost?

FormWhat it doesExample
do / does / did
+ base form		The emphatic auxiliary. It answers a doubt that was never spoken, and in speech it carries the stress. Only in the AFFIRMATIVE, and never with another auxiliary."I do think it is overrated."
"She did warn us."
the very + nounPoints at exactly this one, and no other. The very fact that is the most useful of the family in argument."The very fact that nobody photographs it is the point."
"That is the very street I meant."
It is precisely /
exactly / only because		Isolates the cause and rules out every other. Common when you are about to say something counter-intuitive."It is precisely because it is run-down that it survives."
every bit as · far and away
nothing like · by some margin		Comparative intensifiers. Nothing like is negative and catches people out: nothing like as good means much worse."Far and away the best square in the city."
"It is nothing like as bad as they say."
Fronting for emphasisMoving the complement to the front. Emphatic, slightly literary, and unmistakably fluent when it lands."Beautiful, it is not."
"That I do not accept."

None of these adds information, and all of them add weight. A speaker who never uses them is heard as neutral even when they are not — and in a conversation where you are defending something unpopular, being heard as neutral is the same as losing.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I do agreed with you about the cathedral."
"I do agree with you about the cathedral."
"It is precisely because of it is run-down that the rents are low."
"It is precisely because it is run-down that the rents are low."
"The very fact of that nobody photographs it is the point."
"The very fact that nobody photographs it is the point."

Three mechanical traps. After emphatic do comes the BASE form — the tense is already carried by the auxiliary. Because introduces a clause and because of a noun: never both. And the very fact that takes a bare that, with no of in front of it.

Chapter 4: The City Nobody Defends

The City Nobody Defends

An urbanist who has spent a career on places other people dismiss

Dialogue

Defending the Unfashionable

C
I have made a career out of unfashionable cities, and I will tell you the pattern. Everybody who dislikes one has spent two days in it, both of them in the centre.
H
I do recognise that. Although in fairness, two days in the centre is what most people get.
C
It is, and the centre is usually the worst part. It is teeming with visitors, the prices are absurd, and the people who actually live there left it years ago.
H
So what would you send somebody to see instead? Not a hidden gem from a guidebook — something real.
C
A market at seven in the morning, and then a residential street forty minutes out. The very fact that nothing is happening there is what you are looking at.
H
That is a hard sell to somebody with two days.
C
It is. And it is precisely because it is a hard sell that the cities stay misunderstood. A place that photographs badly is assumed to be soulless, which is a category error.
H
Every bit as much a category error as assuming that a photogenic one has substance.
Comprehension

About Clara

1. What pattern does Clara describe in people who dislike a city?
They have spent two days in it, both of them in the centre.
2. What does she say about city centres?
That they are usually the worst part — teeming with visitors, absurdly expensive, and abandoned by residents.
3. What would she send somebody to see instead?
A market at seven in the morning and a residential street forty minutes out.
4. What does she call a category error?
Assuming that a place which photographs badly is soulless.
Listening 1

The Walk She Recommends

A recorded walking route. Sound first — no text.

1. Where does the walk start, and at what time?
At the market, at seven in the morning, before it fills up.
2. What does she say about the cathedral square?
That it is worth ten minutes and no more, and that it is overrated.
3. Where does the walk end?
On a residential street forty minutes out, where nothing is happening.
Listening 2

Against the Walkable City

A dissenting voice. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he accept about walkable cities?
That they are pleasant, and that the evidence on health and street life is real.
2. What is his objection?
That the policies often price out the residents they claim to serve.
3. What does he say the debate ignores?
The people who commute in from outside and are never in the room.
