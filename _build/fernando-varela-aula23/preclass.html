Lesson 23 -- Pre-class

How Things Used to Be

Twenty-four years at Einstein, then and now: Sarah visits the oncology center and Fernando tells her how things changed. Key words: nowadays, replace, paperwork, equipment, waiting time, rare, common, huge. Structure: used to, didn't use to and Did you use to...? -- I used to work in finance. Nowadays, the waiting time is much shorter.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Nowadays -- at the present time, compared with the past
"Nowadays, most patients book their appointments online."
Replace -- to put something new in the place of something old
"Tablets replaced the paper charts in 2015."
Paperwork -- all the forms and documents you have to fill in
"We used to spend two hours a day on paperwork."
Equipment -- the machines and tools you need for a job
"The new equipment is smaller and much faster."
Waiting time -- how long a person has to wait for something
"The waiting time for a first appointment is shorter now."
Rare -- not common: it does not happen very often
"This treatment used to be very rare in Brazil."
Common -- normal: it happens often, or there are many of it
"Video calls with partners are common now."
Huge -- very, very big
"The network had a huge change in twenty years."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Fernando started at the hospital twenty-four years ago. It was his first job. He used to work in finance, and he used to spend hours on budgets and paperwork. He didn't use to work with other countries.

The hospital used to be much smaller, and patients used to wait weeks for an appointment. Nowadays, Fernando leads the Oncology Network, he has video calls with Boston, and the waiting time is only a few days. When Sarah visited, she asked him: "Did you use to imagine this?" He laughed: "Never."

1. "He used to work in finance." Does Fernando work in finance now?
A Yes, he still works in finance every day, together with the Oncology Network and the partners.
B No. It was true in the past, and it is not true now.
C We do not know.
2. Why does the text say "He didn't use to work" and not "He didn't used to work"?
A Because after did, we use the form without D: use to.
B Because the sentence is about the future, so the verb has to change its form completely.
C Because it is a polite form.
3. Which sentence talks about NOW?
A "Patients used to wait weeks for an appointment at the hospital in São Paulo."
B "He used to spend hours on budgets."
C "Nowadays, the waiting time is only a few days."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used to

GRAMMAR

Used to tells the old story. Nowadays tells the new one.

FormRuleExample
Affirmativesubject + used to + verb (the same for all people)I used to work in finance.
Negativedidn't use to + verb (no D after did)We didn't use to have video calls.
QuestionDid + subject + use to + verb?Did you use to travel a lot?
NowNowadays + present simpleNowadays, I lead the network.
The trapUsed to is only for the past. For a habit now, use the present simpleI usually drive to work (not I use to drive)

Use used to for habits and situations that were true for a long time in the past, and are not true now. For one action in the past, use the past simple: "I started in 2002."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Talking about the past and the present at work. Choose the best thing to say.

A visitor asks about your first job. You say:
A "I use to work in finance, but now I work with the network and with the partners."
B "I used to work in finance."
C "I was used to work in finance."
You want to ask Sarah about her past. You say:
A "Did you use to travel a lot?"
B "Did you used to travel a lot when you were a young consultant in Boston?"
C "You used to traveling a lot?"
A partner asks if the waiting time is still long. You say:
A "Nowadays, the waiting time used to be shorter than in the past, when everything was on paper."
B "Nowadays, the waiting time use to be shorter."
C "No. Patients used to wait weeks. Nowadays, they wait a few days."
A new colleague asks what you miss from the old days. You say:
A "The small team. We used to have lunch together every day."
B "The small team. We are used to have lunch together every day, and we were talking about the budgets."
C "The small team. We use to have lunch."
