One Question

Who Actually Did It?

Two sentences, out loud, right now.

One about something you did yourself last week. One about something you arranged and somebody else did. In English those two sentences are built differently, and using the first shape for the second is how a director ends up sounding like a technician.

Chapter 4: The Code

Who Did It, and Who Arranged It

One structure for work that is not yours

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"Last month we audited the second plant ourselves."

"Last month we had the second plant audited."

"I review the freight reports every Friday."

"I get the freight reports reviewed every Friday."

Two of these say the work came off your own hands. Two of them do not, and in those two something has moved into the blue position, in the middle.

JobFormExample
You did the worksubject + verb + objectWe audited the plant.
Somebody else did it, you arranged ithave + thing + past participleWe had the plant audited.
Same, more informalget + thing + past participleI get the reports reviewed every Friday.
Naming who did it... + by + personWe had the plant audited by an external firm.
A plan, not a factneed to have / want to get + thing + participleI need to have the contracts reviewed.
Something bad that happened to youhave / get + thing + participleWe had a shipment held at customs.
The trapthe thing comes BEFORE the participleNot I had audited the plant by them. Say I had the plant audited.

In one line: have or get, then the thing, then the past participle — and the person who did it is optional.

Real Document

The Sheet That Passes Between Shifts

SHIFT HANDOVER SHEET
plant 2 · passes from the outgoing to the incoming shift leader
Outgoing shiftA (06:00-14:00)
Line statusLine 1 running · Line 2 up and running since 11:20
Open itemBatch 4471 held — artwork not signed off
OwnerShift leader, incoming
Signed off byQuality, on the previous batch only
EscalatedYes — to plant manager, 12:40
Turnaround expectedBefore 18:00
Batch 4471 is waiting on client artwork and is not a production problem. The item was escalated at 12:40 and the plant manager has had the client contacted directly. Accountability for the batch stays with the incoming shift leader until it ships. The annual audit has been moved to the week of the 22nd; compliance paperwork is being prepared by the external firm and does not need anybody on the floor.
1. The sheet names an Owner and a person who signed off, and they are different. Why would a plant keep those separate?
Because signing off is an approval of one piece of work, while the owner answers for the result until it ships. One is a task, the other is accountability.
2. One line describes something that was arranged rather than done by the writer. Which is it?
"the plant manager has had the client contacted directly" — the manager did not make the call, he arranged for it to be made.
3. Why does the note say the audit does not need anybody on the floor?
Because the compliance paperwork is being prepared by the external firm, so the work is outsourced and does not take production time.
Listening 1 · The Plant Director

The First Thing I Stopped Doing

A plant director on what she handed over first, and what it cost. Sound first — no text.

1. What did she stop doing first, and why does she think that is normal?
Scheduling the whole floor, which was the thing she was best at. She says that is usually how it goes.
2. What happened in the first month, and why did she not take the task back?
It went worse, not disastrously but worse. She says if she had taken it back at that point she would have taken it back permanently.
3. What is her involvement now, and what does she say the hard part really is?
She has the schedule sent to her on Friday, reads it, and asks a question about once a month. The hard part is the six weeks after, when you can still do it faster yourself.
Listening 2 · The Consultant

The Decision That Comes Back Up

An operations consultant on why delegation quietly stops working. Sound first — no text.

1. Who calls him in, and what is almost always the diagnosis?
Companies that have grown while the person at the top still works sixty hours. The diagnosis is not that they have not delegated, but that decisions keep coming back up disguised as questions.
2. Why does he say the exchange at the door does not look like a failure?
Because it takes ninety seconds and it feels helpful. But the deciding was the job, and it has just been handed back.
3. What does he ask a director to do for two weeks, and what does it reveal?
Not to stop answering, but to answer with a question: what would you do. Nine times out of ten the person already knows, and the tenth time is a genuine escalation.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I can say that work was done without saying that I did it myself.
I put the thing before the past participle: have the plant audited, not had audited the plant.
I never say I made them to do it. I say I had it done, or I got them to do it.
I can explain the difference between handing over a task and handing over the accountability.
I know the words: to delegate, to sign off, an audit, compliance, a handover, to escalate.
