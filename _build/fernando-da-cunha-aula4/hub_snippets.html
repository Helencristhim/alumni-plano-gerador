Getting Things Done -- Work That Leaves Your Desk

You run five companies with two plants, and almost nothing on your list is work you do with your own hands. Key words: to delegate, to sign something off, an audit, compliance, to outsource, turnaround, a workaround, accountability, to escalate, a handover, to have a lot on your plate, to keep somebody in the loop. Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To delegate -- to give somebody else a task and the authority that goes with it
"I delegate the scheduling, but I do not delegate the client call."
To sign something off -- to approve something formally so that it can go ahead
"Nothing leaves the plant until the shift leader signs it off."
An audit -- an official examination of a process or a set of records by somebody independent
"We have the second plant audited once a year."
Compliance -- the state of following the rules a regulator or a client sets
"Compliance is not a department here. It is a line on every handover sheet."
To outsource -- to pay another company to do work you used to do yourself
"We outsource the freight and we keep the printing in-house."
Turnaround -- the time it takes to complete something and send it back
"The turnaround on an approval should be a day, not a week."
A workaround -- a way of getting past a problem without actually solving it
"Every workaround on the floor is a process somebody has quietly given up on."
Accountability -- being the person who answers for a result, whether or not you did the work
"You can delegate the task. You cannot delegate the accountability."
To escalate -- to move a problem up to somebody with more authority
"If it is still open after two days, escalate it. Do not sit on it."
A handover -- the moment work passes from one person, shift or team to another
"Most of what goes wrong here goes wrong at a handover."
To have a lot on your plate -- expression: to have a large amount of work to deal with at once
"She has a lot on her plate this month, so I moved the audit."
To keep somebody in the loop -- expression: to keep somebody informed as things develop
"Do it your way, but keep me in the loop."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A director walks through a plant on a Tuesday and almost nothing he sees is work he did. The line was set up by a supervisor. The batch was signed off by quality. The annual audit was carried out by an external firm, and the freight was outsourced years ago. If he described the week in English the way he lived it, he would say I audited the plant — and he would be describing a job he did not do.

English has a structure built for exactly this. We had the plant audited. The thing comes first, the past participle comes after it, and the person who actually did the work is optional: we had the plant audited by an external firm. Get does the same job in a slightly more informal register: I get the freight reports reviewed every Friday. Both mean the same thing, and neither means that he picked up a clipboard.

The same shape covers something that simply happened to you: we had a shipment held at customs for nine days. Nobody arranged that. One warning for a Portuguese speaker: the verb you would reach for translates as I made them to do it, and English does not allow that. Either I had it done, with the thing in the middle, or I got them to do it, with a person and a full infinitive. And note what the structure does not solve: accountability stays with you. You can delegate the task, agree what should be escalated, fix the turnaround, and still be the person who answers for the result.

1. Why is "We had the plant audited" better than "We audited the plant" in this text?
A Because "audit" cannot take a normal object.

C Because the audit happened in the past.
2. Where does the thing go in the causative structure?
A Between have or get and the past participle.
B At the end, after the participle.
C Before have or get.
3. The text says accountability stays with the director. What does that mean here?
A That he has to do the work himself after all.
B That the task cannot be given to anybody else.
C That he still answers for the result, even though somebody else did the work.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Causative -- Have and Get Something Done

GRAMMAR

The structure for work that is not yours: the thing first, the past participle after it.

JobFormExample
You did the worksubject + verb + objectWe audited the plant.
Somebody else did it, you arranged ithave + thing + past participleWe had the plant audited.
Same, more informalget + thing + past participleI get the reports reviewed every Friday.
Naming who did it... + by + personWe had the plant audited by an external firm.
A plan, not a factneed to have / want to get + thing + participleI need to have the contracts reviewed.
Something bad that happened to youhave / get + thing + participleWe had a shipment held at customs.
The trapthe thing comes BEFORE the participleNot I had audited the plant by them. Say I had the plant audited.

Never: I made them to review the contracts · I had audited the plant by them · we get the reports review every Friday · I had the schedule made by myself · I had done the plant audit.

Stage 2: Put the Handover in Order

Order

You are handing a process to a manager for the first time. Put what you say in a logical order.

?"From this month, the annual audit is yours."
?"We have it audited by an external firm, so your job is to run the week around them."
?"You own the result, not just the paperwork."
?"Anything you can decide yourself, decide it. Do not bring it to me."
?"If it is still open after two days, escalate it."
?"Do it your way, but keep me in the loop."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to say an external firm audited your plant. You say:
A "I audited the plant by an external firm."
B "We had the plant audited by an external firm."
C "I made an external firm to audit the plant."
You want to say somebody reviews your reports every week, arranged by you. You say:
A "I get the reports reviewed every Friday."
B "I get the reports review every Friday."
C "I am reviewed the reports every Friday."
A shipment was held at customs and nobody arranged it. You say:
A "Customs made us to hold the shipment."
B "We were held a shipment at customs."
C "We had a shipment held at customs for nine days."
A manager asks what he is responsible for. The strongest answer is:
A "You do the task and I answer for it."
B "You own the result, not just the doing. Escalate only what you genuinely cannot decide."
C "Everything comes to me first, and then I decide."
