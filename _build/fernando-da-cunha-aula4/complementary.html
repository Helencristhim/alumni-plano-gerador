Podcast
How I Built This -- Slack and Flickr: Stewart Butterfield

A founder who twice had to hand the technical work to other people and stay in charge of the result. The delegation story runs under the whole interview, and he is unusually honest about the months where it went worse before it went better.

Tip: listen to the first fifteen minutes. Write down two sentences where he describes work that other people did and he arranged.

Talk
TED -- Amy Edmondson, "How to turn a group of strangers into a team" (13 min)

A Harvard researcher on what makes a team able to decide without checking upwards every time. It is the other half of this lesson: the causative gives you the sentence, and this gives you the reason people will actually own the result.

Tip: thirteen minutes, very clear delivery. Try it without subtitles, then say in one sentence what you would have to stop doing for your own team to decide more.

Video
"Think Fast, Talk Smart: Communication Techniques" -- Stanford Graduate School of Business

A lecture on speaking under pressure without preparing every sentence first. Relevant here because handing work over is mostly a spoken act: you say what somebody owns, once, and either it is clear or it comes back to you.

Tip: it is long. Watch the first twenty minutes only, and note the one technique you could use in your next handover.
