Lesson 30 -- Pre-class

Review & Simulation -- Investment Committee

Tuesday, ten o'clock: Eduarda presents the Lane deal to the investment committee, and David Moore, the Chief Risk Officer, grills her on the risks. Key words: deck, base case, grill, conviction, dissent, unanimous, abstain, conditional approval. Review of lessons 26-29: relative clauses, indirect questions, legal modals and cleft sentences.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Deck -- a set of slides for a presentation
"The committee read the deck the night before."
Base case -- the most likely scenario in a financial forecast
"In our base case, Lane closes at a hundred and five million."
Grill -- to ask someone a lot of difficult questions
"David likes to grill presenters on the risks."
Conviction -- a strong belief that something is right
"Eduarda spoke with conviction about the deal."
Dissent -- disagreement with the opinion of the group
"Eduarda listened to the dissent before she answered."
Unanimous -- with every member in agreement
"The vote was not unanimous: four in favor, one abstained."
Abstain -- to choose not to vote
"Paul has a conflict, so he will abstain."
Conditional approval -- a yes that depends on a condition being met
"The committee gave a conditional approval."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Tuesday, Eduarda presents the Lane deck to the investment committee. Her base case is a price that reflects nine and a half times Lane's profit, because NorthPack is a buyer that needs Lane's factories.

Then David Moore starts to grill her. He asks: "Could you tell me how the supplier case is covered?" Eduarda explains that NorthPack is entitled to take any loss from the escrow. David is not sure about the cap, but she listens to his dissent and answers with conviction: "It's the escrow that protects us."

Paul has a conflict, so he decides to abstain. The vote is not unanimous, but what the committee gives is a conditional approval.

1. Why will NorthPack pay a high price?
A Because the committee asked for a unanimous vote before Tuesday's meeting with the partners.
B Because it needs Lane's factories.
C Because the cap is low.
2. Which question is correct?
A "Could you tell me how the supplier case is covered?"
B "Could you tell me how is the supplier case covered by the escrow and by the cap?"
C "Could you tell me how covered the case?"
3. What is the final result?
A A unanimous no.
B A new meeting with NorthPack and the lawyers next month to discuss the cap again.
C A conditional approval.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Review: Four Tools from Lessons 26-29

GRAMMAR

No new grammar in this lesson. In a committee, you need the four tools of this block, each one at the right moment.

ToolFormExample
Define (L26)noun + that / who / whose + clauseNorthPack is a buyer that needs Lane's factories.
Ask politely (L27)Could you tell me / Do you know + wh- or if + subject + verbCould you tell me what your base case is?
The contract (L28)is entitled to / is required to / may not + base verbNorthPack is entitled to take the loss from the escrow.
Spotlight (L29)What ... is ... / It's ... that ...It's the escrow that protects us.

Never: the owner which wants · Could you tell me what is your base case? · is entitled take · What protects us it is the escrow

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose what a confident advisor would say.

The chair asks for your base case. You say:
A "Our base case is a price that reflects nine and a half times Lane's profit."
B "Our base case is a price who reflects the profit of Lane, and also the comparables from the last three years."
C "Base case, good price."
You don't understand a member's doubt. You ask:
A "Why?"
B "Could you tell me which risk worries you most?"
C "Could you tell me which risk does worry you most in this deal, and why do you think so?"
A member says: "Fifteen percent is too high." You answer:
A "You are wrong."
B "What protects us it is the escrow, not the cap, because our exposure is only two million dollars."
C "I understand the dissent, but it's the escrow that protects us."
The vote won't be unanimous. You say:
A "Could we agree on a conditional approval?"
B "OK, let's forget it."
C "We could to ask for a conditional approval, provided that the escrow stays at twelve months."
