Chapter 4 -- Getting There

Tuesday, Ten O’Clock

One deck, five members, one vote

Dialogue

Eduarda and David

D
Thank you, Eduarda. I read the deck last night. Could you tell me what your base case is?
E
Our base case is a price of a hundred and five million. That's about nine and a half times Lane's yearly profit.
D
That's well above NorthPack's first offer. I'd like to know why they would pay that much.
E
Because NorthPack is a buyer that needs Lane's factories. What they're paying for is capacity they can't build in three years.
D
Fine. Now let me grill you on the risks. Is the supplier case covered?
E
Yes. If Lane loses the case, NorthPack is entitled to take the loss from the escrow.
D
I'm not sure I agree with the cap. Some of us may vote against fifteen percent.
E
I understand the dissent. But it's the escrow that protects us, not the cap. Our exposure is only two million.
D
Last question. How much conviction do you have in this deal?
E
A lot. And if the vote can't be unanimous, I'd ask for a conditional approval: yes, provided that the escrow stays at twelve months.
Comprehension

About David

1. When did David read the deck?
Last night.
2. Which risk does David ask about?
The supplier case.
3. What is David not sure about?

Real Document

The Decision Record

From: Eduarda Gabriel, Houlihan Lokey
To: Richard Hale; James Carter
Re: Project Lane -- Investment Committee Decision

Richard, James,

Decision: conditional approval.

Vote: 4 in favor, 0 against, 1 abstained (Paul Grant -- conflict of interest). Not unanimous, but no dissent in the final vote.

Condition: the escrow stays at 12 months. The 15% cap is accepted.

Next step: I send the final terms to NorthPack by Friday.

Best,
Eduarda

1. Was the vote unanimous?
No. Four members were in favor and one abstained.
2. What is the condition?
The escrow stays at twelve months.
3. What will Eduarda do next?
She will send the final terms to NorthPack by Friday.
