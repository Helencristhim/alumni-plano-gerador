Comprehension

About Wes

1. What surprised Wes most about the quiet, and when did it happen?
He had been told the quiet would be the easy part, and by the third week he was craving noise instead.
2. How does Wes explain why the silence felt eerie rather than restful?
His ears had spent thirty years filtering a city, and suddenly there was nothing left to filter.
3. What is Wes still not used to, and what was he actually homesick for?
He is still not used to driving forty minutes to a pharmacy, and he was homesick for knowing how things worked, not for the city itself.

Chapter 4: The Code

One Suffix, Two Different Tenses

used to · be used to · get used to

Grammar Discovery

Three Of These Are Not Last Week’s Rule

"I used to live in a flat with traffic under the window."

"I am used to the silence now, but it took a whole winter."

"It took me about six months to get used to driving everywhere."

"I am still not used to planning a week ahead just to buy rice."

The first one is last week’s rule and it is finished business. In the other three, look at what comes immediately after the word to. It is never a bare verb. Say what it is — and then say whether those three describe the past or now.

FormWhat it doesExample
used to + verbA habit or state that is OVER. Last week’s rule.I used to live in the city.
be used to + -ing / nounYou are ALREADY familiar with it, now.I am used to the silence.
get used to + -ing / nounThe PROCESS of becoming familiar. Takes time.I got used to driving everywhere.
why -ing and not the verbHere to is a preposition, and a preposition takes -ing.I am used to driving. (never: to drive)
negativebe / get take a normal negative, and often yet.I am not used to the dark yet.
any tense you likeUnlike used to, be / get used to move freely in time.You will get used to it.
the test that always worksSwap in accustomed to. If it fits, you need be / get used to.I am accustomed to the quiet.

In one line: used to looks back and stops; be used to and get used to look at now and keep going.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I am used to drive on unpaved roads."
"I am used to driving on unpaved roads."
"I am getting used to wake up at five."
"I am getting used to waking up at five."
"I used to the silence now."
"I am used to the silence now."
"It took me six months to used to it."
"It took me six months to get used to it."

The first two are the same mistake: after used to here, to is a preposition, so the verb takes -ing. The third drops the verb be and turns a present state into last week’s past. The fourth forgets that the PROCESS needs get — without it there is no verb in the sentence at all.

Listening 2

Month Three Is the One That Gets You

An American on why the third month is the hardest. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What are weeks one to four like, according to him?
Everything is a holiday. You photograph the bakery and you tell people the light is different.
2. Why exactly does month three collapse?
The novelty has worn off and the competence has not arrived yet. You are no longer a tourist and not a local either, and there is nothing to be in between.
3. What is his rule, and what changes around the sixth month?
No decision about leaving before month seven, not six. Around the sixth month you stop translating the small things and stop rehearsing sentences before going into the post office.

"I was not used to the silence at all — by week three I was craving noise." Was not used to, because we are describing a state in the past that has since changed.
2. Say the Month 6 line using get used to, and explain why get and not be.
"By month six I had got used to going into the shop without rehearsing the sentence first." Get, because the line records a CHANGE happening, not a state that already existed.

"I am used to the quiet now." / "The quiet became normal at about a year." Both are correct; only the first says anything about how she is today.
