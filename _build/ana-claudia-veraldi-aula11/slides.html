Three sentences that look almost identical: I used to drive, I am used to driving, I am getting used to driving. One is finished, one is finished becoming normal, and one is still happening. Nobody ever explains the difference, so most people pick one and use it for all three.

Chapter 3: The Code

The Same Two Words, Four Times

used to · be used to · get used to — and what follows each one

Grammar Discovery

What Comes After?

"I used to live within reach of everything."

"I am used to driving twenty minutes for bread."

"I am getting used to the silence at night."

"I still cannot get used to everything closing at six."

The orange part is almost the same three times. Look at what comes immediately after it: in the first sentence, a bare verb. In the other three, an -ing form or a noun, never a bare verb. That single difference is the whole rule.

FormWhat it meansExample
used to + verbA habit that is over. Last week’s lesson.I used to live in the city.
be used to + -ingIt is normal to me now. The adjusting is finished.I am used to driving everywhere.
be used to + nounSame thing, with a thing instead of an action.I am used to the silence.
get used toThe process, not the result. It can still be happening.I am getting used to the silence.
get moves freelyAny tense, because it is a normal verb.I got used to it · I will get used to it · I cannot get used to it.
The trapAfter be or get used to, never a bare verb.Not I am used to drive. Say driving.

In one line: no be and no get means the past. With be or get, put -ing after it.

What Still Feels Wrong?

R
And now?
A
Now it has grown on me. I am used to hearing nothing after eight, and when I go back to the city I cannot sleep.
R
That is exactly what happened to me in reverse. I grew up in Mumbai, so I am used to noise the way you are used to silence. My first month here I genuinely thought something was wrong with the building.
A

Then we are the same person. It takes some getting used to, and then one day you are the one who finds the other place strange.
Comprehension

About Ravi

1. Where did Ravi grow up, and what did he think in his first month?
In Mumbai. His first month there he genuinely thought something was wrong with the building, because it was so quiet.
2. What was the hardest thing for him — the culture shock or the language?
Neither. It was that everything closes at six, and he says he was completely thrown by that.
3. Three years later, is he used to it? What does his wife say?
Not yet. He says he is still getting used to it, and his wife says he never will.

Four Time Zones, One Day

0:000:00

Not the hours — letting a message sit. It took him a year, and he says it still takes effort.

You get used to hearing five different Englishes in one meeting and stop noticing whose is whose. She said she could not follow the calls at all, and he could not remember what that felt like.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I keep used to plus the bare verb for a habit that is over.
I say I am used to driving, never I am used to drive.
I use get used to for the process, in any tense: I got used to it, I am getting used to it, I cannot get used to it.
I can sort my own life into normal now, in progress, and still strange, and pick the right form for each.
I know the words: jarring, unsettling, bearable, culture shock, to ease into something, to grow on you, to find your feet.
