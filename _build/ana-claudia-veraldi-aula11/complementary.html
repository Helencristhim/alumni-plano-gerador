Lesson 11 -- Getting Used to the Quiet

Series
Somebody Feed Phil -- Season 7, Episode 1: Mumbai

An American in his sixties spends a week in a city of twenty million people, and the whole episode is him adjusting in real time: the traffic, the heat, the food, the volume. Exactly the movement of tonight’s lesson, in the opposite direction from yours.

Tip: two accents in one episode, American and Indian, the same pair you worked on tonight. Write down three sentences where somebody says what they are or are not used to.

Podcast
Hidden Brain -- You 2.0: Where Happiness Hides

Psychologist Sonja Lyubomirsky on hedonic adaptation: the reason human beings get used to almost anything, good or bad, faster than they expect. It is the science underneath the sentence you practised all evening.

Tip: the host is American with Indian roots and speaks slowly and precisely. Count how many times somebody says get used to, and notice what follows it every single time.

Listen on Spotify ↗
Talk
The Art of Stillness -- Pico Iyer, TED (15 minutes)

A travel writer who has been everywhere argues that the place worth going to is nowhere. The best defence in English of the thing you chose, delivered by somebody who took years to stop finding it strange.

Tip: slow, deliberate British-Indian English, and every word is audible. Watch once with no subtitles, then say the main idea back in three sentences of your own.
