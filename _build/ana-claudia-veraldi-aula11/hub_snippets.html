Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To adjust -- to slowly change your habits so that a new situation feels normal
"It took me a whole year to adjust to the silence."
Culture shock -- the confused feeling you get when everything around you works differently
"Two hundred kilometers, and it was still culture shock."
Overwhelming -- so strong or so much that you cannot deal with it easily
"The first month was overwhelming, and I told nobody."
Eerie -- strange and slightly frightening, in a very quiet way
"The first winter out here was genuinely eerie."
Homesick -- sad because you are far from the place you think of as home
"I still get homesick on Sunday afternoons."
To crave -- to want something so badly that the feeling is almost physical
"After three days in the city I crave the quiet."
Solitude -- time alone that you choose and enjoy
"It stopped being emptiness and became solitude."
Self-sufficient -- able to provide what you need without depending on other people
"Out here you have to be far more self-sufficient."
To make do -- to manage with what you have, because nothing better is available
"The store was shut, so we made do with rice."
To grow on you -- to become something you like, slowly, after not liking it at first
"I hated that color. It grew on me."
A trade-off -- something good you give up in order to get something else
"No hospital nearby. That is the trade-off."
Pitch-black -- completely dark, with no light at all
"By seven in the evening it is pitch-black."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

The first month out here was overwhelming, and Ana told nobody. She was not used to the silence. In the city she had been used to falling asleep with traffic outside the window, and suddenly there was nothing at all, which was eerie rather than peaceful. She got homesick on Sunday afternoons and she craved noise, any noise. Two hundred kilometers from Butantan, and it was still culture shock: one market a week, no delivery, and the road pitch-black by seven. She had to learn to be self-sufficient and to make do with whatever was in the house. That was the trade-off, and she knew it. Somewhere in the second year she stopped noticing. Nowadays she is used to living with the quiet, and the emptiness has turned into solitude. She is still getting used to driving those roads at night, and she probably always will be. Everything else grew on her, exactly as people said it would.

1. Which sentence says that the quiet is completely normal for her today?
A "She was not used to the silence."
B "She is used to living with the quiet."
C "She is still getting used to driving those roads."
2. Which sentence describes a process that has NOT finished?
A "The emptiness has turned into solitude."
B "Everything else grew on her."
C "She is still getting used to driving those roads at night."
3. According to the text, what made the first month so hard?
A The silence, after years of falling asleep with traffic outside.
B The distance from Butantan, which was more than she expected.
C The neighbors, who were not friendly to her at first.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Be Used To and Get Used To

GRAMMAR

Three words, three meanings -- and the one letter that decides which one you said.

FormWhat it meansExample
used to + verbA past habit that has stopped. Lesson 10.I used to live in the city.
be used to + -ing / nounIt is already normal for you. The process is over.I am used to living here.
get used to + -ing / nounThe process itself. Still happening.I am getting used to the dark.
The trapAfter be/get used to, a verb takes -ing, never the base form.I am used to drive → driving
NegativeOnly be or get changes. Used to never moves.I was not used to it at all.
QuestionSame rule, in any tense.Are you used to the quiet yet?
Never "with"It is always to, never with.used with the silence → used to
In one lineNo am and no -ing = the past. With am and -ing = now.
