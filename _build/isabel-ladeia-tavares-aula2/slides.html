Same Fact, Different Page

Chapter 3: The Code

The Sentence With Nobody In It

The English of the method box, and why it hides the person

Grammar Discovery

What Is Missing From All Four?

"The samples were collected in March."

"Two hundred seedlings were assessed at baseline."

"Contamination was ruled out in the second trial."

"The effect has been observed in three independent trials."  still true now

In every one of them, the thing that had something done to it is standing at the front, where the person who did it would normally be. The person is gone, and the paper does not want them back.

UseFormExample
Finished action, one thingwas + third form of the verbThe sample was collected.
Finished action, many thingswere + third formThe samples were collected.
Still relevant nowhas / have been + third formThe effect has been observed three times.
When the person matters+ by + the person or the thingGrowth was assessed by two researchers.
Negativewas / were not + third formThe plants were not watered for six days.
The trapThe verb be never disappearsNot The samples collected in March.

In one line: put the thing first, add was or were, and use the third form of the verb. Add by only when who did it is part of the point.

Listening 1 · A Researcher

The Mistake Every First-Year Makes

Somebody who reads thirty abstracts a week, on how she used to read one paper a day. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. How did she use to read a paper in her first year, and what did it cost her?
From word one to word ten thousand, in order, with a dictionary open. It took an entire afternoon, and by the end she could not have said what the study actually found.
2. What are the three steps in the order she was given?
Read the last sentence of the background, then read the results, then stop. If those two do not interest you, close the tab.
3. Why does she go back to the method, and what does she say about her ratio of abstracts to papers?
Because the method is where you find out whether you believe the results at all. She opens about thirty abstracts a week and reads maybe two papers properly, and she says that ratio is the job, not laziness.
Listening 2 · A Journal Club

Three Rules Before We Start

Your first journal club, and somebody is explaining how the session runs. Write down the three rules. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What are you expected to have read before the session, and what are you not?
At least the abstract and the figures. You are not expected to have read the entire discussion.
2. What is the rule about the order of the discussion, and what reason does he give?
They talk about the method before the results, because you cannot judge a finding until you know how it was measured.
3. What does he say about admitting that you did not understand something?
Anybody may say it. Nobody has read everything, and the person who admits that the baseline was not clear is usually the one who has spotted a real problem with the paper.
Real Document

An Abstract, Box by Box

Seasonal temperature and germination in a shaded understory
Journal of Plant Ecology · peer-reviewed · Vol. 18, No. 3
BACKGROUND
Germination in shaded understory species is known to depend on soil temperature. It is not known how far this holds in the first weeks after the rains begin. This study assesses that period.
METHODS
Samples were collected at four sites in March. Two hundred seedlings were assessed at baseline and again at six weeks. The data were analyzed in three independent trials. Contamination was ruled out by repeating the procedure with fresh material.
RESULTS
Germination fell by two percent below ten degrees. The difference was small and significant, and it appeared at all four sites. Temperature accounted for most of the variation between sites.
CONCLUSIONS
The authors conclude that the evidence suggests a seasonal threshold rather than a gradual effect. A single season is a limitation, and the finding should be tested over a longer period.
1. In which box is the aim of the study, and what is it?
In the last sentence of the background: to assess how far the temperature effect holds in the first weeks after the rains begin.
2. The result was two percent. Why do the authors still call it significant?
Because significant does not mean large. It means unlikely to be chance, and here the same small difference appeared at all four sites.
3. Find the two most careful words in the conclusion, and say what the authors are avoiding.
Suggests and should be tested. They are avoiding the claim that the study has proved a seasonal threshold, and they name the single season as a limitation.
