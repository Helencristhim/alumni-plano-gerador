Lesson 6 -- The Wall Text

Documentary
Conservation: Behind the Scenes at the National Gallery of Art

The conservators of the museum you can walk to open their laboratory: what happens to a canvas that is restored, what an X-ray shows under the paint, and why a brushstroke tells them who touched the picture last.

Tip: American English, spoken slowly by people who explain for a living. Write down every sentence you hear with who, which or where.

Podcast
The Lonely Palette -- Episode 54: Grant Wood’s "American Gothic" (1930)

Thirty-one minutes on one single painting: a portrait everybody in this country recognizes, an era, and a story that the label on the wall never has room for. The host also stops real visitors in the room and asks them what they see.

Tip: this is the whole lesson in podcast form -- one work at a time. Listen once without stopping, then again with the painting open in another window.

Talk
Hopper’s Nighthawks: Look Through the Window -- Nerdwriter1

Six minutes that teach you how to actually look at a painting: where the eye goes first, what the light is doing, and why one striking detail holds the whole canvas together. Exactly what a wall text never tells you.

Tip: watch once with no subtitles and say the main idea out loud in three sentences. Then watch again and copy the way he describes what he sees.
