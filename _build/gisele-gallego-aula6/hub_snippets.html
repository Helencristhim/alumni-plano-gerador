Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

The Wall Text -- The Little Paragraph Next to the Painting

The words a museum uses on every label, and the one structure that holds them together. Key words: a canvas, an exhibit, on loan, a portrait, a landscape, a brushstroke, to depict, a commission, to be restored, an era, striking, muted. Structure: relative clauses with who, which, that and where.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A canvas -- the strong cloth, stretched over a frame, that a painting is made on
"The canvas is much smaller than it looks in photographs."
An exhibit -- a group of works shown together in a museum for a period of time
"The exhibit on the third floor closes on Sunday."
On loan -- lent by another museum or owner, and going back to them later
"This painting is on loan from a museum in Baltimore."
A portrait -- a picture of a person, usually the face and the shoulders
"The portrait on the left is the artist's daughter."
A landscape -- a picture of open country: fields, hills, sky or water
"She painted the same landscape in every season."
A brushstroke -- one movement of the brush that you can still see on the surface
"Stand close and you can count every brushstroke."
To depict -- to show a person, a place or a scene in a picture
"The painting depicts two sisters at a kitchen window."
A commission -- a work that somebody ordered and paid an artist to make
"It was a commission from a family outside Washington."
To be restored -- to be cleaned and repaired so that it looks like the original again
"The painting was restored in 1998, and the work took eleven months."
An era -- a long period of time with its own style or way of life
"This room covers one era, from 1870 to 1910."
Striking -- so strong or unusual that you stop walking and look
"The orange of the cup is striking in a very quiet picture."
Muted -- soft and low in color; the opposite of bright and loud
"The colors are muted: grey, sand and a faded green."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The artist who painted The Sisters at the Window was born in Baltimore in 1861. The canvas which hangs in room 4 depicts two young women at a kitchen window, and the window, which takes up almost half of the picture, looks out on a landscape of flat fields.

It was a commission. The family who paid for it owned a mill outside Washington, and they expected a formal portrait. The room where the two sisters are sitting is almost empty, and nothing in it has been arranged for the viewer. The mill owner never hung the picture.

The colors belong to the muted era of her later work, and the only striking thing is the orange cup that everybody notices first. When the painting was restored in 1998, the restorers found a woman who is standing in the doorway behind the sisters. The museum which owns the canvas is in Baltimore, so here it is only on loan.

1. "The artist who painted this was born in Baltimore." Why who and not which?
A Because who is the formal word and which is the informal one.
B Because an artist is a person, and people take who.
C Because the sentence is in the past.
2. "The window, which takes up half of the picture, looks out on a landscape." Why the commas?
A Because the information is extra: take it out and the sentence still works.
B Because which always takes commas and who never does.
C Because the sentence has more than ten words.
3. "The room where the two sisters are sitting is almost empty." What is missing at the end of where the two sisters are sitting?
A The word in it, which English needs after where.
B A second verb, because where always takes two verbs.
C Nothing. Where already carries the place, so nothing is repeated after it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Joining Two Facts in One Sentence

GRAMMAR

One word in the middle, and nothing repeated after it.

WordComes afterExample
whoa personthe artist who painted this canvas
whicha thingthe canvas which was restored in 1998
thata person or a thing, when the information is essentialthe cup that everybody notices first
wherea placethe room where the sisters are sitting
with commasextra information: the sentence works without itThe window, which takes up half of the picture, looks out on a landscape.
no commasessential information: it says WHICH one you meanThe cup that everybody notices first is orange.

Never: The artist which painted this · The canvas who was restored · The room where the sisters are sitting in it · The woman that she is standing in the doorway · The window, that takes up half of the picture, looks out.

Stage 2: Put the Story in Order

Order

This is the story of one painting, from the day it was ordered to today. Put it in order.

?"In 1889 a family who owned a mill asked Clara Whitfield to paint their two daughters."
?"She painted the sisters in the room where they spent most of that winter."
?"The family who paid for it never hung the picture, and it stayed in a corridor for forty years."
?"In 1998 the canvas was restored, and the work took eleven months."
?"Under the surface the restorers found a woman who had been painted over."
?"Today it is here on loan, until it goes home to Baltimore in March."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

You want to know if the museum owns the painting or borrowed it. You ask:
A "Is this painting on loan, or the museum owns it?"
B "Is this painting on loan, or does the museum own it?"
C "This painting is loan or own?"
A visitor asks you about the artist. You answer:
A "The artist which painted this was born in Baltimore."
B "The artist who painted this was born in Baltimore."
C "The artist who he painted this was born in Baltimore."
You want to point out the room, not the picture. You say:
A "The room where the sisters are sitting is almost empty."
B "The room where the sisters are sitting in it is almost empty."
C "The room which the sisters are sitting there is almost empty."
You are describing the colors of a very quiet picture with one small orange cup. You say:
A "The colors are striking, but the cup is muted."
B "The colors are muted, but the cup on the table is striking."
C "The colors are muted, and the cup on the table is muted too."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Describe one picture, one building or one street that you have really seen, in one minute. Say what it depicts, what the colors are like, and one fact about the person or the place behind it -- and try to put two facts inside one sentence, with who, which, that or where.
