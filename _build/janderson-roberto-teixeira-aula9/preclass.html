Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

Asking Without Sounding Rude -- Requests at a Hotel and at Work

How to ask for something in English without sounding like you are giving an order, and why please is not the word that does the work. Key expressions: a favor, to sort out, in charge of, spare, to be available, to lend, to borrow, to let you know, a hand, urgent. Structure: can, could and be able to.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. These ten stay here in your material -- they do not disappear after the class.

A favor -- something helpful somebody does for you when you ask
"Could I ask you a favor before you leave?"
To sort out -- to solve a problem or organize something that is not working
"Reception said they would sort it out before six."
In charge of -- responsible for an area, a team or a decision
"Who is in charge of the booking system here?"
Spare -- extra, and kept in case somebody needs it
"Do you have a spare key for the meeting room?"
To be available -- free, and possible to use or to speak to
"She is not available until Thursday afternoon."
To lend -- to give something to somebody for a short time
"Could you lend me a charger until lunchtime?"
To borrow -- to take something for a short time and give it back
"Can I borrow your pen for one second?"
To let you know -- to tell somebody something later, when you have the answer
"I will let you know as soon as I hear anything."
A hand -- practical help with something
"Could you give me a hand with these files?"
Urgent -- needing to be dealt with immediately
"It is not urgent, so tomorrow morning is fine."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Janderson arrived at the hotel at half past ten at night with a call at seven in the morning and a room with no desk. He went back down and asked whether he could change it. He did not say give me another room, which is what the Portuguese sentence in his head said. He said could I ask you a favor, which is a different conversation entirely.

The woman at the desk was the person in charge of the rooms that night. She said she could sort out the problem but the other room was smaller, and she would let him know in five minutes whether it was available. He said smaller was fine. It usually is.

Then he asked for two more things, both of them small. Could she lend him a charger, because his was in a bag in another city, and could he borrow an adaptor for the morning. She had spare ones behind the desk. Neither request was urgent, and he said so, which made both of them easier to say yes to.

In the morning the connection failed anyway and somebody had to give him a hand with it, at ten to seven, in a corridor. He got onto the call on time. Nothing in that whole evening required advanced English. It required three small words used on the right step of the ladder.

1. Why does the text say "could I ask you a favor" is a different conversation?
A Because it is longer.
B Because it starts with could, which turns an order into a request.
C Because it contains the word favor.
2. "She would let him know in five minutes." What does this mean?
A She would tell him later, when she had the answer.
B She would allow him to know.
C She would ask him a question.
3. What is the difference between lend and borrow in the third paragraph?
A They mean exactly the same thing.
B She lends it to him; he borrows it from her.
C Borrow is for money and lend is for objects.
4. Why does the text say saying "it is not urgent" helped?
A Because it made both requests easier to say yes to.
B Because it showed he was in a hurry.
C Because urgent requests are always refused.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Can, Could and Be Able To

GRAMMAR

Three words, one ladder, and the polite step is could. Adding please to an order does not make it a request; changing the first word does.

FormWhat it saysExample
Can I / Can youNeutral and everyday. Not rude, just direct. Fine with colleagues."Can I borrow your pen?"
"Can you send it today?"
Could I / Could youOne step softer. The default with a client, a stranger or anybody senior."Could I have a receipt?"
"Could you give me a hand?"
Would you be able toThe softest step, and useful when the answer might be no."Would you be able to join at seven?"
could for past abilitySame word, different job: what was or was not possible in the past."I could not answer the question."
be able toFor tenses where can does not exist: future, perfect, after another verb."Will you be able to send it?"
"I have not been able to reach him."
The trapAfter can and could, the next verb never takes to. And two modal verbs never stand together.NOT "can you to send"
NOT "will you can send"

One habit worth building: after any request, add a sentence that makes refusing easy — it is not urgent, whenever you can, if you are around. In English that is not weakness. It is what makes people say yes.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You need something from somebody who does not have to give it to you. Choose the best thing to say.

You are at a hotel desk and you want a different room:
A Give me another room, please.
B Could I have a room with a desk, please?
C I want to change of room.
You want a colleague to send something today:
A Can you to send it today?
B Can you send it today?
C You can send it today?
You want to ask about tomorrow:
A Will you can send it tomorrow?
B Will you be able to send it tomorrow?
C Will you could send it tomorrow?
You want to make it easy for the other person to say no:
A I need this now.
B It is not urgent. Whenever you can is fine.
C It is very important for me.
You want practical help carrying something:
A Could you give me a hand with this?
B Could you give me an assistance with this?
C Can you make me a help?
