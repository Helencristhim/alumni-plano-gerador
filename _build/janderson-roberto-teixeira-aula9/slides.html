Lesson 9 · The Request

Asking Without Sounding Rude

You need something from somebody who does not have to give it to you. In English the difference between polite and blunt is one small word, and it is not please.

Chapter 1: The Request

The Last Thing You Had to Ask For

Something you needed from a hotel, a colleague or a stranger this month. What was it, and how did you ask?

Where You Start

One Word Between You and Rude

Blunt

"Give me a room with a desk." -- correct English, and nobody says it

Neutral

"Can I have a room with a desk?"

Polite

"Could I have a room with a desk?"

Adding please to a blunt sentence does not fix it; it makes it sound like an order with a decoration on top. What softens a request in English is the verb at the front, and the whole ladder has three steps. You already know all three words. What is missing is knowing which step to stand on.

Chapter 3: The Code

Can, Could, Be Able To

Three steps on the same ladder, and one of them is not about politeness at all

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Can I borrow your pen?"

"Could you give me a hand with these files?"

"I could not answer the question in English."

"Will you be able to send it before six?"

Two of these ask for something. One says what was impossible in the past. One asks about the future. The same little word is doing three different jobs. Which is which?

FormWhat it saysExample
Can I / Can youNeutral, everyday, completely acceptable. Not rude, just direct."Can I borrow your pen?"
"Can you send it today?"
Could I / Could youOne step softer. The default with a client, a stranger or anybody senior."Could I have a receipt?"
"Could you give me a hand?"
Could for the pastSame word, different job: ability that existed, or did not, in the past."I could not answer the question."
"When I was twenty I could run."
be able toFor the tenses where can does not exist: the future, the perfect, after another verb."Will you be able to send it?"
"I have not been able to reach him."
The answerA short answer copies the verb, exactly as in a question."Of course I can."
"I am afraid I cannot, not today."

The polite version of a request is could, not please. Please is a small extra on top of a sentence that is already polite; on a blunt one it sounds like an order with a bow on it.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"Give me a room with a desk, please."
"Could I have a room with a desk, please?"
"Can you to send it before six?"
"Can you send it before six?"
"Will you can send it tomorrow?"
"Will you be able to send it tomorrow?"

An imperative with please is still an imperative, and English hears it as an order. The polite form starts with could. After can and could the next verb has no to: can you send, never can you to send. And two of these verbs never stand together, so the future of can is will be able to. Read the correct versions aloud twice.

Chapter 4: The Front Desk

The Front Desk

Half past ten at night, a room with no desk, and a call at seven in the morning

Dialogue

A Room With a Desk

R
Good evening. Checking in? Could I have your passport, please?
J
Here you are. Could I ask you a favor? I have a call at seven and this room has no desk.
R
Let me look. I can move you to the fourth floor, but the room is smaller.
J
Smaller is fine. Are you the person in charge of the rooms, or should I ask somebody else?
R
It is me tonight. I will let you know in five minutes if it is available.
J
Thank you. And one more thing, if it is not too much. Could you lend me a charger?
R
We have spare ones behind the desk. Is it urgent, or can I bring it up with the key?
J
With the key is perfect. Could you also give me a hand with the connection?
Comprehension

About Ruth

1. What does Ruth ask for first?
His passport.
2. What does she offer to solve the desk problem?
To move him to the fourth floor, in a smaller room.
3. When does she say she will let him know?
In five minutes.
4. What does she have behind the desk?
Spare chargers.
Listening

Press Two for Reception

A recorded hotel message. Sound first -- no text.

1. What number do you press if the problem is urgent?
Zero, which rings the night manager directly.
2. Until what time is the business centre open?
Eleven at night, and it opens again at six in the morning.
3. What can you ask reception to bring to the room?
An adaptor, an extra pillow or an iron, at any hour.
Listening 2

One Small Favor

A voice message from a colleague. Sound first -- no text.

1. What is she asking him to do?
To join the call at four and answer one question about the offshore accounts.
2. Why can she not do it herself?
Because she will be on a plane and will not be able to connect.
3. What does she say if he cannot do it?
That it is not urgent enough to change his day, and she will move the call.

Chapter 6: Your Turn

Asking for Things

Role-Play

Three Requests, One Desk

Scenario: You have just arrived at a hotel and you need three things: a room with a desk, a charger, and a late checkout. Ask for all three, one at a time, and use a different step of the ladder for at least one of them.

Keywords:

Could I have Could you lend me Would it be possible to It is not urgent
Role-Play

Asking a Colleague for Something Awkward

Scenario: You need a colleague to join a call at seven in the morning, which is not her problem and not her account. Ask properly: say why, make it easy to refuse, and offer something back.

Keywords:

Could I ask you a favor if you are available let me know if it is not too much
Free Practice

Something You Actually Need to Ask

Scenario: Think of something you genuinely need from somebody this month, at work or on a trip. Ask for it out loud: explain the situation in two sentences, make the request, and make it easy to say no. Then answer two requests from her.

No keywords. The conversation is yours.

The Habit

One Ladder, Out Loud

Before the next class, take one thing you need and say it three ways out loud: the blunt version, the can version and the could version. Hear the distance between the first and the third.

You will never say the blunt version out loud on purpose. You say it by accident, when you translate from Portuguese at speed.
