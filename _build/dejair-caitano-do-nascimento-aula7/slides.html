Chapter 3: The Code

Preferring Without Saying Anybody Is Wrong

I would rather... than...

Discover the Rule

What Comes Right After Rather?

I would rather say the treatment works less well in severe cases.
I would rather ask after the talk than interrupt her.
We would rather wait for more evidence than publish this now.
I would not rather— sorry: I would rather not answer that today.
Click to reveal the rule
After would rather comes the plain verb, with no to: I would rather say, never I would rather to say. To compare two options, add than + plain verb: I'd rather ask after the talk than interrupt her. The negative is would rather not, in that order. In speech it is almost always I'd rather.

Listening 1

How to Disagree in This Room

Read the questions, then listen.

1. According to her, what is the way of disagreeing that ends a conversation?
Saying that the person is wrong. The way that keeps it alive is to point at the evidence, not at the person.
2. What are the three steps, and in which order?
Say what you agree with, then say what you would challenge, then give your reason.
3. What is her advice if you are not sure of your English?
Say less, not more. A short sentence with a clear reason is stronger than three long ones.

Discussion after the talk

I Agree With the Data, Not the Conclusion

M
We have ten minutes for discussion. Who would like to start?
K
May I raise one point? And I say this with respect, because I like this study very much.
M
Please.
K
I agree with the data. I do not agree with the conclusion. All the older patients came from one hospital, and that hospital only sees severe cases.
M
So you are saying the result may be about severity, not about age.
K
Exactly. I would rather say the treatment works less well in severe cases, and leave age out of it.
D
Can I support that with something from my own work? We see the same thing in leprosy. Our severe cases also come from reference centres, so the group is never neutral.
M
That is a useful point. What would you suggest?
D
I would suggest a second group from a general hospital. Without that, there is not enough evidence for the conclusion.
M
Thank you both. That is exactly the kind of discussion this session is for.

Listening 2

I Am Happy to Be Wrong

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What is the conclusion he challenges, and what does he agree with?
The authors conclude that the treatment does not work in older patients. He agrees with the data, not with the conclusion.
2. What is wrong with how the older group was selected?
They all came from one hospital, and that hospital only sees the most severe cases -- so the result may be about severity, not age.
3. What would he rather say, and how does he end?
That the treatment works less well in severe cases, leaving age out until there is better evidence. He ends by saying he is happy to be wrong.

Three Rounds, Less Help Each Time

Guided

Three Lines, In Order

Your partner gives you a study and a conclusion. Fill the three lines of the discussion card out loud, in order: what you agree with, what you would challenge, and one reason. Then do it again with a second study.

Semi-free

The Author Answers Back

You challenge a conclusion and the author defends it. Hold your position for three exchanges: repeat what you agree with, name the problem again in different words, and say what you would rather conclude. Stay polite and do not give up.

I say this with respectI would ratherpoint outnot enough evidenceI am happy to be wrong
Free

The Floor Is Open

The chair opens the discussion. In ninety seconds: ask for the floor, say what you agree with, name what you would challenge, give one fact as your reason, say what you would rather conclude, and end by inviting an answer. No notes.
