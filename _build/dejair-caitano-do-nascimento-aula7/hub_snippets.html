Holding the Floor -- Opinion, Agreement and Polite Disagreement

Saying what you think in a room where somebody will answer back. Key words: to suggest, to support, evidence, conclusion, to point out, to challenge, to raise a point, to agree with. Structure: would rather for preferences.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. All eight are things people do to each other's ideas in an academic discussion.

To suggest -- to give an idea for other people to consider
"I would suggest measuring pain at home, not at the hospital."
To support -- to give reasons or data that make an idea stronger
"The data supports her conclusion, but only for young patients."
Evidence -- the facts that show something is true
"There is not enough evidence for that conclusion yet."
Conclusion -- what you decide at the end, after looking at the results
"I agree with the method, but not with the conclusion."
To point out -- to make people notice something they did not see
"She pointed out that the group was too small."
To challenge -- to question an idea and ask for better reasons
"Nobody challenged the result, and that surprised me."
To raise a point -- to bring a subject into the discussion
"Can I raise one point before we finish?"
To agree with -- to have the same opinion as somebody
"I agree with you on the method, but not on the numbers."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Kemal asks for the floor. "May I raise one point? I say this with respect, because I like this study very much. I agree with the data. I do not agree with the conclusion." Then he gives his reason: all the older patients came from one hospital, and that hospital only sees severe cases. "So the result may be about severity, not about age. I would rather say the treatment works less well in severe cases, and leave age out of it until we have better evidence." Dejair supports him with his own data and suggests a second group from a general hospital. Nobody says anybody is wrong, and the discussion gets better.
Why does Kemal say "I say this with respect" before he disagrees?
A Because it is a fixed rule of English grammar
B Because it announces the disagreement and takes the threat out of it
C Because he is not sure of his English
Why is it "I would rather say" and not "I would rather to say"?
A Because say is an irregular verb
B Because after would rather the verb comes in its plain form, with no to
C Because there are two verbs in the sentence

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Would Rather

GRAMMAR

Use would rather for a preference. After it comes the plain verb, with no to. In speech it is almost always I'd rather.

preferencewould rather + plain verbI would rather say it is about severity.
two optionswould rather + verb + than + verbI'd rather ask after the talk than interrupt her.
negativewould rather not + verbI would rather not answer that today.
questionWould you rather + verbWould you rather present a poster or give a talk?
about another personwould rather + subject + past verbI'd rather you asked me after the session.

No to after rather: "I would rather to say" is wrong. And the negative is would rather not, in that order.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
You want to disagree with a conclusion in front of the author. Which opening is best?
A "That conclusion is wrong."
B "May I raise one point? I say this with respect, because I like this study very much."
C "Sorry, my English is not good, but..."
Someone asks you a question you cannot answer today. What do you say?
A "I do not know."
B "I would not rather answer that today."
C "I would rather not answer that today. I do not have enough evidence yet."
You want to add your own data to somebody else's point. What do you say?
A "Can I support that with something from my own work?"
B "I want to speak now."
C "I agree of you."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
The chair opens the discussion after a talk whose conclusion you do not fully accept. Record ninety seconds: ask for the floor, say what you agree with, name what you would challenge, give one fact as your reason, say what you would rather conclude, and end by inviting an answer. No script -- just speak.
