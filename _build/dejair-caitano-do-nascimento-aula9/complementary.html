Lesson 9 -- Numbers, Data and Results

Conference Talk
"3 ways to spot a bad statistic" -- Mona Chalabi, TED

Twelve minutes on how numbers get told badly, by somebody whose job is telling them well. Listen for how she says quantities out loud -- most of, a few, a lot of, very little -- and how rarely she says a raw number alone.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Write down five sentences where she introduces a number. Then rewrite each one with your own data, out loud.

Listening
"Are opinion polls accurate?" -- 6 Minute English, BBC Learning English

Six minutes about samples, percentages and how a small group is used to say something about a large one -- your exact problem, in ordinary English, at learner speed.

Tip: listen once with your eyes closed. Then listen again and write down every quantity word you hear: much, many, most, a few, a lot of. Sort them into countable and uncountable.

Conference Talk
"Why you should love statistics" -- Alan Smith, TED

A data journalist on why people misread numbers even when the numbers are right. The last four minutes are about how you present a result so that a non-specialist actually hears it.

Tip: after watching, say your own main finding out loud twice: once as you would say it to a statistician, once as you would say it to a patient. They should not be the same sentence.
