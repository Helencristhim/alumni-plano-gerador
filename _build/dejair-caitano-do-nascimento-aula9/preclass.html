Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

Numbers, Data and Results -- Saying What You Found

Answering "what did your research find?" in ten seconds, with the right quantity words. Key words: data, result, finding, sample, percentage, to increase, to decrease, significant. Structure: quantifiers with countable and uncountable nouns.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. You write all eight of these every week. This lesson is where they leave the page.

Data -- the numbers you collected, before anybody explains them
"We do not have much data on the first three months."
Result -- what the numbers show at the end of a study
"The result was clear: pain scores decreased in most patients."
Finding -- one specific thing a study discovered
"Our main finding is that the pain continues after the infection ends."
Sample -- the group of people you actually studied
"Our sample was small: sixty patients, in three centres."
Percentage -- a number out of one hundred
"A high percentage of our patients stopped before the end."
To increase -- to go up
"Pain increased in a few patients after the third month."
To decrease -- to go down
"Pain scores decreased in most of the group."
Significant -- big enough that it is probably not chance
"The difference is significant, but the sample is small."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Nurten opens with the limitation, before anybody asks. "Our sample was small. Sixty patients, and I will say that at the beginning so nobody has to ask. We do not have much data on the first three months, because the pain diaries came back incomplete." Then the numbers: two years of follow-up on forty-one of them, pain scores decreased in most of that group and increased in a few, and the difference is significant. And then the sentence that makes the whole thing stronger: "I would rather say the treatment helps many patients than say it works, because we still do not know which patients."

A Because data is uncountable in English -- you cannot say one data, two datas
B Because there is very little of it
C Because data is a plural word
Why does she say the sample size at the very beginning?
A Because it is the most interesting part of the study

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Much, Many and the Rest

GRAMMAR

The question is always the same: can you count it one by one? If yes, use many and a few. If no, use much and a little. A lot of and most work with both.

countablemany / a fewmany patients, a few studies, a few centres
uncountablemuch / a littlemuch data, a little evidence, a little pain
botha lot of / most ofa lot of information, most of the group
careful with the articlea few = some · few = almost nonea few patients came back / very few patients came back
uncountable in English onlydata, evidence, research, information, painnever evidences, researches or informations

Data, evidence, research, information and pain are countable in Portuguese and uncountable in English. That single list removes most of the mistakes in an abstract.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

A "Our main finding is that the pain continues after the infection is treated. Our sample is small: sixty patients."

C "We found many datas about the pain."

A "Sorry, I know it is a bad study."
