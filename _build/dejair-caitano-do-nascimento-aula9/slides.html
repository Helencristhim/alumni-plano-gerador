Chapter 3: The Code

One Word in Portuguese, Two in English

Much, many, a little, a few, most

Discover the Rule

Which Ones Can You Count?

We do not have much data on the first three months.
The treatment helps many patients, but not all of them.
There is a little evidence for that, and a few studies.
Pain scores decreased in most of the group.
Click to reveal the rule
If you can count it one by one, use many and a few: many patients, a few studies. If you cannot, use much and a little: much data, a little evidence. A lot of and most work with both. Careful: data, evidence, research, information and pain are uncountable in English, although they are all countable in Portuguese — so never many evidences or two researches.

Chapter 4: In the Room

A Warning, and Then Somebody Doing It Right

With a sample smaller than yours

Listening 1

Significant Does Not Mean Big

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What does significant actually mean in English, according to him?
That the difference is probably not chance. It does not mean big.
2. What should you say instead, if you mean it matters to the patient?
Say exactly that: it matters to the patient.
3. What is his advice about a small number of patients?
Say it early and say it yourself. If the audience finds it, it is a weakness. If you say it first, it is honesty.

Say the Limitation First

P
Good afternoon. Pavel, from Kazan. So, what did you find?
D
Dejair, from Brazil. Our main finding is that the pain continues after the infection is treated.
D
And before you ask: our sample is small. Sixty patients, in three centres.
P
Thank you for saying that first. Not everybody does. How long did you follow them?
D
Two years, but only forty-one of them. Eighteen per cent stopped before the end.
P
And the pain scores?
D
They decreased in most of that group, and increased in a few. The difference is significant, but with sixty patients I would rather say the treatment helps many patients than say it works.
P
That is a careful way to put it. Do you have much data on the first three months?
D
No, and that is our real limitation. The pain diaries came back incomplete.
P
We had the same problem. We solved it with phone calls instead of diaries. I will send you the protocol.
Comprehension

About Pavel

1. How does Pavel react when Dejair says the sample is small?
He thanks him for saying it first, and says not everybody does.
2. What problem did Pavel's group also have, and how did they solve it?
The same problem with the first months. They used phone calls instead of pain diaries.
3. What does he offer at the end?
To send Dejair their protocol.
Chapter 5: Practice

Sixty Patients, Said Well

The same size as your study, and she does not apologise for it

Listening 2

I Would Rather Say It Helps

Read the questions, then listen.

1. How big is her sample, and when does she say it?
Sixty patients. She says it at the beginning, so nobody has to ask.
2. What data is missing, and why?
The first three months, because the pain diaries came back incomplete.
3. Why does she say the treatment helps many patients instead of saying it works?
Because they still do not know which patients -- and that is the question she brought to the congress.

Sixty Patients Is Nothing

A reviewer says sixty patients is too small to conclude anything. Do not apologise. Accept the limitation, say exactly what your data does support, and say what you would rather conclude instead. Then ask him what sample size he would consider enough, and why.
