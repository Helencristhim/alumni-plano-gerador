Grammar Discovery

Imagine you are the CEO

If I were the CEO, I would close twenty branches.
If we cut costs, we would lose our best people.
What would you do if you were in her position?
If I were you, I wouldn’t wait.

If + past, would + verb = an imagined situation, not the real one.

With be, use were for everybody: if I were, if he were. The past here does not mean the past — it means “imagine”. If I were you, I would ... is the classic way to give advice.

The Pattern

Real or imagined?

Imagined — today

"If I were you, I would ..."

Never would after if: "If I were", not "If I would be".

D
David: Well, it depends.
C
Claire: It always depends. I want you to take a position.
D
David: OK. If I were the CEO, I would close sixty branches.
C
Claire: Sixty? That sounds risky.
D
David: I can back it up. Those branches have fewer than ten clients a day.
C
Claire: Let me play devil’s advocate. If you closed them, you would lose those clients.
D
David: Some of them, yes. But most of them already use the app.
C
Claire: And the people who work there? They are stakeholders too.
D
David: Fair point. I wouldn’t fire them. I would move them to the call center. In the long run, the bank would be stronger.
