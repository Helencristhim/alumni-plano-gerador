If You Were the CEO

Eight words for the case discussion (take a position, stakeholder, cut costs, in the long run, risky, gut feeling, devil’s advocate, back up) and the grammar of imagined situations: if + past, would + verb. Around 30 minutes, in one sitting.

Stage 1.1: Words for the Case Discussion

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say each word out loud. Eight words, three minutes.

Take a position — to say clearly what you think and what you would do
"In class, you have to take a position."
Stakeholder — a person or group affected by a decision
"The clients are the most important stakeholders."
Cut costs — to spend less money
"If we cut costs, we would lose good people."
In the long run — after a long time, in the future
"In the long run, the bank would be stronger."
Risky — possibly dangerous, it can go wrong
"It is risky, but I would do it."
Gut feeling — an idea that comes from instinct, not from numbers
"My gut feeling says no."
Devil’s advocate — a person who argues the other side, to test an idea
"Let me play devil’s advocate for a minute."
Back up — to support an idea with facts or numbers
"Can you back it up with a number?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the text. Then answer the questions.

In the second week, the professor gave us a case about a bank with too many branches. Then she looked at me: “Mauricio, if you were the CEO, what would you do?” My gut feeling was clear, but I started with “it depends”. She waited. So I took a position: “If I were the CEO, I would close sixty branches.” A classmate played devil’s advocate: “That is risky. If you closed them, you would lose clients.” I backed it up with one number: seventy percent of the clients already use the app. I also said I wouldn’t fire anyone, because the employees are stakeholders too. “If we only cut costs, we would lose good people. In the long run, the bank would be stronger with them.”
What was his first answer?
A “It depends.”
B “If I were the CEO, I would open more branches in small towns.”
Why is it “if I were the CEO” and not “if I am the CEO”?
A Because the sentence is about the past, when he was a manager at the bank.
B Because it is an imagined situation — he is not the CEO.
How did he back up his position?
A With a number: seventy percent of the clients use the app.
B He said that his gut feeling is always right, so the class should trust it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Imagine — and say what you would do

If + past, would + verb = an imagined situation. With be, use were for everybody.

if + past

If I were the CEO, ...
If we cut costs, ...

would + verb

..., I would close sixty branches.
..., we would lose people.

negative

I wouldn’t fire anyone.
If I didn’t decide, ...

question & advice

What would you do?
If I were you, I would wait.

× “If I would be the CEO” — no would after if: If I were
× “I would to close” — bare verb: I would close

Real plan (lesson 12): if + present, will. Imagined (today): if + past, would.

Stage 4: In the room

Quiz
Which sentence is correct?
A “If I would be the CEO of the bank, I would close the smallest branches.”
B “If I were the CEO, I would close the branches.”
The professor asks: “What would you do?” What is the best answer?
A “If I were the CEO, I would close sixty branches — most clients use the app.”
B “Well, it depends. There are many good arguments on both sides of this case.”
A classmate plays devil’s advocate. What do you say?
A “You are wrong, and I don’t want to discuss it again in this class.”
B “Fair point, but in the long run it would work.”

Stage 5: If you were the CEO

Reflection
Think of one real decision at your bank this year. Record one minute in English. Say: “If I were the CEO, I would ...” Then back it up with one number, name one stakeholder, and say what would happen in the long run.
