Chapter 4: The Code

Increased, Dropped, Reached

The past simple with regular verbs

Grammar Discovery

Four Sentences From the Email

"Sales increased by eight percent."

"We reached ninety-five percent of the target."

"Prices dropped in August."

"We didn’t sign the new contract."

Look at the end of the blue verbs in sentences 1, 2 and 3: -ed. That is the past. Now look at sentence 4: after didn’t, the verb has no -ed.

JobFormExample
Past, regular verbsverb + -edSales increased by eight percent.
Spelling: verb ends in -e+ dWe closed the quarter well.
Spelling: short verb, one voweldouble the last letter + -edPrices dropped in August.
Negativedidn’t + verb (no -ed)We didn’t reach the target.
Three sounds of -ed/t/ reached · /d/ signed · /id/ visitedWe visited two customers.
The trapno -ed after didn’tNot we didn’t reached. Say we didn’t reach.

In one line: regular verbs take -ed in the past; after didn’t, the verb is simple again.

Listening 2 · The Colleague in Italy

Giulia’s Quarter

A key account manager in Italy talks about her quarter. Sound first — no text.

1. How many contracts did Giulia sign in July?
Two small contracts.
2. Why did orders drop in August?
Because many factories in Italy closed for the summer holidays.
3. Did Giulia reach her target?
Yes. She reached one hundred and two percent.
