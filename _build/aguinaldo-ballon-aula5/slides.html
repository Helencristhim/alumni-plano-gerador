Lesson 5 · After the Meeting

What They Told Us

The meeting with the regulator is over. Now a partner in London, a client in New York and a fund in Toronto all want to know the same thing: what did they say?

Chapter 1: First Words

From the Rule to the Report

Last time you described how a rule was made. Today you are the person who was in the room, and your job is to tell everybody else what was said, clearly and accurately.

Think of a recent meeting. Tell me one thing somebody said, and one thing somebody asked.

Today’s Goal

Three Missions

1

Ten words for reporting a meeting.

2

Change what people said into reported speech.

3

Give a debrief of a meeting in under two minutes.

The structure of the day moves words back in time: the regulator says "We will publish the rule in May", and you report the regulator said they would publish the rule in May. A good debrief also chooses the right verb: they confirmed, they warned, they recommended.

One Question

What Did They Say?

Two sentences, out loud, right now.

Report something a client said to you last week, and something a client asked you. Start with "She said..." and "He asked...". Do not worry about the grammar yet.

Chapter 3: The Code

Moving the Words Back in Time

What they said, and how you report it

Grammar Discovery

What Changes When You Report It?

"We will publish the rule in May." → He said they would publish the rule in May.

"The process is slow." → She acknowledged that the process was slow.

"We have received 300 comments." → They told us they had received 300 comments.

"Can you send the draft?" → She asked if we could send the draft.

Compare the words in blue, what the person said, with the words in red, how you report them later. Each verb moves one step back in time. And look at the question: what happens to the word order?

JobFormExample
will becomes wouldsaid (that) + would"We will publish it." → He said they would publish it.
present becomes pastsaid (that) + past simple"It is slow." → She said it was slow.
present perfect becomes past perfecttold us (that) + had + past participle"We have received it." → They told us they had received it.
can becomes couldsaid (that) + could"We can wait." → He said they could wait.
Yes / no questionsasked + if + normal word order"Can you send it?" → She asked if we could send it.
say or tell?say something / tell somebody somethingHe said that... / He told us that... (never "said us")
The trapreporting verbs that are more precise than saidHe confirmed / warned / recommended that...

In one line: move the verb one step back in time; tell needs a person, say does not; a question becomes asked if + normal order.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Debrief, at Real Speed

One conversation, three recordings, one page

Dialogue

The Call With London

O
Hi, Aguinaldo. I have ten minutes before my next call. How did the meeting with the agency go?
A
It went well. The director confirmed that the new rule would be published in May.
O
That is good news. Did he say anything about the transition period?
A
Yes. He said it would be twelve months. But he warned us that the penalties could be higher than in the draft.
O
Higher? Our client will not like that. Did anybody ask why?
A
I did. He told us they had received many complaints about safety. And off the record, he said the minister wanted a stronger rule.
O
Okay. Can you put that in writing for the client, without the off-the-record part?
A
Of course. I will send a short debrief this afternoon, with only what was said in the official statement.
Comprehension

About Olivia

1. How much time does Olivia have?
Ten minutes, before her next call.
2. What does Olivia say about the client's reaction to higher penalties?
She says the client will not like it.
3. What does Olivia ask him to leave out of the written report?
The off-the-record part.
Listening 1 · The Partner

How I Write a Debrief

A partner at an international law firm explains what a good meeting report looks like. Sound first — no text.

1. How long should a debrief be, according to her?
One page, or five sentences in an email.
2. What is the first thing she writes?
What was decided or confirmed.
3. What does she never put in writing?
Anything that was said off the record.
Listening 2 · The Client

The Report That Was Wrong

A company director remembers a meeting report that caused a problem. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the report say the agency had done?
It said the agency had approved the new tariff.
2. What had the agency really said?
That it would probably approve the tariff, but only after the public consultation.
3. What does he ask for in every report now?
The exact words, or at least the exact verb.
Business Radio

The Minister and the Statement

A short radio report on a minister's comments about infrastructure investment. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the minister say about the new program?
She said it would attract fifty billion dollars in private investment.
2. What did she acknowledge?
That some concessions had been delayed.
3. What did the investors' association warn?
That the rules had to be clear before the tenders.
Real Document

The Email Olivia Receives

MEETING DEBRIEF
To: Olivia Grant (London) · Meeting with the National Transport Agency
Date and participantsTuesday · agency director, two technical advisers, A. Ballon
ConfirmedThe final rule will be published in May
Transition periodThe director said it would be twelve months
WarningHe warned that penalties could be higher than in the draft
Reason givenThe agency had received many safety complaints
RequestThey asked if our client could send data on accidents by June 30
Next stepA. Ballon to follow up with the technical team next week
This debrief only includes what was said officially. Each line uses a precise reporting verb, so that nobody reads "said" when the director only "warned" or "asked".
1. What did the director confirm?
That the final rule would be published in May.
2. Turn the line "Request" into direct speech: what exactly did they say?
"Can your client send data on accidents by June 30?"
3. Why does the email use verbs like warned and asked instead of said?
To be precise, so that the client knows exactly what kind of information it is.
