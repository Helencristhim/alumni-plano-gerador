Lesson 04 — Pre-class

The Contest Report

Eight contest words (runner-up, prize, conditions, delay, flat, cancel, rookie, draw) and the past of the verb to be: was and were. Get ready to report a whole weekend.

Stage 1.1: Contest Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Runner-up — the person who finishes in second place
"The champion was the runner-up this year."
Prize — the money or the object you get for winning
"The prize was small, but the photo was everywhere."
Conditions — what the sea and the weather are like on that day
"The conditions were unbelievable on Saturday."
Delay — when something starts later than planned
"At nine o'clock they announced a delay."
Flat — with no waves at all
"The ocean was flat all morning."
Cancel — to say that something will not happen
"Nobody wanted to cancel the contest."
Rookie — a person in their first year in a competition
"He was a rookie last season."
Draw — the list that says who surfs against who
"The draw was ready at six in the morning."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the report. Then answer the questions.

Friday was a bad day. The ocean was flat, there was no swell at all, and the organisers were nervous. At nine o'clock there was a delay. Some parents were angry. Saturday was completely different. The conditions were perfect, the draw was on the wall at six, and the first heats were in the water by seven. The winner was a rookie. He wasn't the favourite, but at the end he was the champion, and the old champion was the runner-up.
Why was there a delay on Friday?
A Because the parents were angry.
B Because the ocean was flat and there was no swell.
Who won the contest?
A A rookie, who wasn't the favourite.
B The old champion.
Why is it "the conditions were" and not "the conditions was"?
A Because it is a question.
B Because "conditions" is plural, and plural takes "were".

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Was and were — the past of the verb to be

In the present you choose between three words. In the past there are only two.

was

I was
He / She / It was
I wasn't

were

You were
We / They were
They weren't

? Was it a good final? / Were you there? (no "did" — the verb jumps to the front, exactly like in Lesson 1)
→ Yes, I was. / No, we weren't.
There: There was a delay. / There were two rookies in the final.

Stage 3: Say it out loud

Speaking

Tap Listen, then Record. Inside a sentence "was" and "were" are short and soft. At the end of a short answer they come back loud.

It was a close final.
The conditions were perfect on Saturday.
I wasn't nervous, actually.

Stage 4: After the contest

Quiz
A friend asks about Saturday. The waves were great. What do you say?
A The waves was amazing.
B The waves were amazing.
You want to know if your friend was at the beach. Which question is correct?
A Were you at the beach?
B Did you were at the beach?
Somebody asks: "Were you nervous?" You weren't. What do you answer?
A No, I didn't.
B No, I wasn't.
The contest started two hours late, but it happened. What was there?
A There was a delay.
B They cancelled the contest.

Stage 5: Your own report

Reflection
Report a real contest or a really good session of yours, out loud, for 30 seconds. Where were you? How were the conditions? Who was there? Were you happy at the end? Check one thing before you send it — every sentence has was or were, and plural takes were.
