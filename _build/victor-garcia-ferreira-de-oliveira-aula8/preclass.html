Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

Midpoint Checkpoint -- Halfway, and the Numbers

Seven lessons ago you spoke for twenty seconds and stopped. This lesson measures what actually moved, and no new grammar is introduced: the seven structures from lessons 1 to 7 come back together, under pressure. Key words: a benchmark, measurable, to assess, a blind spot, consistent, to plateau, to fall behind, a gap, to keep track of something, room for improvement.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A benchmark -- a measurement you take once, so that the next one means something
"The first recording was a benchmark. On its own it proved nothing at all."
Measurable -- able to be counted, so that two people can agree about it
"Two long pauses instead of six is measurable. Feeling more confident is not."
To assess -- to judge how good something is, against something fixed rather than a feeling
"You cannot assess yourself in the middle of speaking. That is what the recording is for."
A blind spot -- the weakness you cannot see, precisely because it is yours
"Everybody has a blind spot, and yours is always the thing you never practise."
Consistent -- happening in the same way every time, and not only on the good days
"One excellent answer is luck. Four in a row is consistent, and that is what gets measured."
To plateau -- to stop improving for a while, after a period of fast progress
"Everybody plateaus around the third month, and almost everybody thinks it means they failed."
To fall behind -- to end up slower than the plan you yourself agreed to
"I fell behind for two weeks in October, and the honest reason was that I stopped recording."
A gap -- the distance between where you are and where you have to be
"Naming the gap is not pessimism. It is the only way to spend the next month well."
To keep track of something -- to record something regularly, so that you can see the change
"If you do not keep track of it, you will remember the two bad days and forget the twenty good ones."
Room for improvement -- the part that is not good yet, said without insulting anybody
"There is room for improvement in the endings, and that sentence has ended a thousand meetings kindly."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions. No new structure here: every one of the seven is already yours.

Halfway through a programme, the useful question is not how do you feel. It is what is measurable. A first recording is a benchmark and on its own it proves nothing; it only means something next to a second one. So: the silences have gone from six to two since the first lesson, and the answer was recorded on the same question, in the same conditions. Two of the seven structures of this programme are already in that paragraph, and neither of them was chosen on purpose.

The rest arrive as soon as you start arguing about what the numbers mean. Nobody translated for me, which is why the pauses moved. That must be the reason, because nothing else changed. If I keep a record every week, I will see a plateau while it is happening; if I had recorded in October, I would not have argued with myself about whether I had fallen behind. And I used to stop completely when I lost a word, which is over, although there is still room for improvement in the endings.

Notice what that second paragraph actually is: reason and result, a modal of certainty, both conditionals, used to, and a concession — six structures, in six sentences, about one subject. That is the whole point of a checkpoint. Nothing new is being taught today. The question is which of the seven arrives without being called, and which two you still have to stop and look for. Those two are the gap, and the next eight lessons are built on exactly one of them.

1. According to the text, why does a benchmark prove nothing on its own?
A Because the first recording is always bad.
B Because it only means something next to a second measurement of the same thing.
C Because feelings cannot be counted.
2. What does the text say is the point of a checkpoint?
A To teach the seven structures again from the beginning.
B To give the learner a score they can compare with other people.
C To find out which structures arrive without being called, and which two still have to be looked for.
3. How many gaps does the text say the next eight lessons are built on?
A Exactly one.
B All of the ones the checkpoint finds.

Stage 2: Put the Checkpoint Answer in Order

Order

Somebody has asked whether your English has improved. Put the answer in the order that ends with a decision rather than a feeling.

?"Yes, and I can be specific about it rather than saying it feels better."
?"The measurable part is the silences, and they have gone from six to two."
?"It was the same question in the same conditions, so it is not guessing."
?"There is still room for improvement in the endings, and I know it."
?"That is the one gap I would name for the second half, and only that one."
?"If I keep track of one number every week, I will see a plateau early."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

Somebody asks whether your English has improved. You want to be believed. You say:
A "Yes, much better, I feel very different now."
B "The measurable part is the silences, and they have gone from six to two."
C "I think so, but it is difficult to say."
You want to name what is still weak, without sounding defeated. You say:
A "There is still room for improvement in the endings."
B "My endings are terrible and I never get them right."
C "I have no problems with the endings at all."
You stopped recording for two weeks and it cost you. You say:
A "I have fallen behind two weeks ago."
B "I fell behind for two weeks, and the honest reason was that I stopped recording."
C "I am falling behind since two weeks."
Somebody says two recordings prove nothing. You concede fairly and hold your position. You say:
A "You are completely wrong about that."
B "Yes, you are right, it probably means nothing."
C "You have a point that two is a small number, and it is still the same question and the same conditions."
