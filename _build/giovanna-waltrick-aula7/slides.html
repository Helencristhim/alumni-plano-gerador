Chapter 3: The Code

How Much, or How Many?

One question for what you count, another for what you measure

Discover the Rule

Why Does One Side Say Much and the Other Many?

How much money do you have?  ·  How many options do you have?
I do not have much time this week.  ·  There are not many affordable brands.
I have a little fabric left.  ·  I have a few pieces left.
I have a lot of fabric.  ·  I have a lot of options.
Click to reveal the rule
Everything depends on one question about the noun: can you count it? Countable nouns have a plural — options, pieces, brands, receipts. Uncountable nouns do not — money, fabric, time, information, luggage. Then: how many + countable, how much + uncountable. A few + countable, a little + uncountable. And a lot of works with both, which is why it is the safest thing to say when you are not sure. One warning: much and many sound natural in questions and negatives (I do not have much time), but in a positive sentence people normally say a lot of.

Comprehension

About Victor

1. What does he ask for before giving the discount?
Her student card.
2. How many colours are there in that fabric, and what is the problem with the dark green?
Three colours. There is not much of the dark green left, only about two and a half metres.
3. What does he offer as a solution, and what does it cost her?
He can order more, but it takes a week.
