Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

How Much, How Many -- The Budget That Does Not Stretch

Talking about quantity, money and what is not enough. Key words: budget, amount, option, size, sale, discount, receipt, try on. Structure: much, many, a little, a few, and countable against uncountable nouns.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. Four of them are about money, four about the shop.

Budget -- the amount of money you have for something
"My budget for the whole project is four hundred reais."
Amount -- how much there is of something you cannot count
"I did not expect that amount of fabric for one piece."
Option -- one of the things you can choose
"I have a few options and very little time."
Size -- how big or small something is
"They only had it in one size, and it was not mine."
Sale -- a period when a shop sells things for less than usual
"I bought the fabric in a sale, so it cost half."
Discount -- money taken off the normal price
"Students get a discount at that shop, but you have to ask."
Receipt -- the paper that proves how much you paid
"Keep the receipt, because you may need to return it."
To try on -- to put clothes on in a shop to see if they fit
"I would rather try it on than send it back later."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

My budget for the final project is four hundred reais, and I already know it is not much. The fabric costs seventy a metre and I need three metres, so that is more than half of everything. There are not many affordable options in that shop, and there is not much of the dark green left — about two and a half metres. I have a few other colours in mind and a little time to decide, but not much. I asked for the student discount, and I keep every receipt.
Why is it "not much of the dark green" but "not many affordable options"?
A Because fabric cannot be counted and options can
B Because one is in the past and the other in the present
C Because much is used with adjectives
"I have a few other colours in mind and a little time." Why a few and a little?
A A few goes with things you count, a little with things you do not
B A few is for the past, a little for the present
C They mean exactly the same thing

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- How Much and How Many

GRAMMAR

Everything depends on one question: can you count the noun?

countablehas a pluraloptions, pieces, brands, receipts
uncountableno pluralmoney, fabric, time, information, luggage
questionshow many + countable · how much + uncountableHow many metres? How much money?
small quantitya few + countable · a little + uncountablea few options, a little time
works with botha lot ofa lot of fabric, a lot of options

Careful 1: information and luggage have no plural in English. "Two informations" is wrong — it is "two pieces of information".

Careful 2: in a positive sentence, a lot of sounds far more natural than much. Keep much for questions and negatives.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Talk about the real budget of a project you are working on. Record ninety seconds: how much money you have, how many things you still need to buy, what is more expensive than you expected, and what you would cut first. Use how much, how many, a little and a few. Do not write a script first — just speak.
