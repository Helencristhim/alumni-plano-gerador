Chapter 4: The Code

How Long, and Still Going

The two present perfects, and the one mistake that gives away every Portuguese speaker

Grammar Discovery

She Has Been Here For Eleven Years

"She has been with the company for eleven years."

"We have been running the programme since March."

"Three of the five have already moved into bigger roles."

"How long have you been thinking about that move?"

Two of these count results. Two describe work that is still going on. Which is which, and what in the verb tells you?

FormWhat it doesExample
have / has + past participlea finished action whose result matters nowThree of the five have moved into bigger roles.
for / sincefor gives the length, since gives the starting pointShe has been here for eleven years.
have been + -ingthe activity itself, still going on, with its duration in viewWe have been running the programme since March.
the difference that mattersthe simple counts RESULTS; the continuous describes the WORKWe have trained forty people. We have been training all year.
the trapthe present simple for duration -- I work here for eleven years. This is the single most common B2 mistake in Portuguese speakers. Say I have worked here for eleven years.

If you take one thing from this hour, take this: duration up to now is never the present simple in English. I am here for eleven years means you are leaving in eleven years.

Chapter 5: The Ear

Where Development Money Goes to Die

A head of learning and a manager who was given a job he was not ready for

Listening 1

Why Training Budgets Fail

A head of learning, speaking at an internal conference. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she say the money goes to, and what does she think of it?
It goes to courses, which she says are the least effective development there is, and everybody in the room already knows it.
2. What does she say actually moves somebody?
A job that is slightly too big for them, with somebody watching. She says that is the whole science.
3. Why does she say companies keep buying courses instead?
Because a course is easy to approve, while a stretch assignment requires a manager to accept risk on their own numbers, and nobody wants to be the manager whose project went wrong.
Listening 2

The Job I Was Not Ready For

A plant manager on the assignment that changed his career. Sound first.

0:000:00
1. How long had he been a supervisor when he got the plant, and what was the situation on the site?
Two years. There were people on that site who had been there longer than he had been alive, and he was going to tell them how to run a shift.
2. What did his director tell him, and what did he do for six months?
That he expected him to get about a third of it wrong and that his own job was to catch the third that mattered. He checked in every Friday for six months, twenty minutes, never longer.
3. When it has gone wrong since, what does he say the cause has been?
Not that the person was not ready, but that he was not watching.

Chapter 6: Your Turn

The Part Where You Talk

The succession review, the development conversation, and the hard one

Guided

Present One Successor

Task: Present one person from your own team as a successor: how long they have been in the role, what they have done, what they have been working on, and how ready they are on a scale of now, one year or two. Use the frames below, then do it again with them covered.

Support:

She has been with us for...She has already...She has been working on...I would grade her readiness at...What is missing is...
Semi-free

The Development Conversation

Task: Your teacher plays a high performer who asks, directly, what comes next for her. There is no open role. Have the conversation without promising anything and without losing her. You may not use the words maybe, hopefully or someday.

Support:

What I can tell you is...You have been ready for...What I cannot promise is...What I would put in front of you is...Let us agree a date to look at this again.
Free

Ask for the Stretch Yourself

Task: Turn it round. You are asking Greg for a stretch assignment of your own -- something in the US expansion. Make the case in two minutes: what you have done, what you have been doing, and what you would need. Your teacher will ask what happens to your current work.

No support. Target: three present perfects used correctly, and an answer to the question you did not want.
