Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

The Language of Growth

The ten terms of succession and development, and the two present perfects that carry every sentence about how long and how far. Key words: succession plan, high potential, talent pipeline, stretch assignment, upskilling, readiness, bench strength, career path, to groom, to backfill. Structure: present perfect and present perfect continuous.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read it inside a real sentence. All ten appear in every succession conversation in an American company.

Succession plan -- a plan naming who could take a role if the person in it left
"The succession plan has one name against four of the nine roles."
High potential -- an employee identified as able to grow two levels or more
"Being a high potential is a bet the company makes, not a reward it gives."
Talent pipeline -- the group of people being prepared for future roles
"Our talent pipeline is thin above supervisor level."
Stretch assignment -- a task deliberately above somebody current level, given to develop them
"A stretch assignment is the cheapest development there is."
Upskilling -- teaching the people you already employ new skills instead of hiring
"Upskilling the night shift cost less than one agency hire."
Readiness -- how close somebody is to being able to do the next role
"We grade readiness as now, one year, or two years -- never as maybe."
Bench strength -- how many capable replacements a company has for its key roles
"Bench strength is the number nobody looks at until somebody resigns."
Career path -- the sequence of roles somebody can move through inside a company
"If there is no career path, the best people write their own somewhere else."
To groom -- to prepare somebody deliberately for a particular role
"He was groomed for that job for three years before anybody told him."
To backfill -- to replace somebody who has left or moved, in the same role
"Every promotion creates a role to backfill, and we forget that every time."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

Ana has been with the company for eleven years and has been running the São Paulo site since March. In that time she has rebuilt the onboarding process and has taken two people from supervisor to shift manager. Her readiness for the next level is graded at one year.

We have been building the pipeline above supervisor level for two years and it has not worked. We have trained forty people and we have promoted four. What we have not been doing is giving anybody a job slightly too big for them, which is the only development that has ever moved anybody in this company.

1. She has been running the site since March. What does this tell you that she ran the site in March does not?
A That she started in March and is still doing it now.
B That she ran it only during the month of March.
C That she will start running it next March.
2. Why for eleven years and since March?
A Because for is used with years and since with months.
B Because for gives the length of time and since gives the starting point.
C Because since is more formal than for.
3. We have trained forty people and we have been building the pipeline for two years. What is the difference?
A The first counts a result; the second describes work that has been going on.
B The first is more recent than the second.
C There is no difference; the two forms are interchangeable.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Two Present Perfects

GRAMMAR

One of these counts results and one describes work in progress. Both connect the past to now, which is why neither of them can be replaced by the present simple -- the mistake that gives away almost every Portuguese speaker at B2.

FormWhat it doesExample
have / has + past participlea finished action whose result matters nowThree of the five have moved into bigger roles.
for / sincefor gives the length, since gives the starting pointShe has been here for eleven years.
have been + -ingthe activity itself, still going on, with its duration in viewWe have been running the programme since March.
the difference that mattersthe simple counts RESULTS; the continuous describes the WORKWe have trained forty people. We have been training all year.
the trapthe present simple for duration -- I work here for eleven years. This is the single most common B2 mistake in Portuguese speakers. Say I have worked here for eleven years.

I work here for eleven years is not a small error: to an American ear it means you are leaving in eleven years. Say I have worked here for eleven years.

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Read each sentence out loud, record it and listen back. The present perfect is slow until it is automatic -- say them until they come out at speed.

She has been with us for eleven years and she has been running the site since March.
I would grade her readiness at one year, not now.
Our bench strength above supervisor level is thin, and I have known that for two years.
Every promotion creates a role to backfill, and we have not counted them.
What I can tell you is what I will do; what I cannot promise you is a date.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

One right answer each. Read the situation before you look at the options.

Greg asks how long Ana has been with the company. What do you say?
A She is with us since 2015.
B She has been with us for eleven years.
C She is with us for eleven years.
D She works here since eleven years.
You want to say that the pipeline work started two years ago and is still going. Which is right?
A We build the pipeline for two years.
B We have been building the pipeline for two years.
C We are building the pipeline since two years.
D We built the pipeline for two years.
A high performer asks what comes next and there is no open role. What do you say?
A Maybe something will open up next year.
B What I can tell you is what I will do; what I cannot promise you is a date.
C Hopefully something will come up soon.
D There is nothing at the moment, I am sorry.
Your budget is going on external courses. What does the evidence in this lesson suggest?
A Courses are the strongest development available.
B A job slightly too big, with somebody watching, moves people more than a course does.
C Development budgets should be cut.
D Certificates are what employees actually want.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
