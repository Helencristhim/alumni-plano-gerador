Four Ways to Look Back

Same Trip, Four Regrets

"It was a mistake not to pack a raincoat."

Correct, and heavy. It takes seven words to say one regret.

"I should have packed a raincoat."

The same regret in four words, and it sounds like you mean it.

"We needn't have taken so much cash."

We took it, and it was not necessary. A different kind of mistake.

"You must have been soaked."

Not a regret. A guess about the past, and you are almost sure.

Looking back needs two tools: one for what you should have done, and one for guessing what happened.

Chapter 3: What I Should Have Done

What I Should Have Done

The modals English uses to look back at the past

Grammar Discovery

Two Regrets, Two Guesses

I should have packed a raincoat.

We needn't have taken so much cash. Everywhere took cards.

You must have been exhausted after that flight.

It can't have been cheap to stay in that hotel.

Which two sentences look back with regret, and which two are guesses about what happened? In the guesses, how sure is the speaker?

ModalWhat it meansExample
should have + past participleit was a good idea, and you did not do it"I should have packed a raincoat."
shouldn't have + past participleyou did it, and it was a bad idea"I shouldn't have bought that sword."
could have + past participlea chance you had and did not take"We could have seen Mount Fuji."
needn't have + past participleyou did it, but it was not necessary"We needn't have taken so much cash."
didn't need to + verbit was not necessary, and usually you did not do it"We didn't need to buy tickets. The museum was free."
must have / can't have + past participleyou are almost sure it happened / almost sure it did not"You must have been tired." "It can't have been cheap."
might have / may have + past participleMaybe it happened, maybe not: "I might have left it on the train."

The Tool

Three Places It Goes Wrong

Trap 1 · "should of" does not exist

In fast speech "should've" sounds like "should of", and many people write it that way. It is always have + past participle: "should have packed", never "should of packed" or "should had packed".

Trap 2 · needn't have is not didn't need to

"We needn't have taken the cash" means we took it, and it was a waste. "We didn't need to take cash" usually means we did not take it, because it was not necessary.

Trap 3 · the opposite of must have is can't have

"He must have seen the sign" = I am almost sure he saw it. The opposite is "He can't have seen it". Never "mustn't have": mustn't is for rules, like "you mustn't smoke here".

Choose the right modal and people know if you are regretting, guessing or just looking back. Choose the wrong one and they hear something you did not mean.

Chapter 4: In Real Life

Dialogue

Nina's Three Regrets

N
Can I tell you my three biggest regrets from Japan, so you don't repeat them?
M
Go on. You must have made a lot of mistakes, then.
N
A few. First, I overpacked. I needn't have taken four pairs of shoes. I wore one.
M
That can't have been easy on the trains, with all those stairs.
N
It was not. Second, I overslept on the day of the temple tour, so I missed out on the best part.
M
You should have set two alarms.
N
I know. And third, I bought a "real" samurai sword at a shop near the station. A total rip-off.
M
It must have cost a fortune.
N
It did. I could have bought ten bowls of ramen with that money. Lesson learned.
Comprehension

About Nina

1. What was Nina's first regret, and why was it unnecessary?
She overpacked. She needn't have taken four pairs of shoes, because she only wore one.
2. What happened on the day of the temple tour?
She overslept, so she missed out on the best part.
3. What did she buy near the station, and what could she have done instead?
A "real" samurai sword, which was a rip-off. She could have bought ten bowls of ramen with that money.
Listening

Looking Back at the Past

Your teacher on regrets, deductions and the three traps. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What are the two things she says you can do when you look back at a trip?
You can regret, or you can guess.
2. What is the difference between "needn't have taken" and "didn't need to buy"?
"Needn't have taken": they took it, and it was not necessary. "Didn't need to buy": it was not necessary, so they did not buy.
3. When do you say "must have", and when do you say "can't have"?
"Must have" when you are almost sure it happened; "can't have" when you are almost sure it did not.
4. Why is "mustn't have" wrong for a guess?
Because "mustn't" is for rules.
Listening 2

A Podcaster's Regrets

Tom, who hosts a travel podcast, remembers his first trip to Japan. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does Tom say he should have done with the hotel?
He should have booked it three months earlier, because he booked at the last minute and paid double.
2. Why does he say he needn't have bought the rail pass?
Because he bought it for the whole week and only used it twice.
