Lesson 37 -- Pre-class

I Should Have Packed... -- Past Modals Review

"It was a mistake not to..." is correct and heavy. This lesson reviews the past modals you already know -- should have and could have -- and adds the new ones: needn't have for things you did that were not necessary, and must have and can't have for guessing what happened. You use them to look back at the mistakes of a trip. Key words: overpack, oversleep, rip-off, last-minute, miss out on, kick yourself, splurge, leave behind, wander off, once-in-a-lifetime, take for granted, lesson learned.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. These are the words people use when they talk about the mistakes they made on a trip.

Overpack -- to take more things on a trip than you need
"I overpacked and carried four pairs of shoes up every staircase."
Oversleep -- to sleep longer than you planned and wake up too late
"I overslept and missed the first train of the day."
Rip-off -- something that costs far more than it should
"The "real" samurai sword was a total rip-off."
Last-minute -- done or decided very late, just before it is too late
"Last-minute tickets always cost more."
Miss out on -- to lose the chance to do or enjoy something
"We missed out on the best temple because we were late."
Kick yourself -- to feel angry with yourself for a mistake you made
"I could kick myself for not taking more photos."
Splurge -- to spend a lot of money on something you enjoy
"On the last night we splurged on a really good dinner."
Leave behind -- to forget to take something with you when you go
"I left my charger behind in the hotel in Kyoto."
Wander off -- to walk away from your group or your plan without noticing
"My little brother wandered off in the market for ten minutes."
Once-in-a-lifetime -- so special that it will probably never happen again
"Seeing Mount Fuji at sunrise was a once-in-a-lifetime moment."
Take for granted -- to not notice or value something because it is always there
"At home I take clean, fast trains for granted."
Lesson learned -- what you say after a mistake that taught you something
"Never buy a sword near the station. Lesson learned."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read Nina's travel regrets and answer the questions. Notice how each modal in bold looks back at the past in a different way.

My first trip to Japan taught me a lot. First, I shouldn't have overpacked. I needn't have taken four pairs of shoes, because I only wore one, and I carried them up every staircase in Tokyo.

Second, I overslept on the day of the temple tour. I should have set two alarms. We could have seen the most famous temple in Kyoto, but we missed out on it.

Third, I bought a "real" samurai sword near the station. My friend said, "That can't have been cheap," and she was right. It must have cost as much as ten dinners.

On the other hand, we didn't need to buy tickets for the museums, because most of them were free for students. Lesson learned: read about the city before you go.

2. "We could have seen the most famous temple." What does this mean?
A They saw it twice.
B They had the chance, but they did not see it.
C They are sure they will see it next time.
3. "That can't have been cheap." How sure is the friend?
A Almost sure it was expensive.
B Not sure at all.
C Sure that it was cheap.
4. "We didn't need to buy tickets for the museums." What probably happened?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Looking Back at the Past

GRAMMAR

Past modals do two jobs: they regret what happened, and they guess what happened. All of them use have + past participle, except "didn't need to".

ModalWhat it meansExample
should have / shouldn't have + past participleregret: a good idea you did not do / a bad idea you did"I should have packed a raincoat."
could have + past participlea chance you did not take"We could have seen Mount Fuji."
needn't have + past participleyou did it, but it was not necessary"We needn't have taken so much cash."
didn't need to + verbnot necessary, so usually you did not do it"We didn't need to buy tickets."
must have / can't have + past participlealmost sure it happened / almost sure it did not"You must have been tired."
might have / may have + past participlemaybe"I might have left it on the train."
Three trapsAlways have, never "should of" or "should had". The opposite of "must have" is "can't have", never "mustn't have". And "needn't have" is not the same as "didn't need to".

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen to each sentence, then record yourself saying it. In fast speech, have almost disappears: should've, needn't've, must've. The stress falls on the modal and on the main verb: I SHOULD have PACKED. That rhythm is what makes a regret sound natural.

I should have packed a raincoat.
We needn't have taken so much cash.
You must have been exhausted after the flight.
That can't have been cheap.
We could have seen so much more.
