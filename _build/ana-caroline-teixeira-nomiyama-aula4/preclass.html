Lesson 04 -- Pre-class

A Page-Turner -- I Was Reading When...

Why thrillers are so hard to stop reading, and how to tell a story in the past. Key words: a page-turner, a thriller, a plot, the main character, a manuscript, to suspect, a clue, scary, a twist, to find out. Structure: past continuous x simple past (while / when).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A page-turner -- a book that is so exciting that you want to keep reading
"That novel was a real page-turner. I finished it in two days."
A thriller -- a book or a film with a lot of suspense, danger and secrets
"I love a good thriller, but I don’t read horror."
A plot -- the main events of a story, in order
"The plot is simple, but the ending is brilliant."
The main character -- the most important person in a story
"The main character is a young writer with a secret."
A manuscript -- the text of a book before it is printed
"She found an old manuscript in a drawer."
To suspect -- to think that something bad is probably true, without proof
"From the first chapter, I suspected the husband."
A clue -- a small piece of information that helps you discover the truth
"The writer hides a clue in chapter three."
Scary -- making you feel afraid
"The film was too scary for me. I watched it with the lights on."
A twist -- a surprising change in a story that you did not expect
"Nobody expected the twist at the end."
To find out -- to discover a fact or a piece of information
"I found out the truth on the last page."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last Saturday, Ana was reading a thriller on the sofa. It was raining, and her husband was cooking pasta in the kitchen. The main character of the book is a young editor. One night, while she was working late, she found an old manuscript in her boss’s office.

The plot was a real page-turner. Ana suspected the boss from the first chapter, and she was looking for every clue. The book wasn’t scary, but it was very tense.

She was reading the last chapter when her husband called her for dinner. She didn’t stop. Two minutes later, she found out the twist: the boss was innocent, and the editor’s best friend wrote the manuscript.

1. Why does the text say “her husband was cooking pasta” and not “her husband cooked pasta”?
A Because it was an action in progress at that moment, like the background of a scene.
B Because cook is a verb of state, and verbs of state always take -ing in the past.
C Because the action was short and finished.
2. What happened while the editor was working late?
A Her boss called her and told her about a secret manuscript that he wrote many years ago.
B She found an old manuscript.
C She went home early.
3. What did Ana do when her husband called her for dinner?
A She stopped reading immediately and went to the kitchen to help with the pasta and the table.
B She went to sleep.
C She kept reading and found out the twist.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Continuous x Simple Past

GRAMMAR

Use was / were + -ing for an action in progress at a moment in the past (the background of the scene). Use the simple past for a short action that cuts in, or for actions one after the other. While usually goes with the long action; when usually goes with the short one. Verbs of state (know, like, want) stay in the simple past.

JobFormExample
Action in progress in the pastI / he / she / it + was + verb-ing; you / we / they + were + verb-ingI was reading in bed.
Short action that cuts insimple past...when the lights went out.
While + the long actionwhile + past continuous, simple pastWhile she was cleaning, she found a manuscript.
One action after the othersimple past, simple pastWhen I finished the book, I went to sleep.
Questions and negativesWas / Were + subject + verb-ing? · wasn’t / weren’t + verb-ingWhat were you doing when you found out?
The trapverbs of state (know, like, want, believe) do not take -ingNot I was knowing the end. Say I knew the end.

Never: I was read... · I was knowing... · While I read, the phone was ringing · When I was finding out the twist...

Stage 2: Put the Night in Order

Order

One chapter becomes the whole book. Put the story of the night in a logical order.

?"At ten o’clock, I started a new thriller."
?"I was reading in bed, and it was raining outside."
?"While I was reading chapter five, I found a small clue."
?"From that moment, I suspected the main character’s brother."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A friend asks why you were tired on Monday. You say:
A "I was reading a thriller until two in the morning. It was a real page-turner."
B "I read a thriller when I was going to sleep at two, and it was being a real page-turner."
C "I was read a thriller until two."
You want to say what was happening when the phone rang. You say:
A "I read a book when the phone was ringing, so I didn’t answer it on time."
B "I was reading a book when the phone rang."
C "I was reading a book when the phone was ring."
You want to ask a colleague about the moment of the twist. You ask:
A "What were you doing when you found out the twist?"
B "What did you doing when you were finding out the twist of the book?"
C "What you were doing when you found out?"
A colleague invites you to watch a horror film. You say:
A "No, thanks. Horror is too scary for me."
B "No, thanks. I prefer a book with a clue on every page and a twist at the end."
C "No, thanks. I was suspecting it."
