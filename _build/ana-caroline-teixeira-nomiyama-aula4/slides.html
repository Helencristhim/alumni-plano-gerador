Chapter 4: The Code

I Was Reading When...

The long action and the short action

Grammar Discovery

The Long Action and the Short One

"I was reading in bed when the lights went out."

"While she was cleaning the old house, she found a manuscript."

"We were watching the film when my husband guessed the twist."

"When I finished the book, I went to sleep."

Look at the red verbs: they are long actions, in progress at a moment in the past. Now look at the blue verbs: short actions that cut in, or that happen one after the other. Red = was / were + -ing. Blue = the simple past.

JobFormExample
Action in progress in the pastI / he / she / it + was + verb-ing; you / we / they + were + verb-ingI was reading in bed.
Short action that cuts insimple past...when the lights went out.
While + the long actionwhile + past continuous, simple pastWhile she was cleaning, she found a manuscript.
One action after the othersimple past, simple pastWhen I finished the book, I went to sleep.
Questions and negativesWas / Were + subject + verb-ing? · wasn’t / weren’t + verb-ingWhat were you doing when you found out?
The trapverbs of state (know, like, want, believe) do not take -ingNot I was knowing the end. Say I knew the end.

In one line: long action in progress = was / were + -ing; short action that cuts in = simple past.

Listening 1 · A Bookseller

The Shelf That Never Stays Full

The owner of a small bookshop in England talks about the books that people buy most. Sound first — no text.

1. Which books sell the most in her shop?
Thrillers and mysteries.
2. What was a customer doing when he came back to the shop?
He was holding the book and asking for the next one by the same writer.
3. What does she say the best thrillers have?
A normal start, a clue that nobody sees, and a twist at the end.
Listening 2 · A Reader

The Night the Lights Went Out

An Australian man remembers a night when he was reading a thriller alone at home. Sound first — no text.

1. Where was he, and what was he doing?
He was at home alone, reading a thriller in bed.
2. What happened while he was reading the last chapter?
There was a storm, and the lights went out.
3. How did he finish the book?
He finished it with the light of his phone.
Listening 3 · A Crime Writer

Where a Twist Comes From

A writer from Germany explains how she found the idea for the twist in her first novel. Sound first — no text.

1. What was she doing when the idea came?
She was waiting for a train in Berlin.
2. What did she see?
A woman who was carrying two identical suitcases.
3. What does she do with every clue now?
She writes it on a card, so she remembers where it is in the story.
