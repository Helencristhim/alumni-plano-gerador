Lesson 21 -- Pre-class

The Offer Call -- Yes, Provided That...

Accepting a CFO offer and setting your conditions in the same sentence: two points to firm up, one middle ground, and everything in writing. Key words: a verbal offer, to put in writing, non-negotiable, leeway, middle ground, to meet halfway, to sweeten the deal, a non-compete clause, severance, to vest, contingent on, to firm up. Structure: conditional linkers (provided that, as long as, unless, on condition that, supposing).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These twelve are for the offer call: what is fixed, what can move, and what goes in the contract.

A verbal offer -- a job offer given by phone or in person, before the contract
“She called on Tuesday to make me a verbal offer.”
To put in writing -- to write an agreement down so that it is official
“Thank you. Could you put that in writing before Friday?”
Non-negotiable -- cannot be discussed or changed
“The reporting line is non-negotiable for me.”
Leeway -- freedom to change something, within limits
“There is not much leeway on the base salary.”
Middle ground -- a position between two sides that both can accept
“We found a middle ground on the start date.”
To meet halfway -- to give up part of what you want so that you both agree
“Could we meet halfway at eighteen months?”
To sweeten the deal -- to add something to make an offer more attractive
“To sweeten the deal, she added a sign-on bonus.”
A non-compete clause -- part of a contract that stops you working for a competitor for a time
“The non-compete clause in the contract was twenty-four months.”
Severance -- money paid when your job ends and it is not your fault
“The severance covers eighteen months of base salary.”
To vest -- of shares: to become fully yours after a period of time
“The shares vest over four years.”
Contingent on -- depending on something else happening first
“The bonus is contingent on company results.”
To firm up -- to make an arrangement final and definite
“Before I sign, I would like to firm up two points.”

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Two days after the last interview, the chief people officer called me with a verbal offer. I thanked her and said I was delighted. Then, before she put it in writing, I asked to firm up two points.

The base salary was fixed, so I did not push on it. I said I could accept it, provided that the bonus was guaranteed in the first year. She said that was contingent on the board, but she thought she could get it.

The second point was the non-compete clause. Twenty-four months was too long, and I told her that unless it was shorter, I would find it hard to sign. She said the chief executive saw it as non-negotiable, so we met halfway at eighteen months, as long as the severance covered the same period. I agreed to start in March, on condition that everything was in writing by Friday.

1. What did he do about the base salary?
A He accepted it, with a condition on the bonus.
B He asked for more, because the base salary was lower than in his current job.
C He refused it.
2. Why did he want a shorter non-compete clause?
A Because the chief executive asked him to propose a shorter one on the phone.
B Because twenty-four months was too long.
C Because of the bonus.
3. What did they agree on the non-compete?
A Twenty-four months, with no change.
B Twelve months, with no severance at all and a later start date.
C Eighteen months, as long as the severance covered the same period.
4. What was his condition for starting in March?
A Everything in writing by Friday.
B A bigger sign-on bonus and a new title, agreed by the board before Friday.
C A meeting with the board.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Yes, on One Condition

GRAMMAR

Provided (that), as long as and on condition that all mean only if. Unless means if ... not. Supposing means imagine that, and it often starts a question. After all of them, use the present, even when you talk about the future.

LinkerExample
provided (that)I could accept the base, provided that the bonus is guaranteed.
as long asEighteen months works, as long as the severance covers it.
on condition thatI can start in March, on condition that it’s all in writing.
unlessUnless the clause is shorter, I’d find it hard to sign.
supposingSupposing the board agrees, when could you start?
The trapsNever provided that it will be: use the present. Unless is already negative: unless it is shorter, not unless it isn’t shorter.

In an offer call, a condition sounds professional, and an ultimatum does not. Put could or would in the other half of the sentence to keep the tone open.

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

The chief people officer is on the phone with an offer. Choose the answer that works best.

“We’d like to make you an offer.” You start with:
A “Yes, of course, I accept everything, thank you so much for this opportunity.”
B “Thank you. Before you put it in writing, could we firm up two points?”
C “What is the salary?”
The base salary is fixed. You want a guaranteed bonus. You say:
A “I could accept the base, provided that the bonus is guaranteed in the first year.”
B “The base is too low, so I will only sign if you give me a much bigger bonus every year.”
C “Bonus, please.”
Which sentence is correct?
A “Unless the clause isn’t shorter, I can’t sign the contract this week.”
B “Provided that the bonus will be guaranteed, I can sign the contract.”
C “As long as the severance covers it, eighteen months works.”
She says the non-compete is non-negotiable. You:
A Say you will walk away immediately unless they remove the clause from the contract.
B Propose a middle ground, with a condition.
C Say nothing and sign.
At the end of the call, you want to protect what you agreed. You say:
A Nothing; you trust her, and you can always ask the headhunter about it later by email.
