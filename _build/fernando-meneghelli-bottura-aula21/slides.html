Chapter 3: The Code

Yes, on One Condition

provided that · as long as · unless · on condition that · supposing

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

“I could accept the base, provided that the bonus is guaranteed in the first year.”

“Eighteen months works, as long as the severance covers the same period.”

“Unless the non-compete is shorter, I’d find it hard to sign.”

“I can start on the first of March, on condition that it’s all in writing by Friday.”

“Supposing the board agrees, when could you start?”

Which words mean only if? Which one means if ... not? What tense comes after them?

The linkerIt meansIn the offer call
provided (that)only ifI could accept the base, provided that the bonus is guaranteed.
as long asonly if (more conversational)Eighteen months works, as long as the severance covers it.
on condition thatonly if (formal, firm)I can start in March, on condition that it’s in writing.
unlessif ... notUnless it’s shorter, I’d find it hard to sign.
supposingimagine that ... (often in a question)Supposing the board agrees, when could you start?
The trapsAfter the linker, use the present, even for the future: provided that the bonus is guaranteed, never will be. Unless is already negative: unless it is shorter, never unless it isn’t shorter. The other half of the sentence can use could or would to sound less demanding.

The Move

Thank, Condition, Write It Down

1. Thank and open

“Thank you. Before you put it in writing, could we firm up two points?”

2. The condition

“I could accept the base, provided that the bonus is guaranteed in the first year.”

3. Close in writing

“On condition that it’s all in writing by Friday, I can start on the first of March.”

Two points, each with a condition. Everything else stays flexible.

Chapter 4: The Call

We’d Like to Make You an Offer

The offer call, and two voices on how it goes well

Dialogue

Two Points to Firm Up

M
Fernando, I'm calling with good news. We'd like to make you a verbal offer, and I'll put it in writing tomorrow.
F
Thank you, Monica. I'm delighted. Before you send it, could we firm up two points?
M
Of course. The base salary is fixed, I'm afraid. There isn't much leeway there.
F
I understand. I could accept the base, provided that the bonus is guaranteed in the first year.
M
That's contingent on the board, but I think I can get it. What's the second point?
F
The non-compete clause. Twenty-four months is long. Unless it's shorter, I'd find it hard to sign.
M
The CEO sees that as non-negotiable. Could we meet halfway at eighteen months?
F
Eighteen months works, as long as the severance covers the same period.
M
That seems fair. Supposing the board agrees, when could you start?
F
On condition that it's all in writing by Friday, I can start on the first of March.
Comprehension

About the Conversation

1. When does Monica say she will put the offer in writing?
Tomorrow.
2. What does she say about the base salary?
It is fixed; there isn’t much leeway.
3. What does she propose for the non-compete clause?
To meet halfway at eighteen months.
Listening

Two Points, Not Ten

A chief people officer on the call after the offer. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she expect after a verbal offer?
A conversation, not just a yes.
2. What do the candidates who handle it well do?
They pick two points and attach a condition to each one.
3. What do the candidates who handle it badly do?
They say yes on the phone and then send ten changes by email.
Listening 2

The One Thing You Walk Away For

A CFO on his own offer. Sound first — no text.

1. What did he nearly do on the call?
He nearly accepted the offer immediately.
2. What was his condition?
That the shares vested over three years instead of five.
3. What did the chief executive add?
A small sign-on bonus that he had not asked for.

The Offer Letter

The Terms in Writing

Offer of Employment — Summary of Terms
Fernando Bottura · Chief Financial Officer
From: Monica, Chief People Officer

Reports to: Chief Executive Officer

Annual bonus: up to 40% of base, contingent on company results

Shares: vest over four years

Non-compete clause: 18 months

Severance: 18 months of base salary

Start date: March 1, provided that we receive your signed acceptance by Friday.

1. What is the bonus contingent on?
Company results.
2. Is the first-year bonus guaranteed in the letter?
No. It is still contingent on company results.
3. Tell Monica one condition before you sign.
I’m happy to sign, provided that the first-year bonus is guaranteed, as we agreed on the call.
