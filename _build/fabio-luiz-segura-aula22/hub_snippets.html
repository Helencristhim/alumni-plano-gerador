Lesson 22 -- Pre-class

The Suite Meeting

A private meeting with a vendor in a hotel suite: you set the agenda, push back without closing the door, and leave with one next step. Key words: term sheet, letter of intent, exclusivity clause, non-disclosure agreement, commercial terms, decision timeline, procurement process, strategic alignment, to nail down, to flag a concern. Structure: it-cleft sentences for emphasis.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A term sheet -- a short document that lists the main conditions of a deal, such as price and scope, before the real contract
"The CEO put a draft term sheet on the table, but I didn't sign anything in Las Vegas."
A letter of intent -- a document that shows serious interest in a deal, but is not a final contract
"A letter of intent is a good signal, but it doesn't commit the bank to anything."
An exclusivity clause -- a part of a contract that stops one side from working with competitors for a period of time
"The exclusivity clause asked us to test no other product for six months."
A non-disclosure agreement -- a contract in which both sides promise to keep the information they share secret
"Before they show us real numbers, we need a mutual non-disclosure agreement."
Commercial terms -- the money side of a deal: prices, fees, payment conditions and discounts
"The technology is strong. The commercial terms are what we still need to discuss."
A decision timeline -- the dates by which an organization plans to make each decision in a process
"Our decision timeline is about six months, and I always say that in the first meeting."
A procurement process -- the official steps a company follows to choose and approve a new supplier
"Every new vendor goes through our procurement process, with legal, risk and compliance."
Strategic alignment -- when two organizations want the same things for the long term, not only for one deal
"Before price, I want to check strategic alignment: do they want to be in Brazil in five years?"
To nail down -- to make a detail or an agreement final and certain
"I'd like to nail down the scope before we talk about rollout."
To flag a concern -- to tell people clearly that you see a possible problem, so they can discuss it
"I want to flag a concern about the exclusivity clause before we go any further."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At big conferences, vendors rent hotel suites for private meetings. The meeting is short, and the vendor usually arrives with paper: a non-disclosure agreement, a letter of intent, sometimes a full term sheet. A good banker reads everything and signs nothing in the room.

To control the meeting, you need to put a spotlight on what matters. In lesson 18 you used one spotlight: What worries me is the timeline. Today you learn a second one, which starts with It: It's the timeline that worries me. The part right after It's gets the spotlight. Then comes that and the rest of the sentence.

You can use it in the past: It was your demo that convinced my team. With a person, you can use who: It's our compliance team who has the final word. And it is perfect to correct a wrong idea politely: It isn't the price that worries us. It's the exclusivity clause.

The vendor hears exactly where the problem is, and the conversation does not become a fight. That is how you flag a concern and still nail down a next step.

1. According to the text, what does a good banker do with the paper the vendor brings?

2. In "It's the timeline that worries me", which part gets the spotlight?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- It-Cleft Sentences

GRAMMAR

It + be + the spotlight + that + the rest.

UseFormExample
Spotlight on a thingIt's + focus + that + restIt's the timeline that worries me.
Spotlight in the pastIt was + focus + that + restIt was your demo that convinced us.
Spotlight on a personIt's + person + who / thatIt's our compliance team who signs off.
Correct a wrong ideaIt isn't X that... It's Y.It isn't the price that worries us. It's the exclusivity clause.
Compare: the wh- spotlightWhat + clause + is + focusWhat worries me is the timeline.

Never: Is the timeline that worries me. · It's the timeline what worries me. · It were your demo that convinced us.

Stage 2: Put the Suite Meeting in Order

Order

You are leading a thirty-minute meeting with a vendor. Put your sentences in a logical order, from the opening to the next step.

?"Let me push back gently on the timeline. Ninety days isn't realistic for us."
?"So let's nail down the next step: a mutual NDA this week, and a call with our procurement team."
?"Thanks for making the time. Before we get into the details, let me share where we are as an institution."
?"That's helpful. Could you walk me through how it connects to legacy systems?"
?"And I want to flag a concern: it's the exclusivity clause that worries our legal team."
?"Today I'd like to understand your solution and agree on one next step."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that keeps the meeting in your hands.

The vendor's CEO starts with twenty slides. You say:
A Wonderful, please show me all the slides, and then we can talk about the price and the contract.
B Stop.
C Before we get into the details, let me share where we are as an institution.
The vendor thinks you are worried about the price. You are worried about exclusivity. You say:
A It's the exclusivity clause that worries us, not the price.
B Is the exclusivity that worries us.
C The price is OK and we have no other problems with the term sheet, so we can sign today.
The CEO asks you to sign a letter of intent today. You say:
A Of course, give me a pen.
B I can't commit today. Every new vendor goes through our procurement process.
C A letter of intent is a final contract, so I need my lawyer here in Las Vegas.
The meeting is ending. You say:
A Thanks, bye.
B We will think about everything and maybe call you next year, or maybe in two years, depending on the market.
C Let's nail down the next step: a mutual NDA this week.
