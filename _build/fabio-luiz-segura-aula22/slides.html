Chapter 4: The Spotlight

It's the Timeline That Worries Me

A second way to put the important part first

Grammar Discovery

Where Is the Spotlight?

"It's the timeline that worries me."

"It was your demo that convinced my team."

"It isn't the price that worries us. It's the exclusivity clause."

"It's our compliance team who has the final word."

How do all four sentences start? Which words get the spotlight, and what comes right after them?

UseFormExample
Spotlight on a thingIt's + focus + that + restIt's the timeline that worries me.
Spotlight in the pastIt was + focus + that + restIt was your demo that convinced us.
Spotlight on a personIt's + person + who / that + restIt's our compliance team who has the final word.
Correct a wrong ideaIt isn't X that... It's Y.It isn't the price that worries us. It's the exclusivity clause.
Review: the wh- spotlight (lesson 18)What + clause + is + focusWhat worries me is the timeline.
The trapnever drop It · never what after the focusNot Is the timeline that... or It's the timeline what...

In one line: It + be + the part you want to spotlight + that + the rest of the sentence.

Grammar Practice

Move the Spotlight

Say the full sentence first, then click to check

“The timeline worries me. (spotlight: the timeline) ___It's the timeline that worries me.”
“Your demo convinced my team. (past, spotlight: your demo) ___It was your demo that convinced my team.”
“Our compliance team signs off on new vendors. (spotlight: the compliance team) ___It's our compliance team that signs off on new vendors.”
“The vendor thinks the price is the problem. The real problem is the exclusivity clause. ___It isn't the price that's the problem. It's the exclusivity clause.”
“We need the security review first. (spotlight: the security review) ___It's the security review that we need first.”
“Integration with our legacy system is the real question. (spotlight: integration) ___It's the integration with our legacy system that's the real question.”

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Two Voices Before the Meeting

A buyer who never signs in Las Vegas, and an invitation

Listening 1 · The Buyer

Never Sign in a Suite

A procurement director at a European bank explains his rules. Sound first — no text.

1. What is his rule about signing?
He never signs anything in a hotel suite.
2. How does he open a suite meeting?
He opens it himself: he says who they are, where they are in the decision, and what he needs to learn in thirty minutes.
3. What does he say usually kills a deal, and what is a normal decision timeline for his bank?
The exclusivity clause, not the price. Six months is a normal decision timeline for them.
Listening 2 · The Invitation

A Message from Dana

The vendor from lesson 21 leaves a voice message. Sound first — no text.

1. Who is in town, and until when?
Their CEO, until Thursday.
2. What does Dana want to talk about, and what does she ask Fabio to do before the meeting?
A proof of concept with Bradesco. She asks him to talk to his legal team about a mutual NDA.
3. What will the CEO probably bring to the meeting?
A draft term sheet.
Real Document

The Draft Term Sheet

Draft term sheet · Proof of concept
Northbridge Payments (fictional) and Bradesco · Not binding · For discussion only

Scope: ISO 20022 translation layer for two corridors, Brazil–US and Brazil–Mexico

Duration: 90 days

Commercial terms: USD 120,000 for the proof of concept

Exclusivity: the bank will not test competing products for 6 months

Decision: the bank confirms go or no-go within 30 days of the end of the test

Illustrative document, written for this lesson. The company is fictional.
1. Which item would you flag as a concern? Say it with It's... that.
"It's the exclusivity clause that concerns us. Six months without testing other products is not possible for a bank our size."
2. Which item does not match a real bank decision timeline, and why?
The decision in 30 days. Every new supplier goes through the procurement process, and that usually takes months.
3. Nail down one next step before the meeting ends.
"Let's nail down the next step: a mutual NDA this week, and a call with our procurement team before the end of the month."
