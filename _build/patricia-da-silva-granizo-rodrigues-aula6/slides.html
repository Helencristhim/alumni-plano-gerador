Chapter 4: The Code

Somebody Else's Words, in Your Sentence

What happens to a verb when it is reported an hour later

Grammar Discovery

He Said, She Told Me

"He said the report had been late for eight months."

"She told me the deadline had changed back in May."

"He asked what the real deadline was."

"She said she would document what they had agreed."

The four originals were spoken in the present. Look at what happened to the verbs and to the word order. What are the two rules?

FormWhat it doesExample
said (that) + backshiftthe present of the original words becomes past when you report laterHe said the report had been late for months.
told + persontold needs an object, said does notShe told me the deadline had changed.
questions lose the inversiona reported question is a statement: no inversion, no question markHe asked what the real deadline was.
would, could, might do not shiftmodals that are already past-marked stay as they areShe said she would document it.
what does not have to shifta situation that is still true, or a standing rule, can stay in the present: he said the deadline is Friday. Both are correct; the present sounds more current.

The most common mistake at your level is not the backshift, it is said me. Told takes a person, said does not. Everything else you already know.

Chapter 5: The Ear

Two People Who Write These For a Living

An investigator and a director who has to deliver the summary upwards

Listening 1

What Has to Be in the Note

An HR investigator, briefing a new team. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What are the three things he says people confuse?
An allegation, which is what somebody claims; hearsay, which is what somebody was told by a third person and did not witness; and a finding, which is what you concluded and can support.
2. What is his example of a sentence he can defend, and one that will cost the company?
He said he had not been told the deadline is defensible. He clearly did not care about the deadline is not.
3. What does he say about a point that was not substantiated?
Write that it was not substantiated. A note that substantiates everything is a note nobody believes.
Listening 2

Two Minutes, Three Versions of Events

A director on how she delivers a summary upwards. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What did she used to do, and why did it not work?
She used to tell it chronologically, and by minute two nobody could hold three timelines in their head.
2. What does she start with now, and what does that achieve?
She starts with what is not in dispute -- that all three agree the meeting happened on the fourteenth and lasted about forty minutes. It takes fifteen seconds and steadies the room.
3. What does she say happens if you start with the disagreement?
Everybody picks a side before they have the facts, and you spend the rest of the meeting arguing with people who chose in the first ten seconds.

Chapter 6: Your Turn

The Part Where You Talk

Somebody else's words, delivered in yours, three times

Guided

Relay Four Statements

Task: Your teacher reads four short statements from four different people. Report all four back, using said, told, asked and a modal. Then do it again with the frames covered.

Support:

He said (that)...She told me...He asked what/whether...She said she would...All three agree that...
Semi-free

Two Minutes to the Executive Team

Task: Deliver the summary from the reading upwards, in two minutes. Start with what is not in dispute, then the divergences one sentence each, then what was and was not substantiated. You may not use the words obviously, clearly or of course.

Support:

All three agree that...Where they diverge is...Two of the three points were substantiated...One was not, because...What I would recommend is...
Free

Your Own Case, No Notes

Task: Take a real situation at JEM System where two people described the same event differently. Report it out loud in two minutes, without naming anybody. Your teacher will interrupt to ask did they say that, or is that your reading of it?

No support. Target: every claim marked as said, told, or concluded -- and at least one thing you cannot substantiate, named as such.
