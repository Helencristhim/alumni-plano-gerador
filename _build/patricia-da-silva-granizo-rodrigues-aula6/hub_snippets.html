Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

Telling the Full Story

The ten terms that decide whether a written record protects you or exposes you, and what happens to a verb when somebody else's words become your sentence. Key words: to flag, to escalate, to walk through, grievance, allegation, to substantiate, findings, to look into, hearsay, on the record. Structure: reported speech.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read it inside a real sentence. Several of these have a legal weight in the United States that they do not have in Portuguese.

To flag -- to bring something to attention early, before it becomes a problem
"I flagged the delay in June and nothing happened until September."
To escalate -- to move a problem up to somebody more senior
"She escalated it to the VP only after two written warnings."
To walk through -- to explain something to somebody step by step
"Let me walk you through what each person told us."
Grievance -- a formal complaint made by an employee
"The grievance was filed on a Monday and acknowledged the same day."
Allegation -- a statement that somebody did something wrong, not yet proven
"An allegation is not a finding, and the note must keep them apart."
To substantiate -- to support a claim with evidence
"Two of the three points were substantiated and one was not."
Findings -- what an investigation concludes, in writing
"The findings go to both parties on the same day, never to one first."
To look into -- to investigate something, usually informally at first
"I said I would look into it and come back within a week."
Hearsay -- something you were told by somebody else and did not see yourself
"Half of what the team reported was hearsay and could not be used."
On the record -- said in a way that can be quoted and attributed to the speaker
"He was willing to say it privately but not on the record."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

The manager said he had not been told that Friday was the deadline. His supervisor told us the change had been communicated by radio in May, but said she had no record of either conversation. A third colleague told the investigator that she had not been in the room, and that everything she knew had come from a conversation in the corridor.

The investigator asked what the real deadline was and asked whether anybody had written it down. Nobody had. She said she would look into the radio log and said she would report back within a week. Two of the three points were substantiated. The third rested on hearsay and was not.

1. He said he had not been told that Friday was the deadline. Why is this safer to write than he clearly did not care about the deadline?
A Because it is shorter.
B Because it reports what he said, which can be defended, instead of interpreting him, which cannot.
C Because the passive is always safer in a report.
2. She told us the change had been communicated. Why told us and not said us?
A Because tell takes a person as its object and say does not.
B Because said can only be used in the past.
C Because told is more formal.
3. She asked what the real deadline was. What has happened to the word order?
A Nothing -- reported questions keep the question order.
B The inversion has gone: the reported question is written as a statement.
C The subject and the object have swapped places.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

You know this structure. What is missing is the automatism, and one detail that catches almost every Brazilian speaker at B2: said does not take a person, told does.

FormWhat it doesExample
said (that) + backshiftthe present of the original words becomes past when you report laterHe said the report had been late for months.
told + persontold needs an object, said does notShe told me the deadline had changed.
questions lose the inversiona reported question is a statement: no inversion, no question markHe asked what the real deadline was.
would, could, might do not shiftmodals that are already past-marked stay as they areShe said she would document it.
what does not have to shifta situation that is still true, or a standing rule, can stay in the present: he said the deadline is Friday. Both are correct; the present sounds more current.

Not everything has to shift. If the situation is still true -- he said the deadline is Friday -- the present is correct and sounds more current. Both forms are right; choose on purpose.

Stage 2: Build the Two-Minute Summary

Order

The order that stops the room choosing a side in the first ten seconds.

?Where they diverge is whether the change was ever communicated.
?All three agree that the meeting happened and that it lasted about forty minutes.
?What I would recommend is that the shift change goes in writing from now on.
?He said he had not been told; she said she had no record of either conversation.
?Two of the three points were substantiated, and the third rested on hearsay.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

One right answer each. Read the situation before you look at the options.

You are writing the record. A manager stated that he was never informed. How do you write it?
A He was clearly not informed.
B He said he had not been informed.
C He said me he was not informed.
D Apparently nobody informed him.
A colleague repeats something she heard in the corridor from a third person. What is that, and what do you do with it?
A It is a finding, and it goes in the conclusions.
B It is hearsay, and it is named as such and kept out of the findings.
C It is an allegation, and it is treated as proven.
D It is evidence, as long as she is willing to repeat it.
One of three points cannot be supported by evidence. What goes in the note?
A Nothing -- leave it out to keep the note clean.
B That the point was not substantiated.
C That the point is probably true but hard to prove.
D That the investigation was inconclusive overall.
You have two minutes with the executive team and three conflicting accounts. Where do you start?
A With the earliest event, and then forward in time.
B With what all three agree on.
C With the account you find most credible.
D With your recommendation, so that nobody argues.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record your answer. Your teacher will ask you about this one at the start of the class.

Record about ninety seconds. Take a real situation where two people described the same event differently -- do not name anybody. Report it: what is not in dispute, where the accounts diverge, and what you could and could not substantiate. Use said, told and asked at least once each. Do not read -- speak.
