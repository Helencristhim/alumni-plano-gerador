Chapter 3: Then and No Longer

The Form That Says It Is Over

Two words, and everybody knows you were there

Grammar Discovery

What Do Three of These Share?

"We used to do the whole filing by hand."

"There used to be three different systems in that group."

"Nobody used to ask about the audit trail. Now it is the first question."

"I am used to travelling every month now."

The three red ones say the same thing: this was true then, and it is not true now. The green one looks almost identical and means something completely different — and it is about today.

JobFormExample

Negativedid not use to (no d)Nobody used to ask · It did not use to matter.

The one that looks the sameto be used to + ingI am used to travelling = it is normal for me now.

In one line: used to says it is over. It is the cheapest way to show you were in this market before the person you are talking to.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Explaining It to Somebody Who Just Arrived

One conversation, two recordings, one notice

Dialogue

You Were Here Before

C
Can I ask you something. I came into this market two years ago. What was it like before?
A
Completely different. Back in the day the whole process was manual. There used to be a room with the returns in boxes, and two people whose entire year was that room.
C
And software did not solve it?
A
Software existed. Nobody used to buy it for compliance, because compliance was not something you bought. What changed was the regulator. They tightened the regulation and started asking for the trail.
C
So the question changed, not the technology.
A
Exactly. It did not use to matter who touched a number. Now that is the whole question. And every group I sell to still has a legacy system somewhere doing something important.
C
Do they know which one?
A
Half of them do not, and that is usually where the conversation starts. Nothing is broken — it just became outdated while it was still working. And you, what did the market look like where you came from?
Comprehension

About the Conversation

1. How does Chen open, and what does he admit about himself?
He asks whether he can ask something, and admits that he came into this market only two years ago.
2. What conclusion does Chen reach halfway through, in his own words?
That the question changed, not the technology.
3. Nowhere in this conversation does anybody say "I have twenty years of experience". So where does the authority come from?
From being able to describe what the market used to be like. Showing what changed proves you were there; saying you were there does not.
Listening 1 · A Sales Director

It Used to Be a Filing Job

Somebody describing the same market twenty years ago. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say the job used to be, and what was in the room he describes?
It used to be a filing job. There was an actual room with the returns in boxes, and two people whose entire year was that room.
2. Why does he say nobody used to buy software for compliance?
Because compliance was not something you bought — it was something you employed people to do.
3. What actually changed, according to him, and what is the question now?
Not the technology, which had been there for years, but the regulator tightening the rules and asking for the trail. The question stopped being can you file it and became can you prove who touched it.
Listening 2 · A Product Director

We Were the Benchmark

How fast a company stops being the one everybody copies. Sound first — no text.

1. How long were they the benchmark, and how long did it take to lose that?
About six years as the benchmark, and eighteen months to become the ones lagging behind.
2. Who noticed first, and how?
Nobody inside the building noticed until a client said it out loud in a meeting.
3. What does she say about how companies fall behind, and why did the overhaul take twice as long?
You do not fall behind by standing still: the rules move while you are doing exactly what worked. The overhaul took two years instead of one because they spent the first six months arguing about whether they needed it.

What Changed, in Two Columns

Read down the middle column and you are describing a world that no longer exists. Read across any single row and you have one sentence that explains, to somebody who arrived last year, why this market buys software now and did not use to.
1. Which row on this table explains why the audit trail became the first question clients ask?
The "who signed" row: it used to be unrecorded, and now it has to be named with a timestamp. That is what an audit trail is for.
2. Which row would worry a finance director most, and why?
The last one. A warning letter costs nothing; a penalty per day turns a small delay into a number the board can see.
3. The middle column describes a world that no longer exists. What does that tell you about how to talk about it?
Every sentence about it needs a form that says it is over — used to. Saying it in the present would make it sound like it is still the rule.

Then and Now

Four Sentences Only You Can Say

1. filing: once a year, on paper → monthly, digital
"Companies used to file once a year, on paper. Now it is monthly and digital only."
2. who signed: nobody recorded it → named, with a timestamp
"It did not use to matter who signed a correction. Now every change is named and timestamped."
3. compliance: people did it → companies buy it
"Nobody used to buy compliance. You employed people to do it."
4. being late: a warning letter → a penalty per day
"There used to be a warning letter. Now there is a penalty for every day you are late."
