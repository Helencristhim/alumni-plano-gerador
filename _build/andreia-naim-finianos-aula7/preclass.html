Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

Used to vs. Now -- How Your Market Changed

Telling somebody who arrived last year what this market was like before they got here. Key words: a legacy system, to phase out, manual, a regulation, to tighten, an overhaul, a benchmark, to lag behind, outdated, to keep up with, back in the day, to move the goalposts. Structure: used to + infinitive, and the difference from being used to something.

Stage 1: Words · 2 min

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A legacy system -- an old system a company still depends on, because replacing it is hard
"Every client still has a legacy system doing something important."
To phase out -- to stop using something gradually, over months, rather than all at once
"They are phasing out the old module, one country at a time."
Manual -- done by a person, by hand, rather than by a machine
"The whole process used to be manual, and two people did nothing else."
A regulation -- an official rule that companies have to follow, written by an authority
"The regulation changed twice in one year and nobody was ready."
To tighten -- to make a rule stricter, so that less is allowed than before
"The regulator tightened the rules and started asking for the trail."
An overhaul -- a complete change of a system, not a repair to the one that is there
"The overhaul took two years and it should have taken one."
A benchmark -- the standard that everybody else in the market is measured against
"For six years we were the benchmark, and then we were not."
To lag behind -- to be slower than the others, and fall further back while everybody moves
"In eighteen months we were the ones lagging behind."
Outdated -- no longer good enough, because everything around it has moved on
"Nothing broke. The system just became outdated while it was still working."
To keep up with -- to move as fast as something that keeps changing, and not fall behind it
"Nobody can keep up with the regulation on their own any more."
Back in the day -- in the past, when things were done in a completely different way
"Back in the day there was a room with the returns in boxes."
To move the goalposts -- to change the rules while somebody is still working to the old ones
"They moved the goalposts twice in one year."

Stage 3: Grammar Tip -- Used To · 2 min

GRAMMAR

Read the rule, then answer three quick questions.

JobFormExample
A habit that is overused to + plain verbWe used to do it by hand.
A situation that is overthere used to beThere used to be three systems.
Negativedid not use to (no d)It did not use to matter.
Questiondid you use to ...?Did you use to file it manually?
Only the past, never the presentfor a habit now: usuallyI usually travel in October — not I use to travel.
The one that looks the sameto be used to + ingI am used to travelling = it is normal for me now.
The traptwo meanings, one word apartI used to travel (not any more) vs I am used to travelling (today).

Never: I use to travel twice a year (meaning now) · It did not used to matter · I used to travelling every month · There used to have three systems

1. "There used to be a room with the returns in boxes." Is the room there now?
A Yes, and it is still used every year.
B No. Used to says the situation was true then and is not true now.
C The text does not say.
2. "It did not use to matter who touched a number." Why is there no letter d in use?
A Because it is a spelling mistake.
B Because the sentence is negative and negatives never take d.
C Because did already carries the past, so the verb goes back to its plain form.
3. The last two sentences are "It is a market I am used to now. It is not the one I started in." What is the difference between am used to and used to?
A I am used to it means it is normal for me today. I used to do it means I did it before and I do not now.
B There is none, they are two ways of writing the same thing.
C Am used to is the plural form.
