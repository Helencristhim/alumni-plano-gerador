Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

The Opinion You Sign -- Advising a Client

A client needs a recommendation, with the risks and the alternatives. Key words: to consider, to avoid, a legal opinion, a risk assessment, to justify, a trade-off, a feasibility study, to postpone, a safeguard, an alternative. Structure: verbs followed by gerund or infinitive.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To consider -- to think carefully about something before you decide
"We should consider waiting until the rule is published."
To avoid -- to stay away from something, or to try not to do it
"I advise you to avoid signing before the audit."
A legal opinion -- a written document in which a lawyer gives professional advice
"The fund asked for a legal opinion on the contract."
A risk assessment -- a study of the possible risks of a decision or a project
"The risk assessment shows three main problems."
To justify -- to give good reasons for a decision
"How can we justify paying a higher price?"
A trade-off -- a situation where you accept losing one thing to gain another
"The trade-off is a lower return for a lower risk."
A feasibility study -- a study that checks if a project is possible and worth doing
"The feasibility study will take four months."
To postpone -- to move an event or a decision to a later date
"The board decided to postpone the vote."
A safeguard -- a rule or clause that protects somebody from a risk
"The contract needs a safeguard against tariff changes."
An alternative -- another possible choice or option
"The alternative is to enter the project in the second phase."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Hannah's company wants to bid for a port concession. Her lawyer, Aguinaldo, recommends waiting for the final rule, because the regulator plans to change the tariff.

Hannah's board does not want to wait. So Aguinaldo suggests a trade-off: the company can bid now, but it should avoid accepting the current tariff clause and ask for a safeguard. In his legal opinion, he also advises the board to consider an alternative: entering in the second phase.

In the end, the board decides to bid and agrees to include the safeguard. Notice the pattern: after recommend, avoid and consider, the next verb takes -ing; after plan, decide and agree, it takes to.

1. What does Aguinaldo recommend?
A He recommends to wait for the final rule.
B He recommends waiting for the final rule.
C He recommends that the company never bids.
2. What trade-off does he suggest?
A Bidding now, but avoiding the current tariff clause and asking for a safeguard.
B Not bidding at all.
C Accepting all the clauses to win the tender.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "The board decided bidding."
B "The board decided to bid."
C "The board decided bid."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Gerund or Infinitive After Verbs of Advice

GRAMMAR

recommend, suggest, consider and avoid + -ing. decide, agree, plan, refuse and need + to. After a preposition, always -ing.

JobFormExample
Verbs + -ingrecommend, suggest, consider, avoid, risk, involveI recommend waiting. / We considered selling.
Verbs + todecide, agree, plan, expect, refuse, fail, needThe board decided to wait. / They refused to sign.
Advise + person + toadvise / ask / tell somebody to doI advise you to avoid signing now.
After a preposition, always -ingbefore, after, without, instead of + -ingDo not sign before reading the clause.
A verb as the subject-ing at the start of the sentencePostponing the vote is the safest option.
The traprecommend never takes toNot I recommend to wait. Say I recommend waiting.

Never: I recommend to wait · The board decided postponing · You should avoid to sign · before to read the clause

Stage 2: Put the Recommendation in Order

Order

A client asks for your advice before a board meeting. Put your answer in a logical order.

?"The project is feasible, and the traffic study is solid."
?"But you should avoid accepting the current tariff clause."
