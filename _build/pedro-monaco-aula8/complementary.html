Lesson 8 -- A Judgment in Plain English

Grammar Video
"How to use reported speech" — 6 Minute Grammar, BBC Learning English

Six minutes on the backshift, the pronouns and the time words that move when you report what somebody said. Connection to Lesson 8: this is the mechanical half of the lesson — the half that has rules you can simply learn.

Tip: after each example, report the same sentence twice — once as if it were finished, once as if it were still true. The second version is the one that matters when you summarise a ruling.

Podcast
"Understanding what the Supreme Court did" — The NPR Politics Podcast

NPR's legal correspondents explain a major decision about who has the final say over federal regulation, in ordinary spoken American English, to an audience of non-lawyers. Connection to Lesson 8: it is a live demonstration of reporting a judgment without breaking it.

Tip: write down every reporting verb they use — said, held, found, argued, concluded. Then ask yourself which of them you would have used, and whether yours would have been stronger.

Reading
"Supreme Court limits scope of environmental review" — SCOTUSblog

A short plain-language summary of a unanimous decision, written by lawyers for a general audience. Connection to Lesson 8: it is the finished version of what this lesson asks you to do — ninety pages reported in a few hundred words without a single upgraded verb.

Tip: read it once, then write your own three-sentence version: who held what, who disagreed, and what the decision does NOT decide. Compare the verbs you chose with the ones in the article.
