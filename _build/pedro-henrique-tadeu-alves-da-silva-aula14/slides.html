Listening 1 · Where Is the Singer?

A Hundred and Four Tracks

A mixing engineer on the session she had to clean up. Sound first — no text.

1. What was wrong with the session the band sent?
It had a hundred and four tracks: fourteen layers of backing vocals, a lot of synths and so much reverb that the chorus sounded far away.
2. What did she do first, and how did she decide what stayed?
She muted everything except the drums, the bass and the lead vocal. Then she added the other parts back one by one: if a part made the song better, it stayed.
3. How many tracks did they keep, and how did the singer react?
About twenty. The singer said it was the first time she could hear her own voice.
Listening 2 · One Voice

The Producers Always Want a Little More

The music director of a live singing show on one difficult week. Sound first — no text.

1. What do the producers always want, according to him?
A little more: more dancers, more fire, more key changes.
2. What happened to the young singer in rehearsal?
She was given a huge arrangement with a choir and an orchestra on tape, and nobody could hear her.
3. What did they keep, and what did he learn?
The piano, a few strings and her voice. He learned that the audience does not count how many things are on the stage: they remember one voice.
