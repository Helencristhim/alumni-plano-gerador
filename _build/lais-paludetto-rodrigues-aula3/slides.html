Chapter 3: The Code

Free, Obliged or Forbidden?

Four small words, three very different lives

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"You have to be home by ten on school nights."

"You don't have to eat dinner with us every night."

"You mustn't use the washing machine after nine."

"Am I allowed to invite a friend over?"

Two of these say no. One says you choose. Which is which?

FormMeaningExample
have to / has toObligation that comes from outside you: a rule, a school, a family.I have to be home by ten.
don't have toNo obligation at all. It is optional, and nobody minds either way.You don't have to cook tonight.
mustn'tForbidden. The opposite of the line above, not a softer version of it.You mustn't smoke in the house.
be allowed toPermission, seen from the person who receives it. Very common in questions.Am I allowed to have friends over?
can / couldCan I is normal and friendly. Could I is softer and safer with somebody you have just met. Would it be okay if I... is the softest of all, for the difficult ones.
The trapdon't have to and mustn't are OPPOSITES. "You don't have to come" means you are free. "You mustn't come" means do not come.

When you ask for something big, go one step softer than you think you need. Nobody has ever been annoyed by a polite question.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I must to be home by ten."
"I have to be home by ten."
"I don't can use the machine at night."
"I can't use the machine at night."
"I have not the permission to go out."
"I'm not allowed to go out."

After must and can the verb goes straight in, with no to. Their negatives are mustn't and can't, never don't must or don't can. And for permission, English says I'm allowed to, not I have the permission.

Detail

How Big Is the Favour?

Small ask → big ask. Go one step softer than you think you need.

Small: pass me the salt — "___Can you pass me the salt?"
Medium: borrow an umbrella — "___Could I borrow an umbrella?"
Big: come home two hours late — "___Would it be okay if I got home a bit later?"
Saying no: they offer more food — "___I'm good, thank you, it was delicious."
Chapter 4: The Kitchen Table

Half an Hour Past Curfew

Wednesday evening, and you need something

Dialogue

Asking Without Arguing

L
Greg, can I bring something up before dinner?
G
Of course. Is everything okay?
L
Everything's fine. The school play is on Wednesday and it finishes at half past ten, so I'd be half an hour past my curfew.
G
Wednesday is a school night, so ten is the rule. But a school event is different. How are you getting home?
L
Emma's mum is driving three of us. Would it be okay if I got home around eleven, just this once?
G
That works. You don't have to ask twice for school things, honestly. You just have to tell us before, not after.
L
Got it. I'll text you when we leave the school. And I'll tidy up the kitchen tonight, since I'm out on Wednesday.
G
Now you're just being considerate to get out of the dishes. It's working.
Comprehension

About Greg

1. What is the rule on school nights, according to Greg?
Ten o'clock, because Wednesday is a school night.
2. What does he ask before he says yes?
How she is getting home.
3. What does he say she really has to do?
Tell them before, not after — she does not have to ask twice for school events.
Listening

The House Tour, Part Two

Greg explains how the house works. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the one rule Greg says he really cares about?
Writing it on the board by the fridge when you take the last of something, like the milk.
2. What are the two curfew times?
Ten on school nights, midnight on Fridays and Saturdays.
3. Why is the washing machine forbidden after nine?
The wall is thin and his wife goes to bed early.
Listening 2

A Note from Your Host Mum

Maya is at work until six and leaves you a message. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say about cooking tonight?
She does not have to cook — there are leftovers in the blue container.
2. What is the rule about inviting friends over?
She is allowed to, but she should tell one of them in the morning, not when the friends are at the door.
3. What does Maya ask her to do on Sundays?
Put her laundry basket outside her room, because Maya does a big wash on Sunday evenings.
