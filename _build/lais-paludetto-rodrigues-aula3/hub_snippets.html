"There are five people in this household, plus a very loud dog."
To tidy up -- to put a room back in order
"I'll tidy up the kitchen after we finish."
Leftovers -- the food left after a meal, kept for later
"There are leftovers in the blue container if you get hungry."
To get along with -- to have a good, easy relationship with someone
"I get along with my host sister better than with my own."
To bring something up -- to start talking about a subject on purpose
"Can I bring something up about Tuesday evenings?"
Considerate -- thinking about how your actions affect other people
"Texting when you are going to be late is just being considerate."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Nobody hands you a list. In my household in Burnaby the rules live on a small board next to the fridge, and half of them are not written anywhere. I have to be home by ten on school nights and by midnight on Fridays. I don't have to eat dinner with them every evening, but they sit down together at seven and it is the best half hour of the day. I mustn't use the washing machine after nine, because the wall is thin and Maya goes to bed early.

The chores took me one week to understand. Mine are the dishes on Tuesdays and the laundry on Sundays, and if I tidy up the kitchen without being asked, nobody says anything, but the whole evening is lighter. Am I allowed to invite friends over? Yes, but I have to say it in the morning, not when they are already standing at the door.

The thing nobody tells you is that the hard part is not the rules. It is bringing something up when it does not work for you. On Tuesdays my family in Sao Paulo calls at seven, exactly at dinner time. I did not say anything for two weeks. When I finally asked, Greg said: just tell us before, not after. Being considerate is not obeying everything. It is saying the thing early, while it is still small.

"I don't have to eat dinner with them every evening" means:
A It is forbidden for her to eat with them.
B She is free to choose — nobody minds either way.
C She must eat with them every evening.
"I mustn't use the washing machine after nine" means:
A It is not necessary, but she can if she wants.
B She usually forgets to use it.
C It is forbidden.
According to the last paragraph, what is the hardest part of living with a host family?
A Learning the rules.
B Doing the chores.
C Saying early that something does not work for you.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Free, Obliged or Forbidden?

GRAMMAR

Four small words decide three very different situations. Two of them look similar and mean the opposite.

FormUseExample
have to / has toObligation that comes from outside you: a rule, a school, a family.I have to be home by ten.
don't have toNo obligation at all. Optional, and nobody minds either way.You don't have to cook tonight.
mustn'tForbidden. The opposite of the line above, not a softer version of it.You mustn't use the machine after nine.
be allowed toPermission, seen from the person who receives it. Very common in questions.Am I allowed to have friends over?
can / could / would it be okay ifThree levels of politeness for the same request. The bigger the favour, the softer the form.
The trapdon't have to and mustn't are opposites. "You don't have to come" means you are free. "You mustn't come" means do not come.

After must and can the verb goes straight in, with no to. And English says I'm allowed to, never I have the permission.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are living with your host family. Choose the best thing to say.

You want to come home an hour after your curfew because of a school event. You say:
A "I will arrive late today."
B "The school play finishes at half past ten. Would it be okay if I got home around eleven?"
C "I must to stay until eleven."
Your host mum offers you more food and you are full. You say:
A "No."
B "I'm good, thank you. It was delicious."
C "I don't want more, thanks, I am very full of this."
You want to know whether you can have friends over. You ask:
A "Am I allowed to invite a friend over on Saturday?"
B "I have the permission of invite friends?"
C "I must invite friends on Saturday?"
Something in the routine does not work for you and it has bothered you for two weeks. The best move is:
A Say nothing and hope it changes.
B Wait until you are annoyed and then explain everything at once.
C Say "Can I bring something up?" and explain it while it is still small.
