Chapter 3 -- The Code

The Deal That Did Not Happen

If we had... we would have...

Discovery

Four Sentences, One Deal That Never Was

All four describe the same negotiation. What do the highlighted words have in common?

"If we had confirmed the specs in March, we would have held the old price."

"If they had not undercut us, we would not have moved at all."

"We would have walked away if the markup had been any thinner."

"Would you have accepted the ballpark if I had sent it on Monday?"

PartFormExample
the conditionif + had + past participle"If we had confirmed the specs..."
the resultwould have + past participle"...we would have held the old price."
negativehadn't / wouldn't have"We wouldn't have moved at all."
questionWould + subject + have + participle"Would you have accepted it?"
either orderresult first = no comma"We would have walked away if the markup had been thinner."

Key idea: this is the only conditional about a past that is closed. Compare the three you now have: "If we lower the price, we will win it" (still open) · "If we lowered the price, we would win it" (imaginary, today) · "If we had lowered the price, we would have won it" (too late, it is over). Two traps: would never goes after if, and in speech "we'd have held" is would, not had.

Practice

Build the Third Conditional

Say the whole sentence first, then click to check

"If they had not undercut us, we ___would not have moved at all." (move, negative)
"I would have called you first if I ___had known about the deadlock." (know)
"___Would you have accepted the ballpark on Monday?" (make it a question)
"We would have shaved two days off if you ___had told us before Friday." (tell)
The Structure

Three Jobs at the Table

The same form, doing three very different things.

1 · Explain a price without blaming anyone

"If the order had landed in March, you would have paid the February number."

The cause is a date, not a person. Nobody has to defend themselves.

2 · Show how close you came to walking away

"We would have walked away if the markup had been any thinner."

Said calmly, in the past, this is leverage. Said as a threat, it is not.

3 · Turn one bad round into next year's agreement

"If we had had a rolling agreement, the April move would have been shared."

The closed past becomes the argument for an open future.

Notice what is missing: "you should have". The third conditional says what the situation caused, not who failed.

1. Which question does he say buyers always get wrong, and what is the better one?
They ask WHY the price went up. The better question is WHEN it went up -- the move is almost never from this week.
2. What would have happened if the purchase order had landed in March?
They would have paid the February number, and nobody would be on that call.
3. What actually moved the price, and by how much?
The raw material moved eleven percent in April.
4. What does he want the buyer to push for instead of only the number?
A rolling agreement, so that a price move is shared between the two sides instead of landing entirely on the buyer.

Dialogue

Round Three with Pittsburgh

M

"Ray, before the number. Your quote moved eight percent between Rev. 1 and Rev. 2. Walk me through what happened in April."

R

"Raw material. If your purchase order had landed before the end of March, you would have paid the February price. It came in on the fourteenth of April."

M

"Understood, and I am not arguing with April. My problem is the payment line. The unit price I can work with. Payment in advance is the only sticking point."

R

"The advance is not greed, it is April again. And I already shaved two days off delivery at no charge, which nobody has thanked me for."

M

"Thank you for the two days. And to be straight with you: we would have walked away last week if the markup had been any thinner on our side."

R

"Then we are in deadlock, because I cannot carry ninety days and you cannot pay on day zero. Somebody has to move."

M

"So let us split the difference: half in advance, half in thirty days. And if you take that, I will firm up the whole year instead of one order."

R

"A full year changes the conversation. If you had opened with that, we would have finished in one call instead of three. Send it today and I will sign it."

Dialogue Review

About Ray

1. What reason does Ray give for the eight percent?
Raw material: the purchase order arrived on 14 April, and the February price was only valid to the end of March.
2. What has Ray already given away, and how does he feel about it?
Two days off delivery at no charge -- and he points out that nobody has thanked him for it.
3. Why does Ray say they are in deadlock?
He cannot carry ninety days and she cannot pay on day zero, so neither side can move from where they are.
4. What finally changes the conversation for him?
A full year instead of a single order -- and he says that if she had opened with that, it would have taken one call instead of three.
