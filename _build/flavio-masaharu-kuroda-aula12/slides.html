Grammar Discovery

Finished, or Still True?

"Verdana chose Invent in January 2025."

"In the second week the nightly sync failed for three days."

"The close has dropped from nine days to four."

"Nobody has typed an invoice by hand since July."

Ask each one the same question: is this finished, or is it still true today? What do the red verbs have in common, and what do the blue ones have in common?

Six lines, and the fifth one is there on purpose. Buyers do not believe a project with no bad week, and a problem that was fixed in three days is more convincing than a result with no story behind it. The first line has a date next to the old number, because a baseline without a date is only an opinion.
1. Why is the bad week on a card that is meant to sell?
Because buyers do not believe projects where nothing went wrong, and a problem fixed in three days shows how the team behaves under pressure.
2. Say the first line as a full sentence, with the right verb forms.
In 2024 the team closed the month in nine days, and since then the close has dropped to four days.
3. Why does the reference line say "so far"?
Because the series is not finished: she has done four calls and will do more, so the result is still open and still true today.
Chapter 6: Your Turn

Two Recordings, Then Your Case

Listening 1 · The Writer

Faster Than What?

A writer on the order of a good case study. Sound first — no text.

1. What mistake does she say most failed case studies made?
They started with the product, when readers care about the customer and about the number before anything changed.
2. What does she say happens to a result without a baseline?
It becomes just an adjective: faster than what, cheaper than when?
3. In what order does she write, and why are the results in the present?
The situation and the decision in the past, with a date, then the results in the present, because the results are still true today. And she always includes what went wrong.
Listening 2 · The Buyer

Forty Seconds, Three Things

A buyer on what he looks for in a case study. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the difference, for him, between a logo and a reference?
A logo on a slide is not a reference; a person who has agreed to take his call is.
2. Why does he want a baseline with a date?
Because an improvement of sixty per cent means nothing if he does not know what it was sixty per cent of.
3. What does he think when a case study has no bad week?
That somebody has removed it, because every implementation he has seen has had one. The honest case studies are the ones that move him.
