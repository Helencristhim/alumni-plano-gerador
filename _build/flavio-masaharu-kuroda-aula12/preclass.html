"We have a track record with mid-market manufacturers."
To cut -- to reduce time, cost or effort by a clear amount
"They cut the close from nine days to four."

"Invoice turnaround time has fallen from three days to one."

"The results have exceeded every target in the proposal."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A good case study has two parts, and English gives each part its own verb form. The first part is the story, and the story is finished. In 2024 the finance team at Verdana closed the month in nine days. In January 2025 they chose a new platform, and in February they ran a pilot in one plant. Every one of those sentences has a finished time, so every verb is in the past simple.

The second part is the result, and the result is still true today. The close has dropped from nine days to four. Adoption has reached ninety per cent. Nobody has typed an invoice by hand since July. These sentences have no finished time: they describe the situation now, so the verb is in the present perfect, have or has plus the past participle.

The small words help. Since goes with a point in time (since July), and for goes with a period (for eighteen months). So far says the series is not finished: the finance director has done four reference calls so far, and she will do more. What never works is the present perfect with a finished time. We have implemented it in 2024 is a mix of the two parts, and it sounds wrong to every native speaker.

One last thing: the baseline needs a date. Nine days in 2024 is evidence. Nine days, with no year, is an opinion.

1. Why are "closed", "chose" and "ran" in the past simple?
A Because they are irregular verbs.
B Because each sentence has a finished time: 2024, January 2025, February.
C Because case studies are always written in the past.
2. What does "The close has dropped from nine days to four" tell the reader?
A That the close is four days today: the result is still true.
B That the close was four days once, a long time ago.
C That the close will be four days next year.
3. What is wrong with "We have implemented it in 2024"?
A Implement is not a real English verb.
B It should be "since 2024".
C In 2024 is a finished time, so the verb must be past simple: we implemented it in 2024.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Simple vs Present Perfect

GRAMMAR

The story in the past simple. The results in the present perfect.

JobFormExample
A finished event, with a timepast simpleThey chose us in January 2025.
A result that is still true nowhave / has + past participleThe close has dropped to four days.
From a point until nowpresent perfect + sinceNobody has typed an invoice since July.
A period until nowpresent perfect + forThey have used it for eighteen months.
A series not finishedpresent perfect + so farShe has done four calls so far.
Negative, result still truehas not + past participleIt has not happened again.
Question, finished timeWhen did + base verb?When did it happen?
Question, until nowHow long have + past participle?How long have they used it?

Never: we have implemented it in 2024 · since eighteen months · nobody typed an invoice since July · when did it happened?
