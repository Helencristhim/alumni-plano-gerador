Chapter 4: The Code

Who, Which, That and Whose

The small words that make a profile findable

Grammar Discovery

What Makes a Request Findable?

"We need a piping engineer who has worked on food plants."

"The package that worries me is the equipment base."

"We want somebody whose drawings we can issue without a review."

"This is the only office which has the headcount for it."

Every sentence names a person or a thing, and then adds the detail that narrows it down. Look at the small word in red: it changes depending on what comes before it, and on whether the next idea is a possession.

JobFormExample
The detail is about a PERSONwho (or that) + verban engineer who has worked on food plants
The detail is about a THINGwhich (or that) + verbthe package that worries me
The detail is a POSSESSIONwhose + nounsomebody whose drawings we can issue
The detail is the object of the clausewho / which / that can be left outthe engineer (who) we mobilized last month
Why it matterswithout the clause, the sentence asks for anybodyWe need a piping engineer finds ten. The clause finds one.
The trapnever repeat the pronoun after the relativeNot the engineer who he has worked. Say the engineer who has worked.

In one line: who for people, which for things, that for both, whose for possession, and never a second pronoun after it.

Read the two profile lines out loud. Each one is a job title plus a relative clause, and the clause is doing all of the work: it turns a category into a person somebody in another country can recognise.
1. Which two lines use a relative clause, and which relative word does each one use?
Profile 1 uses who; profile 2 uses whose.
2. Why does the form say the start date is the real date and not a safety date?
Because a date that is earlier than the real one makes the resource manager move somebody off another project for nothing.
3. Turn the Backfill line into a full question.
Who is going to backfill the engineer whose role we are taking for six months?
Listening 1 · The Resource Manager

What Makes a Request Easy to Fill

A resource manager explains what she needs in a staffing request. Sound first — no text.

1. How many requests does she read in a week?
About twenty.
2. What single thing does she say makes a request fillable?
One sentence after the job title, describing what the person must have done.
3. What happens when somebody gives her a date two weeks too early?
She moves somebody off another project to hit a date that did not exist, and two teams end up unhappy.
Listening 2 · The Engineer on Secondment

The Hole You Leave Behind

An engineer describes what a secondment looked like from the inside. Sound first — no text.

1. How long was his secondment, and where?
Three months, in Brazil.
2. What was the problem he did not expect?
Nobody really covered his desk in Helsinki; the person on paper already had a full workload.
3. What is the first question he asks now when somebody mentions a secondment?
Who is backfilling, and whether that person has been told in advance.
