Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

The Limit Game -- Telling a Product Story

You tell the story of a real product to a foreign jury. Key words: a credit limit, a customer, a behavior, a reward, to earn, an insight, a pain point, a prototype, to launch, so far. Structure: past simple vs present perfect.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A credit limit -- the maximum amount of money you can spend with a credit card
"Some customers have a card, but their credit limit is zero."
A customer -- a person who buys or uses the services of a company
"We talked to more than fifty customers."
A behavior -- the way a person acts, especially again and again
"The game asks for simple behaviors, like paying the bills on time."
A reward -- something good you receive because you did something well
"The reward is a real credit limit."
To earn -- to get something because of your work or your actions
"Customers earn a limit step by step."
An insight -- a new and deep understanding of a problem or of people
"The key insight was simple: here, a credit card is part of the salary."
A pain point -- a problem that makes life difficult for a customer
"No limit on the card was the biggest pain point."
A prototype -- a first, simple version of a product, made to test an idea
"We built a prototype in two weeks."
To launch -- to make a new product available to the public for the first time
"We launched the game inside the app."
So far -- from the beginning until now
"So far, the results have been very good."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Arjun is a product designer in Mumbai. Two years ago, his team talked to young customers. They all had the same pain point: at the end of the month, nothing was left to save.

The team had an insight: saving felt like a punishment. So they built a prototype that worked like a game, and they tested it with fifty people. They launched it last year.

Since the launch, more than two hundred thousand people have used it, and the average savings have doubled. So far, it is Arjun’s favorite project.

1. What was the pain point of the young customers?
A They had no credit card.
B At the end of the month, nothing was left to save.
C The app was too slow.
2. Why does the text say “they launched it last year” and not “they have launched it last year”?
A Because last year is a finished time, so the past simple is used.
B Because the product is not popular.
C Because Arjun is from India.
3. Which sentence is about the result until now?
A “They built a prototype.”
B “They tested it with fifty people.”
C “More than two hundred thousand people have used it.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Simple vs Present Perfect

GRAMMAR

Tell the steps of a finished story in the past simple. Tell the result from the past until today in the present perfect.

JobFormExample
Steps of the story, finishedpast simpleWe talked to customers. We built a prototype.
Words for finished timeat first, then, last year, in 2024, when...When we tested it, users loved it.
Result until nowhave / has + third formMany customers have earned a limit.
Words for until nowso far, since, up to now, this yearSo far we have learned a lot.
QuestionsWhen did...? / Have you ... yet?When did you launch it? Have you measured it yet?
The trapwhen + present perfect does not existNot When have you launched it? Say When did you launch it?

Never: We have launched it last year · When have you launched it? · Since the launch, they earned · We didn’t tested it
