Chapter 4: Where the Object Goes

Where Does the Object Go?

Some verbs open in the middle. Some never do.

Grammar Discovery

Where Is the Object?

"She turned the invitations down."

"She turned them down, one by one."

"She ran into her old boss at a conference."

"She came up with a simple rule."

The red verb opens in the middle for the object. The blue ones never open.

TypeWhere the object goesExample
opens (separable)in the middle or at the endturn the offer down · turn down the offer
opens + pronounpronoun always in the middleturn it down · put it off · set it up
never opens (inseparable)always after the whole verbrun into her · look after them
three wordsnever opens; keep all threecome up with an idea · get round to it
no objectnothing to placecarry on · she gave up
the trapNot turn down it or put off it. Say turn it down, put it off.

In one line: a pronoun goes in the middle when the verb can open. If you are not sure the verb opens, use a noun and put it at the end.

Listening 1 · A Leadership Coach

The Cost of Every Yes

On why saying yes to everything is a decision too. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say every yes costs?
A no to something else, usually something you never chose to give up.
2. What does she ask her clients to write down?
Every request they agreed to in one week, and how long each one took.
3. What does she suggest saying instead of a quick yes?
Let me check and get back to you, so there is time to decide.
Listening 2 · A Language Podcast

Why English Loves Two Small Words

A short piece on where phrasal verbs come from. Sound first — no text.

1. Where does the speaker say most formal verbs in English come from?
From Latin, through French.
2. Where do most phrasal verbs come from?
From the old Germanic part of English, the short everyday words.
3. What advice does the speaker give about formal and phrasal verbs?
Use the formal verb in a written report and the phrasal verb in conversation.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I use a two-word verb in conversation where I used to use the formal one: put off, turn down, set up.
I put a pronoun in the middle when the verb opens: turn it down, put it off.
I never open run into, and I keep all three words in come up with and get round to.
I put the stress on the small word: put it OFF, turn it DOWN.
I know the verbs: to put off, to take up, to give up, to come up with, to figure out, to run into, to turn down, to set up, to carry on, to get round to.
