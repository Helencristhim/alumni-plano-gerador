Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Relay -- to pass a message on to someone else, keeping what the speaker actually said
"I will relay his message to the board this afternoon."
Readout -- a short written summary of what was said and decided in a meeting
"Send me the readout before the family meeting."
Debrief -- to report back on what happened, after a call, a visit or a meeting
"Paul will debrief the plant on Wednesday morning."
Paraphrase -- to say the same idea in your own words instead of the speaker's
"Do not paraphrase the delivery date -- give me his words."
Verbatim -- using the exact words the speaker used, with nothing changed
"His last line, verbatim, was: not before the tenth."
Heads-up -- an early warning, given so that the other side has time to prepare
"He gave me a heads-up two weeks before the invoice."
Clarify -- to explain something again so that no part of it is left in doubt
"Can you clarify what he meant by partial shipment?"
Misquote -- to report someone's words wrongly, by changing them or leaving part out
"I would rather say nothing than misquote a supplier."
Downplay -- to make something sound smaller or less serious than it really is
"The freight desk downplayed the delay, and we lost a week."
Flag -- to point something out so that other people notice it and act on it
"Flag the payment line for the board, not the unit price."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

On Monday a supplier in Pittsburgh leaves Maria Claudia a voicemail. He opens with a heads-up: the May container did not sail on Tuesday, and it is still at the port. By Wednesday that message has to reach three other people, and Maria Claudia is the one who carries it. In the written readout she does not invent anything and she does not soften anything. She writes: "He said the container had not sailed on Tuesday, and he told me the goods had been finished on time." The verbs move one step back, because he spoke on Monday and she is writing on Wednesday. His question goes in too, and it stays a question: "He asked whether we could wait until the tenth" -- no do, no question mark, normal word order. His request goes in with the person attached: "He asked us to call him the same day." One sentence she refuses to paraphrase, so it goes in verbatim, inside quotation marks. Then she flags the single line that decides things: the tenth is a departure date, not an arrival date. What she will not do is downplay the delay to keep the meeting pleasant, or misquote him as saying the goods were late. If anyone needs her to clarify something, she would rather do it before Friday than after. On Wednesday Paul will debrief the plant, and she will relay whatever is new the same afternoon.

1. Why does "did not sail" become "had not sailed" in the readout?
A Because the container will sail later.
B Because he spoke before she wrote, so the verb moves one step further back.
C Because the sentence is a question.
2. What is different about a question once it is reported?
A It keeps "do" and the question mark.
B It becomes a statement, so the answer is no longer needed.
C It takes if or whether, normal word order, no "do" and no question mark.
3. Why does she put one sentence in quotation marks instead of reporting it?
A Because she does not want to paraphrase it: verbatim, anyone can check what he really said.
B Because quotation marks are required in every readout.
C Because she disagrees with that sentence.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

When you report what somebody said, the speaking happened before your sentence, so the verb moves one step back in time. SAY takes no person after it; TELL needs one. A reported question takes if or whether (or a wh- word), keeps normal word order, and loses both "do" and the question mark. A reported request is ask or tell + person + to + verb.

What he saidBecomesExample
present simplepast simple"It is at the port." → He said it was at the port.
past simplepast perfect"It did not sail." → He said it had not sailed.
will / can / mustwould / could / had to"We will confirm." → He said they would confirm.
yes/no questionasked + if / whether"Can you wait?" → He asked whether we could wait.
wh- questionasked + wh- + normal order"What do you need?" → He asked what we needed.
requestasked / told + person + to"Call me today." → He asked us to call him that day.
today / tomorrow / herethat day / the next day / there"I will send it today." → He said he would send it that day.

Key point: three traps. First, "he said me" → "he told me" (or "he said that"). Second, "she asked what did I want" → "she asked what I wanted". Third, "he asked to call him" → "he asked us to call him". And one freedom: when the fact is still true right now, you may keep the present -- "He said the port is closed" is fine if it is closed today.

Stage 2: The Relay in Order

Order

Listen first, then put the steps of a clean relay in the correct order.

?You decide what belongs in the readout and what does not, and you flag the one line that changes a decision.
?You send the written version the same day, saying clearly which parts are verbatim and which parts are your own summary.
?While the other side is still speaking, you write the condition down in their own words.
?You relay it with the name of the person who said it, and you keep the part that is uncomfortable.
?Before the call ends, you read that line back to them and ask them to clarify anything you are not sure about.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best sentence for each situation.

A supplier asked you on Monday: "Can you wait until the tenth?" You are reporting it to the board. Which is best?
A "He asked can we wait until the tenth?"
B "He asked whether we could wait until the tenth."
C "He said us to wait until the tenth."
Someone in the meeting says the goods were produced late. You know that is not what the supplier said. Which is best?
A "Nobody said that. He told me the goods had been finished on time, and that a customs hold stopped the line."
B "It is possible, we do not really know what he said."
C "He said me that the goods were not late."
The supplier's price came with a condition attached. You are passing it on. Which is best?
A "He agreed to hold the price."
B "The price is more or less under control for now."
C "He said that he could hold the price, but only if the volume held."
