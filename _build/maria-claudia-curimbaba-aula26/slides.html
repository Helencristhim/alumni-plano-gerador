Chapter 3 -- The Code

Somebody Else's Words, Your Sentence

He said... He told me... He asked whether...

Discovery

Four Sentences, One Monday Call

All four report the same call. What has happened to the highlighted words?

"Greg said the container had not sailed on Tuesday."

"He told me the goods were finished on time."

"He said the freight desk would confirm the date the next day."

"He asked whether we could wait until the tenth."

What he saidBecomesExample
present simplepast simple"It is at the port." → He said it was at the port.
past simplepast perfect"It did not sail." → He said it had not sailed.
will / can / mustwould / could / had to"We will confirm." → He said they would confirm.
yes/no questionasked + if / whether"Can you wait?" → He asked whether we could wait.
wh- questionasked + wh- + normal order"What do you need?" → He asked what we needed.
request / orderasked / told + person + to"Call me today." → He asked us to call him that day.
today / tomorrow / herethat day / the next day / there"I will send it today." → He said he would send it that day.

Key idea: the verb moves one step back in time, because the speaking happened before this sentence. Three traps: say takes no person and tell needs one ("he said that..." / "he told me that..."); a reported question has no do and no question mark; and when the fact is still true today, the backshift is optional -- "He said the port is closed" is fine if it is closed right now.

The Structure

Three Jobs in the Relay

The same structure, doing three different things in the same meeting.

1 · Carry a hard fact without owning it

"Greg said the container had not sailed on Tuesday, and that the goods had been finished on time."

The fact is his. Your job is that it arrives whole.

2 · Report a question so the answer comes back

"He asked whether we could hold the volume until the twentieth."

A question relayed as a statement never gets answered. Keep it a question.

3 · Report a request so the room knows what to do

"He asked us to confirm the volume before Friday, and to call him the same day if we could not."

Ask + person + to + verb. Without the person, nobody in the room is responsible.

Notice what is missing: "they say that". Name the person. A relay with no name is a rumour, and a rumour cannot be checked.

Reading

The Readout

Meeting Readout -- Pittsburgh Line, 14 May

This readout is the written version of Monday's call with Pittsburgh. Greg Novak began with a heads-up: he said the May container had not sailed on Tuesday and was still at the port. He explained that a customs hold on a different shipment, loaded in front of ours, had stopped the whole line, and he was careful to flag that nothing had been damaged and that the goods themselves had been finished on time.

He told us the line would be loading again on Thursday, and that the freight desk had given him the tenth as the departure date. He refused to give an arrival date. His words, verbatim, were: "I would rather tell you that I do not know than give you a date that moves again next week." He then asked whether the tenth would work for us, and he asked us to call him the same day if it did not, so that he could tell us what the other options cost.

Two notes before the family meeting on Friday. First, please do not paraphrase the departure date: it is the tenth, and it is the only date anyone has committed to. Second, nobody should downplay this in front of the board, and nobody should misquote Greg as saying the goods were late. He did not say that. If anything here is not clear, ask me to clarify it before Friday rather than after. Paul will debrief the plant on Wednesday, and I will relay anything new the same afternoon.

Adapted for class use from an internal meeting readout

Artefact

The Forwarded Email

FW: May shipment -- heads-up before Friday

From: Maria Claudia Curimbaba · To: Board · Cc: Paul Brennan

Before Friday, so that nobody hears this second-hand:

HIS WORDS, VERBATIM

"The May container did not sail on Tuesday. The goods were finished on time. The vessel departs on the tenth. I would rather tell you that I do not know than give you a date that moves again next week."

MY SUMMARY

He said the delay was a customs hold on another shipment, not a production problem. He asked whether the tenth works for us, and he asked us to call him the same day if it does not.

One line to flag: the tenth is a departure date, not an arrival date. Nobody has committed to an arrival date yet.

1. Why does the email separate "his words" from "my summary"?
So that the board can see which sentences are Greg's, word for word, and which are Maria Claudia's reading of them. Anyone who wants to check can check.
2. Which single line does she flag, and why that one?
That the tenth is a departure date, not an arrival date -- it is the line most likely to be repeated wrongly, and the one that changes what the board decides.
Dialogue

Wednesday's Debrief

P

"Maria Claudia, the debrief from the plant. Two things, and one of them you are not going to like."

M

"Give me the one I am not going to like first, and give it to me the way they said it. Do not paraphrase it for me."

P

"The freight desk told me the vessel had left on the tenth, exactly as Greg promised. But the plant manager said she had only booked half the space for the June run."

M

"Half. Did she say why, or did she only say half?"

P

"She said the June volume had not been confirmed by anyone on our side, so she had held the rest back. And she asked whether we wanted her to book the other half today."

M

"Then that is a question waiting for an answer, not a problem. Anything else I should flag for Friday?"

P

"One line, verbatim, because I do not want to get it wrong: if the June volume is not confirmed by the twentieth, the rate goes up. She asked me to tell you today."

M

"Good. I will relay it word for word, and I will not downplay the twentieth. Send me those two lines in writing before the meeting."

Dialogue Review

About Paul's Debrief

1. What does Paul say the freight desk told him?
That the vessel had left on the tenth, exactly as Greg had promised.
2. What did the plant manager book, and what reason did she give?
Only half the space for the June run. She said the June volume had not been confirmed by anyone on their side, so she had held the rest back.
3. What question did the plant manager ask?
Whether they wanted her to book the other half of the space that day.
4. Which sentence does Paul repeat verbatim, and why that one?
"If the June volume is not confirmed by the twentieth, the rate goes up." He repeats it word for word because it carries a condition and a deadline, and she asked him to pass it on the same day.

Listening 2

The Sentence I Changed

Sound first -- no text on screen.

0:000:00
1. What exactly did the supplier say on the Friday call?
That they could hold the price, but only if the volume held. The price was conditional.
2. What did she tell her team, and why did she change it?
That he had agreed to hold the price. She dropped the condition because the call had been friendly and she did not want to end the week on a bad note.
3. What happened three weeks later?
The volume fell, the price went up exactly as he had said it would, and she had to explain to her own board why she had told them something that was never true.
4. What two things does she do now, every time?
She writes any condition down verbatim and reads it back to the person on the call; and when she relays it she names who said it and gives the whole thing, even the uncomfortable half.
