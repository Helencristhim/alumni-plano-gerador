Lesson 07 — Pre-class

How Much? How Many?

Eight words for the trip (luggage, cash, budget, groceries, document, save, cost, afford) and the one question that decides every quantifier: can I count them?

▼

Stage 1.1: Words for the Trip

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Luggage — the bags you take when you travel
"I do not have much luggage. One bag."
Cash — money in notes and coins, not a card
"How much cash do I need for a month?"
Budget — the money you plan to spend, decided before
"My budget for the trip is not very big."
Groceries — the food you buy for the week
"We buy groceries once a week."
Document — an official paper, like a passport or an ID
"I need two documents for the visa."
Save — to keep money instead of spending it
"I am saving a little every month."
Cost — how much money you have to pay for something
"How much does the ticket cost?"
Afford — to have enough money for something
"I cannot afford a hotel, so I am staying with friends."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. Then answer the questions.

I am planning one month abroad and there are a lot of numbers. The flight costs four thousand two hundred, which is more than half my budget. I need two documents, the visa and the insurance, and together they are six hundred more. I am saving a little every month, but it is not enough yet. I am not taking much cash — two hundred for the first days, and a card after that. For food I am going to buy groceries and cook, because eating out every day is how people spend too much money. And luggage: one bag. If I take too many things I cannot afford the extra fee, and I do not want to carry them for a month.
How many documents does she need?
A Two — the visa and the insurance.
B One — only the visa.
Why is it "much cash" and not "many cash"?
A Because cash cannot be counted one by one in English.
B Because cash is always singular.
Why is she going to buy groceries and cook?
A Because she cannot afford the extra luggage fee.
B Because eating out every day is how people spend too much money.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

One question decides it: can I count them?

Countable → many, a few, too many. Uncountable → much, a little, too much.

Countable

How many documents?
a few days
too many bags

Uncountable

How much money?
a little cash
too much luggage

? How much does it cost? — never "how many does it cost".
= I have enough money / enough documents. (enough works for both)
~ a lot of money, a lot of documents (also works for both — use it when you are not sure)

Three that trick Brazilians: luggage, money and advice are uncountable. Never "two luggages", never "many advices".

Stage 1.5: Complete the sentence

Practice

Stage 3: Say it out loud

Speaking

Tap Listen, then Record. In a question the voice goes up at the end — and "how much" runs together, almost one word.

How much does the flight cost?
I do not have much cash on me.
One bag is enough for a month.

Stage 4: In the conversation

Quiz
You are in a shop and you want the price. What do you say?
A How many does it cost?
B How much does it cost?
You want to say you are taking a small number of bags. Which is correct?
A I am taking a few bags.
B I am taking a little bags.
Your friend has five suitcases for one week. What do you say?
A You have too many luggages.
B You have too much luggage.

Stage 5: Your month abroad

Reflection
Write four sentences first: how much money you need for one month, how many documents you have to get, something you have too much or too many of, and something you do not have enough of. Ask yourself before each: can I count them? Then turn the paper over and record 30 seconds saying them — without reading.
