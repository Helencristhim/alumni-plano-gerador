The Frame

Why many here and much there?

How many documents do I need?
How much money do I need?
I am taking a few bags.
I have a little cash for the first days.

Can you count them one by one?

Documents, bags, days — you can count them, so they take many, a few, too many. Money, cash, luggage — you cannot, so they take much, a little, too much.

Before the sentence, one question: can I count them, one by one?

Three that trick everybody

luggage, money and advice cannot be counted in English. Never "two luggages", never "many advices".

Stuck? a lot of works for both. And enough works for both too.

Did You Catch It?

About Rafa’s trip

1. How many documents does he need, and how much do they cost?
Two — visa and insurance — six hundred together.
2. Why is he not taking much cash?
Carrying too much cash makes him nervous, so he is taking a card.
3. Do you agree with taking only one bag? Say why, with a quantifier.
(Your opinion — use "too many", "a few" or "enough".)
Listening 1

A budget that worked

1. How much did she spend in total?
About seven thousand reais for five weeks.
2. What did she spend too much money on?
Eating out in the first week.
3. What is her advice about cash?
Take a little for the first days, and use a card after that.
Listening 2

At the airport

1. How many bags can you take without paying?
One in the cabin and one to check in.
2. What is the weight limit for the checked bag?
Twenty-three kilos.
3. What happens if you have too much luggage?
You pay at the desk, and it costs more than paying online.
