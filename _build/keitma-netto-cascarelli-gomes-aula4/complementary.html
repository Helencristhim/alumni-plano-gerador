Talk
Adam Grant — The surprising habits of original thinkers (TED)

Fifteen minutes on people who changed something at work and on what they did first. Listen for the shape of his sentences: he moves constantly between what somebody did once, in the past, and what has been true ever since.

Tip: write down three sentences he says in the present perfect — have or has plus a verb. Then say the same three about your own team.

Talk
Laura Vanderkam — How to gain control of your free time (TED)

Twelve minutes about how people describe a year to themselves, and why the story almost never matches the calendar. Clear, slow, American English, and full of the two tenses you worked on today.

Tip: she keeps saying I have studied, I have found, they told me. Pause every time and ask yourself why that one is not in the past — the answer is always the time expression.

Podcast
The Indicator from Planet Money — The teacher pay penalty, Meta's major settlement, and a footwear flop

Nine minutes, three separate stories, all of them about how a number moved over time. It is the fastest possible practice for the difference between what happened and what is still happening.

Tip: after each of the three stories, stop the audio and say one sentence in the past and one in the present perfect about what you just heard.
