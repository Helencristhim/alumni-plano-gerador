Chapter 5: Real Speed

Two Recordings, and the Scoreboard

What the plateau is, and why measuring changes it

Listening 1 · A Language Coach

The Month Where Nothing Is Wrong

A coach on the point where most people stop, and why. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say happens in the first two months, and why does it stop?
Everything improves visibly, because everything was missing. It levels off once the basics are in place and the remaining gains are smaller and harder to see.
2. What does she say the plateau actually is?
Not a failure and not a sign of doing something wrong — it is what improvement looks like once it becomes incremental.
3. What does she ask her students to keep, and what does it change?
A record of something specific they could not do a month ago. It changes whether they can see progress at all, and that decides whether they keep it up.
Listening 2 · Science Radio

Why the People Who Measure Are the People Who Continue

A short piece on tracking, and what it does to a habit. Sound first — no text.

1. What was the only difference between the two groups in the study?
One group wrote down one number after each session. Everything else — the material, the schedule, the teacher — was the same.
2. What did the researchers find six months later?
The group who recorded the number were far more likely to still be going, and the gap appeared at exactly the point where progress stopped being obvious.
3. What explanation does the piece give?
Not motivation. Without a record you compare today with how you felt last week, which is unreliable; with a record you compare it with something written down.

What I Can Do Now
