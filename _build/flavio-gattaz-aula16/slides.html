Chapter 4: The Code

Must, Have To, and the Two Negatives

Obligation, prohibition, and freedom

Grammar Discovery

Does the Employee Have a Choice?

Every employee must disclose a conflict of interest in writing.

Suppliers have to register before the tender opens.

You mustn’t accept cash from a supplier, whatever the amount.

You don’t have to report a coffee after a meeting.

Everything in blue is an obligation. The red one is a prohibition: there is no choice. The green one is not a rule at all — it tells you that you are free to decide.

JobFormExample
Obligation (the rule is yours)must + plain verb — never must toEvery employee must disclose it.
Obligation (the rule comes from outside)have to + verb; past: had toSuppliers have to register first.
Prohibition: no choicemustn’t / can’t / are not allowed toYou mustn’t accept cash.
No obligation: you choosedon’t have to / don’t need toYou don’t have to report a coffee.
Formal obligation (written codes)be required to + verbEmployees are required to report a kickback.
Formal prohibitionbe not allowed to / be not permitted to + verbNobody is allowed to punish a whistleblower.

In one line: mustn’t closes the door; don’t have to opens it. And must has no past — use had to.

Chapter 5: Real Pressure

Your Code, and Two Voices

A code of conduct, an interviewer and a site director

Real Document

Six Rules for Every Site

SITE CODE OF CONDUCT
to be signed by every engineer and foreman · issued by Flavio Gattaz, Managing Partner

1. Everybody must wear a harness above two meters. No exceptions, no schedule reasons.

2. You must not accept cash, gifts or favors from a supplier or a subcontractor.

3. A conflict of interest has to be disclosed in writing before any contract is signed.

4. Any engineer can stop a pour. You do not have to ask the director first.

5. Nobody is allowed to punish a person who reports a problem.

6. Any invitation from a supplier has to be reported, whatever it is.

Note: rule 6 was added in March, after an invitation that no rule in the old code covered. If a decision is not on this list, ask one question: would you be comfortable if it were published tomorrow, with your name on it?
1. Which rule gives the engineers freedom instead of an obligation?
Rule 4. Any engineer can stop a pour, and they do not have to ask the director first.
2. Why was rule 6 added, according to the note?
Because of an invitation that no rule in the old code covered — a gray area, and in practice a loophole.
3. What should an engineer do if a decision is not on the list?
Ask one question: would I be comfortable if it were published tomorrow, with my name on it?
Listening 1 · An Interviewer

A Dilemma Is Not the Law

An admissions interviewer on the ethics question. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say that refusing money is not a dilemma?
Because it is simply the law. A dilemma is when two things you believe in point in different directions and you have to choose.
2. What three things does she want to hear about?
Which rule the candidate had to follow, which one he chose to follow, and what it cost him.
3. What does she say about being the hero of the story?
You do not have to be. Some of the best answers end with the candidate admitting he bent a rule once and explaining exactly why he would not do it again.
Listening 2 · A Site Director

The Weekend at the Beach House

A director on an invitation no rule covered. Sound first — no text.

1. Why was the invitation a problem, even though no written rule prohibited it?
They were choosing the steel supplier for their biggest project, and the supplier's company was one of three on the list.
2. What test did he use, and what did he do next?
He asked himself whether he would be comfortable if the story were in the newspaper the next day. He was not, so he declined politely and disclosed the invitation to his uncle the same afternoon.
3. What changed in the code, and what happened to the contract?
They added a rule: any invitation from a supplier has to be reported. The supplier won the contract anyway, three weeks later, on price.
