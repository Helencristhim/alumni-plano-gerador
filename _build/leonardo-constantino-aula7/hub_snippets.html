Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

That Clause, Which Firm, Who Advises -- Using Relative Clauses to Speak With Precision in Tax Meetings

Describing a client structure in one precise sentence instead of five short ones. Key words: subsidiary, jurisdiction, domicile, exposure, provision, override, remittance, exemption. Expressions: fall under a jurisdiction, trigger an exemption, challenge a provision, across the board, a gray area, set aside. Structures: relative clauses -- who, which, that and where, defining and non-defining, and dropping the pronoun in object position.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Subsidiary -- a company that is owned or controlled by another company
"The group holds three subsidiaries outside Brazil."
Jurisdiction -- the country or authority whose laws apply to a case
"The payment falls under a different jurisdiction."
Domicile -- the country a person or company is legally based in for tax purposes
"The fund keeps its domicile in Luxembourg."
Remittance -- money sent from one country to another
"Every remittance passes through the same entity."
Provision -- a single rule or clause inside a law or a contract
"We are challenging the provision, not the whole law."
Exemption -- official permission not to pay a tax that others have to pay
"Pension funds keep the exemption until 2027."
Override -- to have more legal force than another rule, and replace it
"The treaty overrides the domestic provision."
Exposure -- the amount a client can lose if a rule is applied against them
"The Cayman entity carries most of the exposure."
Fall under a jurisdiction -- to be governed by the laws of a particular country or authority
"The Cayman entity falls under a different jurisdiction."
Trigger an exemption -- to cause an exemption to apply automatically
"A remittance under that limit triggers an exemption."
Challenge a provision -- to argue formally that a rule is wrong or invalid
"We decided to challenge the provision in court."
Across the board -- affecting everyone and everything equally, with no exception
"The new rate applies across the board."
A gray area -- a situation where the rules are not clear yet
"That position is a gray area until the court decides."
Set aside -- to cancel a decision officially, usually by a court
"The provision we challenged last year has been set aside."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Leonardo is at a dinner in New York, and an American attorney asks him what he works on. He does not give her a list. "I lead the practice that advises financial institutions," he says. "Take one client that we onboarded in March: a payment group whose holding is in Luxembourg, with two operating companies that run the business in Brazil and Mexico." She wants the detail that matters. "And the money leaves Brazil how?" He answers in one sentence: "Through a fourth entity, which nobody talks about, domiciled in the Cayman Islands. Every remittance passes through it." That is the entity that the tax authority will open first, and Leonardo knows it. The exemption the group claims rests on a provision that the authority is now reading in a different way. "Is that a gray area, or is it simply wrong?" she asks. "A gray area. The provision has never been tested in court, and the partner who signed the original opinion has left the firm." He adds the one piece of good news: a new treaty may override the domestic rule across the board next year. She listens to the whole thing without interrupting once, and then she says the sentence he will remember: the people who can explain a structure in one sentence are the people she calls.

1. "Through a fourth entity, which nobody talks about, domiciled in the Cayman Islands." Why "which" and not "that"?
A Because the entity is a company and not a person.
B Because the clause is between commas and only adds extra information, and "that" never follows a comma.
C Because the sentence is in the passive voice.
2. "The partner who signed the original opinion has left the firm." What does the clause do here?
A It identifies which partner he is talking about.
B It adds extra information about a partner we already know.
C It gives the reason why the partner left.
3. "The exemption the group claims rests on a provision..." Why is there no relative pronoun after "exemption"?
A Because the sentence is informal and the pronoun was forgotten.
B Because "exemption" is not a person, so no pronoun is possible.
C Because the pronoun is the object of the clause, and there it can be dropped.
4. What does the attorney say at the end of the conversation?
A That she will send the file to another firm in Brazil.
B That she calls the people who can explain a structure in one sentence.
C That the Cayman entity should be closed before the ruling.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Relative Clauses

GRAMMAR

A relative clause joins two facts into one sentence. It is the difference between a list and a structure: "The client has a subsidiary. It is in the Cayman Islands. It carries an exposure." becomes "The client has a subsidiary in the Cayman Islands, which carries a significant exposure." Same information, one breath.

FormUseExample
whoPeople onlyThe partner who signed the opinion is in London.
whichThings, companies and ideas — never peopleThe provision, which was added in 2023, is unclear.
thatPeople or things, but only in defining clauses — never after a commaThe subsidiary that files in Brazil is the smaller one.
wherePlaces, countries and jurisdictionsThe jurisdiction where the fund is domiciled sets the rate.
Defining — no commasTells you which one. Remove it and the sentence loses its meaning: "The clients who hold offshore subsidiaries are the ones most affected."
Non-defining — two commasExtra information about something already identified: "Leonardo Constantino, who leads the financial institutions practice at ALMA, presented the scenario." Never that here.
Object positionWhen the pronoun is the object, you can drop it: "The regulation (that) we reviewed last month has been revised."
Two frequent mistakesNever repeat the object: "the provision that we challenged it" — wrong. And never use which for a person: "the partner who advises the fund", not "which".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each real conversation.

A foreign attorney asks what your practice does. You answer:
A "I lead the practice that advises financial institutions on the Brazilian tax reform."
B "I lead a practice. The practice advises financial institutions. The subject is the tax reform."
C "I lead the practice which advises it financial institutions."
You want to add extra information about a framework everyone already knows. You say:
A "The framework, that was introduced in 2023, applies across the board."
B "The framework, which was introduced in 2023, applies across the board."
C "The framework what was introduced in 2023 applies across the board."
You need to say which partner signed the opinion. The correct sentence is:
A "The partner which signed the opinion has left the firm."
B "The partner, who signed the opinion, has left the firm."
C "The partner who signed the opinion has left the firm."
The client asks whether the position is safe. The honest, professional answer is:
A "It is safe. Nobody has ever asked about it."
B "It is a gray area: the provision we are relying on has never been tested in court."
C "I cannot say anything about that provision."
