Chapter 3: The Code

Who · Which · That · Where

Four words that join two facts into one sentence

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The client who restructured last year is now facing a secondary liability."

"Leonardo Constantino, who leads the financial institutions practice at ALMA, presented the scenario."

"The regulation that we reviewed last month has been revised."

"The jurisdiction where the subsidiary is domiciled decides the rate."

Two of these clauses tell you which one. One only adds extra information. One points to a place. Which is which?

PronounUseExample
whoPeople onlyThe partner who signed the opinion is in London.
whichThings, companies and ideas — never peopleThe provision, which was added in 2023, is unclear.
thatPeople or things, but only in defining clauses — never after a commaThe subsidiary that files in Brazil is the smaller one.
wherePlaces, countries and jurisdictionsThe jurisdiction where the fund is domiciled sets the rate.
Defining — no commasTells you which one. Remove it and the sentence loses its meaning: "The clients who hold offshore subsidiaries are the ones most affected."
Non-defining — two commasExtra information about something already identified. Use who or which, never that.
Object positionWhen the pronoun is the object of the clause, you can drop it: "The regulation (that) we reviewed last month has been revised."

The comma is not decoration. Without commas you are identifying; with commas you are only adding. In a legal sentence that difference changes who is covered.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"The framework, that was enacted in 2023, applies across the board."
"The framework, which was enacted in 2023, applies across the board."
"The provision which we challenged it last year has been set aside."
"The provision that we challenged last year has been set aside."
"The partner which advises the fund is based in New York."
"The partner who advises the fund is based in New York."

After a comma, use which or who — never that. And the relative pronoun already is the object, so the sentence does not need it again.

Chapter 4: The Networking Dinner

Meet Diane

Tax attorney in New York · a glass in her hand and thirty seconds of attention

Dialogue

Thirty Seconds at a Dinner

D
So, ALMA. I know the name. Which part of the firm do you sit in?
L
I lead the practice that advises financial institutions: banks, payment companies and funds that operate in Brazil.
D
Payments. That is the part of your reform I do not understand at all. Give me a client.
L
Take one that we onboarded in March. A payment group whose holding is in Luxembourg, with two operating companies that run the business in Brazil and Mexico.
D
And the money leaves Brazil how?
L
Through a fourth entity, which nobody talks about, domiciled in the Cayman Islands. Every remittance passes through it.
D
There it is. That is the subsidiary your authority will look at first.
L
It already has. The exemption they claim depends on a provision that the tax authority is now reading in a different way.
D
Is that a gray area, or is it simply wrong?
L
A gray area. The provision has never been tested in court, and the partner who signed the original opinion has left the firm.
D
Welcome to my life. Half of my files are provisions that nobody has ever challenged.
L
The difference is that a new treaty may override the domestic rule across the board next year, and then the question disappears.
D
Send me your note when that happens. The people who explain a structure in one sentence are the people I call.
