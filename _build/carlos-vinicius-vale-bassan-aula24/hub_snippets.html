The cost overrun goes to the steering committee. Key words: setback, root cause, mitigate, contingency budget, transparent, accountable, offset, downplay. Structure: contrast linkers -- although, even though, despite + noun / -ing, however.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Setback -- a problem that delays progress or makes things worse
"The data errors were a setback, but not a disaster."
Root cause -- the real, original reason for a problem
"The root cause was the quality of the old data."
Mitigate -- to make the effect of a problem less serious
"Here is how we will mitigate the impact."
Contingency budget -- money kept aside for problems you did not plan for
"We will use part of the contingency budget."
Transparent -- open and honest, with nothing hidden
"Tom was transparent with the committee from the first minute."
Accountable -- responsible for something and ready to explain it
"I am accountable for the solution, not only for the problem."
Offset -- to balance a cost with a gain of the same size
"Savings in travel will offset part of the extra cost."
Downplay -- to make something seem less important than it really is
"Never downplay a cost overrun in front of the board."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Monday, Tom and Carlos present the payroll bridge to the steering committee. The news is not good: the project is two hundred thousand dollars over budget. But Tom does not hide it. He says, “Although we are over budget, the go-live date has not changed.”

Then he explains the root cause: the old payroll data. “Even though the errors were in the old system, we are accountable for the fix.” He finishes with the plan: “Despite the setback, the risk is under control. We needed three extra weeks of testing. However, go-live stays on March first.”

Notice the pattern. After although and even though, you need a full sentence: subject + verb. After despite, you need a noun or a verb-ing. However starts a new sentence, with a comma after it.

1. How does Tom report the overrun?
A He hides it on the last slide of the presentation.
B He says it clearly and balances it with the plan.
C He says that another team should explain it.
2. What was the root cause of the problem?
A A change in the go-live date that the committee asked for.
B The travel budget.
C Errors in the old payroll data.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “Despite the delay, the risk is under control.”
B “Despite the project is late, the risk is under control.”
C “Although the project is late, but the risk is under control.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Contrast Linkers: Although, Despite, However

GRAMMAR

Good updates put the bad news and the plan in the same breath. These linkers hold the two halves together.

LinkerFormExample
althoughalthough + subject + verbAlthough we are over budget, the date is the same.
even thougheven though + subject + verb (stronger)Even though it was not our system, we own the fix.
despite / in spite ofdespite + noun, or despite + verb-ingDespite the setback, the risk is under control.
howevernew sentence: However, + sentenceWe are late. However, the client is happy.
One linker onlyalthough OR but, never bothAlthough it is late, we can fix it.

Never: Despite the project is late · Despite of the delay · Although it is late, but we can fix it · Despite be late
