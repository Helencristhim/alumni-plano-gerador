Chapter 4: The Code

Although, Despite, However

The small words that balance a problem and a plan

Grammar Discovery

Bad News, Good News

"Although we are over budget, the go-live date has not changed."

"Even though the delay was not our fault, we are accountable for the solution."

"Despite the setback, the risk is under control."

"The bridge needed three extra weeks of testing. However, the go-live date is the same."

Every sentence has two halves: the bad news and the good news. The blue words hold them together. Look at what comes after each blue word: a full sentence with a subject and a verb, or only a noun?

LinkerFormExample
althoughalthough + subject + verbAlthough we are over budget, the date is the same.
even thougheven though + subject + verb (stronger)Even though it was not our fault, we own the fix.
despite / in spite ofdespite + noun, or despite + verb-ingDespite the setback / Despite being over budget, ...
howevernew sentence: However, + sentenceWe are late. However, the risk is under control.
Trap 1no subject + verb after despiteNot Despite the project is late. Say Although the project is late.
Trap 2one linker, not twoNot Although it is late, but... Use although OR but.

In one line: although / even though + sentence; despite + noun or -ing; However, + new sentence.

Chapter 5: The Update

In Front of the Committee

One status slide, one coach, one update

Real Document

The Status Slide

INTEGRATION STEERING COMMITTEE -- STATUS UPDATE
Payroll Bridge · Presented by Tom Reyes (Brightline Finance) and Carlos Vinicius Vale Bassan (Accenture Strategy)
StatusAmber: over budget, on schedule
Cost overrun200,000 dollars (about 8% of the budget)
Root causeErrors in the old payroll data; three extra weeks of testing
Mitigation120,000 from the contingency budget; 50,000 offset by travel savings
Still open30,000 dollars -- decision needed today
Key messageDespite the extra cost, go-live stays on March 1.
The number comes first. The plan comes next. One sentence balances both.
1. Why is the status amber and not red?
Because the project is over budget, but it is still on schedule.
2. How much of the overrun is still not covered?
Thirty thousand dollars. The committee needs to decide today.
3. Which line balances the bad news and the plan?
The key message: despite the extra cost, go-live stays on March 1.
Listening 1 · The Coach

Three Rules for Bad News

An executive communication coach explains how to report bad news to a committee. Sound first — no text.

1. What should you say in the first minute?
The number, clearly: for example, we are two hundred thousand dollars over budget.
2. Who is accountable for the solution, even if the problem is not yours?
You are: the person who presents.
3. What do committees not forgive?
Surprises.
Listening 2 · Tom’s Update

The Difficult Part First

Tom Reyes opens his update to the steering committee. Sound first — no text.

1. How big is the overrun, in percent?
About eight percent.
2. Why does Tom believe the extra testing was the right decision?
Because the alternative was paying people the wrong salaries.
3. What has not changed?
The go-live date: March first.
