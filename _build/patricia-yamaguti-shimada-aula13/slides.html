Chapter 3: The Missing S

The Smallest Thing in English, and the Hardest

I work · she works

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I work in the afternoon."

"My husband works in the morning."

"I have two children."

"My daughter has a tennis class."

Who it is aboutThe verbExample
I, you, we, theyno SI work in the afternoon.
he, she, it — ONE other personthe verb takes -sMy husband works in the morning.
have → hasthe only strange oneMy daughter has a class.
do → does · go → goesthe -es familyShe does her homework. He goes to school.

When the sentence is about ONE other person — he, she, my husband, my daughter — the verb takes an S. This is the error that comes back for months, for everybody. It is normal.

The Four Family Sentences

Four Sentences and They Know Your Family

"I have two children: a son and a daughter."

the opening — and it invites the next question

"My son is eight and my daughter is six."

ages, with AM and IS — never with HAVE

"My husband works in the morning, and I work in the afternoon."

the S in his verb, and no S in yours

"And you? Do you have children?"

you ask back — and the conversation keeps going

Dialogue

Two Parents, One Line

M
This line is not moving at all, is it? Are those two yours?
P
Yes, they are. I have two children: a son and a daughter.
M
Lovely. How old are they?
P
My son is eight and my daughter is six. And you?
M
One boy, ten. He never gets tired. Is this your first time in London?
P
Yes, it is. We are here for two weeks.
M
And whereabouts do you live back home, if you do not mind me asking?
P
Sorry, I don't understand. Can you say that again, more slowly?
M
Of course. Where do you live in Brazil?
P
In São Paulo. I work in the afternoon, and my husband works in the morning.
M
That is organised. Who takes them to school?
P
He takes them at seven thirty, and I help with the homework after dinner. And you? Do you work at the weekend?
M
Never. Saturday is for football, and I am very bad at it. Look, the line is moving.
Comprehension

What Did Martin Say?

1. How old is his son, and what does he say about him?
Ten. He says the boy never gets tired.
2. What does Martin do at the weekend?
He never works. Saturday is for football, and he says he is very bad at it.
3. What does he say about her family arrangement?
He says it is organised, and asks who takes the children to school.

Chapter 6: Your Turn

From Guided to Free

the line · the week · the conversation that continues

Role-Play — Guided

In the Line at the Park

Scenario: Another parent starts talking to you. Say how many children you have, their ages, and what you and your husband do. Then ask back.

Keywords:

I have two childrenmy son is eightmy husband works...And you?
Role-Play — Semi-free

Describe Your Week

Scenario: Tell me a normal week in your house: who takes the children to school, who works when, what happens after dinner, and what changes at the weekend.

Keywords:

He takes...She plays...We have dinner...At the weekend
Role-Play — Free

Twenty Minutes With a Stranger

Scenario: You are next to another parent for twenty minutes and there is nothing to solve. Just talk: your children, their children, your week, their week. Keep it going.

No keywords this time. Two minutes, no stopping. Different from the model is not wrong — if they understood you, it worked.
