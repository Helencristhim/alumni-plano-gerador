Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

The Feedback Conversation -- Developing Somebody on Your Team

Somebody on your team is good, and stuck. Key words: a direct report, a performance review, a strength, a blind spot, to delegate, to step up, a raise, turnover. Structure: should, have to and must -- advice, rule and decision are three different temperatures.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A direct report -- a person who reports directly to you, with nobody in between
"I have six direct reports and I see four of them every week."
A performance review -- the formal meeting where somebody's results and development are discussed
"Nothing in a performance review should be a surprise."
A strength -- something a person does clearly better than the people around them
"Her strength is the customer. That has never been the problem."
A blind spot -- something about you that everybody else sees and you do not
"His blind spot is that he answers every e-mail himself."
To delegate -- to give somebody a task and the authority to decide about it
"You have to delegate the reporting. All of it, not half."
To step up -- to take more responsibility than your job asks for
"She stepped up in March, when nobody asked her to."
A raise -- an increase in somebody's salary, that stays every month
"She does not want a raise. She wants a bigger territory."
Turnover -- how many people leave a company in a period, and have to be replaced
"Turnover in that team was thirty percent last year."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Erin is a direct report. Her region grew eleven percent and her turnover was thirty. In the performance review, Roberto opens with what is not in question: her strength is the customer. Then the number that worries him.

She names her own blind spot before he does: she does everything herself. So he gives his opinion, and marks it as one: "you should delegate" the whole reporting, not half of it. She objects, because her name is still on the numbers. That is exactly why, he says, "you have to give" the authority with the task — that part is not his opinion, it is how delegation works at all.

Then he offers something: "you do not have to do" it alone. He will sit in the first two reviews. She asks for the northeast territory instead of a raise, and he answers with a decision: "you must step up" before it opens, not after. One more line closes it, and it is a prohibition, not advice: "you must not promise" a date the factory cannot meet.

1. "You should delegate the whole reporting." What is Roberto doing with should here?
A Giving his opinion, and marking it as an opinion that can be discussed.
B Giving an order that cannot be discussed.
C Describing something that already happened.
2. "You do not have to do it alone." What does that sentence mean?
A It is forbidden to do it alone.
B It is not necessary to do it alone. Doing it alone is allowed, just not required.
C She should do it alone.
3. Look at "you must step up" and "you must not promise". What changes between them?
A Nothing. Must and must not are almost the same.
B The second one is weaker than the first.
C The first is a strong obligation to do something; the second is a prohibition, never to do it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Should, Have To and Must

GRAMMAR

Three temperatures for the same idea. The person in front of you hears the difference immediately, so choose on purpose.

UseFormExample
Advice, my opinionshould + naked verbYou should delegate more.
A rule from outsidehave to / has to + naked verbYou have to send it by Friday. She has to sign it.
Strong, and it is my decisionmust + naked verbWe must fix this before the review.
Not necessarydo not have to + verbYou do not have to do it alone.
Forbiddenmust not + verbYou must not promise a date we cannot meet.
QuestionShould I...? · Do I have to...?Should I tell her today? Do I have to sign it?

Never: You should to delegate · She must delegates · He have to send it · It is optional, so you must not do it (that means forbidden) · You musts step up.

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

You are giving feedback to somebody on your team. Put your side in a logical order.

?"Let me start with what is not in question: your strength is the customer."
?"Your region grew eleven percent and your turnover was thirty."
?"You should delegate the whole reporting, not half of it."
?"And you have to give the authority with the task."
?"You do not have to do it alone. I will sit in the first two reviews."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

You are giving your own opinion about how somebody works. You say:
A "You should to delegate more."
B "You should delegate more."
C "You should delegates more."
Finance closes on Friday and nobody can change that. You say:
A "You have to send the forecast by Friday."
B "You should send the forecast by Friday, if possible."
C "You have send the forecast by Friday."
Somebody asks whether they need to attend a meeting. It is optional. You answer:
A "You must not come."
B "You do not have to come."
C "You should not come."
Promising a delivery date the factory cannot meet is forbidden in your company. You say:
A "You do not have to promise a date we cannot meet."
B "You must not promise a date we cannot meet."
C "You must not to promise a date we cannot meet."
