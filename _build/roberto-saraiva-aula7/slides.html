Chapter 3: The Code

How Strong Is the Verb?

Advice, rule, decision — and the one that means the opposite

Discovery

From the Softest to the Strongest

"You should delegate more of the reporting."

"You have to send the forecast by Friday."

"We must fix this before the review."

"You do not have to do it alone."

The three red ones get stronger as you go down, and after every one of them the verb stays naked. The blue one is the trap: it does not forbid anything.

UseFormExample
Advice, my opinionshould + naked verbYou should delegate more.
A rule from outsidehave to / has to + naked verbYou have to send it by Friday. She has to sign it.
Strong, and it is my decisionmust + naked verbWe must fix this before the review.
Not necessarydo not have to + verbYou do not have to do it alone.
Forbiddenmust not + verbYou must not promise a date we cannot meet.
The trapthese two are oppositesdo not have to = it is optional · must not = never do it

In one line: should is my opinion, have to is somebody else’s rule, must is my decision — and do not have to is freedom, not prohibition.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Twenty Minutes, Door Closed

The conversation, the HR director, and somebody who received it

Dialogue

Good, and Stuck

R
Erin, thank you for the time. Let me start with what is not in question: your strength is the customer, and it has never been the problem.
E
Thank you. I hear a but coming.
R
There is one. Your region grew eleven percent and your turnover was thirty. Three people left in a year, and two of them told me the same thing on the way out.
E
Let me guess. That I do everything myself.
R
That is your blind spot, and you just named it yourself, which is half the work. So here is my opinion: you should delegate the whole reporting, not half of it.
E
And if the numbers come out wrong, whose name is on them?
R
Yours. That is exactly why you have to give the authority with the task. And you do not have to do it alone — I will sit in the first two reviews with you.
E
All right. What I want in return is not a raise. I want the northeast, when it opens.
R
Then you must step up before it opens, not after. Show me a team that runs without you for one quarter, and that conversation becomes very short.
Comprehension

About Erin

1. What are her two numbers, and what do they say together?
Her region grew eleven percent and her turnover was thirty. She delivers results and loses people at the same time.
2. She names her own blind spot before he does. What is it, and what is her objection to changing it?
That she does everything herself. Her objection is that if the numbers come out wrong, her name is still on them.
3. What does she ask for at the end, and what does she explicitly not ask for?
She asks for the northeast territory when it opens. She says she does not want a raise.
Listening 1 · The HR Director

How to Open the Hard One

Three rules for the first two minutes of a feedback conversation. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is his first rule, and why?
Nothing in a performance review should be a surprise. If it is the first time the person hears it, the manager failed for eleven months, not the employee.
2. What does he say about the word but?
The word but deletes everything before it. Say the strength, stop, and start a new sentence.
3. What is the difference he insists on between should and have to?
Should is your opinion and can be discussed. Have to is a rule and cannot. If you say should when you mean have to, the person negotiates something that was never negotiable.
Listening 2 · From the Other Side

The Sentence That Changed It

A manager remembering the feedback she did not want to hear. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What exactly did her director say to her, and how did she react at first?
That her team could not work without her, and that this was not a compliment. She was angry for about a week.
2. What made her change her mind?
She counted the decisions that waited for her while she was on holiday: fourteen in nine days.
3. What does she say she does differently now?
She gives the decision away with the task, and she says out loud which things people do not have to bring to her at all.

1. Two lines on this form describe the same person and point in opposite directions. Which two?
Region growth up eleven percent, above plan, and team turnover of thirty percent. She produces results and loses people.
2. One line is an obligation and one is an offer. Say each one out loud with the right strength.

3. She asked for a territory and not a raise. What does that tell a director about how to keep her?
That money is not what is holding her. She wants a bigger job, so the risk of losing her is a promotion somewhere else, not a salary somewhere else.
