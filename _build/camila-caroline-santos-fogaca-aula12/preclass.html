Stage 2: Grammar Tip -- Open questions in the right order

GRAMMAR

The gaps in the form become questions. An open question asks for information, not for yes or no, and English puts its words in a fixed order.

FormWhat it doesExample
question word + do / does + subject + verbasks for information about a fact or a routine"What does the team do every week?"
question word + is / are + subjectasks about a state or a situation"Why is the workload growing?"
Can you tell me + question word + subject + verba softer, indirect question: no do, and the subject comes before the verb"Can you tell me what the need is?"
walk me through + nounasks for a story, step by step"Walk me through a normal week in your team."
Watch outa direct question needs do or does: "What do you need?", not "What you need?". An indirect question has no do and keeps the normal order: "Can you tell me what you need?", not "Can you tell me what do you need?".

One open question, then silence. The hiring manager fills the gaps of the form with the answer.

Stage 3: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

The words behind the gaps you have just found. Listen to each expression and read it in context: they come back in every conversation about a new request.

job requisition -- the formal request to open or fill a position
"The job requisition arrives with only a title and a salary range."
hiring manager -- the manager who asks for the position and will lead the new person
"The hiring manager knows the work better than anybody."
workload -- the amount of work a person or a team has to do
"The workload of the sales team grows every quarter."
business need -- the real reason, in the business, for a request
"A job title is not a business need."
what is driving this request -- a question about the real reason behind a request
"Before the form, I ask: what is driving this request?"
in the short term -- in the next weeks or months, not later
"In the short term, the team can cover part of the work."
bring in -- to hire a person from outside and put them in a team
"Do we need to bring in somebody from outside?"

Stage 8: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Real questions from conversations about a new request. Only one option is both correct and natural.

1. You want to know what the team does. Which question is correct?
A What the team does every week?
B What does the team do every week?
C What does the team does every week?
D What is the team do every week?
2. You want to ask politely about the need. Which question is correct?
A Can you tell me what is the need?
B Can you tell me what does the team need?
C Can you tell me what the team needs?
D Can you tell me the team needs what?
3. The hiring manager only says "it is urgent". What do you ask?
A I understand. What is driving this request?
B I understand. Is it urgent or very urgent?
C I understand. Do you need this person?
D I understand. What drives this request is urgent?
4. The need is only for the next three months. How do you say it?
A The need is only in the short term.
B The need is only in the short time.
C The need is only on short term.
D The need is only for the shortly term.
5. You think a person from inside the company can do the job. What do you say?
A Before we bring in somebody from outside, let's look at the people we already have.
B Before we bring in somebody of outside, let's look at the people we already have.
C Before we bring out somebody from outside, let's look at the people we already have.
D Before we bring on somebody to outside, let's look at the people we already have.
