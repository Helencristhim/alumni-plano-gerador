Chapter 3: Ordering Politely

A, An or Some

The word before the food decides everything

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I would like a table for two, please."

"Would you like an appetizer?"

"I would like some water, please."

"I would like to see the menu, please."

You can count itYou cannot count it
a table, a side dishsome water, some rice
an appetizer, an hoursome bread, some coffee
two main courses, three checkstwo glasses of water

After would like comes a noun ("a table") or to plus a verb ("to see the menu"). Never both, and never a bare verb.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

✗ WRONG

"I want a water."

✓ RIGHT

"I would like some water, please."

✗ WRONG

"I would like two rices."

✓ RIGHT

"I would like some rice."

Water, rice, bread and coffee are uncountable: no a and no plural s. If you really need a number, count the container: "two glasses of water".

Chapter 4: At the Restaurant

Table for Two

Dialogue

Ordering Dinner

Good evening. Do you have a reservation?
T
Yes. I would like a table for two, please.
S
Here is the menu. Would you like something to drink first?
T
I would like some sparkling water, please.
S
Are you ready to order, or would you like a few more minutes?
T
I am ready. I would like an appetizer and then the steak as a main course.
S
How would you like the steak cooked?
T
Medium rare, please.
S
Of course. Would you like a side dish with that?
T
Yes, some rice. And could I have a refill of water later?
S
Certainly. I will bring the check whenever you are ready.
Did You Catch It?

About the Server

1. What does she ask before she shows them the table?
Whether they have a reservation.
2. What two questions does she ask about the steak?
How he would like it cooked, and whether he wants a side dish with it.
3. What does she promise to do at the end?
To bring the check whenever he is ready.

They do not add it to the check, so you leave what you think is fair.
