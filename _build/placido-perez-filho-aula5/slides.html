Chapter 3: The Code

I Was Wondering...

Five ways to sound firm and polite

Grammar Discovery

Same Request, Softer Tone

"I was wondering if you could extend the payment terms."

"Would it be possible to split the order into two batches?"

"We might need a little more time to decide."

"I’m afraid we can’t accept a fifty percent down payment."

Compare with the direct versions: extend the payment terms, split the order, we need more time, no. Look at the blue words: they ask for the same thing, but they leave space for the other side. And the red words: how do you say no without sounding rude?

JobFormExample
A big requestI was wondering if you could + verbI was wondering if you could lower the price.
Is it possible?Would it be possible to + verb?Would it be possible to lock in this price?
Less directmight / could instead of will / canWe might need more time.
Soften a criticismit seems that... / a little / slightlyIt seems that the price is a little high.
Say no politelyI’m afraid + negativeI’m afraid that’s a deal-breaker for us.
The trapI was wondering is a request now, not a storyI was wondering if... = a polite request today.

In one line: past forms and modals make a request softer; I’m afraid makes a no polite.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Deal, at Real Speed

One call, three recordings, one counteroffer

Dialogue

A Call With a Supplier

K
Good morning, Plácido. Did you have a chance to look at our offer?
P
Good morning, Katrin. Yes, thank you. Overall it looks good, but I was wondering if we could talk about the payment terms.
K
Of course. We usually ask for a forty percent down payment.
P
I’m afraid forty percent is a bit too much for us. Would it be possible to start with twenty, and send the order in two batches?
K
Hmm. Twenty is difficult. I could meet you halfway: thirty percent, and two batches.
P
That seems fair. And is the price a firm offer? We would like to lock in this price for six months.
K
Six months might be possible if you confirm the volume this week. That is our only sticking point.
P
Understood. Let me talk to my team, and I’ll follow up by Thursday.
Comprehension

About Katrin

1. What down payment does Katrin usually ask for?
Forty percent.
2. What does Katrin offer when she meets him halfway?
Thirty percent, and two batches.
3. What is Katrin’s condition for six months at this price?
To confirm the volume this week.
Listening 1 · A Negotiation Trainer

Soft Words, Firm Position

A British negotiation trainer explains why polite language makes you stronger, not weaker. Sound first — no text.

1. What mistake do many non-native speakers make, according to her?
They are too direct, and they sound rude without wanting to.
2. What is her example of a soft request?

3. What does she say about the word no?

Listening 2 · A Voicemail

The Counteroffer

A sales manager from a supplier in China leaves a voicemail with a counteroffer. Sound first — no text.

1. Why is he calling?
To answer the buyer’s request for a lower price with a counteroffer.
2. What does he offer?
A three percent discount if the buyer orders sixty tons instead of forty.
3. What will he do after the call?
Follow up with the new quote by email.
Listening 3 · A Buyer’s Story

The Deal-Breaker

An Australian buyer tells a story about a negotiation that almost failed. Sound first — no text.

1. What was the deal-breaker for the supplier?
Payment terms of ninety days.
2. How did the buyer get leverage?
He showed that he could buy from another supplier.
3. How did they finally agree?
They met halfway: sixty days, and a small discount for early payment.
