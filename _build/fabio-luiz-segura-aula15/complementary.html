Talk
Chris Anderson — “TED's secret to great public speaking” (TED, 8 min)

The head of TED explains the one thing every good talk does: it builds a single idea in the listener's mind, step by step. It is the same principle as one talking point on a panel, from the person who has watched more talks than anybody.

Tip: write his main idea as one sentence. Then check your own opening statement: does it build one idea, or five?

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart (Stanford GSB) — “Make Numbers Count: How to Translate Data for Your Audience” with Chip Heath

A Stanford professor argues that numbers are a foreign language most audiences do not speak, and that a presenter's job is to translate them into comparisons people can feel. It is the idea behind every sentence on your speaker card.

Tip: write down every comparison he uses to translate a number. Then write one for Brazil in the same style.

Video
Money Explained — “How Brazil Built the Perfect Payment System” (YouTube)

How the world outside Brazil tells the story of Pix: the numbers, the comparisons and the sound bites a foreign audience finds impressive. Useful to hear which facts about your own country land best in English.

Tip: note the two comparisons the video uses most. Then check them against a real source before you put either on your own speaker card.
