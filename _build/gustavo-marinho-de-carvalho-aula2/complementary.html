Complementary Activities

Optional extras to enjoy outside class. Mark each one as done after you watch or listen to it.

Documentary
The Social Dilemma (2020)

Engineers who built the feeds explain why two people can open the same app on the same morning and see two different worlds. It is the question of this lesson, one level deeper: not why countries disagree about a story, but why they never read the same story in the first place.

Tip: English audio, English subtitles. Stop after the first forty minutes and say out loud, in three sentences, what the film has argued so far.

Podcast
BBC 6 Minute English — “Climate change and extreme weather”

Six minutes, two British speakers, normal speed, on a story that is in the news in every country at the same time and is discussed differently in each one. Exactly the raw material this lesson is about, and the free transcript is on the same page.

Tip: listen once with no text and write the two sentences you would use to hand this story to somebody else. Then open the transcript and check your verbs.

Talk
Alisa Miller — “How the news distorts our worldview” (TED)

Four and a half minutes, one map and one argument: the amount of coverage a country gets has very little to do with how much is happening there. Short enough to watch twice in the same week, and it gives you something concrete to say the next time a client abroad asks what the news is like here.

Tip: it is fast. Watch it once with English subtitles on, then once with them off, and see how much you keep.
