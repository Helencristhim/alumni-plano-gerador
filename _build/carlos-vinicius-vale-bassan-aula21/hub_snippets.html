Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Kickoff -- the first meeting that officially starts a project
"The integration kickoff was on Monday morning."
Steering committee -- a group of senior leaders who guide a project and make the key decisions
"I report to the steering committee every Thursday."
Point person -- the one person that everybody contacts about a topic
"Priya is our point person for HR questions."
Morale -- how confident and positive the people in a team feel
"Morale in the new team is better than last week."
Concern -- a worry about something that could go wrong
"Their main concern was job security."
Reassure -- to say something that makes people feel less worried
"The CEO wanted to reassure the team about their jobs."
Flag -- to point out a problem so that people notice it
"Priya flagged a risk in the payroll system."
Word for word -- using exactly the same words that someone said
"I cannot repeat it word for word, but I can tell you what he meant."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The kickoff with the new team ends at noon, and Diana calls Carlos. She missed it, so he reports back. Mark said that the brand would stay. He told the team that nobody was losing their job this year.

The sales director asked if the bonus plan would change. And Priya, the new point person for HR, asked Carlos to send her the org chart by Wednesday.

Notice the pattern. When you report, the verb moves one step back: will becomes would, is becomes was. Told needs a person; said does not. A question becomes asked if, with normal word order. A request becomes asked + person + to + verb.

1. What did Mark say about the brand?
A That the brand would stay.
B That the brand would change.
C That the brand was for sale.
2. What did Priya ask Carlos to do?
A Call the sales director.
B Send her the org chart by Wednesday.
C Hold a town hall.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “The director asked if would the plan change.”
B “The director asked if the plan would change.”
C “The director said me if the plan would change.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech: Said, Told, Asked

GRAMMAR

When you report what someone said, the verb usually moves one step back. Told needs a person; said does not. Questions and requests have their own patterns.

JobFormExample
Reporting a statementsaid (that) + the verb one step backMark said that the brand would stay.
With the person who heard ittold + person + (that)He told the team that nobody was losing their job.
Reporting a yes / no questionasked + if + normal word orderHe asked if the plan would change.
Reporting a wh- questionasked + when / what + normal word orderShe asked when the payroll would move.
Reporting a requestasked + person + to + verbPriya asked me to send her the org chart.

Never: He said me · She asked when would it move · He asked me send it · She told that the plan would change

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The CEO said: “The brand will stay.” You report it to the board:
A “He said that the brand would stay.”
B “He said me the brand will stay.”
C “He told that the brand stays.”
Priya said: “Please send me the org chart.” You report it:
A “Priya asked me send the org chart.”
B “Priya asked me to send her the org chart.”
C “Priya said me to send the org chart.”
The director asked: “Will the bonus plan change?” You report it:
A “He asked if the bonus plan would change.”
B “He asked if would the bonus plan change.”
C “He said if the bonus plan will change.”
You found a problem that the committee must know about. You say:
A “I need to flag a risk.”
B “I need to flag on a risk.”
C “I need flagging a risk.”
