Chapter 4: The Compression Code

From Verb to Noun

the decision to · the delay in · the loss of

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The decision to publish the numbers was mine."

"The delay in disclosure cost us three weeks."

"The loss of their trust was the price of that delay."

"The introduction of a weekly cadence changed the account."

Each sentence begins with a noun. What was that noun before?

VerbNounNoun phrase
decidedecision (-ion)the decision to publish
delaydelay (same form)the delay in disclosure
loseloss (irregular)the loss of their trust
acceptacceptance (-ance)the acceptance of scrutiny
Why botherThe noun puts the FACT in the subject, so the sentence can judge it: "The delay in disclosure cost us three weeks." One page instead of three.

TO for an action you chose · IN for what went wrong inside a process · OF for everything else. The preposition is never optional.

Language Focus

Which Preposition?

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

✗ WRONG

"The decision of publish the numbers was mine."

✓ RIGHT

"The decision to publish the numbers was mine."

✗ WRONG

"The delay of disclosure cost us three weeks."

✓ RIGHT

"The delay in disclosure cost us three weeks."

✗ WRONG

"The publication our own bad numbers made the good ones believable."

✓ RIGHT

"The publication of our own bad numbers made the good ones believable."

A noun phrase always carries its preposition. Choose it with the noun, not with the verb it came from.

Dialogue

Reading the Draft with Andy

A
I read your one-pager on the plane. It is the shortest thing you have ever sent me, and somehow it says more. Who cut it down?
I
I did. The first draft was two pages of verbs. The decision to publish, the delay in disclosure, the loss of their trust — once those became nouns, the page fit.
A
The board will stop at one line. You call the weekly report a permanent safeguard. Are you sure you want to promise that?
I
Yes. The introduction of that cadence is the only reason the account survived. Making it permanent is not a promise, it is a precedent.
A
And the oversight? If it sits with you, the board reads the whole thing as one person's habit.
I
It moves to operations in January. My name is on the mandate, not on the checklist.
A
One more thing. The candor paragraph is the best writing in the letter and it is also the riskiest. Investors read "our own bad numbers" and hear "more bad numbers are coming".
I
Then they will read the next sentence. The publication of those numbers is what made the good ones believable, and the rigor of the report is what keeps them honest.

Listening 1

The Board Pack Closes Friday

Your operations lead calls with the last corrections. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Which number in the letter does Sofia correct, and how does she know?
The delay in disclosure was three weeks, not two — she went back to the warehouse log and the first warning is dated the eighth.
2. Which line does Sofia want in bold, and why?
That the weekly report has run for thirty-one consecutive Thursdays without a single miss, because it is the one thing nobody can argue with.
3. What does legal require in the wording about oversight?
That the letter says the report moves to operations in January, not that it has already moved, because it has not.
Listening 2

A Call from David Chen

The client has been showing your summary to his own board. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What did David give his own board, and why that document?
The one-page summary rather than the case study, because his board asked purchasing for a supplier report worth copying.
2. What convinced David's board, according to him?
Not the recovery, but the fact that the failure and the fix sit on the same page, in the same size of type.
3. What does David advise AVRT not to hand over, and what should it give instead?
Not a template, which anyone can fill in badly — the rule behind it: publish the number before somebody asks for it.
