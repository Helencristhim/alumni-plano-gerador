Milestone Review -- The Executive Toolkit, Lessons 11 to 20

The year in one page: reading the annual letter to the board and compressing a whole year of work into it. Key words: disclosure, candor, scrutiny, precedent, oversight, cadence, safeguard, mandate, fallout, groundwork, stewardship, rigor. Structure: nominalization (the decision to publish; the delay in disclosure; the loss of their trust), with every structure from Lessons 11 to 20 back in the room.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Disclosure -- the act of making information public, including information that hurts you
"Our first mistake was the delay in disclosure."
Candor -- the habit of saying uncomfortable truths plainly
"Candor is expensive in the first quarter and cheap after it."
Scrutiny -- close, critical examination by people who can judge you
"The acceptance of scrutiny was the hardest decision."
Precedent -- an earlier decision that sets the rule for the ones after it
"I ask the board to treat both as precedent."
Oversight -- regular checking that makes sure work is done properly
"Oversight of the report moves to operations in January."
Cadence -- the regular rhythm at which something is repeated
"The introduction of a weekly cadence changed the account."
Safeguard -- a measure put in place to stop a problem from happening again
"The weekly report is now a permanent safeguard."
Mandate -- the authority a group gives you to act on its behalf
"That is the mandate I am asking you to renew."
Fallout -- the damage that follows a bad event
"Had the report existed, the fallout would have been smaller."
Groundwork -- the preparation that makes a later result possible
"The groundwork is done; what remains is the discipline."
Stewardship -- the responsible care of something that belongs to other people
"The second is a matter of stewardship."
Rigor -- thoroughness that leaves nothing to chance
"The rigor of that report is what keeps the numbers honest."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Izabel writes the letter to the board twice. The first version is a list of events: we delayed, we lost, we rebuilt, we published. The second version says the same thing with nouns. The delay in disclosure cost the company three weeks. The loss of Meridian's trust was the price of that delay. The decision to publish the numbers was hers alone, and the acceptance of scrutiny that came with it was the hardest part. The introduction of a weekly cadence turned the account around, and the renewal of the contract in May required no negotiation. One page, six sentences, and every one of them begins with a fact the board can judge.

B The delay in disclosure.
C Three weeks.
2. Why does the second version of the letter fit on one page?
A Because nouns are shorter words than verbs.
B Because a noun phrase packs a whole clause into the subject.
C Because the numbers were removed from it.

A to

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Nominalization

GRAMMAR

Turning a verb into a noun so a whole clause can sit in the subject — the grammar that makes a one-page summary possible.

VerbNounNoun phrase in a sentence
decidedecision (-ion)The decision to publish the numbers was mine.
delaydelay (same form)The delay in disclosure cost us three weeks.
loseloss (irregular)The loss of their trust was the price of that delay.
acceptacceptance (-ance)The acceptance of scrutiny is what made us credible.
The prepositionTO for an action you chose (the decision to publish, the refusal to argue). IN for what went wrong inside a process (the delay in disclosure, the drop in adoption). OF for everything else (the loss of trust, the publication of the numbers).
Why botherThe noun puts the FACT in the subject, so the sentence can judge it: "The delay in disclosure cost us three weeks." That is how a year fits on one page.
Watch outNever drop the preposition ("the publication of our numbers", not "the publication our numbers"), and never use of before a verb ("the decision to publish", not "the decision of publish").

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each executive situation.

You want the board to judge the three-week wait, not the people. Which sentence does that?
A "We delayed the disclosure for three weeks."
B "The delay in disclosure cost us three weeks."
C "It was delayed by three weeks."
Which sentence uses the right preposition after the noun?
A "The decision of publish the numbers was mine."
B "The decision to publish the numbers was mine."
C "The decision for publish the numbers was mine."
You are writing one line about the weekly report for the board. Which is the most natural in writing?
A "We introduced a weekly cadence and it changed the account."
B "The introduction of a weekly cadence changed the account."
C "Introducing a weekly cadence, the account changed."
Which sentence keeps the preposition the noun phrase needs?
A "The publication our own bad numbers made the good ones believable."
B "The publication of our own bad numbers made the good ones believable."
C "The publication for our own bad numbers made the good ones believable."
