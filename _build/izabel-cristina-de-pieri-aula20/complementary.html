Aula 20 -- Milestone Review -- The Executive Toolkit

Film
The Post (2017)

A publisher has to decide whether to print information that will hurt her own institution, with lawyers, investors and a board in the room. It is the Lesson 20 argument dramatized: the delay in disclosure, the acceptance of scrutiny, and a decision that becomes a precedent. Watch Katharine Graham move from asking permission to giving a mandate.

Tip: after the boardroom scene, write her year in one page. Six lines, each one opening with a noun phrase ("The decision to publish...", "The loss of...", "The renewal of...").

Talk
Margaret Heffernan — "Dare to disagree" (TED)

Heffernan argues that organizations only get better when someone is willing to say the uncomfortable thing early. That is the candor paragraph of your board letter, defended by a former CEO with data instead of adjectives. Free and open on TED, with an English transcript and subtitles.

Tip: list the three costs she names for staying quiet. Then say each one as a noun phrase ("The refusal to disagree...", "The delay in raising it...") and see which version you would put in front of your own board.

YouTube
Helen Sword — "Beware of nominalizations (AKA zombie nouns)" (TED-Ed)

Four minutes on the exact structure from Lesson 20, argued from the other side: Sword shows what happens when every verb becomes a noun and the sentence stops breathing. Watch it as the counterweight to the class, not as a contradiction of it.

Tip: reread your board letter afterwards and mark every noun phrase. Keep the ones that put a fact where the board can judge it, and turn the rest back into verbs. That choice is the real skill.
