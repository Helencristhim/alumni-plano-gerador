Lesson 11 -- Presenting Data

Film
Moneyball (2011)

A baseball manager with no money decides to trust the numbers instead of the opinions of his scouts. Most of the film happens in meeting rooms, where people defend a decision with data and other people push back. It is the exact situation of a commercial review: the chart is on the table, and somebody has to say what it means.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Every time a character explains a number, pause and ask yourself: did he say what it shows, what changed, or what it means?

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart -- Make Numbers Count: How to Translate Data for Your Audience

A free episode from the Stanford business school with Chip Heath, the author of Making Numbers Count. His idea is simple: a number is a foreign language for most of the room, and the presenter has to translate it into something people can see and feel. It is the third step of today's lesson, explained by somebody who wrote a whole book about it.

Tip: listen once without the transcript, then again with it. Afterwards, take one number from your own region and say it out loud in a way that a person outside sales would understand.

YouTube
David McCandless -- The Beauty of Data Visualization (TED)

Eighteen minutes on how a good chart makes a pattern visible that a table of numbers hides. He shows many charts and narrates each one in a few sentences, which is a free lesson in today's three steps. He also explains why the outlier is often the most interesting point on the slide.

Tip: watch it once with subtitles. Then choose one of his charts and describe it out loud using at least two words from today: trend, peak, outlier, breakdown.
