Chapter 3: The Code

How Much, or How Many?

The small words in front of every number

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"A few clinics ordered more than the whole interior."

"There was very little growth in the South this quarter."

"We have fewer prescribers in the interior than in the capital."

"It took a great deal of work to open the new accounts."

Two words come before blue nouns, two before purple nouns. Can you count the noun after each one?

Type of nounQuantifiersExample
Countable (clinics, units, accounts)many, a few, few, fewer, several, a large number ofA few clinics ordered more.
Uncountable (growth, demand, work, time)much, a little, little, less, a great deal ofThere was very little growth.
Bothsome, any, a lot of, plenty of, most, hardly any, noMost clinics · most growth
A specific groupquantifier + of the / of our: most of the growth, none of our clinics, a few of the accounts
A few vs fewa few / a little = some (positive) · few / little = almost none (negative)
Two trapsNever the most part of — just most of the. And never less clinics — countable nouns take fewer.

The Structure

Narrate the Chart in Three Steps

The room can see the chart. Your job is to say what they cannot see.

1 · What it shows

"This line graph shows units per month for the last twelve months, in both regions."

2 · What changed

"The trend is up. Sales peaked in March, dipped in July, and there was a sharp rise in November."

3 · What it means

"Most of that rise came from a few clinics. Very little came from the interior, and that is the opportunity."

Shows → changed → means. Never read the numbers one by one.

Chapter 4: The Chart Review

Before It Goes to Boston

Marta Ribeiro · Commercial Analyst · the chart review

Dialogue

The Chart Review

M
Renato, I built the chart for Boston from the sales data. Do you want to go through it with me before you send it?
R
Yes, please. It is a line graph of units per month, for the last twelve months, with both regions together.
M
Right. So in one sentence, what is the trend?
R
The trend is up. Units rose in the first quarter and peaked in March. Then they dipped in July, which happens every winter.
M
And after July? Most of the people in that room will look at the end of the line first.
R
After July the numbers leveled off, and in November there was a sharp rise. Most of that rise came from a few clinics in Sao Paulo and Curitiba.
M
That is the part they will ask about. Is one of those clinics an outlier?
R
Yes. One clinic in Curitiba ordered almost as much as the whole city of Porto Alegre. I will show it in the breakdown, not on the main chart.
M
Good. And market share went from eighteen to twenty-one percent. How will you say that?
R
It grew by three percentage points, not three percent. And I will say it slowly.
M
Exactly. Narrate the chart, do not read it. Say what changed, and then what it means.

Listening

What the Finance Director Remembers

A finance director on forty country presentations a quarter. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is the problem with most of the presentations he sees?
The chart is fine, but the narration is not: a lot of presenters read the chart to him, line by line.
2. What three things does he want, in order?
What the chart shows, the trend and the moments where it changed, and why.
3. Which part do very few presenters do, and why does it matter to him?
The third part, why. It is the only part he remembers.
4. What does a slide with fifteen numbers tell him?
That the person does not know which number matters.
Listening 2

Three Small Mistakes With Numbers

A presentation coach on what she hears in every session. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Market share goes from eighteen to twenty-one percent. How much has it grown?
By three percentage points — not by three percent.
2. Why does that detail matter in a room of finance people?
It is the difference between sounding careful and sounding careless.
3. When do we say fewer, and when do we say less?
Fewer with things you can count (clinics, units); less with things you cannot count (time, growth).
4. What should you do right after you say a number?
Stop for one second, and then say what it means.
Chapter 5: Practice

Your Chart

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "The most part of the growth came from Sao Paulo."
"Most of the growth came from Sao Paulo." (no "the most part of" in English)
2. "We have less clinics in the interior than last year."
"We have fewer clinics in the interior than last year." (clinics are countable)
3. "There was many growth in November."
"There was a lot of growth in November." (growth is uncountable)
4. "Most of clinics ordered more in November."
"Most clinics ordered more in November." or "Most of the clinics..." (of needs the)
5. "Actually, our market share is twenty-one percent." (you mean: at the moment)
"Currently, our market share is twenty-one percent." (actually means in fact, not at the moment)
