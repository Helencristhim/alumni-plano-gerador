Checkpoint Block 2: Ready for the Panel?

The checkpoint of block 2: thirteen lessons of interview English, one review, and a ten-minute speaking benchmark in class. Key words: to take stock, a plateau, fluency, accuracy, rusty, to fall back on something, second nature, to fine-tune something, a comfort zone, to stretch yourself, to brush up on something, to be on track. Structure: a review of the tools from lessons 11 to 23 -- indirect questions, the passive, inversion, regret, cause and consequence, relative clauses, concession and participle clauses.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To take stock -- to stop and think carefully about where you are, before you decide what to do next
"Before the real interview, it is time to take stock."
A plateau -- a period when you stop improving, after a time of fast progress
"Most advanced learners hit a plateau, and most of them do not notice it."
Fluency -- the ability to speak easily and smoothly, without stopping a lot
"His fluency improved first; the grammar followed."
Accuracy -- the ability to speak without mistakes in grammar or words
"Under pressure, my accuracy drops before my fluency does."
Rusty -- not as good as before, because you have not practiced for a long time
"My English was rusty after years of speaking it only on trips."
To fall back on something -- to use something safe and familiar when a situation is difficult
"When I am nervous, I fall back on short, simple sentences."
Second nature -- something you do so often that you do it without thinking
"After thirteen lessons, the follow-up question is second nature."
To fine-tune something -- to make small changes to something so that it works as well as possible
"The content is ready; now we fine-tune the delivery."
A comfort zone -- a situation where you feel safe and relaxed, because nothing is difficult
"Short answers are my comfort zone; long stories are not."
To stretch yourself -- to try something a little more difficult than what you can already do
"In every answer, stretch yourself with one structure you are not sure of."
To brush up on something -- to practice something you learned before, so that you remember it better
"Before the interview, I want to brush up on my questions for the panel."
To be on track -- to be making the progress you planned, at the speed you planned
"At this speed, you are on track for the interview."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Thirteen lessons ago, the interview was a list of questions you could answer one at a time. Now it is a conversation you can run. Look at what changed. When you did not understand a question, you used to say "What?". Now you ask: I was wondering whether you could repeat that. When you talked about a mistake, you used to apologize. Now you own it: If only I had brought the site manager in earlier.

The stories changed too. A result now has a reason and a consequence: Turnover fell, which is why we kept the plan. People in the story have a short introduction: My uncle, who founded the company, is still on the board. And the cost of the program is admitted, not hidden: Even though the fees are high, the real cost is time.

The last tool was about length. Having run the company for twelve years, I know where it is weak. One sentence instead of two. None of these tools is new today. The question for the checkpoint is a different one: which of them are second nature, and which ones do you still leave on the page when you are under pressure?

1. According to the text, what do you say now when you do not understand a question?
A "I was wondering whether you could repeat that."
B "What?"
C "Sorry, my English is a little rusty today, so could you please speak more slowly?"
2. Which sentence from the text gives a result and its consequence?
A "My uncle, who founded the company, is still on the board of directors."
B "If only I had brought the site manager in earlier."
C "Turnover fell, which is why we kept the plan."
3. What is the question for the checkpoint, according to the last paragraph?
A Which new tools you will learn in the next block, before the interview.
B Which tools are second nature, and which ones you still avoid under pressure.
C How many lessons are left.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Block 2 Tools

GRAMMAR

Nothing new: one line for each tool you used in lessons 11 to 23.

ToolJob in the interviewExample
Indirect questionsask politely, or ask for repetitionI was wondering whether you could repeat that.
The passivefocus on the action, not the personEvery application is read by two people.
Regretown a mistakeIf only I had asked for help earlier.
Cause and consequenceconnect a result to a decisionTurnover fell, which is why we kept the plan.
Relative clausesintroduce a person or a projectMy uncle, who founded the company, is on the board.
Concessionadmit a cost and keep the decisionEven though the fees are high, the real cost is time.
Participle clausestwo sentences in oneHaving run the company, I know its weak points.

Never: Could you tell me what is the program known for? · Despite the program is flexible · Few of applicants · Revenue rose in twelve percent

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The interviewer speaks very fast and you miss the question. You say:
A "Could you tell me what was the question that you asked me a moment ago?"
B "What?"
C "I'm sorry, would you mind repeating the question?"
The panel asks about a mistake you made on a project. You start with:
A "If only I had brought the site manager in earlier."
B "It was not really my fault, because the client changed the plans many times."
C "If only I brought the site manager."
A friend asks how your English is going. You want to say you are making the progress you planned. You say:
A "I am on a plateau."
B "I think I am on track."
C "I am in my comfort zone with everything now, so it is all going well."
You want to admit the cost of the program and keep your decision. You say:
A "Even though the program is expensive, I want to do it."
B "Despite the program is expensive, I want to do it."
C "The program is expensive, so maybe I will think about it again next year."
