Lesson 7 · What Should We Do Next?

Advice, or Not Negotiable?

Two words decide how a recommendation lands in a senior room. One of them invites an argument. The other one ends it, and you only get to use it a few times.

Chapter 1: First Words

What Would You Recommend?

Before that: one comparison with than, and one superlative, about your own business.

Now the real one. Name one thing your company should do next quarter, and one thing it has to do whether anybody likes it or not.

Today’s Goal

One Sentence, and How Hard to Push

1. The Words

a recommendation, an approach, a concern, an issue, a solution, a counter, a discount...

2. One Structure

should, must and have to — and the bare verb that follows all three

3. Your Answer

one recommendation, one obligation, and one thing nobody should do

Should is an invitation to argue with you. Must is a door closing. Both are useful, and the second one costs something every time you use it.

One Question

And What Must You Not Do?

Name one thing your company must not do next year, and say it in one sentence.

Then say why, in one more sentence, as if somebody in the room already disagrees with you.

Chapter 3: The Code

An Open Door, and a Closed One

Should, must, have to

Grammar Discovery

Which One Would You Argue With?

"We should move the whole budget into coverage."

"We have to sign the contracts before the fifteenth."

"We must not cut the price to buy coverage."

"You do not have to read the full report. The summary is enough."

The blue parts are four different amounts of pressure. Two of them leave the door open and two do not. Now look at the red verbs: all four are bare, and that is the same in every one.

JobFormExample
Advice, door openshould + bare verbWe should move the budget.
Obligation from outsidehave to / has to + bare verbWe have to sign before the fifteenth.
Strong, from youmust + bare verbWe must align before the board.
Forbiddenmust not + bare verbWe must not cut the price.
Not necessarydo not have to + bare verbYou do not have to read the full report.
QuestionShould we...? · Do we have to...?Should we wait for the board?
The trapno to after should or mustNot we should to move. Say we should move.

In one line: must not is forbidden, do not have to is optional, and they are not the same thing at all.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Recommendation, and the Pushback

One conversation, three recordings, one page

Dialogue

She Does Not Agree

D
Give me your recommendation in one sentence, and then I am going to disagree with it.
M
We should move the whole workshop budget into coverage, and we must not cut the price to get there.
D
Then here is my concern. A discount would give us the same coverage in six months instead of eighteen.
M
That is a fair concern, and the six months is real. What is not real is that the coverage stays. The discount moves volume for two quarters and then the volume goes wherever the next discount is.
D
You are asking this board for eighteen months of patience. That is a lot to ask.
M
I am. And I would rather ask for it now than explain in two years why we earned nothing. You have to earn the counter. Nobody has ever bought it.
D
All right. What do you have to have from us, and what would you simply like to have?
M
I have to have the budget signed before the fifteenth. I would like a second field team, and I do not have to have it this year. And you — what would make you comfortable at the six-month mark?
Comprehension

About Diane

1. What does she announce she is going to do, before he has even spoken?
She says she is going to disagree with his recommendation, and asks for it in one sentence first.
2. What is her concern, and what alternative does she put on the table?
That a discount would reach the same coverage in six months instead of eighteen.
3. What are the two things she asks him to separate at the end?
What he has to have from the board, and what he would simply like to have.
Listening 1 · A Director

One Sentence, and Then the Defence

A marketing director putting a recommendation to a board. Sound first — no text.

1. What is her recommendation, and how does she describe it?
Move the whole workshop budget into coverage and stop spending on anything else in that segment. She says it is advice and that the room can disagree with it.
2. What is the part that is not advice, and why?
Signing the distributor contracts before the fifteenth, because it is in the contract and nobody in the room can move it.
3. What does she say they must not do, and what is her reason?
They must not cut the price to buy coverage. If they buy the first sale with a discount, they teach the workshop what the product is worth and do not get that back.
Listening 2 · A Podcast

The Word You Only Get to Use Twice

A business podcast on should and must in senior rooms. Sound first — no text.

1. How does he describe should, and why does he say that is useful?
As an invitation. It leaves the door open, which is right when you want the room to argue, because a recommendation that survives an argument is worth four that did not have one.
2. How does he describe must, and what does he advise about it?
As a door closing. He says you spend something every time you use it, so spend it rarely.
3. What happens to the people who arrive with everything at must?
After twenty minutes the room stops hearing the difference, so the one thing that really was non negotiable arrives sounding like all the others.
Listening 3 · Business Radio

There Is No Cheap Way In

A business radio report on winning the independent workshop. Sound first — no text.

1. Who does the report say makes the decision, and under what conditions?
The mechanic, and he decides in front of a customer who is already impatient, so he recommends what he trusts and what he has in his hand.
2. What has happened every time companies tried to buy that position with discounts?
The discount moves volume for two quarters, and then the volume goes wherever the next discount is.
3. What does the report say does work, and how long does it take?
Showing up, training the mechanic, and being the brand that answers the phone when a customer complains. About eighteen months per region, with no six-month version.
