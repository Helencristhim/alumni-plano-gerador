Complementary Activities

Optional extras to enjoy outside class. Mark each one as done after you watch or listen to it.

Talk
TED -- Adam Galinsky, "How to speak up for yourself" (15 min)

Worth a second visit now that you have the language for it. The first time you watched this it was about the room. This time listen to the sentences themselves: almost every example he gives is a recommendation with the pressure turned up or down on purpose.

Tip: write down three sentences he uses to disagree without breaking the relationship. They are the same shape as "that is a fair concern, and".

Podcast
NPR Planet Money -- "How are gas prices set?"

The chain behind every price recommendation in your own market, followed step by step. Half the episode is people explaining why a price cannot move, which is exactly the have-to and must-not distinction you practised today.

Tip: the page has the full transcript. Find three sentences where somebody says something is not possible, and rewrite each one with have to or must not.

Listen on NPR ↗
Video
INSEAD -- "Harvard’s Michael Porter on long-term strategy in a downturn"

An interview built almost entirely on should and must not: what a company has to protect, what it is free to drop, and what it can never do even when the money is tight. It is the register of today’s lesson, at senior speed.

Tip: stop the video every time he says a company should or must not do something, and say the sentence back about your own business.
