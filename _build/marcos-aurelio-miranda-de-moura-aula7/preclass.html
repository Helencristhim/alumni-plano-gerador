Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

What Should We Do Next? -- Advice, or Not Negotiable?

Two words decide how a recommendation lands in a senior room. One invites an argument, the other one ends it. Key words: a recommendation, an approach, a concern, an issue, a solution, to push back, a counter, a mechanic, to earn, a discount. Structure: should, must and have to.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A recommendation -- what you think the company should do, said in one sentence
"My recommendation is one sentence, and then I will defend it."
An approach -- the way you decide to solve something, out of several possible ways
"The issue is not whether the approach works. It is whether we have the patience."
A concern -- something somebody in the room is worried about, said politely
"That is the concern I hear most often, so let me answer it first."
An issue -- a problem that has to be solved before the work can go on
"There is one issue and it is not the budget. It is the eighteen months."
A solution -- the thing that makes the problem go away
"The solution is slower than the problem, and that is the whole difficulty."
To push back -- to disagree and say why, without ending the conversation
"If somebody pushes back on the eighteen months, that is a fair concern."
A counter -- the place in a shop where the customer and the seller actually talk
"You have to earn the counter. Nobody has ever bought it."
A mechanic -- the person who repairs the car and tells the driver which oil to use
"The mechanic decides, and he decides in front of an impatient customer."
To earn -- to get something because you deserved it, and not because you paid for it
"Show up, train the mechanic, and earn the position over eighteen months."
A discount -- money taken off the price to make somebody buy
"The discount moves volume for two quarters and then the volume leaves."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A recommendation is not only what you say. It is how hard you push. "We should move the whole budget into coverage" is advice: the door is open and the room can disagree with you, which is usually what you want. "We have to sign the contracts before the fifteenth" is different. That pressure does not come from you at all — it is in the contract, and nobody in the room can move it. And "we must not cut the price" closes the door completely. Notice one thing that never changes: the verb after all three is bare. Never we should to move, never we must to align.

The pair that costs the most in a real meeting is must not against do not have to, because they look similar and mean opposite things. We must not cut the price is forbidden. You do not have to read the full report is optional: read it if you like, the summary is enough. Say the first when you mean the second and you have just banned something nobody wanted to ban.

Now the part that is about the room and not about grammar. Should is an invitation, and a recommendation that survives an argument is worth four that never had one. Must is a door closing, and you spend something every time you use it, so use it rarely: the person who arrives with everything non negotiable is the person the room stops listening to. When somebody pushes back, the strongest answer has two halves in one breath — concede what is true in the concern, and keep the position: "That is a fair concern, and the discount still only buys two quarters. You have to earn the counter."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Should, Must and Have To

GRAMMAR

Must not is forbidden. Do not have to is optional. They are not the same thing at all.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to give advice the room can argue with. You say:
A "We should to move the budget into coverage."
B "We should move the whole budget into coverage."
C "We must move the budget, it is not for discussion."
The deadline is in the contract and nobody can move it. You say:
A "We have to sign the contracts before the fifteenth."
B "We have sign the contracts before the fifteenth."
C "We should sign the contracts before the fifteenth, more or less."
Cutting the price is forbidden. You say:
A "We do not have to cut the price."
B "We must not cut the price."
C "We must not to cut the price."
Somebody disagrees with your recommendation. You say:
A "You are right. Let me think about a different approach."
B "No. The recommendation stays."
C "That is a fair concern, and I am keeping the recommendation. Here is why."
