Lesson 04 — Pre-class

If You Do, We Will

Eight words from the contract — timeline, milestone, scope creep, retainer, escalate, streamline, follow up, hand over — and the two ways to say if: the real offer, and the picture you paint without committing. Around fifteen minutes.

Stage 1.1: Words from the contract

Vocabulary

You already manage all eight of these in Portuguese. Tap Listen and say each one out loud.

Timeline — the dates from the start of a project to the end
"Can we agree on a timeline before we sign?"
Milestone — a fixed point in the timeline that has to be reached
"The first milestone is the parallel payroll run."
Scope creep — work that grows past the contract, without anyone signing for it
"That is scope creep, and it has cost us two people."
Retainer — a fixed monthly fee that keeps the service available
"Most of our contracts work on a monthly retainer."
Escalate — to take a problem to someone more senior
"If it is not solved in 24 hours, we escalate it to a partner."
Streamline — to make a process simpler and faster, with fewer steps
"If we streamline the approval, we save four days a month."
Follow up — to contact someone again about something you already started
"I will follow up with a proposal by Friday."
Hand over — to pass work, data or responsibility to someone else
"If they hand over clean data, onboarding takes four weeks."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

A reply to a client who asked for something outside the scope. Read it once, then answer.

Hi David,

Two extra reports a month is not in the current scope, but I am not going to say no.

If we move you to a monthly retainer, both reports will sit inside it and you stop worrying about what is included. If your team sends the data by the tenth, the reports will be ready in the same month rather than the following one.

On the timeline: if they hand over clean data in week one, we will finish the onboarding in four weeks instead of six.

And a thought. If we handled your whole back office, you would not need three people on this internally — you would keep one and move the other two. Something to consider.

If that works for you, I will follow up with a revised proposal today.

Fábia
Does Fábia refuse the two extra reports?
A Yes — she says they are not in the scope.
B No — she accepts them, on the condition that the contract changes to a retainer.
Why "if we handled... you would not need" and not "if we handle... you will not need"?
A Because it is a picture she is painting, not an offer on the table yet.
B Because it already happened in the past.
What is missing from every "if" half of these sentences?
A A subject.
B The word will — it never goes inside the if half.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

A real offer, or a picture you paint

In a negotiation these are not two tenses. They are two different levels of commitment.

If + present, will

If you sign this week, we will start on Monday.
If they hand over the data, we will finish in four weeks.

You are committing. Only say it if you can do it.

If + past, would

If we handled everything, you would cut two roles.
If you had a partner here, what would change?

You are exploring. Nothing is promised yet.

The one mistake: "If you will sign this week..." The if half never takes will. The will lives in the other half of the sentence.

Openers worth memorising: "If that works for you..." · "In that case, we would..." · "Let’s say, hypothetically..."

Stage 4: In the negotiation

Quiz
A client asks you to go live two weeks earlier. It is possible, but only if they send the data now.
A "If you will send the data now, we can do it."
B "If you send the data this week, we’ll go live two weeks earlier."
You want the client to imagine outsourcing everything, without promising anything.
A "If we handled the whole back office, you would keep one person instead of three."
B "If we handle the whole back office, you will keep one person instead of three."
A request arrives that is clearly outside the contract. What protects you best?
A Do it once and mention it later if it happens again.
B "Of course — anything outside the agreed scope is quoted separately. If that works for you, I’ll send it today."

Stage 5: The request outside the scope

Reflection
Record ninety seconds in English. A client you value asks for something clearly outside the contract, and says the previous provider never charged for it. Answer: accept the request, attach a condition, explain what changes, and close with a next step. Use one first conditional and one second conditional. Do not write it down first.
