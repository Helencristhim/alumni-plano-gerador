The Pattern

A promise, or a picture?

If they hand over clean data, onboarding will take four weeks.
If you sign this week, we will start the parallel run in October.
If we handled your whole back office, you would cut two roles.
If you had a partner here, how would that change your month?

If + present, will = a real offer · If + past, would = a hypothesis

The second one is not about the past. It is a picture you paint without committing to anything — which is why it is the safer one to open with.

The Pattern

Two tools, two moments

Real offer — on the table

If you send the data, we will start on Monday.
If you sign this week, we will meet the deadline.
If it is not solved in 24 hours, we will escalate it.

Hypothesis — exploring

If we handled everything, you would cut two roles.
If you had a partner here, what would change?
If we streamlined it, you would save four days.

The one mistake to avoid: "If you will sign this week..." — the if half never takes will. The will lives in the other half.

Useful openers: "If that works for you..." · "In that case, we would..." · "Let’s say, hypothetically..."

Practice

Build the condition

If they (hand over) clean data, onboarding (take) four weeks.
If they hand over clean data, onboarding will take four weeks.
If you (sign) this week, we (meet) the deadline.
If you sign this week, we will meet the deadline. (never "if you will sign")
If it (not / be) solved in 24 hours, we (escalate) it to a partner.
If it is not solved in 24 hours, we will escalate it to a partner.
If we (handle) your whole back office, you (cut) two roles.
If we handled your whole back office, you would cut two roles.

Listening 1

Saying yes, with a condition

1. What is the client asking for?
Two extra reports a month, which are not in the scope.
2. Does she refuse?
No — she says yes, on the condition that they move to a retainer.
3. What will happen if they send the data by the tenth?
The reports will be ready in the same month, not the following one.
Listening 2

Painting the picture

1. How many people does the client have doing this internally?
Three — two in payroll and one part-time on tax.
2. What would change if they outsourced?
They would keep one person and move the other two to the finance team they actually need.
3. Why does she not give a price at the end?
Because she asks them to check the internal cost first, so the comparison is theirs.
Your Card

The onboarding timeline

Onboarding plan

Six weeks — or four, if

Week 1Kick-off. If they hand over the employee data in week one, we save two weeks.
Week 2–3Data migration and compliance check. First milestone: clean register.
Week 4Parallel payroll run. If the numbers match, we go live the following month.
Week 5–6Go live. Monthly retainer starts. Anything outside the scope is quoted separately.
Which parts of this timeline depend on the client, not on you?
"The handover in week one and the data quality. If they hand over clean data in week one, we finish in four weeks instead of six."
Say the last line as a sentence that prevents scope creep.
"Anything outside the agreed scope will be quoted separately, so there are no surprises on the invoice."
