Lesson 27 -- Pre-class

Hotels, Reservations, and Handling Problems Like a Pro

Get ready for a late arrival at a hotel in Lyon: a room that was double-booked, a leak, and a bill with a mistake. Learn to say what is being done and what has been done with the passive ("Your room is being cleaned", "The leak has been fixed"). Key words: double-booked, under renovation, leak, non-refundable, room rate, a hold, to be overcharged, to waive, goodwill gesture, to look into.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Double-booked -- when the same room or seat is given to two people by mistake
"I'm sorry, your room was double-booked."
Under renovation -- being repaired and improved, so you cannot use it now
"The pool is under renovation until Friday."
Leak -- water that comes out of a pipe or a ceiling by accident
"There is a leak in the bathroom ceiling."
Non-refundable -- if you cancel, you cannot get your money back
"My rate is non-refundable, so I can't cancel."
Room rate -- the price of a hotel room for one night
"The room rate is one hundred and eighty euros a night."
A hold -- money that a hotel blocks on your card for a few days
"The hotel put a hold of two hundred euros on my card."
To be overcharged -- to pay more than the correct price
"I think I was overcharged for the parking."
To waive -- to decide that someone does not need to pay a fee
"They waived the late checkout fee."
Goodwill gesture -- something a company gives you to say sorry, like a discount
"As a goodwill gesture, breakfast is free tomorrow."
To look into -- to try to find out the cause of a problem
"I'll look into it right away."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Lucimara arrives at her hotel in Lyon at eleven p.m., after a long flight. She is tired, but there is a problem: her room was double-booked, and another guest is in it. Her rate is non-refundable, so she can't just go to another hotel.

The manager looks into it. "A new room is being prepared for you right now," he says. Twenty minutes later, the suite is ready, and the noise from the floor that is under renovation has stopped. The next morning, the second charge on her card has been canceled, and the late checkout fee has been waived as a goodwill gesture. Only one thing hasn't been done yet: the hold on her card will be released after she checks out.

1. Why can't Lucimara go to another hotel?
A Because all the other hotels in Lyon are full during the trade fair week, and she has no time to look.
B Because her rate is non-refundable.
C Because she likes the suite.
2. What is happening while Lucimara waits?
A A new room is being prepared for her.
B The manager is calling the other guest and asking him to leave the room right away.
C Her card is being canceled.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "The late checkout fee has waived by the manager this morning."
B "The second charge has been canceled."
C "The room is cleaning now."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive with Being and Been

GRAMMAR

How to say what is being done now, what has been done, and what has not been done yet.

FormUseExample
is / are being + past participleit is happening nowYour room is being cleaned.
has / have been + past participleit is finished, and the result is important nowThe leak has been fixed.
hasn't / haven't been + past participle ... yetit has not happened up to nowMy room hasn't been cleaned yet.
Has / Have ... been + past participle?a question: is it done?Has my bill been corrected?
RuleUse the passive when the thing (the room, the leak, the bill) is more important than the person who does the work. Never forget being or been: "The room is being cleaned", not "The room is cleaning"; "It has been fixed", not "It has fixed". The simple passive from Lesson 10 still works for facts and finished past actions: "The room was cleaned yesterday."
