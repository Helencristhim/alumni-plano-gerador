Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A regulatory sandbox -- a controlled space where companies can test a new model under relaxed rules for a limited time
"The agency created a regulatory sandbox for electric bus concessions."
A tariff review -- the official process of recalculating what users pay for a public service
"The tariff review for the third batch of highways starts in October."
A contingent liability -- a cost that may have to be paid in the future if a specific event happens
"The guarantee is a contingent liability on the state’s balance sheet."
Environmental licensing -- the process of getting official permission to build a project that affects the environment
"Environmental licensing took longer than construction."
A fiscal framework -- the rules that limit how much a government can spend and borrow
"The new fiscal framework leaves little room for public guarantees."
Administrative discretion -- the freedom officials have to decide how a rule is applied
"Investors dislike administrative discretion when it has no clear limits."
Due diligence -- the detailed investigation an investor does before signing a deal
"The fund’s due diligence found three problems in the contract."
A material breach -- a failure to meet a contract that is serious enough to allow the other side to act
"Missing the investment schedule twice is a material breach."
Force majeure -- an event nobody could control or prevent, such as a flood, that excuses a party from its obligations
"The operator declared force majeure after the bridge collapsed in the flood."
A grandfathering clause -- a rule that allows existing contracts to continue under the old conditions
"Thanks to a grandfathering clause, the new rule does not touch contracts signed before 2025."
A rebalancing claim -- a request by an operator to restore the financial balance of a contract after an event it did not cause
"The floods led to a rebalancing claim of two hundred million reais."
A public consultation -- a period when anyone can send comments on a proposed rule before it is final
"The public consultation received more than four hundred contributions."
A step-in right -- the right of lenders to take control of a project if the operator fails
"The banks used their step-in right to replace the operator."
A termination payment -- the money a government must pay an operator if it ends a concession early
"The termination payment would cost more than the road itself."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

When you tell an investor what a regulator said, you are doing more than repeating words. You are telling them how firm the promise was, and whether it is still true. English does both jobs with two tools: the reporting verb and the tense.

The reporting verb measures certainty. If the director confirmed that the tariff review would begin in October, that is a decision. If she indicated or suggested that tariffs could fall, that is a signal. If she clarified something, she was correcting a misunderstanding, and if she acknowledged that operators had filed a rebalancing claim, she was admitting a problem.

The tense usually moves one step back: “The review will begin” becomes “She confirmed that the review would begin.” But when the fact is still true today, you can keep the present: “She confirmed that contracts signed before 2025 are protected by a grandfathering clause.”

Sometimes nobody wants to own the information. Then English reports it without a person: “The operator is said to be preparing a claim.” For anyone doing due diligence, that structure is a warning. And two Portuguese habits never work here: “she said me” and “she explained me.” Say she told me and she explained it to me.

2. When can you keep the present tense in reported speech?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech and Reporting Verbs

GRAMMAR

The verb measures the promise; the tense says whether it is still true.

JobFormExample
A decisionconfirmed, announced, stated + thatShe confirmed that the review would begin in October.
A signalindicated, suggested, signaled + thatShe indicated that tariffs could fall.
A correction or admissionclarified, acknowledged + thatShe acknowledged that claims had been filed.
Backshiftwill → would, is → was, has → hadThe review would begin in October.
Still truekeep the presentOld contracts are protected.
No personsubject + is said to + verbThe operator is said to be preparing a claim.

Never: she said me that · she explained me the rules · she suggested to delay the auction · it is said that the fund to have signed
