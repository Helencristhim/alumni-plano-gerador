Chapter 4: The Code

How Sure Was the Speaker?

The reporting verb measures the promise; the tense says whether it is still true

Grammar Discovery

Three Verbs Step Back in Time. One Does Not.

"The minister indicated that the framework would be revised by the fourth quarter."

"The agency clarified that operators were not expected to cut investment."

"Sources suggested that the fund had already committed."

"The regulator confirmed that the auction is still scheduled for March."

In the red sentences, the verb moves one step back in time. Why is the blue sentence allowed to stay in the present? And look at the reporting verbs: which one would you build a financial model on, and which would make you build two?

JobFormExample
Report a statementreporting verb + that + one step back in timeShe said that the review would start in May.
Keep what is still trueno backshift when it is still true todayThe agency confirmed that the auction is in March.
A firm statementconfirmed, announced, statedThe ministry confirmed the date.
A signal, not a promiseindicated, suggested, signaledThe minister indicated tariffs could fall.
A correction, warning or admissionclarified, warned, acknowledgedShe acknowledged that claims had been filed.
Report without a personsubject + is said to / is expected to + verbThe operator is said to be preparing a claim.
The trapnever said me, never explained meNot She explained me the rule. Say She explained the rule to me.

In one line: The reporting verb tells the investor how firm the promise was; the tense tells them whether it is still true.

Quick Fire

One Question at a Time

Listening 1 · The News

The Agency Responds

A business news report on a regulator’s statement. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the agency confirm, and what did it clarify?
It confirmed that the tariff review will go ahead in October, and clarified that it would follow the formula written into each contract.
2. How many operators had filed rebalancing claims, and how will the claims be assessed?
Seven operators, and the claims will be assessed individually.
3. What did the director decline to say, and what did the analyst warn?
Whether the floods would be treated as force majeure. The analyst warned that the uncertainty could delay two auctions planned for the first half of next year.
Listening 2 · The Lawyer

Two Clauses Investors Skip

A contracts lawyer speaks at an investor roundtable. Sound first — no text.

1. Which two clauses does the lawyer say investors skip?
The force majeure clause and the grandfathering clause.
2. Why does he say force majeure is not a formality, and what should investors read in it?
Because in a country with floods and droughts it decides who pays when the road is under water. Investors should read the list of events and who has to prove what.
3. What happened to the fund he mentions?
It lost a year of returns because its lawyers assumed the old rules would continue, and the grandfathering clause said otherwise.
