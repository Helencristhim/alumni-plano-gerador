Ten Days, Solved -- Review of Lessons 1 to 9

Four hiccups in ten days, two sentences each, and a trip you can tell properly. Key words: an itinerary, a hiccup, smoothly, to keep track of, a backup plan, worst case, in hindsight, to bounce back, a takeaway, to think on your feet. Structure: a review of the nine structures of Lessons 1 to 9.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An itinerary -- the plan of where you go and when, day by day
"The itinerary was ten days, and three of them had nothing planned."
A hiccup -- a small problem that delays you but does not stop you
"Four hiccups in ten days is a normal number, and none of them was a disaster."
Smoothly -- without problems and without stopping
"The first attempt did not go smoothly, and the second one took forty seconds."
To keep track of -- to stay informed about something as time passes
"I keep track of the claim in a note on my phone."
A backup plan -- a second plan you keep ready in case the first one fails
"By the next morning there was a backup plan: a shirt from the airport shop."
Worst case -- the least good result that could happen
"Worst case, we lose an afternoon, and that did not happen."
In hindsight -- looking back, knowing now what you did not know then
"In hindsight, the whole conversation was one sentence long."
To bounce back -- to recover quickly after something goes wrong
"The first attempt went badly, and the next morning we bounced back in forty seconds."
A takeaway -- the one useful thing you keep from an experience
"The takeaway is not that the English was good. It was quick."
To think on your feet -- to decide quickly, while the situation is still happening
"Three people gave three answers, so I had to think on my feet."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Nothing in this lesson is new. Every structure in it has already had a whole lesson of its own, and the only thing that changes today is that they all arrive at the same time, inside one story: ten days, four hiccups, and two sentences for each hiccup.

Look at what each one is for, rather than what it is called. To say that something started and is not finished, English uses have or has with the past participle: "my bag has not arrived yet". To say how long that has been true, it adds been and -ing: "we have been travelling since five". To ask for something without sounding like an order, it uses could you or would you mind. And to negotiate a way out of a problem, it uses if with the present and will in the other half: "if the room is not ready by four, I will need something else".

The other four do the rest of the work. Because and so put a reason next to a fact, which is what turns "it was good" into an answer. Must and have to read a rule off a page. The past simple and the past continuous tell what happened while something else was going on. And comparatives decide between two options out loud: "this room is quieter than the last one".

The ninth one is the newest, and it is the one that ends an argument before it starts. To repeat what somebody said, English uses told with a person after it and said with nothing after it, and it moves the whole sentence one step into the past: "she told me it left at nine". Put those nine together and you have the block. In hindsight, none of them is difficult. What is difficult is choosing one in the second when a real person is waiting for you to speak, and that is the only thing left to practise.

1. Why does the text say "my bag has not arrived yet" instead of "my bag did not arrive"?
A Because it started before now and is still not finished.
B Because it is more polite at an airport desk.
C Because arrive is an irregular verb.
2. According to the text, what do because and so actually do for you?
A They make a sentence longer, which sounds more advanced.
B They put a reason next to a fact, which is what turns "it was good" into an answer.
C They replace the need for a comparative.
3. What does the text say is the only difficult part left?
A Learning the nine structures, because there are too many of them.
B Remembering the names of the tenses.
C Choosing one of them in the second when a real person is waiting for you to speak.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Nine Structures of Lessons 1 to 9

GRAMMAR

One table for the whole block. Read it by the job, not by the name.

JobFormExample
Give a reasonbecause · so · that is whyIt was quiet, so we slept.
Rules and permissionmust · have to · be allowed toYou have to check in by two.
Started, not finishedhave / has + past participle, with yet, already, stillMy bag has not arrived yet.
Tell what happenedpast simple + past continuous, with when and whileWe were waiting when they called.
Ask politelycould you · would you mind · I was wondering ifCould you store the bags?
Negotiate a way outif + present, will · unlessIf it is not ready by four, I will need something else.
Say how longhave been + -ing, with for and sinceWe have been travelling since five.
Compare and decide-er than · more than · the mostIt is quieter than the other one.
Repeat what you were toldtold + person · said alone · tense one step backShe told me it left at nine.

Never: my bag did not arrive yet · travelling since two days · he said me · more quiet than · if it will not be ready

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You are at the baggage desk and your suitcase is not on the carousel. You say:
A "My bag did not arrive yet, sorry, my English is not very good."
B "My bag has not arrived yet. Here is the tag. When can you deliver it to the hotel?"
C "Where is my bag? Nobody here knows anything."
The room is not ready and you have been up since five. You say:
A "We have been travelling since five, so could you store the bags and tell me a realistic time?"
B "We are travelling since five, I want the room now."
C "It is fine, we will wait here, do not worry about us."
Three people have given you three different times for the same trip. You say:
A "Somebody here said me the wrong time."
B "The woman on the phone told me it left at nine, the site says ten thirty, and the driver told me it was cancelled. Which of the three is right?"
C "Nobody in this company knows what time anything is."
Somebody asks how the trip was. You say:
A "It was good, thank you."
B "It was good but the bag, you know, the bag, and after was fine."
C "It was better than the last one, because we planned less. The only real hiccup was the bag, and it arrived on day two."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

In sixty seconds, tell the whole ten-day trip: where you went, what went wrong, what you said about it, and what you would do differently. Problems included. Record it now, and record it again the week before you fly -- the difference between the two is the point of this block.
