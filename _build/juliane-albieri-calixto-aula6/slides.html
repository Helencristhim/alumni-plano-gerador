Chapter 4: The Code

We Used to...

Old habits, one structure

Grammar Discovery

Four Sentences About Old Habits

"I used to listen to rock on the radio."

"We used to buy CDs and read the lyrics."

"I didn’t use to like pop music."

"Did you use to go to concerts?"

Do you listen to rock on the radio now? Probably not. Did you do it once, or many times? Many times. That is a habit that ended: used to + the simple verb. Now look at the red words: with did and didn’t, the -d goes away: use to.

JobFormExample
A past habit that endedused to + verbI used to listen to the radio.
A past state that changedused to + be / like / haveI used to have a lot of CDs.
Negativedidn’t use to + verbI didn’t use to like pop.
QuestionsDid you use to + verb?Did you use to sing in the car?
One time onlypast simple, not used toI went to a concert in 1998.
The trapno used to for the presentNot I use to stream music. Say I usually stream music.

In one line: used to + verb for habits that ended; with did and didn’t, use to.

Listening 1 · The Mixtape

Mike’s Cassettes

Mike, from Ohio, remembers how he used to make mixtapes in the nineties. Sound first — no text.

1. What did Mike use to do on Sunday evenings?
He used to wait for his favorite songs on the radio and record them.
2. Who did he use to make mixtapes for?
For his friends, and once for a girl he liked.
3. What does he do now?
He makes playlists on his phone in two minutes.
Listening 2 · The Radio DJ

Thirty Years on the Radio

A radio DJ from London talks about how music and listeners have changed. Sound first — no text.

1. How long has he worked on the radio?
For thirty years.
2. How did listeners use to ask for a song?
They used to call the station or send a letter.
3. What surprises him about young listeners today?
They know the old rock songs, often from covers.
