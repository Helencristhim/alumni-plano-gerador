A break from work: music. Key words: a playlist, a track, an album, the lyrics, a band, a cover, a chorus, to stream, a hit, catchy. Structure: used to for past habits.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A playlist -- a list of songs that you choose to listen to together
"I have a playlist for long drives."
A track -- one song on an album or a playlist
"The third track is my favorite."
An album -- a collection of songs that an artist releases together
"I used to listen to the whole album, from the first song to the last."
The lyrics -- the words of a song
"I used to sing the lyrics without understanding them."
A band -- a group of musicians who play together
"My favorite band was from the eighties."
A cover -- a new version of an old song, played by another artist
"This is a cover of an old rock song."
A chorus -- the part of a song that repeats, often after each verse
"Everybody sings the chorus at the concert."
To stream -- to listen to music on the internet without downloading it
"Today I stream everything on my phone."
A hit -- a song that is very popular and successful
"It was a big hit in the nineties."
Catchy -- easy to remember and pleasant, so you sing it all day
"The chorus is so catchy."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

In the nineties, Mike used to wait for his favorite songs on the radio. He used to record every hit on a cassette and make mixes for his friends, track by track.

He didn’t use to skip songs. When he bought an album, he used to listen to all of it and read the lyrics.

Now Mike streams everything on his phone and makes a playlist in two minutes. But his favorite band is still the same.

1. What did Mike use to do with the songs from the radio?
A He used to record them on a cassette.
B He used to download them.
C He used to skip them.
2. Does Mike still make mixes on cassettes?
A Yes, every Sunday.
B No: it was an old habit. Now he makes playlists on his phone.
C Yes, but only for his friends.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “He didn’t used to skip songs.”
B “He didn’t use to skip songs.”
C “He not used to skip songs.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used to -- Old Habits

GRAMMAR

Use used to + verb for habits and states that existed in the past and ended. With did and didn’t, write use to. For one time only, use the past simple.

JobFormExample
A past habit that endedused to + verbI used to listen to the radio.
A past state that changedused to + be / like / haveI used to have a lot of CDs.
Negativedidn’t use to + verbI didn’t use to like pop.
QuestionsDid you use to + verb?Did you use to sing in the car?
One time onlypast simple, not used toI went to a concert in 1998.
The trapno used to for the presentNot I use to stream music. Say I usually stream music.

Never: I use to listen (past) · Did you used to · I didn’t used to · I used to go to a concert in 1998
