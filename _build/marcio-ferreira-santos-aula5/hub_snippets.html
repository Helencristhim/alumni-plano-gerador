Booth Ready -- Holding Your Ground in a Case Discussion

Taking a position and disagreeing well in an academic room. Key words: hypothesis, framework, methodology, insight, assumption, constraint, to calibrate, to iterate. Structure: modal verbs (might, could, should, must) for degrees of certainty.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Hypothesis -- an idea you want to test, not a fact yet
"My hypothesis is that demand is elastic in this category."
Framework -- a structure that organizes how you think about a problem
"We apply the same framework to every category."
Methodology -- the set of steps you follow to reach a result
"The conclusion is only as strong as the methodology."
Insight -- what the numbers teach you, not the numbers themselves
"The insight is that customers only remember twenty prices."
Assumption -- something you accept as true without testing it
"I would challenge the assumption behind that number."
Constraint -- a limit you cannot change, like time or budget
"Our biggest constraint is the size of the data team."
To calibrate -- to adjust a model so that it matches reality better
"We calibrate the model every quarter with real sales."
To iterate -- to repeat a process, improving it a little each time
"We iterate the pricing rules until the error is small."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

The data in this case might suggest that the discount worked, because volume went up in every store. But volume is not the question. Margin fell in nine of the twelve categories, so the discount could be a mistake, or it could be a deliberate investment in market share. We cannot know from one table. A company should never measure a pricing decision with a single number, and margin must be measured at SKU level. Before the next rollout, the team should calibrate the model with real sales. You do not have to rebuild the whole framework, but you must not present the conclusion as a fact.

1. Why does the text say "the data might suggest" instead of "the data suggests"?
A The speaker is not sure, so she offers a careful hypothesis.
B The speaker is completely sure and wants to close the discussion.
C "Might" is always used with data in academic English.
2. What is the difference between "you do not have to" and "you must not"?
A "Do not have to" means it is not necessary; "must not" means it is forbidden.
B They mean exactly the same thing.
C "Must not" is the polite version of "do not have to".
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "The team should to calibrate the model."
B "The team should calibrates the model."
C "The team should calibrate the model."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Modal Verbs

GRAMMAR

Four small words that show exactly how sure you are.

ModalHow sureUse and example
mightLeast sureA careful hypothesis. "This might explain the drop."
couldPossibleAn option or alternative reading. "It could be an investment."
shouldRecommendedA professional recommendation. "Companies should test elasticity."
mustMost sureA strong conclusion. "Margin must be measured at SKU level."
Two rulesA modal always takes the base verb: no to, no -s. And must not = forbidden, while do not have to = not necessary.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each real situation in a case discussion.

You have an idea but you are not sure. You say:
A "This must explain the drop in volume."
B "This might explain the drop in volume."
C "This might to explain the drop in volume."
Another participant says something you disagree with. The best move is:
A "That is a fair point, but I would challenge the assumption behind it."
B "No, that is completely wrong."
C Say nothing and wait for the lecturer.
The lecturer asks you a question and you need a moment. You say:
A "Let me think about that for a second."
B "I do not know, sorry."
C Nothing, and look at the table in silence.
Reading the case twice is a good idea, but nobody checks. You say:
A "You do not have to read it twice, but it helps."
B "You must not read it twice, but it helps."
C "You should not read it twice, but it helps."
