Chapter 3: The Code

Degrees of Certainty

might · could · should · must

Grammar Discovery

Read and Notice

"This might explain the drop in volume."

"The discount could be an investment in market share."

"Companies should test elasticity before a rollout."

"Margin must be measured at SKU level."

Put the four orange words in order, from least sure to most sure.

ModalHow sureUse it to
mightLeast sureOffer a careful hypothesis without committing to it.
couldPossiblePut an option or an alternative reading on the table.
shouldRecommendedGive a professional recommendation, not an order.
mustMost sureState a strong conclusion or a rule you defend.
The formEvery modal takes the base verb: no to, no -s, no -ing. Say "it might explain", never "it might to explain" or "it might explains".

In a case room, might and could open a discussion. Should and must close one.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"This might to explain the drop in volume."
"This might explain the drop in volume."
"The model should works in every region."
"The model should work in every region."
"You must not read the case twice." (meaning: it is not necessary)
"You do not have to read the case twice." (it is not necessary)

A modal always takes the base verb. And be careful: must not means it is forbidden, while do not have to means it is not necessary.

Chapter 4: The Case Discussion

Someone Disagrees

And that is exactly what the room wants

Dialogue

The Case Discussion

P
My reading is simple. The discount worked. Volume went up in every single store.
M
That is a fair point, but I would challenge the assumption behind it. Volume is not the question here.
P
Go on. What is the question, then?
M
Margin. It fell in nine of the twelve categories, so the discount might have bought volume we did not need.
P
Or the company could have decided to buy market share on purpose. We cannot see that in the table.
M
Agreed, and that is my point. With this methodology, both readings survive. We should ask for the SKU-level data.
P
I like that. So your recommendation is to hold the decision until we can calibrate the model?
M
Exactly. A pricing decision must never be measured with a single number.
Comprehension

About Priya

1. What was Priya's first reading of the case?
That the discount worked, because volume went up in every store.
2. What alternative explanation does she offer?
That the company could have decided to buy market share on purpose.
3. How does she summarize his recommendation?
To hold the decision until they can calibrate the model.

Closing the Session

Because margin is the question, and margin fell in nine of the twelve categories.

Listening 2

The Unwritten Rules

A participant explains how the sessions really work. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is a cold call, and how should you prepare for it?
The lecturer may ask you to open the case with no warning, so you should read it twice and arrive with one clear position.
2. What does he say people from outside find strange?
That you are expected to disagree.
3. What will the lecturer assume if you never push back?
That you did not prepare.
