Lesson 25 -- Pre-class

The Post-Mortem -- What Would Have Happened

The payroll bridge is live, and it is time to look back. Key words: post-mortem, hindsight, in retrospect, avoidable, oversight, warning sign, foresee, pay off. Structure: the third conditional -- if + had + past participle, would have + past participle.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Post-mortem -- a meeting after a project to review what went well and what went wrong
"Let's do a quick post-mortem before the next deal starts."
Hindsight -- understanding a situation only after it has happened
"In hindsight, the old data was the real risk."
In retrospect -- when you think now about something in the past
"In retrospect, we should have tested earlier."
Avoidable -- possible to prevent if you act in time
"Most of the delay was avoidable."
Oversight -- a mistake you make because you did not notice something
"Not checking the old data was an oversight."
Warning sign -- something that shows a problem may happen soon
"The strange numbers in December were a warning sign."
Foresee -- to know that something will happen before it happens
"Nobody could foresee how bad the old data was."
Pay off -- to bring a good result after effort or risk
"The three extra weeks of testing paid off."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The payroll bridge went live on March first, and every salary was correct. Now Carlos leads the post-mortem with Diana. They do not look for a guilty person. They look for lessons.

“The old data was our biggest oversight,” Carlos says. “If we had checked it in January, we wouldn’t have needed three extra weeks of testing.” Diana agrees, and adds: “If Tom hadn’t reported the problem early, it would have been a crisis.”

Notice the pattern. None of these things happened: they are an imagined past. In the if part, use had + past participle. In the result, use would have + past participle. Never use would in the if part.

1. What is the goal of the post-mortem?
A To find the person who made the mistake and report them.
B To find lessons for the next project.
C To change the go-live date.
2. Did the team check the old data in January?
A Yes, and that is why the project was on time.
B No. That is why they needed three extra weeks of testing.
C Yes, but only after the committee asked them to do it.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “If we would have checked the data, we would have saved time.”
B “If we had checked the data, we would have saved time.”
C “If we had check the data, we would saved time.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Third Conditional: If We Had..., We Would Have...

GRAMMAR

Use the third conditional to talk about a past that did not happen: an imagined condition and its imagined result.

PartFormExample
Imagined conditionIf + had + past participleIf we had checked the data, ...
Imagined resultwould have + past participle... we would have saved three weeks.
Negativehadn’t ... / wouldn’t have ...If he hadn’t reported it, it would have been a crisis.
Possible resultcould have / might have + past participleWe could have finished earlier.
Orderresult first = no commaWe would have saved time if we had tested earlier.

Never: If we would have checked · If we had check · we would saved · it would be a crisis (for the past)
