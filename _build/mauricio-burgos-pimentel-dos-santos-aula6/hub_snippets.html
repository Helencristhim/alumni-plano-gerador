Stage 1.1: Words for an Argument

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say each word out loud with your own numbers. Eight words, three minutes.

Strategy — the big plan for how to win
"Our strategy is to grow in the middle market."
Approach — the particular way you choose to do something
"Their approach is slower but safer."
Insight — something you understood that was not obvious
"The best insight came from the smallest team."
Sector — a part of the economy, like banking or retail
"Our sector moves more slowly than technology."
Scale — how big something is, in numbers
"The scale is different, but the problem is the same."
Sustainable — something that can continue for a long time
"Growing that fast is not sustainable."
Trend — the direction something is moving over time
"The trend is better than the number."
Trade-off — what you give up to get something else
"Speed or safety — that is the trade-off."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the text. Then answer the questions.

People say our sector is slower than technology, and they are right. But the scale is different too: we have 1,200 clients and eight people, whereas a technology team of eight might have four. Last year we grew eight percent; this year, four. On paper this year is worse. In practice it is better, because last year’s growth came from clients we should never have taken. The trend matters more than the number. Our strategy is to grow the middle market and drop nobody. It is the slowest option on the table and, I would say, the most sustainable. The trade-off is cost: our approach is more expensive than theirs. Unlike them, though, we still know our clients’ names. That was the best insight anyone gave me this year, and it was not mine.
Why does he say this year is better than last year?
A Because the number is bigger.
B Because last year’s growth came from clients they should not have taken.
Why is it "slower than" and not "more slow than"?
A Because slow is a short word, so it takes -er.
B Because it is a comparison of sectors.
What is the trade-off in his strategy?
A It is more expensive than the alternative.
B It is faster but riskier.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Three ways to put two things side by side

Short word → -er. Long word → more. Top of the list → the -est / the most.

More

slow → slower
big → bigger
complex → more complex

Equal

as big as
not as fast as
the same as

The top

the biggest
the most sustainable
the best / the worst

× "more slow" — short word, so: slower
× "more sustainable that ours" — the word is than, not that
× "worst than last year" — two things: worse. Only the top of the list is the worst.

To open a comparison: compared to · unlike · whereas.

Stage 4: In the room

Quiz
Somebody says your market is too different to compare. What is the strongest reply?
A "Yes, it is difficult to compare."
B "The scale is different, but the problem is the same."
Which sentence is correct?
A "This year is worst than last year."
B "This year is worse than last year."
You want to say your approach costs more but lasts longer.
A "It is more expensive, but it is more sustainable. That is the trade-off."
B "It is more expensive and more sustainable, the same."

Stage 5: Defend a position

Reflection
Record two minutes in English. Compare your desk to a desk half its size in another country: which one would you rather run, and why? Use at least three comparisons (one with -er, one with more, one with the most or the best), one number, and name the trade-off out loud. Finish by asking your teacher which one she would pick.
