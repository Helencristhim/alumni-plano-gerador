The Pattern

Look at the endings

Our sector is slower than technology.
Their approach is more sustainable than ours.
That was the best insight of the week.
The trade-off is worse than we expected.

Short word → -er. Long word → more

One or two syllables take -er (slower, bigger, easier). Three or more take more (more sustainable, more complex). For the top of the list: the + -est, or the most. And two irregulars you will use every day: good → better → the best, bad → worse → the worst.

The Pattern

Three ways to put two things side by side

One is more

X is bigger than Y.
X is more complex than Y.
X is better than Y.

They are equal

X is as big as Y.
X is not as fast as Y.
The scale is the same.

Top of the list

X is the biggest in the sector.
X is the most sustainable.
That was the worst quarter.

To open a comparison: compared to · unlike · whereas. "Unlike our sector, technology moves fast." — "We grew 4%, whereas they grew 12%."

Did You Catch It?

About the two markets

1. What does David say is different, and what is the same?
The scale is different; the problem is the same — too many small clients, not enough people.
2. What is his strategy, and how does he compare it to Claire’s?
Grow the middle and drop nothing — slower than hers, but more sustainable.
3. What is the trade-off, in his words?
They pay in cost; Claire pays in relationships.
Listening 1

Two years, one number

1. What are the two growth numbers?
Eight percent last year and four percent this year.
2. Why does she say this year is better, even though the number is worse?
Last year’s growth came from clients they should not have taken, and half are gone; this year’s came from existing clients.
3. What does she say is the most expensive mistake in her sector?
Celebrating the wrong number instead of the trend.
Listening 2

A disagreement on the panel

1. What does he agree the American model is better at?
It is faster and cheaper per transaction.
2. What does the European model keep, in his view?
The client relationship inside the bank.
3. What is his conclusion about which is better?
Neither — they are optimised for different things, and the trade-off is speed against closeness.
