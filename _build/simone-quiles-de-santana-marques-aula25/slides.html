Chapter 3 -- The Comparison Code

Slower · More Complicated · The Most

How to compare one country with another

Discovery

Listen and Notice

What do the orange words have in common? When is it -er, and when is it more?

"CADE is slower than the authority in Spain."

"The Brazilian side is more complicated than the Spanish side."

"The Spanish process is not as fast as we would like."

"The clearance is the longest step in the whole deal."

FormUseExample
short word + -er thancompare two things (slow, long, high, cheap)"CADE is slower than the Spanish authority."
more / less + long word + thancompare two things (complicated, expensive)"Brazil is more complicated than Spain."
(not) as + adjective + asthe same, or not the same"Spain is not as fast as we would like."
the + short word + -estthe top of a group of three or more"The clearance is the longest step."
the most + long wordthe top of a group, long word"Brazil is the most complicated side."

Key idea: short words take -er / -est; long words take more / the most. Never both: "more slower" is wrong. Two irregular ones you use every week: good, better, the best; bad, worse, the worst.

The Structure

Two Things or the Whole Group?

Same deal, three countries. Only the size of the comparison moves.

Two things · -er / more

"The clearance in Brazil is slower and more expensive than in Spain."

Two countries side by side. Always with than.

The same, or not · as ... as

"The New York side is not as complicated as the Brazilian side."

A softer way to say that one is less difficult.

The whole group · the -est / the most

"Brazil is the most complicated jurisdiction in this deal."

Three or more. Always with the.

In a cross-border call, the client always asks the same question: where is it faster, and where is it cheaper?

Dialogue

The Call with New York

R

"Good morning from New York. Simone, before we start, is it true that the clearance in Brazil takes longer than in Spain?"

S

"Yes. The antitrust clearance is the longest step. Our local counsel says about ninety days, so it stays a condition precedent."

R

"And Madrid? Is the Spanish side as slow as the Brazilian side?"

S

"No, it is faster. The only problem in Madrid is the apostille on each power of attorney."

R

"Fine. For us, the biggest issue is tax. Your withholding tax is higher than we expected."

S

"I understand. We will check the treaty, because nobody wants double taxation. Can your tax counterpart send us the numbers?"

R

"Tom will send them today. And the contract: we want English as the prevailing language. It is safer than two versions."

S

"Agreed. But the price is in dollars, and the exchange rate is less stable than last year."

R

"Then let us fix the rate on the signing date. That is the simplest solution for all three sides."
