Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

An Artist Statement, Line by Line -- Reading and Rewriting Your Own Words

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An artist statement -- a short text in which an artist says what the work does and why
"Most open calls ask for an artist statement of no more than three hundred words."
To foreground -- to put one thing at the front, so that it is noticed first
"The new version foregrounds the patients, not the hospital."
To interrogate -- to question an idea closely and critically instead of accepting it
"The work interrogates the way a clinic turns a life into a file."
Materiality -- the physical qualities of what a work is made of, and what they do to it
"The materiality matters here: it is the same paper the hospital uses."
A preoccupation -- a subject an artist keeps returning to, year after year
"Care has been her central preoccupation since 2015."
To situate -- to place a work inside a wider context, tradition or debate
"The second paragraph situates the project within the medical humanities."
Jargon -- words that only insiders understand, used where plain ones would do
"A jury reads four hundred statements, and jargon is the first thing it skips."
To engage with -- to work seriously with a subject or a thinker, not just mention them
"The project engages with Kleinman on illness and meaning."
Legible -- clear enough for a reader to follow and understand
"A good statement makes the work legible without explaining it away."
Opaque -- hard to see through or understand, sometimes on purpose
"Some statements stay opaque on purpose, and juries rarely reward it."
At stake -- what could be won or lost, and so what really matters in a work
"In one sentence, tell the jury what is at stake."
To distill -- to reduce something to its most essential part
"Distill ten years of research into three sentences."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The first version of the artist statement began like this: My practice is an ongoing investigation of the waiting room. Through the accumulation of small objects and the documentation of the hours patients spend there, the work interrogates the organization of time.

The editor read it and asked one question for every noun: who is doing it? Nobody collected the objects and nobody recorded the hours. The artist had disappeared from her own work, and the text had become opaque. So they kept the one abstract noun that was precise, materiality, and cut the rest of the jargon.

The second version reads: I record how long each patient waits, and I collect what they leave behind. The work foregrounds the absence of the patient. It is shorter, it is legible, and a jury can tell in ninety seconds what is at stake.

1. "The documentation of the hours." Which verb is hiding inside the noun?
A Document. The noun is the verb frozen, and the sentence no longer says who does it.
B Hour. The noun is made from the word that follows it.
C Doctor. Documentation is the work that doctors do.
2. Why did the editor call the first version opaque?
A Because it was too short to explain the project.
B Because the words were spelled in a very formal way.
C Because every process was a noun, so nobody in it was doing anything.
3. Why did they keep the word materiality?
A Because the jury asked for it by name.
B Because it is precise and has no shorter, plainer way to say it.
C Because it is the longest word in the statement.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Nominalization

GRAMMAR

The grammar of every institutional text you read.

RuleFormExample
Verb into noun-tion, -ment, -ance, -al, -ureorganize → organization, engage → engagement, refuse → refusal
Adjective into noun-ity, -ness, -encefragile → fragility, absent → absence, empty → emptiness
The patternthe + NOUN + of + whatthey document the hours → the documentation of the hours
What it gives youa whole process in one wordThe documentation took three years.
What it coststhe person doing it disappearsthe documentation of the hours — by whom?
The balanceone abstract noun per sentence, and you as a subject somewhereI record how long each patient waits.

Never: the organize of time · the absent of the patient · the documentation the hours (no of) · the fragileness of the paper.

Stage 2: Put the Statement in Order

Order

Six sentences of a legible artist statement. Put them in the order a jury expects to read them.

?"I am a Brazilian artist working between contemporary art and health."
?"Since 2015, my central preoccupation has been the language of care."
?"I record what patients and caregivers say, and I keep their words intact."
?"The work engages with the medical humanities, and with Arthur Kleinman in particular."
?"Its materiality is deliberate: hospital paper, hospital light, hospital furniture."
?"What is at stake is who gets to tell the story of an illness."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the sentence you would actually say.

A juror asks what you do. You answer:
A "My practice is an investigation of the documentation of care."
B "I record what patients say about illness, and I turn it into sound and paper."
C "The organize of care is my work."
An editor says your second paragraph is opaque. You reply:
A "Good. Could we cut the jargon and put me back as the subject of the verbs?"
B "Opaque is the style of the art world, so it stays."
C "I will add more abstract nouns to explain it."
You want to keep one abstract noun because it is precise. You choose:
A "The accumulation of the realization."
B "The investigation of the investigation."
C "The materiality of the work matters: it is hospital paper."
The jury wants one sentence on what matters. You say:
A "What is at stake is who gets to tell the story of an illness."
B "At stake it is the story of the illness, who tells."
C "The stake is interesting."
