Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

The Day I Decided to Leave -- The Story of a Turning Point

Years of background and one short morning that ended it. Key words: a turning point, to snap, gridlock, relentless, restless, daunting, to mull something over, to pack up, to talk somebody out of something, on a whim, the last straw, to take the plunge. Structure: past simple against past continuous, told as a story.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A turning point -- the moment when a situation changes direction for good
"That Tuesday was the turning point, and I knew it."
To snap -- to suddenly lose patience after putting up with something for years
"I was sitting in traffic and I just snapped. Quietly."
Gridlock -- traffic so heavy that nothing moves at all
"Two hours of gridlock, twice a day, for eleven years."
Relentless -- never stopping and never getting any easier
"The noise was relentless. You stop hearing it and it is still there."
Restless -- unable to sit still or be satisfied where you are
"They said I was being restless. I was restless for two years."
Daunting -- frightening because it is large or difficult
"Packing eleven years into boxes was daunting for about a week."
To mull something over -- to think about something slowly, for a long time, before deciding
"We spent that whole spring mulling it over."
To pack up -- to put everything you own into boxes and leave
"In September we packed up the apartment."
To talk somebody out of something -- to persuade somebody not to do the thing they had decided to do
"Two people talked me out of it in one afternoon."
On a whim -- suddenly, with no planning at all
"It was not on a whim. I mulled it over for two years."
The last straw -- the final small problem that makes you finally act
"People ask what the last straw was. It was not the train."
To take the plunge -- to finally do the big thing you kept postponing
"In March I packed up the apartment and took the plunge."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

For eleven years Ana was living in Sao Paulo and spending three hours a day in the car. The traffic was relentless, and she was telling everybody she was fine.

Then one Tuesday she was sitting in gridlock when she snapped. Quietly. It was not one big problem, it was the last straw: the small one that came after a thousand others. That afternoon was the turning point.

It was not on a whim. While she was mulling it over, her husband found the house. Two people tried to talk her out of it and said she was being restless. In March she packed up the apartment and took the plunge. It was daunting for about a week.

1. Why "she was sitting in gridlock when she snapped" and not "she sat in gridlock when she snapped"?
A Because sitting is longer than snapping as a word.
B Because the sitting was the background that was already going on, and the snapping was the event that cut it.
C Because sit is an irregular verb.
2. "While she was mulling it over, her husband found the house." Why while and not when here?
A Because while introduces the long background, and when introduces the short event.
B Because when cannot start a sentence in English.
C Because there are two people in the sentence.
3. "In March she packed up the apartment and took the plunge." Why are both verbs in the plain past?
A Because March is a month and months take the simple past.
B Because the two actions happened at the same time.
C Because they are a sequence of finished events, one after the other.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Background and the Event

GRAMMAR

Which layer of the story each tense carries.

FormIts jobExample
was / were + -ingThe background: already going on, and it lastedI was sitting in gridlock.
plain pastThe event: it happened once and it was overI snapped.
whenIntroduces the event... when I made the decision.
whileIntroduces the backgroundWhile I was mulling it over ...
A sequenceEvents in a row: all plain pastI packed up and took the plunge.
State verbsknow, understand, want, believe take NO -ingI knew it before I said it.

Never: I sat in gridlock when I snapped · While I mulled it over · I was knowing the answer · In March I was packing up and I was taking the plunge.
