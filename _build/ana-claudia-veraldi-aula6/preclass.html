Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

The Day I Decided to Leave -- The Turning Point

Telling the story of the day everything changed. Key words: a turning point, the last straw, to snap, gridlocked, to dawn on somebody, on a whim, to talk somebody out of it, reckless, to hand in your notice, to go through with, to burn your bridges, second thoughts. Structure: past simple vs past continuous -- the scene that was already running, and the event that changed it.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A turning point -- the moment where an important change begins
"That Tuesday was the turning point of my whole life."
The last straw -- the final small problem that makes you act, after many others
"Fifty five minutes for a mile and a half. That was the last straw."
To snap -- to suddenly lose your patience after holding it for a long time
"It was not the worst morning. It was the morning where I snapped."
Gridlocked -- so blocked by traffic that nothing moves at all
"The avenue was completely gridlocked in both directions."
To dawn on somebody -- to become clear to somebody for the very first time
"It slowly dawned on me that I did not have to live that way."
On a whim -- suddenly, with no planning at all behind it
"People think I moved on a whim. It took me four months."
To talk somebody out of it -- to persuade somebody not to do what they were planning
"My brother spent a month trying to talk me out of it."
Reckless -- acting without thinking about the risk
"A colleague told me I was being reckless at forty three."
To hand in your notice -- to tell your employer formally, in writing, that you are leaving
"Six weeks later I handed in my notice."
To go through with -- to actually do the difficult thing you had decided to do
"I nearly did not go through with it. Twice."
To burn your bridges -- to destroy any chance of going back to what you left
"Everybody warned me I was burning my bridges. They were wrong."
Second thoughts -- the doubts that arrive after you have already decided
"I had second thoughts at three in the morning, every week."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

It was an ordinary Tuesday in March. Ana was sitting in her car on the same avenue she drove down every morning. The rain was falling, nothing was moving, and the radio was playing something cheerful. The road was completely gridlocked.

Then she looked at the clock. Fifty five minutes, two and a half kilometers. That was the last straw. Something snapped. She turned the radio off and drove the rest of the way in silence. While she was driving, it dawned on her that she did not have to keep living that way.

It looked like a decision taken on a whim. It was not. She spent four months doing the arithmetic, her brother tried to talk her out of it, and she had second thoughts almost every week. But she went through with it. That Tuesday was the turning point.

1. Why does the text say "was sitting" and not "sat" in the first line?
A Because sitting in a car is always described with -ing in English.
B Because it is the background: it was already happening when the important thing arrived.
C Because the action was finished and complete.
2. "Then she looked at the clock. Something snapped." Why past simple here?
A Both happen in a second, and both move the story forward.
B Because they matter less than the rain and the radio.
C Because past simple is more formal than past continuous.
3. In "While she was driving, it dawned on her", which half is the long action?
A "It dawned on her" — the realization lasted the whole drive.
B "She was driving" — while always introduces the longer action.
C Neither. Both halves are equally long.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Simple vs Past Continuous

GRAMMAR

The continuous is the camera. The simple is the news.

FormWhat it doesExample
was / were + -ingThe background, already runningNothing was moving.
past simpleThe event that moves the story onThen my phone rang.
when + past simpleThe interruptionI was sitting there when the call came.
while + past continuousThe long action the event lands insideWhile I was waiting, I decided.
two past continuousTwo long actions at the same timeShe was cooking while I was packing.
past simple in sequenceOne thing after anotherI stood up, walked out and drove home.
stative verbsknow, want, understand, mean: never -ingI knew it was the last straw.

Never: I was deciding to leave · when I was sitting, the phone was ringing · I was knowing. A decision, a phone ringing and a moment of knowing are all too short to be the scenery.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

A colleague asks where you were when you decided to leave. You answer:
A "I was deciding it in the traffic on a Tuesday."
B "I was sitting in traffic on a Tuesday when it happened."
C "I sat in traffic when it was happening."
You want to describe the scene around you at that moment. The natural version is:
A "The rain was falling, nothing was moving and the radio was playing."
B "The rain fell, nothing moved and the radio played."
C "The rain was fall, nothing was move and the radio was play."
Somebody says it sounds like you left on a whim. You correct them politely:
A "No. You do not understand my situation at all."
B "It looked like a whim from outside. It actually took me four months."
C "I was knowing exactly what I did from the first day."
A colleague admits she is having doubts about the same move. You answer honestly:
A "I had second thoughts every week for a year, and I still went through with it."
B "Do not worry, everything is always perfect afterwards."
C "If you have doubts, you are clearly making a mistake."
