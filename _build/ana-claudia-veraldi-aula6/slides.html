Fifty Five Minutes, One Mile

A British man on the exact morning he stopped. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What was happening around him while he sat in the car?
Nothing was moving in either direction, the road was completely gridlocked, and the radio was playing something cheerful.
2. What exactly made that morning different from every other one?
Nothing, really. He says it was not the worst morning he had ever had — it was simply the one where something snapped. Fifty five minutes for a mile and a half.
3. What did he do that same day, and what did he do six weeks later?
That day he did nothing at all: he drove to the office, sat through three meetings and went home. Six weeks later he handed in his notice.
Chapter 4: The Code

The Scene and the Event

was sitting · happened · while · when — the four pieces every story needs

Grammar Discovery

What Do the Orange Verbs Do?

"I was sitting in traffic when it happened."

"Nothing was moving, and the radio was playing."

"While I was washing up, I decided."

"I stood up, walked out and drove home."

Two of these verbs take time, and two of them happen in a second. The long ones paint the room. The short ones move the story. That is the whole thing.

FormWhat it doesExample
past continuous (was / were + -ing)The background, already running.Nothing was moving.
past simpleThe event that moves the story on.Then my phone rang.
when + past simpleThe interruption.I was sitting in traffic when the call came.
while + past continuousThe long action the event lands inside.While I was waiting, I decided.
two past continuousTwo long actions at the same time.She was cooking while I was packing.
past simple in sequenceOne thing after another.I stood up, walked out and drove home.
stative verbsknow, want, understand, mean — never take -ing.I knew it was the last straw.

In one line: the continuous is the camera; the simple is the news.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I was deciding to leave on that Tuesday."
"I decided to leave on that Tuesday."
"I was sitting in traffic when my phone was ringing."
"I was sitting in traffic when my phone rang."
"I was knowing it was the last straw."
"I knew it was the last straw."
"When I was driving home, I was deciding to sell the apartment."
"While I was driving home, I decided to sell the apartment."

Three of these four are the same error: a point in time dressed up as a background. A decision, a phone ringing, a moment of knowing — none of them lasts long enough to be the scenery.

Comprehension

About Greta

1. What is Greta thinking of doing, and what does her family say about it?
She is thinking of making the same move Ana made. Everyone in her family says she is being reckless.
2. What does Greta say is actually stopping her?
Second thoughts, every single week. Not the plan itself — the doubt that keeps coming back after she has decided.
3. What does Greta assume about Ana's decision? Find her exact words.
"So it happened on a whim, then." She assumes it was sudden. Ana corrects her: it looked like a whim, but it took four months of arithmetic.
Listening 2

The Eight Months Nobody Mentions

A German woman on the part that comes after deciding. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. How long did the deciding take, and how long did the doubting take?
The deciding took about four minutes, on a Sunday, while she was washing up. The doubting took the rest of the year — second thoughts roughly every second week.
2. What did her brother and her colleague say to her?
Her brother told her she was burning her bridges. A colleague said she was being reckless at forty three.
3. What did her grandmother say, and how does she describe her own decision at the end?
Her grandmother said she was not deciding whether to be happy, but which problems she would rather have. She calls her decision a frightened one, taken very slowly — not a brave one taken on a whim.

1. Say the 07:40 line as a full sentence. Which tense, and why?
"At twenty to eight I was still sitting on the same avenue, nothing was moving and the radio was playing." Past continuous — all three are scenery, not events.
2. Say the 08:09 line. Why is this one different?
"At nine minutes past eight I turned the radio off." Past simple — it happened in a second and it moved the story on. It is the news, not the camera.
3. Join 08:07 and 08:09 into one sentence, using when.
"I had been there for fifty five minutes and I was still going nowhere when I turned the radio off." The long one sets the scene; when introduces the event.
