Lesson 22 -- Hedging & Softening Language

Series
The Diplomat -- Season 1, Episode 1 "The Cinderella Thing"

Kate Wyler, a career diplomat, becomes the American ambassador in London in the middle of an international crisis. Connection to Lesson 22: diplomats rarely say "no" directly. Listen for how characters soften bad news and disagree without breaking the relationship.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Write down three sentences with "might", "could" or "I would suggest", and say which blunt sentence each one replaces.

Talk
"The secret to giving great feedback" -- LeeAnn Renninger, TED

A short talk from TED's The Way We Work series on a simple formula for feedback that people actually accept. Connection to Lesson 22: it is constructive feedback in four steps, the same structure Eduarda used with Ryan and with the client CEO.

Tip: listen once without subtitles. Then write the four steps and one hedged sentence for each step about a real model or deck at work.

Video
"How to speak so that people want to listen" -- Julian Treasure, TED

A sound expert explains the habits that make people stop listening, like complaining and dogmatism, and how tone changes the way a message comes across. Connection to Lesson 22: hedging is one of the tools that keeps the other side listening.

Tip: watch the first eight minutes. Pick one habit he describes and say how you could avoid it in your next feedback conversation.
