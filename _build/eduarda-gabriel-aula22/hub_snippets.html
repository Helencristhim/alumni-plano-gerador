Lesson 22 -- Pre-class

Hedging & Softening Language

Project Atlas: a client CEO built his own business plan, and the revenue forecast is too high. Key words: blunt, sugarcoat, caveat, constructive, tentative, reservation, room for improvement, come across as. Structure: hedging with might / could, it seems that, a bit / slightly and I would suggest + verb-ing.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Blunt -- saying something very directly, without trying to be polite
"Don't be too blunt with the client."
Sugarcoat -- to make bad news sound better than it really is
"I won't sugarcoat it: the forecast is too high."
Caveat -- a warning that limits or qualifies what you say
"The plan is strong, with one caveat: the growth rate."
Constructive -- helpful and intended to make something better
"Thank you for the constructive feedback."
Tentative -- not certain or final; it can still change
"These are only tentative numbers."
Reservation -- a doubt you have about a plan or an idea
"I have some reservations about the growth rate."
Room for improvement -- space to make something better than it is now
"The model is good, but there is some room for improvement."
Come across as -- to give other people a particular impression
"I don't want to come across as rude."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Marcus Hale, the CEO of Atlas Logistics, built his company's business plan himself. His forecast shows revenue growing twenty percent a year. Eduarda's team thinks that is too high, but Marcus is proud of the plan, and her director warns her: do not be blunt, but do not sugarcoat it either.

So Eduarda starts with what works: the cost structure is excellent. Then she adds one caveat: It seems that the growth rate might be a little optimistic. The fact is the same, but the words are softer. She does not want to come across as negative.

Finally, she gives a way forward: I would suggest adding a second scenario with ten percent growth. She calls the new numbers tentative and offers to review them together. Marcus still has a reservation or two, but he thanks her for the constructive feedback.

1. Why does Eduarda say the growth rate "might be a little optimistic"?
A Because she is not sure about anything in the plan and wants Marcus to make the final decision alone.
B To sugarcoat the problem so Marcus never sees it.
C To soften the message without changing the fact.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "I would suggest adding a second scenario."
B "I would suggest to add a second scenario with lower growth."
C "I suggest you to add a second scenario."
3. What does Eduarda praise before she gives the caveat?
A The revenue forecast of twenty percent a year, because it is very ambitious.
B The cost structure.
C The second scenario.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Hedging and Softening

GRAMMAR

Use these small words to make a message softer. The facts stay the same.

JobFormExample
Less certainmight / may / could + verbThe forecast might be too high.
Your impressionIt seems (that) + clauseIt seems that the margins are high.
A smaller problema little / a bit / slightly + adjectiveThe deadline is a bit tight.
A recommendationI would suggest + verb-ingI would suggest checking the data.
An offerWe could + verbWe could review it together.
Negative, softenedmight not / may not + verbThis approach might not work for investors.

Never: I suggest you to check... · It might to be... · I would suggest to add...

Stage 2: The Feedback in Order

Order

Six moves from the Project Atlas feedback, out of order. Listen first, then put them in the right order.

?"She suggested a second scenario with lower growth."
?"Ryan took the feedback well and updated the model before the meeting."
?"Eduarda opened Ryan's model the night before the client meeting."
?"Instead of being blunt, she started with what worked in the model."
?"Then she added a caveat: the growth rate might be a little optimistic."
?"She saw that the revenue forecast was too high."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose what a diplomatic professional would say.

A junior analyst's slides have too much text. You say:
A "Your slides are bad. There is too much text."
B "The slides are perfect, so please don't change anything before the client meeting."
C "The slides might be a bit heavy. I would suggest cutting some text."
The client asks if the dates are final. They can still change. You say:
A "These dates are tentative, so they might change."
B "These dates are blunt."
C "These dates are completely final, and nothing can change them, not even the client."
You want to recommend a lower forecast. You say:
A "I suggest you to lower the forecast before you send the plan to the investors."
B "I would suggest lowering the forecast."
C "I would suggest to lower the forecast."
A colleague gives you helpful comments on your memo. You say:
A "Thanks for the constructive feedback."
B "Thanks for sugarcoating my memo for me, I really appreciate all the time you spent on it."
C "Your feedback came across as a caveat."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
