Chapter 3 -- The Code

It Might Be a Little High

Softening a message without changing the facts

Grammar Discovery

What Do the Purple Words Have in Common?

"The growth rate might be a little optimistic."

"It seems that the margins are slightly higher than the market."

"I would suggest adding a second scenario."

"We could review the numbers together on Monday."

Remove the purple words. What happens to each message?

UseFormExample
Make a fact less certainmight / may / could + verb"The forecast might be too high."
Show it is your impressionIt seems (that) / It appears (that) + clause"It seems that the margins are high."
Make the problem smallera little / a bit / slightly + adjective"The deadline is a bit tight."
Recommend, not orderI would suggest + verb-ing / We could + verb"I would suggest checking the data."
Neversuggest + you toNot "I suggest you to check..."
Grammar Rule

Blunt or Hedged?

BluntHedged (same fact)
"The forecast is wrong.""The forecast might need another look."
"The margins are too high.""It seems that the margins are slightly high."
"Change the model.""I would suggest changing the model."
"The deadline is impossible.""The deadline could be a bit tight."
Clear, but it can come across as rude.Still clear, and the relationship stays safe.

Chapter 4 -- Getting There

Project Atlas: The Soft No

One conversation, two recordings, one email

Dialogue

Eduarda and Ryan

R
Eduarda, did you have a chance to look at my model? I'm a bit nervous about tomorrow.
E
I did, Ryan. First, the structure is really clear, and the debt schedule is excellent.
R
Thanks. But I can feel a "but" coming. Please don't sugarcoat it.
E
Okay, one caveat. It seems that the growth rate might be a little optimistic. Twenty percent a year is high for this market.
R
I used the CEO's own numbers. I didn't want to come across as negative.
E
I understand. But we have to be honest with the client, not blunt. I would suggest adding a second scenario with ten percent growth.
R
That makes sense. Should I call the numbers tentative in the deck?
E
Yes, that's a good idea. And if the CEO has reservations, we listen first.
R
Got it. I'll update the model tonight. Thanks for the constructive feedback.
E
Anytime. It's a strong model, Ryan. There's just some room for improvement.
Comprehension

He will update the model.

Real Document

The Recommendations Email

From: Eduarda Gabriel, Houlihan Lokey
To: Marcus Hale, CEO, Atlas Logistics
Re: Project Atlas -- Our Initial Recommendations

Dear Marcus,

Thank you for a very open discussion yesterday. Your plan is ambitious, and the cost structure is one of the strongest we have seen this year.

We do have one caveat. It seems that the revenue forecast might be slightly optimistic for the current market. Investors could ask difficult questions about a growth rate of twenty percent a year.

I would suggest presenting two scenarios: your original plan and a more conservative case with ten percent growth. We could review both together on Thursday, if that works for you.

Best regards,
Eduarda

1. What part of the plan does Eduarda praise first?
The cost structure.
2. What is the caveat in the email?
The revenue forecast might be slightly optimistic for the current market.
3. What does she recommend?
Presenting two scenarios: the original plan and a conservative case with ten percent growth.

Self-Assessment

What I Learned

I know eight key terms for giving feedback.
I can make a fact less certain with "might", "may" or "could".
I can show my impression with "It seems that...".
I can make a problem smaller with "a bit", "a little" or "slightly".
I can recommend with "I would suggest" + verb-ing, never "suggest you to".
I can give honest feedback to a client without being blunt.
