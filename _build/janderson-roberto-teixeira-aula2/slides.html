Chapter 3: The Code

What Already Happened

One tense, three shapes, and one word that carries the past for all of them

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"We booked a table and the restaurant was closed."

"I took a shortcut and I ended up in a small square."

"I did not go anywhere on Sunday."

"Did you take the day off?"

All four sentences are about the same weekend. In the first two the past is inside the verb. In the last two the verb looks like the present. Where did the past go?

FormWhat it saysExample
Regular verbs: verb + EDMost verbs simply take -ed. The same form for I, you, he, she, we and they — no S anywhere."We booked a table."
"I walked away."
Irregular verbs: a new wordAbout a hundred common verbs change completely. You learn them one at a time, and the ten most common cover half of everything you say.go → went · take → took
have → had · be → was / were
Negative: did not + plain verbDID carries the past, so the main verb goes back to its plain form."I did not go anywhere."
NOT "I did not went"
Question: did + subject + plain verbExactly the same idea. DID comes first and the verb stays plain."Did you take the day off?"
NOT "Did you took"
The exception: to beWas and were never borrow DID. They make their own negative and their own question."The restaurant was not open."
"Were you on your own?"

The whole tense is one decision: is the past inside the verb, or inside DID? It is never inside both.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I did not went anywhere on Sunday."
"I did not go anywhere on Sunday."
"You booked a table on Saturday?"
"Did you book a table on Saturday?"
"Did the museum was crowded?"
"Was the museum crowded?"

Once did appears, the past is already there, so the main verb goes back to its plain form — did not go, did you book. A question in English needs that did at the front; a rising voice is not enough, as it is in Portuguese. And was and were work alone: they never take did. Read the correct versions aloud twice.

Comprehension

About Claire

1. What did Claire book, and for when?
A table for lunch.
2. Why could she not have lunch there?
The restaurant closed for a private party.
3. What does she say about museums on a Sunday?
That they are always crowded.
4. How does she react when he says his daughter loved it?
She says that is the correct answer.
Listening

The Message About Saturday

A voice message from a friend. Sound first -- no text.

1. What time did they book the table for, and what happened when they arrived?
One o'clock, and the restaurant was closed.
2. Why did they give up on the square?
It was so crowded that they gave up after ten minutes.
3. How does she describe the afternoon in the park?
The best afternoon she had all month.
Listening 2

Eleven Days and Nowhere to Go

A short personal story from a podcast. Sound first -- no text.

1. How many days off did he take, and where did he travel?
Eleven days, and he did not travel anywhere.
2. Why were the first three days terrible?
He felt guilty and he checked his email far too often.
3. What did he end up knowing better?
His own neighbourhood, better than after fifteen years of living in it.

The Habit

Yesterday, Out Loud

Before the next class, say three sentences about yesterday out loud, once a day. One thing you did, one thing you did not do, and one question you would ask somebody else about their day.

The past tense does not become automatic by reading a table. It becomes automatic by leaving your mouth.
