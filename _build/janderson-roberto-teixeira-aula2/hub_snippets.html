Lesson 02 -- Pre-class

The Weekend That Actually Happened

How to answer "how was your weekend?" in forty seconds instead of four: one thing you did, one thing that did not go to plan, and one question back. Key expressions: a day off, to catch up on, nearby, to turn out, on my own, worth it, crowded, to book, a shortcut, to end up. Structure: past simple, regular and irregular verbs.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. These ten stay here in your material -- they do not disappear after the class.

A day off -- a day when you do not go to work
"I had one day off in three weeks, so I planned it carefully."
To catch up on -- to finally do something you did not have time for before
"At the weekend I like to catch up on the things I did not have time for."
Nearby -- close to where you are
"I found a small museum nearby and I decided to go."
To turn out -- to end in a way you did not expect
"I stayed until the end because I wanted to know how it would turn out."
On my own -- alone, without anybody else
"By half past one I was completely on my own in a part of the city I did not know."
Worth it -- good enough for the time or the money you spend
"The queue was long, but my daughter loved it, so it was worth it."
Crowded -- with too many people in one place
"The museum was so crowded that the queue went around the corner."
To book -- to reserve a table, a room or a ticket before you arrive
"We wanted to book a table for one o'clock."
A shortcut -- a faster way to get from one place to another
"I took a shortcut through a street I had never seen before."
To end up -- to be somewhere, or do something, that you did not plan
"When I walk with no plan, I always end up somewhere new."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Janderson had one day off in three weeks and he planned it carefully. He booked a table for lunch, he found a small museum nearby, and on Friday night he wrote the whole thing down.

Nothing worked. The restaurant closed for a private party and nobody called him. The museum was so crowded that the queue went around the corner, and after twenty minutes in the sun he gave up. He did not eat anything until two o'clock and he did not enjoy the first hour at all.

Then he took a shortcut through a street he did not know and he ended up in a small square with four tables outside a bakery. He sat there for two hours, completely on my own — his words, and the grammar is his too. He read half a newspaper and he did not look at his telephone once.

It turned out to be the best day he had all month, and none of it was planned. On Monday morning a colleague asked him: did you go anywhere at the weekend? Was it worth it? And this time he had something to say. He also caught up on two nights of sleep, which he says was the real achievement.

1. "He did not eat anything until two o'clock." Why is it eat and not ate?
A Because the sentence is about the present.
B Because did already carries the past, so the main verb goes back to its plain form.
C Because eat is an irregular verb and irregular verbs never change.
2. Which of these verbs from the text is regular?
A went
B booked
C took
3. "Was it worth it?" Why is there no did in this question?
A Because was and were make their own questions and never borrow did.
B Because the question is informal.
C Because the sentence has no subject.
4. According to the text, why did the day turn out well?
A Because the restaurant opened later.
B Because the part he did not plan was the part he enjoyed.
C Because the museum was empty in the afternoon.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Past Simple

GRAMMAR

One tense covers everything that already finished. The whole difficulty is one question: is the past inside the verb, or inside did? It is never inside both.

FormWhat it saysExample
Regular verbs: verb + EDMost verbs simply take -ed, and the form is the same for every subject. No S anywhere."We booked a table."
"He walked away."
Irregular verbs: a new wordAbout a hundred common verbs change completely. The ten most common cover half of what you say every day.go → went · take → took · have → had
find → found · eat → ate · sit → sat
Negative: did not + plain verbDid carries the past, so the main verb loses it."I did not go anywhere."
NOT "I did not went"
Question: did + subject + plain verbSame idea, and English needs that did. A rising voice is not enough, as it is in Portuguese."Did you take the day off?"
NOT "You took the day off?"
The exception: to beWas and were work alone. They never borrow did."The restaurant was not open."
"Were you on your own?"
The words that give it awayYesterday, last weekend, two hours ago, in twenty nineteen, when I was twenty — all of them close the door on the present."Last weekend I took a day off."

You already recognise every one of these forms when you read them. The work now is producing them at speed, in a corridor, before you have had time to think.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

It is Monday morning and a colleague stops you in the corridor. Choose the best thing to say.

She asks how your weekend was. The best answer is:
A "Fine, thank you."
B "Quiet. I did not do very much on Saturday, and on Sunday I took my daughter to a museum. What about you?"
C "I am going to a museum with my daughter."
You want to say that you stayed at home and did nothing:
A "I did not went anywhere."
B "I did not go anywhere."
C "I not went anywhere."
You want to ask about her Saturday:
A "You did something on Saturday?"
B "What did you do on Saturday?"
C "What did you did on Saturday?"
You want to ask whether the restaurant was open:
A "Was the restaurant open?"
B "Did the restaurant was open?"
C "Did the restaurant open was?"
The queue was terrible but your daughter loved it. You say:
A "It was very full of people but the final result was positive."
B "It was crowded, but it was worth it."
C "It had many people, but valued the pain."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer, and nobody is scoring it.
