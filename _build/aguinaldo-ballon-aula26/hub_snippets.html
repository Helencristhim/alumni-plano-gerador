The Language of Professional Biography -- From a List to a Story

How to write a speaker bio that people remember, turning key actions into events. Key words: an appointment, tenure, a turnaround, a career path, to oversee, to spearhead, a stint, a legacy, a transition, hands-on. Structure: his appointment as, the restructuring of, his decision to, after the sale of.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An appointment -- the act of choosing someone for an official job
"His appointment as CEO surprised the market."
Tenure -- the period of time when someone holds an important job
"During his tenure, the company doubled its revenue."
A turnaround -- a big change that makes a failing company successful again
"The turnaround took three difficult years."
A career path -- the series of jobs a person has during working life
"Her career path went from law to banking and back."
To oversee -- to be responsible for work and make sure it is done well
"He oversaw the drafting of the new port rules."
To spearhead -- to lead an important new project or change
"She spearheaded the first private port concession in the state."
A stint -- a short period of time in a job or a place
"After a two-year stint in London, he came back to Brazil."
A legacy -- something important that remains after you leave
"His legacy is a regulatory framework that still works."
A transition -- a change from one situation, job or state to another
"The transition from CEO to lawyer took one year."
Hands-on -- involved in the practical work, not only in decisions
"As CEO, he was very hands-on with the regulators."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The New York forum asks Aguinaldo for a bio of one hundred words. His first draft is a list: I studied, I worked, I was appointed, I sold, I returned.

So he rewrites it as a story. "His appointment as CEO led to the turnaround of a company in crisis. During his tenure, revenue doubled. After the sale of the company, he returned to law."

He ends with the most personal event: "His decision to start a doctorate surprised his colleagues." The bio is shorter, more formal, and easier to remember. Notice: the verbs became nouns, and the nouns tell the story.

1. What was the problem with his first draft?
A It was a list.
B It was too short, so the forum asked him to add more information about his university years.
C It was written in Portuguese, so the forum could not publish it in the program.
2. Why does the text say "his decision to start" and not "his decision of starting"?
A Because "of" is never used after a noun in professional English, only after verbs.
B Because "starting" is an informal word, and a bio must always be very formal.
C Because decision is followed by to + a base verb.
3. What happened after the sale of the company?
A He became CEO.
B He returned to law.
C He moved abroad.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Nominalization: From Actions to Events

GRAMMAR

In a bio, key actions become nouns. The noun is followed by as, of or to, and after, following and during come before it.

JobFormExample
Choose for a jobhis / her appointment as + jobHis appointment as CEO ...
A process or changethe + noun + of + thingThe restructuring of the company ...
A decision or a planhis / her decision to + base verbHis decision to start a doctorate ...
A time markerafter / following / during + nounAfter the sale of the company ...
Common pairsappoint → appointment · decide → decision · leave → departure · sell → salefollowing his departure from the board
QuestionWhat led to + noun? · When was his appointment?What led to the turnaround?

Never: His appoint as CEO · his decision of returning · the restructuring in the company · During he was CEO...

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A bio needs one formal sentence about how you became CEO. You write:
A "His appoint as CEO in 2015 led to the turnaround of the company in only three years."
B "His appointing for CEO in 2015 led to the turnaround of the company in only three years."
C "His appointment as CEO led to a turnaround."
You explain why you went back to law. You say:
A "My decision to return to law came from the regulatory work."
B "My decision of returning to law came from the regulatory work I did as CEO of the company."
C "My decide to return to law came from the regulatory work I did as CEO of the company."
You give a time marker with a noun. You say:
A "During he was CEO of the company, the revenue doubled and the company sold two businesses."
B "During his tenure, revenue doubled."
C "While his tenure as CEO of the company, the revenue doubled and the company sold two businesses."
He worked at a federal agency for only one year. It was:
A a legacy.
B a turnaround.
C a stint.
