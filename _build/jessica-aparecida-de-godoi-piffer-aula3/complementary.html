Talk
Elizabeth Loftus — “How reliable is your memory?” (TED)

Seventeen minutes from the researcher behind the study in this lesson’s first recording. She spends the talk on cases where a confident, honest witness was completely wrong, and on how easily a question can rewrite what somebody remembers.

Tip: she narrates several cases. Pick one and retell it out loud in five sentences, using was or were plus -ing for the background of each scene.

Podcast
Criminal — “Mantrap” (episode 366)

Thirty minutes of an interviewer doing exactly what the second recording described: opening with what people were doing, never with what they saw, and leaving long silences that the other person fills.

Tip: count how many times the host asks a follow-up question instead of moving on. Then count how many she asks in the first five minutes alone.

Radio
99% Invisible — “Structural Integrity”

The story of an engineer who received one question from a student, went back to his own calculations, and found something he had missed. The whole episode is a timeline: what was already happening, what interrupted it, and what he did next.

Tip: after listening, write the timeline of the episode in six lines with times or dates. It is the same exercise you did in class, on somebody else’s story.
