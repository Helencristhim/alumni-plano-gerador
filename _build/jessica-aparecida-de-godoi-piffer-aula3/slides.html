Chapter 3: Two Things at Once

The Background and the Interruption

One of them was already running when the other one arrived

Grammar Discovery

Which Action Started First?

"I was checking the supplier list when I found the second receipt."

"While she was waiting for the lift, she noticed the open door."

"The meeting started at nine and finished at ten."

"At ten past nine, while the others were presenting, I was reading the file."

The red verbs were already running. The blue ones arrived, and they arrived once.

JobFormExample
the background, already runningwas / were + verb-ingI was checking the list.
the event that arrivespast simple...when I found the receipt.
the word that marks each onewhile + background · when + eventWhile she was waiting, she noticed it.
two backgrounds at onceboth continuousThey were presenting while I was reading.
a finished sequencepast simple all the wayIt started at nine and finished at ten.
verbs that refuse the -ingknow, believe, understand, wantNot I was knowing. Say I knew.
the trap"When I arrived, she wrote the report" means she started AFTER you got there. Almost never what you mean.

In one line: the -ing form was already running. The past simple is what walked in.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Interview, At Full Speed

One conversation, two recordings, one statement form

Dialogue

Four Minutes and One Timeline

P
Thanks for coming down. Alan Pierce, internal review. This is not about you, and it will take four minutes.
J
That is fine. I have five before my next call.
P
Tuesday morning. What were you doing at around ten forty?
J
I was checking the quarter file at my desk. I had been there since about nine thirty.
P
And did anything interrupt you?
J
Yes. While I was working, somebody walked past with a box of files. I glanced up, I did not recall the face, and I went back to the file.
P
You said around ten forty. The badge log has you back from the coffee area at ten fifty-two. Which one is right?
J
Then it was after ten fifty-two, and around ten forty was too vague. I would rather correct it now than contradict myself in writing.
Comprehension

About Pierce

1. How does Pierce open the conversation, and what does he promise?
He introduces himself as Alan Pierce from internal review, says it is not about her, and promises it will take four minutes.
2. What is the first thing he asks about Tuesday morning?
Not what she saw, but what she was doing at around ten forty.
3. How does he point out the problem with her time?
He does not say she is wrong. He puts her "around ten forty" next to the badge log's ten fifty-two and asks which one is right.
Listening 1 · A Memory Researcher

Why Confident Witnesses Are Often Wrong

A researcher on what happens to a memory every time you tell it. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say happens to a memory every time you tell it?
You do not replay the original event. You rebuild it, and you save the new version over the old one.
2. What did the study with the two questions find?
People asked how fast the cars "smashed" gave higher speeds than people asked how fast they "hit", and a week later some of them remembered broken glass that was never there.
3. What does she say confidence tells you about accuracy?
Almost nothing. She says the most confident witnesses are not the most accurate ones, and that this surprises everybody, including the witnesses.
Listening 2 · An Interview Trainer

Three Rules for Asking About a Timeline

A trainer on how to get an exact answer without pushing. Sound first — no text.

1. What is his first rule about the opening question?
Never start with what somebody saw. Start with what they were doing, because that gives them a place to stand.
2. What does he say about the word "around"?
Treat it as a warning. "Around ten" usually means the person does not know, and he asks what else was happening at that moment instead of asking for the time again.
3. What is his third rule, about silence?
After an answer, say nothing for four seconds. He says most of what he has ever learned arrived in that gap.

Now You Build the Timeline
