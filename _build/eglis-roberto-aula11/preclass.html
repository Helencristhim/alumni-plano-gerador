Lesson 11 -- Pre-class

Did You Hear What He Said? -- The Message Chain

How to pass on what people said at a party: what they said, what they asked, and what they told you to do. Key words: a rumor, gossip, to pass on, to exaggerate, to make up, to leave a message, to mishear, word of mouth, to get the wrong idea, to find out. Structure: reported speech (He said he was selling his boat. She asked me if I was coming. He told me to call him.).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example.

A rumor -- a story that people pass on, but nobody knows if it is true
"There is a rumor that the marina is going to close."
Gossip -- talk about other people's private lives
"The club bar is full of gossip on Friday nights."
To pass on -- to give a message or a story to another person
"Can you pass on a message to Jake for me?"
To exaggerate -- to make something sound bigger or worse than it really is
"He always exaggerates. It was a small wave, not a storm."
To make up -- to invent a story that is not true
"Nobody saw that. I think he made it up."
To leave a message -- to give information for someone who is not there
"She was not home, so I left a message with her husband."
To mishear -- to hear something in the wrong way
"I misheard him. He said thirteen, not thirty."
Word of mouth -- information that travels when people talk to each other
"We found this little bar by word of mouth."
To get the wrong idea -- to understand a situation in the wrong way
"Don't get the wrong idea. We are just friends."
To find out -- to learn a fact that you did not know
"I found out the real story the next morning."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Saturday, Jake had a party. Paulo arrived very late, so the next morning his friend Marta passed on the messages. “Jake said that he was moving to Lisbon next year,” she told him. “Claire asked if you could bring your guitar next time. And the neighbor told us not to park in front of his garage.”

Jake’s real words were “I am moving to Lisbon.” Marta tells the story later, so the verb goes one step back: am moving becomes was moving, can becomes could, will becomes would.

A question changes too: “Can he bring his guitar?” becomes “She asked if you could bring…” — no question order. And an order becomes told + person + to / not to + verb. Paulo found out the real story: Jake is only moving next year, not next week.

"Jake said that he was moving to Lisbon." What were Jake’s real words?
A "I am moving to Lisbon."
B "He was moving to Lisbon, and he said it to everybody at the party on Saturday night."
C "I was moving to Lisbon."
Which sentence reports Claire’s question correctly?
A "Claire asked if you could bring your guitar."
B "Claire asked could you bring your guitar to the next party at Jake’s house?"
C "Claire said you if could bring your guitar."
The neighbor said: "Don’t park in front of my garage." Which sentence is correct?
A "The neighbor said us that we don’t park in front of his garage on Saturday night."
B "The neighbor told us not to park in front of his garage."
C "The neighbor told us to not parking there."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- He Said, She Asked, They Told Me To

GRAMMAR

When you pass on someone’s words later, the verb usually goes one step back in time.

JobFormExample
A statementsaid (that) / told + person (that) + one step back“I am selling my boat.” → He said he was selling his boat.
One step backam/is → was · can → could · will → would · went → had gone“I will call.” → She said she would call.
A yes/no questionasked (+ person) + if + normal word order“Are you coming?” → She asked me if I was coming.
A wh- questionasked + where/what/who + normal word order“Where is the party?” → He asked where the party was.
An order or a requesttold / asked + person + to / not to + verb“Call me.” → He told me to call him.

Never: He said me · She asked where was the party · He told me that I call him · She asked me do I like it

Quiz

Your friend left the party early. Jake said: "I’ll call you tomorrow." You pass on the message:
A "Jake said he would call you tomorrow."
B "Jake said me that he will call to you tomorrow, after he wakes up and has his coffee."
C "Jake told he call you."
A woman at the bar asked you: "Where is the marina?" Later you tell your friend:
A "She asked me where was the marina."
B "She asked me where the marina was."
