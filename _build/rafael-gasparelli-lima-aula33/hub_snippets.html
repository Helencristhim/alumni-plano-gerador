Lesson 33 -- Pre-class

International Travel -- The Missed Connection

How to solve a travel problem at the airline desk and then report what the agent asked and told you. Key words: connecting flight, layover, overbooked, to rebook, voucher, baggage claim, upgrade, compensation. Structure: reported questions and instructions with asked me if, asked me where, told me to and told me not to.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example.

Connecting flight -- a second flight that you take to continue your trip
"My connecting flight to Chicago leaves at three."
Layover -- the time you wait at an airport between two flights
"I have a two-hour layover in Miami."
Overbooked -- when an airline sells more seats than the plane has
"The flight is overbooked, so they need volunteers."
To rebook -- to change your ticket to a new flight or a new date
"The agent rebooked me on the eight p.m. flight."
Voucher -- a paper or a code that you can use instead of money
"They gave me a meal voucher for twenty-five dollars."
Baggage claim -- the place where you pick up your bags after a flight
"My bag never arrived at baggage claim."
Upgrade -- a better seat or room than the one you paid for
"The airline gave me an upgrade to business class."
Compensation -- money or a service that you receive after a problem
"Passengers can ask for compensation after a long delay."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Rafael is flying to Chicago to sign a deal, but he misses his connecting flight in Miami. At the desk, the agent says: “Do you have a checked bag?” Later, Rafael tells Claire: “The agent asked me if I had a checked bag.” The question mark is gone, and the verb moves to the past.

The agent also asks: “Where is your final destination?” Rafael reports: “She asked me where my final destination was.” There is no did, and the order is the order of a normal sentence.

Then the agent gives two instructions: “Go to gate D12. Do not leave the gate area.” Rafael reports: “She told me to go to gate D12, and she told me not to leave the gate area.”

The agent asked: "Do you have a checked bag?" How does Rafael report it?
A "She asked me do I have a checked bag for the flight to Chicago tonight."
B "She said me if I have a bag."
C "She asked me if I had a checked bag."
Which sentence is correct?
A "She asked me where was my final destination, because the computer at the desk was very slow."
B "She asked me where my final destination was."
C "She asked me where did my destination be."
The agent said: "Do not leave the gate area." What is the correct report?
A "She told me not to leave the gate area."
B "She told me don't leave."
C "She told me to not leaving the gate area until the flight to Chicago was ready for boarding."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Report Questions and Instructions

GRAMMAR

A reported question has no question mark and no do / did. After told me, use to + verb.

The agent saysFormYou report
"Do you have a bag?"asked me if + subject + past verbShe asked me if I had a bag.
"Where are you going?"asked me where + subject + past verbShe asked me where I was going.
"Go to gate D12."told me to + base verbShe told me to go to gate D12.
"Do not leave."told me not to + base verbShe told me not to leave.
"Please wait here."asked me to + base verbShe asked me to wait there.

Never: She asked me where was my gate · He asked me did I have · She told me go · She said me to wait
