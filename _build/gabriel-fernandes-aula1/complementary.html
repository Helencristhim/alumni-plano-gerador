Complementary Activities

None of this is homework and none of it has to be handed in. It is English going in through your ears while you do something else. Tick the box when you have watched or listened to it, just so you remember what you have already seen.

Video
Harvard University — “A Day in the Life of a First-Year Student”

Filmed by the university itself, not by a student channel. One first-year, from breakfast in Annenberg Hall to the walk back at night. It is the ordinary version of the place you keep talking about, and that is exactly why it is worth watching.

Tip: watch it once without subtitles. Then watch it again and write down three things that surprised you. Those three things are your next conversation with your teacher.

Podcast
Inside the Yale Admissions Office — “What Yale Looks For: Special Mythbusters Edition”

Two admissions officers take apart the things applicants believe. The one that matters most for you: the myth that you should send everything you have ever done. Their line is “we admit people, not accomplishments”. The full transcript is on the page, so you can read along while you listen.

Tip: read the transcript with the audio playing. Reading and hearing the same sentence at the same time is the fastest way to fix a word you have only ever seen written.

Talk
TED — Angela Lee Duckworth, “Grit: The Power of Passion and Perseverance”

Six minutes. A researcher who left a consulting job to teach maths finds that the best predictor of who finishes something is not intelligence. Watch it because it is the argument behind every question an admissions officer will ask you about what you kept doing when it stopped being fun.

Tip: this one has a full transcript on TED. If a sentence goes past you, do not rewind immediately — finish the talk first, then go back. Understanding the whole before the parts is the habit worth building.
