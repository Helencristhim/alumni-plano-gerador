LessonStructureThe one thing to remember
1Question formsThe helper comes before the person, always.
2Present perfect vs past simpleIs there a date? Yes, simple. No, perfect.
3Modals of permission and obligationdon't have to and mustn't are opposites.
4First conditionalNo will in the if half.
5Second conditionalBoth halves step back. If I were you.
6Passive voiceA form of to be plus a past participle.
7Reported speechEverything steps back one tense. Told takes a person.
8Relative clausesThe relative word replaces the subject. Never repeat it.
9Gerunds and infinitivesAfter a preposition, always -ing.

Common Mistake

The Last Three Fixes

"She said me that the flight was delayed."
"She told me that the flight was delayed."
"I have packed my suitcase yesterday."
"I packed my suitcase yesterday."
"If I would have more time, I would visit Vancouver Island."
"If I had more time, I would visit Vancouver Island."

Three errors from three different lessons, and all three come from translating word by word. When you are not sure, say the shorter version and keep going.

Comprehension

About Greg and Emma

1. What has Emma already done?
She has made a list of places she wants to take her.
2. What time does Greg suggest for the family calls, and why?
After six in the evening his time, because of the time difference.
3. What does he say about the food she should bring?
Whatever she would make at home on a normal Sunday — they are not fussy and they are curious.
Listening

The Last Message Before You Travel

The international office, three practical things. Sound first — no text.

1. Where should the documents go, and why?
In the hand luggage, never in the checked suitcase — about one in twenty suitcases is delayed.
2. What is the problem with the time difference?
Vancouver is five hours behind Sao Paulo, so the family may call at four in the morning. Agree a time first.
3. What is the advice, rather than the instruction?
Say yes to the first three invitations, even — especially — if you are tired.
Listening 2

A Voice Note from Burnaby

Greg, one week before your flight. Sound first — no text.

1. What has Emma done, and what has she prepared?
She has moved her things out of the small room, mostly, and made a list of places longer than the six months.
2. What does he ask her to bring?
One thing from home that she can cook for them — they are not fussy and they are curious.
3. What does he say about the first week?
Not to worry: everybody arrives exhausted and slightly panicked, and by the second Saturday it feels normal.
