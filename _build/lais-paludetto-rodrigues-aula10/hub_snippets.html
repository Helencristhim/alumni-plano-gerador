Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. Do not translate — the example sentence is the meaning.

Departure -- the act of leaving, and the time when you leave
"My departure is on the fourteenth, at eleven at night."
To pack -- to put your things into a bag or a suitcase for a trip
"I packed twice and took half of it out both times."
Checklist -- a list of things to check or do, one by one
"Make a checklist. Your memory will not survive the last week."
Time zone -- one of the areas of the world with the same clock time
"Vancouver is five hours behind, which is one time zone problem too many."
Souvenir -- a small thing you buy to remember a place
"The best souvenir I brought back was a bus ticket."
To keep in touch -- to keep talking to somebody who is far away
"We said we would keep in touch and, unusually, we did."
Farewell -- a goodbye, especially before a long time apart
"My farewell dinner was mostly my friends arguing about music."
To miss out on -- to lose the chance to do something
"I said no twice in September and I still think I missed out."
To make the most of -- to get as much as possible out of a situation
"Six months is short. Make the most of the boring Tuesdays."
Milestone -- an important moment that marks how far you have come
"The first time I dreamed in English was a milestone."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Two weeks before departure the whole thing stops being an idea. I made a checklist, because my memory was not going to survive the last week. The documents go in the hand luggage, never in the suitcase: if the suitcase is delayed, and one in twenty is, I will still be able to register. I have already packed twice and taken half of it out both times.

The time zone is the part nobody thinks about. Vancouver is five hours behind Sao Paulo, so my family told me to agree a calling time before I fly, which is the sort of thing you only learn by getting it wrong at four in the morning. Greg, my host dad, asked me to bring one thing from home that I can cook, and he said they were not fussy.

Everything else is the farewell part, which is heavier than the packing. If I were somebody else, I would probably spend the last week worrying. Instead I am planning to make the most of it: see the people who matter, take one small souvenir of home, and keep in touch with the friends that I will actually keep in touch with, which is a shorter list than I thought.

And one decision, taken in advance. If anybody invites me to anything in the first month, I will say yes, even when I am tired. Everybody who has done this says the same thing: the people who miss out on the six months are the ones who said no in September. That is the only milestone I can control.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Whole Block, in Nine Lines

GRAMMAR

No new grammar today. Everything you already have, in one place, one last time.

FormUseExample
1. Question formsThe helper comes before the person, always.How long are you staying?
2. Present perfect vs past simpleIs there a date? Yes, simple. No, perfect.I've packed. / I packed yesterday.
3. Modals of permission and obligationdon't have to and mustn't are opposites.You don't have to cook. / You mustn't be late.
4. First conditionalNo will in the if half.If I miss it, I'll walk.
5. Second conditionalBoth halves step back. If I were you.If I were you, I'd say yes.
6. Passive voiceA form of to be plus a past participle.The documents are checked.
7. Reported speechEverything steps back one tense. Told takes a person.She told me it was Thursday.
8. Relative clausesThe relative word replaces the subject. Never repeat it.The street where I grew up.
9. Gerunds and infinitivesAfter a preposition, always -ing.looking forward to seeing snow

Nine lines, ten weeks. Two of them were the ones you said you hated, and by now you cannot tell which.
