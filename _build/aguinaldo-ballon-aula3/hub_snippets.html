If the Tender Fails -- Talking About Risk

An investor asks what will happen if something goes wrong. Key words: a tender, a bid, to award, a tariff, exposure, a penalty, to comply with, a shortfall, a guarantee, uncertainty. Structure: first and second conditional.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A tender -- an official process where companies compete for a public contract
"The state will open a tender for the new highway."
A bid -- the offer a company makes in a tender, with its price and conditions
"Our client lost the bid by a small margin."
To award -- to give a contract officially to the winner of a tender
"The contract was awarded to a Spanish group."
A tariff -- the price users pay for a public service, such as a toll or electricity
"If the tariff goes up, users will complain."
Exposure -- how much a company can lose if something goes wrong
"The fund wants to reduce its exposure to currency risk."
A penalty -- money a company must pay when it breaks the rules of a contract
"If the work is late, the concession will pay a penalty."
To comply with -- to follow a rule, a law or a contract
"The company must comply with the new safety rules."
A shortfall -- the difference between the money you need and the money you have
"A fall in traffic would create a revenue shortfall."
A guarantee -- a promise, usually from a bank or the state, to pay if something fails
"The investors asked for a state guarantee."
Uncertainty -- a situation where you do not know what will happen
"Uncertainty about the rules is the biggest risk for investors."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The state is preparing a tender for a new highway, and three groups want to make a bid. Before they decide, their lawyers study the risks.

The first risk is real: if the environmental agency delays the license, the project will start a year later. The second is also real: if traffic falls, the concession will have a shortfall, so the investors want a state guarantee.

One lawyer also imagines a better world: if the rules were more stable, investors would accept lower returns, and the tariff would be lower for the users. Today, the rules change too often, so there is a lot of uncertainty.

1. Is a delay in the license a real possibility, according to the text?
A Yes, it is a real risk.
B No, it is an imaginary situation.
C The text says it will never happen.
2. Are the rules stable today?
A Yes, they are very stable.
B No. That is why the lawyer says "if the rules were more stable".
C The text does not say.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "If traffic will fall, the concession will have a shortfall."
B "If traffic falls, the concession will have a shortfall."
C "If traffic fell, the concession will have a shortfall."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- First and Second Conditional

GRAMMAR

A real possibility: if + present, will. An imaginary situation: if + past, would.

JobFormExample
A real possibility (first conditional)If + present simple, will + verbIf the regulator delays the license, we will ask for more time.
An imaginary or unlikely situation (second conditional)If + past simple, would + verbIf the rules were more stable, more funds would invest.
With be, in the second conditionalIf I / he / it were ...If I were the regulator, I would publish it.
The order can changeresult first, no commaWe will ask for more time if the regulator delays it.
Less certain resultmight or could instead of will / wouldIf traffic falls, we might need a guarantee.
The trapnever will after ifNot If the regulator will delay. Say If the regulator delays.

Never: If the regulator will delay · If the rules are more stable, investors would invest · If I was the regulator, I will publish · If traffic falls, it would have a shortfall (for a real risk)

Stage 2: Put the Risk Answer in Order

Order

An investor asks about the biggest risk of the project. Put your answer in a logical order.

?"That is a good question, and I want to be honest about it."
?"The biggest risk is a delay in the environmental license."
?"If the agency delays the license, the tender will move to next year."
?"If that happens, we will ask for a new schedule."
?"The contract allows it, so there will be no penalty."
?"Of course, if the rules were more predictable, we would not need this plan."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

An investor asks about a real risk next month. You say:
A "If the agency will delay the license, we ask for more time."
B "If the agency delays the license, we will ask for more time."
C "If the agency delayed the license, we will ask for more time."
You want to describe an imaginary situation. You say:
A "If the rules were more stable, more funds would invest."
B "If the rules are more stable, more funds would invest."
C "If the rules would be more stable, more funds invest."
Somebody asks what you would change as a regulator. You say:
A "If I am the regulator, I will change everything."
B "If I were the regulator, I would publish the decisions earlier."
C "If I was the regulator, I publish the decisions earlier."
Traffic may fall next year. The best answer to an investor is:
A "If traffic falls, the concession will have a shortfall, so we have asked for a guarantee."
B "Traffic will not fall, do not worry."
C "If traffic would fall, it will be a problem."
