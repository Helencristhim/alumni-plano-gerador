Lesson 3 · What Could Happen

If the Tender Fails

An investor never asks only where the project is. The next question is always about the future: what happens if something goes wrong? Today you answer that question in English.

Chapter 1: First Words

From Where It Is to What Could Happen

Last time you told a fund what has already happened and what has not happened yet. Today the fund asks about tomorrow: the risks, the scenarios and the plan B.

Think of a real project. What is the biggest risk, and what will you do if it happens?

Chapter 3: The Code

Possible or Imaginary?

Two conditionals for two kinds of future

Grammar Discovery

What Is the Difference Between These Sentences?

"If the regulator delays the license, we will ask for more time."

"If traffic falls, the concession will have a shortfall."

"If the rules were more stable, more funds would invest in Brazil."

"If I were the regulator, I would publish the decisions earlier."

The first two sentences talk about something that can really happen. The last two talk about something that is not true today. Look at the verbs in blue and red: what changes?

JobFormExample
A real possibility (first conditional)If + present simple, will + verbIf the regulator delays the license, we will ask for more time.
An imaginary or unlikely situation (second conditional)If + past simple, would + verbIf the rules were more stable, more funds would invest.
With be, in the second conditionalIf I / he / it were ...If I were the regulator, I would publish it.
The order can changeresult first, no commaWe will ask for more time if the regulator delays it.
Less certain resultmight or could instead of will / wouldIf traffic falls, we might need a guarantee.
The trapnever will after ifNot If the regulator will delay. Say If the regulator delays.

In one line: real possibility: if + present, will; imaginary situation: if + past, would.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Question Every Investor Asks

One conversation, three recordings, one page

Dialogue

The Investor Asks "What If?"

S
Thank you for the update. Now I need to ask about the risks. What is your biggest worry?
A
The environmental license. If the agency delays it, the tender will move to next year.
S
And if that happens, what will you do?
A
We will ask for a new schedule. The contract allows it, so there will be no penalty.
S
What about traffic? Our model assumes the numbers from 2024.
A
If traffic falls, the concession will have a shortfall. That is why we have asked for a state guarantee.
S
Honestly, if the rules were more predictable here, my board would approve this in one meeting.
A
I agree. If I were the regulator, I would publish the decisions earlier. For now, we reduce the uncertainty with the contract.
Comprehension

1. What does Sophie ask about first?
The risks, and his biggest worry.
2. What does Sophie say her model assumes?
The traffic numbers from 2024.
3. What would her board do if the rules were more predictable?
Approve the project in one meeting.
Listening 1 · The Risk Analyst

The Three Scenarios I Always Write

A risk analyst at an infrastructure fund explains how she prepares a project for the board. Sound first — no text.

1. How many scenarios does she write for every project?
Three.
2. What happens in her worst scenario for a toll road?
Traffic falls by twenty percent and the concession has a shortfall.
3. What does she say the board wants to see?
Not a perfect project, but a clear plan for each scenario.
Listening 2 · The Consultant

If I Were a Regulator

A consultant imagines what he would change in infrastructure regulation. Sound first — no text.

1. What would he publish if he were a regulator?
The calendar of decisions for the whole year.
2. Why would investors accept lower returns, according to him?
Because there would be less uncertainty.
3. What does he say is the real cost of uncertainty?
Higher tariffs for the users.
Listening 3 · Business Radio

A Tender With No Bids

A short radio report on a tender that nobody wanted. Sound first — no text.

1. How many bids did the state receive?
None.
2. What did the companies say was the problem?
The tariff was too low and there was no guarantee.
3. What will the state do if it changes the rules?
Open the tender again in six months.
