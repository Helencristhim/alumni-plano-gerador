Chapter 3

The Trend Code

Up or down, by how much, fast or slow

Grammar Discovery

How Much, How Fast?

"Online sales increased by eighteen percent."

"The checkout time dropped from ninety seconds to forty-five."

"There was a sharp drop in support tickets in September."

"App users increased steadily every month."

What do the orange words have in common? What does "by" tell us? And "from ... to"?

UseFormExample
The differenceverb + by + numberSales increased by eighteen percent.
The start and the endverb + from ... to ...It dropped from ninety to forty-five.
How it changed (verb)verb + adverb (-ly)Users increased steadily.
How it changed (noun)there was + a + adjective + nounThere was a sharp drop.
Compare with the targethigher / lower thanSales were higher than our target.

Use by, not "in". Percent never takes -s. Adjective before a noun (a sharp drop), adverb after a verb (dropped sharply).

Grammar Practice

Say It the Other Way

Say it first, then click to check

"Sales increased sharply." → "There was a ___sharp increase in sales."
"There was a steady drop in tickets." → "Tickets ___dropped steadily."
"Q2: R$ 50 million. Q3: R$ 59 million." → "Sales increased ___from R$ 50 million to R$ 59 million."
"Q2: 1,000 tickets. Q3: 700 tickets." → "Tickets dropped ___by three hundred."
"Target: 15%. Result: 18%." → "The result was ___higher than the target."
Style

By or To?

by = the difference

Sales increased by 18%.
Tickets dropped by 300.
Time dropped by 45 seconds.

to = the final number

Sales increased to R$ 59 million.
Tickets dropped to 700.
Time dropped to 45 seconds.

"Support tickets dropped by thirty percent, to seven hundred a month. We were above our target."

Chapter 4

The Results Call

Greg and Daniela check the Brazil numbers

Dialogue

Checking the Numbers

G
Hi Daniela. Before Thursday, can you give me the Brazil numbers for the third quarter? Let us start with online sales.
D
Sure. Online sales increased by eighteen percent. Our target was fifteen, so we were above target.
G
Excellent. And the checkout? That is the number the directors really want to see.
D
There was a sharp drop in the average checkout time. It dropped from ninety seconds to forty-five.
G
From ninety to forty-five? So customers wait half the time now. What about support tickets?
D
They dropped by thirty percent, to seven hundred a month. The new system has fewer problems.
G
Great. And the mobile app? The directors love that percentage.
D
App users increased every month, a steady increase. But we had two hundred and fifty thousand users, and the target was three hundred thousand.
Comprehension

About Greg

1. When is the meeting with the directors?
On Thursday.
2. According to Greg, which number do the directors really want to see?
The checkout time.
3. How does Greg explain the change in the checkout time?
Customers wait half the time now.
Listening 1 — The Coach

A Story, Not a List

A voice message from Sarah, your English coach. Sound first — no text.

1. Which small word does Sarah use for the difference?
By: sales increased by eighteen percent.
2. Where is the stress in "fifteen", and in "fifty"?
Fifteen: at the end (fif-TEEN). Fifty: at the start (FIF-ty).
3. What do the directors want to know first, according to Sarah?
If the result was higher or lower than the target.
Listening 2 — Greg’s Numbers

The US Quarter

A voice message from Greg about the US stores. Sound first — no text.

1. How much did online sales increase in the US stores, and what was the target?
By only four percent. The target was ten percent.
2. What happened to support tickets in August, and why?
They increased sharply, by forty percent, because of the bug in the payment page.
3. What does Greg want Daniela to prepare for Thursday?
Two or three minutes about the new checkout system: how her team did it.
