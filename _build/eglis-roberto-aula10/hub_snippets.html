Stories from the Water -- Close Calls at the Marina Bar

How to tell a story about something that almost went wrong: what would have happened if, and what you should have done. Key words: a close call, to capsize, a gust of wind, to drift, to run aground, a lifejacket, reckless, to panic, the coast guard, in one piece. Structure: the third conditional (If I had checked the forecast, we would not have left) for an imaginary past, and should have for a regret about the past.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example.

A close call -- a situation where something bad almost happened
"It was a close call. We almost hit the rocks."
To capsize -- when a boat turns over in the water
"A big wave nearly made us capsize."
A gust of wind -- a sudden, strong movement of wind
"A gust of wind hit the sail and we went over."
To drift -- to move slowly with the water or the wind, without control
"The engine stopped and the boat started to drift."
To run aground -- when a boat hits the bottom of the sea and cannot move
"We almost ran aground near the beach."
A lifejacket -- a jacket that keeps you on top of the water
"Always wear a lifejacket when you sail alone."
Reckless -- doing dangerous things without thinking about the result
"It was reckless to sail without checking the forecast."
To panic -- to suddenly feel so afraid that you cannot think clearly
"Do not panic. Call the coast guard on the radio."
The coast guard -- the service that helps boats and people in danger at sea
"The coast guard arrived in twenty minutes."
In one piece -- safe, without being hurt or damaged
"We got back to the marina in one piece."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last summer, Paulo and his brother went sailing. They did not check the forecast. In the afternoon, a gust of wind hit the boat, and they almost capsized. “If we had checked the forecast, we would not have left that day,” Paulo says now.

He uses the third conditional to imagine a different past: if + had + past participle, then would have + past participle. In real life, they did NOT check the forecast, and they DID leave.

Paulo also has a regret: “I should have worn a lifejacket.” He did not wear one. Should have + past participle is for something that was a good idea, but did not happen. Luckily, they got back in one piece.

"If we had checked the forecast, we would not have left." Did they check the forecast?
A No. They did not check it, and they left.
B Yes. They checked it twice, but they decided to leave because the morning was very beautiful.
C We do not know.
"I should have worn a lifejacket." What does Paulo mean?
A He wore a lifejacket and he is happy about it, because it saved his life that day.
B He did not wear one, and now he thinks it was a mistake.
C He must wear one next time.
Which sentence is correct?
A "If we would have checked the forecast, we would not have left the marina that afternoon."
B "If we checked the forecast, we would not have leave."
C "If we had checked the forecast, we would not have left."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- If I Had Known, and I Should Have

GRAMMAR

An imaginary past with if + had, and a regret with should have. Both use the past participle.

JobFormExample
An imaginary past (it did not happen)If + had + past participle, would have + past participleIf we had checked the forecast, we would not have left.
The other orderwould have + past participle + if + had + past participleWe would have hit the rocks if Tom had not taken the helm.
Short forms in speechIf I’d known… / I would’ve…If I’d known, I would’ve stayed.
A regret about the pastshould have / shouldn’t have + past participleI should have worn a lifejacket.
Asking about a regretWhat should you have done?What should you have done?

Never: If I would have known · If I had saw · I should went · I would have leave

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

A friend asks about your worst day at sea. You say:
A "It was a close call. If I had checked the forecast, I would not have gone."
B "If I would have checked the forecast, I would not have gone out to sea that afternoon with my friends."
C "If I check the forecast, I don't go."
You did not wear a lifejacket, and now you think it was a mistake. You say:
A "I should wear a lifejacket."
B "I should have worn a lifejacket."
C "I should went with a lifejacket, but I forgot it in the car that morning at the marina."
The coast guard helped you. Without them, you would have hit the rocks. You say:
A "If the coast guard did not come, we run aground on the rocks near the beach, I am sure."
B "If the coast guard had not came, we would run aground."
C "If the coast guard had not come, we would have run aground."
You want to ask a sailor about his regret. You ask:
A "What should you have done?"
B "What you should have done?"
C "What did you should do differently on that day at sea, when the wind changed?"

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Think of a close call in your life: at sea, on the Camino, on the road, anywhere. Record about one minute: what happened, what would have happened if things had been different, and what you should have done.
