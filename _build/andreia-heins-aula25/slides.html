Chapter 3: The Code

The Chair's Steering Wheel

Small words that move the discussion

Grammar Discovery

Words That Steer

Dr. Reed thinks the robot saves time. That said, the cost is still very high.
On that note, let me bring in our next panelist.
Coming back to our main question: is the training worth the cost?
As for the nurses' role, Ms. Park, what is your view?
Anyway, we are running out of time, so let's move to questions.
Click to reveal the rule
Discourse markers come at the start of the sentence and tell the audience where the discussion is going. Each one has a job: that said, = a contrast (like however); on that note, = a link from what was just said to the next step; coming back to + noun = refocus after the discussion got sidetracked; as for + noun = open a new part of the topic; anyway, = close a digression and move on. After coming back to and as for comes a noun (as for the cost), never as for to.

Chapter 4: The Session

A Panelist Who Does Not Stop

Advice, an opener and a real session

Listening 1 · A Voice Message

Advice From David

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What should she do if a panelist overruns?
Interrupt politely, not at the end, and give the panelist thirty seconds to finish.
2. What should she do with a quiet panelist?
Bring the panelist in with a direct question.
3. Why is it important to finish on time?
Because people always remember a session that ends on time.
Listening 2 · The Opener

Welcome to the Session

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What two pieces of housekeeping does the moderator give?
The exits are at the back of the room, and the coffee break is at three thirty.
2. How long is each time slot?
Ten minutes for each of the three panelists.
3. When can the audience ask questions?
At the end: there are fifteen minutes for questions and discussion.
