Open a congress session, protect the time slots and keep the discussion on topic. Key words: moderator, panelist, time slot, overrun, housekeeping, hand over to, bring in, round of applause, interrupt, get sidetracked. Structure: discourse markers -- that said, on that note, coming back to, as for, anyway.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. Five words are about the session and its people; five are about what the chair does.

Moderator -- the person who leads a discussion and gives each speaker a turn
"As the moderator, I keep the discussion on time and on topic."
Panelist -- one of the experts who speak in a panel discussion
"Each panelist has ten minutes to present."
Time slot -- the period of time that is planned for one speaker or activity
"Your time slot is from two fifteen to two twenty-five."
Overrun -- to continue for longer than the planned time
"If one speaker overruns, the next speaker loses time."
Housekeeping -- short practical information at the start of an event, like exits and breaks
"Before we start, a little housekeeping: the coffee break is at three."
Hand over to -- to give the turn to another speaker
"Now I would like to hand over to Dr. Park."
Bring in -- to invite somebody to join the discussion
"Let me bring in Ms. Park, who has not spoken yet."
Round of applause -- a moment when the audience claps for somebody
"Please join me in a round of applause for our speakers."
Interrupt -- to stop somebody while they are speaking
"Sorry to interrupt, but we are a little over time."
Get sidetracked -- to start talking about something different from the main topic
"We got sidetracked, so let me come back to our main question."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Last year, Carla, a nurse from Recife, chaired her first session at a congress in Miami. At the start, she gave some housekeeping and introduced the panelists. The first speaker was excellent. That said, he overran by six minutes, so Carla interrupted him politely. Later, a question from the audience got sidetracked into hospital parking. Carla smiled and said, "Coming back to our main question, what does the robot change for nurses?" "As for the training," she said to the quietest panelist, "what is your experience?" At the end, she said, "On that note, we have to finish. Please join me in a round of applause." The session ended exactly on time.
Why does Carla say "Coming back to our main question"?
A To start a completely new topic that nobody discussed before, because the panelists were tired of the old one
B To finish the session
C To bring the discussion back after it got sidetracked
In "The first speaker was excellent. That said, he overran", what does "that said" show?
A A result, like "so", because the speaker was excellent and that is why he spoke for a long time
B A contrast, like "however"
C A new speaker

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Discourse Markers for the Chair

GRAMMAR

Discourse markers come at the start of a sentence and tell the audience where the discussion is going. A chair uses them to steer the session.

that said,contrast (= however)The robot is fast. That said, it is expensive.
on that note,link to the next stepOn that note, let me hand over to Dr. Park.
coming back to + nounrefocusComing back to our main question...
as for + nounopen a new part of the topicAs for the training, what is your view?
anyway,close a digression, move onAnyway, let's go to questions.

With a question: As for the cost, do you think it will go down? With a negative: That said, the data doesn't show it yet.

After "coming back to" and "as for" comes a noun: "as for the cost" -- never "as for to the cost".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
A panelist is five minutes over his time slot. What do you say?
A "Sorry to interrupt, but we're a little over time. Could you finish in thirty seconds?"
B "Stop now."
C Nothing. You wait until he finishes, even if the next speakers lose all of their time and the session ends late.
The discussion got sidetracked. Which sentence brings it back?
A "As for the parking, does anybody else have a story about it from their own hospital?"
B "That said, parking."
C "Coming back to our main question: what does the robot change for nurses?"
The session is over and it is exactly on time. How do you close it?
A "Okay, finished, you can all go now, and please do not forget to take your bags and your coats with you."
B "Thank you all. Please join me in a round of applause for our panelists."
C "Anyway, next topic."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Ninety seconds. You are the moderator of a session on the nurse's role in robotic surgery. Open it: welcome the audience, introduce yourself, give the housekeeping, explain the rules for questions, and hand over to the first speaker. Use at least two markers: as for, on that note, that said. No script -- just speak.
