Lesson 27 -- Objection, Politely

Talk
Adam Galinsky -- "How to speak up for yourself" (TED, 15 min)

A Columbia Business School professor explains why speaking up is hard, and how to be assertive without paying a social price: the same balance you need when you cut in at a meeting.

Tip: write down the moments when he says that being too soft and being too aggressive both fail. Which side do you fall on in English?

Film
Philadelphia (1993)

A lawyer fired from a big Philadelphia firm sues his old partners, and a small-time attorney takes the case. The trial scenes are full of objections, sustained and overruled, and of lawyers who keep the floor under pressure.

Tip: in the courtroom scenes, count the objections and note what the judge says each time: sustained or overruled.

Podcast
We Interrupt -- "Episode 14: On Conversational Style" with Deborah Tannen

A whole podcast about interruptions. The linguist Deborah Tannen explains why some people hear overlap as enthusiasm and others hear it as rudeness, the same idea as the linguist in this lesson.

Tip: listen for the moments when the host and the guest talk at the same time. Who gives the floor back, and with which words?
