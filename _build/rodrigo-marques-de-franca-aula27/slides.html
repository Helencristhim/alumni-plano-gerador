Chapter 3: The Code

Come In, Hold On, Pick Up

Three moves, and one small word that makes them polite.

Grammar Discovery

What Makes Them Polite?

1. Sorry, could I just come in here?

2. May I just add one thing about the stock?

3. If I could just finish this point, I'll hand it straight back to you.

4. As I was saying, the notice period was ninety days.

Which sentences ask for the floor? Which one keeps it? Which one takes it back? And which small word appears in three of them?

MovePatternExample
Ask for the floorCould / May + I + just + verb?Could I just come in here?
Apologize firstSorry to + verb, but ... · Sorry for + -ingSorry to cut in, but the date is wrong.
Keep the floorIf I could just + verb ... · Let me just + verbLet me just finish this point.
Give it backGo ahead. · Please, you first.Sorry, go ahead.
Take it backAs I was saying, · To get back to my point, · Where was I?To get back to my point, the price is too high.
The trapsafter could, may and let: no toNot Sorry for interrupt; not Let me to finish; not As I was say

In one line: ask with could or may, soften with just, and come back with as I was saying.

Chapter 4: Real Voices

Around the Table

One meeting, two recordings, one set of ground rules

Dialogue

The Settlement Meeting

C
Thanks for making the time, Rodrigo. Let me start with some history. My client has worked with yours since 2019, and back then the volumes were excellent, and the trade fairs in Chicago were...
R
Sorry to cut in, Claire, but could I just suggest an agenda? We only have an hour, and I don't want us to go off on a tangent.
C
Fair enough. So, point one: the termination. We say ninety days' notice was not enough, because my client...
R
May I just come in on that? The contract says ninety days. Clause twelve is very clear.
C
If I could just finish this point, Rodrigo. I'm not saying the clause is unclear. I'm saying my client had six months of stock in a warehouse in New Jersey.
R
Of course. Sorry, go ahead.
C
Thank you. So the real issue is the stock: about four hundred thousand dollars of it. That's what we came here to discuss.
R
Understood. As I was saying, the notice was valid. But to get back to your point, we may be able to buy back part of that stock.
C
Now that is a conversation worth having. Shall we take ten minutes and then pick up where we left off?
R
Good idea. Ten minutes, and then we'll talk numbers.
Comprehension

About Claire

1. Since when has Claire’s client worked with Rodrigo’s client?
Since 2019.
2. According to Claire, what is the real issue?
The stock in the warehouse in New Jersey: about four hundred thousand dollars of it.
3. What does Claire suggest at the end?
A ten-minute break, and then to pick up where they left off.
Listening 1 · The Linguist

Fast Talkers, Quiet Ones

A linguist on why interrupting is not always rude. Sound first — no text.

1. Is interrupting always rude, according to her?
No. In some families people overlap and finish each other’s sentences, and nobody is offended.
2. What does the fast talker think about the quiet one?
That the quiet one has nothing to say.
3. What should you do when someone cuts in on you?
Don’t fight. Let them finish, and then say, "As I was saying..."
Listening 2 · The Mediator

Never the Person

A former judge on objections and interruptions. Sound first — no text.

1. What happens when he overrules an objection?
The witness goes on and answers the question.
2. What do the best lawyers do when the other side starts to ramble?
They wait for a breath and say, "May I just come in on that point?"
3. What is his simple advice?
Interrupt the argument, never the person, and take the floor back with a smile.
