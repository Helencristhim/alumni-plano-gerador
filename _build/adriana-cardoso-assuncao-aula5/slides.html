Chapter 3: The Code

Two Ways to Ask

The direct question, and the one that opens with a frame

Grammar Discovery

What Changes Between the Two?

What is the attrition rate?  →  Could you tell me what the attrition rate is?
Which period are you asking about?  →  May I ask which period you are asking about?
Did the pilot work?  →  I was wondering whether the pilot worked.
Who owns this initiative?  →  Do you know who owns this initiative?
Click to reveal the rule
A direct question inverts: What is the rate? · Did the pilot work? An indirect question puts a frame in front (Could you tell me…, May I ask…, I was wondering…, Do you know…) and then the sentence goes back to normal order: subject before verb, and no do/does/did. So it is what the rate is, never what is the rate; and whether the pilot worked, never whether did the pilot work. For yes/no questions the connector is whether or if. Two exceptions worth knowing: when the question word IS the subject (who owns, what happened) nothing moves; and the indirect version ends with a period when the frame is a statement (I was wondering whether the pilot worked.).

Chapter 4: The Room

Three People, Three Ways of Asking

The silence, the interruption, and the same question twice

Listening 1

Three Seconds Are Nothing

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What does he say about the silence after a hard question?
That it is never as long as it feels: three seconds sound like thirty inside your head and like nothing in the room.
2. Why does asking before answering make the answer better?
Because half of the hard questions in a meeting are hard only because they are vague.
3. What should you do when you genuinely do not know?
Say it and give a date — "I do not have that number with me, and I will send it to you by Friday."

Asking Back

M
Adriana, why is your attrition so high?
A
That is a fair question. Could you tell me which period you are asking about? The last quarter and the last three years look very different.
M
The last quarter. And I am assuming it is about pay.
A
That is the assumption everybody starts with, and it is our blind spot. May I ask what you have seen in the other regions?
M
The same pattern, honestly. So what is your answer?
A
Let me be candid. The first month is where we lose people, and the two sites with the most overtime are the two sites we lose them from.
M
And how much has that cost us this year?
A
I do not have that figure with me, and I am not going to guess in front of this group. I will follow up by Friday with the cost per exit.
Comprehension

About Michael

1. What does he assume the cause of the attrition is?
Pay — he says he is assuming it is about pay.
2. What has he seen in the other regions?
The same pattern.
3. What is the last thing he wants to know?
How much the attrition has cost the company this year.
Chapter 5: Practice

Six Questions, None of Them Prepared

Answer, or ask back — but do not stop

Listening 2

How That Room Works

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What does the vice president do after he asks a question, and why?
He says nothing for a long time. He is waiting to see whether you add something you were not asked.
2. Why does the finance director interrupt?
Not to attack — he is checking whether you know the number well enough to keep going.
3. What is the last thing she tells you, and what does it mean?
That nobody in that room will ask about your English — it is a conversation only you are having.
