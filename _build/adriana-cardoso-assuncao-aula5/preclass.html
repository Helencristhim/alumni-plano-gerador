Lesson 05 -- Pre-class

The Question You Did Not Prepare

Asking back instead of freezing. Key words: to clarify, to probe, assumption, to follow up, blind spot, candid, to hedge, takeaway. Structure: question formation and indirect questions.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These are the words for the conversation about the conversation.

To clarify -- to make something clearer when it was not understood
"Let me clarify what I meant by early attrition."
To probe -- to ask more questions to get to what is underneath
"He is not attacking you. He is probing to see if the number holds."
Assumption -- something you believe is true without checking it
"That is an assumption. The data says something else."
To follow up -- to check later that something has actually happened
"I will follow up with the exact figure by Friday."
Blind spot -- something everyone else can see that you cannot
"Every leadership team has a blind spot. Ours is the night shift."
Candid -- honest, even when it is uncomfortable
"Let me be candid: we are still in the middle of that process."
To hedge -- to make an answer less certain on purpose
"I am not hedging. I genuinely do not have the number today."
Takeaway -- the one thing people remember after the conversation
"If there is one takeaway from this meeting, it is the two sites."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

The question came without warning: why is your attrition so high? She did not answer it. She asked one back: could you tell me which period you are asking about? The room waited, and the question got better, because the last quarter and the last three years are not the same story. Then the vice president said he was assuming it was about pay. May I ask what you have seen in the other regions? she said. He had seen the same pattern. Only then did she answer, and the answer was short. At the end he wanted the cost per exit, and she did not have it. I do not have that figure with me, and I am not going to guess. I will follow up by Friday. Nobody in the room thought less of her for it — and she had not once said the word sorry.
Why does she ask "could you tell me which period you are asking about?" and not "which period are you asking about?"
A Because the direct question is grammatically wrong
B Because the indirect version is softer in a meeting with senior people
C Because she does not know the direct form
In "may I ask what you have seen", why is it not "what have you seen"?
A After the frame, the word order goes back to normal: subject before verb
B Because the tense changed
C Because it is a yes/no question
What does she do when she does not have the figure?
A She estimates, so as not to look unprepared
B She says so and commits to a date
C She apologizes for her English

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Direct and Indirect Questions

GRAMMAR

A direct question inverts the subject and the verb. An indirect question puts a frame in front, and then the sentence goes back to normal order — subject before verb, and no do / does / did.

wh- questionCould you tell me...What is the rate? → Could you tell me what the rate is?
with an auxiliaryMay I ask...Which period are you asking about? → May I ask which period you are asking about?
yes/no questionwhether / ifDid the pilot work? → I was wondering whether the pilot worked.
question word = subjectnothing movesWho owns this? → Do you know who owns this?

The trap: the inversion survives inside the indirect question. "Could you tell me what is the rate?" is the most audible mistake an advanced Portuguese speaker makes in English. It is "what the rate is".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
You want to ask politely what the attrition rate is. Which sentence is correct?
A "Could you tell me what is the attrition rate?"
B "Could you tell me what the attrition rate is?"
C "Could you tell me what does the attrition rate?"
You want to know whether the pilot worked, politely. Which one?
A "I was wondering whether the pilot worked."
B "I was wondering did the pilot work."
C "I was wondering whether did the pilot work."
A director asks a question and you do not have the number. What is the professional answer?
A "Sorry, my English is not good enough for that."
B "I do not have that figure with me. I will follow up by Friday."
C "Maybe around fifteen percent, I think."
The question you were asked is too vague to answer. What do you do first?
A Answer the version of it that you prepared
B Ask an indirect question back to find out what they want to know
C Say you will answer it later by email

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Think of the hardest question anybody has ever asked you at work — the one you could answer and did not. Record yourself: say the question in English, ask one indirect question back, and then answer it. Take the three seconds before you start. Do not apologize for anything.
