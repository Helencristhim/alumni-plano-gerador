Complementary Activities

Extra material between classes. Nothing here is graded and nothing here is compulsory — each one runs in fifteen to thirty minutes, which is the time you said you have. All three are free, open and reachable in one click.

Lesson 1 -- Where the World Reaches You

Talk
"How to Seek Truth in the Era of Fake News" — Christiane Amanpour, TED

Fifteen minutes with a correspondent who has covered war, revolution and three decades of political leadership, in conversation with Chris Anderson. Connection to Lesson 1: this is the vocabulary of today in the mouth of somebody who lives by it — coverage, outlets, vested interests and the difference between reporting and spin. The page carries the full English transcript beside the video.

Tip: watch once with English subtitles, then again without. Write down two claims she makes and decide, for each one, whether she presents it as established or as her own inference.

Podcast
"How To Spot Misinformation" — Life Kit, NPR

Twenty minutes of clear, unhurried American English on how a false story travels and why an emotional reaction is the first warning sign. Connection to Lesson 1: it is the practical version of the same question — what do you take at face value, and what do you take with a grain of salt. The whole transcript sits on the page next to the audio.

Tip: listen to the first ten minutes without reading, then open the transcript and check what you missed. Fifteen minutes is enough — the second half can wait for another day.

Grammar Video
"Modals of Deduction and Speculation" — BBC Learning English, 6 Minute Grammar

Six minutes on exactly the structure of today: must have, might have, can't have, and where the line between them falls. Connection to Lesson 1: this is the grammar you asked to have outside the class rather than inside it, and it runs in the time it takes to drink a coffee.

Tip: pause after each example and say one sentence of your own about a story in this week's news — one with must have, one with may well have, one with can't have.
