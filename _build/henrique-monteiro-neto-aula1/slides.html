Chapter 3: The Code

What You Know and What You Guess

One auxiliary carries the whole scale

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The leak must have come from inside the ministry."

"They may well have known about it for months."

"She can't have expected that reaction."

"In hindsight, we should have seen it coming."

Four claims about the past, and not one of them is knowledge. Which speaker is most certain, and which is least?

FormWhat it doesExample
must have
+ past participle		A conclusion drawn from evidence. Confident — but still a conclusion, not knowledge."It must have come from inside."
may / might / could haveA genuine possibility, no more. May well have raises it to likely, and still stops short of proof."They may well have known for months."
can't / couldn't haveThe negative of must have. English never uses mustn't have in this meaning."She can't have expected that."
should / ought to haveRetrospective judgement: the right thing was not done, and we can see that now."We should have seen it coming."
would have
+ past participle		The unreal past — what was going to be true under a condition that never happened."A week earlier, it would have changed the vote."

The entire scale lives in one auxiliary. Choose it badly and you either sound unsure of what you know, or certain of what you are guessing.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"It mustn't have been easy for them."
"It can't have been easy for them."
"They must knew about it before the vote."
"They must have known about it before the vote."
"In hindsight, we should saw it coming."
"In hindsight, we should have seen it coming."

English splits the scale in two: must have for the confident conclusion and can't have for its negative — mustn't have does not exist in this meaning. And after every one of these modals comes have + past participle, never a simple past.

Chapter 4: The Table in Sao Paulo

A Correspondent at the Table

She has covered four continents, and she wants to know where you read

Dialogue

Where Do You Actually Read?

D
Everybody at this table has an opinion about the election. I am curious about yours — but first, where do you actually get your news?
H
Four outlets, deliberately. Two I agree with, two I do not. Anything that appears in only one of them, I follow closely before I repeat it.
D
That is more discipline than most journalists have. Does it change your mind often?
H
Rarely. But last year I was swayed by a piece on migration that I had every intention of disagreeing with.
D
Those are the good ones. What did it get right?
H
It refused to make a sweeping statement. Every number came with the reason it might be wrong.
D
You would be surprised how hard that is to sell to an editor. Most of them want a headline, not a caveat.
H
I can imagine. Although in hindsight, the pieces I still remember from the nineties were all written like that.
Comprehension

About Diane

1. What does Diane want to know before she hears his opinion?
Where he actually gets his news.
2. What does she say about his method?
That it is more discipline than most journalists have.
3. Why does she call those articles "the good ones"?
Because they are the ones that change your mind.
4. What does she say most editors want?
A headline, not a caveat.
Listening 1

The Paper With Eleven Journalists

A short bulletin. Sound first — no text.

1. Which paper broke the story, and how big is it?
A regional paper in the north, with eleven journalists.
2. How long had the documents been publicly available?
Since March — and nobody had read them.
3. What had happened by Friday?
The minister involved had resigned.
Listening 2

How the Front Page Gets Decided

An editor, talking to a room. Sound first — no text.

1. How does the newsroom decide what goes on the front page?
About fifteen people sit in a room for forty minutes every morning and argue.
2. What are the two kinds of story he says they choose between?
One that matters and nobody will read, and one everybody will read that changes nothing.
3. What does he offer as their defense when they get it wrong?
That you can see it, because it is printed with their names on it.
