Lesson 2 -- The Long Argument

Podcast
"History Is Over" — Throughline, NPR

An hour on the idea that history had reached its end after 1989, and on what the following thirty years did to that claim. Connection to Lesson 2: this is the same argument as today's text, told by the people who lived through it, and it is thick with the vocabulary of the lesson — precedent, upheaval, received wisdom. The full transcript sits on the page beside the audio.

Tip: fifteen minutes is plenty. Listen to the first segment without reading, then open the transcript and find one claim the hosts present as established and one they present as their own reading.

Talk
"A New Understanding of Human History" — David Wengrow, TED

An archaeologist arguing that the standard story of how societies became unequal does not survive contact with the evidence. Connection to Lesson 2: it is a revisionist account delivered out loud, and you can hear exactly where he concedes, where he presses and where he admits the record is silent. The English transcript is on the page.

Tip: watch with subtitles the first time. Note down every sentence in which he says what would prove him wrong — that is the move the lesson is training.

Grammar Video
"Inversion" — BBC Learning English, English Class

A short, precise treatment of exactly today's structure: which adverbials trigger inversion, what happens to the auxiliary, and where the pairs go wrong. Connection to Lesson 2: this is the grammar you asked to keep outside the class, and it fits comfortably inside fifteen minutes.

Tip: pause after each example and rebuild it as a plain sentence, then invert it again out loud. The point is the word order in your mouth, not on the page.
