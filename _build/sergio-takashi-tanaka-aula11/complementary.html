Talk
"Why we make bad decisions" -- Daniel Gilbert (TED)

A Harvard psychologist explains why people are so bad at estimating odds and value, using simple bets and everyday examples. Connection to Lesson 11: this is your subject in plain English -- probability, expected value, and the difference between what is likely and what only feels likely.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Every time he describes a chance, stop and say the same idea with a modal: "it may happen", "it is unlikely", "the odds are low".

Podcast
"Freakonomics Radio" -- Stephen J. Dubner

A free weekly show that takes one question and follows the data behind it, in clear American English. Connection to Lesson 11: the hosts almost never say "this will happen" -- they say what is likely, what the odds are, and which assumption the number depends on. That is exactly the register you need in a committee.

Tip: pick any episode and listen once for the main idea. Write down two sentences where the speaker hedges (may, might, could, likely) instead of promising, and reuse one at work this week.

"'May', 'might' and 'could' -- 6 Minute Grammar" -- BBC Learning English

A short, free grammar programme on the three modals you practiced today, with clear examples and a quick quiz at the end. Connection to Lesson 11: it drills the exact form you need -- modal plus base verb -- and the small differences in certainty between may, might and could.

Tip: watch once, then pause after each example and repeat it out loud. Finish by saying three sentences about your own work: one with may, one with might, one with could.
