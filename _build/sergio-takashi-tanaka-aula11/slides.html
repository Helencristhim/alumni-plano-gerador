The Certainty Scale

Three Ways to Say the Same Number

100% — a promise

"The exposure will rise."

70% — probable

"The exposure is likely to rise."

40% — possible

"The exposure could rise."

Same data, three different promises. In English the certainty lives in the verb — choose the wrong one and you promise what you cannot deliver.

Chapter 3: The Code

Modals of Probability

may · might · could · must · cannot

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The exposure may rise if the market moves against us."

"A downgrade is unlikely, but it could happen."

"That figure cannot be right; it is far too high."

"The loss must be from the energy desk."

What do the orange words tell us — when something happened, or how sure the speaker is?

ModalHow sure you areExample
mustAlmost sure it IS true (a deduction from the facts).The loss must be from the energy desk.
may / might / couldIt is possible — you are not sure either way.The exposure may rise next week.
cannotAlmost sure it is NOT true.That number cannot be right.
likely / unlikely toProbability without a modal: "Exposure is likely to rise." After a modal, always use the base verb: it might fall — never "might to fall", never "might falls".

One small word carries your whole level of certainty. Choose it on purpose, not by accident.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"A breach can happen next quarter."
"A breach could happen next quarter."
"The rate might to fall in September."
"The rate might fall in September."
"That number must not be right."
"That number cannot be right."

For a future possibility use may, might or could (not "can"). After a modal use the base verb ("might fall"). And to say you are sure something is not true, use cannot be — "must not" means it is forbidden.

Chapter 4: The Committee Question

Read the Forecast Note

the one-page note that goes to the committee

The Artifact

Q4 Forecast Note

Risk Committee — Forecast Note
Prepared by: Sergio Tanaka, Risk Management
For: Claudia Reis, Committee Chair
Period: Q4 — FX exposure outlook

1. Forecast. Our forecast shows FX exposure at 2.8 billion reais by December. This is an estimate, not a final figure.

2. Probability. Exposure is likely to rise from today's level. It could cross the internal limit if the currency weakens by more than four percent.

3. Assumption. The estimate rests on one assumption: that funding costs stay flat until December.

4. Downside. A breach before September is unlikely. The odds are low, but they are not zero.

5. Recommendation. We anticipate more volatility after the central bank meeting, so a cautious limit review this month is advised.

1. How sure is the note that exposure will rise?
It says exposure is likely to rise — probable, but not a promise.
2. What single assumption does the estimate rest on?
That funding costs stay flat until December.
3. What does the note say about a breach before September?
It is unlikely — the odds are low, but they are not zero.
