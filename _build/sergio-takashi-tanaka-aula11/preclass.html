Lesson 11 -- Pre-class

Hedging & Probability

Answering the committee about the future without promising what you cannot promise. Key words: likely, unlikely, forecast, estimate, uncertainty, assumption, anticipate, odds, cautious, confident. Structure: modals of probability (may, might, could, must, cannot) and the degree of certainty each one carries.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Likely -- probable and expected to happen
"A rate cut is likely before the end of the year."
Unlikely -- not probable and not expected to happen
"A default is unlikely, but we still watch it every week."
Forecast -- a statement about what will probably happen
"Our forecast shows a small rise in exposure next quarter."
Estimate -- an approximate number, not an exact one
"This is only an estimate, so please treat it with care."
Uncertainty -- the state of not knowing what will happen
"There is a lot of uncertainty in the market this month."
Assumption -- something you accept as true without proof
"The model rests on one assumption about interest rates."
To anticipate -- to expect something and prepare for it
"We anticipate more volatility after the central bank meeting."
Odds -- the chance that something will happen
"The odds of a downgrade are low this year."
Cautious -- careful, because something may go wrong
"The committee prefers a cautious forecast to an optimistic one."
Confident -- sure that something is true or will happen
"I am confident about the September figure."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

The committee wanted one answer: will the exposure rise in Q4? Our forecast points up, so I said the exposure is likely to rise. I was careful with the words. The number is an estimate, not a fact, and it rests on one assumption about funding costs. The limit could be crossed if the currency weakens, although a breach before September is unlikely — the odds are low. The position has not moved since Monday, so the exposure must already be near the upper band. One thing is certain: that old report cannot be right, because we closed the position in June. There is real uncertainty ahead, so we anticipate more volatility and stay cautious. I am confident about the data, not about the timing.

1. Why does the writer say the exposure "must" already be near the upper band?
A It is a deduction: the position has not moved since Monday.
B It is an obligation from the regulator.
C It is only a small possibility.
2. What does "could" show in "The limit could be crossed if the currency weakens"?
A That the writer is certain it will happen.
B That it is possible, but not certain.
C That it already happened last quarter.
3. Why does the writer use "cannot be right" about the old report?
A He is almost sure it is NOT true, because the position was closed in June.
B He is asking the committee for permission to change it.
C He thinks there is a small chance it is wrong.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Modals of Probability

GRAMMAR

One small word carries your whole level of certainty. Choose it on purpose.

ModalHow sure you areExample
must + base verbAlmost sure it IS true — a deduction from the facts you have.The loss must be from the energy desk.
may / might / could + base verbIt is possible — you are not sure either way. Use these for a future possibility, never "can".The exposure may rise next week.
cannot + base verbAlmost sure it is NOT true. Careful: "must not" means forbidden, not "impossible".That number cannot be right.
is likely / unlikely toProbability without a modal: "Exposure is likely to rise", "A breach is unlikely". After any modal, always use the base verb: it might fall — never "might to fall", never "might falls".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best option for each moment in a committee meeting.

The chair asks if exposure will rise. Your forecast points up, but it is only an estimate. Which is best?
A "Yes, it will rise."
B "Based on our forecast, it is likely to rise."
C "I cannot say anything about that."
You want to say a limit breach is possible but not certain. Which is best?
A "The limit can be crossed."
B "The limit could be crossed if the currency weakens."
C "The limit might to be crossed."
A number in an old report is impossible, because that position was closed. Which is best?
A "That number must not be right."
B "That number may not be right, I think."
C "That number cannot be right."
You want the committee to know what your estimate depends on. Which is best?
A "The estimate rests on one assumption: that funding costs stay flat."
B "The number is the number."
C "I am sure about everything in this report."
