Complementary Activities

Optional extras to enjoy outside class. Mark each one as done after you watch or listen to it.

Talk
TED -- Hans Rosling, "The best stats you’ve ever seen"

The most famous example of today’s lesson: a lot of data, and a man who turns it into a story the whole room remembers. Watch how few numbers he actually says out loud, and how every one of them is compared to something.

Tip: pause after five minutes and tell the story of what you just saw in three sentences, using roughly, a trend and a few.

Talk
TED -- David McCandless, "The beauty of data visualization"

A designer on why a breakdown on a page says more than a list of figures. Useful for the next board pack you prepare: it is about which numbers to show, not about how to make them pretty.

Tip: write down every quantifier you hear (a lot of, a few, much, many). You will hear more than you expect.

Watch on TED ↗
Podcast
The Indicator from Planet Money -- "To Err Is Human, To Revise Divine"

A short episode about why one month of data can lie, and why people look at the trend over three months instead. It is the finance director’s rule from today’s first listening, explained with the numbers of a whole country.

Tip: the transcript is on the page. Find one sentence with a percentage and one with an average, and say them out loud.
