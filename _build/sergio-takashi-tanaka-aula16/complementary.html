"How to disagree productively and find common ground" -- Julia Dhar (TED)

A free twelve-minute talk by a world debate champion on how to answer the other side without turning the room against you. Connection to Lesson 16: her whole method is the two-half answer you practiced today -- separate the idea from the person, name the common ground, and only then turn.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Every time she describes a disagreement, pause and build one sentence about it with although, even though or however.

Podcast
"5 Ways To Be More Open-Minded" -- Life Kit, NPR

A free episode of about twenty minutes in clear American English, with the full transcript on the same page. Connection to Lesson 16: it is about the hardest half of handling an objection -- hearing the valid part of what the other side is saying before you answer it.

Tip: listen once without the transcript, then read it and pick three points you disagree with. Say each one back out loud in two halves: the valid part first, then your turn with although or however.

Video
"Linking words of contrast" -- BBC English Class, BBC Learning English

A short free class on exactly the code you learned today: although, though, even though, despite and in spite of, and what each one takes after it. Connection to Lesson 16: it drills the difference between the connectors that need a clause and the ones that need a noun -- the mistake that costs you the sentence in a real meeting.

Tip: pause after each example and say the same idea with a different connector. If the sentence still works, you have understood the pattern.
