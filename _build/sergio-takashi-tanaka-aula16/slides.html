Chapter 3: The Code

Agreeing and Still Holding

although · even though · despite · however

Grammar Discovery

Read and Notice

"Although the fee is one percent, the package is still the best on the table."

"Even though we asked twice, they did not move on the fee."

"Despite the higher fee, this deal is cheaper over five years."

"Your concern about the size is valid. However, we can cap the exposure in the contract."

What do the orange words do to the two halves of each sentence?

ConnectorWhat comes after itExample
although · though · even thoughsubject + verb (a full clause), inside ONE sentenceAlthough the fee is high, we accept it.
despite · in spite ofa noun or an -ing form — never a subject + verbDespite the fee, we accept it. Despite paying more, we accept it.
however · neverthelessa NEW sentence (or a semicolon), then a commaThe fee is high. However, the package is the best.
while · whereassubject + verb, to put two facts side by sideWhile the fee is higher, the tenor is longer.
where it sitsThe concession can come first, with a comma: Although the fee is high, we accept it. Or second, with no comma: We accept it although the fee is high. Never "although... but", never "despite the fee is high", never "however the fee is high, we accept".

The concession is not a loss. It is what buys you the right to be heard on the half that matters.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"Although the fee is high, but we accept it."
"Although the fee is high, we accept it."
"Despite the concentration is high, the price is good."
"Despite the high concentration, the price is good."
"However the concern is valid, the deal still works."
"The concern is valid. However, the deal still works."

One turn per sentence: although already carries the contrast, so no but follows it. Despite takes a noun, not a clause. However is not a conjunction — it opens a new sentence and takes a comma.

Detail

The Two-Half Answer

Give them their point · then turn

Objection: "One percent is more than we paid last year." → "___You are right that it is more than last year. However, the five-year cost of this package is lower."
Objection: "One hundred and eighty with one bank is too much." → "___That concern is valid, and I have taken it on board. Although the amount is large, we can cap the exposure in the contract."
Objection: "We should wait for the quarterly numbers." → "___Even though the numbers come out in three weeks, the offer expires on Friday."
Accepting with a condition attached: "___We accept the fee, with one caveat: the grace period stays in writing."

Listening

Marina Reads the Room

A voice note the night before. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Which objection does Marina say is the real one?
The concentration — the size of the exposure with one bank. The fee is the easiest one to argue about, and that is exactly why it is a trap.
2. What two things does she want Sergio to arrange?
A cap written into the Nordbank contract, and a conversation with Meridian about taking forty of the one hundred and eighty.
3. What does Marina say about tone when he answers an objection?
Agree with the part that is valid before he turns. If he tells the committee their concern is wrong, he has lost them, however good the rest of the answer is.
Listening 2

Paulo on the Shape of an Answer

A colleague from the credit desk. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What are the two halves of an answer, according to Paulo?
First you give the other side their point in their own words; then you turn, with although, even though or however, and give your strongest fact.
2. What happens if Sergio uses only one of the two halves?
Only the second half and he sounds like a man who has not listened. Only the first half and he has agreed — the meeting is over.
3. Which small mistake does Paulo warn him about?
Putting "but" in the second half after starting with "although". One turn is enough: "Although the fee is high, we accept it."

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I can say that a concern is valid before I answer it.
I use "although" and "even though" with a subject and a verb, and never add "but".
I use "despite" with a noun, and "however" at the start of a new sentence.
I know the words of pushback: objection, concern, sticking point, caveat, common ground.
I can name the sticking point and answer it without giving away the rest.
