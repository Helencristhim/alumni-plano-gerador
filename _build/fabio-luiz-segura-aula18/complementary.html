Talk
Nancy Duarte — “The secret structure of great talks” (TED)

A presentation designer shows the shape that great speeches share: they move between what is and what could be, and they end with a clear call to action. It is the narrative arc of your pitch, drawn on a screen.

Tip: draw the shape she describes on paper. Then mark where your context, problem, angle and invitation go.

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart (Stanford GSB) — “Lean Messaging: How Simple Messages Really Stick”

A marketer and entrepreneur explains how to build a short message that people remember and repeat, starting from what the listener needs and not from what you want to say. Useful for your positioning statement.

Tip: after listening, rewrite your positioning statement in fifteen words or fewer.

Listen on Stanford GSB ↗
Video
Simon Sinek — “Start with why: how great leaders inspire action” (TEDxPugetSound, YouTube)

One of the most watched talks about persuasion. He argues that people respond to why you do something before what you do, which is a strong test for the context part of your pitch.

Tip: write one sentence that says why your area exists, not what it does. Try it as the second sentence of your pitch.
