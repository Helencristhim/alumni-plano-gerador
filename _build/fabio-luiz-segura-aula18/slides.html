Chapter 4: The Spotlight

What Matters Is This

A structure that makes the listener wait for the important part

Grammar Discovery

Four Sentences With a Spotlight

"What we're really trying to solve for is speed at the border."

"What makes us different is that we see the gap from both sides."

"The reason I bring this up is that the border is still slower than the country."

"What I'd love to explore is one corridor, together."

What do the red parts have in common? Where does the important information come — at the start or after is?

TypeFormExample
Plain sentencesubject + verb + informationWe need speed at the border.
Spotlight on a thingWhat + subject + verb + is + nounWhat we need is speed at the border.
Spotlight on an ideaWhat + verb + is that + sentenceWhat makes us different is that we see both sides.
Spotlight on a reasonThe reason + sentence + is that + sentenceThe reason I bring this up is that the border is slow.
QuestionWhat + subject + verb + is ...?So what you're saying is that the pilot comes first?
The trapone is, always · no of after is · no that before a nounNot What we need speed. Not What we need is of a partner.

In one line: what + subject + verb + is — the listener waits, and the important part lands at the end.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

A Model Pitch, and a Coach

Hear the arc, then hear the energy

Listening 1 · The Model Pitch

Twelve Days to Twelve Minutes

A banker from Chile pitches her area at a conference. Sound first — no text.

1. How does she open the pitch?
With a question: how long does it take to pay a supplier in another country?
2. What problem does she describe, and with which number?
Her small exporters wait up to twelve days to receive a payment from abroad, while payments inside Chile take seconds.
3. What exactly does she invite the listener to do?
To have a thirty-minute coffee tomorrow to compare corridors and see if one pilot makes sense for both sides.
Listening 2 · The Coach

Three Minutes, Three Speeds

A presentation coach on energy across a short pitch. Sound first — no text.

1. What mistake do most people make with energy in a pitch?
They start with all their energy and have nothing left for the end, where the request is.
2. How should the first sentence sound, and why?
Slower and a little lower, because it makes people lean in and pay attention.
3. How should the voice move in the invitation?
It goes up and then comes down gently, so it sounds like an open door and not like the end of a speech.
