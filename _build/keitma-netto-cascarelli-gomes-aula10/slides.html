Chapter 4: The Clause That Adds the Scope

Who, That, Where

A claim is an adjective. A clause is a fact with a shape

Grammar Discovery

Same Person, Different Answer

"I am a senior accountant."

"I am the accountant who runs the close for Brazil."

"I work in a team that covers six countries, in an office where nine languages are spoken."

"I am the person the auditors come to when a number needs explaining."

The first sentence gives a title. The next three give the same job to the same person. What is the coloured word attached to — and what does the half after it do that the title could not?

WordWhat it attaches toExample
whoa personthe accountant who runs the close
that · whicha thing, a team, a processa team that covers six countries
wherea placean office where nine languages are spoken
whosepossessiona client whose invoices always arrive late
no word at allwhen the noun is the object of the clausethe person the auditors come to — no who needed
extra information, in commasa detail you could remove without losing the sentenceMy manager, who has been there twelve years, signs it off.
the trapthe relative word IS the subject — do not repeat itNot the person who he prepares it. Say the person who prepares it.

In one line: a title is a label and a clause is a description. Everything an interviewer wants to know about your scope lives in the half that comes after who, that or where.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

From the Other Side of the Table

Two voice notes and one job description

Listening 1 · A Voice Note

What I Am Actually Testing

A hiring manager for finance roles, being honest about the forty minutes. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say he is not testing, and why not?
Your accounting — because the CV already told him you can do the work, and the reference will tell him the rest.
2. What is he working out in those forty minutes, and why does it matter so much?
Whether you can explain something complicated to somebody outside your field — because that is eighty per cent of a senior finance job and nobody can teach it quickly.
3. What three things does he want you to describe about your role?
Who comes to you and with what, which decisions are yours and which are somebody else's, and what happens in your process when you are on holiday.
Listening 2 · A Recruiter

An Adjective Is Not a Strength

A recruiter on the question that catches almost everybody.

1. Which question does she say catches people, and what is wrong with the usual answer?
“Tell me about a strength” — almost everybody answers with an adjective, which says nothing and sounds identical to the four candidates before them.
2. What does she say works instead, and what example does she give?
An adjective plus evidence, in one breath — “I am the person who finds the difference before the auditor does, and last year that saved us three weeks of review.”
3. What is her advice about the weakness question?
Do not perform humility. Name something real, say what you are doing about it, and move on before it becomes the subject of the interview.
