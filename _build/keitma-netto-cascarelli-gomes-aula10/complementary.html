Talk
Casey Brown — Know your worth, and then ask for it (TED)

Nine minutes on describing what you do in a way that somebody else can price. Her whole argument is that most people undersell not because they are modest but because they have never put their own scope into a sentence.

Tip: after watching, write the sentence she is asking for — about your own work — using two relative clauses. Then read it out loud and see whether you believe it.

Think Fast, Talk Smart: Communication Techniques (Stanford)

Worth returning to now that you have ten lessons behind you. The second half is entirely about answering a question you did not prepare for — which is what an interview is, and what a global call is too.

Tip: this time watch the last twenty minutes, and try his structures out loud on the interview questions from today's lesson.

Planet Money — Older workers aren't retiring. Should they be forced to?

Half an hour about careers, timing and what happens to people in the middle of a working life. It is the subject of this lesson from the outside, in fast unscripted American English.

Tip: listen for how the journalists describe somebody's job. Almost every description is a relative clause — write down three of them.
