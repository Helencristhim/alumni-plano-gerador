Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Dial in -- to join a phone or video call
"Tom will dial in from New York at nine."
Loop in -- to include someone in a conversation or an email so they know what is happening
"Let me loop in Tom: he knows the tax side."
Chime in -- to add your comment to a conversation that is already happening
"Paulo, feel free to chime in on the Brazilian side."
Talk over -- to speak at the same time as another person, so nobody can hear them
"Sorry, we are talking over each other. Ryan, go ahead."
Cut out -- to stop being heard for a moment because of a bad connection
"Sorry, Lucia, you cut out. Could you repeat the date?"
Hand over -- to give the turn to speak, or a task, to another person
"I will hand over to Paulo for the documents."
Park -- to stop discussing a topic now and come back to it later
"Let us park the interest question and come back to it at the end."
On the same page -- agreeing and understanding a situation in the same way
"Before we close, let me check that we are all on the same page."
Takeaway -- the main point or action that people should remember after a meeting
"The key takeaway is the new date: the fifteenth of April."
Consensus -- an agreement that everyone in a group accepts
"We have consensus on the date, but not yet on the interest."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Tomorrow Simone will chair a call with five people in three cities. Javier and Lucia will dial in from Madrid, and Ryan will loop in Tom Walsh from the tax team in New York. Paulo Mendes, the local counsel, will join from Sao Paulo. The last call was a disaster: two people talked over each other, and Lucia cut out twice. This time Simone has a plan. She will open with the agenda, then hand over to each person by name, so that people can chime in without fighting for the turn. If the interest question gets too big, she will park it. To check agreement, she will use question tags: we agreed on April, didn't we? and the indemnity covers the debt, doesn't it? Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Question Tags

GRAMMAR

A question tag is a short question at the end of a sentence: didn't we?, isn't it?. On a call, it checks that people agree and passes the turn to them. The tag uses the same auxiliary as the sentence (be, do, did, can) and a pronoun. A positive sentence takes a negative tag; a negative sentence takes a positive tag.

FormUseExample
be (positive)isn't it? / aren't we?"The date is fixed, isn't it?"
present simple (positive)don't / doesn't + pronoun"The indemnity covers the debt, doesn't it?"
past simple (positive)didn't + pronoun"We agreed on April, didn't we?"
modal (positive)the same modal + n't"Tom can send them, can't he?"
any negative sentencea positive tag"Paulo isn't here yet, is he?"

Key point: use a pronoun, never a name: "Tom can send them, can't he?" And use falling intonation when you expect yes: you are checking, not really asking.

Quiz

C "We agreed April, yes or no?"
