Chapter 4: The Code

The System as the Subject

When the action matters more than the person

Grammar Discovery

Who Does the Action?

"A fall is detected in two seconds."

"The alerts are sent to the nurse’s phone."

"The footage is stored for thirty days."

"Faces are not recognized, and the images can be seen only by the security team."

In these sentences, the person who does the action is not important, or we do not know who it is. The subject is the thing: a fall, the alerts, the footage. The verb in blue has two parts: is / are + the third form.

JobFormExample
One thingis + third formThe footage is stored on site.
Many thingsare + third formThe alerts are sent to the nurse.
Negativeis not / are not + third formFaces are not recognized.
QuestionIs / Are + subject + third form?Is the footage stored in the cloud?
With cancan be + third formThe images can be seen only by security.
Who does it (if important)by + personThe alerts are checked by the nurse.

In one line: when the action matters more than the person: is / are + the third form of the verb.

This is how the system describes its work: the subject is the event, not the person. Falls are detected, people are flagged, footage is stored. The two lines in color are what a manager reads first in the morning.
1. Turn the Falls detected line into a sentence in the passive.
Two falls were detected, and the alerts were sent to the nurses in three seconds. (Or, describing the system: falls are detected and alerts are sent in three seconds.)
2. What was the false alarm?
A patient who was sitting on the floor to pray.
3. Explain the last three lines to a client in two sentences.
The footage is stored on the hospital server for thirty days. Faces are not recognized, and the images can be seen only by the security team.
Listening 1 · The Data Protection Officer

Three Questions Before Any Camera

The data protection officer of a hospital in the United Kingdom explains what she asks every supplier. Sound first — no text.

1. What is her first question to every supplier?
What exactly is recorded, and what is analyzed.
2. How long is footage kept in her hospital?
Thirty-one days, and then it is deleted automatically.
3. Who can see the footage?
Only three people from the security team, and every access is recorded.
Listening 2 · Chen, the Engineer

How Our System Works

Chen, the engineer from Shenzhen, explains his video analytics system to a group of hospital managers. Sound first — no text.

1. How many feeds can one server analyze?
Up to sixty-four feeds.
2. What happens when a person enters a restricted area?
The person is flagged on the screen and an alert is sent to the security team.
3. How much did false alarms fall in the last version?
By half.
Listening 3 · The Nurse Manager

The Alert on My Phone

A nurse manager in a hospital in Singapore talks about the fall alerts in her ward. Sound first — no text.

1. How many beds are there in her ward?
Forty beds.
2. What did the nurses think in the first month?
They were not happy: there were too many false alarms.
3. What changed after the system was adjusted?
Almost all the alerts are real now, and falls are seen in seconds, not in minutes.
