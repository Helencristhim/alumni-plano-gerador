Lesson 12 -- Pre-class

What Do You Think? -- An Opinion That Holds Up

Saying what you think and keeping it alive for more than one sentence. Key words: argue, point, reason, choice, value, prefer, trend-driven, versatile. Structure: because, so, but, that's why and for example.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. Six work in any discussion, and two come from your field.

To argue -- to give reasons to show that your opinion is right
"I would argue that a good jacket is better value than four cheap ones."
Point -- one idea or opinion that somebody gives in a discussion
"That is a good point, but I see it differently."
Reason -- the thing that explains why you think or do something
"The reason I prefer vintage is that it lasts."
Choice -- something you decide on when there are two or more possibilities
"With a small budget, every choice has to work hard."
Value -- how useful or important something is, compared to its price
"I do not think of it as a green decision. I think of it as good value."
To prefer -- to like one thing more than another
"I prefer versatile pieces to trend-driven ones."
Trend-driven -- decided by what is popular right now, not by what you need
"A trend-driven piece sells fast, but it also stops selling fast."
Versatile -- useful in many different ways or situations
"These black pants are versatile, so I wear them three times a week."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

A friend asked me if fashion is a waste of money. I said no, but some fashion is. I prefer versatile pieces because I wear them all the time. For example, I have a black jacket that I wear almost every week. It was not cheap, so I thought about it for a month before I bought it. Now it is the best value in my wardrobe. Trend-driven pieces are different. They look great for six weeks, but after that nobody wants them. That's why I always ask one question before I buy something: will I still wear this next year?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Five Words That Connect an Opinion

GRAMMAR

An opinion with one sentence stops the conversation. Each of these five words does one job, and together they turn one sentence into three.

because+ the reasonI prefer vintage because it lasts longer.
so+ the result, same sentenceIt is versatile, so I wear it every week.
but+ the other sideIt is cheap, but it does not last.
That's why+ the result, new sentenceMy budget is small. That's why every choice matters.
For example+ the proofFor example, my jacket is from 1998.

Careful 1: because and so go in opposite directions. "I bought it because it was cheap" = "It was cheap, so I bought it."

Careful 2: because + a sentence. Because of + a noun. "Because of it lasts" is wrong.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
A classmate asks: "Is fast fashion always bad?" Which answer keeps the conversation going?
A "Yes."
B "Not always, because of it is cheap and some people have no other choice in their city."
C "Not always. It is cheap, so some people have no other choice."
You agree with part of what somebody said, but not with all of it. What do you say?
A "I see your point, but I see it differently."
B "You are wrong, and I do not want to talk about fashion with you anymore."
C "I agree."
Somebody says your opinion has no proof. What is the best next sentence?
A "That's why I think it, because I think it is true, and that is my opinion."
B "For example, I wear one jacket every week."
C "So."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
You are the guest on a university fashion podcast. The question is: what matters more, style or sustainability? Speak for one to two minutes. Give your opinion, at least one reason with because, one example with for example, and one result with so or that's why. Do not write a script first — just speak.
