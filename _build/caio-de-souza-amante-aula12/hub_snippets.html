Lesson 12 -- Pre-class

The Language of Performance -- Reviewing What People Do, Not Who They Are

A buyer reads your team through the way you describe it: activities are evidence, adjectives are not. Key words: a performance review, accountable, to underperform, ownership, to mentor, a KPI, to exceed expectations, candid. Structure: the -ing form as a subject and after prepositions, it is worth + -ing, adjective + to.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A performance review -- a meeting where a manager and an employee talk about how well the employee has worked
“Her performance review is on Friday, and it will be the first one after the audit.”
Accountable -- responsible for a result and expected to explain it
“Marina is accountable for every project that we deliver to banks.”
To underperform -- to do less well than people expected
“Telling someone they are underperforming is hard, but waiting is worse.”
Ownership -- the attitude of treating a problem as your own job to solve
“Taking ownership of the late project was the moment I knew he was ready.”
To mentor -- to help a less experienced person grow by giving advice over a long time
“Mentoring the two new managers will be her main goal next year.”
A KPI -- a number that shows how well a person or a team is doing
“The KPI that matters most for delivery is projects finished on time.”
To exceed expectations -- to do better than what was planned or expected
“She exceeded expectations on every KPI except hiring.”
Candid -- honest and direct, even when the truth is uncomfortable
“I want a candid review, not a polite one.”

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Most founders are good at hiring and bad at reviewing. Hiring feels like building something; reviewing feels like judging somebody you hired yourself. So the review gets postponed, and when it finally happens it is full of adjectives: she is great, he is a bit slow. A buyer who reads those reviews learns nothing about the team, and the team is a large part of what the buyer is paying for.

Performance language in English depends on one form: the -ing form used as a noun. Managing the largest account is her real job. Missing two deadlines in a row is the problem, not the person. Hiring slowly was the best decision we made in 2020. The subject of each sentence is an activity, and that is exactly the point: you are reviewing what somebody does, not who they are.

The same form follows every preposition: she is good at keeping clients, thank you for staying late, instead of blaming the team. Two more patterns complete the toolkit. It is worth plus -ing says something deserves the effort: it is worth investing in her. An adjective plus to says how hard or easy something is, or how you felt: she is hard to replace, I was surprised to see the numbers.

The trap for a Portuguese speaker is the first word of the sentence. In Portuguese the bare verb can open a sentence, and the direct translation produces hire slowly was the best decision, which is not English. Put -ing on the verb and the sentence works: hiring slowly was the best decision. The same thing happens after worth: not it is worth to invest, but it is worth investing.

1. According to the text, why do reviews full of adjectives fail?
A Because adjectives are too informal for a document that the lawyers of the buyer will read during due diligence.
B Because a buyer who reads them learns nothing about the team.
C Because the team members are offended by them.
2. Why does the text recommend an activity as the subject of the sentence?
A Because you are reviewing what somebody does, not who they are.
B Because English sentences cannot start with the name of a person.
C Because it makes the sentence shorter and more formal, which is what buyers expect from the CEO in a written review.
3. What is wrong with hire slowly was the best decision?
A Slowly has to come before hire.
B Was should be were, because the sentence is about all the people that the company hired over the years.
C The verb that opens the sentence needs the -ing form.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- -ing as a Subject, After Prepositions, and Adjective + to

GRAMMAR

Review the activity, not the person. The activity takes -ing.

JobFormExample
An activity as the subject-ing + verbManaging the largest account is her real job.
After a prepositionat / for / of / instead of / before + -ingShe is good at keeping clients.
Saying something deserves the effortit is worth + -ingIt is worth investing in her.
Saying how hard or easyadjective + to + verbShe is hard to replace.
Saying how you feltadjective + to + verbI was surprised to see the numbers.
Plans for a person (Lesson 11)verb + to · verb + -ingWe plan to promote him. We avoid promoting too early.
The trapthe verb that opens the sentence takes -ingHiring slowly was the best decision. Not hire slowly was.

Never: hire slowly was · she is good at keep · it is worth to invest · thank you for stay
