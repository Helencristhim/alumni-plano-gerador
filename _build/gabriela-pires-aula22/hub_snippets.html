Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Ouça cada palavra e leia o exemplo. Toque em Listen para ouvir. / Listen to each word and read the example.

Aunt -- the sister of your mother or your father
"My aunt Cristina has two children." (Tia)
Uncle -- the brother of your mother or your father
"My uncle Paulo tells terrible jokes." (Tio)
Cousin -- the son or the daughter of your aunt and uncle
"My cousins are eight and eleven years old." (Primo / prima)
Grandparents -- the mother and the father of your mother or father
"My grandparents live in another neighborhood." (Avós)
Twins -- two brothers or sisters who were born on the same day
"Kate and Ryan are twins." (Gêmeos)
Only child -- a person who has no brothers and no sisters
"I am an only child." (Filho único / filha única)
Stepbrother -- the son of the new husband or wife of your mother or father
"My stepbrother Danny is nineteen." (Meio-irmão / irmão por parte de casamento)
Get along -- to have a good, friendly relationship with someone
"We get along very well." (Se dar bem)
Look like -- to have a similar face or appearance to another person
"People say I look like my mother." (Parecer-se com)

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Leia o texto e responda às perguntas. / Read the text and answer the questions.

Ryan shows Gabriela a photo of his family in Chicago. "This is Kate, my twin sister," he says. "And this is Danny, my stepbrother. At first it was strange, but now we get along really well." Kate and Ryan do not look like each other at all. Her hair is red and his hair is dark. Ryan's grandparents live in the same street, so their house is always full of people. His aunt Laura and his uncle Steve have three children, and those cousins come every Sunday. Gabriela smiles: "My family is smaller. I am an only child."

B His grandparents.
C His stepbrother's mother.
3. Why does Gabriela say her family is smaller?
A Because she is an only child.

C Because her grandparents live in Chicago.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Possessive Adjectives

GRAMMAR

Em inglês, a palavra que diz DE QUEM concorda com o DONO, nunca com a coisa. Ryan é homem, então é his sister. Kate é mulher, então é her brother. E não muda no plural: his cousin, his cousins. / In English the possessive word agrees with the OWNER, never with the thing after it.

Owner / DonoWord / PalavraExample / Exemplo
I / eumyMy aunt lives in Sao Paulo.
you / vocêyourIs your family big?
he / elehisRyan and his sister.
she / elaherKate and her cousins.
we / nósourOur family is big.
they / elestheirTheir house is small.
a name / um nome'sMy mother's sister is my aunt.

Key point / Ponto-chave: pergunte sempre "quem é o DONO?". Ryan (homem) → his; Kate (mulher) → her. E nunca escreva "theirs children": antes do substantivo é their.
