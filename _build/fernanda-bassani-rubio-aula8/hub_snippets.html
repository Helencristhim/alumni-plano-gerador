Which Option Is Better? -- Compare and Choose

The samples are late and the customer is waiting: air or sea? Key words: an option, to compare, a cost, reliable, quality, air freight, sea freight, a competitor. Structure: comparatives and superlatives.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The customer wants the new samples quickly, and Fernanda has two options. She asks the logistics team to compare them. Air freight is faster than sea freight: five days, not five weeks. But the cost is higher.

Sea freight is the cheapest option, and the shipping company is very reliable. For big orders, it is the best option.

But a competitor is testing a similar product at the same factory, so time is more important than cost. The quality is the same. Fernanda decides: the samples go by air.

1. How long does sea freight take?
A Five days.
B Five weeks.
C Two weeks.
2. Why does Fernanda choose air freight?
A Because it is the cheapest.
B Because time is more important than cost, and a competitor is at the same factory.
C Because the quality is better by air.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “Air is more fast than sea.”
B “Air is faster than sea.”
C “Air is fastest than sea.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Comparatives and Superlatives

GRAMMAR

To compare two things, use -er or more + than. To choose one from a group, use the -est or the most. Good, better, the best is irregular.

JobFormExample
Short word (1 syllable)word + -er + thanAir is faster than sea.
Long word (2+ syllables)more + word + thanThis supplier is more reliable than the other one.
The top of the group, short wordthe + word + -estSea is the cheapest option.
The top of the group, long wordthe most + wordIt is the most reliable supplier.
Irregulargood → better → the best · bad → worse → the worstFor samples, air is the best option.
The trapnever more + -erNot more faster or more better. Say faster, better.

Never: more fast · more better · the most cheap · more reliable Supplier A (without than)
