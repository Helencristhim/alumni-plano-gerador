Chapter 4: The Code

Faster, Cheaper, the Best

Comparatives and superlatives

Grammar Discovery

Four Sentences From the Email

"Air freight is faster than sea freight."

"This supplier is more reliable than the other one."

"Sea freight is the cheapest option."

"For samples, air is the best option."

Sentences 1 and 2 compare two things, with than. Sentences 3 and 4 choose one from all, with the. Short words take -er / -est; long words take more / the most.

JobFormExample
Short word (1 syllable)word + -er + thanAir is faster than sea.
Long word (2+ syllables)more + word + thanThis supplier is more reliable than the other one.
The top of the group, short wordthe + word + -estSea is the cheapest option.
The top of the group, long wordthe most + wordIt is the most reliable supplier.
Irregulargood → better → the best · bad → worse → the worstFor samples, air is the best option.
The trapnever more + -erNot more faster or more better. Say faster, better.

In one line: short word + -er / -est; long word + more / the most; good, better, the best.

Listening 1 · Wei Explains

Wei Compares the Options

Wei, at the plant in Shanghai, explains the shipping options. Sound first — no text.

1. How long does it take by air, and by sea?
Five days by air; about five weeks by sea.
2. How much does air freight cost for twelve boxes?
About two thousand dollars.
3. Which option does Wei think is best, and why?
Air, because the customer wants to start the trial quickly.
Listening 2 · Two Suppliers

The Better Supplier

A purchasing manager in India compares two packaging suppliers. Sound first — no text.

1. Which supplier is cheaper, and by how much?
Supplier A, by about fifteen percent.
2. How many times was Supplier A late last year?
Four times.
3. Which supplier does he choose, and why?
Supplier B, because it is more reliable, and time is the most important thing.
