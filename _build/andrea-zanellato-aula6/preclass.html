Period Drama Night -- Films Set in the Past

Big houses, costumes and family secrets. Key words: a period drama, a costume, the plot, to be set in, a main character, a scene, a review, a spoiler. Structure: past continuous and past simple, with when and while.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A period drama -- a film or series about life in another time in history
"Downton Abbey is a famous period drama."
A costume -- the clothes an actor wears in a film
"The costumes in that film are beautiful."
The plot -- the story of a film or a book: what happens
"The plot is simple, but the actors are great."
To be set in -- to happen in a place or a time (for a story)
"The film is set in England in the 1800s."
A main character -- the most important person in a story
"Elizabeth is the main character of the film."
A scene -- one part of a film in one place and time
"My favorite scene is the dance at the ball."
A review -- an article with an opinion about a film, a book or a show
"I always read a review before I choose a film."
A spoiler -- information that tells you the end of a story before you watch it
"Please, no spoilers! I didn’t watch the last episode."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Hannah writes reviews for a magazine. Her favorite scene is from a period drama set in a big country house. A family was having dinner. It was raining, and everybody was talking.

While the father was telling a story, a servant came in with a letter. The father read it, and the plot of the whole series changed.

Mike was flying to London when he found a period drama. The main character was fighting for the right to study, and the costumes were beautiful. No spoilers here!

1. What was the family doing in Hannah’s favorite scene?
A They were having dinner.
B They were dancing in the garden of the big country house.
C They were reading letters.
2. Where was Mike when he found the period drama?
A At home, on the sofa, with his family on a Saturday night.
B On a plane to London.
C At the cinema.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “The family were have dinner when the letter arrived.”
B “The family was having dinner when the letter arrived.”
C “The family having dinner when the letter was arriving in the house.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Continuous and Past Simple -- When and While

GRAMMAR

Use was / were + -ing for a long action in the past (the background of a scene). Use the past simple for a short action that interrupts it. When goes with the short action; while goes with the long one.

JobFormExample
Long action (background)was / were + verb-ingShe was walking in the garden.
I / he / she / itwas + -ingHe was reading a letter.
you / we / theywere + -ingThey were dancing.
Short action (interrupts)past simple...when she saw him.
when / whilewhen + short action · while + long actionWhile they were eating, a letter arrived.
The trapno -ing without was / wereNot she walking. Say she was walking.

Never: They was dancing · She were reading · I watching the film · While I watched, the phone was ringing (for a short action)
