Chapter 1: First Words

The Call Nobody Wants to Make

When did you last have to tell somebody that things were worse than they thought — a client, a colleague, your family? How did you start?

Today’s Goal

Honest First, Then Useful

1. The Words

to break the news, to sugarcoat, a long shot...

2. One Structure

What we can do is... · It's X that..., not Y

3. Your Turn

warn · pause · tell · reframe

People forgive bad news. They do not forgive the feeling that you were not honest. The skill is to be honest first, and useful straight after.

One Question

Tell Her Right Now

Your client is sure she will recover two million dollars from a supplier who delivered late. You have just read the contract: a clause caps the supplier's liability at the price of the order, about four hundred thousand. She calls you. Tell her, out loud, right now.

Then do it again — and this time, end with something she can do.

Chapter 3: The Code

Move the Spotlight, Keep the Facts

How English puts the focus exactly where you want it

Grammar Discovery

Same Facts. Where Is the Spotlight?

What the contract does is cap their liability.

What we can do is claim the direct costs.

It's the lost profits that the clause excludes, not the delay.

What worries me is the timing, not the merits.

Say each one the simple way. The facts stay the same — so what do the red words do?

JobFormExample
Spotlight on the solutionWhat + subject + verb + is + focusWhat we can do is claim the direct costs.
Spotlight on an actionWhat + subject + does / did + is + verbWhat the clause does is cap the liability.
Correct a misunderstandingIt is / was + focus + that + rest (+ not...)It's the profits that it excludes, not the delay.
Name the real problemWhat worries me / concerns me is...What worries me is the timing.
Soften a suggestionWhat I'd suggest is (that)...What I'd suggest is that we wait for the award.
Limit, honestlyAll we can do is + verbAll we can do now is wait.
The trapsthat (not what) after It's... · no extra it after What... Not It's the cap what worries me. Not What we can do it is.

In one line: the facts stay the same — the cleft moves the listener's attention from the problem to the next step.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Bad News, at Full Speed

One call, two recordings, one email

Dialogue

Can We Sue or Not?

C
Rodrigo, just tell me. They delivered three months late. Can we sue them for the two million or not?
R
I'll be upfront with you, Claire, and I'm not going to sugarcoat it. The claim for lost profits is a long shot.
C
A long shot? We bore the brunt of it, not them. My team worked every weekend in March.
R
I know, and you have every right to vent. What the contract does is cap their liability at the price of the order.
C
So we just accept it? That's ridiculous. Nobody told me that when we signed.
R
No, we don't just accept it. What we can do is claim the direct costs, and those are well documented. It's the lost profits that the clause excludes, not the delay itself.
C
And how much is that worth?
R
About a third of what you hoped. It's a setback, but there is a silver lining: that part of the case is strong, and it should settle quickly. Let's weigh up both options before you decide.
Comprehension

About Claire

1. What happened with Claire's supplier, and how much does she want to claim?
The supplier delivered three months late. She wants to claim two million.
2. Why does Claire say that her company bore the brunt of the delay?
Because her team had to work every weekend in March.
3. How does Claire react when she hears about the cap?
She says it is ridiculous and complains that nobody told her about it when they signed.
Listening 1 · The Doctor

Let the Silence Do Its Work

A doctor on how she learned to give bad news. Sound first — no text.

1. What went wrong the first time she gave bad news?
She said everything in one breath, in the corridor, and kept talking because the silence frightened her. Nobody heard anything after the first sentence.
2. What does she do now before she gives the facts?
She sits down at the same level, gives a warning first, and then stops and lets the silence work.
3. What do people never forget, according to her?
The feeling that somebody was not honest with them.
Listening 2 · The Complaints Desk

Never Say Calm Down

A former airline manager on handling angry customers. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he tell new staff never to say, and why?
Calm down — because nobody has ever calmed down because somebody told them to.
2. How long do most people take to run out of anger?
About ninety seconds, if you do not interrupt.
3. What does he say instead of "there is nothing we can do"?
"What we can do today is this." The conversation moves to the next step, not the last mistake.
Real Document

After the Call, in Writing

Your claim against Norland Supply: where we stand
Confidential · attorney-client privileged

FROM: Rodrigo Marques de França · TO: Claire Benson

Claire, thank you for the call this morning. To confirm what we discussed: the claim for lost profits is unlikely to succeed. What the contract does is limit Norland's liability to the price of the order, about 400,000 dollars.

It is the lost profits that the clause excludes, not the delay. What we can do is claim your direct costs (overtime, storage and air freight), which are well documented. I would expect a settlement in three to four months.

Could you let me know by Friday whether you would like us to proceed?

Direct costs documented: 410,000 dollars · liability cap: 400,000 dollars · reply requested by Friday
1. Where in the email does the bad news appear?
In the first paragraph, straight after the thank-you: the claim for lost profits is unlikely to succeed.
2. Which three direct costs can Claire still claim?
Overtime, storage and air freight.
3. What does Rodrigo need from Claire, and by when?
A decision on whether to proceed, by Friday.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I give bad news in the right order: warning, fact, reason, next step.
I let the other person vent, and I name the feeling before I give the facts.
I reframe with What we can do is... instead of There is nothing we can do.
I correct a misunderstanding with It's... that..., not..., and I never say It's... what...
I know the words: to sugarcoat, to be upfront, a long shot, a silver lining, damage control.
