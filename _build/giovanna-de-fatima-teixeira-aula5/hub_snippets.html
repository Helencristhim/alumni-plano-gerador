Right Now vs Every Day

Answer the phone in the middle of your shift and say what is happening at that minute, without losing your routine. Take your time, and repeat as often as you like.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Three of them are about your work, three about time, and three about the phone.

Measure -- to find the size, the length or the depth of something
"I measure the tunnel wall every Monday."
Wait for -- to stay in one place until something or someone arrives
"We are waiting for the truck."
Take a look -- to look at something quickly, to check it
"Can you take a look at the drawing?"
Busy -- working on something now, with no free time to stop
"Sorry, I am busy at the moment."
Meeting -- a time when people sit together to talk about work
"The meeting starts at seven."
Right now -- at this exact minute, not before and not after
"What are you doing right now?"
At the moment -- now, but not every day -- it can change tomorrow
"At the moment I am working on the south tunnel."
Hold on -- wait a short time -- you say it on the phone
"Hold on, I am writing it down."
Speaking -- the word you say on the phone to mean "that is me"
"Giovanna speaking."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the text out loud, then answer the questions below. Read slowly -- nobody is timing you.

It is nine in the morning and my phone is ringing. I answer it: "Giovanna speaking." It is Greg, from Seattle. He asks if I am busy right now. I am: I am measuring the tunnel wall at the moment, with the tape in my hand. Every Monday I measure this wall and I write the numbers in my report, so this is my routine, not a surprise. But today the site is different. My crew are waiting for a delivery at the south gate, and the truck is not coming. My supervisor is having a meeting with the client, so nobody is answering the office phone. Greg asks me to take a look at a drawing tonight. "Hold on," I say, "I am writing it down." I always read the drawings at home, so tonight is fine.

1. The text says "Every Monday I measure this wall" and "I am measuring the tunnel wall at the moment". Why are the two forms different?
A The first one is a routine; the second one is happening while she speaks.
B The first one is the past and the second one is the present.
C There is no difference. You can use any of the two.
2. What is missing in this sentence: "My supervisor having a meeting"?
A Nothing. The sentence is correct.
B The verb be: "My supervisor is having a meeting."
C The word "the" before "meeting".
3. Which time words go with am, is or are + -ing?
A every Monday, always, never
B right now, at the moment, today
C yesterday, last week, in 2019

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Right Now vs Every Day

Grammar

Two forms of the same verb. One is your routine; the other is this exact minute.

FormWhen you use itTime words
I measureyour routine -- true every weekevery Monday, always, usually, never
I am measuringthis minute -- the tool is in your handright now, at the moment, today
The testAsk yourself: is it happening while I speak? Yes → am / is / are + -ing. No, it is my routine → the simple verb.
Sentence typeStructureExample
PositiveI am / he is / we are + verb-ingI am measuring the wall.
Negativenot comes after am / is / areHe is not answering the phone.
Questionam / is / are jumps in front of the personWhat are you doing right now?
Spellinghave → having · take → taking · sit → sitting · wait → waiting
CarefulNever drop the verb be. "I measuring" does not exist in English -- say I am measuring.
