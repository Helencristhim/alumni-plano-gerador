On Track or Behind Schedule?

Get ready to manage a project. Key words: task, progress, on track, behind schedule, to postpone, extension, to submit, workload. Structure: by for deadlines, until for actions that continue, before and after + time or -ing.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Eight words and expressions to manage a project. Tap Listen to hear each one.

Task -- one piece of work that a person must do
"Every task on the list has a name and a date."
Progress -- how much of the work is done now
"We check the progress every Monday morning."
On track -- going as planned, with no delay
"The rest of the project is on track."
Behind schedule -- later than the plan said
"Because of the rain, we are a little behind schedule."
To postpone -- to move an event to a later date
"We had to postpone the meeting until Monday."
Extension -- extra time that you get to finish something
"Can we ask the board for a two-week extension?"
To submit -- to send or give work to someone for a decision
"We must submit the report by Friday."
Workload -- the amount of work that one person has to do
"Paulo's workload is too heavy this month."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read Rafael's status email to the board, then answer the questions.

Dear board members, here is the progress of our two projects. The Cold Chain Project is on track: the first ten containers will be ready by March 15. One task is behind schedule: the temperature tests. The test company is closed until Monday, so we will submit the results by next Friday. Claire Morgan gave us a one-week extension.

The new farms project had a problem: it rained until last Thursday. We will finish the irrigation by the end of October and plant the first trees after that. Paulo's workload is very heavy, so I suggest we postpone the farm visit until November. We will check everything again before sending the next report.

1. When will the first ten containers be ready?
A By March 15.
B Until March 15.
C After the tests.
2. Why does the text say "closed until Monday" and not "closed by Monday"?
A Because it is a deadline.
B Because the company stays closed, and then opens on Monday.
C Because Monday is in the past.
3. What does Rafael suggest about the farm visit?
A To do it before the rain.
B To cancel it.
C To postpone it until November.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- By, Until, Before, After

GRAMMAR

Use by for a deadline, until for an action that continues, and before or after for the order of events.

UseStructureExample
by + timea deadline: at that time or beforeSend it by Friday.
until + timean action continues, then stops at that timeWe work until six.
before + time / -ingearlier thanRead it before signing.
after + time / -inglater thanWe'll call you after the audit.

Never: submit it until Friday (for a deadline) · before of the meeting · after to check

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose what a project lead would say in each situation.

The client needs the report on Friday at the latest. You say:
A "We'll send it until Friday."
B "We'll send it by Friday."
C "We'll send it before of Friday."
The office is open from 8 a.m. and closes at 6 p.m. You say:
A "The office is open until 6 p.m."
B "The office is open by 6 p.m."
C "The office is open after 6 p.m."
You need more time for a task. You ask the client:
A "Can you give us an extension until to Friday?"
B "We are on track, so we need more time."
C "Can you give us a one-week extension?"
You explain the order of the work. You say:
A "We check the containers before sending the fruit."
B "We check the containers before to send the fruit."
C "We check the containers before of sending."
