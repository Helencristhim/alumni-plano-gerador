Lesson 12 -- Pre-class

The Call for Proposals -- Reading an Open Call

A full open call for an art and health residency, and the grammar that says how heavy each rule is. Key words: an open call, a residency, eligible, a deadline, a submission, mandatory, a criterion, a track record, to comply with, a stipend, to shortlist, to disqualify. Structure: requirement language (must, should, be required to, do not have to).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An open call -- a public invitation for artists to send proposals for a project, a show or a residency
"The open call closes on March 15, and nobody gets an extension."
A residency -- a period when an artist lives and works inside an institution, usually with support
"The residency lasts four months, inside a public hospital."
Eligible -- allowed to apply because you meet all the conditions
"You are not eligible if you held the same residency in the last five years."
A deadline -- the last day or time when something can be sent or done
"The deadline is midnight in Lisbon, not midnight in Brazil."
A submission -- the complete set of documents you send when you apply
"Incomplete submissions are not read at all."
Mandatory -- required by the rules; it cannot be skipped
"The budget is mandatory; the catalog is optional."
A criterion -- a standard used to judge or decide something (plural: criteria)
"Care for the people involved is the third criterion."
A track record -- the record of what you have achieved before
"You need a track record of at least five years of independent work."
To comply with -- to obey a rule or meet a requirement
"Every project must comply with the ethics policy of the hospital."
A stipend -- a fixed sum paid to cover living costs during a project
"The stipend covers rent and food, but not materials."
To shortlist -- to choose a small group of the best applicants for the final round
"The panel will shortlist ten artists for an interview."
To disqualify -- to exclude someone because they broke a rule
"A missing consent form will disqualify the application."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The Harbor Foundation has published an open call for three hospital residencies. You do not have to be a medical professional, but you must show a track record of five years of independent work.

To be eligible, applicants must not be enrolled in a degree program. Every submission must include a proposal, a portfolio and a budget, and a letter of support is not required, although it is strongly recommended.

Projects that record patients are required to comply with the ethics policy of the hospital. Applicants should explain how consent will be obtained, and residents are expected to present their work at the end.

1. "You do not have to be a medical professional." What does this mean?
A Only people who work as medical professionals in a hospital can apply.
B Medical professionals can apply, but it is not necessary to be one.
C Only medical professionals can apply.
2. "Applicants must not be enrolled in a degree program." What does this mean?
A Being a student during the residency is forbidden.
B Being a student during the residency is possible, but it is not necessary.
C Being a student is recommended.
3. Which document can you leave out without being disqualified?
A The budget and the production plan.
B The portfolio.
C The letter of support.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Requirement Language

GRAMMAR

How heavy is each rule: required, forbidden, recommended or free.

RuleFormExample
Requiredmust / need to / be required toEvery submission must include a budget.
Forbiddenmust not / may not / be not allowed toYou must not be enrolled in a degree program.
Not necessarydo not have to / do not need toYou do not have to be a doctor.
Recommendedshould / be strongly recommendedYou should send a letter of support.
Expectedbe expected toResidents are expected to present their work.
In the pasthad to (must has no past)Last year we had to send a video.

Never: must to submit · mustn’t (when you mean not necessary) · is require to · we must send last year.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the sentence you would actually say.

A friend asks if she needs a degree in the arts. The call says no. You say:
A "You do not have to have a degree in the arts."
B "You must not have a degree in the arts if you want to apply."
C "You must to have a degree in the arts."
The call forbids applicants who are students. You say:
A "Students do not have to apply, but they can if they want to."
B "Applicants must not be enrolled in a degree program."
C "Students should apply."
A letter of support is not mandatory but helps. You say:
A "A letter of support is required."
B "You must not send a letter of support with your application."
C "It is not required, but you should send one."
Last year a video was mandatory. You say:
A "Last year we must send a video."
