Chapter 4: What Is Required

Required, Recommended, Optional

The grammar of the small print

Grammar Discovery

What Happens If You Ignore It?

"You must be able to show a track record of at least five years."

"Applicants must not be enrolled in a degree program during the residency."

"You do not have to be a medical professional."

"They should explain how consent will be obtained."

"Any project that records patients is required to comply with the ethics policy."

Every red phrase tells you how heavy the rule is: what gets you disqualified, what makes you weaker, and what you can ignore.

RuleFormExample
Requiredmust / need to / be required to + verbEvery submission must include a budget.
Forbiddenmust not / may not / be not allowed to + verbApplicants must not be enrolled in a degree program.
Not necessarydo not have to / do not need to / be not required toYou do not have to be a medical professional.
Recommendedshould / be (strongly) recommended / be encouraged toYou should explain how consent will be obtained.
Expected of yoube expected to + verbResidents are expected to present their work.
The trapmust not ≠ do not have tomust not = forbidden · do not have to = you are free to choose
In the pasthad to (must has no past form)Last year we had to send a video.

In one line: must and must not are rules; should is advice; do not have to is freedom.

Listening 1 · The Coordinator

Read the Eligibility Section Twice

Somebody who coordinates open calls, on why one in ten applications is never read. Sound first — no text.

1. How many applications does she read, and how many are disqualified before anybody looks at the art?
Around four hundred every year. About one in ten is disqualified because the applicant is not eligible or a mandatory document is missing.
2. What is the difference between must and should in a call, according to her?
Must is not a suggestion. Should means nobody will reject you without it, but the strongest applications usually have it.
3. What three things does the program look for?
A track record, a clear proposal, and evidence that you have thought about the people you will work with.
Listening 2 · The Clinician

Why So Many Rules About Consent?

The clinician on a hospital panel, on why the call is strict about recording patients. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the patient required to do, and what is the artist expected to do?
The patient is required to sign a form. The artist is expected to explain, in plain language, what will happen to the recording.
2. Why does he say a patient is not in the same position as a visitor in a gallery?
Because a patient cannot always leave, and may feel they have to say yes.
3. What does he need to see at the shortlist stage?
Not a perfect ethics plan, but evidence that the artist has asked the right questions. An application that never mentions consent shows the artist has not imagined the ward yet.

Five Sentences for an Open Call

Every submission must include a budget and a portfolio.
