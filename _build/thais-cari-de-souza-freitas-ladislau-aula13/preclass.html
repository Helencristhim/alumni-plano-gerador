Lesson 13 -- Pre-class

Asking the Right Question -- Polite Questions for the VP

Questions and requests that sound senior. Key words: a concern, a clarification, to double-check, to elaborate, to confirm, a priority, a request, feasible, to get back to, a heads-up. Structure: indirect questions (Could you tell me where it is?) and polite requests (I was wondering if, Would you mind + -ing).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A concern -- something that worries you a little and that you want to mention
"I have one concern about the time."
A clarification -- an explanation that makes something clear
"The new rule needs a clarification."
To double-check -- to check something again, to be completely sure
"I would like to double-check the agenda."
To elaborate -- to give more detail about something you said
"Could you elaborate on that point?"
To confirm -- to say that something is true or final
"The time is not confirmed yet."
A priority -- something more important that comes first
"The April cohort is our first priority."
A request -- the act of asking for something politely or formally
"Please send me the request by email."
Feasible -- possible to do, in a realistic way
"Thirty minutes might be feasible."
To get back to -- to answer somebody later, after you find the information
"I will get back to you tomorrow."
A heads-up -- a short warning before something happens
"Could you give me a heads-up if it changes?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

When you ask a senior person a question, English likes a polite frame in front of it. "Where is the report?" becomes "Could you tell me where the report is?" Look at the end of the second question. After the frame, the order changes: first the subject, then the verb. It is "where the report is", not "where is the report".

The same happens with do and does. "When does the call start?" becomes "Do you know when the call starts?" The does disappears, and the -s goes back to the verb. For a yes or no question, use if: "Do you know if the time is confirmed?"

To ask for something, two frames work very well. "I was wondering if we could have thirty minutes." And "Would you mind sending me the agenda?" After would you mind, the verb takes -ing. And be careful with the answer: "Not at all" means yes, I will do it. Polite is not weak, as long as the request is still clear.

B "Could you tell me where the report is?"

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Indirect Questions and Polite Requests

GRAMMAR

After the polite frame: subject first, then the verb. No do or does.

FrameWhat followsExample
Could you tell mewh- + subject + verbCould you tell me where the report is?
Do you knowwh- + subject + verb, no do / doesDo you know when the call starts?
Do you know ifif + subject + verbDo you know if the time is confirmed?
I was wondering ifif + subject + could + verbI was wondering if we could have thirty minutes.
Would you mindverb + -ingWould you mind sending me the agenda?
AnswerNot at all = yes, I will"Would you mind...?" — "Not at all."

Never: could you tell me where is it · do you know when does it start · would you mind to send · I was wondering if could we
