Chapter 3: The Code

Where Is It, or Where It Is?

One question, two shapes

Grammar Discovery

What Happens to the Order?

"Where is the report?" → "Could you tell me where the report is?"

"Is the time confirmed?" → "Do you know if the time is confirmed?"

"Can we have thirty minutes?" → "I was wondering if we could have thirty minutes."

"Send me the request." → "Would you mind sending me the request?"

Look at the blue parts. Where is the verb is in the first question, and where is it in the second? What comes after would you mind?

FrameWhat followsExample
Could you tell me + wh-wh- word + subject + verb (no question order)Could you tell me where the report is?
Do you know + wh-wh- word + subject + verb, no do / doesDo you know when the call starts?
Do you know ifif + subject + verb, for a yes / no questionDo you know if the time is confirmed?
I was wondering ifif + subject + could / would + verbI was wondering if we could have thirty minutes.
Would you mindverb + -ingWould you mind sending me the request?
The answer to would you mindNot at all = yes, I will do it"Would you mind...?" — "Not at all."

In one line: after the polite frame, the question stops being a question — subject first, then the verb, and no do or does.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Thirty Minutes, Politely

A call, a request, and two listenings

Dialogue

Can We Have Thirty?

M
Thais, the VP's quarterly call is on Thursday. I am putting the agenda together. Do you have anything for it?
T
I do. Could you tell me how long the credit part is? I have one concern about the time.
M
Twenty minutes, but it is not confirmed yet. Why?
T
I was wondering if we could have thirty. The new rule needs a clarification, and I do not want to rush it.
M
That might be feasible. Would you mind sending me a short request by email? I need it in writing.
T
Not at all. And do you know who else is presenting? I would like to double-check the order of the priorities.
M
Risk and pricing. I will get back to you tomorrow with the final order.
T
Perfect. Could you give me a heads-up if anything changes? I can elaborate on the rule if the VP has questions.
Comprehension

About Mark

1. What is Mark preparing, and for when?
The agenda of the VP's quarterly call, on Thursday.
2. How long is the credit part, according to Mark, and is it final?
Twenty minutes, but it is not confirmed yet.
3. What does Mark ask Thais to do, and what will he do tomorrow?
He asks her to send a short request by email. Tomorrow he will get back to her with the final order.
Listening 1 · A Voice Message

A Message from the VP's Office

The VP's chief of staff, before Thursday. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she need from Thais, and by when?
The final numbers for the credit part, by Wednesday at noon.
2. What does the VP want to understand on Thursday?
Why approvals fell in May, and whether it is a decision or a problem.
3. What does she ask Thais to do if thirty minutes is not enough?
To let her know today, so she can move the pricing part to the end.
Listening 2 · A Coach

Polite Is Not Weak

A communication coach on asking up. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does a direct question sometimes sound rude in English, according to him?
Because English uses distance to show respect, and a direct question removes the distance.
2. What mistake do people make when they try to be polite?
They become so polite that they never actually ask. The request disappears.
3. What is his formula?
A polite frame, a clear request, and the reason, in that order.
