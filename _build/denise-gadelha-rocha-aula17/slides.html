Chapter 3: The Code

How Much Force Is In That Sentence?

Would rather, had better, may as well — and who you can say each one to

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I would rather hold off until the January renewals than commit capacity now."

"I would rather you didn't announce it before the numbers are signed off."

"We had better confirm the aggregate before we promise anything to Milan."

"Neither of us can move on the date, so we may as well split the difference."

Three of them are followed by the plain verb: hold, confirm, split. One of them is not. What is different about that sentence — and is it really about the past?

FormWhat it doesExample
would rather + plain verbyour own preference. Neutral, safe in every direction. Never to after itI would rather hold off until January.
would rather + person + past verba preference about what somebody else does. The past form is distance and courtesy, not timeI would rather you didn't announce it yet.
would sooner / would just as soonthe same as would rather. Just as soon is the softest decline in English — a preference you will not fight forI would just as soon have it in writing.
had better + plain verba recommendation with a consequence behind it. There is always an unspoken or elseWe had better confirm before Friday.
had better notthe negative goes after better. Never had not better, never had better toWe had better not put this to the committee yet.
may as well / might as wellthe option that is left when nothing better remains. Acceptance, not enthusiasm — and everyone hears thatWe may as well split the difference.
The two trapsNever would rather to hold and never had better to confirm — the verb is plain. And I would rather you don't is the most common B2 slip of all: it has to be didn't.

The Executive Point

Same Request, Four Different Amounts of Force

Softest — a preference you will not fight for

"I would just as soon decide this week."

Clear, and safe in every direction

"I would rather we decided this week."

The argument is over — nothing better is left

"We may as well decide this week."

Strongest — carries an unspoken or else

"We had better decide this week."

Downwards and between peers, this sounds like care. Said upwards, to the person who outranks you, it sounds like a warning — and nobody will ever tell you that it did.

One request, four ranks. This is the part of English that no course teaches and every meeting charges you for.

Chapter 4: The Priorities Call

Andreas, Central Europe

One slot, two regions. Whatever you do not say now, you will both say in front of Milan.

Dialogue

One Slot, Two Regions

A
Denise, before we take this to Milan — we both want the same slot in the treaty, and they will fund one of us this quarter. I would rather we agreed a position between us than let them choose for us.
D
So would I. I have weighed up three ways of splitting it, and I am bringing the options sheet rather than a recommendation.
A
Good. Then tell me your order, and tell me where your wall is. I have no appetite for discovering that in front of them.
D
On balance, option two. You take the treaty slot now, and we hold off on the Brazilian layer until the January renewals.
A
That costs you a quarter of the year. Honestly, it sounds like the lesser evil for me and the expensive one for you, which makes me wonder what you want in return.
D
The reporting date. That is my sticking point, and there is no wiggle room in it: my numbers go to Milan on the fifth, so the Brazilian layer has to be confirmed by the second. On everything else we can split the difference.
A
The second is tight. Moving my broker deadline is a non-starter, but I would rather find those two days somewhere than argue about them in front of the head.
D
Then we had better put both dates on the sheet as one line, with the fallback underneath. If they cannot fund the layer in January, I would just as soon take the twelve-month extension as go back and ask twice.
A
Agreed. Send it tonight and I will sleep on it. If I have not written by nine, you may as well take that as a yes.
Comprehension

About Andreas

1. What does he say he has no appetite for?
Discovering where her wall is in front of Milan.
2. What does he say is a non-starter for him?
Moving his broker deadline.
3. What does he say silence from him would mean?
That it is a yes — if he has not written by nine, she can take that as agreement.
Listening

The Sentence That Sounded Like a Threat

A group executive on the day he used the wrong modal upwards. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What exactly did he write to the head of the division?
That they had better confirm the capacity before Friday.
2. Why does he say it landed as an instruction with a threat behind it?
Because had better always carries an or else, and the or else is only yours to give if you are the one who can act on it — he was four levels below.
3. In which directions does he say the same phrase is perfectly fine?
Between peers and downwards. Only upwards does it land like a warning.
4. What does he say the safe version costs, and what does it leave you?
Three words — "I would rather we confirmed the capacity before Friday". Same request, no threat, and if the answer is no it still leaves you somewhere to stand.
Listening 2

Three Options, One Page

A regional director on why she stopped walking in with a recommendation. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she take into the room instead of a recommendation?
Three options and what each one costs, on one page.
2. Why does she say the sheet produces a shorter argument?
A recommendation invites the other person to argue with her; an options sheet invites them to argue with the options, which is shorter and far less personal.
3. What does she say a sheet with no view on it looks like?
Like you could not make up your mind — so she always writes which one she would go for, and why.
4. What happens, in her experience, when the sticking point is left unsaid?
The other side spends the whole meeting testing where the wall is, and you spend it defending a line you never drew.
Chapter 5: Practice

The Sentences You Will Actually Say

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "I would rather to postpone the Brazilian layer until January."
"I would rather postpone the Brazilian layer until January." (would rather takes the plain verb — never to)
2. "I would rather you don't announce it before the numbers are signed off."
"I would rather you didn't announce it before the numbers are signed off." (a preference about somebody else takes the past form — and it is not past time)
3. "We have a compromise with the broker on Thursday."
"We have a commitment to the broker on Thursday." (a compromise is a halfway agreement; compromisso is a commitment)
4. "According to me, option two is the lesser evil."
"In my view, option two is the lesser evil." (according to always reports somebody else — never yourself)
The Real Thing

The Options Sheet

Options sheet — for Group Underwriting, Milan
Treaty capacity, Q1 — Brazil and Central Europe
D. Gadelha Rocha
A. Brandt
Option 1

Split the slot down the middle.

Buys: both regions start in January. Costs: neither region can write the large accounts, and both come back in Q2.

Option 2

Central Europe takes the slot now; Brazil holds off to January.

Buys: one fully funded position and a signed treaty. Costs: one quarter of Brazilian growth, and the layer has to be confirmed by the second.

Option 3

Both regions go outside the treaty for the layer.

Buys: independence from the Q1 allocation. Costs: roughly nine points of margin, and a precedent Milan will not want repeated.

PreferredOption 2. On balance we would rather fund one position properly than two halfway.

Your lineWrite your own. What is the one thing you cannot move on this quarter?
1. Why does the sheet name the sticking point before anybody has asked for it?
Because an unspoken wall gets tested all meeting. Named in one line, it stops being a negotiation and becomes a constraint everybody plans around.
2. What would change if "Preferred" said "we had better fund one position properly"?
It would stop being a preference and become a warning to the people who allocate the capacity — correct English, wrong rank, and nobody in Milan would say so out loud.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I state a preference with the cost attached, instead of only naming the option I want.
I use would rather with the plain verb, and the past form when the preference is about somebody else.
I know what had better does to a sentence, and I keep it away from the people who outrank me.
I name my sticking point before anybody asks for it, and I say where there is wiggle room.
I can decline something without refusing it, using would just as soon.
