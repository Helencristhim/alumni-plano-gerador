Lesson 17 -- The Language of Executive Preference

Series
Suits -- any negotiation scene in a partner's office

Nobody in this series ever says "I want option two". They say I would rather, we may as well, and occasionally you had better — and the whole balance of the scene turns on which one they choose and who they are saying it to. Watch one negotiation with the sound up and the subtitles off, and listen only for the modal.

Tip: find one line where a character uses had better with somebody senior to them. Notice what happens in the room immediately afterwards, and bring the line to class.

Podcast
WorkLife with Adam Grant -- the episodes on negotiation and disagreeing well

Grant spends whole episodes on the thing today's grammar exists for: how to want something clearly without turning the other person into an opponent. Listen for how often the strongest negotiators state a preference and its price in the same sentence, and how rarely they issue an instruction to anyone.

Tip: write down two sentences in which somebody says what they want and what it costs them, in one breath. That is the shape you will practise in the role-play.

YouTube
Amy Cuddy -- Your Body Language May Shape Who You Are (TED)

The talk is about the room before the sentence: how much authority you appear to have before you have said anything at all. It belongs to this lesson because force is not only lexical — the same "I would rather" carries one weight when you are apologising for taking the floor and another when you are not.

Tip: record yourself saying the survival phrases twice, sitting differently each time. Play both back and decide which one you would fund.
