Chapter 3: The Code

A Two-Centimeter, Well-Defined Plaque

Adjective order and compound adjectives

Grammar Discovery

Where Do the Adjectives Go?

"There is a small, round, erythematous papule on her cheek."

"He has a two-centimeter plaque on his elbow."

"We saw three small, pink papules on his neck."

"It is a well-defined, scaly plaque."

Look at the red words: in what order do they come, and where is the noun? Now the blue ones: why two-centimeter, and not two-centimeters?

JobFormExample
Adjectives go BEFORE the nounadjective(s) + nounan erythematous plaque (never a plaque erythematous)
The clinical ordernumber → size → shape → color → surface → nounthree small, round, pink, scaly papules
Number + measure as an adjectivenumber-noun, singular, with a hyphena two-centimeter plaque · a three-week history
Adverb + participlewell- / ill- / poorly- + participlea well-defined border · an ill-defined edge
Noun + participlenoun-shaped / noun-coloreda dome-shaped papule · a skin-colored nodule
After the nounlocation and details with a prepositiona plaque on the left elbow, with silvery scale

In one line: the adjectives come before the noun, from number and size to color and surface; a measure used as an adjective takes a hyphen and no plural.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Description at Full Speed

One phone call, two recordings, one consult request

Dialogue

The Call at Ten P.M.

P
Hi, Diego. You paged me about the patient in room twelve. Describe the lesion for me.
D
Sure. There is a two-centimeter, well-defined plaque on her left shin.
P
Color and surface?
D
It is erythematous and slightly raised, with a thin scale. The center is crusted.
P
Is it ulcerated?
D
No, it isn’t. But the edge on the lower side is ill-defined.
P
Any other lesions?
D
Yes. There are three small, round papules around it, and no lesions on the other leg.
P
Okay. Mark the border with a pen and take a photo. I’ll see her first thing in the morning.
D
Will do. I’ll send you the photo and the measurements tonight.
Comprehension

About Dr. Parker

1. Why can Dr. Parker not see the patient?
She is at home, on the phone, at ten at night.
2. What two things does she ask about after the size and shape?
The color and the surface, and then whether it is ulcerated.
3. What does she ask Diego to do, and when will she see the patient?
Mark the border with a pen and take a photo. She will see the patient first thing in the morning.
Listening 1 · An Attending

Describe It in One Sentence

An attending on the order she wants in every description. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the order she wants in every description?
How many, how big, what shape, what color, what surface, and then where.
2. What mistake does she hear from many international residents?
They put the adjectives after the noun, like a plaque erythematous.
3. Why does she say "two-centimeter" and not "two-centimeters"?
Because a number and a measure before a noun work as one adjective, with a hyphen and no plural.
Listening 2 · A Resident

The Photo That Did Not Arrive

A resident on a night when the description had to do all the work. Sound first — no text.

1. Why did the attending have to decide without a photo?
The hospital system was down, so the photo did not arrive.
2. How did he describe the lesion?
A one-centimeter, dome-shaped, skin-colored nodule on the scalp, with a small ulcerated center.
3. What did the attending decide, and what did the biopsy show?
To biopsy it in the morning. It was a basal cell carcinoma, and it was removed with clear margins.
