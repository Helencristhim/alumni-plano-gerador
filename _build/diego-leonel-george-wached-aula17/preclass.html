Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A macule -- a small flat spot with a different color from the skin around it
"She has a brown macule on her left cheek."
A papule -- a small raised solid bump, less than one centimeter
"There is a pearly papule on the side of his nose."
A plaque -- a raised flat-topped area, more than one centimeter wide
"Psoriasis often shows thick plaques on the elbows."
A nodule -- a solid lump that goes deeper into the skin than a papule
"The nodule feels firm and is not tender."
A pustule -- a small bump filled with pus
"Acne can show papules and pustules together."
Erythematous -- red, because there is more blood in the skin
"The plaque is erythematous and scaly."
Well-defined -- with a clear, sharp edge
"The lesion is round and well-defined."
Ill-defined -- with an edge that is hard to see
"An ill-defined border is a reason to look closer."
Raised -- higher than the skin around it
"Run your finger over it: is it flat or raised?"
Crusted -- covered with dry blood or fluid on the surface
"The lesion is crusted in the center."
Ulcerated -- with an open sore on the surface
"An ulcerated nodule needs a biopsy."
A distribution -- the pattern of where lesions appear on the body
"The distribution is symmetric, on both forearms."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

When an American dermatologist describes a lesion, the adjectives come before the noun, in a fixed clinical order: number, size, shape, color, surface. Three small, round, pink papules. A well-defined, erythematous plaque. The location comes last, with a preposition: on the left elbow.

Some adjectives are made of two words joined by a hyphen. A number and a measure become one adjective, with no plural: a two-centimeter plaque, a three-week history. An adverb and a participle work the same way: a well-defined border, an ill-defined edge. So do a noun and a participle: a dome-shaped papule.

Portuguese puts the adjectives after the noun, so Brazilian doctors often say a plaque erythematous of two centimeters. In English, the same idea is a two-centimeter, erythematous plaque. After the verb be, the adjectives go after it: the border is ill-defined and slightly raised.

1. Which description is correct?
A A plaque erythematous and scaly of two centimeters on the elbow of the patient
B A two-centimeter, erythematous, scaly plaque on the elbow
C A two-centimeters plaque erythematous
2. Why do we write "a three-week history" without an S?
A Because a number and a measure before a noun work as one adjective, with no plural.
B Because history is always singular.
C Because week is an irregular noun.
3. Which sentence follows the clinical order?
A She has a pink, round, small papule on her cheek, near the nose and the eye.
B She has a papule small, round and pink.
C She has a small, round, pink papule on her cheek.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Adjective Order and Compound Adjectives

GRAMMAR

The adjectives come before the noun, from number and size to color and surface. A measure used as an adjective takes a hyphen and no plural.

JobFormExample
Positionadjectives + nounan erythematous plaque
Ordernumber, size, shape, color, surfacethree small, round, pink, scaly papules
Number + measurenumber-noun (singular)a two-centimeter plaque
well- / ill- + participlecompound adjectivea well-defined border
noun + participlecompound adjectivea dome-shaped, skin-colored nodule
After bebe + adjective(s)The border is ill-defined and raised.

Never: a plaque erythematous · a two-centimeters nodule · a pink, small papule · a border well defined · a nodule of skin color

Stage 2: Put the Description in Order

Order

You are describing a patient to your attending on the phone. Put the sentences in a clear order: the patient, the main lesion, its details, other lesions, and the plan.

?"A sixty-year-old man has a new lesion on his scalp."
?"It is a one-centimeter, dome-shaped, skin-colored nodule."
?"The surface is shiny, with a small ulcerated center."
?"The border is well-defined and slightly raised."
?"There are no other lesions on the scalp or the face."
?"I would like to biopsy it in the morning."
