Lesson 11 -- Pre-class

Opening and Closing a Meeting -- Open, Move, Close

Thirty minutes, three items, and a buyer with a hard stop. Key words: an agenda, to chair, an item, to move on, to sum up, an action item, an owner, to wrap up. Structure: signposting -- let's, I'd like to, moving on to, to sum up -- the sentences that tell the room where the meeting is.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An agenda -- the list of points a meeting will cover, in order
"The agenda has three items and we have thirty minutes."
To chair -- to lead a meeting: open it, keep the time and close it
"I am chairing the export call on Thursday."
An item -- one point on the agenda
"Item two is the delay at the port."
To move on -- to stop one point and go to the next
"We have five minutes left, so let's move on."
To sum up -- to say the main points again, short, at the end
"To sum up, we agreed on the new price."
An action item -- a task that somebody must do after the meeting
"That is an action item, and it needs a name."
An owner -- the one person responsible for a task
"Every action item has one owner and one date."
To wrap up -- to finish a meeting, or a point, in a clean way
"Let's wrap up. We are two minutes over."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Roberto is going to chair a thirty-minute call with Megan, a buyer in Chicago. He opens it in one minute: "Let's start. There are three items on the agenda." Then the first one: "I'd like to begin with the delay at the port." Notice the verb after let's and after I'd like to: start, begin — no to, no -ing.

The first item ends with an action item: his team sends a document to customs today, and the owner has a name. Then he tells the room where the meeting is going: "Moving on to item two, the price." After moving on to comes a noun — item two, the price.

The price takes too long, and he does not fight it: "Let's move on and come back to it on Thursday." Five minutes before the end, he wraps up: "To sum up, we agreed on two things." Every item had a goal, every task had a name and a date, and the meeting finished early.

1. "Let's start with the delay." What comes after let's?
A The verb with no to and no -ing, as in let's start.
B The verb with to, as in let's to start.
C Only a noun, never a verb, as in let's the delay or let's the price at the port.
2. "Moving on to item two." What comes after moving on to?
A A verb with no to, exactly the same as after let's: moving on to discuss, moving on to decide.
B A noun: item two, the price, the samples.
C Nothing. The sentence ends with to.
3. Why does Roberto say "moving on to item two" out loud?
A Because he forgot what was on the agenda.
B Because it is the polite way to finish the call.
C Because people cannot see the agenda in their heads, so he tells them where the meeting is.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Signposting a Meeting

GRAMMAR

The short sentences that tell the room where the meeting is. Some take a verb, one takes a noun, and one takes a whole sentence.

UseFormExample
Open the meetingLet's + verb (no to)Let's start with item one.
Introduce a pointI'd like to + verbI'd like to begin with the numbers.
Change the itemMoving on to + nounMoving on to item two / the price.
Go backGoing back to + nounGoing back to the price for a second.
CloseTo sum up, + full sentenceTo sum up, we agreed on two things.
The trapno to after let's · no -ing after I'd like toNot let's to start. Not I'd like to starting. Not moving on the item two.

Never: Let's to start · I'd like to beginning · Moving on the item two · To sum up, we agreed three things

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

You are opening the call. You say:
A Let's start. There are three items on the agenda.
B Let's to start, we have three items.
C Okay, so, I think maybe we can start now with the first one of the items.
One item is taking much too long. You say:
A Stop. Next item.
B This is important, but let's move on and come back to it on Thursday.
C We can continue with this item for as long as everybody needs, there is no problem at all with the time.
You want to go to the next point. You say:
A Moving on the item two.
B Moving to on item two.
C Moving on to item two.
You are closing the meeting. The best last sentence is:
A To sum up, we agreed on two things, and each action item has an owner and a date.
B Okay, bye, everybody.
C Thank you everybody, it was a really great meeting, and I think we all know what we have to do now, so see you soon.

Talk
David Grady -- "How to save the world (or at least yourself) from bad meetings" (TED@State Street)

Six minutes, very funny and very American, on the meeting invitation that arrives with no agenda and no goal. Everything he asks for is what tonight's first minute gives the room: why we meet, the items, and what we want at the end.

Tip: before your next meeting, send the agenda first. Then watch the talk again and count how many of his complaints you avoided.
