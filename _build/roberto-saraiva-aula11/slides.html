Chapter 3: The Code

Say Where You Are

The sentences that move a meeting from one item to the next

Discovery

What Comes Right After?

"Let's start with the delay at the port."

"I'd like to begin with last week's numbers."

"Moving on to item two, the price."

"To sum up, we agreed on two things."

Each blue phrase tells the room where the meeting is. Look at the red words: two of them are verbs, one is a noun, and one is a whole sentence.

UseFormExample
Open the meetingLet's + verb (no to)Let's start with item one.
Introduce a pointI'd like to + verbI'd like to begin with the numbers.
Change the itemMoving on to + nounMoving on to item two / the price.
Go backGoing back to + nounGoing back to the price for a second.
CloseTo sum up, + full sentenceTo sum up, we agreed on two things.
The trapno to after let's · no -ing after I'd like toNot let's to start. Not I'd like to starting. Not moving on the item two.

In one line: LET'S and I'D LIKE TO take a verb. MOVING ON TO takes a noun. TO SUM UP takes a sentence.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Thirty Minutes, Three Items

The call, the coach, and the last five minutes

Dialogue

The Call With Megan

M
Hi, Roberto. I have a hard stop at ten, so thirty minutes, okay?
R
Thirty minutes is enough. Let's start. There are three items on the agenda, and I'd like to begin with the delay at the port, because it affects the other two.
M
Fine. The container is still in Savannah. Customs asked for one more document.
R
Then that is an action item, and my team is the owner: we send the document today. Moving on to item two, the price for the third quarter.
M
On price, I need to talk to my director first. I can't decide that today.
R
That is fair. Let's move on and come back to it on Thursday. Item three is the samples for the fair.
M
We received them, and they look great. We need fifty more by the tenth.
R
Good. Let's wrap up. To sum up: we send the document today, you talk to your director, and we ship fifty samples by the tenth.
M
Perfect. And we finished five minutes early. That never happens.
Comprehension

About Megan

1. Why does she want the call to be short?
She has a hard stop at ten, so she only has thirty minutes.
2. What is her answer on the price for the third quarter?
She cannot decide today. She needs to talk to her director first.
3. What does she need for the fair, and by when?
Fifty more samples, by the tenth.
Listening 1 · The Coach

The First Sixty Seconds

Somebody who trains people to chair meetings. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What three things does he say to say in the first sixty seconds?
Why we are here, the agenda (how many items and in what order), and what you want to have at the end.
2. What does he mean by what you want to have at the end?
A decision, a date or a name.
3. Why do you have to say it every time you change item?
Because people cannot see your agenda in their heads, so you have to tell them where you are.
Listening 2 · The Last Five Minutes

Good Meetings That Failed

A manager on the way she closes every meeting. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What problem does she describe with good meetings?
Everybody agreed, but two weeks later nothing had happened.
2. When does she stop the discussion?
Five minutes before the end, even when it is a good discussion.
3. What is the rule she never breaks?
Every action item gets one owner and one date: a name, not a team, and a date, not soon or next week.

The Agenda You Send Before the Call

WEEKLY EXPORT CALL · AGENDA
Chair: Roberto Saraiva · Thursday, 9:30 AM CT · 30 minutes
Why we meetunblock the June order
Item 1 · 10 mindelay at the port of Savannah
Item 2 · 10 minprice for the third quarter
Item 3 · 5 minsamples for the fair
Wrap-up · 5 minaction items, owners, dates
What we need at the end: one decision, three owners, three dates.
1. Open the meeting with this agenda: why, how many items, and what you want at the end.
Let's start. We are here to unblock the June order. There are three items on the agenda, and I'd like to begin with the delay at the port. At the end, I would like one decision and an owner for each action item.
2. Item two has ten minutes, and you are at fifteen. What do you say?
This is important, but we are over time. Let's move on and come back to the price on Thursday.
3. Why does the wrap-up have its own five minutes on the agenda?
Because without it the meeting ends with no owners and no dates, and nothing happens after the call.
