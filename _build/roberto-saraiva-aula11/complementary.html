Lesson 11 -- Opening and Closing a Meeting

Talk
David Grady -- "How to save the world (or at least yourself) from bad meetings" (TED@State Street)

Six minutes, very funny and very American, on the meeting invitation that arrives with no agenda and no goal. Everything he asks for is what tonight's first minute gives the room: why we meet, the items, and what we want at the end.

Tip: before your next meeting, send the agenda first. Then watch the talk again and count how many of his complaints you avoided.

Talk
Jason Fried -- "Why work doesn't happen at work" (TEDxMidwest)

A company founder on why so many meetings stop real work, and what to do instead. Useful when you decide which meetings you should chair at all, and which ones should be an e-mail.

Tip: listen for his advice about the next meeting on your calendar. Would it pass his test?

Podcast
Freakonomics Radio -- "How to Make Meetings Less Terrible"

An hour of American English, with researchers and managers, on what good meetings have in common: a clear goal, fewer people, and a real end. Good for the car, and there is a transcript on the page.

Tip: listen for every time somebody says agenda, action item or owner. Write down one idea you will use when you chair your next call.
