Dialogue

Decoding Penang

F
Hafiz says the team will do their best to meet the November date. That sounds good, right?
M
I'm afraid not. Don't take that at face value. In Penang, 'we'll do our best' is often a polite no.
F
So the subtext is: we'll be late. Should I ask him directly?
M
I don't think so. Not in writing. He hinted at a problem because he didn't want to say it.
F
He also glossed over the quality tests. Am I reading too much into it?
M
I don't think so. Two soft sentences in one email is a pattern.
F
Then I'll call him in our overlap window. Can we live with December as a fallback?
M
I suppose so. But let's not be vague with the board. December is the real date.
Comprehension

About Marcus

1. According to Marcus, what does "we'll do our best" often mean in Penang?
It is often a polite no. She should not take it at face value.
2. Why does Marcus think Hafiz hinted at the problem instead of saying it?
Because Hafiz did not want to say it directly, especially not in writing.
3. How does Marcus answer the question about December, and what does he add?
With "I suppose so", a reluctant yes. He adds that they should not be vague with the board: December is the real date.
Listening 1 · A Message from Monterrey

Yes, But...

Lucia calls about the October volumes. Sound first — no text.

1. What does Lucia say about the extra October volume at first?
That in principle it should be fine and her team is very motivated.
2. What problems does she mention after that?
Two of the four lines have a maintenance window in October, and she would need to check with the union about extra shifts.
3. What does she really mean, and what does she ask for?
That the extra volume is probably not possible in October. She asks if they could talk about November instead, and suggests a call.
Listening 2 · News from the Top Floor

Reading the CFO

Daniel passes on what the CFO said. Sound first — no text.

1. What words did the CFO use about Fabiana's proposal?
He called it an interesting proposal and said it was worth looking at in the next budget cycle.
2. Why does Daniel think the CFO's reply is lukewarm?
Because "interesting" and "the next budget cycle" usually mean not now, and he did not ask any questions.
3. What does Daniel suggest Fabiana do?
Not read too much into one meeting, but ask Priya for fifteen minutes with the CFO before the budget closes, with one number.
Real Document

The Email from the CFO's Office

RE: SUPPLIER RESILIENCE BUDGET · 2027
To: Fabiana Michelly Silva · From: Office of the CFO · Cc: Priya Nair

Dear Fabiana,

Thank you for sharing your proposal. It raises some interesting points, and we appreciate the work behind it.

At this stage, some of the cost assumptions may need revisiting, and the timing is not ideal given current priorities. We would be happy to look at a revised version in due course.

Best regards,
Office of the CFO

1. Find two euphemisms in the email. What do they really mean?
Any two: "some interesting points" (not convinced), "may need revisiting" (the numbers are wrong), "the timing is not ideal" (not now), "in due course" (no date, maybe never).
2. Is the email a yes, a no, or a not yet?
A not yet, close to a no: the budget is not approved now, but a revised version is possible.
3. Will the CFO approve the budget this month? Answer in two or three words.
I don't think so. / I'm afraid not.
