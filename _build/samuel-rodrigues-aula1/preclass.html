Who Is Samuel in English? -- The Professional Voice

Before the lesson: the eight words you use every day in Portuguese, now in English — counterparty, clause, shareholder, counsel, draft, execute, matter, scope — and the one grammar contrast that decides how senior you sound in a first call: what you do against what you are doing. Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Eight words from your own practice. Listen to each one and read the example out loud — slowly.

Counterparty -- the other side in a contract or a negotiation
"The counterparty is represented by American counsel."
Clause -- one numbered part of a contract
"Clause 12 covers late delivery."
Shareholder -- a person or company that owns part of a business
"Every shareholder signed the agreement."
Counsel -- the lawyer who advises a company
"Outside counsel reviewed the draft last week."
To draft -- to write the first version of a document
"I drafted the lease for that client last year."
To execute -- to sign a document so it takes legal effect (not "to carry out")
"Both parties executed the agreement on Friday."
Matter -- a piece of legal work a lawyer is responsible for
"I am handling the Atlas matter this month."
Scope -- how far a clause or a duty goes
"We need clarity on the scope of the indemnity."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the briefing. Notice the verbs in bold: some describe the practice, some describe this week.

Samuel works at a law firm in São Paulo. He handles contract and corporate matters, and he normally drafts the first version of an agreement himself. His firm advises energy clients across Brazil.

This month is different. He is handling a supply agreement with an American counterparty, and the two sides are negotiating the scope of the indemnity. At the moment they are waiting for the regulator. Nobody is discussing price: the commercial terms are closed.

1. Which sentence describes Samuel's practice in general, not this month?
A He is handling a supply agreement.
B He handles contract and corporate matters.
C They are waiting for the regulator.
2. Why is "are negotiating" in the continuous form?
A Because it is a permanent duty of the firm.
B Because it is happening around now, in this matter.
C Because it happened and finished last year.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Present simple — your practice

What is always true: your role, your firm, what you normally do.

  • +  I handle corporate matters. / He handles them.
  • −  I do not handle litigation.
  • ?  What do you handle on your side?

Present continuous — your week

What is true around now, and will change: this matter, this month.

  • +  I am handling the Atlas matter.
  • −  We are not discussing price.
  • ?  What are you working on this week?

Signal words. usually, normally, every year → simple. this month, at the moment, right now → continuous. In a first call, use both, in this order: what you do, then what you are doing.

Stage 2: Put the Call in Order

Order

This is how you open a call. Put the four moves in the right order.

?"Good morning, Diane. Thanks for making the time."
?"Before we start, may I suggest an agenda?"
?"I handle contract and corporate work, and I am handling this matter end to end."
?"Let us take the scope of the indemnity first, and the regulatory risk second."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
A foreign client asks what you do. Which answer sounds most senior?
A "I am a lawyer. I do many things."
B "I handle contract and corporate work. At the moment I am handling a supply agreement."
C "I am handling contracts since fifteen years."
The counterparty starts the call by opening a topic you wanted to leave for later. What do you say?
A "Before we go there, may I suggest we start with the indemnity?"
B "No. We talk about that later."
C Say nothing and follow their order.
You did not understand a term the other side used. What is the professional move?
A Nod and check it after the call.
B "Could you clarify the scope of that clause?"
C "Sorry, my English is not good."
They ask for a decision you are not ready to give. What do you say?
A "Yes, no problem." (and check later)
B "Let me come back to you on that before Friday."
C "I cannot answer. I am not the right person."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
You are opening the first call on a shareholders' agreement with a counterparty you have never met. In about ninety seconds: say what you handle, say what you are working on this month, and propose the agenda. Pause one second before you start.
