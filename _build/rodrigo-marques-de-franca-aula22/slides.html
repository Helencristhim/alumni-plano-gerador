Chapter 4: The Code

Take the Person Out

Nominalization: the action becomes a noun

Grammar Discovery

Who Did What?

"There has been a delay in the delivery of the documents."

"We will send the signed copy upon receipt of payment."

"Following our discussion this morning, please find the revised draft attached."

"We would welcome your confirmation of the new date."

Who did what in each sentence? Is there a person in the red words? What kind of word are they?

JobFormExample
Verb becomes noundeliver → delivery · receive → receipt · fail → failurethe delivery of the documents
Take the blame outThere has been + a / an + nounThere has been a delay in payment. (not: You paid late.)
Formal linkersfollowing / upon / prior to / pending + nounFollowing our call, ... Pending your approval, ...
A firm requestWe would be grateful for / We would welcome + nounWe would be grateful for payment by Friday.
Who or whatnoun + of + objectconfirmation of the new date
A soft admissionan oversight / an error + on our partThe missing page was an oversight on our part.
The trapsa noun after the linker, never a verbNOT following we discussed · NOT upon receive · NOT there has been a delayed

In one line: turn the action into a noun, and the person leaves the sentence — the deadline stays.

Real Document

An Email from London

To: Rodrigo Marques de França · From: Harriet Lowe, Lowe & Pryce LLP, London

Re: Share Purchase Agreement · Revised Draft

Dear Rodrigo, following our call on Monday, many thanks for the revised draft. It is an interesting approach, and we can see the thinking behind it. There are just a few small points we would ask you to bear in mind. Regrettably, our client is not in a position to accept the new liability cap in Clause 11. We also noticed that Schedule 3 was not attached; we assume this was an oversight. Finally, just a gentle reminder that we are still waiting for the signed board minutes, which we requested on October 2. We would be grateful for your confirmation of the closing date at your earliest convenience.

Kind regards, Harriet Lowe · Partner

1. Does Harriet like the revised draft?
"Not really. An interesting approach and a few small points mean that she wants changes, maybe a new draft."
2. Which sentence is a firm no?
"Regrettably, our client is not in a position to accept the new liability cap in Clause 11."
3. Is she annoyed about anything? How do you know?
"Yes, the board minutes. A gentle reminder and the date of her first request show that she has been waiting."
4. Find three nouns that hide a verb. What is the verb behind each one?
"Our call (we called), your confirmation (to confirm), an oversight (to overlook)."

Listening 1 · The American in London

Very Interesting

A project manager from Boston, on her first month in a London office. Sound first — no text.

1. How did she understand the director's reply at first?
She was delighted. She thought he loved her plan.
2. What had the director actually done?
He had asked her boss to replace the whole plan.
3. What two changes did she make?
She started every email with one sentence that had nothing to do with the task, and she asked a follow-up question whenever someone said something was quite good.
Listening 2 · The Litigator

The Noun Does the Work

A litigation lawyer, on how he writes to the other side. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does he avoid writing "Your client failed to pay"?
Because the other lawyer has to defend his client, and the next letter is angrier.
2. What changes when he writes "There has been a failure to pay"?
The facts are the same, but there is nobody to defend.
3. Where is the firmness in his letters?
In the deadline and in what happens after it, not in the adjectives.
