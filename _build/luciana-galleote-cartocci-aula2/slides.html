Chapter 4: The Code

Asking Politely

Four requests, one polite structure

Grammar Discovery

Four Requests, One Structure

"Could you repeat the last number, please?"

"Would you mind slowing down a little?"

"Could you tell me what this word means?"

"Do you know when the delivery starts?"

Look at the red words: they make the request polite. After could you, the verb is simple. After would you mind, it takes -ing. And inside could you tell me... and do you know..., the second part is not a question anymore: the verb in blue comes after its subject.

JobFormExample
Ask to repeatCould you + verb...?Could you repeat that, please?
Ask to slow downWould you mind + verb-ing?Would you mind speaking more slowly?
Ask for a meaningCould you tell me what + subject + verb?Could you tell me what it means?
Ask for informationDo you know when / where + subject + verb?Do you know when the meeting starts?
Check a numberDid you say + A or B?Did you say fifteen or fifty?
The trapno question order insideNot what does it mean? Say could you tell me what it means?

In one line: could you + verb; would you mind + -ing; inside a polite question, subject before verb.

Two lines in color are the gaps: a number you did not catch and a word you did not know. That is normal. The professional move is to ask about them before the next meeting, not to hope they are not important.
1. Which question do you ask about the budget?
Just to double-check: did you say fifteen or fifty thousand?
2. Which question do you ask about the word retrofit?
Could you tell me what retrofit means? (It means to update an old building or system with new technology.)
3. Sum up the notes in one sentence.
To sum up, we are meeting on Thursday at ten, and I will send the company profile before that.
Listening 1 · The Meeting Coach

Three Steps When You Get Lost

A meeting coach in London explains what to do when you miss part of a conversation. Sound first — no text.

1. How long does he say the pause usually takes?
About five seconds.
2. What is his first step?
Say that you did not catch it, and ask the person to rephrase.
3. What should you do at the end of the call?
Sum up what you understood and check the numbers.
Listening 2 · The Engineer from Shenzhen

A Product in Sixty Seconds

An engineer from a camera company in Shenzhen presents a new product at a fair. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the camera do at night?
It records in color, even with very little light.
2. How many hospitals use the camera now?
More than three hundred hospitals, in China and in Asia.
3. When can the company deliver to Brazil?
In about six weeks.
Listening 3 · Anna, with a Bad Line

The Call from the Airport

Anna calls Luciana from an airport, with a lot of background noise. Sound first — no text.

1. Where is Anna, and why is it difficult to hear her?
At an airport; there is a lot of background noise.
2. What does Anna want to change?
The date of the meeting, from Tuesday to Thursday.
3. What does Anna ask Luciana to send?
The company profile, before Thursday.
