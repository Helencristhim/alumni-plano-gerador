Lesson 02 -- Pre-class

Say That Again -- Following a Meeting in English

Nobody understands everything in a meeting. Key words: to catch, to follow, to clarify, to rephrase, to slow down, to sum up, to double-check, a key point, background noise, the gist. Structure: polite requests and indirect questions.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To catch -- to hear and understand something that someone says
"Sorry, I didn’t catch the last number."
To follow -- to understand a conversation or an explanation while it continues
"It is hard to follow a call when four people speak at the same time."
To clarify -- to make something clear or easier to understand
"Could you clarify the second point, please?"
To rephrase -- to say the same idea again with different words
"Let me rephrase that: we need the proposal by Friday."
To slow down -- to speak or do something at a lower speed
"Would you mind slowing down a little?"
To sum up -- to say the most important ideas again in a few words, at the end
"To sum up, we agree on the price and the date."
To double-check -- to check something again to be sure it is correct
"Let me double-check the date: is it the fifteenth?"
A key point -- one of the most important ideas in a conversation or a text
"The key point is the delivery time."
Background noise -- sounds around you that make it difficult to hear
"There is a lot of background noise on your line."
The gist -- the general meaning of something, without the details
"I did not understand every word, but I got the gist."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Luciana is on a call with a partner from Germany. The partner speaks fast, and there is some background noise. After a long sentence, Luciana says: “Sorry, I didn’t catch that. Would you mind repeating it?”

Later, the partner uses a word Luciana does not know. She asks: “Could you tell me what that word means?” The partner explains it in a simple way, and the meeting continues.

At the end, Luciana does not remember every word, but she has the gist and the key points. She says: “To sum up, we are meeting on Thursday. Just to double-check: did you say fifteen units?”

1. Why does Luciana say “would you mind repeating it” and not “would you mind to repeat it”?
A Because after would you mind, the verb takes -ing.
B Because the partner is German.
C Because it is a question about the past.
2. Which question is correct?
A “Could you tell me what does that word mean?”
B “Could you tell me what that word means?”
C “Could you tell me what means that word?”
3. What does Luciana do at the end of the call?
A She asks the partner to repeat the whole meeting.
B She sums up the key points and double-checks a number.
C She stays quiet because she did not understand every word.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Polite Requests and Indirect Questions

GRAMMAR

Short, polite questions keep you in the meeting. Watch the verb after would you mind, and the word order inside could you tell me.

JobFormExample
Ask to repeatCould you + verb...?Could you repeat that, please?
Ask to slow downWould you mind + verb-ing?Would you mind speaking more slowly?
Ask for a meaningCould you tell me what + subject + verb?Could you tell me what it means?
Ask for informationDo you know when / where + subject + verb?Do you know when the meeting starts?
Check a numberDid you say + A or B?Did you say fifteen or fifty?
The trapno question order insideNot what does it mean? Say could you tell me what it means?

Never: Could you tell me what does it mean · Would you mind to repeat · Could you repeat again · I didn’t understood

Stage 2: Put the Clarification in Order

Order

A partner said something you did not understand. Put your conversation in a logical order.

?"Sorry, I didn’t catch that."
?"Would you mind saying it again, a little more slowly?"
?"Thank you. Could you tell me what retrofit means?"
?"I see. So what you are saying is that we update the old system."
?"Just to double-check: did you say fifty thousand?"
?"Perfect. To sum up, we update the system for fifty thousand."
