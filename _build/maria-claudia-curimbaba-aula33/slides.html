The Vocabulary of Governance

Vocabulary

Words 1-5

0 / 5 words revealed

How a company is controlled and who decides what
Governance
The system of rules and people that controls how a company is run and how decisions are made
"Good governance protects a family business from family arguments."
A written rule that everybody in the company follows
Policy
An official written rule or plan that tells people what to do in a situation
"Our gift policy says no gift above one hundred dollars."
The document that says how employees must behave
Code of conduct
A set of rules about how the people in an organization must behave
"Every new employee signs the code of conduct."
Not optional; you have no choice
Mandatory
Required by a rule or a law, so you have no choice
"The compliance training is mandatory for all managers."
Telling people a fact they have a right to know
Disclosure
The act of making information known, especially information that was private
"The disclosure must be made before the board meets."

When your personal interest could affect your decision at work

Checking carefully before you sign or buy
Due diligence
The careful checks you do before a deal, to find any risks
"We did our due diligence on the gallery before we signed."
When a rule or a contract is broken
Breach
An action that breaks a rule, a law or an agreement
"A late disclosure is a breach of our policy."

Oversight
The responsibility of checking that work is done correctly and that rules are followed

A person who tells the authorities or the company that someone is doing something wrong

Chapter 3 -- The Code

Must, Must Not, Do Not Have To

three words that look the same and mean very different things

Discovery

Required, Forbidden or Optional?

Read each sentence. Is the action required, forbidden, or optional?

"Every sponsorship must be approved by the board."

"We are required to disclose any conflict of interest."

"Managers must not accept gifts above one hundred dollars."

"You do not have to attend the board meeting."

JobFormExample
required (rule, law)must + verb · be required to · be obligated to"We are required to disclose it."
required (passive, in policies)must be + participle"It must be approved by the board."
forbiddenmust not · be not allowed to · be prohibited from + -ing"Managers must not accept gifts."
optionaldo not have to · do not need to"You do not have to attend."
in the pasthad to · was required to"We had to report it last year."

Key idea: must not means it is forbidden; do not have to means it is optional. In a compliance text, mixing them changes the rule. Also: "we are required to", never "we required to"; and "must" has no past -- use "had to".

The Structure

Three Jobs in a Rule

Three ways the same small words protect the people who follow the rule.

1 · Say what is required

"Every sponsorship must be approved by the board."

Nobody can say later that they did not know.

2 · Say what is forbidden

"Managers must not accept gifts above one hundred dollars."

The line is clear before anybody is tempted to cross it.

3 · Say what is optional

"You do not have to attend, but you must read the minutes."

People know where their choice begins -- and where it ends.

Notice what is dangerous: "you must not attend" and "you do not have to attend" look similar in Portuguese. In English, one is a prohibition and the other is a choice.

Artefact

The Sponsorship Policy

JOINT VENTURE -- SPONSORSHIP AND GIFTS POLICY

Section 4 · approved by the board · applies to all managers

4.1 Approval

Every sponsorship above USD 5,000 must be approved by the board.

4.2 Disclosure

Managers are required to disclose any conflict of interest before the vote.

4.3 Gifts

Managers must not accept gifts above USD 100 from suppliers.

4.4 Due diligence

The partner of any event must be checked before a contract is signed.

4.5 Optional

Board members do not have to attend sponsored events.

A breach of this policy must be reported to the audit committee, which has oversight of all payments.

Dialogue

The Rules of the Sponsorship

T

"Good news: the partners liked the idea of one evening at the gallery. Before we sign, though, there are a few rules we have to follow."

M

"Of course. I read the policy last night. The sponsorship must be approved by the board, because it is above five thousand dollars."

T

"Exactly. And there is something I am required to tell you. The caterer for the evening is owned by my brother-in-law."

M

"Thank you for telling me. That is a conflict of interest, so the disclosure must be made before the vote, not after."

T

"I agree. I will put it in writing today. Do I have to leave the meeting when the board votes?"

M

"You do not have to leave the room, but you must not vote on the caterer. Somebody else should choose it."

T

"Fair enough. And the gallery? Our auditors will ask about them."

M

"We did our due diligence last month. I can send you the report, so the audit committee has everything it needs."
