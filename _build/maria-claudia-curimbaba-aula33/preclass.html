Lesson 33 -- Pre-class

Must, Required, Obligated -- Compliance and Governance

The language of rules: what is required ("must", "be required to"), what is forbidden ("must not") and what is optional ("do not have to"). New vocabulary: governance, policy, code of conduct, mandatory, disclosure, conflict of interest, due diligence, breach, oversight, whistleblower.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Governance -- the system of rules and people that controls how a company is run and how decisions are made
"Good governance protects a family business from family arguments."
Policy -- an official written rule or plan that tells people what to do in a situation
"Our gift policy says no gift above one hundred dollars."
Code of conduct -- a set of rules about how the people in an organization must behave
"Every new employee signs the code of conduct."
Mandatory -- required by a rule or a law, so you have no choice
"The compliance training is mandatory for all managers."
Disclosure -- the act of making information known, especially information that was private
"The disclosure must be made before the board meets."
Conflict of interest -- a situation in which your private interests could influence a decision you make for your company
"The caterer is owned by his brother-in-law, so there is a conflict of interest."
Due diligence -- the careful checks you do before a deal, to find any risks
"We did our due diligence on the gallery before we signed."
Breach -- an action that breaks a rule, a law or an agreement
"A late disclosure is a breach of our policy."
Oversight -- the responsibility of checking that work is done correctly and that rules are followed
"The audit committee has oversight of all payments."
Whistleblower -- a person who tells the authorities or the company that someone is doing something wrong
"A whistleblower must be protected, not punished."

GRAMMAR

Before the gallery evening can go ahead, Maria Claudia reads the joint venture's policy. It is clear: every sponsorship above five thousand dollars must be approved by the board, and managers are required to make a disclosure of any conflict of interest before the vote. The caterer is owned by Tom's brother-in-law, so Tom must not vote on the caterer -- but he does not have to leave the room. The gallery is fine: they did their due diligence last month. Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Must, Must Not, Do Not Have To

GRAMMAR

In a rule, one small word decides if an action is required, forbidden or optional. Mixing them changes the rule.

JobPhrasesExample
requiredmust, be required to, be obligated toWe are required to disclose it.
required (policies)must be + participleIt must be approved by the board.
forbiddenmust not, be not allowed toManagers must not accept gifts.
optionaldo not have to, do not need toYou do not have to attend.
pasthad to, was required toWe had to report it last year.

Key point: "must not" = forbidden; "do not have to" = optional. "We are required to", never "we required to". "Must" has no past: use "had to".

Quiz

A You must not accept it.
