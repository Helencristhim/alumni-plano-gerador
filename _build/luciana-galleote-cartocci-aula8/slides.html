Chapter 4: The Code

Rules, Needs and Choices

Four small words that change everything

Grammar Discovery

Five Sentences From a Tender

"Every bidder must submit the proposal before the deadline."

"We have to show two certificates from our partner."

"The solution needs to comply with the specification."

"We don't have to visit the site. It is optional."

"The winner mustn't change the engineers without approval."

The blue verbs say this is necessary. The red verbs look similar, but they are very different: one says you can choose, the other says it is not allowed. Which is which?

JobFormExample
A written rule, a strong obligationmust + verbEvery bidder must submit by May 30.
An obligation from outside (client, law, partner)have to / has to + verbShe has to sign every page.
Something necessary for the resultneed to / needs to + verbThe solution needs to comply with the specification.
Not necessary: you can choosedon’t / doesn’t have toWe don’t have to visit the site.
Not allowedmustn’t + verbYou mustn’t send it late.
Past and future (must has no past)had to / will have toLast year we had to show three projects.
The questionDo we have to / Do we need to...?Do we have to send the original?

In one line: must, have to and need to = necessary; don’t have to = optional; mustn’t = not allowed.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

One Notice, Three Voices

A tender notice, three recordings

Real Document

The Notice on Your Desk

TENDER NOTICE · SUMMARY
São Lucas Hospital (example) · prepared for Luciana Galleote Cartocci
ObjectVideo surveillance and access control, two buildings
DeadlineProposals by May 30, 5 p.m. Late proposals are not accepted
EligibilityGold partner certificate + three similar hospital projects
Specification320 IP cameras, 99.9% uptime
WarrantyFive years, on site
Service levelResponse in four hours, 24/7
Penalty2% of the monthly fee for each late response
Site visitOptional
An example summary, not a real hospital: a tender team usually puts the notice on one page like this. Every line is a rule, an obligation or an option, and that is why the language of tenders is must, have to and don’t have to.
1. Turn the Deadline line into a sentence with must.
Every bidder must submit the proposal by May 30, at 5 p.m.
2. Is the site visit necessary? Say it in one sentence.
No. We don't have to visit the site: it is optional.
3. What happens if the winner responds late?
It has to pay a penalty: 2% of the monthly fee for each late response.
Listening 1 · The Tender Consultant

Read the Requirements First

A consultant in London who helps companies with public tenders gives his first piece of advice. Sound first — no text.

1. Why are most proposals rejected, according to him?
For simple reasons: a company forgot a certificate, or it sent the documents after the deadline.
2. Do you have to be the biggest company to win?
No. You need to be the most organized one.
3. What is his tip?
Make a checklist of every requirement, and ask a colleague to check it again before you submit.
Listening 2 · Priya

Is Our Joint Bid Eligible?

Priya, a bid manager at a global technology partner, leaves a message about the hospital tender. Sound first — no text.

1. What will Priya send this week?
A letter from the manufacturer, so Luciana does not have to ask for it.
2. What is the problem with two of the certificates?
They expired last month, so the engineers need to take the exam again.
3. How long must the warranty be?
Five years, not three.
Listening 3 · The Hospital IT Director

Three Kinds of Requirements

The IT director of a hospital in Chicago explains how his team writes a tender. Sound first — no text.

1. Why must every supplier offer support twenty-four hours a day?
Because a hospital never closes.
2. Do suppliers have to visit the hospital before the tender?
No, but the suppliers who visit usually write better proposals.
3. What mustn’t the winner do without approval?
Change the engineers on the project.
