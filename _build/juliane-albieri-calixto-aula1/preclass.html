What I Actually Do -- Explaining Your Job

A speaker from abroad asks what you do. Key words: a product designer, a feature, a user, a flow, a stakeholder, to lead, to be in charge of, to align, a deadline, to roll out. Structure: present simple vs present continuous.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A product designer -- a person who designs how a digital product works and how it looks
"I am a product designer: I design the logic behind the screens."
A feature -- one function of an app or a product
"The new feature shows the credit limit of each card."
A user -- a person who uses a product or a service
"Every decision starts with the user."
A flow -- the steps a user follows to finish a task in an app
"We are testing a shorter flow to ask for a new card."
A stakeholder -- a person who has an interest in a project and has a say in it
"I meet the stakeholders every Monday."
To lead -- to be the person who guides a team and makes it work
"I lead a team of six designers."
To be in charge of -- to be the person responsible for something
"I am in charge of the credit card area of the app."
To align -- to agree with other people on the same goal or plan
"We need to align with the business team before the launch."
A deadline -- the date or time when work must be finished
"The deadline for the new flow is next Friday."
To roll out -- to make a new product or feature available to people, often step by step
"We are rolling out the feature to ten percent of the users first."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Oliver is a product designer at a small bank in London. He works in the mobile app team. On Mondays he usually meets the stakeholders, and on Thursdays the team tests the screens with real users.

But this month is different. At the moment, the team is designing a new flow to open an account in five minutes. The deadline is very close, so these days Oliver is working on Fridays too.

His colleague Priya is a product manager. She decides what the team builds first. Right now she is rolling out a new payment feature to a small group of users.

1. Why is Oliver working on Fridays these days?
A Because he always works five days a week.
B Because the deadline of the new flow is very close.
C Because Priya asked him.
2. Which sentence describes a routine?
A “On Thursdays the team tests the screens with real users.”
B “At the moment, the team is designing a new flow.”
C “Right now she is rolling out a new feature.”
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “Priya decide what the team builds first.”
B “Priya decides what the team builds first.”
C “Priya is deciding always what the team builds.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple vs Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

Use the simple form for your job, your routine and facts. Use am / is / are + -ing for projects that are happening now and will end.

JobFormExample
Routine, facts, your jobpresent simpleI lead a team. She works with data.
A project of now, temporaryam / is / are + verb-ingWe are testing a new flow.
Words for routineevery day, usually, always, on MondaysI usually meet the team on Mondays.
Words for nownow, at the moment, this month, these daysThese days I am preparing a presentation.
Verbs of stateknow, need, want, like: simple onlyI need more time. Not I am needing.
The traphe / she / it + -s in the simpleNot she work. Say she works.

Never: I am leading a team every day · She work in product · At the moment we work on it · I am needing more time
