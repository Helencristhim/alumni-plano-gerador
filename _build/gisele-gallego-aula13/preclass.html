Lesson 13 -- Pre-class

On Hold -- The Phone Call, the Service and the Complaint

One phone call, from the music to the case number. Key words: to be on hold, a rep, an automated menu, to get through, a case number, to escalate, a supervisor, a refund, a billing error, to hang up, to get the runaround, to be fed up. Structure: the present perfect continuous, for how long something has been going on.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To be on hold -- to wait on the phone until somebody can talk to you
"I have been on hold for forty minutes, and the music is the same song."
A rep -- the person who answers the phone for a company
"The first rep was nice, but she could not see my account."
An automated menu -- a recorded voice that gives you options to press
"The automated menu has seven options, and none of them is my problem."
To get through -- to finally reach the person you are calling
"I have been trying to get through to the bank all morning."
A case number -- the number a company gives your problem
"Could you give me a case number, so I don’t have to explain everything again?"
To escalate -- to send a problem to a person with more power
"The rep could not fix it, so she escalated it to her manager."
A supervisor -- the person in charge of the reps
"If you can’t approve it, could I speak to a supervisor, please?"
A refund -- money that a company gives back to you
"They finally agreed to a full refund of eighty-nine dollars."
A billing error -- a mistake on a bill, like a charge that appears twice
"There’s a billing error: you charged me twice for October."
To hang up -- to end a phone call
"Please don’t hang up. I am going to transfer you."
To get the runaround -- to be sent from person to person, and nobody solves your problem
"I called three times and got the runaround every time."
To be fed up -- to be tired and annoyed because something has gone on too long
"Honestly, I’m fed up. This is my fourth call this week."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

It is Tuesday morning, and Gisele has been on hold with her internet company for forty minutes. She has been trying to get through to a person since last Monday. The automated menu has seven options, and none of them says “you charged me twice.”

Finally a rep answers. “I’m sorry about the wait. We have been getting a lot of calls this week.” Gisele explains the billing error: two charges of eighty-nine dollars for October. “I have called three times,” she says, “and every time I got the runaround. Honestly, I’m fed up.”

The rep cannot approve a refund, so he escalates the problem to his supervisor. Before she hangs up, Gisele asks for a case number. She never says “I am waiting since Monday” — because the waiting started before and is still going on.

1. “Gisele has been on hold for forty minutes.” Is she still waiting?
A No. She waited forty minutes yesterday, and now that call is over and finished.
B Yes. It started before and is still going on.
C We cannot know.
2. Why does Gisele say “I have called three times” and not “I have been calling three times”?
A Because she is counting finished calls: how many, not how long.
B Because “have been calling” is only used in questions with how long and never in sentences.
C Because the calls were in the past.
3. Why does the rep escalate the problem?
A Because Gisele asks him to transfer her to the technical support team.
B Because he wants to end the call.
C Because he cannot approve the refund himself.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Present Perfect Continuous

GRAMMAR

How long and still going on: have been + -ing. How many: have + past participle.

UseFormExample
Affirmativehave / has + been + verb-ingI have been waiting for an hour.
Negativehave not / has not + been + verb-ingThey haven’t been answering my emails.
QuestionHow long + have / has + subject + been + verb-ing?How long have you been waiting?
for / sincefor + a period · since + a starting pointfor forty minutes · since Monday
How many (finished)have / has + past participleI have called three times.
State verbsknow, want, need, have (own): no -ingI have known about it since Monday.

Never: I am waiting since Monday · I have been calling three times · She has been knowing · How long are you waiting?
