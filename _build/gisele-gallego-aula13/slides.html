Chapter 3: The Code

Still Waiting, or Done Three Times?

How long versus how many

Grammar Discovery

Which One Counts?

"I have been waiting for forty minutes."

"She has been calling them since Monday."

"We have been getting a lot of calls this week."

"I have called three times."

The orange ones started before and are still going on. The blue one counts finished calls — it answers how many, not how long.

UseFormExample
Started before, still going onhave / has + been + verb-ingI have been waiting for an hour.
How long: a periodfor + forty minutes / a week / two daysShe has been calling for a week.
How long: a starting pointsince + Monday / nine o’clock / OctoberI have been trying since Monday.
QuestionHow long + have / has + subject + been + verb-ing?How long have you been waiting?
How many: finished actionshave / has + past participle (no -ing)I have called three times.
State verbs: no -ingknow, have (own), want, need → present perfect simpleI have known about it since Monday.

In one line: how long and still going on = have been + -ing; how many = have + past participle.

Comprehension

About Kevin

1. Why does Kevin say the wait was so long?
Because his company has been getting a lot of calls this week.
2. What does Kevin see when he checks the account?
Two charges of eighty-nine dollars for October.
3. What can Kevin not do, and what is he going to do instead?
He cannot approve a refund himself, so he is going to escalate the problem to his supervisor right away.
Listening 1 · The Tricks

What Nobody Tells You About Calling

A neighbor explains how to survive a customer service call here. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What can you do to get through to a real person faster?
Say representative two or three times to the automated menu, and the machine usually gives up.
2. Why should you ask for a case number right away?
Because if the call drops, you do not have to start from zero.
3. What is her record on hold, and what should you say if the rep cannot solve the problem?
Fifty-five minutes. Do not hang up: ask politely if they could escalate it, or if you could speak to a supervisor.

Listening 3 · An Opinion

Patience Is a Strategy

Why one woman stopped yelling at customer service. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Why does she say yelling at the rep makes no sense?
Because the rep is not the company: they did not make the billing error or write the automated menu.
2. What happens when you yell, and what happens when you are calm?
When you yell, they follow the script and you get the runaround. When you are calm, they start working for you.
3. What three things does she say on every call?
Her name, what she has been trying to do and for how long, and what she wants: a refund, a technician or a date.
