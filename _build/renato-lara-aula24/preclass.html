Stage 1.1: Team Kit

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes. "Kit" here means team clothes and things.

Kit — the clothes and things a team uses
"The squad gets new kit on Wednesday."
Hoodie — a warm top with a part that covers your head
"My hoodie is black with a yellow logo."
Logo — a small picture that shows the name of a club or company
"The club logo is a wave."
Label — a small piece of paper or cloth with information on it
"Write your name on the label."
Van — a big car for carrying people or things
"The boards go on the roof of the van."
Swap — to give something and get something else back
"Let's swap hoodies back."
Belong to — to be the property of somebody
"This van belongs to the club."
Lost property — a place for things people forgot or lost
"Your towel is in lost property."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read Renato's message to his host family after training. Then answer the questions.

Big news: I'm in the squad! Dana gave us the new kit today. Every surfer has a black hoodie with the club logo, so I wrote my name on the label. But in the van there was a hoodie with no name. Was it mine? Was it Kai's? We checked: it was his. Jess has a pink towel, and Harper says it's hers. The jerseys aren't ours, they belong to the club. Tomorrow Kai and I are going to swap hoodies back. And Bev, is the blue towel in the laundry yours? I think mine is in lost property.
"Was it mine?" Why "mine" and not "my"?
A There is no noun after it, so we use mine.
B Because the sentence is in the past, and the past of "my" is always "mine".
"Harper says it's hers." Whose towel does Harper think it is?
A She thinks it is her own towel, not Jess's.
B Jess's.
Which sentence is correct?
A The jerseys belong to the club.
B The jerseys are of the club, not of the surfers.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Possessive pronouns: the word stands alone

my, your, her need a noun after them. mine, yours, hers replace the noun, so they stand alone.

With a noun → alone

my hoodie → mine
your towel → yours
his board → his
her leash → hers
our van → ours
their kit → theirs

Never

It's my.
the hoodie of Kai
It's mine hoodie.
Who towel is this?

Positive: This hoodie is mine.  ·  Negative: That towel isn't hers.  ·  Question: Whose board is this? Is it yours?
A person's name: add 's — Kai's hoodie, Jess's towel. Not "the hoodie of Kai".
Sound: "whose" sounds like "who's" (HOOZ). The s in yours, hers, ours is a z.

Stage 4: In the van

Quiz
Kai shows you your hoodie. What do you say?
A That's mine! Thanks, Kai.
B That's my! Thanks, Kai, I was looking everywhere for it.
You want to know who owns the blue leash.
A Whose leash is this?
B Who is the owner of this leash here in the van?
Harper says the board is Jess's board. Say it shorter.
A It's hers.
B It's her.
The coach owns the van keys. Say it.
A They're Dana's.
B They're of Dana, the coach of the squad.
You lost your towel at school. Where do you look?
A In lost property.
B In the logo.

Stage 5: The kit mix-up

Reflection
You opened your bag at home, and the hoodie in it isn't yours. Record 30 seconds for Kai: say whose hoodie you have, how you know, and when you can swap. Check one thing before you send it: no noun after mine, yours, his or hers, and never "is my".
