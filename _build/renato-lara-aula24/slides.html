Grammar Discovery

Mine? Yours?

This hoodie isn't mine. It's Kai's.
Is this towel yours, Renato?
Jess says the pink towel is hers.
The jerseys aren't ours. They belong to the club.

What do the orange words have in common?

my hoodie = mine · your towel = yours · her board = hers

These words replace the thing, so no noun after them: "It's mine", never "It's mine hoodie" or "It's my". To ask about the owner, use Whose: "Whose hoodie is this?"

The Code

Before the noun, or alone

With a noun → alone

my hoodie → mine
your towel → yours
his board → his
her leash → hers
our van → ours
their kit → theirs

Never

It's my.
alone: It's mine.
the hoodie of Kai
a person: Kai's hoodie
Who is this towel?
owner: Whose towel is this?

The question: Whose is this? — It's mine. / It's Jess's.

Sound Check

Two small sounds

Whose hoodie is this?

"whose" is HOOZ: no w sound

It's not mine. I think it's yours.

the s in "yours" is a z: YORZ

The message from the coach

Kai, Harper, Jess and Renato. Tom is the reserve.

A hoodie, a yellow jersey and a sticker for the board, on Wednesday.

No. They're the club's and go back to Dana after every comp. First training is Thursday at six.
