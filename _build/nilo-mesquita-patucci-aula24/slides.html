Chapter 3: The Emphasis Code

Say It With Emphasis

what ... is · the reason why ... is · it was ... that

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"What I want to stress is that an allegation is not a fact."

"What we learned was that trust takes time."

"The reason why we renewed is that the sponsor has a great track record."

"It was the compliance team that found the problem."

Look at the orange words. Each sentence could be shorter. Why does the speaker use these longer forms? What comes after them?

FormUseExample
What + subject + verb + is / wasputs the focus on the idea at the endWhat I want to stress is that...
The reason why + clause + is thatputs the focus on the reasonThe reason why we renewed is that...
It is / It was + X + that / whoputs the focus on one person or thingIt was the compliance team that found it.
The point is that / The thing is thata simple way to introduce the key messageThe point is that we need more time.

These are cleft sentences. Speakers use them to make the key message stronger. Use is for the present and was for the past. After "The reason why...", say is that, not "is because".

Chapter 4: The Conference Guide

How to Shine on a Panel

A guide for speakers at the mini conference

Reading

How to Shine on a Panel

Speaking on a Panel: A Short Guide for Participants

At the end of each block, the program organizes a mini conference. It is not a keynote event with a big stage. It is a panel of three participants and one moderator, in front of about forty colleagues. Each panelist has a time slot of three minutes, and then the audience asks questions.

What most speakers forget is the time. Three minutes feels long, but it goes very fast. The best panelists rehearse their talk at least twice, with a timer, and they cut anything that is not essential.

The second piece of advice is to start with one key message. The audience will not remember ten ideas. It is the one clear idea at the beginning that they take home. Say it in the first thirty seconds, and repeat it at the end.

Third, use one real case. A short story from your own work engages the audience much more than general statistics. Tell what happened, report what people said, and say what you would do differently.

Finally, help the moderator. Give the moderator one question that you would like to answer. The reason why this works is simple: the Q&A starts with an easy question, and you feel confident. The best insight often comes in the Q&A, not in the talk.
