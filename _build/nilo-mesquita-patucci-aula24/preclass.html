Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Keynote -- the main talk at a conference, usually by an important speaker
"The keynote opened the conference on Monday morning."
Panel -- a small group of speakers who discuss a topic in front of an audience
"There are three speakers on the panel this afternoon."
Moderator -- the person who leads a discussion and gives the turn to each speaker
"The moderator introduced each speaker and controlled the time."
Panelist -- one of the speakers in a panel discussion
"Each panelist spoke for three minutes."
Time slot -- the period of time that you have in a schedule or a program
"My time slot is from 2:10 to 2:15."
Insight -- a new and deep understanding of a situation or a problem
"Her talk gave me a real insight into sponsor relations."
Takeaway -- the main idea that people remember after a talk or a meeting
"The main takeaway was simple: be transparent."
Key message -- the one most important idea that a speaker wants to communicate
"Say your key message in the first thirty seconds."
Rehearse -- to practice a talk or a performance before you give it
"I rehearse every presentation twice with a timer."
Engage -- to get and keep the attention and interest of people
"A real story engages the audience more than numbers."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Cleft Sentences for Emphasis

GRAMMAR

How to make your key message stronger in a talk.

FormUseExample
What + subject + verb + is / wasputs the focus on the idea at the endWhat I want to stress is that trust takes time.
The reason why + clause + is thatputs the focus on the reasonThe reason why we renewed is that the sponsor is reliable.
It is / It was + X + that / whoputs the focus on one person or thingIt was the audit team that found the problem.
The point is thata simple way to introduce the key messageThe point is that we need more time.
RuleUse is for the present and was for the past: "What I learned was...". After "The reason why...", say "is that", not "is because". Use who or that for a person, and that for a thing.
