Lesson 28 · Defending Your Position

Stand By Your Point

Boston wants to cut the simulator training in half. You do not agree. Today you learn to make a clear claim, accept the other side and defend your position with good reasons.

Warm-up

From Rapport to Debate

First: how can you be straightforward without coming across as blunt? Use two words from last lesson. Then: Boston sends you a proposal you do not agree with. Do you say so in the meeting, or do you stay quiet?

The Target

Three Moves of a Good Argument

1. Make a clear claim in one sentence.

2. Accept what is true in the other view.

3. Stand by your position with evidence and look for common ground.

Diagnostic

Your Position

Paul from Boston says: "Forty hours on the simulator is too long and too expensive. Twenty is enough." You think new nurses need forty. In two or three sentences, accept one point of his and defend your view.

Do not worry about the grammar yet. Just defend your position.

Grammar Discovery

What Do the Colored Words Have in Common?

"Although the training takes time, it reduces errors."

"Even though twenty hours looks cheaper, one mistake costs much more."

"While I understand your concern, safety comes first."

"Despite the smaller budget, the benefits outweigh the costs."

Which part accepts the other side? What comes after the colored word: a full sentence or only a noun?

LinkerWhat comes nextExample
althoughsubject + verbAlthough the course is expensive, we need it.
even thoughsubject + verb (stronger contrast)Even though it looks cheaper, it costs more.
whilesubject + verb (polite, to accept first)While I understand your concern, safety comes first.
despitenoun or -ing (no full sentence)Despite the cost / Despite being busy, ...
The other partYour position. No but in the same sentence: Although it is long, but ... is wrong.

Accept with the linker, then defend. Despite + noun, the others + a full sentence.

Listening 1 · The Other Side

A Message from Boston

Paul explains his position before the meeting. Sound first — no text.

1. What does Paul want to change?
He wants to cut the simulator training for new nurses from forty hours to twenty.
2. What are his two reasons?
The nurses are already very busy, and the budget is smaller this year.
3. What could make Paul change his mind?
A compelling argument with data.
Listening 2 · The Defense

Andreia Answers

In the meeting, Andreia defends her position. Sound first — no text.

1. Which of Paul's points does Andreia accept?
His point about the budget. She says it is valid.
2. What evidence does she give from last year?
Nurses with forty hours of practice were faster and more confident in real surgeries.
3. What is her final proposal?
Forty hours for new nurses and twenty hours for experienced nurses.
