Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

Who Is Braulio? -- The First Conversation

Answering the oldest question at any table -- "so, what do you do?" -- in the year everything changed. Key expressions: retirement, to wind down, a turning point, to settle into, demanding, relentless, fulfilling, to take up, to make up for, at a loose end, current affairs, avid. Structure: past simple for the career that closed, present simple for the new habits, present continuous for what is still changing.

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. These twelve stay here in your material -- they do not disappear after the class.

Retirement -- the time of life after you stop working for good
"Retirement arrived faster than I expected."
To wind down -- to slowly reduce your activity before stopping completely
"I did not stop overnight. I wound down over two years."
A turning point -- the moment when a situation starts to change in an important way
"That year was a turning point, and I knew it at the time."
To settle into -- to become comfortable in a new place, job or routine
"I am still settling into a week with nothing fixed in it."
Demanding -- needing a lot of your time, energy and attention
"It was a demanding job, and for many years I liked that."
Relentless -- never stopping and never getting any easier
"Six days a week for thirty years is relentless, whatever you call it."
Fulfilling -- giving you a real sense of satisfaction
"Helping with my grandchildren is more fulfilling than I expected."
To take up -- to start doing a new activity or hobby
"Everybody tells me to take up something. I am still choosing."
To make up for -- to do something good to compensate for what you lost
"I have thirty years of Sundays to make up for."
At a loose end -- having free time and not knowing what to do with it
"On Tuesday afternoons I am completely at a loose end."
Current affairs -- the important political and social events happening now
"Current affairs are the only conversation I never get tired of."
Avid -- extremely keen and enthusiastic about something
"I am an avid reader of newspapers, in two languages."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

For more than thirty years the week never changed. He left the house before seven and he came back at ten at night, and Saturday was the only day that belonged to him. It was a demanding job and, for most of those years, he liked that about it. Looking back, the word for it is relentless.

Then, this year, he retired. It did not happen overnight — he had two years to wind down — but the year itself was a turning point, and he knew it while it was happening.

Now the week is his. He reads three newspapers every morning, before anybody else is awake, because he is an avid follower of current affairs and always was. Twice a week he goes to the club, and on Wednesday evenings he has a cooking class. He helps with his grandchildren, which is the most fulfilling part of the week and also the most tiring.

And some of it is still unfinished. He is still settling into a week with nothing fixed in it. On a quiet Tuesday afternoon he is completely at a loose end, and he is looking for something new to take up. He says he has thirty years of Sundays to make up for, and he is in no hurry at all.

1. Why does the text say "he left the house before seven" and not "he leaves"?
A Because that period is finished and closed — it belongs to the past.
B Because it happened only once.
C Because the sentence is about a habit that continues today.
2. "He reads three newspapers every morning." What does the present simple tell us here?
A That he is reading them at this exact moment.
B That it is a habit — something he does again and again.
C That he read them once, in the past.
3. Why is "he is still settling into" in the present continuous and not the present simple?
A Because it is a permanent fact about him.
B Because "settle" is an irregular verb.
C Because it is in progress and not finished — it is still changing.
4. The text says the year "was a turning point". What does that mean?
A That it was the moment when the situation started to change in an important way.
B That it was a difficult year.
C That nothing important happened that year.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Three Tenses, One Story

GRAMMAR

The three forms that tell anybody who you are, in under a minute.

FormWhat it saysExample
Past simple
worked · retired · left		Finished, and closed. The time is over: for thirty years, this year, in March, yesterday."I worked there for thirty years."
"I retired this year."
Present simple
read · go · help		What is generally true, and what you do again and again: every morning, twice a week, usually, never."I read three newspapers every morning."
"Twice a week I go to the club."
Present continuous
am settling · am looking		In progress right now, temporary, still changing: at the moment, this year, still. This is the tense of a life that has just turned over."I am still settling into the routine."
"I am looking for something to take up."
Now that + presentThe new situation, given as the reason for the new habit. Two ideas in one short sentence, and it sounds natural in English."Now that I have time, I read the news properly."
The trapA closed period never takes the continuous, and a habit never does either. English keeps the continuous for what is happening or changing now.NOT "I am working there for thirty years."
NOT "Every morning I am reading."

Notice how short each example is. Knowing a longer structure is not a reason to use it: the short sentence you finish is worth more than the elegant one you abandon halfway.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are on a flight, and the person in the next seat has started a conversation. Choose the best thing to say.

She asks: "So, what do you do?" The best answer is:
A "I am working in the same company since thirty-two years."
B "Nothing, now. I retired this year, after thirty years at the same company."
C "I am retired since this year, more or less."
She asks what you do with your mornings. You answer:
A "I read three newspapers before anyone else is awake."
B "Every morning I am reading three newspapers."
C "In the mornings I readed the newspapers."
You want to say that the new routine is not finished yet. You say:
A "I settle into the new routine at the moment."
B "I am still settling into the new routine."
C "I am settle into the new routine still."
She asks whether you miss the work. The most natural and most interesting answer is:
A "Yes and no." (and you stop there)
B "I miss the people. I do not miss arriving home at ten at night."
C "I would have liked to have been able to continue working there for more years."
She says she edits a politics podcast. You want to keep the conversation on that:
A "Then we are going to get on well. Ask me anything about Brazilian politics."
B "Interesting." (and you look out of the window)
C "I am not understanding nothing about podcasts."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer, and nobody is scoring it.

Somebody in the next seat has just asked you what you do. Answer in about ninety seconds: who you are now, what you did before this year, one ordinary week as it looks today, and one thing in the news you have an opinion about. Keep the sentences short -- eight words is enough for most of them. The point is to finish every sentence you start.
