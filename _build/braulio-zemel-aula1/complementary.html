Complementary Activities

Extra material to keep the language moving between classes. Nothing here is an exercise and nothing here is graded -- watch or listen when you feel like it, and mark it as done. All three are free and open: one click, no account, no payment.

Lesson 1 -- Who Is Braulio?

Talk
"What Makes a Good Life? Lessons from the Longest Study on Happiness" -- Robert Waldinger, TED

Twelve minutes from the director of a Harvard study that has followed the same people for over seventy-five years, from their twenties into their nineties. Connection to Lesson 1: the whole talk is about the question you answered today — what a life adds up to after the career ends. The page has the complete English transcript next to the video.

Tip: watch it once with English subtitles on, then a second time with them off. If you catch about half the second time, that is already a win — the ear is what we are training, and the transcript is there when you want to check.

Podcast
"How to Rethink What You Spend Your Time -- And Life -- Doing" -- Life Kit, NPR

Twenty minutes of calm, clearly spoken American English about deciding what to do with time that is suddenly yours. Connection to Lesson 1: this is the vocabulary of today's lesson in the wild — a turning point, taking something up, being at a loose end. The page carries the full transcript beside the audio.

Tip: listen to the first ten minutes without reading, and write down two things they say about choosing how to spend time. Then open the transcript and check. Reading it in English, not through a translator, is the habit this programme is building.

Grammar Video
"Present Simple and Present Continuous" -- BBC Learning English, 6 Minute Grammar

Six minutes on exactly the line you drew in class today: the present simple for what is always true, and the present continuous for what is happening or changing right now. Connection to Lesson 1: this is the difference between "every morning I read the news" and "at the moment I am settling into a new week".

Tip: pause after each example and say one sentence about your own week — one habit, then one thing that is still changing. If both come out in the same tense, say them again.
