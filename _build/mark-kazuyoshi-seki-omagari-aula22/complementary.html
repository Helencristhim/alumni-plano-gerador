Lesson 22 -- If I Had Known, Now I Know

Film
Klaus -- the redemption arc, done properly

A selfish postman is sent to the worst place in the world and spends the whole film being offered the same choice again and again, each time at a higher cost. It is the cleanest example of what today's reading calls a real redemption arc, and the dialogue is simple enough to follow without subtitles.

Tip: stop the film at the halfway point and write one mixed conditional about the main character -- if he had not been so selfish at the start, what would his life look like at that moment?

Podcast
Freakonomics Radio -- How to Make a Bad Decision

Why smart people choose badly under pressure, and why the reason is almost never a lack of information. It is the adult version of what you did in class: separating the decision itself from the person who made it. The page has the audio and the full transcript.

Tip: listen once, then find one bad decision in the episode and write it in both shapes -- what the person would be doing now, and what they would have done if they were slightly different.

Listen on Freakonomics ↗
YouTube
TED -- How to make hard choices (Ruth Chang)

Fifteen minutes arguing that hard choices are hard precisely because no option is better, and that the person you become is decided in exactly those moments. She speaks slowly and repeats her key sentences, which makes her one of the easiest TED speakers to follow at your level.

Tip: pause every time she says "if". Write the sentence down and decide which half is in the past and which half is now -- she uses both shapes without naming them.
