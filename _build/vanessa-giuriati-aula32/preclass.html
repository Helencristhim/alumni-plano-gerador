Lesson 32 -- Pre-class

Telling Personal Stories with a Backstory

Tell stories that keep people listening: start with the big moment and go back in time with the past perfect (had + past participle). Key words: out of the blue, in the nick of time, stranded, it dawned on me, a close call, a blessing in disguise, long story short, meanwhile.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Out of the blue -- Suddenly and unexpectedly, with no warning
"Out of the blue, an old friend called me."
In the nick of time -- At the last possible moment, just before it is too late
"We got to the station in the nick of time."
Stranded -- Stuck somewhere, with no way to leave or travel on
"We were stranded at the airport for ten hours."
It dawned on me -- Used to say you suddenly understood something
"It dawned on me that I had left my passport at home."
A close call -- A situation where something bad almost happened, but did not
"Nobody was hurt, but it was a close call."
A blessing in disguise -- Something that seems bad at first but is good in the end
"Missing that flight was a blessing in disguise."
Long story short -- Used to skip the details and go to the end of a story
"Long story short, we got home at midnight."
Meanwhile -- While something else was happening
"I was waiting at the gate. Meanwhile, my bag was flying to Madrid."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Last summer, my sister and I went to a concert in Rio. We had bought the tickets six months before, and we had booked a hotel near the stadium. On the way, our taxi broke down, and we were stranded on the highway for an hour. When we finally got to the gate, the band had already started. Then it dawned on me that I had left the tickets in the hotel room! Meanwhile, my sister was talking to a security guard. Out of the blue, he said, "Your names are on the list. You had registered online." We got in in the nick of time for our favorite song. It was a close call. Long story short, losing the tickets was a blessing in disguise: the guard took us to a better area, near the stage.

1. When they got to the gate, what had already happened?
A The band had already started.
B The concert had finished and everyone had gone home.
C The stadium had closed the gates.
2. What did the writer realize at the gate?
A That she had bought tickets for a different concert in Rio.
B That she had left the tickets at the hotel.
C That her sister had lost her phone.
3. Why was losing the tickets a blessing in disguise?
A The guard took them to a better area, near the stage.
B They got their money back.
C They left early and avoided the heavy traffic on the highway that night.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past perfect for the backstory

GRAMMAR

When you tell a story in the past, use had + past participle for something that happened BEFORE the main moment.

StructureUseExample
had + past participleThe earlier actionWhen I arrived, the film had started.
had never / had alreadyBefore that momentI had never flown alone before.
hadn't + past participleDid not happen beforeI hadn't checked the time.
past simpleThe main events, in orderI ran, I called, I waited.

Use the participle: 'had gone', never 'had went'. Don't put every verb in the past perfect: only the backstory. In speech, 'I had' often becomes I'd: 'I'd forgotten'.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

You arrived at the meeting, but it started before you got there. Best choice:
A "When I arrived, the meeting had already started."
B "When I arrived, the meeting has already started."
C "When I had arrived, the meeting already started before me and my team."
You want to skip the details of a long story. Best choice:
A "Short story long, we got home."
B "Long story short, we got home."

Something bad almost happened at work, but it didn't. Best choice:
A "It was a close call."

A "I was standed at the station."
