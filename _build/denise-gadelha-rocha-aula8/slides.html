Where It Actually Happens

During

After

Chapter 3: The Code

To Do, or Doing

The verbs of influence, and what follows them

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"We agreed to escalate it before the board met."

"They avoided committing to a date."

"I recommend socialising it with Colombia first."

"He persuaded me to sound them out."

Two of these take to and two take -ing. Is there a rule behind it, or does something else decide?

GroupVerbs you will actually useExample
Verb + toagree · decide · offer · refuse · plan · hope · fail · manageWe agreed to escalate it.
Verb + -ingavoid · recommend · suggest · consider · involve · risk · delay · justifyThey avoided committing to a date.
Verb + person + topersuade · ask · advise · encourage · remind · expectHe persuaded me to sound them out.
After a prepositionAlways -ing, with no exception: committed to reviewing, interested in hearing, instead of escalating.
The trap inside the trapcommitted to is a preposition, not an infinitive: committed to reviewing the limits, never committed to review.
When the meaning changesI stopped to explain = I paused in order to explain · I stopped explaining = I explained, and then I gave up.
I remembered to send it = I did not forget · I remember sending it = I have the memory of having sent it.

The Structure

Four Conversations, in This Order

All four happen before the meeting. None of them has slides.

1 · Sound out the sceptic

"Before I put this on an agenda, where would you land on it?"

2 · Win over the one with the vested interest

"I know what this costs your market. Let me tell you what it buys it."

3 · Recruit the champion

"If this comes up when I am not in the room, would you be willing to get behind it?"

4 · Tell the decision-maker it is coming

"Two markets are already behind it. What I need from you on Thursday is the decision itself."

By the meeting, nobody is surprised, and someone other than you is arguing for it.

H

D

Ask me that on Wednesday, after I have seen the number. But you have asked the right question, which is more than most people do.
Comprehension

About Henrik

1. What does he warn her about at the start?
That tighter limits in her region mean his own capacity gets rationed too.
2. What does he want to know before going further?
Whether she has sounded out Milan.
3. What is his answer at the end, exactly?
Not yes — ask again on Wednesday, after he has seen the number. But he says she asked the right question.
Listening

Consensus Is Not Buy-In

A group executive on why proposals die after they are approved. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does he say silence in a meeting usually means?
Not agreement — it means people have decided the conversation is not worth having in public.
2. What is his test for real buy-in?
Whether somebody would defend it in a room you are not in.
3. What does he say about naming the trade-off yourself?
If you name it, it is a judgement. If they find it, it becomes a weakness in your case.
4. What does he say about people who only present?
They are surprised twice: once when the proposal is approved, and again six months later when nothing has happened.
Listening 2

The Conversations You Skip

A director on influencing in a language that is not yours. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What did she notice about her own influence when she moved to an English-speaking group?
Her presentations were fine and her influence collapsed — because influence lives in the unscripted conversations, and those were the ones she avoided.
2. Why are the informal conversations harder than the formal ones?
There is no preparation, no slides and no agenda — and everything that makes a presentation safe is missing.
3. What did she start doing about it?
She prepared the informal conversations the way she prepared presentations — three sentences, written down, before picking up the phone.
4. What does she say about how those calls sounded?
Slightly stiff at first, and nobody minded — the awkwardness was hers alone, and it went away after a month.
Chapter 5: Practice

Your Four Conversations

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "The group is committed to review the limits this quarter."
"...committed to reviewing the limits." (here "to" is a preposition, so it takes -ing)
2. "I suggest him to sound out Milan first."
"I suggest sounding out Milan first." or "I suggest that he sounds out Milan first."
3. "Colombia decided supporting the proposal."
"Colombia decided to support the proposal." (decide takes to)
4. "Two markets support to the proposal already."
"Two markets support the proposal already." (support takes no preposition)

Because the objection you have not heard yet is the one that kills you in the room. And the decision-maker should hear that two markets are already behind it.
