Chapter 4: The Code

Three Small Verbs

should · could · might

Grammar Discovery

Advice, Suggestion or Guess?

"You should go early, before the first boat arrives."

"We could take the ferry instead."

"It might be too crowded in August."

"You should not judge a city at noon in August."

One is advice. One is a suggestion. One is a guess. And the verb after all three has the same shape — which one?

VerbWhat it doesExample
shouldadvice — the right thing to doYou should go early.
coulda soft suggestion — one option among othersWe could take the ferry.
mighta guess — you are not sureIt might be crowded.
The shapeAlways the bare verb after them: should go, never should to go, never should goes. Same for he, she and it: she might come.
Negative and questionAdd not: should not, could not, might not. For questions, move the verb to the front: Should I book it? — never Do I should book it?

The three of them are how you sound certain, open or careful — without changing a single other word in the sentence.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"You should to visit the villages inland."
"You should visit the villages inland."
"She might goes there in October."
"She might go there in October."
"Do I should buy the ticket online?"
"Should I buy the ticket online?"

Modal verbs travel light: no to after them, no s for he or she, and no do in the question. The modal itself goes to the front.

Chapter 5: Two Opinions

Who Do You Believe?

A local host, and a guide who disagrees with her

Listening

Give the Coast Two Days, and Inland One

A local host answers the question she gets every week. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she say about the old town in August, and what does she suggest instead?
In August it might be overrated. She says you should see it at eight in the morning, when the shops are still closed.
2. What is her real advice about the coast and the villages inland?
Give the coast two days, and then take the bus inland for one day, because almost nobody does it.
3. Why does she say the inland day is the one people remember?
Because lunch there is typical, it lasts three hours and it costs half the price. Everything else, she says, they could have seen in a photograph.
Listening 2

Reconsider the Hour, Not the Place

A guide argues the other side. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is his opinion about places that a lot of people visit?
That a place is not overrated simply because many people go there. He says the walls are unique and there is no second version of them in Europe.
2. What does he think people really mean when they call a place overrated?
That the experience is crowded, which for him is a different complaint.
3. What does he recommend, and what is the price of it?
Pay for the guided walk at opening time. It costs more, it starts too early and you might be tired, but you stand there with about thirty people instead of three thousand.
