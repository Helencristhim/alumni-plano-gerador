Overrated or Worth It -- Holding an Opinion in English

Agreeing, disagreeing and defending a verdict about a place. Key words: overrated, worth it, perspective, unique, typical, off the beaten track, to argue, to reconsider. Structure: the modal verbs should, could and might for advice, possibility and speculation.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Overrated -- not as good as most people say it is
"Every summer somebody writes that Dubrovnik is overrated."
Worth it -- good enough to justify the time, the money or the effort
"The walls alone make the whole trip worth it."
Perspective -- the way one person sees a situation, from where they stand
"The city has not changed. Only your perspective has."
Unique -- the only one of its kind, with nothing else like it
"The walls are unique. There is no second version of them."
Typical -- normal or usual for a place or a group of people
"Lunch up there is typical, slow and half the price."
Off the beaten track -- away from the places most tourists go
"Give yourself one afternoon off the beaten track."
To argue -- to give reasons for or against an idea, with someone who disagrees
"That is exactly why people still argue about it every summer."
To reconsider -- to think about a decision again and possibly change it
"You could always reconsider it later, from a quieter street."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A friend wrote to me last week and asked whether the old town is overrated. My honest answer is that it depends on the hour. If you arrive at noon in August, it might be the worst two hours of your trip. You should go at eight in the morning instead, when the shops are still closed and the stone is completely yours.

And you could always take the bus inland for one afternoon, off the beaten track. The villages up there are not unique in any dramatic way, but lunch is typical, it lasts three hours, and it costs half the price. Should you book that walk in advance? Probably yes in August. It might not be necessary in October, but I would not risk it. If the place disappoints you, reconsider the hour before you reconsider the city.

1. Why does the writer say "it might be the worst two hours" instead of "it is the worst two hours"?
A Because "might" is a more polite way of saying exactly the same thing.
B Because he is guessing. He is not sure it will happen to that person.
C Because the sentence is about the past and "is" would be wrong.
2. The text says "you should go at eight" and "you could always take the bus". What is the difference?
A "Should" is advice he really wants you to follow. "Could" is one option among others.
B "Should" is for the morning and "could" is for the afternoon.
C "Could" is the past of "should", so it describes something finished.
3. Look at the question in the text: "Should you book that walk in advance?" What is missing, compared to a normal question?
A Nothing is missing. The word "do" was simply forgotten.
B The subject is missing, because modal verbs have no subject.
C There is no "do". The modal itself moves to the front of the question.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Should, Could and Might

GRAMMAR

Three small verbs that change how strong you sound, without changing anything else in the sentence.

VerbWhat it doesExample
shouldadvice -- the right thing to doYou should go early.
coulda soft suggestion -- one option among othersWe could take the ferry.
mighta guess -- you are not sureIt might be crowded.
NegativeAdd not: you should not judge a city at noon · it might not be open on Monday.
QuestionThe modal moves to the front: Should I book it? · Could we take the ferry? Never "Do I should...".
The shape after themAlways the bare verb: should go, not "should to go" · she might come, not "she might comes". Modal verbs never take to and never take s.

A useful pair for opinions: "You should see it early" sounds like real advice, while "You could see it early" leaves the decision with the other person.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Somebody disagrees with you at a dinner table. Choose the best thing to say.

Someone says your favourite city is overrated. You disagree, but you want to keep the conversation friendly. You say:
A "No. You are completely wrong about that."
B "I am not sure I agree. I think the hour matters more than the place."
C "Maybe. I do not have any opinion about this."
You want to give a friend real advice about the best time to visit. You say:
A "You should to go at eight in the morning."
B "You should go at eight in the morning."
C "You shoulds going at eight in the morning."
You are not sure whether the museum opens on Monday. The honest answer is:
A "It might not be open on Monday. We should check."
B "It might not to be open on Monday."
C "It not might be open on Monday."
You want to ask a local whether booking in advance is necessary. You ask:
A "Do I should book the walk in advance?"
B "I should to book the walk in advance?"
C "Should I book the walk in advance?"

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Choose one famous place you think is overrated and one nobody talks about that is absolutely worth it. Take about ninety seconds.
