Lesson 11 -- Pre-class

The Role in One Sentence

Your role, how big it is and who calls you, in one sentence. Key words: to oversee, entity, subsidiary, ownership, to be accountable for, cross-functional, stakeholder, liaison, expertise, end to end. Structure: non-defining relative clauses -- which, who, where, between commas.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To oversee -- to be responsible for a process and check it, without doing every step yourself
"I oversee the close for Brazil, from the first entry to the sign-off."
An entity -- a legally separate company inside a larger group
"Each entity has its own company code in SAP."
A subsidiary -- a company that is owned and controlled by a larger company
"The Brazilian subsidiary closes two days before the group."
Ownership -- being the person who answers for a process or a result
"I have ownership of the close, so the questions come to me."
To be accountable for -- to be the person who must explain a result if something goes wrong
"I am accountable for the numbers that go to the auditors."
Cross-functional -- involving people from more than one team or department
"The year-end review is a cross-functional project with tax and treasury."
A stakeholder -- a person or team that is affected by your work and cares about the result
"Every stakeholder gets the same recap after the call."
A liaison -- the person who connects two teams and passes information between them
"I am the liaison between the shared service center and the auditors."
Expertise -- deep knowledge or skill in one specific area
"Her expertise is in intercompany reconciliations."
End to end -- from the very first step to the very last one
"I run the audit end to end, from the kickoff to the final meeting."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Two introductions to the same new controller. The first: I am a senior accountant. I work on Brazil. I do the close. I also talk to the auditors. Four sentences, all true, and at the end he still does not know how big the role is. The second: I look after the Brazil close, which covers several entities, and I am the liaison with the external auditors, who usually come to me first. One sentence, and he knows the scope, the ownership and when to call.

The difference is a non-defining relative clause: a piece of extra information that sits between two commas, or after one comma at the end. Use which for things and processes, who for people, and where for places and systems: SAP, where we post every entry, is the main system. The test is simple. Take the clause out and the sentence is still complete and still true: the close takes five days.

This is not the clause from the last lesson. The report that triggered the finding was late has no commas, because the clause tells you which report — without it, you do not know. A clause between commas never tells you which one; it tells you something more about one you already know. And that is why one word is not allowed after the comma: that. The close, which I oversee is correct. The close, that I oversee is not.

1. Why does the text say the first introduction does not work?
A Because he still does not know how big the role is.
B Because it contains grammar mistakes that a native controller would notice immediately.
C Because it is too long for a first call.
2. What is the test for a clause between commas?
A It must always start with that, because that is the most common relative word in spoken English.
B You can take it out and the sentence is still complete and true.
C It tells you which one of several things you mean.
3. Which word is not allowed after the comma?
A Where, because a clause between commas can only describe people and processes.
B Who
C That

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Extra Information Between Commas

GRAMMAR

Commas around the extra. Which, who, where. Never that.

ShapeWhat it doesExample
thing, which ... ,extra information about a thing or a processThe close, which I oversee, takes five days.
person, who ... ,extra information about a personMark, who leads the team, asked me.
place or system, where ... ,extra information about a place or systemSAP, where we post every entry, is the main system.
person, whose ... ,extra information about something that belongs to a personDaniel, whose team covers six countries, joined late.
..., which ... (at the end)a comment on the whole idea before itWe closed two days early, which had never happened before.
no commas (lesson 10)tells you WHICH one — it cannot be removedThe report that triggered the finding was late.
the one that travels with itresponsible for, accountable for, due on a dayI am responsible for the reconciliation, which is due on Friday.

Never: the close, that I oversee, takes five days · SAP, which we post every entry · responsible of the reconciliation · due in Friday

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A new controller asks what you do. The answer that gives him the scope is:
A I am a senior accountant. I work on Brazil. I do the close, and I also talk to the auditors sometimes.
B I look after the Brazil close, which covers several entities.
C I look after the Brazil close, that covers several entities.
You want to add one detail about your manager without starting a new sentence. You say:
A Mark who leads the Latin America team asked me for this, and he needs it before Friday.
B Mark, which leads the Latin America team, asked me for this.
C Mark, who leads the Latin America team, asked me for this.
The auditor asks about the system you use. You say:
A SAP, where we post every entry, is the main system.
B SAP, which we post every entry, is the main system for the close and for the reporting.
C SAP, that we post every entry there, is the main system.
An interviewer asks what you are accountable for. You say:
A I am responsible of the numbers, which go to the auditors every quarter.
B I do many things, it is complicated.
C I am accountable for the numbers that go to the auditors, which means the questions come to me first.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Ninety seconds, no notes. You are introducing yourself to a global team meeting. Describe your role, two colleagues you work with every week and one system you depend on — each one with an extra piece of information between commas. Finish with what you are accountable for.
