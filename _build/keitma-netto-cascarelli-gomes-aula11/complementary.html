Talk
Simon Sinek — How great leaders inspire action (TED)

Eighteen minutes on why people remember the reason behind what you do far longer than the title. He builds almost every explanation with an extra piece of context in the middle of the sentence, which makes him a perfect model for today's structure.

Tip: write down two sentences where he adds information between commas. Then say your own role the same way: what you do, and the extra detail that makes it yours.

Video
Kara Ronin — How to confidently introduce yourself to a new team

A short, practical video with real example introductions for the first meeting with a new team or a new manager. It is exactly the situation of today's dialogue: the night before you meet somebody who will ask what you do.

Tip: pause after each example and rebuild it with your own role, adding one clause with which or who between commas.

Podcast
The Indicator from Planet Money — New college degrees designed to job-proof graduates

Ten minutes about careers, skills and what a role really covers. Radio hosts often introduce a guest with a name and then an extra clause about who that person is — listen for it, at full native speed.

Tip: count every which, who and where you hear in the first five minutes. Write down three of them and bring them to the next class.
