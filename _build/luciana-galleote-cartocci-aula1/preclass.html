Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A family-owned company -- a company that one family owns and runs, often for many years
"Teleinfo is a family-owned company. I have worked here for twenty-six years."
To specialize in -- to focus your work on one area and be an expert in it
"We specialize in infrastructure for hospitals."
Structured cabling -- an organized system of cables that connects all the computers and devices of a building
"Structured cabling is the base of every hospital network."
Access control -- a system that decides who can enter a building or a room
"The new wing has access control on every door."
A data center -- a place with many servers that store and process the data of a company
"The hospital is building a second data center."
To provide -- to give a service or a product that people need
"We provide installation and maintenance."
A client base -- all the clients of a company
"Our client base includes the ten biggest hospitals in the country."
A track record -- the results of your past work, which show that you are good at it
"We have a strong track record with hospitals."
End to end -- from the first step to the last step of a project
"We manage the project end to end, from design to maintenance."
To expand -- to grow and do business in more places or with more clients
"We want to expand to new clients with our American partner."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The meeting starts at nine. The partner manager asks each company to present itself. The first CEO says: “We provide security software for banks, and at the moment we are opening an office in Mexico.”

The second CEO says: “We specialize in infrastructure for hospitals. Our engineers visit every site before a proposal. This month we are installing access control in a new hospital wing.”

Notice the pattern. When the sentence describes the company, the verb is simple: provide, specialize, visit. When the sentence describes the project of the month, the verb takes am, is or are plus -ing.

1. Does the first company open an office in Mexico every year?
A Yes, that is its main business.
B No. Its business is security software; the office in Mexico is a project of now.
C The text does not say.
2. Why does the second CEO say “our engineers visit every site” and not “are visiting”?
A Because it is a routine: before every proposal.
B Because it happened once, in the past.
C Because it is a task only for this month.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “At the moment we install cameras in the new wing.”
B “At the moment we are installing cameras in the new wing.”
C “At the moment we are install cameras in the new wing.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple vs Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

The simple form says what the company is and does. The -ing form says what it is doing now.

JobFormExample
A fact about the companypresent simple: we + verbWe provide infrastructure for hospitals.
A fact with he / she / itpresent simple + -sThe company specializes in hospitals.
A project happening nowam / is / are + verb-ingAt the moment we are expanding our work.
A project for this montham / is / are + verb-ing + this monthThis month we are installing cameras.
The two questionsWhat does your company do? / What are you working on?What does your company do? — What are you working on now?
The trapstate verbs stay simpleNot we are having many clients. Say we have many clients.

Never: We are having many clients · Every year we are providing maintenance · At the moment we install cameras · The company provide (with it, say provides)
