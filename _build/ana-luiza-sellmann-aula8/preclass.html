Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

It Was Made by Hand -- When the Thing Matters More Than Who Did It

Describing how things are made, and reporting problems without blaming anybody. Key words: handmade, a workshop, to ruin, on display, a craft, sold out, a deliverable, to outsource. Structure: the passive voice (be plus the past participle) in the present, the past and the present perfect, and when to add "by somebody".

On display -- being shown to the public
"The work on display in the front room is for sale."
A craft -- an activity where things are made with your hands
"Ceramics is a craft, and it takes years."
Sold out -- all the places or pieces are gone
"The Saturday workshop is already sold out."
A deliverable -- the finished thing you have to hand to the client
"The final deliverable has not been signed off yet."
To outsource -- to give work to a company outside yours
"The photography was outsourced this time."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

There is a small ceramics studio near my flat where everything is made by hand. The clay is dug about two hours from the city and is left to rest for three weeks before it is used. Each bowl is shaped on the wheel and then fired twice. The workshops are held on Saturday mornings and they are usually sold out two months in advance. If a piece is ruined in the kiln, it is not thrown away: it is broken and the material is used again.

At work I use the same structure all day, for a different reason. The concept was approved on Friday. The films are being edited this week and the first cut will be delivered on Thursday. The photography was outsourced, and the images have already been sent to us. Two things are still open: the final deliverable has not been signed off by the client, and nobody has been told who is presenting on Monday. Notice that I said the brief was not sent on time, and not "you did not send it". The fact is the same. The conversation is not.

1. Why does the studio text say "the clay is dug" instead of "somebody digs the clay"?
A Because the interesting part is the clay and the process, not the person.
B Because nobody is allowed to dig the clay.
C Because the studio wants to hide who does it.
2. What is the difference between "the brief was not sent on time" and "you did not send it"?
A The first one is more formal, and the second is a grammar mistake.
B Both report the same fact, but the first one does not point at a person.
C The first one means somebody else sent it.
3. In "the images have already been sent", which part carries the time?
A The word "sent".
B The verb "be" (have been). The participle never changes.
C The word "already", which replaces the verb.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Thing Goes First

GRAMMAR

One move: be carries the time, and the verb becomes the participle.

TimeFormExample
Presentam / is / are + participleThe workshops are held on Saturdays.
Pastwas / were + participleThe concept was approved on Friday.
Present perfecthas / have been + participleThe images have been sent.
Happening nowis / are being + participleThe films are being edited this week.
Negative and questionThe negative goes on be: "It wasn't approved." / "It hasn't been signed off." Questions invert be: "Was it approved?" / "Has it been sent?"
by + personAdd it only when the person is interesting: "made by hand", "made by students". Most sentences do not need it at all.

Use this when you do not know who did it, when it does not matter who did it, or when you would rather not say. That last one is the reason it appears in every status update in the world.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

The client asks where the campaign stands. Speak for about ninety seconds.
