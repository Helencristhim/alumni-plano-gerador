Chapter 3: The Code

The Thing Goes First

And the person goes to the end, or nowhere at all

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Every piece was made by hand."

"The clay is dug two hours from the city."

"The concept has been approved."

"The films are being edited this week."

In all four sentences, who did it? Look at where that person is.

FormWhenExample
be + past participleThe whole structure. Only be changes; the participle never does.It is made. It was made. It has been made.
PresentA routine, a process, a fact.The workshops are held on Saturdays.
PastA finished moment.The concept was approved on Friday.
Present perfectDone, and it matters now.The images have been sent.
by + personOnly add it if the person is interesting: "made by hand", "made by students". Most of the time you leave it out.
Why use itYou do not know who; it does not matter who; or you do not want to say who. The third one is the most useful at work.

One move: the thing goes to the front, be carries the time, the verb becomes the participle.

The Real Reason

Same Fact, No Finger Pointing

SOUNDS LIKE AN ACCUSATION

"You didn't send the brief on time."

"Marina broke the file."

SOUNDS LIKE A REPORT

"The brief wasn't sent on time."

"The file was damaged."

The fact is identical. The temperature of the room is not.

Use it to describe a problem. Do not use it to hide your own responsibility — native speakers hear that too.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"The concept approved on Friday."
"The concept was approved on Friday."
"Every piece was made by hand by the studio by four people."
"Every piece was made by hand."
"The images have been send to the client."
"The images have been sent to the client."

The verb be is never optional here, the second verb is always the participle, and one by is already one more than most sentences need.

At the Studio

O
Take your time. Everything you see here was made in this room, by four people.
A
All of it handmade? Even the ones at the back?
O
Those were made by students, so they are not for sale. They are kept here until somebody comes and collects them.
A
Is the clay brought from somewhere else, or is it local?
O
It is dug about two hours from here, and then it is left to rest for three weeks before it is used.
A
Three weeks before anybody touches it. That is a proper craft. Is the Saturday workshop sold out?
O
It is, I am afraid. But a new group is opened in June. And if a piece is ruined in the kiln, we do not throw it away, the material is used again.
A
Put me down for June, then. I have been painting for a few months and everything I make is ruined anyway.
Comprehension

About the Studio

1. Why are the pieces at the back not for sale?
They were made by students and they are kept there until somebody collects them.
2. What happens to the clay before it is used?
It is dug two hours away and then left to rest for three weeks.
3. What happens to a piece that is ruined in the kiln?
It is not thrown away. The material is used again.
Listening

The Workshop Was Cancelled

A small disaster, nothing serious. Sound first — no text.

1. Why was Saturday's workshop cancelled?
The kiln was broken during the week and it has not been repaired yet.
2. What have they been offered instead, and why is that good?
Two places in the June group, which is usually sold out by March.
3. What happened to her bowl?
Nothing bad. It was not ruined, it is just not finished.
Listening 2

Nobody Has Been Told Yet

Daniel, with a status update before the meeting. Sound first — no text.

1. What was approved, and when?
The concept, on Friday.
2. Which part was outsourced, and where is it now?
The photography. The images have already been sent to them, so that part is done.
3. What are the two things that are still open?
The final deliverable has not been signed off, and nobody has been told who is presenting on Monday.
