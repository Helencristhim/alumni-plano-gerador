"What you need to know about inversion" -- BBC Learning English, Live Class

The full set of negative openings from this lesson: not only, never, at no point, rarely, only when. Connection to Lesson 5: this is the four-word opening that buys you a whole sentence of thinking time while it sounds like emphasis.

Tip: every time an example appears, pause and rebuild it with a fact from your own last role. Say it out loud at full speed — slow practice does not transfer to a room where you are under scrutiny.

"How to stay calm under pressure" -- Noa Kageyama and Pen-Pen Chen, TED-Ed (5 min)

Five minutes on why people who genuinely know something lose access to it when they are being judged. It is about musicians and athletes, and it describes precisely what happened to you in that interview: the knowledge was there and the retrieval was not. Full transcript beside the video.

Tip: watch it once and write down the single sentence that explains the mechanism. Then read it back next to your own account of the interview. The point is not to feel better — it is to stop treating a retrieval problem as a language problem.

"How to stay calm when you know you will be stressed" -- Daniel Levitin, TED (12 min)

A neuroscientist on why we make bad decisions under stress, and on building the plan BEFORE the pressure arrives rather than trying to think clearly inside it. That is exactly what the four-second protocol is: a decision taken in advance, so that nothing has to be decided in the moment.

Tip: after watching, write your own version of the protocol card in your handwriting, with your sentences. Keep it next to the keyboard for every online interview. Pilots use checklists for the same reason, and nobody thinks less of them for it.
