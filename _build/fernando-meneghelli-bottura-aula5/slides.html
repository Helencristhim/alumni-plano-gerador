Chapter 3: The Code

A Sentence That Buys You a Sentence

Emphasis at the front, thinking time behind it

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

Not only did we protect the margin, we also closed three days earlier.

At no point did the board ask me to reverse that decision.

Never have I reported to a board that spoke only Portuguese.

Only when the reporting line moved did I decide to leave.

Each sentence starts with a negative or limiting phrase. What happens to the subject and the verb after it?

Opening phrasePlain versionInverted
Not only ...We did not only protect the margin.Not only did we protect the margin...
At no point ...The board never asked me to reverse it.At no point did the board ask me to reverse it.
Never ...I have never reported to that kind of board.Never have I reported to that kind of board.
Only when ...I decided to leave when the line moved.Only when the line moved did I decide to leave.
The mechanicsAfter the opening phrase the sentence behaves like a QUESTION: auxiliary first, then subject, then the main verb in its base form. did we protect, have I reported, did the board ask.
Why it is useful todayIt is emphasis, and it is also time. Not only did we is four words that commit you to nothing while your brain finds the rest of the sentence. That is why it belongs in a lesson about the blank.

One inversion per answer. Two in a row and it stops sounding senior and starts sounding rehearsed.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"Not only we protected the margin, we also closed earlier."
"Not only did we protect the margin, we also closed earlier."
"At no point did the board asked me to reverse it."
"At no point did the board ask me to reverse it."
"Never reported I to a board that spoke only Portuguese."
"Never have I reported to a board that spoke only Portuguese."

The auxiliary moves in front of the subject, and the main verb stays in its base form. It behaves exactly like a question.

The Protocol

Three Steps, Four Seconds

1. Say something true

"There are two parts to that."

2. Name the destination

"Let me start with the working capital side."

3. Start concrete

"Let me give you the number first, and then the reasoning."

Silence is what convinces you the blank is real. Break the silence in under two seconds and the rest comes back on its own.

Chapter 4: In the Room

The Blank, Live

An evaluator who has never met you, and one question you cannot see coming

Dialogue

Four Seconds That Nobody Noticed

N
Let us change direction. Working capital. How did you manage it in a group where the clients paid in ninety days and the media owners wanted thirty?
F
There are two parts to that.
N
Go on.
F
Let me start with the number, and then the structure behind it. The gap was about sixty days across nine entities.
N
Sixty days is a lot of cash. What did you do with it?
F
I lost the thread for a second there. Let me pick it up: we moved the media payment terms first, because that was the side we controlled.
N
And the client side?
F
Not only did we shorten the client terms, we also stopped invoicing at month end, which took four days out on its own.
N
Good. And for what it is worth, I did not notice anything at all.
Comprehension

About Nadia

1. What is the situation she describes in her first question?
Clients paying in ninety days while the media owners wanted to be paid in thirty.
2. How does she react when he says there are two parts to the question?
She simply says "go on" — she treats it as a normal opening, not as hesitation.
3. What does she say at the end, and what does it tell you?
That she did not notice anything at all — the pause that felt long to him was invisible from the other side of the table.
Listening

Why It Is Not a Language Problem

A coach on what actually happens under evaluation. Sound first — no text.

1. What example does she give to prove it is not about vocabulary?
Standing in front of a fridge unable to remember the word for something you eat every day — in your own language.
2. What are the three steps, and how long does she say the protocol takes?
Say something true out loud, name where you are going, and start with something concrete. Four seconds.
3. Why does she say step one matters more than the other two?
Because silence is what convinces you the blank is real.
Listening 2

A CFO Who Went Blank

In front of a board, in his second year. Sound first — no text.

1. What did he do at the time, and why does he say he would not recommend it?
He kept talking, faster, hoping to arrive somewhere — and after about forty seconds somebody had to offer him a moment.
2. What does he say was the worst part of that episode?
Being given the moment rather than taking it himself.
3. What does he do now instead, and why does it work?
He gives the number first, because a number is easier to retrieve than an argument — and by the time it is out of his mouth, the argument is back.
