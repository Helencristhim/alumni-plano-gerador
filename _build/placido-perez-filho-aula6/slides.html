Chapter 4: The Code

Used To, Would, Get Used To

Four structures, one story

Grammar Discovery

The Same Two Words, Two Meanings

"We used to play in the street until dark."

"On Sundays, the whole town would meet in the square."

"After forty years, I’m used to the traffic."

"It took my parents years to get used to living in an apartment."

Blue: habits in the past that do not exist anymore. Red: something that is (or became) normal for you. Look at what comes after each one: a simple verb, or a noun and an -ing form?

JobFormExample
A past habit or stateused to + verbWe used to play outside.
A repeated past actionwould + verb (actions only)The town would meet on Sundays.
Past statesused to, never wouldWe used to live in a small town.
Normal for me nowbe used to + noun / -ingI’m used to the traffic.
Becoming normalget used to + noun / -ingI got used to living in a big city.
The trapbe / get used to + -ing, not + verbNot I’m used to wake up early. Say I’m used to waking up early.

In one line: used to + verb = an old habit; be / get used to + noun or -ing = something normal now, or becoming normal.

A profile like this turns into a story with used to and would: people used to meet in the square; on Saturdays, everybody would go to the market.
1. Turn the line about children into a sentence with used to.
Children used to play in the street until dark.
2. Turn the market line into a sentence with would.
On Saturdays, everybody would go to the market at six in the morning.
3. If you moved to this town today, what would be hard to get used to?
Open answer. For example: I would have to get used to the quiet evenings.
Listening 1 · A Village in England

The Village I Grew Up In

A British man in his seventies remembers the village where he grew up. Sound first — no text.

1. How many people lived in his village?
About four hundred.
2. What would his father do every Saturday?
Take him to the market in the next town.
3. What was hard to get used to when he moved to London?
Nobody said good morning to him.
Listening 2 · From a Small Town to Mumbai

Ten Minutes to Everywhere

An Indian woman talks about moving from a small town to Mumbai for work. Sound first — no text.

1. How long did everything use to take in her hometown?
About ten minutes.
2. How long is her commute in Mumbai?
An hour and a half.
3. What does she say she is used to now?
The crowds and the noise.
Listening 3 · A Psychologist on Nostalgia

Why We Look Back

An American psychologist explains what nostalgia does for us. Sound first — no text.

1. What did psychologists use to think about nostalgia?
That it was a kind of sadness, even an illness.
2. What does research show today?
That nostalgia makes people feel more connected and less lonely.
3. What is her advice?
Look back, but do not live there.
