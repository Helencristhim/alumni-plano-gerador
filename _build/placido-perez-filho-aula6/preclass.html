Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

The Town of Five Thousand -- Then and Now

From a small town to a big city: the journey millions of families made. Key words: a hometown, to grow up, close-knit, carefree, to move away, bustling, to settle in, to adjust to, to look back on, nostalgia, a sense of belonging, to fit in. Structure: used to, would, be used to, get used to.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A hometown -- the town or city where you were born or grew up
"My hometown had only one main street."
To grow up -- to change from a child into an adult
"I grew up in a small town in the interior."
Close-knit -- where everybody knows and helps each other
"It was a close-knit town: everybody knew your name."
Carefree -- without worries or responsibilities
"Those were carefree years: no traffic, no locks on the doors."
To move away -- to leave a place and go to live somewhere else
"We moved away when I was seven."
Bustling -- full of people, noise and activity
"The city was bustling, even at night."
To settle in -- to start to feel comfortable in a new place
"It took my family a year to settle in."
To adjust to -- to change your behavior so you can live with a new situation
"Children adjust to a new city faster than adults."
To look back on -- to think about something in the past
"I look back on those years with a smile."
Nostalgia -- a happy and slightly sad feeling when you think about the past
"I feel nostalgia every time I hear the church bells."
A sense of belonging -- the feeling that you are part of a place or a group
"In a small town you have a strong sense of belonging."
To fit in -- to feel accepted as part of a group
"At school in the city, I didn’t fit in at first."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Marco grew up in a small, close-knit town. As a child, he used to play football in the street, and every Sunday his family would have lunch with his grandparents. Those were carefree years.

When he was ten, his family moved away to a bustling city. At first he didn’t fit in at school, and he had to adjust to the noise. But he settled in, and slowly he got used to living in an apartment.

Today he is used to the city, but when he looks back on his hometown, he feels nostalgia and a sense of belonging.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used To, Would, Be Used To, Get Used To

GRAMMAR

Used to + verb and would + verb tell old habits. Be used to and get used to + noun or -ing tell what is normal, or became normal, for you.

JobFormExample
A past habit or stateused to + verbWe used to play outside.
A repeated past actionwould + verb (actions only)The town would meet on Sundays.
Past statesused to, never wouldWe used to live in a small town.
Normal for me nowbe used to + noun / -ingI’m used to the traffic.
Becoming normalget used to + noun / -ingI got used to living in a big city.
The trapbe / get used to + -ing, not + verbNot I’m used to wake up early. Say I’m used to waking up early.

Never: We would live in a small town · I’m used to wake up early · I use to walk · get used the noise
