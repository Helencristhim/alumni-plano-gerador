Chapter 4: The Code

The Sentence That Hides Who Acts

Four lines from the text, and the person who is never mentioned

Grammar Discovery

Who Signed, Who Ratified?

"The treaty was signed in March."

"A reservation can be made at the moment of ratification."

"The text has been ratified by forty states."

"Students are often told that the treaty is not the problem."

In each sentence, who did the action? Is that person mentioned? Why would a legal text choose to hide the person, or to mention it only with by?

JobFormExample
The action matters more than who did itbe + past participleThe treaty was signed in March.
Say who did it, when it matterspassive + by + agentIt was ratified by the congress.
A result that is still truehas / have been + past participleIt has been ratified by forty states.
Possibility or obligationmodal + be + past participleA reservation can be made. The text must be approved.
Something in progressis / are being + past participleThe case is being heard this week.
A general beliefit is said / believed that + clauseIt is often said that treaties are weak.
The trapnever was ratify; never was ratified fromNot ratified from the congress. Say ratified by the congress.

In one line: Legal English puts the action first: be + past participle, and by only when the actor matters.

Quick Fire

One Question at a Time

Listening 1 · A Lecture

Three Moments, One Treaty

A law professor opens a class on treaty law. Sound first — no text.

1. What mistake does the professor say appears in almost every first essay?
Students write that a country that signed a treaty is bound by it.
2. What are the three moments she describes, in order?
The text is adopted, then signed, then ratified.
3. Which moment creates a legal obligation, and what question should students ask when they read a headline?
Ratification creates the obligation. They should ask: signed, or ratified?
Listening 2 · A Radio Explainer

Can a Country Just Leave?

A short radio explainer on withdrawal from a treaty. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the short answer to the question, and what does he mean by not overnight?
Yes, a country can leave, but withdrawal only takes effect after a waiting period, often one year.
2. What is the country still expected to do during the waiting period?
To comply with every provision, because it is still a party.
3. What do some agreements say about the first years, and what should listeners check?
That no one can withdraw during the first years after entry into force. Listeners should check the date the notice was given.
