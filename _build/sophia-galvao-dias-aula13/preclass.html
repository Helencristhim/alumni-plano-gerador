Lesson 13 — Pre-class

Match Day

About ten minutes. Eight match words — kick-off, half-time, chant, the stands, referee, penalty, equaliser, scarf — and the live words of every match: just, already, yet.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud.

Kick-off -- the moment when a football match starts
"The match hasn't started yet. Kick-off is at four."
Half-time -- the short break in the middle of a match
"It's half-time and nobody has scored yet."
Chant -- a short song that fans sing together, again and again
"The fans have just started a new chant."
The stands -- the rows of seats where the fans sit or stand
"We have never sat that high in the stands."
Referee -- the person who makes sure the players follow the rules
"The referee has already shown two yellow cards."
Penalty -- a free kick from close to the goal, with only the goalkeeper to beat
"Have you ever taken a penalty?"
Equaliser -- the goal that makes the score the same for both teams
"They have just scored the equaliser!"
Scarf -- a long piece of cloth fans wear around the neck in the team colours
"I have had this scarf since I was eight."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the messages from the stadium. The words in bold are live (right now) or the story (with a time).

4:02 — We are in the stands! Kick-off has just happened, and I have already lost my voice. Nobody has scored yet.

4:40 — Half-time. The referee has shown three yellow cards. This is the loudest match I have ever been to.

Next day — So, the story: we scored the equaliser in the last minute, everybody jumped, and my dad hugged a stranger.

"Kick-off has just happened." When did it happen?
A It has not happened yet.
B A second ago.
C Last week.
"Nobody has scored yet." What do we know?
A There were no goals in the whole match.
B There are no goals now, but there could be soon.
C Somebody scored a minute ago.
In the Next day message, why is it we scored and not we have scored?
A Because score never takes have.
B Because she is not sure.
C Because it is the story of a finished match, at a finished time.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Just, already, yet

  • just = a second ago: They have just scored!
  • already = sooner than expected: I have already lost my voice.
  • yet = not until now, at the end: Nobody has scored yet. Have they scored yet?

The pair

  • Have you ever been to a match? → Yes, I have! I went last year. We won! The present perfect opens; the past simple tells the story.

Remember: your life up to now is have, never had.
