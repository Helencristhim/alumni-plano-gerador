Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

The Thing I Kept Working On

Everybody talks about starting and finishing. The part that breaks people is the middle. Key words: to stick with something, to walk away from something, a dead end, to second-guess yourself, momentum, a rabbit hole, to park something, to commit to something, a false start, to narrow something down, diminishing returns, to call it. Structure: the first verb chooses the shape of the second -- decide to, keep -ing, and a preposition always takes -ing.

"At some point somebody has to call it, and it is never the person who made it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Nine hours on two seconds of audio. I decided to fix a transition that was not even wrong, I kept trying different versions, and I avoided asking anybody whether it mattered. Look at those three verbs, because they are the grammar of this lesson: decide wants to, keep wants -ing, avoid wants -ing. There is no difference in meaning that explains it. The verb that comes first decides the shape of the one that follows, and you learn the lists by ear.

The first list takes to: decide, agree, refuse, manage, offer, plan, promise, need, want. I agreed to park it. We managed to narrow it down to three takes. The second list takes -ing: keep, avoid, finish, give up, consider, suggest, imagine, risk. I gave up looking for a better version. And there is one rule with no exceptions at all: after a preposition, always -ing. A number stops me from second-guessing myself. I am tired of arguing about eight bars.

A few verbs take both with the same meaning — start, begin, continue, like, love, hate. I started mixing and I started to mix are the same sentence. But three of them change meaning completely, and they are the ones worth knowing. I stopped mixing means I finished; I stopped to listen means I paused in order to listen. I remember hearing that version is a memory; remember to send it is a task. Try moving the vocal is an experiment; try to move it is an attempt that might fail.

The vocabulary of the middle is the rest. A rabbit hole is a detail you follow until you forget the main job. Diminishing returns is the hour where more work stops helping. You park something on purpose and you walk away from a dead end, and either way you lose momentum. A false start costs a day. And somebody eventually has to call it — which is easier if you decided in advance how long you were willing to stick with it.

1. Why is it "I kept trying" and not "I kept to try"?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Gerunds and Infinitives

GRAMMAR

One decision: which verb came first, and which list is it in?
