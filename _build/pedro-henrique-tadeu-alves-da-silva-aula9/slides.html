Chapter 3: The Code

The Verb Before Decides

Not the meaning. The verb that came first.

Grammar Discovery

What Decides -ing or to?

"I decided to keep working on it."

"I kept trying different versions."

"I avoided asking anybody for help."

"I finally agreed to park it."

Two of them take to and two take -ing. There is no difference in meaning between keep and continue, and yet one of them accepts both. What decides it is not logic.

JobFormExample
These verbs take todecide, agree, refuse, manage, offer, plan, promise, need, wantI decided to park it. She refused to commit. We managed to call it.
These verbs take -ingkeep, avoid, finish, give up, consider, suggest, imagine, riskI kept trying. I avoided asking. We gave up looking for a better take.
After a preposition, always -ingof / from / about / at / in + verb-ingIt stops me from second-guessing myself. I am tired of arguing about it.
Both, same meaningstart, begin, continue, like, love, hateI started mixing = I started to mix.
Both, DIFFERENT meaning: stopstop + -ing = end it · stop + to = pause in order toI stopped mixing (I finished). I stopped to listen (I paused so that I could listen).
Both, DIFFERENT meaning: remember / try-ing looks back · to looks forwardI remember hearing it (the memory). Remember to send it (the task). Try moving it (experiment). Try to move it (attempt).
The trapthis is a list, not a logicNot I decided trying, not I kept to try. The verb before decides, and you learn it by ear.

In one line: the first verb chooses the shape of the second, and only stop, remember and try change meaning when the shape changes.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Being Stuck, at Full Speed

One conversation, two recordings, one session log

Dialogue

Two Weeks on Eight Bars

D
I have been on the same eight bars for two weeks. I keep going back to it and I cannot tell any more whether it is good or whether I am just tired.
P
Have you played it to anybody?
D
No. I have been avoiding it, which I know is the answer to your next question.
P
I once spent nine hours on two seconds of audio. A transition. It was not even wrong, it just was not beautiful, and I refused to leave it. I kept trying versions until about three in the morning.
D
And did you fix it?
P
Nobody has ever mentioned it. Not once. That is the whole story. I lost the momentum of an entire session and hit diminishing returns around hour three without noticing.
D
So what do you do now, when it happens?
P
I park it. I stop working on the difficult part and I finish something small and boring instead. Then I come back two days later and the problem has usually changed shape.
D
Two days feels like giving up. I have a deadline on the fourteenth.
P
Then decide now how many more hours you are willing to spend, and write the number down. It stops you from second-guessing yourself every four hours. And you, how do you usually know when to call it?
Comprehension

About Dara

1. How long has Dara been working on the same part, and what can she no longer tell?
Two weeks on the same eight bars. She cannot tell any more whether it is good or whether she is just tired.
2. Has she played it to anybody, and what does she say about that?
No. She has been avoiding it, and she says she already knows that is the answer to his next question.
3. Why does she reject the idea of waiting two days?
Because it feels like giving up, and she has a deadline on the fourteenth.
Listening 1 · Nobody Warns You About the Middle

The Ones Who Survive Are Not the Stubborn Ones

Eleven days when the work is no longer new and not yet good. Sound first — no text.

1. Which part of a project does she say actually breaks people?
Not the start and not the finish: the eleven days in between, when the thing is no longer new and it is not yet good.
2. What happens to the stubborn ones, and what do the survivors do instead?
The stubborn ones keep pushing on the same idea for a week and end up with a slightly better version of something that was never going to work. The survivors park it, finish something small and boring on purpose, and come back two days later.
3. What is the second thing the survivors do, and why does she say the number does not have to be right?
They decide out loud how much longer they are willing to spend on it. The number does not have to be right, because having one stops you from second-guessing yourself every four hours.
Listening 2 · Nine Hours, Two Seconds

The Feeling of Being Close Is Not Information

A transition between a verse and a chorus, and a whole night. Sound first — no text.

1. What exactly was wrong with the two seconds of audio, and what did he do about it?
Nothing was wrong. It was just not beautiful, and he refused to leave it. He kept trying different versions and avoided going to bed.
2. What does he say happened around hour seven, and what happened after the mix went out?
He stopped being a producer and became a person defending a decision. Nobody mentioned the transition, then or ever.
3. What does he say about the feeling of being close, and what does he do now instead?
It is not information: it feels identical whether you are twenty minutes from finishing or four hours from realising it is a dead end. Now he decides in advance how long he is willing to spend, writes the number down, and when he gets there he either calls it or asks somebody else to listen.

1. Two lines on this log tell the whole story on their own. Which are they?
"Time budget set: 45 minutes" and "Actual: 9 hours 10 minutes". The gap between the plan and the reality is the entire lesson.
2. Version 3 was delivered, out of 31. What does that number argue?
That the useful work happened in the first forty minutes, and that the other twenty-eight versions were produced after the point where more effort stopped improving the result.
3. What does the Second opinion line say, and why does it matter here?
Not requested. Asking anybody would have ended the loop in two minutes, and avoiding that question is what made the nine hours possible.

Now You Are the One Stuck

Keywords:

Scenario: Tell the story of something you spent far too long on. What you decided to do, what you kept doing, what you avoided doing, when you should have parked it, and how you finally called it. Finish with what you would tell somebody in the middle of it right now.
