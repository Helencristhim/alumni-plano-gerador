Lesson 3 -- Past vs. Now

Podcast
NPR -- How I Built This: "Walker & Company: Tristan Walker"

Recorded live, in front of an audience, which is exactly the pressure you are training for. He tells fifteen years in one hour and the setbacks are in the middle of the story, not hidden at the end: the meetings that went nowhere, the investors who said no, and what he did next. Fast American English with a transcript on the page.

Tip: listen for the moment he names a year. Every time he does, the verb goes to the past. Count three of those and three sentences where he says what has been true since.

Talk
Astro Teller -- "The unexpected benefit of celebrating failure" (TED, 15 min)

The head of Google’s moonshot lab explaining why the setback is the useful part of the story, and why a team that hides it learns nothing. Directly relevant to the choice you practised today: telling a partner what went wrong is not a confession, it is evidence of how you work.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Write down one project he describes in four sentences — where it started, what went wrong, what they did, what is true now. Then do the same about a project of your own.

Watch on TED ↗
Series
Formula 1: Drive to Survive -- any episode (Netflix)

Forty minutes of people explaining, on camera and in fast English, what went wrong and what they did about it. The shape is always the four movements you practised: the season started here, this went wrong, we changed that, and here is where we are now. British and American voices side by side, at real speed.

Tip: pick one team principal and write down the four movements of his season in four sentences. Note which ones name a race or a month, and which ones do not.
