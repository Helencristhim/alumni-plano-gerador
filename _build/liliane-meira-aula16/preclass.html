Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A benchmark -- a fixed task you repeat later to measure your progress
"The two-minute recording from lesson 1 is my benchmark."
To signpost -- to tell listeners where your talk is going before you go there
"Signpost your three parts in the first sentence."
To pace yourself -- to control your speed so that you do not rush or get tired
"Pace yourself: five minutes is longer than it feels."
A filler -- a sound or word like um or you know that fills a silence
"A pause sounds more confident than a filler."
To stumble -- to make a small mistake while speaking and lose the flow for a moment
"If you stumble over a word, just say it again slowly."
To wrap up -- to finish a talk or a meeting in a clear way
"Let me wrap up with the one idea I want you to keep."
To look back on -- to think about something that happened in the past
"When I look back on the first lessons, I hear how much I hesitated."
A breakthrough -- a sudden important step forward after a difficult period
"The breakthrough came when I stopped translating in my head."
To build on -- to use something you already have as a base for the next step
"This block builds on everything we did in the first one."
To hold back -- to not say or do as much as you could, often because you are afraid
"In the first recording I held back and used only short sentences."
Self-assessment -- judging your own work or progress honestly
"The self-assessment asks what improved and what still needs work."
To step out of your comfort zone -- to do something new or difficult that makes you a little nervous
"Five minutes in English is a step out of my comfort zone."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

In her first lesson, Liliane recorded two minutes about her work. Fifteen lessons later, she prepares five. This time she has a plan, and each minute has a tool.

She opens with a cleft: What I make are sound works from hospital wards. Then the story: by the time she left photography, she had worked with images for twenty years, and it was a nurse who changed everything. Since 2015 she has been recording the night shift.

For the critics, she concedes: admittedly, the sample is small, but it can't be ignored. And for the future, a conditional: if a hospital abroad invited her, she would go. The last thirty seconds wrap up the first idea.

1. Why does she open with What I make are...?
A Because it puts the work at the start and the important words at the end.
B Because she wants to talk about her education and her first exhibitions before anything else.
C Because it is a question.
2. "She had worked with images for twenty years." When did that happen?
A After she started working in hospitals, during the years when she recorded at night.
B Before she left photography.
C Right now.
3. What does it can't be ignored show?
A That she is not sure at all and thinks the critics are probably right about her research.
B That she does not care.
C That she is sure the sample matters, even if it is small.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are on a panel, at a fair and with clients of the agency. Choose the best thing to say.

You want to tell the audience how your talk is organized:
A I will tell you three things: what I make, how it started, and where it is going.
B I will talk about many things, and maybe some of them will be interesting for some of you here.
C First I talk, after I talk more.
Somebody asks how long you have been working in hospitals:
A I am working in hospitals since 2015, and before that I was a photographer for many years.
B I've been working in hospitals since 2015.
C I work in hospitals since 2015.
A critic says your sample is too small. You partly agree:
A You are absolutely wrong.
B Admittedly, it is small, but it is a real one.
C Yes, it is very small, so it is probably useless and I will stop the research next year.
You stumble over a difficult word in the middle of your talk:
A Sorry, sorry, my English is terrible, I am very nervous today, please excuse me, everyone.
B Say the word again, slowly, and continue.
C Stop and change the subject.
You have thirty seconds left:
A Let me wrap up with the one idea I want you to keep.
B So, um, you know, I think that is more or less everything, or maybe not, I am not sure.
C And another thing about my first exhibition...

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Before the lesson, record a first version of your benchmark: three minutes on your own work. Say what you make, how it started, what you have been doing, one limit of your research and what comes next. Use at least three tools from Block 1. In class you will record the full five minutes.
