Chapter 4: The Whole Talk

Before You Go On

One conversation, two recordings, one benchmark card

Dialogue

Five Minutes on Stage

J
Liliane, welcome to Conversations. For people who don't know you yet, what do you make?
L
What I make are sound works from hospital wards. I've been recording how patients and caregivers talk about pain since 2015.
J
You started as a photographer, didn't you?
L
I did. By the time I left photography, I had worked with images for almost twenty years, and I needed a different medium.
J
So what was the breakthrough?
L
It was a nurse who changed everything. She asked me why nobody records the night shift, and that question is still the premise of my work.
J
Some critics say that sound work about illness is heavy-handed. Do they have a point?
L
Admittedly, some of it is. But the best pieces are restrained. They leave space for the listener.
J
We have one minute left. Would you work with a hospital outside Brazil?
L
If a hospital invited me, I would go, but only with consent from every patient. Let me wrap up there: listening is where the work begins.
Comprehension

About Jonah

1. What program is Jonah hosting?
Conversations, a talk program at an art fair.
2. What does Jonah already know about Liliane’s past?
That she started as a photographer.
3. Which criticism does Jonah bring up?
That some critics say sound work about illness is heavy-handed.
Listening 1 · A Speaking Coach

Five Minutes Is Longer Than You Think

Somebody who prepares speakers for panels, on planning a short talk. Sound first — no text.

1. What do people who give a good five-minute talk do with their plan?
They let the listener see it: they signpost the parts before they start.
2. What does she recommend instead of a filler?
A pause. Silence is better than um, you know or like.
3. What should you do in the last thirty seconds?
Wrap up: go back to the first idea, say it in a new way, and stop.
Listening 2 · A Language Teacher

Which Mistakes Disappeared?

Somebody who teaches adults, on how to measure progress at B2. Sound first — no text.

1. What task do his students record again every few months?
Five minutes about their own work.
2. How do the pauses change at B2?
They stop happening in the middle of a phrase and start happening between ideas.
3. What does he ask his students to notice?
Not how many mistakes they made, but which mistakes have disappeared.
