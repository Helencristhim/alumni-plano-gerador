Lesson 14 -- Pre-class

The Year Ahead — Forecasts and Roadmaps

Reading an annual roadmap and talking about next year. Key words: roadmap, outlook, guidance, headwind, tailwind, pass-through, run rate, on track. Structure: four futures -- will, going to, the present continuous and the future perfect.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Roadmap -- a plan that shows the main steps and dates for the months ahead
"The roadmap has four price reviews next year."
Outlook -- what you expect to happen in the next months
"Our outlook for the first quarter is cautious."
Guidance -- the numbers a company tells investors to expect
"We are keeping our margin guidance for next year."
Headwind -- a problem from outside that slows you down
"Food inflation is our biggest headwind next year."
Tailwind -- something from outside that helps you move faster
"Lower freight costs will be a tailwind in the second half."
Pass-through -- the part of a cost increase that you move into the shelf price
"We are going to limit the pass-through to two percent."
Run rate -- what the yearly result will be if the current pace continues
"At the current run rate, we will reach the target in October."
On track -- going as planned, toward the goal and on time
"The new pricing tool is on track for March."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Next year will be a hard year, and the roadmap says so on page one. The outlook is cautious: food inflation will stay high until June, and it is the main headwind. Lower freight costs will be a tailwind after that. Some things are already decided. The team is going to review prices every quarter, and it is going to limit the pass-through on basic items to two percent. Two meetings are already in the calendar: the pricing director is meeting the board on February 12, and the regions are presenting their targets in March. The new pricing tool is on track: by the end of March, the team will have finished the rollout. And the numbers? The company is keeping its guidance. At the current run rate, it will have reached the volume target by October.

1. Why is it "the pricing director is meeting the board on February 12" and not "will meet"?
A The meeting is already arranged, with a date and other people.
B Because "meet" is an irregular verb, and irregular verbs never take will in a business text.
C Because it is only a prediction.
2. What does "by the end of March, the team will have finished the rollout" mean?
A The rollout starts at the end of March.
B The rollout will be complete at the end of March or before.
C The team finished the rollout last March, and today it is already in the past for everyone.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "We are going to review prices every quarter."
B "We going to review prices every quarter."
C "We are go to reviewing the prices of all the basic items every quarter."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Four Ways to Talk About the Future

GRAMMAR

Each future sends a different message. Choose the one that matches what you mean.

FormUse it forExample
will + verbA prediction or opinion, a promise, or a decision you make now.Inflation will stay high. / It won't be easy.
be going to + verbA plan you already decided, or a prediction with evidence now.We are going to review prices every quarter. / We aren't going to match every promotion.
Present continuousA fixed arrangement: a date, a place, other people.I am meeting the board on Monday. / Are you flying back on Sunday?
will have + participleFuture perfect: finished before a point in the future. Look for by + a date.By March, we will have finished the rollout. / Will you have finished by March?
Quick testOpinion? will. Decided? going to. In the calendar? -ing. Done by a date? will have + participle. In speech, all of them contract: I'll, we're going to, we'll have.
