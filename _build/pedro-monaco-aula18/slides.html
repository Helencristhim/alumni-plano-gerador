Chapter 3: The Code

Four Ways to Talk About the Future

will, going to, the present continuous and the future perfect

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I 'm flying to London on Monday morning."

"I 'm going to ask the client for a letter of invitation."

"Don't worry, I'll send you my itinerary tonight."

"By the time I land, the meeting will have started."

All four orange parts talk about the future. Which plan was decided before today, which one was decided right now, which one involves other people, and which one will already be finished?

FormWhat it says about the planExample
willA decision you make NOW, an offer or a promise, or a prediction based on your opinion."Don't worry, I'll send you the itinerary tonight."
be going toA plan you decided BEFORE now, or a prediction from evidence you can see."I'm going to ask for a letter of invitation."
present continuousAn ARRANGEMENT already fixed with other people, usually with a time or a place."I'm meeting the client on Tuesday at ten."
present simpleA TIMETABLE: flights, trains, the official program of a conference."The flight leaves at 11:40 on Sunday."
will have + past participleThe FUTURE PERFECT: an action that will be complete before a point in the future, usually with by."By Thursday, we will have agreed on every clause."
after when, before, as soon asIn a time clause about the future, use the PRESENT, never will."When I land, I'll call you." not "When I will land"

Your first language solves most of this with one form. English uses the form to tell the listener how fixed the plan is.

Meaning

One Trip, Four Futures

The same trip, seen from four different angles

"I think I'll go to London in the spring."

An idea, not a plan yet. Nothing is booked.

"I'm going to visit the London office this year."

A decision. You have made up your mind, but there is no date yet.

"I'm meeting their legal team on the twelfth."

An arrangement. The date is in two calendars, yours and theirs.

"By the end of the trip, I will have met every partner."

A finished result, seen from a point in the future.

Listening 1

Say Less at the Border

An immigration lawyer in London on what business travelers get wrong. Sound first — no text.

1. What three things is the officer going to ask?
Why you are there, how long you are staying, and where you are staying.
2. What did her client plan to do that was a problem?
He was going to spend a week training the London team, which is work and not a permitted activity for a visitor.
3. How will the client do the training instead?
Online, from Sao Paulo, next month.
Listening 2

The Trip Is Confirmed

A Brazilian lawyer describes his week in London, before he leaves. Sound first — no text.

1. Where is his layover, and when does he land in London?
In Lisbon; he lands at Heathrow on Monday afternoon.
2. What is he doing on Tuesday and Wednesday?
He is meeting the client's legal team to negotiate the distribution agreement.
3. What does he expect to have done by Thursday evening?
To have agreed on every clause except the price.
