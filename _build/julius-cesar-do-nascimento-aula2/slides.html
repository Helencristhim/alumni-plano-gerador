Chapter 4: The Code

It Is Poured, It Was Designed

When the process matters more than the person

Grammar Discovery

Five Tenses, One Pattern

"For a hundred years, car bodies have been built in the same way."

"Molten metal is poured into a giant mold."

"The process is still being improved."

"Cast iron will be needed for many years."

Look at the red verbs. Every one has a form of be and a past participle (built, poured, improved, needed). What changes is only the tense of be: have been, is, is being, will be. And who builds, pours or improves? The text does not say, because the process is the important part.

JobFormExample
Present simpleam / is / are + past participleMolten metal is poured into the mold.
Past simplewas / were + past participleThis mold was designed in Japan.
Present perfecthas / have been + past participleCar bodies have been built this way for a century.
Present continuousam / is / are being + past participleThe process is being improved.
Future and modalswill be / can be / must be + past participleCast iron will be needed for years.
The trapbe in the right tense + the past participle, never the -ing or the base formNot “The mold has been damage.” Say The mold has been damaged.

In one line: be (in any tense) + past participle: is poured, was designed, has been replaced, is being tested, will be needed.

Listening 1 · A Process Engineer

How a Casting Is Made

An American process engineer at an aluminum foundry describes his production line. Sound first — no text.

1. At what temperature is the metal melted?
At about seven hundred degrees Celsius.
2. How long does it take to make one casting?
Less than two minutes.
3. What happens to the extra metal around the edges?
It is collected as scrap and melted again the same day.
Listening 2 · A Machine Builder

The Machine Behind the Giant Castings

The sales manager of an Italian company that builds casting machines talks about a new kind of order. Sound first — no text.

1. How long has the company been building casting machines?
For more than fifty years.
2. What were they asked to build a few years ago?
A very big machine that could cast a large part of a car in one piece.
3. What is his advice to customers?
Start with one part, learn, and then decide.
Listening 3 · A Car Maker Buyer

What Buyers Are Asking For

A buyer at a French car maker explains what has changed in his job. Sound first — no text.

1. Why are fewer engine parts needed now?
Because they are building more electric cars.
2. What kind of parts does he need now?
Lightweight aluminum components that are cast, machined and checked by the same supplier.
3. What question is always asked in their meetings now?
How much of the supplier’s aluminum is recycled.
