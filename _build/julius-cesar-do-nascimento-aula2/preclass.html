Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A casting -- an object made by pouring hot liquid metal into a mold
"This casting weighs more than two hundred kilos."
A mold -- a hollow shape that gives liquid metal its final form
"Every new part needs a new mold."
Molten -- so hot that it has become liquid (for metal or rock)
"Molten iron is about four times hotter than an oven."
To pour -- to make a liquid flow from one container into another
"The metal is poured slowly to avoid air bubbles."
An alloy -- a metal that is made by mixing two or more metals
"This aluminum alloy is light and very strong."
Cast iron -- a hard, heavy type of iron that is shaped in a mold
"Brake discs are usually made of cast iron."
Aluminum -- a light, silver-colored metal that does not rust easily
"Electric cars use more aluminum than traditional cars."
To machine -- to cut and shape a metal part with a precise machine tool
"After casting, the holes are machined to a tenth of a millimeter."
A component -- one of the parts that a machine or a car is made of
"We supply more than forty different components to that customer."
Lightweight -- not heavy; made to weigh as little as possible
"Car makers are asking for lightweight parts."
To weld -- to join two pieces of metal by heating them until they melt together
"In the past, the seventy pieces were welded together by robots."
Scrap -- old or extra metal that can be melted and used again
"All the scrap from the line is melted again the same day."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Every morning in a modern foundry, the furnaces are checked, and the metal is melted. When it is molten, it is poured into a mold. A few minutes later, the casting comes out, and the extra metal around the edges is cut off. That scrap is melted again, so very little is wasted.

For decades, most car parts were made of steel or cast iron, and many of them were welded together. In the last few years, a lot of that has been changed. Big aluminum parts have been designed for electric cars, because a lightweight car needs less energy.

Today, a new alloy is being tested in many plants, and customers want every component to be machined in the same place. Engineers believe that more aluminum will be needed in the next ten years.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive in Different Tenses

GRAMMAR

The passive is always a form of be + the past participle. Only the tense of be changes: is poured, was designed, has been replaced, is being tested, will be needed. Use it when the process or the result matters more than the person who does it.

JobFormExample
Present simpleam / is / are + past participleMolten metal is poured into the mold.
Past simplewas / were + past participleThis mold was designed in Japan.
Present perfecthas / have been + past participleCar bodies have been built this way for a century.
Present continuousam / is / are being + past participleThe process is being improved.
Future and modalswill be / can be / must be + past participleCast iron will be needed for years.
The trapbe in the right tense + the past participle, never the -ing or the base formNot “The mold has been damage.” Say The mold has been damaged.

Never: The part was casted · The alloy is testing now · It has been install · It will shipped
