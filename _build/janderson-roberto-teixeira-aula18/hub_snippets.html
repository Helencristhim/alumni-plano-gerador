He Said, She Told Me -- Passing On the Meeting

How to pass on what people said in a meeting, on a call or in an email. Key expressions: minutes, an agenda, to mention, to point out, to complain, to promise, an update, to forward, a follow-up, on the same page. Structure: reported speech for statements, with say and tell.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. These ten stay here in your material -- they do not disappear after the class.

Minutes -- the written notes of what people said and decided in a meeting
"Rachel took the minutes on Monday morning."
An agenda -- the list of things to talk about in a meeting
"The new fund was the first item on the agenda."
To mention -- to say something quickly, without many details
"He mentioned that the fee was a little higher."
To point out -- to tell people something important that they did not notice
"Sophie pointed out that the minimum was too high."
To complain -- to say that you are not happy about something
"Three clients complained about the monthly report."
To promise -- to say that you will certainly do something
"Paul promised that the report would be shorter."
An update -- the newest information about something
"Could you send me a quick update after the call?"
To forward -- to send an email that you received to another person
"I will forward the minutes to you after lunch."
A follow-up -- a call or message after a meeting, to continue the conversation
"Paul wants a follow-up with every client."
On the same page -- understanding and agreeing about something in the same way
"After the call, we were all on the same page."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Monday morning Janderson was on a call with a client, so he missed the team meeting. Rachel took the minutes and forwarded them to everybody after lunch.

The first item on the agenda was the new fund. Paul said that the fund was ready and that it would launch in March. He mentioned that the minimum investment was two hundred thousand dollars, and Sophie pointed out that this was too high for some clients.

Then Rachel told the team that three clients complained about the monthly report. They said that it was too long and that they could not find the important numbers. Paul promised that the team would make a shorter version by April.

At two o'clock Rachel sent Janderson a short update. She told him that Paul wanted a follow-up with every client before the launch. Janderson called his first client at three, because he wanted everybody on the same page before March.

1. "Paul said that the fund was ready." What were Paul's exact words?
A "The fund is ready."
B "The fund was ready yesterday, and now it is closed to new clients."
C "Was the fund ready?"
2. What did Sophie point out?
A That the fund would launch in April, one month later than the plan.
B That the report was ready.
C That the minimum investment was too high for some clients.
3. What did the clients say about the monthly report?
A That it was too short and that they wanted more pages, more charts and more numbers.
B That it was too long and that they could not find the important numbers.
C That it was perfect.
4. Why did Janderson call his first client at three?
A Because the client complained to Paul about the fees on Monday morning.
B Because Rachel forgot to send the minutes.
C Because he wanted everybody on the same page before March.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

Reported speech is how you pass on what another person said. You use say or tell, and the verb usually moves one step back in time.

FormWhat it saysExample
say (that) + sentenceNo person after said."Paul said that he was busy."
tell + person + (that)Tell always needs a person."Paul told me that he was busy."
am / is → was · are → wereThe verb be moves back."I am ready." → "She said that she was ready."
present simple → past simplewant becomes wanted, know becomes knew."I want a lower fee." → "He said that he wanted a lower fee."
will → would · can → couldFuture and ability move back too."I will call." → "He said that he would call."
I, my → he, his / she, herThe person changes."My report is late." → "She said that her report was late."

The five-second test: is there a person right after the verb? Then it is told. No person? Then it is said. And in conversation you can leave out that: "He said he was busy."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are passing on messages at work. Choose the best thing to say.

Paul said, "The fund is ready." You tell a colleague:
A Paul said that the fund was ready.
B Paul said me that the fund is ready for the clients in March.
C Paul told that the fund was ready.
A client said to you, "I want a lower fee." You tell your manager:
A The client told that he want a lower fee next year for his account.
B The client told me that he wanted a lower fee.
C The client said me that he wanted a lower fee.
Rachel said, "I will forward the minutes." You tell Sophie:
A Rachel said that she will forwarded the minutes to everybody after lunch.
B Rachel told that she would forward the minutes.
C Rachel said that she would forward the minutes.
Mr. Alves said, "I can't come on Friday." You tell Tom:
A Mr. Alves said me that he can not come on Friday, because he is traveling.
B Mr. Alves told me that he could not come on Friday.
C Mr. Alves told that he could not come.
You end a call with a client and want to check that you both understand the plan:
A I just want to be sure we are on the same page.
B I just want to be sure we are in the same page.
C I just want to be sure that we are on the same paper before I send you the follow-up.
