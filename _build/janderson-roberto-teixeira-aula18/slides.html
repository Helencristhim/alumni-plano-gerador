Chapter 1: The Meeting

The Meeting You Missed

Where You Start

What He Said, What You Pass On

What Paul said

"I am ready. I will call the clients."

What you pass on

"Paul said that he was ready and that he would call the clients."

The difference

The person changes, and the verb moves back in time

You do this every week: after a meeting, after a call, after an email. Today you learn the two verbs and the one change that make it sound natural.

Chapter 3: The Code

Said That, Told Me That

Two verbs and one small change in time

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Paul said that the fund was ready."

"She told me that the report was too long."

"He said that he would call this week."

"They told her that they could not find the numbers."

Look at the words after said and after told. Then look at the blue words. What happened to the verbs?

FormWhat it saysExample
say (that) + sentenceNo person after said."Paul said that he was busy."
tell + person + (that)Tell always needs a person: me, us, her, the client."Paul told me that he was busy."
am / is → was · are → wereThe verb be moves back."I am ready." → "She said that she was ready."
present simple → past simplewant → wanted, know → knew."I want a lower fee." → "He said that he wanted a lower fee."
will → would · can → couldFuture and ability move back too."I can't come." → "He said that he could not come."
I, my → he, his / she, herThe person changes to the one you are talking about."I will call." → "She said that she would call."

In conversation, that is often left out: "He said he was busy." Both are correct.

Comprehension

About Rachel

1. What did Rachel tell Janderson about the new fund?
She told him that it was ready and that it would launch in March.
2. What did Sophie point out, according to Rachel?
That the minimum investment was too high for some clients.
3. What did the clients tell Rachel?
They told her that the monthly report was too long.
4. What did Paul promise, according to Rachel?
A shorter version of the report by April.
Listening

Say Something, Tell Somebody

A short radio tip for the office. Sound first -- no text.

1. What are the two verbs for passing on what people said?
Say and tell.
2. What is the difference between them, according to the speaker?
You say something, but you tell somebody something.
3. How do you report "I will call you"?
He said that he would call.

The Habit

One Message, Passed On

Before the next class, after one meeting or call each day, say out loud one sentence about it: the client said that, my manager told me that, they promised that.

You pass on messages every day at work. Doing it in English takes one sentence and one minute.
