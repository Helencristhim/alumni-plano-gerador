Opening the Presentation

Get ready to open a presentation. Key words: audience, slide, topic, purpose, overview, main point, handout, feedback. Structure: signposts to start, change and finish a talk (to begin with, moving on to, let's turn to, finally, to sum up).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

8 words and expressions for this lesson. Tap Listen to hear each one.

Audience -- the people who watch and listen to a presentation
"Our audience today is the board of Harbor Fresh."
Slide -- one page of a presentation on the screen
"On this slide, you can see the new containers."
Topic -- the subject that you talk about
"The topic of my talk is the Cold Chain Project."
Purpose -- the reason why you do something
"The purpose of this talk is to show our progress."
Overview -- a short description of the main parts of something
"Let me give you a quick overview."
Main point -- one of the most important ideas in a talk
"I have three main points today."
Handout -- a printed page that you give to the audience
"You can find all the numbers in the handout."
Feedback -- comments about your work that help you improve
"Claire gave me some useful feedback on my slides."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the opening of Rafael's presentation, then answer the questions.

Good morning, everyone, and thank you for coming. My name is Rafael Gasparelli Lima, and I'm a director at Picunha Group. The topic of my presentation is the Cold Chain Project, and the purpose of this talk is to show our progress.

Let me give you a quick overview. I have three main points. To begin with, I'll talk about the new containers. Then I'll move on to the temperature tests. Finally, we'll look at the costs and the next steps. You can find all the numbers in the handout. There will be time for questions at the end. So, let's begin with the containers.

1. What is the purpose of Rafael's talk?
A To show the progress of the project.
B To sell new containers to a different client.
C To ask the audience for feedback on the slides.
2. Which signpost does Rafael use for his FIRST point?
A Finally
B Then I'll move on to
C To begin with
3. When can the audience ask questions?
A During the overview, after each one of the three points.
B At the end of the talk.
C Before the talk starts.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Signposts

GRAMMAR

Signposts are words that show the audience where you are in the talk: the start, a change, or the end.

JobSignpostExample
StartTo begin with, / First of all,First of all, thank you for coming.
Show the planI have three main points.the containers, then the tests, and finally the costs
Change the pointMoving on to + noun / Let's turn to + nounLet's turn to the costs.
FinishFinally, / To sum up,To sum up, we are on track.

Never: in first place · resuming (for "to sum up") · passing to the next point · present about

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose what a good presenter would say in each situation.

You start your presentation. You say:
A "In first place, I want to thank all of you very much for coming."
B "Resuming, thank you for coming."
C "First of all, thank you for coming."
You finish point one and start point two. You say:
A "Passing to the tests, which are the second point of my talk."
B "Moving on to the tests."
C "To sum up, the tests."
You want to explain the reason for your talk. You say:
A "The purpose of this talk is to get a decision."
B "The topic of this talk is because we need a decision today."
C "The handout of this talk is a decision."
You give the last sentence of your talk. You say:
A "To begin with, the project is on track and our clients are happy."
B "To sum up, the project is on track."
C "Moving on to the project, which is on track."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Think of a real presentation that you will give at Picunha or at a legal event. In one minute, record your opening: thank the audience, say the topic and the purpose, give an overview of your main points with to begin with, then and finally, and say when people can ask questions.
