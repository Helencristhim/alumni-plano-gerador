Chapter 3: Signposts

Signs for the Audience

Words that show where you are in the talk

Grammar Discovery

Start, Change, Finish

To begin with, I’ll talk about the containers.

Moving on to the second point: the tests.

Let’s turn to the costs now.

To sum up, the project is on track.

Which words start the talk? Which words change the point? Which words finish it?

JobSignpostExample
StartTo begin with, / First of all,First of all, thank you for coming.
Show the planI have three main points.Then the tests, and finally the costs.
Change the pointMoving on to + noun / Let’s turn to + nounMoving on to the tests.
FinishFinally, / To sum up,To sum up, we are on track.

Start = to begin with. Change = moving on to / let’s turn to + noun. Finish = to sum up.

Chapter 4: The Rehearsal

Practice Before Thursday

One rehearsal, two openings, one plan

Dialogue

Can I Hear Your Opening?

C
So, Rafael, the presentation to our directors is on Thursday. Can I hear your opening?
R
Sure. Good morning, everyone, and thank you for coming. My name is Rafael, from Picunha Group.
C
Good start. Now tell them the purpose of the talk.
R
The purpose of this presentation is to show the progress of the Cold Chain Project.
C
Perfect. Then give a short overview. Our audience likes a clear plan.
R
I have three main points. To begin with, the containers. Then the tests. Finally, the costs.
C
Very clear. Do you want questions during the talk, or at the end?
R
At the end, please. I’ll also send a handout with all the numbers.
C
Good idea. One piece of feedback: don’t read your slides. Look at the people.
R
Thanks, Claire. That’s really helpful. I’ll practice again tonight.
