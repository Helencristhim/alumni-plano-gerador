How the Program Was Built

How the Program Was Built

The program was designed in Brazil, by a team of three people, and it was never meant to travel. A first version was tested in two sites for six months, and the findings were not the ones anybody expected. The training was not the problem. The problem was that nothing had ever been standardized: every manager was doing something different, and each of them was convinced that his version was the company standard. Once the process was streamlined and written down, the same results were reproduced in the other sites without anybody from the original team being present.

That is the part of the story that gets copied, and it is also the part that is usually left out of the document. Most case studies say what was done. Very few say why it was done, and the reader who has to repeat it in another country needs the rationale more than the steps. This one was rewritten twice for that reason. It was cut to four pages, the reasoning behind each decision was made explicit, and only then was it published. It has since been adopted by three regions, and the framework has been embedded in the onboarding of every new manager.

Grammar Discovery

What Is Missing from All Four?

The first version was tested in two sites for six months.
Nothing had ever been standardized.
The case study has been adopted by three regions.
The document has to be cut to four pages.
Click to reveal the rule
The passive voice: the right form of be + the past participle. The tense lives in be, never in the participle — is written, was written, has been written, had been written, will be written, has to be written. Use it when the ACTION matters more than the person: in reports, procedures and case studies, that is almost always. The person can still come back at the end with by — designed by a team of three — and you add that only when the author is part of the information. Two traps for a Portuguese speaker: the participle is irregular half the time (written, given, taken, shown), and English does not use se constructions, so se fez becomes it was done.

GLOBAL PEOPLE CONFERENCE · OCTOBER

CASE STUDY — BRAZIL

Context: the program was designed in Brazil and was never meant to travel.

Method: a first version was tested in two sites for six months.

Findings: the process had never been standardized; the training was not the problem.

Rationale: the framework was written down so that it could be reproduced without us.

Outcome: it has been adopted by three regions.

FIVE FIELDS · NO NAMES

Preparing the case study for the global conference

Whose Document Is It?

M
Congratulations. Your case study has been selected for October. Now it has to be cut to four pages.
A
Four is fine. What is being cut, though? If the method goes, the whole thing becomes a story about us.
M
The feedback was that the reader is told what was done, but not why it was done.
A
That is fair. The rationale is in my head, not on the page. It can be added without making the document longer.
M
Good. And four people from the global team want to review it before it is published.
A
May I ask what each of them is reviewing? Four reviewers with no scope is how a document gets edited until nobody can tell who wrote it.
M
Two for content, two for language. Does that work?
A
It does. My name is on it, so I would like the final version to be approved by me before it goes out.

Somebody asks who designed the program, and the point you want to make is about the program, not the people.

  • The passive lets you put the program first.
  • You can still add the team at the end, with by.

  • Past passive: was plus the past participle.
  • Nobody needs the agent here.

The global team asks what has happened to the case study since last year.

  • Present perfect passive: has been plus the participle.
  • Three regions is the number you want in the sentence.

You want to say the document still has to be cut to four pages.

  • Modal plus be plus participle.
  • Say the deadline in the same sentence.

  • Say what you do not want done to it.
  • Your name is on it: say that too.
