Complementary Activities

Optional extras to enjoy outside class -- podcasts, audiobooks and music only. Mark each one as done after you listen to it.

Podcast
NPR Life Kit -- "Here’s how to get the most out of your doctor’s appointment"

Eighteen minutes on the consultation seen from the other chair: how patients are told to prepare, what they are told to say, and what to do when they feel the doctor is not hearing them. Clear American English, and the full transcript sits on the same page.

Tip: listen once without the transcript. Then read it and write down every question a patient is advised to ask. In your next clinic, notice which of those questions you already answer without being asked.

Audiobook
Danielle Ofri -- "What Patients Say, What Doctors Hear" (audiobook preview)

A physician writing about the exact hour you practised today: the interview as the strongest diagnostic tool in medicine, and the distance between what the patient says and what the doctor writes down. The free preview is around ten minutes of the opening chapter, read at a comfortable speed.

Tip: listen twice. The second time, stop after every sentence the author quotes from a patient and say it out loud yourself, copying the rhythm.

Music
Bill Withers -- "Lean on Me"

Three minutes, very clear American diction, and a lyric built almost entirely on the present simple: we all have pain, we all have sorrow, sometimes in our lives we all have pain. It is the tense of this lesson, sung slowly enough to hear every ending.

Tip: read the lyrics while you listen and underline every verb. Almost all of them are present simple, and that is the point -- the song says what is always true, never what is happening right now.
