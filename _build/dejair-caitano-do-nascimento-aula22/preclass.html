Lesson 22 -- Pre-class

Your Bangkok Moment -- Five High-Stakes Scenes

The real congress in real time: the registration line, two questions at once in the workshop, a doubtful number at your poster, a skeptical colleague and the follow-up. Key words: fair question, limitation, to back up, to concede, common ground, time slot, to think on your feet, to sum up. Structure: review -- five steps for a hard question.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These are words for the moments when the clock is running and people are looking at you.

Fair question -- a question that is reasonable and correct to ask
"That's a fair question. Let me explain how we measured pain."
Limitation -- a weak point of a study that the researchers know about
"The main limitation of our study is the small sample."
To back up -- to give evidence that shows something is true
"Can you back up that number with data from other centers?"
To concede -- to accept that the other person is right about one point
"I concede that the sample is small, but the pattern is clear."
Common ground -- ideas or interests that two people share
"We found common ground: we both work on nerve damage."
Time slot -- a short period of time in a program for one talk or activity
"My time slot is ten minutes, at half past two."
To think on your feet -- to think and answer quickly, without time to prepare
"In the Q&A, you have to think on your feet."
To sum up -- to say the main points again in a short way
"To sum up, the test is cheap, fast and useful."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Marta is a pain researcher from Belém. At her first congress abroad, she had a ten-minute time slot in a workshop. At the end, a man asked her a question with a word she did not know. She did not panic. She said: "Sorry, could you tell me what you mean by attrition?" He explained: it means patients who leave a study. Marta said: "So you're asking if many patients left, is that right?" Then she answered: "That's a fair question. I concede it is a limitation. I'm not sure, but the number might be lower in the next study, because we now call every patient each month. If you're interested, I'll send you the protocol." After the session, the man came to talk to her. Today they work on the same project.
Marta says "So you're asking if many patients left, is that right?" Why?
A To check the question and get a few seconds to think
B To change the topic of the conversation, because she does not want to talk about her patients in front of the audience
C To ask the man a new question
Why does Marta say "the number might be lower", not "the number is lower"?
A Because she wants to finish her answer quickly, so she uses the shortest verb she knows for the future
B Because she is not sure, so she uses hedging
C Because "might" is the past of "may"

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Five Steps for a Hard Question

GRAMMAR

No new grammar today: this is the order of the tools you already know. A hard question has five steps.

1. repairindirect question: subject + verbCould you tell me what you mean? (not what do you mean)
2. checkSo you're asking if... is that right?So you're asking if the effect lasts, is that right?
3. hedgemight / may / could + verbIt might be lower in older patients.
4. explainbecause / that's because / soThe answers were reliable, because a nurse read the questions.
5. inviteif + present, will + verbIf you're interested, I'll send you the protocol.

Steps 1 and 2 give you time to think. You do not need all five every time -- but this is the order.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
The moderator asks you two questions at once. What do you say first?
A "So you're asking two things. Let me start with the first."
B "I will answer the second question first, because it is easier, and then maybe the first one later."
C "Next question, please."
A researcher at your poster says you had no control group. It is true. What is the best answer?
A "That is not important at all, because everybody in my group thinks that our study is perfect."
B "You are wrong."
C "That's a fair question. I concede it is a limitation, and we want one in the next study."
Your time slot is almost over. What do you say?
A "To sum up, the test is fast and useful."
B "I have twelve more slides, so I will speak very fast and show you all of them now."
C "Finished."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Two minutes. Imagine you are at your poster in Bangkok. A researcher says: "Your number looks too high. How can you back it up?" Answer with the five steps: repair if you need to, check the question, concede what is true, explain with because, and invite the researcher to continue. No script -- just speak.
