Lesson 26 -- Pre-class

The Award -- Reporting Verbs

How to read an arbitral award, or a news story, and see who says what: a side contends, a tribunal finds. Key words: an award, to uphold, to dismiss, a finding, to be entitled to, unfounded, to skim, the fine print, verbatim, at face value, to read too much into, tellingly. Structure: reporting verbs and their patterns -- contends that, denies + -ing, finds that, holds that, rejects + noun, admits that.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An award -- the final written decision of an arbitral tribunal
"The award arrived by email at six in the evening."
To uphold -- to accept that a claim or a decision is correct
"The tribunal upheld two of our three claims."
To dismiss -- to reject a claim or a case officially
"The claim for lost profits was dismissed."
A finding -- a fact or conclusion that a tribunal or a study decides after looking at the evidence
"The key finding is on page twelve."
To be entitled to -- to have the right to have or do something
"The buyer is entitled to a full refund."
Unfounded -- not based on facts or evidence
"The rumors about the merger were completely unfounded."
To skim -- to read something quickly to get the main idea
"I skimmed the report on the train and read it properly at home."
The fine print -- the details in a document that are easy to miss, but often important
"Always read the fine print before you sign."
Verbatim -- using exactly the same words
"She quoted his email verbatim in her reply."
At face value -- as something first seems, without asking whether it is really true
"Don't take a headline at face value."
To read too much into -- to think that something has more meaning than it really has
"He didn't reply today, but I wouldn't read too much into it."
Tellingly -- in a way that shows something important
"Tellingly, nobody from the board came to the meeting."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The award arrived on Thursday evening. Rodrigo skimmed the last page first, the way every lawyer does, and then went back to the beginning. Paragraph 41 was the one he wanted: "The Tribunal finds that the machines did not reach the agreed speed." Brenner had denied breaching the contract for two years, and the tribunal rejected its training argument as unfounded. Tellingly, it quoted Brenner's own engineer verbatim: he had admitted that the software had a defect.

But the fine print was less happy. The tribunal held that the liability cap applied, and it dismissed the claim for lost profits above it. When the CFO called, Rodrigo did not let her take the result at face value. "We won," he said, "but we won one and a half million, not four."

1. "The Tribunal finds that the machines did not reach the agreed speed." What does finds tell you?
A That this is only the claimant's position, which Brenner contested at the hearing and the tribunal will examine later.
B That the tribunal is not sure.
C That the tribunal accepts it as a fact.
2. Why did Rodrigo not let the CFO take the result at face value?
A Because Atlântica won much less money than it claimed.
B Because Atlântica lost the case.
C Because the award had not been signed by all three arbitrators and could still change before Monday.
3. "Brenner had denied ___ the contract for two years." Which form is correct?
A to breach
B breaching
C that breach

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reporting Verbs

GRAMMAR

The reporting verb tells you who is speaking and whether the writer believes it. Each verb also has its own pattern. To report a document, use the present simple.

JobVerbs and patternExample
A side’s positioncontends / argues / maintains / claims + thatThe Claimant contends that the machines were slow.
The decisionfinds / holds / concludes + thatThe Tribunal holds that Brenner is in breach.
Saying nodenies + -ing · rejects + nounBrenner denies breaching the contract.
Yes to something negativeadmits + that / + -ingThe engineer admitted that there was a defect.
How sure is the writer?claims (distance) · suggests (not strong yet) · shows / finds (accepted)The study suggests that coffee may help.
Reporting a documentpresent simpleThe award states that interest runs from May.

Never: denies to breach · contends the Tribunal that · the award says me · suggested him to pay

Stage 2: Put the Summary in Order

Order

A good summary of an award goes from what the parties say to what the tribunal decides. Put the sentences in that order.

?"It holds, however, that the liability cap applies."
?"The Tribunal finds that the machines never reached the agreed speed."
?"The claim is therefore upheld in part."
?"The Claimant contends that the machines were too slow."
?"The Respondent denies breaching the contract."
?"It rejects the Respondent's training argument as unfounded."

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen, then record yourself. Stress the reporting verb.

The Tribunal holds that the contract is valid.
The respondent denies knowing about the problem.
The claimant contends that the delay was deliberate.
The Tribunal rejects the argument that the cap does not apply.
In short, the award finds in our favor on two points out of three.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose what a careful reader would say.

A friend sends you the headline "Chocolate makes you smarter, study proves." What do you say?
A "If a study proves it, it must be true, because scientists always check everything very carefully before they publish."
B "The study denies to be wrong."
C "I'd read the study first. It probably only suggests a link."
You report the other side's position to your client, without agreeing with it. You say:
A "Brenner contends that the cap applies."
B "Brenner shows that the cap applies."
C "Brenner has proved, with all the documents in the file, that the cap applies."
At the hearing, the engineer said: "Yes, the software had a defect." You report it:
A "The engineer denied that the software had a defect."
B "The engineer admitted that the software had a defect."
C "The engineer admitted to have a defect in the software, as he explained at the hearing in May."
The tribunal accepted part of your client's claim. You tell the client:
A "The tribunal upheld the claim in part."
B "The tribunal dismissed everything."
C "The tribunal upheld everything we asked for, including all of the lost profits and the costs."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Think of a decision, a report or a news story you read this week (no client names). Record a sixty-second summary: what each side or source says, and what the decision or the writer concludes. Use at least four reporting verbs with two different patterns (verb + that, deny or admit + -ing), and choose each verb carefully: does the writer believe it, or not?
