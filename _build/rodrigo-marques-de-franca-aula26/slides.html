Chapter 4: The Code

The Verb Takes a Side

Reporting verbs: who says it, and who believes it

Grammar Discovery

Who Says What?

"The Claimant contends that the machines never reached the agreed speed."

"The Respondent denies breaching the contract."

"The Tribunal finds that the machines did not reach the agreed speed."

"The Tribunal rejects the argument that the cap does not apply."

Who is speaking in each sentence? Does the Tribunal believe it? What comes after each red verb?

JobVerbs and patternExample
A side’s position (not proven)contends / argues / maintains / claims / alleges + thatThe Claimant contends that the machines were slow.
The decisionfinds / holds / concludes + thatThe Tribunal holds that Brenner is in breach.
Accepted, neutralnotes / observes / accepts + that or + nounThe Tribunal notes that the emails describe the defect.
Saying nodenies + -ing · rejects + nounBrenner denies breaching the contract. The Tribunal rejects the argument.
Yes to something negativeadmits + that or + -ingThe engineer admitted that the software had a defect.

The trapsno person after contend / say · -ing after denyNOT denies to breach · NOT contends the Tribunal that · NOT the award says me

Real Document

A Story in the Paper

The Morning Ledger · Business · 3 min read · Fictional newspaper, written for this lesson

Four-Day Week Leaves Staff Happier, Study Finds

A six-month trial at forty companies found that employees who worked four days a week took fewer sick days and reported lower stress. The researchers claim that productivity did not fall, although they admit that most companies measured it in their own way. Critics argue that the trial included only firms that wanted to take part. One retailer, which left the trial after three months, denies having any problems with its staff, and insists that it simply needed more people on Fridays. The authors suggest that larger trials are needed before governments act.

1. Which result does the journalist present as accepted? Which verb shows it?
"Fewer sick days and lower stress. The verb is found: the trial found that..."
2. Does the journalist fully believe the productivity result? How do you know?
"Not fully. The researchers claim it, and they admit that companies measured it in their own way."
3. What does "denies having any problems" tell you about the retailer?
"That someone thinks it had problems with its staff, and the retailer says no."
4. Find one verb + that and one verb + -ing in the story.
"Argue that, suggest that, insists that; denies having."

"The study ___finds that coffee drinkers live a little longer." (presented as an accepted result; it starts with f)
Listening 1 · The Science Journalist

Shows, Claims, Suggests

A science journalist, on the small verbs in every story. Sound first — no text.

1. What is she telling the reader when she writes that a study shows something?
That she believes it.
2. What did the coffee study actually find?
That people who drank coffee lived a little longer, which is not the same as proving that coffee helps you live longer.
3. What is her advice to readers?
Find the reporting verb first, because it tells you how sure the writer is.
Listening 2 · The Fact-Checker

Did He Admit It?

A magazine fact-checker, on choosing the right verb. Sound first — no text.

1. What does admitted mean, according to him?
That the person accepted something negative about himself.
2. What can happen when a writer chooses an unfair verb?
The magazine may get a letter from the person's lawyer.
3. When do they not use quotation marks?
When the quote isn't verbatim, word for word. Then they report it.

"The Claimant contends that the delay was deliberate." (no person after contend)

"The award says that we won two points." (say + that; tell + person)
"The Tribunal suggested Brenner to pay the costs."
"The Tribunal ordered Brenner to pay the costs." (order + person + to; suggest that + clause)

Chapter 7: Wrap-Up

Five Sentences for Reporting a Decision

Survival Card

Five Phrases for an Award

The Tribunal holds that the contract is valid.
The respondent denies knowing about the problem.
The claimant contends that the delay was deliberate.
The Tribunal rejects the argument that the cap does not apply.
In short, the award finds in our favor on two points out of three.
Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I read an award and separate what each side contends from what the tribunal finds.
I use reporting verbs with the right pattern: contends that, denies breaching, rejects the argument.
I choose the verb that shows how sure the writer is: claims, suggests, shows, finds.
I report what someone said fairly, without a dramatic verb.
I know the words: an award, to uphold, to dismiss, a finding, the fine print, verbatim.
