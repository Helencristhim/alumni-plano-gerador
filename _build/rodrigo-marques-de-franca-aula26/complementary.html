Lesson 26 -- The Award

Talk
Damon Brown -- "How to choose your news" (TED-Ed, 5 min)

A short animated lesson on how to read the news critically: who is the source, what do they actually know, and which words show how sure the writer is. The same habit as the science journalist in this lesson.

Tip: every time the narrator reports what someone says, note the verb he chooses, and ask yourself whether it shows belief or distance.

Film
Spotlight (2015)

A team of Boston Globe journalists investigates a scandal and has to prove every claim before it can print a single word. The film is full of the language of reporting: who said it, who denied it and what the documents show.

Tip: listen for said, claimed, denied and confirmed in the editors’ meetings, and notice why one verb is safe to print and another is not.

Podcast
Freshfields -- "The Arbitration Act 2025 is now in force. What are the key things you need to know?"

A short episode in which three London arbitration lawyers explain the reforms to the English Arbitration Act, including summary dismissal and the law that governs arbitration agreements. Clear, professional and close to the award you read in this lesson.

Tip: write down every verb they use to report what the Act or the courts say (provides, states, holds, makes clear) and compare them with the ones in this lesson.
