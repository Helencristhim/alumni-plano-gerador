Grammar Review

What Job Do the Purple Words Do?

"Speaking of long journeys, shall we get down to business?"

"It seems the price might be slightly below what the family expected."

"If you kept the whole team, Victor would accept a lower price."

"Wouldn't you agree that a loyal team is part of what you are buying?"

What job does each one do? Which lesson is it from?

JobFormExample
Open or change topic (L24)Speaking of / Before + -ing"Before getting into the price, ..."
Soften (L22)It seems / might / could + base verb"The price might be slightly low."
Propose (L21)If + past, would + base verb"If you kept the team, we would accept."
Persuade (L23)Wouldn't you...? / Why not + base verb?"Why not agree on the principles today?"
Neverwould after if / to after why not or beforeNot "If you would keep" / "Why not to sign"

Chapter 4 -- Getting There

NorthPack: The Buyer

One negotiation, two recordings, one summary

Dialogue

Eduarda and Mark

M
Eduarda, good to see you again. How was the flight from Brazil?
E
Long, but worth it. Speaking of long journeys, this sale is a big step for Victor. Shall we get down to business?
M
Let's. Our offer is on the table: ninety million, with sixty days of exclusivity.
E
Thank you. It seems the price might be slightly below what the family expected.
M
What would it take to bridge the gap?
E
If you kept the whole team for three years, Victor would accept a lower price. For him, job losses are a deal-breaker.
M
Three years is long. I can offer two, provided that the factory hits its targets.
E
Wouldn't you agree that a loyal team is part of what you're buying? For the family, three years is non-negotiable.
M
Fair point. But I see one red flag: your largest client's contract ends next year.
E
Let's circle back to that tomorrow, with the data. Victor has the final say, but why not agree on the main principles today, while we have momentum?
Comprehension

About Mark

1. What is Mark's offer?
Ninety million, with sixty days of exclusivity.
2. How long does Mark offer to keep the team, and on what condition?
Two years, provided that the factory hits its targets.
3. What red flag does Mark see?
The contract with the largest client ends next year.

Real Document

The Deal Update

From: Eduarda Gabriel, Houlihan Lokey
To: Victor Lane, Founder, Lane Packaging
Re: NorthPack Meeting -- Where We Are

Dear Victor,

NorthPack put ninety million on the table today, with sixty days of exclusivity. It seems the price might still be a little low, but they are clearly interested.

They offered two years of job protection. I made it clear that three years is non-negotiable for you. If they accepted three years, I believe they would ask for a slightly lower price, and we could bridge the gap with an earn-out.

They raised one red flag: the contract with your largest client. Wouldn't you agree that a short call with that client this week would help? You have the final say, of course.

Best regards,
Eduarda

1. How many years of job protection did NorthPack offer?
Two years.
2. How could they bridge the gap on price, according to Eduarda?
With an earn-out.
3. What does Eduarda suggest about the red flag?
A short call with the largest client this week.
