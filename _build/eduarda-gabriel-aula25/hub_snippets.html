Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Deal-breaker -- a condition that, if it is not met, makes you stop the deal
"For the family, job losses are a deal-breaker."
Red flag -- a sign that something may be wrong or risky
"A falling client base is a red flag for any buyer."
On the table -- offered, and ready to be discussed
"Their offer of ninety million is on the table."
Non-negotiable -- cannot be changed or discussed
"Three years of job protection is non-negotiable."
Bridge the gap -- to reduce the difference between two positions
"A longer earn-out could bridge the gap on price."
Final say -- the right to make the last decision
"The founder has the final say on the sale."
Momentum -- the energy that keeps a process moving forward
"Let's keep the momentum and meet again this week."
Exclusivity -- an agreement that only one buyer can negotiate for a period of time
"They want sixty days of exclusivity."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Eduarda meets Mark Holloway, the CEO of NorthPack, to negotiate the sale of Lane Packaging. After a little small talk, she says: Speaking of long journeys, shall we get down to business? Mark puts ninety million on the table and asks for sixty days of exclusivity.

Eduarda does not say no. She softens her answer: It seems the price might be slightly below what the family expected. Then she makes a proposal: If you kept the whole team for three years, Victor would accept a lower price. For the family, job losses are a deal-breaker, and three years is non-negotiable.

Mark sees a red flag in the client contracts. Eduarda asks: Wouldn't you agree that a loyal team is part of what you are buying? Victor has the final say, but they agree on the main principles and keep the momentum. An earn-out could help them bridge the gap.

1. How does Eduarda answer the offer of ninety million?
A She says no immediately and asks Mark to come back next month with a much better offer.
B She softens her answer and makes a proposal.
C She accepts it right away.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "If you kept the whole team, Victor would accept a lower price."
B "If you would keep the whole team for three years, Victor would accept a lower price."
C "If you keep the team, Victor would accepted."
3. Who has the final say on the sale?
A Mark Holloway, because NorthPack is the company that is paying for Lane Packaging.
B Eduarda.
C Victor.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Review: Four Tools for a Deal

GRAMMAR

No new grammar in this lesson. You combine the four structures from lessons 21 to 24 in one meeting.

JobFormExample
Open (L24)Speaking of + noun / Before + -ingBefore getting into the price, ...
Soften (L22)It seems + might / could + base verbThe price might be slightly low.
Propose (L21)If + past simple, would + base verbIf you kept the team, we would accept.
Set a condition (L21)Provided that + present simpleProvided that the factory hits its targets, ...
Persuade (L23)Wouldn't you...? / Why not + base verb?Why not agree on the principles today?
Next stepLet's + base verb / Shall we...?Let's meet again on Thursday.

Never: If you would keep · The price might low · Why not to sign · Before to start · Provided that it will

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose what a skilled negotiator would say.

The buyer's offer is too low. You want to keep the conversation open. You say:
A "It seems the price might be slightly low."
B "This price is wrong and we will not accept it, so please make a new offer today."
C "The price might low."
You would accept less money if the buyer kept the team. You say:
A "If you would keep the team for three years, we would accept a lower price for the company."
B "If you kept the team, we would accept a lower price."
C "You keep team, we accept."
The buyer raises a red flag and you don't have the data with you. You say:
A "That's not true. Our client contracts are perfect, and you should trust us on this."
B "No comment."
C "Fair point. Can we circle back to it tomorrow, with the data?"
The meeting is ending and you want to keep the deal moving. You say:
A "Why not meet again on Thursday?"
B "Why not to meet again on Thursday, while we still have some momentum in this deal?"
C "Bye. Call us."
