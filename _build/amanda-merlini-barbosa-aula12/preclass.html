The Short Verbs That Run the Deal

The follow-up after the call, and a call of your own. Key words: to look into, to draw up, to carry out, to turn down, to put forward, to bring up, to rule out, to fall through. Structure: phrasal verbs -- separable, inseparable, and where the pronoun goes.

Stage 1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Eight short verbs that run a negotiation. Listen to each one and read it in context.

to look into -- to investigate; to try to find out the facts
"We have looked into your concern about clause 7.2."
to draw up -- to prepare a written document, such as a contract or a plan
"I will draw up a revised version of clause 7."
to carry out -- to do a task that was planned, such as an audit or a test
"The verification body will carry out the site visit in May."
to turn down -- to say no to an offer or a request
"My client has not turned the compromise down."
to put forward -- to suggest an idea or a plan so that others can consider it
"Your commercial director put forward a compromise."
to bring up -- to mention a subject so that people discuss it
"Thank you for bringing up the exclusivity point so early."
to rule out -- to decide that something is not possible or will not be considered
"My client has ruled out any change to the fixed price."
to fall through -- to fail before it is completed (a deal or a plan)
"I would not like the deal to fall through over the price."

Stage 3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about what it says.

Read a contract and you will find formal verbs: the parties investigate, prepare, conduct and reject. Listen to the people who negotiate that contract and you will hear something else: they look into a problem, draw up a clause, carry out a visit and turn down an offer.

Most of these phrasal verbs are separable. With a noun, you can say "draw up the clause" or "draw the clause up". With a pronoun, there is only one option: the pronoun goes in the middle. Ingrid will draw it up. The buyer has not turned it down. The board has ruled it out. Never "draw up it".

A few are inseparable. The object always comes after the whole verb, pronoun included: we have looked into it, never "looked it into".

And some have no object at all. A deal can fall through, but you cannot fall it through. In a meeting, the short verb sounds natural; in the contract itself, the formal verb is still the right choice.

1. According to the text, where does the pronoun go with a separable phrasal verb?

B In the middle, between the verb and the small word.
C Before the verb.
2. Which sentence follows the rule for look into?
A We have looked into it.
B We have looked it into, and we will send our answer.
C We have into looked it.
3. According to the text, when is the formal verb still the right choice?
A In a phone call with a partner you know well.

Stage 4: Grammar Tip -- phrasal verbs

GRAMMAR

Separable, inseparable or with no object: the type decides where the pronoun goes.

TypeVerbsExample
separable + noundraw up, carry out, turn down, put forward, bring up, rule outdraw up the clause / draw the clause up
separable + pronounthe pronoun goes in the middleIngrid will draw it up. Never draw up it.
inseparablelook into, follow up on, sign off onWe have looked into it.
no objectfall through, walk awayThe deal could fall through.
Negative and questionthe order does not change: "They have not turned it down." "Have you looked into it?"
Registercontract: investigate, prepare, conduct, reject, propose, raise, exclude. Meeting and email: the phrasal verb.

Quick test before you speak: is the object a pronoun? Then it goes in the middle, unless the verb is look into.
