Where does it go in each sentence? And why does the last one have no object at all?

TypeVerbsExample
separabledraw up, carry out, turn down, put forward, bring up, rule outdraw up the clause / draw the clause up

Sound Check

Say It Clearly

the stress on the particle · the sentences of this lesson

Why this one is hard for a Portuguese speaker

The habit: stressing the verb and saying the small word quietly, so that turn it down sounds like TURN it down.

In English: the stress goes on the particle: turn it DOWN, draw it UP, rule it OUT. The pronoun in the middle is short and weak, and the t of it often sounds like a quick d.

The test: say "carry it out" as one word, with the strongest beat on out.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

The Board and the Room

What the board decided, and the verbs people really use in a meeting

Listening 1

After the Board

Rafael leaves a voicemail after the board meeting. Sound first, no text.

1. How did the board react to the six-month idea?
Nobody turned it down, which Rafael sees as good news.
2. What question could Rafael not answer, and what does he ask Amanda to do?
Whether they can sell the credits to somebody else immediately if the buyer backs out after the site visit. He asks her to look into it today.
3. What has the board ruled out, and what does the finance team want to do?
Any discount on the price. The finance team wants to carry out its own review of the payment dates before signing.
Listening 2

The Document and the Room

A trainer on formal verbs and phrasal verbs. Sound first, no text.

1. Why does she say the idea that phrasal verbs are informal is only half true?
Because the contract uses formal verbs, but in the meeting about the contract people use phrasal verbs.
2. What happens if you only use the formal verbs in a meeting?
You sound like you are reading the agreement out loud.
3. Which detail gives non-native speakers away?
The position of the pronoun: draw it up, never draw up it. With look into, the pronoun stays at the end.
