Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read it inside a real sentence. These are the words the exam text will use.

Compliance -- the fact of following what a rule requires
"The ship was in compliance with the rule."
Liability -- legal responsibility for damage, loss or injury
"The flag state has liability for the certificate it gave."
Jurisdiction -- the legal power a state has over a place, a ship or a person
"The ship is under the jurisdiction of her flag state."
Amendment -- an official change to a legal text
"The amendment started eighteen months after it was adopted."
Ratification -- the official act by which a state accepts a treaty
"Ratification by thirty states was needed."
Annex -- an extra section of a convention with the detailed technical rules
"The technical rules are in the annex."
Convention -- an international agreement between states, such as SOLAS
"The convention gives the duties of flag states."
Protocol -- a later document that adds to or changes a convention
"The protocol of 1988 changed the survey rules."
Provision -- one specific rule inside a legal text
"The port state checks this provision."
Enforcement -- the act of making people obey a rule, with a penalty if they do not
"Enforcement belongs to the port state."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Clefts and Relative Clauses (Reading)

GRAMMAR

A cleft points at one thing: It was X who..., What ... was X. A relative clause says which one: the state whose flag..., the society to which.... You only need to read them and find the noun.

FormWhat it doesExample
It was + X + who / thatthe writer points at X and excludes the othersIt was not the surveyor who made a mistake. = The surveyor did not make the mistake.
What + verb + was + XX comes at the end, where the emphasis isWhat stopped the ship was an amendment. = An amendment stopped the ship.
preposition + whichformal English: the preposition goes before whichthe society to which the owner sent the surveys = the society the owner sent the surveys to
whose + nounpossession; it works for states and companies toothe state whose flag she flies = the state of her flag
The reading trapa clause is not always about the nearest noun. Ask: which noun makes sense? A wrong option in the exam often attaches the clause to the wrong noun.

You do not need to write these structures. You need to read them and find the noun they are about.
