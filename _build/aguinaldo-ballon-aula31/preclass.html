Lesson 31 -- Pre-class

The Language of Negotiation -- Offers as Conditions

How to turn a demand into an offer at the negotiating table. Key words: a counteroffer, leverage, a sticking point, middle ground, to bargain, a walk-away point, a package deal, to meet halfway, non-negotiable, to sweeten the deal. Structure: conditional bargaining -- if you could..., we would be willing to; what if we added...; unless.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A counteroffer -- an offer you make in reply to an offer you do not accept
"Their counteroffer was better than we expected."
Leverage -- the power to influence the other side in a negotiation
"The delay in the right of way gives us leverage."
A sticking point -- the one issue that stops the two sides from agreeing
"The investment schedule is the main sticking point."
Middle ground -- a position between two positions, that both sides can accept
"Two years is a reasonable middle ground."
To bargain -- to discuss the conditions of a deal to get a better result
"We are ready to bargain on the timing, not on the total."
A walk-away point -- the point where you stop negotiating and leave without a deal
"Our walk-away point is a three-year delay."
A package deal -- an agreement where several issues are decided together, not one by one
"We would accept it only as part of a package deal."
To meet halfway -- to accept part of what the other side wants, if they do the same
"If you meet us halfway on the date, we can move on the penalty."
Non-negotiable -- not open to discussion; it cannot change
"The safety standards are non-negotiable."
To sweeten the deal -- to add something to an offer to make it more attractive
"To sweeten the deal, we could add a new berth."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At a negotiating table, a demand closes the door: "Give us two more years." An offer with a condition opens it: "If you could give us two more years, we would be willing to accept a penalty." While you are still exploring, use could and would: they say that nothing is decided yet.

To float a new idea, use what if with the past simple: "What if we added a new berth?" The past here does not mean the past. It means: this is only a hypothesis. To set a limit, use unless with the present: "We accept the date, unless the penalty is above five percent."

Only when you are ready to close do you switch to will: "If you meet us halfway, we will sign this week." And remember the rule from the beginning of the course: never put will in the if part. It is "if you give", never "if you will give".

1. When do you use could and would at the table?
A When you are still exploring and nothing is decided.
B When you want to close the deal with a firm promise that the other side can put in writing.
C When you want to refuse an offer.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "What if we add a new berth to the project, and pay for it with our own money?"
B "What if we added a new berth?"
C "What if we will add a new berth?"
3. Which sentence closes the deal?
A "If you could meet us halfway, we would think about it, maybe, after the next board meeting."
B "If you meet us halfway, we will sign this week."
C "If you will meet us halfway, we sign."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Conditional Bargaining

GRAMMAR

Explore with could and would. Close with will. Never will after if.

MoveFormExample
Exploreif you could..., we would...If you could give us two years, we would accept a penalty.
Flexibilitywe would be willing to + verbWe would be willing to discuss it.
Float an ideawhat if + past simple?What if we added a berth?
Limitunless + presentunless the penalty is above five percent
Closeif + present, will + verbIf you meet us halfway, we will sign.

Never: if you will give · what if we add (for a hypothesis) · willing discuss · unless it is not

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The agency asks what you propose. You are still exploring. You say:
A "If you could give us two more years, we would be willing to accept a penalty."
B "Give us two more years, and no penalty, because the delay was not our fault at all."
C "If you will give us two years, we accept."
You want to float a new idea without promising it. You say:
A "We will add a new berth."
B "What if we added a new berth?"
C "What if we will add a new berth to the project next year, and maybe a second one?"
Claire asks: "Is there anything you will not discuss?" You say:
A "Maybe. I am not sure. It depends on many things, and I need to talk to my client first about all of them."
B "Everything is negotiable."
C "The safety standards are non-negotiable. Everything else is on the table."
You are ready to close the deal. You say:
A "If you put it in writing today, we will sign this week."
B "If you would put it in writing, we could maybe think about signing it at some point."
C "If you will write it, we sign."
