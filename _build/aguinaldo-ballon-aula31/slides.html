Chapter 3: The Code

If You Could, We Would

Offers as conditions

Grammar Discovery

A Demand, or an Offer?

Demand: "Give us two more years." → Offer: "If you could give us two more years, we would be willing to accept a penalty."

Demand: "No penalty." → Offer: "We could accept the date, unless the penalty is above five percent."

Demand: "Reduce the penalty." → Offer: "What if we added a new berth? Would you reduce the penalty then?"

Demand: "Move on the date." → Offer: "If you meet us halfway on the date, we will drop the claim."

Look at the blue words. Which offers are tentative, still exploring? Which one is a firm promise? What tense comes after what if we?

MoveFormExample
Explore (tentative)if you could + verb, we would / we could + verbIf you could give us two years, we would accept a penalty.
Signal flexibilitywe would be willing to + verbWe would be willing to discuss the schedule.
Float an ideawhat if + we / you + past simple?What if we added a new berth?
Set a limitunless + present simpleWe accept, unless the penalty is above five percent.
Close (firm)if + present simple, will + verbIf you meet us halfway, we will drop the claim.
The trapwill in the if partNever if you will give us. Say if you give (firm) or if you could give (tentative).

In one line: explore with could and would, close with will, and never put will after if.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Two Years Late, One Table

A renegotiation, a mediator and a veteran

Dialogue

Two Years, Five Percent

C
Let me be direct. The investment is two years late, and the contract says the penalty is ten percent.
A
I understand. But most of that delay came from the right of way, which was the state's obligation. That gives us some leverage, I think.
C
Some. So what are you proposing?
A
If you could give us two more years, we would be willing to accept a smaller penalty. Let us say three percent.
C
Three is too low. The penalty is the main sticking point for my board.
A
Then what if we added a new berth to the project, at our cost? Would your board accept five?
C
A new berth would sweeten the deal. Five might be possible, unless the berth is delayed too.
A
It will not be. If you meet us halfway at five, we will sign the new schedule this week, as a package deal.
Comprehension

About Claire

1. What penalty does the contract set, according to Claire?
Ten percent.
2. Why does Claire reject three percent?
Because the penalty is the main sticking point for her board, and three is too low.
3. On what condition does Claire say five might be possible?
Unless the new berth is delayed too.
Listening 1 · The Mediator

The Rules of the Table

A mediator opens a renegotiation session. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she ask each side to say first?
What is non-negotiable for them, in one sentence.
2. Why does she want every proposal as a package?
Because nothing is agreed until everything is agreed.
3. What does she say about walk-away points?
That each side should know its own, but nobody has to say it at the table.
Listening 2 · The Veteran

Never Give Without Getting

A veteran negotiator on conditional offers. Sound first — no text.

1. What is his most important rule?
Never give anything without getting something back: every concession is a condition.
2. Why does he start with the condition, not the concession?
Because if you say the concession first, the other side stops listening before the condition.
3. When does he switch from would to will?
Only at the end, when he is ready to close the deal.
