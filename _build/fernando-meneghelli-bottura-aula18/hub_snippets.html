The Ninety-Day Question -- What You Would Recommend to the Board

Answering the ninety-day question as a chair wants to hear it: three formal recommendations, in order, with one thing you would not do yet. Key words: a quick win, a roadmap, low-hanging fruit, to streamline, a cost base, to phase in, to ring-fence, to defer, to put forward, to endorse, governance, headroom. Structure: the subjunctive for formal recommendation (I recommend that the board approve...).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These are the words of a board paper: what you would do first, what you would protect, and what can wait.

A quick win -- a small improvement you can deliver early, that people notice
“A weekly cash report was the quick win of my first month.”
A roadmap -- a plan that shows the steps and the order you will take them in
“I would bring the board a roadmap, not a list of problems.”
Low-hanging fruit -- gains that are easy to get and need very little effort
“The supplier payment terms were the low-hanging fruit.”
To streamline -- to make a process simpler and faster
“We streamlined the monthly close from ten days to five.”
A cost base -- all the regular costs of running the business
“Two thirds of an agency’s cost base is people.”
To phase in -- to introduce something gradually, in stages
“I recommend that we phase in the new system over two quarters.”
To ring-fence -- to protect money so that it can only be used for one purpose
“The training budget was ring-fenced, even in a bad year.”
To defer -- to delay a decision or a payment until later
“I would defer the office project until the cash position is clear.”
To put forward -- to formally suggest an idea for other people to consider
“I put forward three options and recommended one of them.”
To endorse -- to say officially that you support a proposal
“The audit committee endorsed the plan before it went to the full board.”
Governance -- the rules and processes that decide how a company is directed and controlled
“Good governance means the board hears bad news early.”
Headroom -- the space you still have before you reach a limit
“We had enough headroom in the budget to absorb the exchange rate.”

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The chair asked me the question every candidate gets: ninety days in, what would you bring to this board? I did not say that I would listen. I said I would bring three recommendations, in order.

The first was a quick win. I recommend that the board approve a five-day close from the second quarter, and I would insist that every unit head sign off their own figures before they reach finance.

The second was about cash. I would suggest that the board ring-fence the technology budget and that we defer the new office. It is essential that the plan be endorsed by the audit committee first. The third, I said, depends on what I find, so I would put forward a roadmap in month three.

1. How does he answer the chair's question?
A By saying that he would spend the first ninety days listening to the team and meeting every client.
B With three recommendations, in order.
C With a full roadmap for the year.
2. What is his quick win?
A A five-day close from the second quarter.
B A new office, which he would open in the second quarter of the year.
C A new technology budget.
3. What does he say about the technology budget?
A That it should be cut.
B That it should be spent on the new office, which the creative side wants.
C That the board should ring-fence it.
4. When would he put forward the roadmap?
A In month three.
B On his first day, before he meets anybody in the finance team or the units.
C Never.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Recommend That the Board Approve

GRAMMAR

After verbs and adjectives that ask for a decision (recommend, suggest, propose, insist, essential, vital, important) + that, the verb stays in the base form for every person and every tense. No -s, no do, no was.

FormMeaningExample
recommend / suggest / insist + that + base formYou ask somebody else to decideI recommend that the board approve the plan.
it is essential / vital + that + base formWhat has to happenIt is essential that the CFO attend.
Passivebe + past participleIt is essential that the forecast be reviewed first.
Negativenot + base form, no doI would suggest that we not hire before June.
QuestionThe question goes on the main verbWould you recommend that the board defer it?
The trapThe past does not change it: The chair insisted that the CFO attend, not attended.

In an interview, use it for the decision you want the board to take. It sounds like a board paper, not a wish.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

The person asking is the chair of the board. Choose the answer that works for her.

The chair asks what you would bring to the board after ninety days. You start with:
A I would spend the first ninety days listening, learning and meeting as many people as possible.
B Three recommendations, in order, starting with a quick win.
C A list of every problem you expect to find.
You want the board to approve a faster close. The most formal sentence is:
A Maybe we could think about looking at the close at some point next year.
B I recommend that the board approves a five-day close.
C I recommend that the board approve a five-day close.
You do not want to hire until the close is fixed. You write:
A I would suggest that we not hire before the close is fixed.
B I would suggest that we don't hire before the close is fixed, if the board agrees.
C I would suggest that we didn't hire.
You would like to start a new office project, but cash is tight. The strongest answer is:
A Start it anyway, because it was your idea.
B Say you would defer it until you know your headroom.
C Leave it out of the answer and hope that nobody on the board asks about it.
The chair asks why the board should trust your forecast. You say:
A It is essential that the forecast is reviewed by the business units, as it was last year.
B Forecasts are always right.
C It is essential that the forecast be reviewed by the business units first.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. Nobody is correcting this one, and there is no score.

Choose a company you know well. Record ninety seconds answering the chair: ninety days in, what would you bring to this board? Give three recommendations in order: one quick win, one thing you would defer, and one that depends on what you find. Use "I recommend that the board..." and "it is essential that ... be ...". Then listen once and count how many verbs after "that" you kept in the base form.
