Chapter 3: The Code

Recommend That the Board Approve

The subjunctive for formal recommendation

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

“I recommend that the board approve a five-day close from the second quarter.”

“It is essential that the forecast be reviewed by the business units first.”

“I would suggest that we not hire before the close is fixed.”

“The chair insisted that the CFO attend every audit committee meeting.”

The board is singular, and the fourth sentence is in the past. Why do the orange verbs never change?

FormWhat it doesExample
recommend / suggest / propose / insist + that + subject + base formAsks somebody else to take a decisionI recommend that the board approve the plan.
it is essential / vital / important + that + subject + base formSays what has to happenIt is essential that the CFO attend.
that + subject + not + base form / be + past participleThe negative and the passive: no do, no -s...that we not hire. ...that the forecast be reviewed.
In an interviewUse it for the decision you want the board to take. It sounds like a board paper, not a wish.

The Move

Found, Recommend, Not Yet

1. What I found

“In my first month I would expect to find that the close takes nine days.”

2. What I recommend

“I recommend that the board approve a five-day close from the second quarter.”

3. What I would not do yet

“I would suggest that we defer the new office until we know our headroom.”

A recommendation is a decision you ask somebody else to take. Make it sound like one.

Ninety Days In

C
We ask every candidate the same thing. Ninety days in, what would you bring to this board?
F
Three recommendations, in order. The first is a quick win: I would recommend that we streamline the monthly close, from nine days to five.
C
Five days. Is that realistic in an agency group?
F
It is, if the units send their numbers on day three. So I would insist that every unit head sign off their own figures before they reach finance.
C
And the second?
F
The second is about cash. I would suggest that the board ring-fence the technology budget, and that we defer the new office until we know our headroom.
C
That will not be popular with the creative side.
F
Probably not. That is why it is essential that the plan be endorsed by the audit committee before it comes to the full board.
C
And the third?
F
The third depends on what I find. I would rather put forward a roadmap in month three than promise one today.

Whether a five-day close is realistic in an agency group.
3. How does she react to the second recommendation?
She says it will not be popular with the creative side.
Listening

The Weak Answer and the Strong One

The chair of a media group. Sound first — no text.

1. What question does she always ask?
What they would bring to the board after ninety days.
2. What three things does the strong answer give her?
One problem they expect to find, one quick win for the first quarter, and one thing they would deliberately not do yet.
3. Which of the three is the rarest, and why does it matter to her?
The thing they would not do yet: it shows they understand the board has limited attention and limited cash.
Listening 2

Recommend Something

A former finance director on his early board papers. Sound first — no text.

1. What did his chair write on one of his board papers?
A single note in the margin: recommend something.
2. What did he recommend about hiring?
That the board approve a hiring freeze in the central functions until June.
3. How did the room hear the formal recommendation?
Not as arrogance: they thought he had done the work.
