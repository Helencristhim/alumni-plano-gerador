Complementary Activities

Optional extras to enjoy outside class. Mark each one as done after you watch or listen to it.

Series
Explained — “The World’s Water Crisis”

Every episode is around twenty minutes and does exactly what this lesson asked you to do: take one contested subject, lay out what each side actually wants, and name the cost of every solution. The narration is fast, and the visuals carry half the meaning, which makes it easier than it sounds.

Tip: at the end, say out loud in two sentences what you would do and what the downside would be. That is the whole exercise.

Podcast
BBC 6 Minute English — “Sharing the road with driverless cars”

Six minutes, two British speakers, normal speed, on a change that has a clear upside and a downside nobody has settled. It is the same argument as the text in this lesson, with different vehicles. Free transcript on the same page.

Tip: listen once with no text, then say one sentence with if plus would about your own city. Check the shape against the transcript.

Talk
Julia Dhar — “How to disagree productively and find common ground” (TED)

A world debate champion on why most arguments fail before anybody says anything of substance. Twelve minutes, and the practical part is the last four: what to say when you want the other person to keep talking instead of defending themselves.

Tip: write down the one technique you already use and the one you never do. Bring both to the next lesson.
