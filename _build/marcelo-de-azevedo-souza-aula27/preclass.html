Lesson 27 -- Pre-class

The Passive Voice in Action -- Describing Processes, Systems and Events

How to describe a process, report an event and say what has been done, when the action matters more than the person. Key words: a bottleneck, to be held up, a batch, to dispatch, to be flagged, lead time, a hand-off, to clear customs, traceability, a recall, behind the scenes, streamlined. Structure: the passive voice in the present (is scanned), the past (was dispatched) and the present perfect (has been cleared).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example.

A bottleneck -- a point where work slows down because too much has to pass through it
"The loading gate was our biggest bottleneck."
To be held up -- to be delayed by a problem
"The truck was held up at the port."
A batch -- a group of products that are made or sent together
"Every batch is given a number."
To dispatch -- to send goods to a place for a purpose
"We dispatch forty trucks every night."
To be flagged -- to be marked as a possible problem, so that someone checks it
"If the temperature changes, the pallet is flagged."
Lead time -- the time between an order and its delivery
"Our lead time is three days."
A hand-off -- the moment when work or goods pass from one team to another
"The hand-off to the carrier is where things go wrong."
To clear customs -- to get official permission for goods to enter a country
"The shipment cleared customs on Wednesday."
Traceability -- the ability to follow a product through every step of its journey
"Traceability is not optional with medicines."
A recall -- when a company asks for a product back because it may be unsafe
"A recall is the worst day of the year."
Behind the scenes -- in the part of an activity that the public does not see
"Nobody sees what happens behind the scenes."
Streamlined -- made simpler and faster by removing unnecessary steps
"The process is much more streamlined now."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Fiona visits Marcelo’s distribution center for her column. He explains the routine: “Every box is scanned at the door. Then it is stored in the cold room, and the temperature is recorded every five minutes.” Nobody says who scans or who records: the boxes are more important than the people.

Then he talks about last week: “A shipment was held up at the port, because some documents were missing. It was cleared by customs on Wednesday.” These are finished events, so he uses was + past participle.

Finally, the result that matters today: “The batch has been dispatched, and the process has been streamlined.” But when Fiona asks who changed the carriers, he answers: “I decided it.” When the responsibility is his, he uses the active.

"Every box is scanned at the door." Who scans the boxes?
A We do not know, and it is not important here.

C Marcelo scans every box.
Why does Marcelo say "The shipment was cleared by customs"?
A Because it is a routine that happens every day, at every port, with every shipment that arrives.
B Because it is a finished event, and by shows who did it.
C Because customs is a person.
Which sentence is correct?
A "The batch has dispatched to the pharmacies in Campinas by the night team."
B "The batch is dispatch every night."
C "The batch has been dispatched."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive Voice

GRAMMAR

The passive puts the object first. The tense is in BE, the meaning is in the past participle.

UseFormExample
A routine or a processam / is / are + past participleEvery box is scanned.
A finished eventwas / were + past participleThe truck was held up.
A result that matters nowhas / have been + past participleIt has been cleared.
Who did itby + agent, only if it mattersIt was approved by the agency.
The questionIs / Was / Has ... been + past participle?Has it been flagged?

Never: The boxes are scan · It has cleared by customs · The batch dispatched by the team

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

A visitor asks how the temperature is controlled. You say:
A "It is recorded every five minutes."
B "It records every five minutes by the system, and somebody looks at the numbers in the morning."
C "It recorded every five minutes."
A client asks about an order that is late. You say:
A "It held up at the port yesterday, but now it is clearing by customs and it arrives tomorrow."
B "It was held up at the port, but it has been cleared."
C "It is held up yesterday."
Your decision to change the carrier caused a delay. The board asks what happened. You say:
A "Mistakes were made, and the delay was caused by a number of different factors in the process."
B "I changed the carrier, and that caused the delay. Here is how we will fix it."
C "The carrier was changed."
A colleague says that the new process has fewer steps and is faster. You say:
A "Yes, it is much more streamlined."
B "Yes, it is a bottleneck, because now everything passes through one person in the team."
C "Yes, it is a recall."
