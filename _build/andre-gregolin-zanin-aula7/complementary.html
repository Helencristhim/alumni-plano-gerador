Series
Explained (Vox) — any episode

Eighteen minutes per episode, each one taking a system apart and showing how it actually works. The narration is almost entirely passive, because that is what explaining a system sounds like in English, and the pace is fast without being unfair.

Tip: pick any episode, watch five minutes with English subtitles, and count the sentences with no person in them. Then say two of them out loud in the active and notice what changes.

Talk
Ken Robinson — “Do schools kill creativity?” (TED)

The most watched TED talk ever made, and it is about exactly what you argued in class today: what an education system selects for, and what it quietly leaves out. Funny, nineteen minutes, and the English is clear enough to follow without subtitles by now.

Tip: watch it once for the argument and once for the language. Write down three sentences where he describes what is done to students rather than what students do.

Podcast
BBC Learning English — “Present and past passives” (6 Minute Grammar)

Six minutes, spoken, on the two tenses you will use most. Useful mainly for the rhythm: hearing native speakers run be plus the participle together is what makes the structure stop sounding memorised.

Tip: shadow it. Play a sentence, pause, and say it back at the same speed. Repeat until the auxiliaries stop taking up space.
