Chapter 3: Where Did the Doer Go?

Where Did the Doer Go?

Same fact, two arrangements, two different points

Grammar Discovery

Same Fact, Different Front

"Eighty thousand people take the exam." → "The exam is taken by eighty thousand people."

"They published the results yesterday." → "The results were published yesterday."

"They have already announced the list." → "The list has already been announced."

"You must submit the form." → "The form must be submitted."

Every pair says the same thing. Look at what sits in first position, and at what happens to the verb. The red sentence cares about who did it. The green one cares about what happened — and in three of the four, the doer has simply gone.

JobFormExample
The action matters, not the doeram · is · are + past participleThe exam is taken by eighty thousand people.
The same, finished, with a timewas · were + past participleThe form was submitted last week.
The same, still countinghave · has been + past participleThe results have already been published.
With a modalmodal + be + past participleApplications must be submitted before November.
Naming the doer, when it mattersby + the doerCandidates are selected by a committee of three.
The informal versionget + past participleI got accepted. He got eliminated in the first round.
The trapthe doer takes by, never forNot taken for millions. Say taken by millions.

In one line: put the important thing in front, and let be plus the participle carry the rest.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Explaining Your Country to Somebody Else

One conversation, three recordings, one status page

Dialogue

So How Does It Actually Work Here?

H
Explain it to me properly. In the States you apply to twelve places and they look at everything — grades, essays, sport. How does it work here?
A
Almost nothing like that. Here you are selected by one exam. Two days, and that is the whole thing.
H
One exam. So nothing else is looked at at all?
A
At the big public universities, essentially no. The criteria are published in advance and they do not change. Nothing else is considered.
H
That is either much fairer than ours or much worse, and I honestly cannot decide which.
A
Both, I think. It is widely believed to be fair, because everybody undergoes exactly the same two days. But everything before those two days is not equal at all.
H
And what happens after? How do you even find out?
A
A list is published online, at midnight, with numbers instead of names. And you get in or you do not. What happens in your system — who actually decides?
Comprehension

About Hannah

1. How does she describe the American system, in her first line?
You apply to twelve places and they look at everything: grades, essays and sport.
2. What does she say she cannot decide?
Whether the Brazilian system is much fairer than theirs or much worse.
3. What two things does she want to know at the end?
What happens after the exam, and how you even find out.
Listening 1 · An Admissions Office

How the Decision Is Actually Made

An admissions officer explaining a process to new applicants. Sound first — no text.

1. What happens first when your file arrives, and how often is missing material requested?
The file is received and checked. Anything missing is requested once, by email, and only once.
2. What happens when the two readers give scores that are far apart?
The file is sent to a third reader, and the first two scores are not shown to that person.
3. What two things does she say at the very end?
That nothing is decided before publication day, and that nothing is decided by one person alone.
Listening 2 · A Writing Podcast

The Passive Has a Bad Reputation

An editor on why the advice to avoid the passive is mostly wrong. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say the passive actually does?
It puts the important thing at the front. If the important thing is what happened, the passive is the correct choice.
2. Where does he agree that the advice is right?
Where the passive is used to hide somebody — as in mistakes were made, or the account was closed.
3. What is his rule, in his own words?
Not avoid the passive, but know who you left out and be able to say why.
Listening 3 · World Service

Two Ways of Choosing Four Hundred People

A short radio piece comparing two admission systems. Sound first — no text.

1. How are candidates chosen in the first system, and what else is considered?
They are ranked by a single examination taken on the same two days by everyone, and nothing else is considered at all.
2. What is weighed together in the second system?
Grades, essays, references and activities, all read one by one by a committee.
3. What does the research suggest about both systems?
That both predict the first year well and the fifth year badly, and that the strongest single predictor is where the candidate went to school.
Real Document

The Page You Refresh at Midnight

APPLICATION STATUS · CANDIDATE PORTAL
last updated automatically · do not reply to this page
CandidateAndré Gregolin Zanin
ApplicationReceived on 12 November, 21:47
DocumentsOne item was requested and has been provided
First evaluationCompleted — scores are not shown
Second evaluationIn progress
DecisionWill be published on 18 January, 00:00, for all candidates
Every line on this page is written without a person in it. That is not an accident and it is not politeness: the office is telling you that no individual decided any of this, and that there is nobody to write to. Reading that correctly is a language skill.
1. What happened with the documents, and how many times was something requested?
One item was requested and it has been provided.
2. What is said about the scores of the first evaluation?
It is completed, but the scores are not shown.
3. According to the note at the bottom, what is the page actually telling you by leaving out the people?
That no individual decided any of it, and that there is nobody to write to.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I can describe a process without inventing a person to be the subject of every sentence.
I never forget the be after a modal. I say must be submitted, not must submitted.
I use the past participle, not the infinitive: are evaluated, not are evaluate.
I say by eighty thousand people, never for eighty thousand people.
I know the words: an application, a requirement, to evaluate, to select, criteria, to undergo, to submit, to get something done, it is widely believed that, to be considered a.
