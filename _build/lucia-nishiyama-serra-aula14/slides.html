Chapter 4: The Future Code

Will · Going To · -ing

Three forms, three different messages

The Future Map

One Future, Five Moves

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I will take a baseline in week one, so we can measure the change."

"I am going to map out the first ninety days this weekend."

"I am presenting the plan to the board on Tuesday."

"As soon as the trajectory is clear, I will move to a two-week cadence."

All four point at the future. What is different about each one?

FormMessageExample
will + verbYou decide as you speak, you predict, or you promiseI will take a baseline in week one.
am / is / are going to + verbThe decision existed before this conversation, or the evidence is already in front of youI am going to map out the first ninety days.
am / is / are + -ingA fixed arrangement: there is a time, and usually other peopleI am presenting the plan on Tuesday.
present simpleTimetables and schedules that nobody controlsThe onboarding week starts on the sixth.
time clauseAfter when, as soon as, before, after, until, English marks the future only once, in the other half: "As soon as the trajectory is clear, I will move to a two-week cadence."

Negative: I will not commit to a date yet · Question: What are you going to do in the first thirty days?

Focus

Which Future Are You Choosing?

WILL = decided now

You decide while you speak, you predict, or you promise.

"I will take a baseline in week one."

probably · I think · I promise

GOING TO = already decided

The plan existed before the question, or the evidence is visible now.

"I am going to map out the first ninety days."

the plan is · look at the trajectory

-ING = in the calendar

A fixed arrangement with a time, and usually other people.

"I am presenting the plan on Tuesday."

on Tuesday · at nine · in March

In a senior interview you usually need all three: what is already decided, what is already booked, and what you predict.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I will send you the plan when I will finish it."
"I will send you the plan when I finish it."
"I will to map out the first ninety days."
"I will map out the first ninety days."
"I go to San Francisco next Tuesday for the review."
"I am going to San Francisco next Tuesday for the review."

Mark the future once per sentence. Will never takes to. And a booked trip is am going to or am going, never the present simple.

Grammar Practice

Choose the Right Future

Say the full sentence out loud, then tap to check

"I ___will take a baseline in week one." (a promise, decided as you speak)
"I ___am going to map out the first ninety days this weekend." (already decided)
"I ___am presenting the plan to the board on Tuesday." (already in the calendar)
"As soon as the trajectory ___is clear, I will change the cadence." (a time clause -- no will here)
"The onboarding week ___starts on the sixth of January." (a schedule nobody controls)
Dialogue

Walk Me Through the First Ninety Days

M
Before the offer goes out, walk me through your first ninety days.
L
In week one I will take a baseline, so that in ninety days we can both see exactly what moved.
M
And before that number exists, what happens?
L
I am going to map out the first thirty days this weekend, and I am meeting two of your engineering leads on Thursday, so the plan will not be written in a vacuum.
M
Good. What worries you?
L
Hiring is going to be our biggest headwind. The cost trajectory already says so, and I would rather forecast that now than explain it in June.
M
And the legacy platform?
L
We are sunsetting it in March. As soon as the migration is stable, I will move the team to a two-week reporting cadence and look for one quick win the board can see.
M
That is the answer I wanted. Say it exactly like that on Thursday.

Listening 1

What the Hiring Manager Listens For

A hiring manager explains the answer he wants. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What does he say a weak candidate does with the whole answer?
Puts the whole answer in will, so everything sounds like it was invented during the interview.
2. Which form does he say makes a candidate sound prepared, and why?
Going to -- because it says the decision was already made before the conversation started.
3. What is the one thing he asks every candidate to name?
The headwind -- the thing that is going to make the plan harder.
Listening 2

The Voice Memo for January

A program director records the plan for the quarter. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00

The steering meeting -- she is presenting the plan on the fourteenth of January.
2. What has already been decided about the legacy platform?

3. What does she say she will only do once the baseline exists?
Commit to the two-week reporting cadence.

Self-Assessment

What I Learned

I use will when I decide as I speak, when I predict and when I promise.
I use going to when the decision was already made, and the present continuous when it is already in the calendar.
I never say will to, and I never mark the future twice after when or as soon as.
I know the words: quick win, map out, baseline, trajectory, forecast, cadence, headwind, sunset.
I can give a one-minute plan for my first ninety days, out loud.
