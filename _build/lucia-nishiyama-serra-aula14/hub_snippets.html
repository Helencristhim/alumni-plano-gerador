Future Forms -- Plans, Predictions and the First Ninety Days

Answering the plan question the way a senior candidate answers it. Key words: quick win, to map out, baseline, trajectory, to forecast, cadence, headwind, to sunset. Structure: future forms -- will for decisions made as you speak, be going to for decisions already made, the present continuous for fixed arrangements, and no will inside a time clause.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Quick win -- a small result you can deliver fast, to build trust early
"I am going to look for one quick win in the first month."
To map out -- to plan something in detail, step by step, before you start
"I am going to map out the first ninety days this weekend."
Baseline -- the first measurement, used later to show how much changed
"I will take a baseline in week one, so we can measure the change."
Trajectory -- the direction and the speed something is moving over time
"The cost trajectory is going to worry the board."
To forecast -- to say what you expect to happen, based on data you already have
"I will forecast the savings before the budget meeting."
Cadence -- the regular rhythm of meetings or reports
"We are moving to a two-week reporting cadence in January."
Headwind -- a force that makes progress slower and harder
"Hiring is going to be our biggest headwind next year."
To sunset -- to retire an old system gradually, on a planned date
"We are sunsetting the legacy platform in March."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Lucia is writing the answer she will give when the interviewer asks about her first ninety days. She starts with a promise she is making right now: "In week one I will take a baseline, so we can measure the change." Then she reports a decision she made long before this conversation: "I am going to map out the first thirty days before I start." Then the strongest line of all, because it is already in somebody else's calendar: "I am presenting the plan to the board on Tuesday." Next comes the prediction, and the evidence is already visible in the numbers: "Hiring is going to be our biggest headwind, and the cost trajectory already says so." She closes with a condition of time, where English marks the future only once: "As soon as the migration is stable, I will move the team to a two-week reporting cadence."

1. Why does Lucia say "I am going to map out the first thirty days" instead of "I will map out the first thirty days"?
A Because the decision was already made before this conversation started.
B Because going to is the polite form and will is informal.
C Because the sentence talks about the past.
2. In "I am presenting the plan to the board on Tuesday", why is the present continuous the right choice?
A Because the presentation is happening at this exact moment.
B Because it is a fixed arrangement, with a time and other people already involved.
C Because the present continuous is always used with the word board.
3. In "As soon as the migration is stable, I will move the team", why is there no will in the first half?
A Because the migration is happening right now.
B Because will can only appear at the end of a sentence.
C Because after as soon as, when, before and until, English marks the future only once.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Future Forms

GRAMMAR

Three ways to point at the future, and what each one tells the listener.

FormUseExample
will + verbYou decide as you speak, you predict, or you promise.In week one I will take a baseline.
am / is / are going to + verbThe decision existed before the conversation, or the evidence is visible now.I am going to map out the first ninety days.
am / is / are + verb-ingA fixed arrangement: there is a time, and usually other people.I am presenting the plan on Tuesday.
Negative and questionwill not + verb · am not going to + verb · What are you going to do?I will not commit to a date yet. What are you going to do first?
present simpleTimetables and schedules that nobody controls.The onboarding week starts on the sixth.
Two trapsNever will to: "I will map out the plan." And never mark the future twice: "As soon as the trajectory is clear, I will move to a two-week cadence."
