Talk
"On being wrong" -- Kathryn Schulz (TED2011)

A free talk of about seventeen minutes on what it feels like to be wrong: exactly like being right, until the moment you find out. She takes apart the story we tell after the event. Connection to Lesson 18: it is the argument behind a blameless post-mortem -- the person who missed the step did not feel careless at the time.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Every time she describes a mistake, pause and say one sentence about it: "If they had known, they would have ..."

Podcast
"The most common regrets and what to do about them" -- Life Kit, NPR

A free episode of about twenty minutes in clear American English, with the full text on the same page. Daniel Pink went through thousands of regrets and sorted them into four kinds. Connection to Lesson 18: regret is the native home of "should have" and "could have" -- the episode is twenty minutes of your grammar spoken naturally.

Tip: listen once without reading, and count how many times you hear "should have" or "could have". Then say three of your own about last quarter at work.

Video
"How to use the third conditional" -- 6 Minute Grammar, BBC Learning English

A short free class on exactly the code you learned today: the if half with "had", the result half with "would have", and the contractions that make it sound natural at speed. Connection to Lesson 18: it drills the two errors that cost you the sentence in a real meeting -- "would" in the if half, and the past simple where the participle belongs.

Tip: pause after each example and rebuild it about your own desk. If you can say it at full speed with "would've" and still get the if half right, you have it.
