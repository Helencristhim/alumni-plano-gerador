Chapter 3: The Code

A Soft Start, a Firm No

The small words that make a no easy to hear

Discovery

What Comes Before the No?

"I see your point, but our costs went up."

"I'm afraid we can't keep the old price."

"I'm not sure that's the best option."

"Maybe we could meet halfway."

The red part is the message. Without the blue part, is the message different, or only harder to hear?

UseFormExample
Agree with one part firstI see your point, but + sentenceI see your point, but our costs went up.
Give bad newsI'm afraid + sentenceI'm afraid we can't keep the old price.
Disagree softlyI'm not sure + sentenceI'm not sure that's the best option.
Offer an alternativeMaybe we could + verbMaybe we could meet halfway.
The trapagree is a verb: no I'm before itNot I'm not agree, but I don't agree. Not I'm agree, but I agree.

In one line: the soft words go FIRST, the message stays the same, and MAYBE WE COULD takes a verb.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Director Says No

The call, the coach, and a message from Paula

Dialogue

Megan Brings a No

M
Hi, Roberto. I talked to my director on Tuesday. I'm afraid the news is not great.
R
Okay. What is his point of view?
M
He wants to keep the old price. No increase this year.
R
I see your point, but I'm afraid we can't do that. Our costs went up eight percent this year.
M
I understand. But he is going to push back on four percent. For us, that is a lot.
R
That's a fair point. So let's start with the common ground: we both want the June order on time.
M
Yes, of course. So what can we do?
R
Maybe we could meet halfway. Two percent now, and two percent in October. It's a compromise, and you have time to plan.
M
I like that better. But I'm not sure he will say yes without something more.
R
Then here is an alternative: if you order twenty percent more, the second increase waits until January.
Comprehension

About Megan

1. What is her director's point of view on the price?
He wants to keep the old price, with no increase this year.
2. What does she say about four percent?
Her director is going to push back on it, because for Lakeshore it is a lot.
3. Is she sure her director will accept the compromise?
No. She likes it better, but she thinks he needs something more to say yes.

Listening 2 · A Message From Paula

Your Own Team Disagrees

A voice message from Paula about your proposal. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Does Paula agree with the whole proposal?
No. She sees the point and knows they need the order, but she is not sure October works.
2. Why is October a problem for her?
The new costs start in July, so for three months they lose money on every box.
3. What alternative does she suggest?
The second increase starts in August, not in October.
Real Document

The Proposal in Writing

Subject: Third-quarter price · two options
From: Roberto Saraiva, Bom Sabor · To: Megan Ross, Lakeshore Foods

Hi Megan,

Thank you for a good call today. I see your director's point, and I know this year is hard for Lakeshore too. I'm afraid we can't keep the old price, because our costs went up eight percent.

Our common ground is the June order, so here are two options:

Option A: two percent now, and two percent in October.
Option B: two percent now, and the second two percent in January, with a twenty percent bigger order.

I'm not sure which one is better for you, so I'm happy to talk on Monday.

Best,
Roberto

1. Find two soft phrases in the email.
I see your director's point · I'm afraid we can't keep the old price · I'm not sure which one is better for you.
2. What is the common ground in the email?
The June order: both companies want it.
3. Why does the email give two options, not one?
Because a no with an alternative keeps the deal on the table, and Megan can choose.
