I See It Differently -- Disagree Without Breaking the Deal

The price for the third quarter comes back, and the buyer's director says no. Key words: a point of view, to disagree, to push back, a fair point, common ground, a compromise, to meet halfway, an alternative. Structure: polite disagreement -- I see your point, but; I'm afraid; I'm not sure; maybe we could -- the small words that make a no easy to hear.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A point of view -- the way one person sees a situation
"From our point of view, the new price is fair."
To disagree -- to have a different opinion from another person
"I disagree with the idea, but I understand the reason."
To push back -- to say no to an idea and give a reason
"Her director is going to push back on four percent."
A fair point -- you say it when the other person is partly right
"That's a fair point, but our costs went up this year."
Common ground -- the things that both sides already want or agree on
"Our common ground is simple: we both want the June order."
A compromise -- a solution where each side gives a little
"Two percent now and two in October is a fair compromise."
To meet halfway -- each side moves to the middle
"You want zero and we want four, so let's meet halfway."
An alternative -- another option that you can choose
"If the price is a problem, here is an alternative."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Megan calls Roberto with bad news. Her director wants to keep the old price. Roberto does not say no and stop. He says: "I see your point, but our costs went up." First he agrees with one part, then he gives his message after but.

Then the hard part: "I'm afraid we can't keep the old price." The message is still no. I'm afraid only makes it easier to hear. When Megan says her director is going to push back, Roberto answers: "That's a fair point." He finds the common ground — they both want the June order.

Then he offers a way out: "Maybe we could meet halfway." After maybe we could comes a verb with no to: meet, try, talk. Megan answers: "I'm not sure he will say yes." So Roberto gives one more alternative, and the deal stays on the table.

1. "I see your point, but our costs went up." What does Roberto do first?
A He agrees with one part, then he gives his message.
B He says no to everything Megan says, and then he explains the costs.
C He changes his price.
2. "I'm afraid we can't keep the old price." Is this a yes or a no?
A A yes, because afraid means that Roberto is worried and wants to accept the old price.
B It is not an answer.
C A no, but easier to hear.
3. What comes after "maybe we could"?
A A verb with to, as in maybe we could to meet halfway or maybe we could to talk.
B A verb with no to: meet, try, talk.
C A verb with -ing, as in meeting.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Polite Disagreement

GRAMMAR

The soft words go first. The message stays the same, but it is easier to hear.

UseFormExample
Agree with one part firstI see your point, but + sentenceI see your point, but our costs went up.
Give bad newsI'm afraid + sentenceI'm afraid we can't keep the old price.
Disagree softlyI'm not sure + sentenceI'm not sure that's the best option.
Offer an alternativeMaybe we could + verbMaybe we could meet halfway.
The trapagree is a verb: no I'm before itI don't agree. · I agree.

Never: I'm not agree · I'm agree · I'm afraid of we can't · Maybe we could to meet

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

The buyer says: "Your price is too high." You say:
A I see your point, but our costs went up.
B No. You are wrong about our price.
C Okay, no problem, we can change the price for you today, and we can talk about the costs another time.
You must give bad news about the delivery date. You say:
A I'm afraid of we can't ship before the tenth, because the container is still at the port.
B I'm afraid we can't ship before the tenth.
C The tenth. Not before.
You do not agree with your colleague's plan. You say:
A I'm not agree with the plan.
B I'm not agree with this plan, but I understand it, and maybe we can talk about it later this week.
C I'm not sure that's the best option.
You want zero, the buyer wants four. You offer:
A Maybe we could to meet halfway.
B Maybe we could meet halfway at two.
C We cannot accept four, and we are not going to change our position on this price, not this year.
