Chapter 4: Two Futures

Evidence, or Decision?

going to reads the world. will decides.

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The container is late, so the collection is going to arrive after the opening."

"Fine. We will move the campaign to July."

"We are going to hire six people in May and six in September."

"Do not worry, I will send you the numbers this afternoon."

FormUse it forExample
going to + verbEvidence you can point at right now, or a plan you already made.The dock is full, so we are going to miss July.
will + verbA decision taken at the second you speak. A promise, an offer, a refusal.Fine, we will move the campaign.
will for what you thinkAn opinion or a prediction with no evidence in front of you.I think the numbers will improve in August.
Negativewill not (won’t) · am not / is not / are not going toI will not promise it. We are not going to cut the budget.
QuestionWill you...? asks for a decision. Are you going to...? asks about the plan.Are you going to need more people?

going to reads the world or repeats a plan. will decides. If you can point at the evidence, you cannot use will.

Grammar Practice

Which Future Is It?

Say the full sentence, then click to compare

1. Look at the shipping report. The collection ______ (arrive) after the opening.
"Look at the shipping report. The collection is going to arrive after the opening."
2. Fine. We ______ (move) the campaign to July.
"Fine. We will move the campaign to July."
3. We ______ (hire) six people in May. It is already agreed.
"We are going to hire six people in May. It is already agreed."
4. I ______ (not / promise) the fourth site, but I ______ (fight) for it.
"I will not promise the fourth site, but I will fight for it."
Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I will to send the forecast this afternoon."
"I will send the forecast this afternoon."
"The container is late, so the collection will arrive after the opening."
"The container is late, so the collection is going to arrive after the opening."
"We not going to cut the budget."
"We are not going to cut the budget."

After will the verb comes bare, with no to. And when the evidence is already on the table, English refuses will: the sentence has to say going to.

The Quarterly Call

G
Right, Felipe. Before the numbers, one question. Is the second distribution center going to open in June or in July?
F
June. The building is ready and the team starts in May, so it is going to open on the ninth.
G
Good. Then I will put June in the regional forecast today.
F
There is one problem. The container from Tiruppur is three weeks late, so the spring collection is going to arrive after the opening.
G
How much of the collection?
F
About forty per cent. If we open with sixty, the store will look empty in the photographs.
G
Then we will move the campaign to July and open quietly in June. Does that work for you?
F
It does. I will talk to marketing this afternoon and confirm by Thursday.
G
And the budget? Are you going to need more people for the second site?
F
Twelve, but not all at once. We are going to hire six in May and six in September.
G
Fine. I will not touch your headcount line, then. Send me the plan before the board pack closes.
F
I will send it tomorrow morning. And Graham, could you tell me who signs the capacity study?
