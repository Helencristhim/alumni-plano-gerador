Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

The Plan for Next Quarter -- Presenting Results and Plans

The words of a quarterly review: forecast, target, budget, quarter, to roll out, bottleneck, stakeholder, capacity. Structure: going to for the evidence you can point at and for the plan you already have, will for the decision you take while you speak. Mission: present a quarter in four sentences and hold a decision under pressure.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Forecast -- the number you expect for a future period, based on what you know now
"The forecast is ninety-four per cent in July and ninety-seven in August."
Target -- the number you have agreed to reach
"The target is ninety-eight per cent on-time delivery."
Budget -- the money a team is allowed to spend in a period
"The budget is six per cent below last quarter."
Quarter -- a period of three months in a company year
"Once a quarter the same meeting happens in every retail company on earth."
To roll out -- to introduce something new across a company, site by site
"We are going to roll out the new picking system in three sites."
Bottleneck -- the one point in a process that slows everything else down
"The inbound dock at Guarulhos is the bottleneck of the whole quarter."
Stakeholder -- a person or a group with a real interest in how a project ends
"Marketing is a stakeholder in this decision, so they hear it before the board does."
Capacity -- the maximum a site, a machine or a team can handle
"We are at ninety per cent of capacity, and that is why nothing has room to go wrong."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Here is next quarter in six lines. The target is ninety-eight per cent on time. The forecast is ninety-four in July, and everybody in the room can see why: the inbound dock is full, so we are going to miss the target in the first month. Nobody decided that. It is written in the shipping report, and you only have to read it out loud.

The rest of the meeting is different. Somebody asks what we do about it, and I say: we will move the campaign to July, and I will confirm with marketing this afternoon. Those two are decisions, taken at the second I speak them. The hiring is a third case: we are going to hire six people in May and six in September, because that plan already existed before I walked into the room. Three futures in one paragraph, and only one of them is a promise.

1. Why does the text use we are going to miss the target and not we will miss?
A Because the evidence is already visible: the dock is full and the report shows it.
B Because miss is a negative verb and negatives take going to.
C Because it is about July, and July is far away.
2. Why is we will move the campaign in the will form?
A Because moving a campaign is always a promise.
B Because the decision is taken at the second he speaks it, in front of the room.
C Because there is no evidence about campaigns.
3. Why is the hiring in going to?
A Because the plan already existed before the meeting started.
B Because hiring involves people and people take going to.
C Because it happens in two different months.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Will and Going To

GRAMMAR

Two futures that share a calendar and nothing else. The question is never when it happens. It is who decided, and when.

FormUse it forExample
going to + verbEvidence in front of you right now.The dock is full, so we are going to miss July.
going to + verbA plan that already existed before this conversation.We are going to hire six people in May.
will + verbA decision taken at the second you speak. Also promises and offers.Fine, we will move the campaign. I will send it tonight.
will + verbWhat you think or expect, with no evidence on the table.I think the numbers will improve in August.
No to after willwill send, will move, will confirm. Never will to send.
Negatives and questionswill not / won’t · are not going to. Will you...? asks for a decision; Are you going to...? asks about the plan.

One question sorts almost everything: can I point at the reason right now? If yes, it is going to. If the sentence is you deciding, it is will.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are in the quarterly review. Choose the best thing to say.

The shipping report shows a three week delay. You say:
A The collection will arrive after the opening.
B The collection is going to arrive after the opening.
C The collection arrives after the opening, I think.
Your director asks you, right now, to move a deadline, and you agree. You say:
A Fine, I am going to move it to the fifteenth.
B Fine, we will move it to the fifteenth.
C Fine, I will to move it to the fifteenth.
The hiring plan was approved last month. Somebody asks about it. You say:
A We will hire six people in May.
B We are going to hire six people in May.
C We are hire six people in May.
Somebody asks whether you can guarantee the fourth site. You cannot. You say:
A I will fight for it, but I will not promise it today.
B I am going to fight for it, but I do not promise nothing.
C I will to fight for it, but no promise.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think about the next three months in your own operation. Record about ninety seconds. Start with the target and the forecast, and say the number for each. Then explain the gap between them using going to, because the reason is already visible. Then name one decision you would take live in that meeting, using will, and one thing you would refuse to promise. Use at least four words from this lesson. Do not stop to correct yourself.
