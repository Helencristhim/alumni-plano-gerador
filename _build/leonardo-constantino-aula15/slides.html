Discovery

Listen and Count

"I have advised this group since 2019, and last December the reform changed the question. The framework was published in December and has not been amended since. If the second phase is implemented as published, the exposure rises by eleven percent. If it were postponed to 2028, we would still recommend the restructuring. The provision that creates the problem is not the one the market talks about. We advised your finance director in March to avoid signing any new agreement. We recommend restructuring now, and we have decided to model a fourth case. There might be a narrower window than the board assumes, so you may want to decide this quarter."

Every colour is a different structure, and not one of them is new. How many can you name before you look?

The formWhat it does in this roomThe line above
Present perfect vs past simpleSeparates the relationship that is still running from the event that closed."I have advised this group since 2019" · "the reform changed the question"
Passive voicePuts the rule at the centre instead of the person, which is how a framework is described."The framework was published... has not been amended"
First conditionalThe scenario the board treats as likely, so the tense stays real."If the second phase is implemented, the exposure rises..."
Second conditionalThe scenario you are modelling but not predicting. The past form is what marks the distance."If it were postponed... we would still recommend..."
Relative clauseNarrows one item out of many, which is the whole job of a precise adviser."The provision that creates the problem..."
Reported speechPuts your team's earlier advice on the record without quoting anybody."We advised your finance director in March to avoid..."
Gerund vs infinitiveThe verb decides the shape, and the wrong shape is the error a board actually hears."recommend restructuring" · "decided to model"
Hedging modalsSays how certain you are. This is the one that makes a board trust the rest."There might be..." · "you may want to..."
The pointNothing here is new. Fifteen sessions taught these eight one at a time. A real presentation never asks for one at a time — it asks for all of them, in nine sentences, while somebody interrupts you.
Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"The exposure will be eleven percent and the authority will accept our position."
"In our view the exposure is around eleven percent, and the authority is likely to accept the position."
"Your finance director said to me: do not sign anything, I am worried."
"We advised your finance director in March that he should not sign any new agreement before this decision."
"We recommend to restructure the group and we suggest to decide this quarter."
"We recommend restructuring the group, and we would advise you to decide this quarter."

Certainty you cannot defend costs you the second meeting. A direct quotation sounds like gossip, not a firm position. And recommend takes the gerund — that one is not style, it is grammar, and a board hears it.

Chapter 4: The Board

Meet Vera

Group finance director, Halversen Group · she signs · she interrupts

Dialogue

The Question at Minute Four

L
Thank you for the time. I appreciate the opportunity to present our position, and I'll keep it to twelve minutes.
V
Vera Lindqvist, group finance. Twelve is generous. Start with what you want us to do.
L
Then I'll start there. We recommend restructuring into a single Brazilian entity before the second phase. The regulatory framework was published in December and has not been amended since, which is why the exposure is calculable today.
V
Eleven percent. Where does that number come from, and how confident are you in it?
L
Let me come back to that in one minute, because the second scenario changes the answer. If I give you the number now, you'll hear it out of context.
V
Fine. One minute.
L
If the phase is implemented as published, the burden rises by roughly eleven percent, and it falls on the withholding tax on the intercompany fee. If it were postponed to 2028, we would still recommend the same restructuring, because doing it later costs more than doing it now. Now, your question. Eleven is our modelling, not the authority's. In our view there might be two points either side of it.
V
Two points either side is a wide door. What did your team tell us in March?
L
We advised your finance team in March to avoid signing any new intercompany agreement before this decision was taken, and that advice stands. The provision that creates the problem is not the one the market talks about, which is why nobody has priced it yet.
V
And if we wait for the guidance? Everybody in this room would prefer to wait.
L
Then you decide in December with the information you already have and four months less to act. I can't tell you the guidance won't come. I can tell you it was promised in June and it hasn't arrived. We would recommend deciding this quarter.
V
That is the clearest twelve minutes we have had on this. Send us the advisory mandate.
L
Thank you for your confidence in our firm. It will be with you on Monday, and I look forward to continuing this conversation.
Comprehension

About Vera

1. What does she ask for before anything else?
What he wants the board to do. She tells him to start with the recommendation, not with the background.
2. What are her two questions about the eleven percent?
Where the number comes from, and how confident he is in it.
3. What does she call "a wide door", and why does that matter to her?
Two points either side of eleven percent. She signs, so the width of the range is her risk, not his.
4. What does she say the rest of the room would prefer to do?
Wait for the guidance the tax authority promised.
5. How does she end the meeting, and what does that tell you?
She asks for the advisory mandate. She was convinced by the range being honest, not by it being narrow.
Listening

The Call from the Car Park

Twenty minutes after the board, somebody who was in the room. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What happened at minute four, and why does she say it mattered?
The finance director interrupted him, as he always does, and he did not lose the thread: he said he would come back to it in one minute and then actually came back to it. She says that is the thing people cannot fake.
2. What does she want him to write down about the eleven percent?
That he hedged properly. He said "in our view" and "there might be", and he did not overstate a single number. A lawyer who overstates on a first presentation never gets a second one.
3. What is the one criticism she gives, and how does she describe it?
He still speeds up when he is nervous, and twice he ran three sentences together where a pause would have done more work. She says that is not English, it is delivery, and he can fix it in a month.

The Next Thirty Days

Why people lose the language after the course, and the three habits that keep it. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does he say is the single biggest predictor of still speaking well in a year?
Whether you took a call in English in the first month after the course ended. Not a perfect call — any call. If you wait until you feel ready, the language cools down, and cold language is harder to restart than tired language.
2. What is the second habit, and what mistake do people make with it?
Keep one live thing: one podcast you finish, one client relationship in English, one newsletter on a Monday. One. People who try to keep five keep zero.
3. What does he say to do about the exam, and why?
Book the date before you feel prepared. A date on a calendar organizes the whole year behind it, and someone preparing for a real exam in November studies differently from someone who intends to take one eventually.
