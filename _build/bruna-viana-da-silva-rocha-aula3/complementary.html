Film
The Terminal (2004) — Steven Spielberg

A man lands at JFK, his passport stops working at the booth, and he spends the whole film inside the terminal learning to say what he needs at a counter. Every scene Bruna will live in her first hour is in this movie, slowed down and explained by what happens next.

Tip: watch the first thirty minutes with English subtitles and write down every question an officer or an agent asks him. Four of them are the questions from this lesson, word for word.

Podcast
NPR Life Kit — "Yes, get to the airport early. But here are some travel tips you might not know"

Two American hosts and a flight expert on the things that actually go wrong at an airport: the line you should not stand in, the app that rebooks you faster than the counter, and why the first flight of the day is a different flight. Normal speed, full transcript on the same page.

Tip: listen once with the transcript closed. Then open it and find three sentences built on for or since. Read those three out loud at the speaker's speed, not slower.

Video
Rachel's English — "The R in American English: 5 Powerful Tips"

The hardest consonant in American English, and the one sitting inside reservation, Rocha, concierge and are you. She films the mouth from the front and from the side, so you can copy the shape instead of guessing at it.

Tip: after the video, say "I have a reservation under the name Rocha" ten times. The r in your own last name is the one an American at a front desk will be listening for.
