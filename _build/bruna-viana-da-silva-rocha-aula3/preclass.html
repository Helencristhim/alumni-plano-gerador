The airport and the hotel, from the door to the key. Key words: customs, a carry-on, an overhead bin, a check-in counter, a concierge, a lobby, complimentary, a reservation, to go through security, to fill out a form. Structure: for and since with the present perfect — how long something has been true, and when the past form comes back instead.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

You land at seven in the morning, you have not slept, and the first American who talks to you is an officer at customs with one question: How long are you staying? Four words, and the answer is the whole lesson. Two weeks is a length of time. Since Tuesday is a point on the calendar. English keeps those two apart with two tiny words, and it never lets you swap them.

For carries a piece of time: for two weeks, for forty minutes, for nineteen years. Since carries the point it started: since Tuesday, since March, since I was a teenager. "We have been in this line for forty minutes" and "We have been in this line since six" describe the same forty minutes from two different windows.

Now the verb. If the thing is still happening right now — you are still in the line, you still have the passport — you need have plus the third form: "I have had this passport since 2019." If it is over, the have goes and the past form comes back, even when for is still there: "We waited at customs for forty minutes, and then we picked up our bags." Same for, different verb, because the waiting finished.

And the question you will hear at every check-in counter, every hotel lobby and every seat next to yours: How long have you been here? How long have you worked here? How long have you had that bag? Answer it with for or since, and you sound like somebody who lives in the language instead of somebody translating it.

1. Why does the text say "for forty minutes" and "since six" are the same forty minutes?
A Because "for" gives the length of the time and "since" gives the point it started.
B Because "for" is used in questions and "since" is used in answers.
C Because both words mean the same thing and you can choose either one.
2. "We waited at customs for forty minutes." Why is there no "have" in that sentence?
A Because "for" never goes with "have".
B Because the waiting is over — they already picked up their bags.
C Because forty minutes is too short for the present perfect.
3. Somebody asks you "How long have you had that passport?" What does the question want?
A The date you will renew it.
B Whether you like the photo in it.
C Either a length ("for six years") or a starting point ("since 2019").

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- For and Since

GRAMMAR

For carries a length of time. Since carries the point it started. And if the thing already finished, the have goes and the past form comes back.

JobFormExample
Started before now, still truehave / has + third form + since + a pointI have had this reservation since March.
Started before now, still truehave / has + third form + for + a lengthWe have been in this line for forty minutes.
Asking about itHow long + have / has + subject + third form?How long have you worked here?
Over, with the point it happenedthe past form + in / on / lastI made the reservation in March.
Over, with how long it lastedthe past form + for + a lengthWe waited for forty minutes, and then they called us.
What decidesis it still happening now, or did it finish?If it finished, the have goes.
since = a point on the calendarsince 2019 · since March · since TuesdayI have wanted this since I was a teenager.
for = a piece of timefor two weeks · for forty minutes · for nineteen yearsHe has done this job for nineteen years.

Never: I have this reservation since March · I am here since Tuesday · since forty minutes · How long do you work here? · I have made the reservation last week

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The officer at customs asks how long you are staying. You have two weeks booked. You answer:
A "I am here since two weeks."
B "For two weeks. I fly back on the twenty-second."
C "I have been here since two weeks ago from now."
You are at the front desk and the room is still yours from March. You say:
A "I have a reservation under the name Rocha. I made it in March."
B "I have made a reservation under the name Rocha in March."
C "I make a reservation under the name Rocha since March."
You want to know how long the person behind the desk has been in that job. You ask:
A "How long do you work here?"
B "Since how long are you working here?"
C "How long have you worked here?"
The line at security is over and you are telling a friend about it. You say:
A "We have waited for an hour and then they opened a second lane."
B "We waited for an hour, and then they opened a second lane."
C "We are waiting for an hour, and then they opened a second lane."
