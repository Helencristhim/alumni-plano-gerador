Chapter 3: The Code

Still True, or Already Over

Two words, and the verb that tells them apart

Grammar Discovery

Two Are Still True. Two Are Over.

"I have had this reservation since March."

"We have been in this line for forty minutes."

"I made the reservation in March, and I paid for it that night."

"We waited at customs for forty minutes, and then we picked up our bags."

The blue sentences are still true at this second. The red ones closed and will not come back. Look at what happened to the verb — and notice that for is sitting in both groups.

JobFormExample
Started before now, still truehave / has + third form + since + a pointI have had this reservation since March.
Started before now, still truehave / has + third form + for + a lengthWe have been in this line for forty minutes.
Asking about itHow long + have / has + subject + third form?How long have you worked here?
Over, with the point it happenedthe past form + in / on / lastI made the reservation in March.
Over, with how long it lastedthe past form + for + a lengthWe waited for forty minutes, and then they called us.
What decidesis it still happening now, or did it finish?If it finished, the have goes.
since = a point on the calendarsince 2019 · since March · since TuesdayI have wanted this since I was a teenager.
for = a piece of timefor two weeks · for forty minutes · for nineteen yearsHe has done this job for nineteen years.

In one line: since points at a moment, for measures a piece of time — and the have only survives while the thing is still happening.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Three Voices You Will Hear on Day One

A front desk, a gate, and a phone call to your room

Dialogue

Nine at Night at the Front Desk

M
Good evening. Checking in?
B
Yes — I have a reservation under the name Rocha. R-O-C-H-A.
M
Rocha, got it. Two nights, king room. Can I see a photo ID, and could you fill out this form for me?
B
Here you go. We have been up since five this morning, so tell me if the handwriting is bad.
M
Long day. Where did you fly in from?
B
São Paulo. We had a four-hour layover in Houston, and the line at customs took another forty minutes.
M
Then you will like this: breakfast is complimentary and it runs until ten thirty. Elevators are past the lobby, on the right.
B
Perfect. And one more thing — how long have you worked here? I need somewhere to eat tonight, and I only trust the people who know the block.
Comprehension

About Marcus

1. What two things does Marcus ask her for before he gives her the room?
A photo ID, and for her to fill out a form.
2. What does he say about breakfast, and what does he say about the elevators?
That breakfast is complimentary and runs until ten thirty, and that the elevators are past the lobby, on the right.
3. Nobody asked him for either of those two pieces of information. Why does he give them anyway?
Because that is how somebody at an American front desk keeps the exchange going: they hand you what you will need next before you know you need it.
Listening 1 · The Gate

“The Plane Is Here. The Crew Is Not.”

A gate agent at four in the afternoon, with news nobody wants. Sound first — no text.

1. Why is the flight not boarding on time?
The aircraft has arrived but the crew has not. They are flying in from Denver and they land in about twenty minutes, so boarding starts at four fifteen instead of three forty.
2. What happens to a carry-on that does not fit in the overhead bin?
The flight is full, so they will check it at the door, free of charge.
3. What does he tell passengers with a connection in New York to do?
Come to the counter now, before boarding, and not after — he says the line after boarding is the wrong line to be in.
Listening 2 · The Phone in Your Room

“Two Things, and Then I Will Let You Sleep.”

The front desk calling room 1104 at nine at night. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say about breakfast?
It is complimentary for everybody on that rate, it is on the second floor, and it runs from six thirty to ten thirty.
2. Why should they come down before nine?
Because the concierge desk in the lobby closes at nine tonight, earlier than usual, and Ray is the one who can get them a table.
3. What do guests keep getting wrong in the hotel?
They take the express elevators, which do not stop on the second floor. You have to take the one by the gift shop.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I can answer “How long are you staying?” at a desk, without stopping to translate.
I use for for a piece of time and since for a point on the calendar, and I never swap them.
I drop the have when the thing already finished, even when for is still in the sentence.
I can check into a hotel: the reservation, the form, the breakfast and the question back.
I know the words: customs, a carry-on, an overhead bin, a check-in counter, a concierge, a lobby, complimentary, a reservation, to go through security, to fill out a form.
