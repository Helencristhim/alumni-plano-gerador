Lesson 05 -- Pre-class

From Photograph to Practice -- Telling a Career Turn

The story you are asked for in every interview: how you got from there to here. Key words: a turning point, to make a living, to outgrow something, a commission, to be self-taught, a detour, to second-guess yourself, a leap, to be pigeonholed, formative, to let something go, in hindsight. Structure: past perfect and narrative tenses.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A turning point -- the moment after which the direction of a life or a career is different
"The turning point was small and undramatic."
To make a living -- to earn enough money from something to live on
"I was making a living. That was exactly the problem."
To outgrow something -- to develop past something that used to be enough for you
"The commissions kept answering a question I had outgrown."
A commission -- a paid request to make a specific piece for somebody
"Every year somebody commissioned something, and every year it was the same commission."
To be self-taught -- to have learned something without formal training
"She is self-taught, and it shows in the best possible way."
A detour -- a long way round that turns out to matter
"Put the detour in. Nobody on a panel has a straight line either."
To second-guess yourself -- to keep questioning a decision long after you have made it
"I second-guessed myself for three years before I could say it out loud."
A leap -- a decision taken without knowing where you land
"It looked like a leap. It had been prepared for four years."
To be pigeonholed -- to be permanently labeled as one kind of practitioner
"For years I was pigeonholed as a photographer, and the label arrived faster than I could correct it."
Formative -- having shaped the way you work ever since
"Those years were formative, which is not the same as pleasant."
To let something go -- to stop doing something that still works, on purpose
"The hardest part was letting go of something that was not failing."
In hindsight -- looking back, with what you know now
"In hindsight the commissions were the problem."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

She was largely self-taught. Every winter she used to print a new series, and the gallery would sell it before March. For a decade she made a living that way, and every year there was at least one commission.

Then a curator asked her what the photographs were for. By that afternoon she had been photographing the same subject for nine years, and she had already stopped believing in the answer she gave. The turning point was that sentence, and nothing more dramatic than that. She did not photograph anything for eleven months.

What made it hard was not failure. She was letting something go that still worked. For three years afterwards she second-guessed herself, partly because the art world had pigeonholed her long before she decided to change. In hindsight, she says, the first decade was formative: the discipline of printing is still in everything she makes. What looked like a leap had in fact been prepared for years, and the detour, as she calls it now, is the half of the work most people never see.

1. "By that afternoon she had been photographing the same subject for nine years." What does this form tell you?
A That she photographed it once, nine years earlier.
B That the nine years were already complete and still running when the curator asked the question.
C That she is still photographing it today.
2. "Every winter she used to print a new series." Why used to and not the past simple alone?
A Because it was a repeated habit over years that has now ended, and used to marks exactly that.
B Because it happened only once.
C Because used to is more polite than the past simple.
3. "She had already stopped believing in the answer she gave." Which happened first?
A Giving the answer, then losing the belief.
B Losing the belief, and only afterwards giving the answer she no longer believed.
C The two happened at exactly the same moment.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Perfect and Narrative Tenses

GRAMMAR

Two layers of the past: the story line, and what was already true when the story got there.

LayerFormExample
The story linepast simpleA curator asked me a question.
Already finished before ithad + past participleI had already stopped believing in them.
Still running up to ithad been + verb-ingI had been photographing it for nine years.
Old habit, now overused to + base verbI used to print a series every winter.
Repeated past actionwould + base verb, never for statesThe gallery would sell it by March.
Signpostsby the time, by then, already, before thatBy the time I left, I had been there ten years.

Never: I used to leave in 2014 · by then I had took the decision · when I was young I would be shy · when the curator asked, I already stopped believing.

Stage 2: Put the Story in Order

Order

Six lines of the story. Put them in the order that makes the turn make sense to a listener.

?"I came to this from photography, and I was largely self-taught."
?"For a decade I made a living from editions and commissions."
?"By then I had been circling the same question for nine years."
?"The turning point was one question I could not answer honestly."
?"Letting go was hard precisely because the work was not failing."
?"In hindsight those years were formative, and I say so on purpose."

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen, then record yourself. Let had already collapse the way a native speaker does.

By the time I left, I had been working on the same question for years.
The medium changed. The question did not.
I was not letting go of a failure, and that is what made it hard.
In hindsight, those years were formative rather than incidental.
I put the detour in my biography, and I do not apologize for the years.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a selection panel would want to hear.

An interviewer asks how long you had been in photography before the change. You answer:
A "When I changed, I already worked ten years with it."
B "By the time I changed, I had been working in photography for ten years."
C "I am working in photography for ten years before."
You want to describe something you did every winter for a decade. You say:
A "Every winter I used to print a new series."
B "Every winter I was using to print a new series."
C "Every winter I would be a printer of series."
A panel asks whether you regret the earlier decade. You answer:
A "It was a waste of time, but I am here now."
B "I prefer not to talk about that period."
C "In hindsight those years were formative, and the discipline of printing is still in everything I make."
You want to say you stopped something on purpose, although it was still working. You say:
A "I let it go while it was still working, and that is what made it difficult."
B "It failed, so I stopped."
C "I was pigeonholed, so I had no choice about anything."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. This one is the benchmark for the whole block.

In three minutes, tell your whole story: where you started, what had already changed when the turn arrived, what you let go of, and what the earlier years gave you that you still use. Use at least three past perfect forms, and do not apologize for a single year.
