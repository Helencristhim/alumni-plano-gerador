Podcast
"An Expert Translation of Diplomatic Speak" — NPR

A short segment in which a Washington correspondent takes official phrases apart and says what each one actually means. Connection to Lesson 6: it is today's lesson performed live, on real statements, in a few minutes. Transcript on the page.

Tip: pause after each phrase he quotes and give your own translation before you hear his. You will be right more often than you expect — the vocabulary from today is most of the work.

Talk
"How Language Shapes the Way We Think" — Lera Boroditsky, TED

A cognitive scientist on how the grammar available to you changes what you notice and what you remember — including who you hold responsible for an accident. Connection to Lesson 6: the section on agency is precisely what a nominalisation does to a sentence, tested experimentally.

Tip: watch to the part about how speakers of different languages remember who broke the vase. Then reread paragraph two of today's text and see whether it looks different.

"Nominalization" — C1 Advanced Grammar (Cambridge CAE)

A focused lesson on turning verbs and adjectives into nouns at C1 level, with the word families that have to be learned individually. Connection to Lesson 6: the grammar kept outside the class, as you asked, in a form that fits a single sitting.
