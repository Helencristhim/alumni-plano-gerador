Chapter 4: The Code

I Saw It. I Didn’t Apply.

Regular verbs, irregular verbs and did

Grammar Discovery

Four Sentences From the Story

"I saw the posting on a Monday."

"I wanted the job, but I didn’t apply."

"Last month a recruiter called me."

"Did you send your CV?"

Look at the blue verbs. Wanted and called end in -ed: regular. Saw does not: irregular. Now the red words: after did and didn’t, the verb goes back to the simple form: apply, send.

JobFormExample
Regular verbsverb + edwant → wanted · call → called · apply → applied
Irregular verbsa new form, learn it as a groupsee → saw · send → sent · think → thought · get → got
Negativedidn’t + verbI didn’t apply.
QuestionDid + subject + verb?Did you send your CV?
Short answersYes, I did. / No, I didn’t.Did you apply? No, I didn’t.
The trapno past form after didNot I didn’t applied. Say I didn’t apply.

In one line: regular verbs take -ed, irregular verbs have their own form; after did and didn’t the verb is simple.

Real Document

One Posting on One Page

JOB POSTING -- HR MANAGER, LATIN AMERICA
Sample posting · fictional company
CompanyInternational logistics company
LocationSão Paulo · hybrid
Experience10+ years in HR, all areas
Team6 people, reports to the HR director
RequirementFluent English
DeadlineFriday, 11:59 p.m.
A posting lists the requirements. Many good candidates read them and close the page. A recruiter reads a posting very differently: the requirements are the ideal, not always a must.
1. How many years of experience does the posting ask for?
Ten or more, in all HR areas.
2. What is the deadline?
Friday at 11:59 p.m.
3. Tell a short story in the past about this posting.
I saw the posting, I read the requirements, and I applied before the deadline.
Listening 1 · A Recruiter Talks

Tom’s Advice

An American recruiter talks about candidates who do not apply. Sound first — no text.

1. Where does Tom work?
In Miami.
2. Why didn’t many of the best people apply?
They thought their English was not good enough.
3. What was Tom’s advice?
Read the requirements, but don’t give up before the deadline.
Listening 2 · Applying With Fear

Sophie’s Application

A woman tells the story of an application she almost did not send. Sound first — no text.

1. Where was the job?
In Edinburgh, in Scotland.
2. When did Sophie send her application?
On Tuesday at eleven at night, one hour before the deadline.
3. Did Sophie get that job?
No, she didn’t. But two months later the recruiter offered her a better one.
