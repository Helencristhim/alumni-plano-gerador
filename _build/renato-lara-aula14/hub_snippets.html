Stage 1.1: Stats Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Ranking — the list that says who is first, second and third overall
"He is fourth in the national ranking."
Average — the number in the middle, when you add everything and divide
"Her average score this season is seven point two."
Leaderboard — the screen that shows the scores during the event
"The leaderboard changed twice in one minute."
Event — one competition inside a longer season
"He has entered five events this year."
Qualify — to get a good enough result to go to the next stage
"She has qualified for the final twice."
Percentage — how many out of a hundred
"His heat win percentage is sixty per cent."
Total — all of the numbers added together
"The total was fifteen point four."
Gap — the difference between two numbers
"The gap between first and second is two points."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the season summary. Then answer the questions.

How many events has she entered this season? Five, all of them. She has qualified for four finals, which is the best record on the tour, and her average total is fourteen point one. Second place has won two events but has not had much consistency, so her average is lower. The gap between them is two hundred and forty points, which sounds like a lot of points and is not: one good heat closes it. How much time have they spent training? Nobody puts that on the leaderboard, and it is the number that decides everything.
How many finals has the leader qualified for?
A Four, out of five events.
B Two.
Why is second place's average lower?
A Because she has won two events.
B Because she has not had much consistency.
Why is it "how much time" and not "how many time"?
A Because you cannot count time one by one.
B Because time is a long word.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

How many, how much, a lot of

The question is always the same: can you count it one by one?

You can count them

How many events have you entered?
He has won a lot of heats.
She has made three finals so far.
a few waves, too many people

You cannot count them

How much time have you spent?
He has not had much luck.
I have spent a lot of money on wax.
a little water, too much wind

Countable: events, heats, waves, points, finals → how many, a few, too many.
Uncountable: time, water, money, luck, training → how much, a little, too much.
a lot of works on both sides. When you are not sure, use it and keep talking.
Numbers: the decimal is point, not comma: fourteen point one. And per cent comes after the number: sixty per cent.

Stage 5: Your season in numbers

Reflection
Describe your season out loud for 30 seconds, using only numbers and what they mean. How many events have you entered? How many finals? What is your average? How much time have you spent training? Check one thing before you send it — every "many" has something countable after it, and every "much" has something you cannot count.
