Grammar Discovery

Two questions, two words

How many events have you entered?
How much time have you spent training?
He has won a lot of heats this season.
He has not had much luck with the draw.

Can you count it?

How many and a lot of go with things you can count: events, heats, waves, points. How much and much go with things you cannot count one by one: time, water, luck, money. And notice the tense: with a season that is still open, all of this rides on the present perfect.

a little water, too much wind

a lot of is the easy one: it works on both sides. When you are not sure, use it.

Listening 1

The numbers before the final day

1. How many events has the leader entered, and how many finals has she qualified for?
All five events, and four finals. It is the best record on the tour.
2. What is the difference between first and second place?
Second has a lower average (13.6 against 14.1) but has won two events, so more big results and less consistency.
3. How big is the gap, and what does the speaker say about it?
Two hundred and forty points. It sounds like a lot and it is not: one good heat closes it.
Listening 2

How many events is too many?

1. What does he say about juniors who enter twelve events?
He has coached kids who have entered twelve and have not improved at all.
2. Why not?
Because they have spent all their time travelling and no time training.
3. What does he say matters more than the number of events?
How much time you have spent in the water between them.
