Grammar Discovery

Something always stands in front of you

Where are you staying?
How long are you staying in Australia?
Do you have any food with you?
Did you pack this bag yourself?
Four questions. In every one, something is in front of "you".

A question needs a helper before the subject

The order is: (question word) + helper + subject + verb. With be, "be" is the helper and it moves on its own: are you staying. With every other verb, you borrow do, does or did — and after them the main verb goes back to its base form: Did you pack, not "did you packed".

The Code

Helper first, verb last

The form

Are you staying with a host family?
Where are you staying?
Do you have any food?
Did you pack this bag?
What does this word mean?

two traps

Never How long you are staying?
the helper comes before "you", always
Never Did you packed?
after do, does and did, the base form comes back

The tense lives in the helper, never in both: Did you pack it? — Does she work there?

Sound Check

Three questions that come out as one word

Where are you staying?

"are you" collapses: "where-a-ya stayin"

Do you have any food with you?

"do you" becomes one sound: "d'ya"

Did you pack this bag yourself?

"did you" becomes "dij-ya" — the question they always ask

Listening 1

Twenty five minutes to Perth

1. What does he tell the passengers to do before the plane lands?
Fill in the card the crew gave them, and read question nine slowly.
2. Who gets in trouble, and who does not?
Nobody gets in trouble for writing yes. People get in trouble for writing no when the answer is yes.
3. What should you do if you are not sure about something in your bag?
Write yes and ask an officer inside.
Listening 2

The desk at passport control

1. What does she ask about the host family?
Who he is staying with, and whether he has their address on his phone.
2. Which two questions are about the bag?
"Did you pack this bag yourself?" and "Is there anything in there that is not yours?" Then she asks about food.
3. What does she ask him to do at the end?
Look at the camera, and not smile.
