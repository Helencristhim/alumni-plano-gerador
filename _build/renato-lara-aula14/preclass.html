Vocabulary

Officer — the person in uniform who checks your passport and asks the questions

Stamp — the mark an officer puts in your passport with one hit
"She puts a stamp in it and gives it back."

Customs — the place at an airport where officers check what you are bringing in
"After baggage claim you walk to customs."
Quarantine — the rule that stops food, plants and dirt from coming into a country
"Sand on your fins is a quarantine problem too."
Baggage claim — the place where you pick up your bag after the flight
"Where is baggage claim?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the questions they ask at the border. Then answer the questions.

You land in Perth and follow the signs to arrivals. At passport control an officer takes your passport, puts a stamp in it, and asks you four questions. Where are you coming from? How long are you staying in Australia? What are you doing here for six months? Who are you staying with? Then you go to baggage claim, take your bag off the belt and walk to customs. There a second officer reads the card you had to fill in on the plane. Do you have any food with you? Did you pack this bag yourself? Does your board bag have sand in it? If you wrote yes, you declare it at the red line and an officer opens the bag — that is quarantine, and it takes two minutes.
In "How long are you staying in Australia?", what comes in front of "you"?
A "are" — the helper, and it always comes before the subject in a question.
B "How long" — and that is the only thing that changes in a question.
Why is it "Did you pack this bag" and not "Did you packed this bag"?
A Because "pack" is an irregular verb.
B Because "did" already carries the past, so the main verb goes back to its base form.
Why does "Does your board bag have sand in it?" use "does" and not "do"?
A Because the subject is "your board bag" — one thing, third person.
B Because the question is about the past.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Questions — the helper goes in front

The order is (question word) + helper + subject + verb. With be, "be" is its own helper. With every other verb, borrow do, does or did.

The form

Are you staying with a host family?
Where are you staying?
Do you have any food?
Did you pack this bag?
What does this word mean?

two traps

Never How long you are staying?
the helper comes before "you"
Never Did you packed?
the base form comes back

One tense, one place: "Did you pack it?" → the past is in did, never in both words.
The classic: "What means this word?" is wrong. Say What does this word mean?
In speech: "do you" sounds like d'ya and "did you" sounds like dij-ya. That is why fast questions are hard to catch.
