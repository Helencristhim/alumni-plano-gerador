Chapter 3: The Code

The Small Verb

I am, you are, he is, she is, it is

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Have in Common?

"I am Camillo Furlan."

"I am not from Germany."

"She is a sales manager."

"Are you from Brazil?"

Look at the orange words. They are all the same verb. What changes: the person, or the verb?

PersonFormExample
II am / I’mI am Camillo. I’m from Brazil.
Youyou are / you’reYou are very kind.
He / She / Itis / ’sShe is from Germany. It is a German brand.
Negativeam not / is not / are notI am not a mechanic. It isn’t big.
QuestionAm I...? / Are you...? / Is she...?Are you from Brazil?
AgeI am + number + years oldI am 32 years old. (Never: I have 32 years.)

In one line: I am, you are, he/she/it is. For a question, the verb goes first: Are you...? Is she...?

Chapter 4: At the Booth

At the Booth

One conversation, two recordings, one badge

Dialogue

Hi, Can I Help You?

L
Hi, welcome to Kessler Brakes. Can I help you?
C
Hi. I am Camillo Furlan. I am from Brazil.
L
Nice to meet you, Camillo. I am Laura. I am the sales manager here.
C
Nice to meet you too. Are you from Germany?
L
Yes, I am. I am from Munich. Are you a mechanic?
C
No, I am not. I am a business owner. I have three repair shops.
L
Three! That is great. Our brand is new in Brazil.
C
Interesting. Here is my business card.
L
Thank you. And here is my card. Our booth is open until six.
Comprehension

About Laura

1. What is Laura's job?
She is the sales manager.
2. Where is Laura from?
She is from Munich, in Germany.
3. What is new in Brazil, according to Laura?
Her brand, Kessler Brakes.
4. When does her booth close?
At six.
