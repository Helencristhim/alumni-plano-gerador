Lesson 10 -- Compliance and the Cost of Getting It Wrong

Grammar Video
"Grammar Lesson: 3rd and mixed conditionals" — British Council

The two forms this lesson is built on, taught side by side: a past that did not happen, and the mixed version where the consequence is still with you today. Connection to Lesson 10: this is the tense of every review meeting you will ever sit in.

Tip: pause after each example and say the same sentence about something small from your own week. The form only becomes automatic on things that do not matter.

Why fining a company for wrongdoing is so difficult to get right, and what a penalty is actually supposed to achieve. Clear American English. Connection to Lesson 10: it is the last paragraph of today's text argued out loud by economists rather than lawyers.

Reading
"Wells Fargo to pay $3.7 billion settling charges it wrongfully seized homes and cars" — NPR

A news report on a real compliance failure, with the numbers attached: the penalty, the harm, and what the regulator said. Connection to Lesson 10: read it as the enforcement report the lesson describes, and look for the moment where somebody could have looked.

Tip: after reading, write the three counterfactuals the article implies but never states — what would have happened if each of those things had been caught earlier. That exercise is the whole grammar of this lesson applied to a real case.
