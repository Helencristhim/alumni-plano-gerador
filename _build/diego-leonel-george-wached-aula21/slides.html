Chapter 3: The Code

If I Had, I Would Have

Third and mixed conditionals: the past that did not happen

Grammar Discovery

Real or Imagined?

"If we had done the biopsy at the first visit, we would have found it earlier."

"If the nurse hadn’t escalated, we wouldn’t have caught the reaction in time."

"If I had trained in the United States, I would be more confident now."

"If we had biopsied it in March, the patient wouldn’t need a big surgery now."

Did these things really happen? Look at the red verbs: what form comes after if, and what form comes in the result? Now the blue ones: is that result in the past, or now?

JobFormExample
Imagined past, past result (third)If + had + past participle, would have + past participleIf we had done the biopsy, we would have found it.
NegativeIf + hadn’t..., wouldn’t have...If she hadn’t escalated, we wouldn’t have caught it.
Less certain resultcould have / might have + past participleThe reaction might have become serious.
Imagined past, result NOW (mixed)If + had + past participle, would + verb (+ now)If I had trained in the US, I would be more confident now.
Result firstno commaWe would have found it earlier if we had done the biopsy.

In one line: had in the if part, would have in the result; if the result is still true now, use would + verb. Never would in the if part.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Debrief at Full Speed

One conversation, two recordings, one form

Dialogue

The Debrief

P
Diego, let’s debrief Tuesday’s case. What happened with Mr. Lopez?
D
He came in with a changing mole. At his first visit, we called it a benign nevus. That was an oversight.
P
Okay. Looking back, what were the red flags?
D
The border was irregular, and it was growing. If we had double-checked the old photos, we would have seen the change.
P
Good. And what about the handoff?
D
The notes didn’t mention the growth. If I had asked the night resident, I would have escalated it the same day.
P
It was a judgment call, and you were new on the team. Don’t second-guess every decision.
D
I know. But I take responsibility. If I had known the system better, I would be faster with it now.
P
That’s accountability. So how did it end?
D
It was a near miss. The biopsy showed a melanoma in situ, so a small excision is enough.
Comprehension

About Dr. Parker

1. What does Dr. Parker ask about first, and what does she ask about after that?
First, what happened with Mr. Lopez. Then, the red flags, and after that, the handoff.
2. Why does she say it was a judgment call?
Because there was no clear rule, and Diego was new on the team.
3. What does she tell Diego not to do?
Not to second-guess every decision.

Event: Changing pigmented lesion on the back; the biopsy was done six months after the first visit. Outcome: melanoma in situ. No harm to the patient (near miss).

What happened: At the first visit, the lesion was called a benign nevus. The irregular border was overlooked, and the growth was not in the handoff notes.
