Lesson 21 -- Pre-class

When the Attending Asks -- The Debrief

A debrief after a difficult case: what happened, what could have changed it, and what you will do next time. Key words: a debrief, a near miss, an oversight, to overlook, a red flag, to escalate, a judgment call, to second-guess, to double-check, a handoff, to take responsibility, accountability. Grammar: third conditional (If I had..., I would have...) and mixed conditionals (If I had..., I would be... now).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A debrief -- a meeting after an event to talk about what happened and what to learn
"We had a short debrief after the difficult case."
A near miss -- a mistake that almost hurt a patient, but did not
"The wrong dose was a near miss: the nurse stopped it."
An oversight -- a mistake that happens because you did not notice something
"Not reading the old chart was an oversight."
To overlook -- to not notice or not think about something important
"It is easy to overlook a small lesion on the scalp."
A red flag -- a sign that something may be seriously wrong
"An irregular border is a red flag in a mole."
To escalate -- to tell a more senior person because a problem is serious
"The nurse escalated the rash to the attending at once."
A judgment call -- a decision you make with your own opinion when there is no clear rule
"Biopsy or follow-up? It was a judgment call."
To second-guess -- to doubt a decision after you make it
"Don’t second-guess every decision you made on call."
To double-check -- to check something again to be sure it is right
"I always double-check the allergies before I prescribe."
A handoff -- the moment one doctor passes a patient and the information to another doctor
"The allergy was not in the handoff notes."
To take responsibility -- to say clearly that a decision or a mistake was yours
"I take responsibility for the delay."
Accountability -- being responsible for your decisions and their results
"Good teams have accountability, not blame."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Friday, Diego has his first debrief with Dr. Parker. A patient came back after six months with a bigger mole, and the biopsy showed an early melanoma. Diego explains the oversight: If we had compared the photos at the first visit, we would have seen the change.

He also talks about the handoff: If the night resident had written about the growth, I would have escalated it the same day. Then he thinks about the present: If I had known the hospital system better, I would be faster with it now.

Dr. Parker listens and says it was a judgment call. Diego takes responsibility, and they write a plan for next time. In the end, it was a near miss: the patient only needs a small excision.

1. "If we had compared the photos, we would have seen the change." Did they compare the photos?
A No, they didn’t, so they didn’t see the change.
B Yes, they compared them, and that is how they saw the change at the first visit.
C They will compare them next week.
2. "If I had known the hospital system better, I would be faster with it now." When is the result?
A In the past, at the first visit of the patient, six months ago.
B Now.
C In the future, after the next debrief with his attending.
3. Why was the case a near miss?
A Because the biopsy was normal.
B Because Dr. Parker didn’t want to talk about it in the debrief on Friday.
C Because the melanoma was early, and a small excision is enough.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Third and Mixed Conditionals

GRAMMAR

Use these to talk about the past that did not happen, and about how it would change things now.

JobFormExample
Affirmative (third)If + had + past participle, would have + past participleIf I had read the chart, I would have seen the allergy.
NegativeIf + hadn’t..., wouldn’t have...If she hadn’t called, he wouldn’t have stopped the drug.
QuestionWhat would you have done if...?What would you have done if the biopsy had been positive?
Less certaincould have / might have + past participleThe reaction might have become serious.
Result now (mixed)If + had + past participle, would + verbIf I had trained in the US, I would be more confident now.

Never: If I would have read... · I would saw... · I would have get...

Stage 2: Put the Story in Order

Order

A resident tells the story of a near miss. Put the sentences in the right order: the mistake, the call, the imagined past, the responsibility and the plan.

?"I prescribed an antibiotic without reading the old chart."
?"Two hours later, the pharmacist called about an allergy."
?"If she had not called, the patient would have taken the drug."
?"The next morning, I told my attending and took responsibility."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

In a debrief, your attending asks: "What could we have done differently?" You say:
A If we would have compared the photos, we would have seen the change in the mole.
B If we had compared the photos, we would have seen the change.
C Nothing. It was not my fault.
A nurse stopped a wrong dose that you prescribed. Nobody was hurt. Your attending asks what happened. You say:
A It was a near miss. I didn’t double-check the dose, and I take responsibility.
B The nurse made a problem of nothing, because the dose was only a little higher than the normal one.
C It was a red flag of the nurse.
A program director asks why you chose dermatology. You want to connect a past decision with your life now. You say:
A If I hadn’t done a rotation in dermatology, I wouldn’t have been a dermatologist now.
B I chose it.
C If I hadn’t done a rotation in dermatology, I wouldn’t be a dermatology resident now.
A new resident keeps asking you if every decision she made on call was wrong. You say:
A Most of them were judgment calls. Don’t second-guess every decision.
B Yes, maybe.
C You should escalate every decision to the attending, even the small ones, because that is always safer.
