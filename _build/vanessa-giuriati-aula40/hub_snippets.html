Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Comfort zone -- The situations where you feel safe and relaxed, with no stress
"Speaking in meetings is outside my comfort zone."
Hold back -- To not say or do something because you are nervous or too careful
"I used to hold back in meetings, even with a good idea."
Self-conscious -- Worried about what other people think of you
"I feel self-conscious about my accent."
Awkward -- Not comfortable, and a little embarrassing
"The first video call in English was really awkward."
Gradually -- Slowly, a little at a time
"Gradually, I'm getting used to speaking English at work."
Second nature -- Something you do so often that you do it without thinking
"After a few months, small talk became second nature."
Come a long way -- To make a lot of progress since the beginning
"You've come a long way since Lesson 1!"
Keep it up -- To continue doing something good in the same way
"Your English is much better. Keep it up!"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Two years ago, I started a new job at an international company, and the first months were really awkward. I wasn't used to speaking English every day, and I felt self-conscious about my accent. In meetings, I always held back: speaking up was far outside my comfort zone. My manager said, "Don't worry. You'll get used to it." She was right. Gradually, I got used to the fast meetings and the different accents. Now I'm used to giving presentations in English, and small talk has become second nature. I'm still getting used to phone calls, but I know I've come a long way. Last week, my manager just smiled and said, "Keep it up."

2. What is the writer used to now?
A Giving presentations in English.
B Phone calls in English with the whole international team, every single morning.
C Holding back in meetings.
3. What is she still getting used to?
A Fast meetings.
B Phone calls.
C The different accents of her colleagues in the international meetings.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Normal now, or still new?

GRAMMAR

Use be used to when something is normal for you. Use get used to for the process, when something new becomes normal, step by step. After both, use the -ing form or a noun.

StructureUseExample
be used to + -ing / nounIt is normal for youI'm used to working late.
not be used to + -ing / nounIt is still new and strangeI'm not used to the cold.
get used to + -ing / nounIt becomes normal, step by stepI'm getting used to my new job.
will get used toIt will become normalYou'll get used to it.

Say 'I'm used to speaking', not 'I'm used to speak'. Used to + verb, with no be, is different: it is a past habit ('I used to live in Rio').
