Chapter 3: The Code

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Have in Common?

"I'm calling about my order."

"The team is packing your boxes."

"We aren't working on Saturday."

"Is the truck leaving today?"

Look at the orange words. Every day, or right now? What two pieces do you see?

JobFormExample
Right nowam / is / are + verb-ingI'm calling. They're packing.
Negativeam not / isn't / aren't + verb-ingWe aren't working today.
QuestionAm / Is / Are + subject + verb-ing?Is the truck leaving?
Spellingleave → leaving, ship → shippingThe truck is leaving.
On the phoneThis is + name / I'm calling about...This is Camillo. I'm calling about my order.
The traproutine = present simpleEvery day: I call suppliers. Now: I'm calling you.

In one line: for right now, use am / is / are + verb-ing. Every day is the present simple.

Chapter 4: The Call

The Call

One conversation, two recordings, one order page

Dialogue

I'm Calling About My Order

L
Kessler Brakes, this is Laura speaking.
C
Hi, Laura. This is Camillo, from Brazil. I'm calling about my order.
L
Hi, Camillo! Good to hear from you. Your order is in the warehouse. The team is packing it right now.
C
Great. Is the truck leaving today?
L
Yes, it is. It's leaving at four. The delivery in Brazil is next week.
C
Perfect. And the invoice?
L
I'm sending it by email now. Sorry, Camillo, another customer is calling me. Can I call you back later?
C
Sure. I'm working at the shop all afternoon.
L
Great. Talk to you soon. Bye!
Comprehension

What Does Laura Say?

1. What is the team doing right now, according to Laura?
They are packing his order.
2. What time is the truck leaving?
At four.
3. Why does Laura need to call him back?
Because another customer is calling her.
