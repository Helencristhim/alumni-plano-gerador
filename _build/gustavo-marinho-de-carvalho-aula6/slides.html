Grammar Discovery

Are Any of These Still True?

"I used to travel every month, and now I do not."

"Every Monday we would meet at eight, whether there was anything to say or not."

"I used to have an office with my name on the door."

"I went to London in 2019, once, for four days."

The red ones are habits and states that are over. The blue one is a repeated action inside a story, and it does the same job — but look at the third sentence and ask yourself whether you could use the blue word there. The orange one is not a habit at all. It happened once.

JobFormExample
A habit or a state that is overused to + base verbI used to travel every month.
A repeated action inside a storywould + base verbEvery Monday we would meet at eight.
A STATE that is over — only used to worksused to, never wouldI used to have an office. Not I would have an office.
One completed event, not a habitpast simpleI went to London in 2019.
A habit you still havepresent simple, never use toI work from home now.
Question and negativedid + use to, with no dDid you use to travel? I did not use to.
The trapto be used to is a different thingI am used to it = I am accustomed to it, now.

In one line: used to means it is over. am used to means you are accustomed to it, and it is still going on.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Nothing here is slowed down for you

Listening 1 · Business Radio

The Reason Goes, the Habit Stays

A short radio piece on why habits at work outlive their reasons. Sound first — no text.

1. What quality does she say habits at work have?
While they last they look like the only possible arrangement, and the moment they end nobody can explain why they were there.
2. What does she say happened to the reason behind a habit?
It carried something real at the beginning, and then the reason quietly went away while the habit stayed.
3. What question does she recommend asking, and what question does she reject?
She recommends asking what exactly we would lose if it disappeared tomorrow. She rejects asking whether it is good, because that is hard to answer.
Listening 2 · A Voice Note

The Hour I Was Really Buying

A voice note about a habit somebody kept for years and then dropped. Sound first — no text.

1. What did he use to do before every meeting, and when did he do it?
He used to print every document and every set of numbers, one copy for each person, and he would do it the night before.
2. What does he say the honest reason was?
Holding paper made him feel ready in a way that a screen did not, and that had nothing to do with the meeting.
3. What did he discover when it became impossible, and what did he actually need?
That nobody had ever read them, not once. What he needed was ten minutes of reading beforehand.
Real Document

What Changed, the Short Version

What has changed since 2019 — the short version
Internal notice · sent to everybody who works with us

Days in the office. We used to expect five days from everybody. We no longer do. Tuesday to Thursday is when most people are in, and that arrived on its own rather than by decision.

The Monday meeting. Every Monday we would meet at eight to report what had already been written down on Friday. That has gone for good, and nobody has asked for it back.

Printing. We are phasing out the printers on the second floor. If a document has to be read face to face, say so in advance and we will arrange it.

What has not changed. Dates are still confirmed in writing, and you still do not have to reply outside working hours.

Sample internal notice written for this lesson. The company is invented.
1. Find the sentence about a state that is over. Why can it not use would?
“We used to expect five days from everybody.” Expect is a state, not a repeated action, and would only works for repeated actions.
2. Which line describes a repeated past action, and how do you know?
“Every Monday we would meet at eight.” It happened over and over, on a fixed day, inside a story about how things were.
3. The last paragraph deliberately uses two structures from earlier lessons. Which, and why here?
are still confirmed and you do not have to. A notice about change works better when it also says clearly what has NOT changed, and what carries no obligation.
