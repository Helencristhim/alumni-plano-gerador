Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

The Way We Used to Work -- Habits That Are Over

A habit looks like the only possible arrangement, right up to the day it ends. Key words: a shift, face to face, to be the norm, to phase out, overnight, a habit, to look back on, for good. Structure: used to and would for past habits.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A shift -- a change from one situation to another
"The shift did not happen slowly. It happened in a fortnight."
Face to face -- with the other people physically in the same room
"Some conversations are still better face to face, and that is one of them."
To be the norm -- to be the usual and accepted thing
"Five days in the office used to be the norm. It is not any more."
To phase out -- to stop using something gradually, in stages
"They did not ban it. They phased it out over two years."
Overnight -- very suddenly, in a very short time
"It changed overnight, and it has not gone back."
A habit -- something you do regularly, almost without thinking
"It was never a rule. It was a habit, and nobody noticed the difference."
To look back on -- to think about a time in the past, from where you are now
"When I look back on it, I am not sure it ever made sense."
For good -- permanently, with no going back
"Some of it came back. That part went for good."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

English has a shape that Portuguese does not, and it exists for one job only: saying that something was a habit and is now over. I used to travel every month. We used to expect five days in the office from everybody. Both sentences carry a second piece of information without stating it — that this is no longer true. The past simple does not do that. I travelled every month leaves the listener wondering whether you still do.

There is a second form for the same territory. Every Monday we would meet at eight. Would works for a repeated action inside a story about how things were, and it sounds more like narrative than used to. But it has a limit that catches people out: it does not work with states. I used to have an office is correct; I would have an office is not. If the verb is have, be, know, like or want, only used to is available.

Two more things. In the question and the negative, the d disappears, because did is already carrying the past: did you use to travel, I did not use to. And then the one that catches every learner at least once. I am used to it is not a past habit at all — it means you are accustomed to something, now, in the present. I used to work from home means you stopped. I am used to working from home means you are comfortable with it and still doing it. Same three words, opposite information.

1. What does “I used to travel every month” tell the listener that “I travelled every month” does not?
A That it is no longer true.
B That it happened only once.
C That it was unpleasant.
2. Why is “I would have an office” wrong?
A Because would is only for the future.
B Because have is a state, and would only works for repeated actions.
C Because offices are countable.
3. What is the difference between “I used to work from home” and “I am used to working from home”?
A There is no real difference.
B The first is formal and the second is spoken.
C The first means you stopped. The second means you are comfortable with it and still doing it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used To and Would

GRAMMAR

used to means it is over. am used to means you are accustomed to it, and it is still going on.

JobFormExample
A habit or a state that is overused to + base verbI used to travel every month.
A repeated action inside a storywould + base verbEvery Monday we would meet at eight.
A STATE that is over — only used to worksused to, never wouldI used to have an office.
One completed event, not a habitpast simpleI went to London in 2019.
A habit you still havepresent simple, never use toI work from home now.
Question and negativedid + use to, with no dDid you use to travel? I did not use to.
The trapto be used to is a different thingI am used to it = accustomed to it, now.

Never: I would have an office · I used to work from home now · Did you used to travel? · I used to the new schedule
