Lesson 10 -- Pre-class

Next Steps in Writing -- What Is Agreed After the Call

They said yes, or close enough. From here the deal is six dates and the names attached to them, and in English each kind of date takes a different verb. Key words: a kick-off, a handover, onboarding, a go-live, a pilot, to lock in, lead time, a contingency, to align, a steering group. Structure: will, going to, present continuous and present simple for the future.

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A kick-off -- the first meeting where a project formally starts
"The kick-off is on the fourth. Everything before that is still selling."
A handover -- passing responsibility from one team to another
"The handover from sales to delivery is where most projects lose two weeks."
Onboarding -- getting a new customer set up and working
"Onboarding is four weeks, and three of them are theirs, not ours."
A go-live -- the date the system starts being used for real
"Go-live is the first of July, and nobody moves it after the invitations go out."
A pilot -- a small first version of the project, used to test it
"Two customers, one month. A pilot exists to find out, not to prove."
To lock in -- to fix something so that it cannot change
"Let us lock in the kick-off date now, while everybody is still in the room."
Lead time -- the time between asking for something and getting it
"The lead time on their firewall change is six weeks. That is the plan, not our work."
A contingency -- extra time or money kept aside for what goes wrong
"Two weeks of contingency, and I would rather say so than hide it in the estimate."
To align -- to make sure everybody has the same understanding
"We align on the plan in a meeting, not in an email. Emails hide disagreement."
A steering group -- the small group that decides when the project has a problem
"Three people in the steering group, and one of them has to be able to say yes."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

After the yes there is a gap, and in most companies it is about three weeks long. Sales has finished and delivery has not started, so nobody owns anything. The cure is one sentence said before the end of the call where the customer agrees: let us lock in the kick-off date now, while everybody is still in the room.

From there the project is six dates and six names. The kick-off, the handover from sales to delivery, onboarding, the pilot, the steering group, and the go-live. Somewhere in the middle sits the line that breaks plans: a lead time that belongs to the customer, like a firewall change that takes twenty minutes of work and six weeks of waiting.

English marks how real each date is by the form of the verb, and this is the part that Portuguese does not do. "We are meeting on the fourth of March" means it is in the calendar. "They are going to hire a project manager" means the decision was already taken, before this conversation. "I will send you the plan tonight" means you are deciding as you speak. And "the pilot starts on Monday" is a fixed timetable, like a train.

Two mechanical rules. Never will plus -ing: we will meeting does not exist. And after when or if, English stays in the present, so it is when we have the budget, not when we will have. Finally, write the contingency down. Two weeks hidden inside an estimate get used silently and become a delay; two weeks written down get used openly and stay part of the plan. Then align in a meeting rather than by email, because email hides disagreement.

1. "We are meeting on the fourth of March." What does the form tell the other side?
A That the meeting is happening right now.
B That the date is already in the calendar, not a plan or an intention.
C That the meeting might be moved.
2. Why does the text say the customer's lead time is the line that breaks plans?
A Because it is long, it is real, and nothing in anybody's system reminds the customer that it exists.
B Because customers are usually slow at everything.
C Because firewall changes are technically very complex.
3. Why write the contingency down instead of hiding it in the estimate?
A Because customers always ask for it in writing.
B Because hidden time gets used silently and becomes a delay, while written time gets used openly and stays part of the plan.
C Because a written contingency cannot be used.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Talking About the Future

GRAMMAR

One question sorts them all: when was it decided?

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The kick-off is already in both calendars for Tuesday. You say:
A "We will meeting on Tuesday."
B "We are meeting on Tuesday at ten."
C "We will meet on Tuesday, I think."
You decide, right there in the call, to send the plan that evening. You say:
A "I will send you the plan tonight."
B "I am going to send you the plan tonight, I have just thought of it."
C "I send you the plan tonight."
You want to name a wait that belongs to the customer, without blaming them. You say:
A "Your IT is always very slow with these things."
B "The firewall change has a six-week lead time on your side, so it is on the plan from day one."
C "If your team is late, the project will be late."
The customer has just said yes and the call is nearly over. The most valuable sentence is:
A "Great news, I will be in touch soon."
B "We will send everything by email later this week."
C "Shall we lock in the kick-off now, while everybody is here?"
