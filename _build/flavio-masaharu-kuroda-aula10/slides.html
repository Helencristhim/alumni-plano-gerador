In English the form of the verb already tells the other side whether the date is in a calendar, in a plan, or only in somebody's head. Getting it wrong makes a firm date sound loose — or a wish sound like a commitment.

Chapter 4: The Code

Four Ways to Say the Future

And each one says how real the date is

Grammar Discovery

Four Sentences, Four Forms

"We are meeting on the fourth of March." (it is in the calendar)

"They are going to hire a project manager." (decided last week)

"I will send you the plan tonight." (deciding now, as I speak)

"The pilot starts on Monday and ends on the thirtieth." (fixed timetable)

All four are about the future, and every one uses a different form. Ask each one the same question: when was this decided? The blue note is the answer.

JobFormExample
Already in the calendaram / is / are + verb-ingWe are meeting on the fourth.
A plan decided before nowbe going to + bare verbThey are going to hire a manager.
A decision made as you speakwill + bare verbI will send it tonight.
A fixed timetablepresent simpleThe pilot starts on Monday.
A promise or an offerwillI will chase it myself.
A prediction with evidencebe going toAt this rate we are going to miss July.
Never will after when or ifpresent, as in lesson 7Not when we will have the budget
Never will plus -ingwill + bare verbNot we will meeting; say we are meeting.

In one line: the -ing form means it is booked, going to means it was already decided, and will means you are deciding right now.

Firewall change6-week lead time — their IT
PilotStarts 6 April, two customers
Steering groupMonthly, first one after go-live
Go-live1 July, with two weeks of contingency
Only one of these six lines belongs to somebody outside both companies, and it is the one with the six-week lead time. It is also the only line that will not send anybody a reminder. The contingency is written down on purpose: two weeks that nobody has to ask for, and that nobody has to pretend do not exist when they are used.
1. Which of the six lines is the riskiest, and why?
The firewall change. It belongs to their IT team, it has a six-week lead time, and it is the only line that will not remind anybody it exists.
2. How would you say the kick-off line and the pilot line out loud, and why are the two forms different?
We are meeting on the fourth of March, because it is in the calendar; and the pilot starts on the sixth of April, because it is a fixed timetable.
3. Why does the note say the contingency is written down on purpose?
Because two weeks hidden inside the estimate get used silently and become a delay. Two weeks written down get used openly and remain part of the plan.
Chapter 6: Your Turn

Two Recordings, Then You

Listening 1 · The Gap

The Three Weeks Nobody Owns

A project manager on the space between the yes and the kick-off. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say happens between the yes and the kick-off?
Usually three weeks in which nobody owns anything, because sales has finished and delivery has not started.
2. What is the one thing she says to do before the end of the call where the customer says yes?
Lock in the kick-off date while everybody is still in the room, because a date that is agreed later is a date that slips.
3. What does she say about lead times that belong to the customer?
That they are the ones that break the plan, because they are real, they are long, and nothing in anybody's system reminds the customer that they exist.
Listening 2 · From the Customer

The First Two Weeks Tell Him Everything

A customer on what happens after he signs. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say the first two weeks after signature tell him?
What the next two years will look like. He says he judges the whole relationship by what happens in that fortnight.
2. What does he say about the salesperson disappearing after the signature?
That it is the most common thing that happens and the thing he remembers longest, because it tells him who he was really dealing with.
3. What does he say he wants written down, even when it is bad news?
The contingency and the lead times that belong to his own side. He says a plan that hides them is a plan that will surprise him later.
