Chapter 4: The Code

Just, Already and Yet

Three words that say where the project is

Grammar Discovery

What Do the Red Words Tell You?

"We have just received the first draft."

"The regulator has already approved the plan."

"We have not signed the term sheet yet."

"Have you sent the minutes yet?"

All four sentences use have + past participle. The red words add a meaning: a moment ago, earlier than expected, or not done, but expected. Look at where each word sits in the sentence.

JobFormExample
It happened a moment agohave + just + past participleWe have just received the draft.
It is done, maybe earlier than expectedhave + already + past participleThe regulator has already approved it.
It is not done, but we expect ithave not + past participle ... yetWe have not signed it yet.
Asking if something is doneHave you + past participle ... yet?Have you sent the minutes yet?
Positionjust and already go before the participle; yet goes at the endWe have already started. / We have not started yet.
The trapjust now and ago go with the past simpleNot We have received it two days ago. Say We received it two days ago.

In one line: just = a moment ago, already = done (maybe early), yet = not done but expected, at the end of the sentence.

The email repeats the facts the meeting agreed on, so that everybody stays on the same page. Notice that every line answers one question: is it done, or not done yet?
1. Which two items are not done yet?
The environmental license and the term sheet.
2. Turn the line "Contract draft" into a full sentence with just.
"We have just received the contract draft, and we will send our comments by Friday."
3. Why does the email repeat what the meeting agreed?
So that everybody stays on the same page and there is a written record.
Listening 1 · The Fund Manager

What I Ask in the First Meeting

A pension fund manager explains how she prepares for a meeting in a new country. Sound first — no text.

1. What have her analysts already done before she travels?
They have already read everything that is public: the contract, the regulator's decisions and the news.
2. What are her three questions?
What has already been approved, what has not happened yet, and what has changed in the last month.
3. What did the team in Chile do that impressed her?
They answered the three questions and sent the minutes before she got back to the hotel.
Listening 2 · The Lawyer

When the Investor Pushes Back

A lawyer who advises foreign funds in Latin America describes a difficult meeting. Sound first — no text.

1. How long has he worked with foreign funds?
For about fifteen years.
2. What happened after two meetings with the client who defended every point?
Nobody had moved.
3. What has the fund already asked for?
A new draft.
