Lesson 02 -- Pre-class

The Room With the Fund -- A Meeting With an Investor

An international fund wants to know where the project is. Key words: an agenda, the minutes, to follow up, to push back, a pension fund, due diligence, a draft, a deadline, a term sheet, to be on the same page. Structure: present perfect with just, already and yet.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An agenda -- the list of topics to discuss in a meeting
"The first item on the agenda is the timeline."
The minutes -- the written record of what people said and decided in a meeting
"Can you send the minutes by Friday?"
To follow up -- to contact somebody again to continue something you started
"I will follow up with the fund next week."
To push back -- to disagree with or resist a proposal
"The investors pushed back on the timeline."
A pension fund -- an organization that invests money to pay future pensions
"A Canadian pension fund owns part of the concession."
Due diligence -- a careful check of a company or project before an investment
"Their lawyers are doing due diligence on the contracts."
A draft -- a first version of a document that is not final yet
"We have just received the first draft of the agreement."
A deadline -- the date or time when something must be finished
"The deadline for comments is the end of the month."
A term sheet -- a short document with the main conditions of a deal, before the contract
"The term sheet is only three pages long."
To be on the same page -- expression: to agree and understand a situation in the same way
"Before we continue, I want to make sure we are on the same page."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Emma works for a Canadian pension fund. Before her trip to Rio, her team has already read the concession contract, and her lawyers have just started due diligence.

In the meeting, the Brazilian team is clear. The regulator has already approved the plan. The environmental license has not been issued yet. They have just received the first draft of the agreement.

Emma pushes back on the deadline, but the team does not argue. They agree on the facts and send the minutes the same afternoon. Emma has not decided yet, but she has already asked for a second meeting.

1. Has the regulator approved the plan?
A Yes, it has already approved the plan.
B No, it has not approved it yet.
C The text does not say.
2. What does "the license has not been issued yet" mean?
A The license will never be issued.
B The license is not ready now, but it is expected.
C The license was issued a moment ago.
3. Where does yet go in a negative sentence?
A Before the verb: "has yet not decided".
B At the end: "has not decided yet".
C After have: "has yet decided".

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Just, Already and Yet

GRAMMAR

Three small words with the present perfect that say exactly where a project is.

JobFormExample
It happened a moment agohave + just + past participleWe have just received the draft.
It is done, maybe earlier than expectedhave + already + past participleThe regulator has already approved it.
It is not done, but we expect ithave not + past participle ... yetWe have not signed it yet.
Asking if something is doneHave you + past participle ... yet?Have you sent the minutes yet?
Positionjust and already go before the participle; yet goes at the endWe have already started. / We have not started yet.
The trapjust now and ago go with the past simpleNot We have received it two days ago. Say We received it two days ago.

Never: We have yet not signed · We have just received it two days ago · The regulator has approved already not the plan · Did you already send the minutes yesterday?

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

An investor asks if the plan is approved. It was approved in August. You say:
A "Yes, the regulator has already approved it."
B "Yes, the regulator has approved it yet."
C "Yes, the regulator already approves it."
The contract is not signed, but you expect to sign it. You say:
A "We have yet not signed the contract."
B "We have not signed the contract yet."
C "We did not already sign the contract."
You received the draft ten minutes ago. You say:
A "We have just received the draft."
B "We have received the draft ten minutes ago."
C "We just have received the draft."
At the end of the meeting, you want to confirm everybody agrees. You say:
A "Are we in the same page?"
B "Before we finish, let me make sure we are on the same page."
C "We are in the same paper, right?"
