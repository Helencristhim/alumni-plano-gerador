Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

Laís Presents -- A Real Class Presentation in English

Standing up for twelve minutes in front of everybody. Key words: outline, audience, slide, to rehearse, handout, to sum up, to point out, confident, eye contact, to run out of time. Structure: gerunds and infinitives -- the verb before decides what comes next.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. Do not translate — the example sentence is the meaning.

Outline -- the plan of your main points, in the order you will say them
"Learn the outline, not the words. The words will come."
Audience -- the people who are listening to you
"Look at the audience, not at the screen behind you."
Slide -- one screen of a presentation
"If you run out of time, skip a slide, never the ending."
To rehearse -- to practise out loud, standing up, before the real thing
"Reading it silently is not rehearsing, it is reading."
Handout -- a paper you give to the people listening
"One page of handout beats three extra slides."
To sum up -- to say your main points briefly, at the end
"To sum up, three things: the street, the school and the food."
To point out -- to draw attention to something specific
"I want to point out one detail in this photograph."
Confident -- sure of yourself, and calm about it
"You do not have to feel confident. You have to look up."
Eye contact -- looking into the eyes of the people you are talking to
"Three seconds of eye contact per person is enough."
To run out of time -- to have no time left
"If you run out of time, do not speed up. Skip a slide."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

I decided to write the ending first, which sounds backwards and is not. Everybody remembers the last twenty seconds and almost nobody remembers the middle. Then I built the outline around it: an opening, three points, and the ending I had already written. I avoided making the slides until the outline was finished, because making slides feels like working and is not.

Then I practised standing up, out loud, in my room with the door closed. Three times. Reading it silently is not rehearsing, it is reading, and the difference shows up at minute two. I stopped memorising the sentences after the first attempt, because people who memorise stop breathing and then panic.

On the day I managed to look up. That is the whole trick: not feeling confident, just keeping eye contact with three different people. I'm quite good at explaining things to my friends, so I tried to talk to the audience the same way. I ran out of time at minute eleven, skipped a slide instead of speeding up, and still finished by summing up in three sentences.

Why does the writer say she wrote the ending first?
A Because it is the shortest part.
B Because people remember the last twenty seconds and almost nobody remembers the middle.
C Because the teacher told her to.
In "I'm good at explaining things", why is it explaining and not to explain?
A Because at is a preposition, and after a preposition the verb always takes -ing.
B Because it is in the past.
C Because good is an adjective.
"I stopped memorising the sentences" means:
A She paused in order to memorise them.
B She stopped doing it — the memorising ended.
C She never started.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- -ing or to? The Verb Before Decides

GRAMMAR

There is no logic here, only lists. But one shortcut covers half of the mistakes.

FormUseExample
enjoy, avoid, finish, keep, practise, suggest, imagine, missverb + -ingI enjoy speaking.
I practised standing up.
decide, want, plan, hope, need, learn, manage, agree, offerto + verbI decided to start.
I managed to finish.
any preposition (at, of, about, in, for, without, before, after)always -ing. This is the shortcut and it never fails.good at explaining
nervous about presenting
stop, remember, tryBoth are possible and the meaning changes. I stopped speaking = I went quiet. I stopped to speak = I paused in order to speak.
at the start of a sentenceEnglish prefers -ing.Presenting in another language is slower, not harder.
after would like / would loveto + verb.I'd love to answer that.

After a preposition it is always -ing. Learn that one rule properly and the rest is just exposure.

Reflection
