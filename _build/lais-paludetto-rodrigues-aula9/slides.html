Chapter 3: The Code

-ing or to?

The first verb decides. There is no logic, and there is a shortcut.

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I enjoy speaking in front of people."

"I decided to start with a photograph."

"I avoid reading the slides out loud."

"I'm good at explaining things to my friends."

Two use -ing and two use to. What decides it — the second verb, or the word before it?

After thisComes thisExample
enjoy, avoid, finish, keep, practise, suggest, imagine, missverb + -ingI enjoy speaking.
I practised standing up.
decide, want, plan, hope, need, learn, manage, agree, offerto + verbI decided to start.
I managed to finish.
any prepositionThis is the shortcut, and it never fails: after at, of, about, in, for, without, before, after the verb always takes -ing. "good at explaining", "nervous about presenting", "without looking at the screen".
stop, remember, tryThese take both, and the meaning changes. I stopped speaking = I went quiet. I stopped to speak = I paused in order to speak. Remember to send it = do not forget. Remember sending it = you have the memory.
at the start of a sentenceUse -ing: "Speaking in another language is slower, not harder."

There is no logic to memorise, only lists. But after a preposition it is always -ing, and that single rule covers half of your mistakes.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I'm good at explain things."
"I'm good at explaining things."
"I enjoy to speak in front of people."
"I enjoy speaking in front of people."
"To present in another language is slower."
"Presenting in another language is slower."

After a preposition, always -ing. After enjoy, avoid and practise, always -ing. And at the start of a sentence, English prefers -ing to the infinitive.

Advice from Somebody Who Just Did It

S
You're tomorrow, right? Can I tell you what I got wrong this morning?
L
Please. I've finished my outline but I haven't rehearsed out loud yet.
S
Then do that tonight. I spent four hours on the slides and fifteen minutes on the talking. Guess which one mattered.
L
That bad? What actually happened?
S
I forgot to look up. Eleven minutes reading the screen, zero eye contact. I have no idea if anyone was listening.
L
Okay, I'll avoid doing that. I'm planning to memorise the first sentence and then just talk.
S
That's smart. And decide your ending now. I ran out of time and skipped mine, which was the only good part.
L
So: rehearse standing up, look up, and protect the ending. I can manage three things.
Comprehension

About Sam

1. How did he divide his preparation time?
Four hours on the slides and fifteen minutes on the talking.
2. What did he forget to do during the presentation?
Look up. He read the screen for eleven minutes with no eye contact.
3. What does he say he skipped, and why does it matter?
His ending, because he ran out of time — and it was the only good part.
Listening

Three Things Before You Start

Your teacher, five minutes before the class ends. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say not to memorise the whole thing?
Because students who memorise stop breathing after two minutes and then panic.
2. What does she say rehearsing actually means?
Standing up and speaking out loud at home — reading it silently is just reading.
3. What should you do if you run out of time?
Not speed up — skip a slide and keep the ending, because people remember the last twenty seconds.
Listening 2

What He Would Do Again

Sam presented this morning and he is being very honest. Sound first — no text.

1. How much time did he spend on each part of the preparation?
About four hours on the slides and about fifteen minutes on practising the talking.
2. What happened to his ending?
He ran out of time and skipped it, which was the one thing he should never have skipped.
3. What would he do differently?
Practise standing up, out loud, in his room with the door closed, and stop caring about the font.

Role-Play

Interrupted in the Middle

Scenario: Present the middle section. Twice, somebody in the audience will interrupt with a question. Answer it, and get back to your outline without losing your place.

Keywords:

Good question I'll come back to that Which brings me to The second thing I want to point out
Free Practice

The Whole Presentation

Scenario: Stand up and deliver the whole thing, from the opening to "I'm happy to answer questions". Your object, your photograph, your food, and the ending you decided first.

No keywords, no interruptions, no stopping. If you lose a word, go around it and keep going.
