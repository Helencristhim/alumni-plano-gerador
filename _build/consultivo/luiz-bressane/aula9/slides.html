Lesson 09

Two Accounts of
the Same Night

Nobody is lying.
They still do not say the same thing.

1 · What she asked for

Three minutes, and she has read neither

From: a visiting colleague, another jurisdiction

Sample message, written for this lesson. No real case, no real person.

“I have both files but I will not have time to read them before we talk. Can you give me three minutes on where the two accounts stop agreeing? I need to know which parts are in the documents and which parts are your reading — I have to repeat this to someone else afterwards.”

What does she need from you, and what will she do with it?

2 · Before you read

Two honest people, two different nights

Which is more likely to be exact about the hour — the person who was there, or the record written afterwards?
Which is more likely to know who spoke first?
If the two disagree, does that make one of them false?
3 · Read them once

Read both, for what they are

Witness account — Ms. Ferraz

Sample document, written for this lesson. Not a document of any real proceeding.

I got home a little before eleven. There were four of them on the corner, by the gate.

One of them called out to me first. I did not speak until he did. I answered, and then I went in.

I did not see anyone leave. I was not there long enough to say what happened after.

Incident report — officer on duty

Sample document, written for this lesson. Not a document of any real proceeding.

Attendance recorded at 23:40. Three individuals present at the location on arrival.

The complainant states that she addressed the group. No further exchange reported.

No mention of any individual leaving the scene. This report records what was found on arrival and what was stated at the time.

4 · Where it says so

Six statements, and where each one comes from

Say which text supports each statement.

Two of them are not supported by one text alone. Finding those is the point.

5 · What kind of claim

What the text says, and what you concluded

Six sentences someone might say about these two accounts.

Classify each one, then check.

6 · Say where it came from

Attach the source, while you say it

Same six statements. Now say each one with its source built in.

According to the account, …
The report states …
She claims that … — the report says the opposite.
Both of them …  ·  Neither of them mentions …
As far as I can tell, … — that is my reading, not theirs.
7 · Your briefing

Brief her, and hold it

Your teacher is the colleague.
She has read neither account, and she has another call after this one.

What does she need to know, and what does she have to decide?

Open with what each text is, in one sentence.
Give the divergences, and say which text each one comes from.
Say where the two agree — it counts.
Mark the part that is your reading, not the text’s.
Close with what neither text says, and what you would ask for.
According to…  ·  The report states…  ·  She claims…
Both accounts…  ·  Neither of them mentions…
As far as I can tell…  ·  What I would want to ask is…
8 · What just happened

Again, in ninety seconds

She has now read the report — but not the account.

Same three minutes of content. Ninety seconds. What goes first?

What changes when she has read one of them
You no longer have to explain the report. You do have to say where the account differs from what she already read — and that is now the whole briefing.
