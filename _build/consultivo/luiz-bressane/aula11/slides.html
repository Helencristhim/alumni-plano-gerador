Lesson 11

What Would
Have Changed

You argue in hypotheticals every week.
Today you do it in English, against someone who disagrees.

1 · The case

One fact, eleven days late

Case summary

Sample summary.

The statement was not recorded on the night of the incident. It was taken eleven days later, after the witness had spoken to two other people who were present.

The defence argues the delay explains the inconsistencies. The prosecution argues the inconsistencies are the point.

There are now three versions of what was said on the corner.

In one sentence: what would have been different?

2 · Say it once

He has read it, and he disagrees

Owen, a colleague. Three sentences.

Speed
What does he grant you, and what does he refuse?
Which of his three sentences is hardest to answer?
3 · Where it comes from

Three moves, one turn

Six sentences about the same case.

Classify each one, then check.

4 · The form under it

Concede, then turn

Which of these give the point away, and which keep it?

5 · Say it again

Fast, and without thinking about it

I give you the fact. You give me the hypothetical — in one sentence.

If it had been…, we would not be…
Had she been…, we would have…
Even if…, it would not change…
That is a fair point — the difference is…
6 · Hold it

Now one of yours

A decision in your own work that turned on something happening at the wrong moment.

What would have been different — and what does that change now?

Answer the objection before it is made
And what does that buy us? is the question you should expect. The answer is never “because it is true”. It is always what it changes about the decision in front of you.
7 · What just happened

Three claims, and someone who disagrees

Your teacher is Owen. He has read your note and he does not accept it.

Say it. When he pushes back, say it again — differently.

Start from the fact, not from the hypothetical.
Say what would have been different, and what that changes now.
When he objects — concede what is true, then turn.
If he brings a new fact, adjust the claim. Do not repeat it unchanged.
If… had been…, we would not be…  ·  Had she been…
Even if…, it would not change…
That is a fair point — the difference is…
8 · One more

Same claim, one sentence

Let’s put down what worked and one thing to build on.

You now know the objection that is coming.

Say it so that the objection is already answered.
