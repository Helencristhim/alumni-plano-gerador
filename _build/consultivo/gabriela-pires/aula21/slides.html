3 · Where the auxiliary goes

Find the little word that moved

In each question below, where is the word do, did, are or can?

4 · The rule, in one line

It only swaps two things

You are new here.  →  Are you new here?
She can help you.  →  Can she help you?
You chose biology.  →  Did you choose biology?

In the first two, one word changed places. In the third, a new word appeared — and something else changed too. What?

Two things, and that is all The auxiliary goes in front of the subject. If there is no auxiliary in the sentence, do, does or did comes in to be one.

And then the main verb goes back to its plain form: did you choose, never did you chose. The past is already carried by did, and English never marks it twice.

6 · Ask about my week

The same six, said properly

Go back to the six questions you asked at the start of the hour. Your teacher has them written down. Ask them again.

Your teacher reads back what you said.
You say it again, the way you would say it now.
If it was already right, your teacher tells you so and moves on.
One of the six was probably right the first time. Find out which.
6 · Write three questions

Three you really want answered

Write three questions you would genuinely like to ask your teacher and have never asked. Write them, do not say them yet.

Ways to start

What · Where · When · Why · Who · How · Do you… · Did you… · Have you ever…

Writing first is allowed Writing before speaking is how you produce your longest sentences. Use it here on purpose — and then, on the next screen, put the paper down.
7 · Find out something I have not told you

Three things, and no paper

Turn your three questions over. Find out three things about your teacher that you did not know when the hour started — by asking.

Ask your three questions, without reading them.
After every answer, ask one more question about the answer.
Your teacher answers short, so you have to keep asking.
At the end, say the three things you found out.
The second question is the real one Anyone can ask a question they prepared. The one that comes out of the answer you just heard is the one that makes it a conversation.
