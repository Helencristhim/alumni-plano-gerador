3 · Where the auxiliary goes

Find the little word that moved

In each sentence below, where do the words “do”, “did”, “are”, and “can” go?

4 · The rule, in one line

It only swaps two things

You are new here.  →  Are you new here?
She can help you.  →  Can she help you?
You chose biology.  →  Did you choose biology?

In the first two, one word changed places. In the third, a new word appeared — and something else changed too. What?

How to make the question With be and can, the verb goes in front of the subject.
You are new. → Are you new? · She can help. → Can she help?

With other verbs, put do, does or did in front of the subject.
Present: do → Do you know Ana?
Present, with he, she or it: does → Does she know Ana?
Past: did → Did you choose biology?

After do, does or did, the main verb is in its base form. Did already shows the past, so the verb does not change: Did you go?, not Did you went?
