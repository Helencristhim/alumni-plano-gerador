Lesson 22

What I
Want to Do

Someone new wants to know about you — what you study, what you want to do, and what you think of the series and movies you watch.

How many times has someone asked you what you want to do in the future this year?

1 · Who is asking, and why

Nina, first week

Nina · 16 · exchange student

She arrived last week and knows two people. She has never been to Brazil and knows nothing about how school works here. She is in her last year too, and she has to decide the same thing you have to decide. She watches a lot of series, and she wants to know what people here watch.
You have about two minutes before the break ends.

What can you tell and ask her first, and what you can say later?

2 · Your first try

Before anyone helps you

Your teacher is Nina. Tell her about you, what you study and what you want to do, and what you think of a series or movie you watch.

3 · What she actually asks

Four questions Nina can ask you

Can you answer all of them? Which one is the hard one, and what makes it hard?

So what do you study?
And what do you want to do after school? Why that?
What are you watching at the moment? Is it any good?
What if it does not work out?
3 · Someone else answering

Another person, same four questions

Read it once, and compare it with what you said to Nina.

What does he do that you did not do?

Rafa, 17, answering Nina

“I am in my last year. I do biology and chemistry, because I want to study medicine — or I think I do. Honestly, I am not sure yet. What I like is the part where you actually help someone, so if medicine does not work out, I would still want something with people in it. At the moment I am watching a series about young doctors, and I really like it, because it shows the bad days too, not only the exciting ones. What about you? What are you watching?”

4 · What made that one work

Three kinds of sentence

Every line below does one of three jobs (a doubt, a fact, or a reason). Which one does each?

4 · The question nobody prepares for

“What if it does not work out?”

Three of these answers keep you in the conversation. Mark the three.

5 · Say it in three parts

The fact, the reason, the doubt

Take a moment to write three lines — one of each kind — and then say them without looking.

I am in my last year and I do…
I want to…, because what I like is…
I am not sure yet, but I think…
Not being sure is also a sentence I am not sure yet, but… lets you talk about a plan you have not decided, and then say what you do know.
5 · What you think of it

A series or a movie, and why

Your teacher is Nina and tells you what she thinks about three series or movies. Each time, say what you think, with a reason, and ask her why she thinks that.

I really like it, because…  ·  I did not like it, because…
I have not seen it, but I watched…, and it is…
Really? Why do you say that?  ·  I see what you mean, but…
5 · The unexpected one

Four questions, no warning

Your teacher asks four questions you have not seen. Answer each one in two sentences: the answer, and then one more thing.

Let me think…  ·  Honestly?  ·  That is a good question.
I am not sure yet, but…
I have never thought about it. I suppose…
6 · The whole conversation

Nina, for real this time

The conversation with Nina again. Start from the beginning. Talk about yourself, listen to her four questions, and ask one of yours.

Try to speak without stopping.
Say a reason for what you want to do, before she asks for it.
Say one sentence about what you are not sure about.
Say what you think of the series or movie, with a reason.
Answer the question you did not prepare for.
Ask her something back, before the break ends.
7 · What worked, and one upgrade

Two versions of one sentence

Let us put down what worked and one thing to build on.

8 · Once more, in another order

She talks first this time

Same conversation, other way round: Nina starts by telling you about herself and about a series she likes, and she does not ask you anything. You have to get in.

Ask her something first, without waiting for a question from her.
Say your three parts — the fact, the reason, the doubt — in any order.
Say what you think of the series she likes, and ask her why she likes it.
Use the clearer version of your sentence.
End the conversation yourself.
