Lesson 22

What I
Want to Do

Two minutes about you, to someone who has never been to Brazil — and one question you did not see coming.

How many times has someone asked you that this year?

1 · Who is asking, and why

Nina, first week

Nina · 16 · exchange student

Made up for this lesson

She arrived last week and knows two people. She has never been to Brazil and knows nothing about how school works here. She is in her last year too, and she has to decide the same thing you have to decide.
You have about two minutes before the break ends.

What does she need to hear first, and what can wait?

What you study, and what that means here.
What you want to do after school.
Why that, and not something else.
Something you want to know about her.
2 · Two minutes, right now

Before anyone helps you

Your teacher is Nina. Talk for two minutes about you, what you study and what you want to do. Nothing on this screen will help you, and nothing gets corrected.

This is the only two minutes of the block with no preparation at all. What comes out here is what you already have — and everything in the next hour is measured against it.

3 · What she actually asks

Four questions, and one of them is hard

These are the four Nina asks. Three of them you can answer today. One of them you probably cannot answer in Portuguese either.

So what do you study?
And what do you want to do after school?
Why that?
What if it does not work out?

Which one is the hard one, and what makes it hard?

3 · Someone else answering

Another person, same four questions

Read it once, and compare it with what you said five minutes ago.

Rafa, 17, answering Nina

“I am in my last year. I do biology and chemistry, because I want to study medicine — or I think I do. Everyone at home already talks about it like it is decided, and honestly I am not sure yet. What I like is the part where you actually help someone, so if medicine does not work out I would still want something with people in it. Anyway. What about you? Have you decided?”

What does Rafa do that you did not do?

4 · What made that one work

Three kinds of sentence, and you need all three

Every line below does one of three jobs. Which one does each?

4 · The question nobody prepares for

“What if it does not work out?”

Three of these answers keep you in the conversation. Mark the three.

5 · Say it in three parts

The fact, the reason, the doubt

Take one minute to write three lines — one of each kind — and then say them without looking.

I am in my last year and I do…
I want to…, because what I like is…
I am not sure yet, but I think…
Not being sure is a sentence, not a silence I am not sure yet is one of the most useful things you can say about your own future, in any language. It keeps you talking instead of stopping.
5 · The unexpected one

Four questions, no warning

Your teacher asks four questions you have not seen. Answer each one in two sentences: the answer, and then one more thing.

Let me think…  ·  Honestly?  ·  That is a good question.
I am not sure yet, but…
I have never thought about it. I suppose…

The first three of those are from the lesson before this one. They work here too.

6 · The whole conversation

Nina, for real this time

The whole thing, from the beginning. Two minutes about you, her four questions, and one of yours — with nothing on the screen.

Two minutes about you, without stopping.
A reason for what you want to do, before she asks for it.
One sentence about what you are not sure about.
Answer the question you did not prepare for.
Ask her something back, before the break ends.
7 · What worked, and one upgrade

Two versions of one sentence

Let us put down what worked and one thing to build on. Then write the same sentence twice: the way it came out, and the way you would say it now.

8 · Once more, in another order

She asks first this time

Same conversation, other way round: Nina starts by telling you about herself, and asks nothing for the first minute. You have to get in.

Ask her something in the first minute, without waiting to be invited.
Say your three parts — the fact, the reason, the doubt — in any order.
Use the upgraded sentence you just wrote.
End the conversation yourself.
Two minutes, twice The first two minutes of this lesson had no preparation and no model. These ones had four screens and an hour. The difference between them is what these four lessons were for — and it is the thing worth writing down.
