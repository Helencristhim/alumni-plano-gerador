Lesson 19

Two Reviews,
One Movie

Two people watch the same movie.
One review is positive, the other is negative — and both of them have a point.

What is the last movie or episode you finished — and would you tell a friend to watch it?

“Is it any good?”

The situation

Someone asks you about a movie you have just seen. You answer with one sentence, and the answer you choose decides whether the conversation carries on.

Which of these actually answers the question?

2 · Before you read

Two people, one movie

Maya and Chris watched the same movie. Maya recommends it. Chris does not.

What kind of reason makes someone love a movie?
What kind of reason makes someone give up on one?
Can both of them be right about the same movie?
3 · Read them once

Who says watch it?

Read both reviews once and say which one recommends the movie.

Maya_17

The story is simple: a girl walks home, alone, across the country. Nothing explodes. But the two main characters feel like real people, and the ending stays with you for days. It is slow, and that is the point. Worth watching.

chris.reads

Everyone at school says this movie is amazing. I do not agree. The photography is beautiful, but two hours is a long time for a story you can guess in ten minutes. The ending is the only surprise, and it comes too late. I would not recommend it.

5 · What each line is doing

Three jobs, six lines

Every line below does one of three jobs. Which job does each one do?

5 · The line that convinces

The opinion is not the argument

Here is all that is left of each review when you take the reasons out. Reading only these two lines, would you watch the movie?

Maya: “Worth watching.”
It is worth watching, because the two main characters feel like real people.
Chris: “I would not recommend it.”
I would not recommend it, because you can guess the whole story in ten minutes.
Opinion, reason, recommendation The opinion is what you think. The reason is the part the other person can actually use. The recommendation is what they asked you for in the first place.

6 · Two reasons, one turn

Do not stop at the first one

Take one of the titles you just talked about and say it again, this time with two reasons in the same turn.

…, and the other thing is…
Also, …
And it is not only that — …
Two reasons, not the same one twice “It is boring, and nothing happens” is one reason said twice. “It is slow, and the ending does not pay off” is two.

Something you chose

Choose something you have watched, read or listened to — not the movie from the reviews. Tell your teacher about it as if they had never heard of it.

Say what it is, in one sentence.
