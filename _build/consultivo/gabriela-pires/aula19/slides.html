Lesson 19

Two Reviews,
One Film

Two people watch the same film.
One says yes, the other says no. Both are right about something.

What is the last film or episode you finished — and would you tell a friend to watch it?

1 · Someone asks you

“Is it any good?”

The situation

Someone asks you about a film. You have five seconds and one sentence. “It is good” ends the conversation. Anything else keeps it going.

Which of these actually answers the question?

“It is good.”
“It is good, because the characters feel real.”
“Yeah.”
“It is slow, but that is why I liked it.”
“I do not know.”
2 · Before you read

Two people, one film

Maya and Chris watched the same film. Maya recommends it. Chris does not.

What kind of reason makes someone love a film?
What kind of reason makes someone give up on one?
Can both of them be right about the same film?
3 · Read them once

Who says watch it?

Read both reviews once and say which one recommends the film. Do not stop on any word.

Maya_17 · four stars

“Worth it”

The story is simple: a girl walks home, alone, across the country. Nothing explodes. But the two main characters feel like real people, and the ending stays with you for days. It is slow, and that is the point. Worth watching.

chris.reads · two stars

“Not for me”

Everyone at school says this film is amazing. I do not agree. The photography is beautiful, but two hours is a long time for a story you can guess in ten minutes. The ending is the only surprise, and it comes too late. I would not recommend it.

Both reviews are written for this lesson. The film, the site and the two reviewers do not exist.

Six reasons, two people

Read the reviews again and say who gives each reason. One of them is not as simple as it looks.

4 · Is it in the review?

What they actually say

Three of these are true. Mark the three, and be ready to point at the line that proves each one.

5 · What each line is doing

Three jobs, six lines

Every line below does one of three jobs. Which job does each one do?

5 · The line that convinces

The opinion is not the argument

Look at the two reviews again. Cover the reasons with your hand and read only the opinions.

“Worth watching.”
“I would not recommend it.”

With only these two lines, would you watch the film or not?

Opinion, reason, recommendation The opinion is your taste, and nobody can argue with it. The reason is the only part the other person can use. And the recommendation is what they actually asked you for. All three fit in about fifteen words — and the middle one is the one people remember.
It is worth watching, because the characters feel like real people.
I would not recommend it, because you can guess the whole story.
6 · One reason, out loud

Say it with the reason

Your teacher names something below. You say one sentence about it — what you think, and why.

What your teacher will name

A series you gave up on · A film everyone likes and you do not · The last episode you watched · A book you had to read for school

I think it is worth watching, because…
What I liked was…
The problem is…
I would not recommend it, because…
6 · Two reasons, one turn

Do not stop at the first one

Take one of the things you just talked about and say it again — this time with a second reason, without waiting to be asked.

…, and the other thing is…
Also, …
And it is not only that — …
Two reasons, not the same one twice “It is boring, and nothing happens” is one reason said twice. “It is slow, and the ending does not pay off” is two.
7 · Your recommendation

Something you chose

Choose something you have watched, read or listened to — not the film from the reviews. Tell your teacher about it as if they had never heard of it.

Say what it is, in one sentence.
Say what you think about it.
Give two different reasons.
End with what your teacher should do about it.
It is about…
I think it is worth… / I would not recommend…
… because… And the other thing is…
You should watch it. / Do not waste two hours on it.
