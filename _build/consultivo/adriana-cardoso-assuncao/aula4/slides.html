Lesson 04

Fact
or Forecast?

In this lesson you read a news article that mixes facts, plans and forecasts,
and you tell a CEO what is certain and what is not.

What news did someone repeat to you this week that you were not sure was true?

1 · Who is asking

An article reaches the CEO, with one question on top

The CEO of your company forwarded a news article to the leadership team with one line: “Is this going to happen to us?”

Who is asking
The CEO. The leadership meeting starts right after your conversation.
What they need
What the article actually reports, and what is only planned, expected or possible.
What you have
The article, and nothing else about the company in it.
What you want by the end
The CEO does not repeat a forecast as a fact in the meeting.

What does the CEO need from you by the end of the conversation?

2 · Before you read

A closed route, and a warehouse far from it

Answer with what you expect. You check your answers when you read the article.

When a major shipping route closes, what changes first for a warehouse team in Brazil?
In an article like this, which information is usually a fact, and which is a forecast?
Who would give a newspaper a number about people leaving a company: the company, or someone else?
3 · Read it once

Read it once, for the story

Read the article once. What has already happened, and what has not happened yet?

Longer routes, longer shifts: a shipping detour reaches warehouse jobs

For eleven weeks, container ships that used to take one of the shortest routes between Asia and the Atlantic have been going the long way around, after the route was closed to commercial traffic. The detour adds nine to twelve days to each trip, according to two of the largest carriers.

In Brazil, the effect is reaching warehouses far from the ports. Ships now arrive in groups instead of on a regular schedule. Norvale Logistics, which employs 1,312 people at three distribution centers in São Paulo state, says it moved its largest site to 12-hour shifts on arrival days in May.

The company says it will hire up to 300 temporary workers before November, but it has not said for how long. A union representative at the largest site says at least 60 people have left since April, many of them “because of the shifts.” Norvale has not confirmed that figure.

Brightline Advisory, an industry consultancy, estimates that between 800 and 1,200 warehouse jobs in the state could move to temporary contracts if the detour lasts into next year.

The carriers expect the route to reopen by the end of the year. A shipping analyst quoted by the newspaper said it “could easily be March.” Norvale declined to comment on its plans for its Campinas site.

5 · How sure it is

Six phrases from the article, and how sure each one is

Each of these phrases tells the reader how sure the information is. Is it already happening, planned or expected, or only possible?

5 · The same number, three ways

The same number, and one sentence says something else

The company says it will hire up to 300 temporary workers. Two of these sentences say what the article says. Which one says something different, and how many people does your listener imagine?

“Norvale will hire up to 300 temporary workers.”
“Norvale will hire at least 300 temporary workers.”
“Norvale will hire as many as 300 temporary workers.”
Is it the most, or the least? Up to 300 and as many as 300 make 300 the highest number: the company could hire 120. At least 300 makes 300 the lowest number: it could be 500. The union’s “at least 60 people” works the same way: 60 is the lowest figure, and the real one could be higher.
“up to 300” · “as many as 300” — 300 is the most.
“at least 60” — 60 is the least.
“between 800 and 1,200” — a range. Say both ends.
6 · Say how sure it is

The facts and the forecasts, with who says them

Listen to each sentence and say it yourself. Then give your teacher the three pieces of information a CEO would ask about first, each with who says it and how sure it is.

Sentence 1“According to the carriers, the detour adds nine to twelve days to each trip.”
Sentence 2“The company says it will hire up to 300 temporary workers before November.”
Sentence 3“A union representative says at least 60 people have left since April, but the company has not confirmed it.”
Sentence 4“A consultancy estimates that between 800 and 1,200 jobs could move to temporary contracts.”
Sentence 5“The carriers expect the route to reopen by the end of the year, but that is a forecast, not a fact.”

A model briefing

It is an article about a closed shipping route and what it is doing to warehouse jobs. Some of it has already happened: according to the carriers, each trip now takes nine to twelve days longer, and Norvale moved its largest site to 12-hour shifts in May. Some of it is a plan: the company says it will hire up to 300 temporary workers before November. One number is not confirmed: a union representative says at least 60 people have left since April. And some of it is only possible: a consultancy estimates that between 800 and 1,200 jobs in the state could move to temporary contracts if the detour lasts into next year. The carriers expect the route to reopen by the end of the year, but an analyst says it could be March. The article says nothing about closing a site. My reading is that the shifts are the part to watch.
