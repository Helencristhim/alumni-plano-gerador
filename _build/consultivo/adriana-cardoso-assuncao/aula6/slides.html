3 · The time words decide

Look at the time words, then at the verb

In each pair, the time words are different and the verb is different. What do the time words tell you about the verb?

“Turnover rose to 14.6% in the second quarter.”  ·  “It has gone up again since July.”
“We hired 40 people in May.”  ·  “So far, 22 of them have stayed.”
Is the period closed, or still open? When the time words close the period — in May, in the second quarter, between January and June — the verb is usually in the past simple. When the period is still open until now — since July, this year, so far — the verb is usually in the present perfect.
“rose … in the second quarter” — the quarter is over.
“has gone up … since July” — from July until today.
“has stayed … so far” — the count is still running.
4 · The rule you were using

Two periods, two verbs

Read the table. Then look at the sentence you said at the start of the lesson: was it about a closed period or an open one?

A closed periodA period still open
What it saysThe change happened in a time that is overThe change covers a time that continues until now

The changerose from 9.2% to 14.6% · went up by 5.4 points · has stayed at around 31
Soundhas is usually weak and short: it’s gone up, turnover’s risen.
Not how long ago A change that happened yesterday still takes the past simple if you say yesterday. What decides the verb is whether the period you name is over.
