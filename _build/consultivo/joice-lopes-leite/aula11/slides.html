3 · What changed

Three of these are questions

4 · The form under it

One line, after you tried

Action → do
Do you work here? Do you teach math? Do you use AI?

State → are
Are you a teacher? Are you from Chile? Are you tired?

Not yes or no → the question word goes first
What do you teach? Where do you work? How many students do you have?

A statement with a question mark — You work in a school? — is understood, and it is not the form.

Do you like your school, or are you like your school? And — ___ you tired?

5 · Twelve, fast

Do, are, or a question word

Your teacher gives you the subject. You make the question, immediately.

6 · Your six questions

One at a time, with the page open

The six exchanges are still on screen. Use them.

Ask your teacher six questions about her work.

Where do you…?  ·  What do you…?  ·  Do you…?
Are you…?  ·  How many…?  ·  How long…?
7 · Keep it going

Six in a row, nothing on screen

Your teacher is someone you have just met at the meeting.

Six questions, one after the other. At least two of them come from the answer you just heard.

Start with where and what — the easy two.
Then ask something that only makes sense after her answer.
If you miss something, ask for it again before you continue.
Do not stop to translate. Keep going with what you understood.
8 · Half the time

Same six, someone else

A different person, and half the time.

Which two questions do you keep?

When there is less time
Keep the two that get the longest answers. A question that can be answered with yes gives you nothing to ask next — and the next question is the whole point.
