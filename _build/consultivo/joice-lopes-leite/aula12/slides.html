1 · The room

Three of them, and you

Who is on the call

Marta — teacher, Chile. Teacher training in twelve schools. First time in this group.

Paulo — coordinator, Portugal. A public school with nine hundred students. Families and data protection.

Sofia — researcher, Mexico. Studies how teachers learn to use new tools. Two years in the group.

You — and nobody is going to say your name.

Who would you ask first, and what would you ask them?

2 · Try it once

One minute, starting now

No notes, nothing on the screen.

Who are you, and what do you do?

3 · What they say first

Four sentences, and no more

One introduction, four sentences

“Hi, my name is Joice. I am from São Paulo, in Brazil. I work with teacher training, and I am also a school director. This year I am working with AI in schools.”

What does the sentence about this year do that the others do not?

6 · Round one

One minute, then two questions

Your teacher is Marta, Paulo and Sofia. The conversation goes on without you.

Nothing on the screen. Come in, ask two questions — and answer the one they ask you.

Speed
7 · Feedback

Two things, and one number

What gets measured today
Whether you came in without being called. How many questions you asked. Whether you asked for the surprise question to be repeated instead of guessing. Those three are the block, and they are now on record.

Which was harder — asking, or answering?

8 · Round two

The same, in thirty seconds

Someone is leaving the room, and they ask who you are on the way out.

Thirty seconds. What stays?

What thirty seconds is for
Not a shorter introduction — a different one. Keep what is new this year and you hand them a question. Keep the job title and you hand them nothing.
