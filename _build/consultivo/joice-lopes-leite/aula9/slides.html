2 · Before you read

What is in a letter like this

An invitation gives you information. What else does it always do?
Which is more important — what the event is, or when you must answer?
They invited you. What do you think they want from you?
3 · Read it once

Four things, and one date

From: the organising team

Subject: Invitation — international education meeting

Dear Joice,

We are very happy to invite you to our international education meeting in Belém, in November. It is three days, and everything is in English.

Teachers and school leaders from eight countries are coming. In the mornings we have short talks about technology in schools. In the afternoons we work in small groups.

We would like you to bring one experience from your own school and share it with your group. It can be small. Ten minutes is enough.

Please tell us by 30 September if you can come.

Best wishes,
The organising team

What is the meeting about? And what do they want from you?

7 · Your one minute

Tell a colleague what you were invited to

Your teacher is a colleague. She has never heard of this meeting.

What is it, when is it, and what will you do there?

Start with what the meeting is about.
Say when it is, and where.
Say what they ask you to bring.
Say what your part is, and how long it is.

8 · Thirty seconds

Again, in the corridor

The same colleague, but now she is walking to a class.

Thirty seconds. What stays?

When there is less time
The subject and the date stay. The eight countries and the mornings go. If she only hears one thing, it should be that you are going to Belém in November — and that you have ten minutes to fill.
