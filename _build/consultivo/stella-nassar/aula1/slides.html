1 · What you walked into

Four people, one presentation

Where you are

You are in a two-week online summer seminar in art history, in a group of four: Priya, Nate, Tomas and you. The group has to choose one work, present it together, and send the slides by Sunday. You opened the chat after school and there are forty messages.

What does the group have to decide, and who is waiting on you?

2 · Before you read

Group chats decide things sideways

In a group chat, how do you know something was actually decided, and not just suggested?
Who ends up doing the job nobody claims?
What is easier to miss in a chat than in a conversation: the date, the decision, or the disagreement?

7 · Your turn with Tomas

Tell Tomas what he missed

Your teacher is Tomas.
He muted the group two hours ago and he is about to walk into class.

What was decided, what is still open, and what are you taking?

Open with the decision, in one sentence, not with who said what.
Say the two dates out loud, before anyone asks.
Name the job nobody picked up.
Say what you are taking, and what you are not.
Close with the one thing the group still has to answer.
