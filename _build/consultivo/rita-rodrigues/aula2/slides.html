Lesson 02

Sorry, Could You
Say That Again?

In this lesson you listen to part of a management meeting,
and you ask questions when something is not clear.

In a meeting, what do you do when you do not understand a date or a number?

1 · Who is in the meeting

Monday, the management meeting

It is Monday morning. The management team is in the weekly meeting, and the laptops from Ryan’s email are on the list. Ryan and Nora join the meeting for this point.

Who is there
Paula, the CEO. Ryan, the project coordinator on your team. Nora, from IT, also on your team.
The topic
The new laptops for the sales team.
Where you are
In the meeting, with the other directors.
What you need
The new dates, and what Paula decides.

What do you already know about the laptops from Ryan’s email?

2 · Before you listen

Before you hear the meeting, what do you expect?

Answer with what you expect. You check your answers when you listen.

Do you think the delivery date changes again?
What do you think Paula decides about the training?
What would you want to ask Ryan?
3 · Listen once

Listen once, for the main news

Listen to this part of the meeting once, and answer the two questions below.

Speed
Is the news about the delivery good or bad?
What does Paula decide about the training?

5 · What each question does

Questions for when something is not clear

What does the speaker want in each of these questions? Choose one answer for each.

5 · How Paula checks

What Paula repeats, and why

Listen for Paula’s two questions. What does she repeat from what Ryan and Nora said? Why does she repeat it?

Repeat the part you need.
Repeat the part you need Paula repeats only the information she needs to be sure about: a day, a time. The other person can answer with one word, and the meeting keeps going.
“Sorry, could you say that again?” — you did not hear it.
“Sorry, did you say …?” — you heard it, and you want to check one detail.
“So …, right?” — you say what you understood, and the voice goes up at the end.
6 · Asking in time

Your teacher says it fast, and you check

Listen to the four questions. Then your teacher says some short updates. After each one, ask a question to check what you did not hear or are not sure about. You can use the questions you heard.

Question 1“Sorry, could you say that again?”
Question 2“Sorry, did you say Friday or Monday?”
Question 3“Just to check, is that this week?”
Question 4“So the meeting is at ten, right?”
7 · The next point

The meeting continues, and you check

Choose option A or option B. Your teacher gives an update.

Option A — The next point

Paula talks about the new office hours and the date of the next management meeting.

Option B — A meeting from your work

Tell your teacher what the meeting is about. Your teacher plays a colleague who gives an update in that meeting.

Listen to the update.
Ask when a day, a time or a number is not clear.
At the end, say what you understood, and check if it is right.
Sorry, could you say that again?
Sorry, did you say …?
Just to check, …?
So …, right?
7 · Your update, their questions

Now you give the update, and they ask

Give a short update about the laptops or about your own work. Your teacher asks you two questions. Answer them, and ask your teacher to repeat if you need to. Then ask one question.

A quick update: …
Sorry, I didn’t catch that. Could you say it again?
I’m not sure. I can check and tell you.
Does anyone have a question?

What worked, and once more

Let us put down one thing that worked and one thing to build on.

The same update, faster and with a change

Your teacher gives the update from option A or B again, a little faster, and one detail is different. Find the change, and check it before the end.
