Lesson 04

The Weekly
Check-in

In this lesson you give a short update on your work in a team meeting,
answer questions about it, and ask one.

In your meetings at work, what do people usually ask you about?

1 · What you have to do

The check-in, and it is your turn

Every Monday, the team has a short check-in. Each person gives an update on their work, and the others can ask questions.

Your role
A member of the team. You give your update.
Who is listening
Paula, the manager, and the team.
What can happen
Someone asks a question, and you may not know the answer.
What you want by the end
The team knows where your work is, what comes next, and what you need.

What does the team need to know from your update?

2 · Say it now

Before anything else, give your update

Choose option A or option B. Give your update to the team now, the way you would today.

Option A — The laptops

The new laptops for the sales team are here. Nineteen work, and one has a problem with the screen. Give the update on the laptops.

Option B — Your work

Give an update on something you are working on. You can change names and numbers.

Where is the work now?
What happens next?
Do you need anything from the team?
3 · How a colleague says it

The same kind of update, from a colleague

Listen to a colleague’s update. Which parts of it were also in yours? Which part was not?

“On my side, the new system is on the laptops.”
“Nineteen laptops are working, but one still has a problem with the screen.”
“The next step is to get a new laptop from the supplier.”
“I need Ryan to call the supplier this week.”
“About the date, I am not sure yet. I will check and get back to you.”
4 · Answers that keep it moving

When you do not have the answer

Paula asks: “When will the last laptop be ready?” For each reply, does the meeting keep moving, or does it stop?

4 · When you do not know

What the good replies have in common

Look at these two replies again. What do they give Paula, even without the answer?

“I am not sure yet. I will check with the supplier and tell you today.”
“I do not have the date now. Can I send it to you after the meeting?”
A next step, and a time Both replies say what you will do and when. Paula does not get the date, but she knows when she will get it, and the meeting can move on.
“On my side, …” — where your work is now.
“The next step is …” — what comes next.
“I need …” — what you need, and from whom.
“I will check and get back to you by …” — when you do not know yet.
5 · Answering in the meeting

Four questions, the best reply

Choose the best reply to each question. Check, and then say your four replies out loud.

6 · Your update

Your update in the check-in, with questions

Use the same option as at the start of the lesson. Give your update to the team, and answer the questions you get.

Say where your work is now.
Say what comes next.
Say what you need, and from whom.
Answer the questions. If you do not know, say when you will check.
On my side, …
The next step is …
I need … to …
I’m not sure yet. I’ll check and get back to you by …
6 · Their questions

After your update, your question to the team

Your teacher asks one more question about your update. Answer it. Then ask the team one question about their work or about the next step.

Answer the question, and give a reason if you can.
Ask the team one question.
If the answer is not clear, check it.
Can I ask about …?
Who is going to …?
So …, right?
7 · Your two versions

Your two updates, side by side

The update you gave at the start of the lesson, and the one you just gave.

One sentence Take one sentence from the fourth box into the next round.
8 · Again, with a change

The same check-in, and Paula changes the date

Give your update again. This time Paula asks you to finish two days earlier, and asks something you do not know. Decide what you can do, and say it.

Give your update with your sentence from the fourth box.
When Paula changes the date, say what is possible and what is not.
When you do not know, say when you will check.
Finish with what you will do next.
I can finish … by …, but …
If we need it earlier, …
I’ll check and get back to you by …
