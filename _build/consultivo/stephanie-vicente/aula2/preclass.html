1 · What this call is

This is the calendar entry for the call you will hear in class.

Fortnightly — coursebook working group
When: Thursday, 09:00–09:30
With: Marta (chair), Peter, Rachel
Agenda: Unit 7 — open items

Mark the two things this entry does not tell you.

How long the call is
Whether anything will be decided
Who is chairing
Whether you are expected to speak
Which unit is being discussed
How many people are on it
“Open items” is exactly as far as the entry commits. Nothing in it says a decision will be reached, and nothing in it says anything about you.

2 · Key language

to chair a meeting
to sequence something
a proposal

to give time back to something

3 · What the speaker is doing

Six sentences from meetings. Match each one to what the speaker is doing with it.

“My view has not changed.”
“What if we moved it to the start?”
“So you would keep it, but not where it is.”
“You are right about the hours. Not about the text.”
“Sorry — is that decided or not?”
“Let us take it as a proposal and move on.”
Why these six
All six happen in the call you will hear. The one that decides the lesson is the third: restating what someone else said is how a conversation gets moved without contradicting anyone.

4 · Two lines, once

Two lines from the beginning of the call. Listen once, without stopping.

What is the speaker doing?

Giving her own opinion on the coursebook
Opening the item and handing the turn to someone
Asking Peter to change his view
She names the item, names the person and gives the turn away. That is chairing, and it is the whole of what she does.
