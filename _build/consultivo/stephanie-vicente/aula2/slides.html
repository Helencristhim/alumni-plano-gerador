1 · Who is in the call

A call you are on, and not in

The fortnightly call with the publisher’s team. Three people are talking about the unit your group starts next month.

Who is there
Marta, who chairs. Peter and Rachel, who wrote different parts of the unit.
Where you are
On the call. Camera on, nobody has asked you anything.
What you need
To know whether the second reading text is staying, because your group starts in four weeks.

Ask you. If the question gets asked, you ask it.

5 · What each move does

Not what they say — what it does

Six turns from the call.

Classify each one, then check.

“Unit seven is too long for the hours we have.”
“So we agree the unit is too long. We disagree about which part goes.”
“If the second text moved to the start of the unit…”
“It is the only place where they read two sources against each other.”
“That is a different proposal, and it is a better one than mine, honestly.”
“I am not sure we agree it is too long.”

One of those six turned the call around. Which one, and what did it do?

“I am not sure we agree it is too long.” — it refuses the summary before arguing with the proposal.

Three ways to move a conversation without contradicting anyone:

“I am not sure we agree that…” — refuse the summary, not the person.
“It is too long the way it is sequenced.” — accept the fact, move the cause.
“That is a different proposal.” — name what just happened before answering it.

7 · Your call

Come in, and hand it back

The call runs in three parts and stops after each one.
At each stop, you decide.

Part

What do you need settled before your group starts?

Come in by naming something that was said.
Ask one thing, not three.
Say why it matters to you, in one clause.
Answer what comes back.
Hand the call back to whoever was speaking.
