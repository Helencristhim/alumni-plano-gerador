Aula 01 · Reading / R1 — Lesson 01 — Before you read either one

1 · Who is each document for

Two documents describe the same course. One is a lesson plan; the other is the syllabus the course was sold with.

Mark the two statements that are true of a course syllabus and not of a lesson plan.

It is written by a teacher
People outside the classroom rely on it
It says what will happen in one session
Changing it needs someone else to agree
It can be adjusted while the course runs
It lists every task the students will do
A syllabus is a commitment: it is read by people who were not in the room, and it is not the teacher’s to change alone. A plan is an instrument for one lesson.

4 · The plan, on its own

Read the lesson plan. Nothing else — you are not comparing anything yet.

Lesson plan — Unit 7, Reporting what happened
Unit 7 runs over 18 teaching hours, from week 5 to week 7. The unit closes with a written report of 180–220 words. The report is corrected by a classmate against the checklist on page 4, and then rewritten once; the teacher reads the rewritten version. The unit assessment is in week 7, in the last session. There is a listening task in session 4 built on a recorded staff meeting; it is not assessed.

Mark the two things the plan does not tell you.

How long the unit runs
Why the report is corrected by a classmate
When the assessment happens
How long the written report is
Whether anyone approved these choices
What the listening task is built on
The plan says what will happen. It never says why, and it never says who agreed to it — and that is normal for a plan.

5 · The syllabus, on its own

Now the syllabus. Again on its own.

Course syllabus — General English B1, second semester
The semester is divided into four units. Unit 7 is allocated 20 teaching hours, and no unit may run beyond its allocation without approval from the coordination. Written work produced at the end of a unit is corrected by the teacher, with written feedback returned to the student within one week. Each unit is assessed in the week after it ends. This syllabus states what the course commits to; it does not list every task a teacher may use.
A unit that needs more hours than it was given
The end-of-unit written work
A task that this document does not mention
The last line is the one to hold on to
A document that does not claim to list everything is not disagreeing with a document that lists more. You will need that distinction in class.
