3 · Read them once

Read both, for what they are

Lesson plan — Unit 7, Reporting what happened

Sample document.

Unit 7 runs over 18 teaching hours, from week 5 to week 7. The unit closes with a written report of 180–220 words.

The written report is corrected by a classmate against the checklist on page 4, and then rewritten once. The teacher reads the rewritten version.

The unit assessment is in week 7, in the last session, and covers the reporting language of the unit.

There is a listening task in session 4 built on a recorded staff meeting. It is not assessed.

Course syllabus — General English B1, second semester

Sample document.

The semester is divided into four units. Unit 7 is allocated 20 teaching hours, and no unit may run beyond its allocation without approval from the coordination.

Written work produced at the end of a unit is corrected by the teacher, with written feedback returned to the student within one week.

Each unit is assessed in the week after it ends. For Unit 7 this is week 8.

This syllabus states what the course commits to. It does not list every task a teacher may use.

What is each of these documents for, and who is each one written for?

4 · Where it says so

Six statements, and where each one comes from

Say which document supports each statement.

One of them is supported by neither. Finding it is the point.

Lesson plan — Unit 7  ·  Course syllabus — B1, second semester

Plan: 18 teaching hours, weeks 5–7 · report corrected by a classmate, then rewritten once · assessment in week 7 · listening task in session 4, not assessed.

Syllabus: Unit 7 allocated 20 teaching hours · end-of-unit written work corrected by the teacher, feedback within one week · assessment in the week after the unit ends, week 8 · states what the course commits to, does not list every task.

Going over the allocated hours needs approval from above.
Someone other than the teacher corrects the report first.
The students hear a recording of a meeting.
Unit 7 ends with a piece of written work.
The listening task was removed from the course.
Feedback on the written work comes back within one week.
Where each one is decided
The hours, the corrector and the assessment week are stated in both documents, and they disagree. The listening task appears in the plan only — and the syllabus says it does not list every task, so its silence is not disagreement.
5 · What kind of claim

What the document says, and what you concluded

Six sentences someone might say about these two documents.

Classify each one, then check.

“The course was shortened at some point.”
“The syllabus allocates 20 hours to Unit 7.”
“We should fix the assessment week before the group starts.”
“The plan has the assessment one week earlier than the syllabus.”
“Peer correction was put in to save the teacher time.”
“The syllabus does not claim to list every task.”

Two of them are conclusions. What tells you that, if the words look the same?

Someone with the document in front of them could disagree with a conclusion — and could not disagree with a quotation.

So the English has to say which one you are giving them:

“The syllabus says twenty hours.” — a quotation. It ends the discussion.
“My reading is that the course was shortened.” — a conclusion. It invites one.
“I would fix the week before the group starts.” — a recommendation. It asks for a decision.

ModelThe two documents disagree on three things. The syllabus says twenty teaching hours and the plan has eighteen. They do not line up on who corrects the written work either — the syllabus says the teacher, the plan says a classmate. And according to the plan the assessment is in week seven, which is one week before the syllabus puts it. My reading is that the plan was written from an older version, but neither document actually says that.
