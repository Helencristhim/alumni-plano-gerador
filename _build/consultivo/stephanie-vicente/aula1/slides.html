Lesson 01

Two Documents,
One Course

In this lesson you compare a lesson plan and a course syllabus,
and you tell your coordinator where they disagree.

When did you last have to read two documents that were supposed to say the same thing?

1 · What she asked for

Two documents, and one of them is wrong

Your coordinator forwarded you the lesson plan for Unit 7 and the syllabus the course was sold with. A teacher had told her they do not match.

Who is asking
The course coordinator. She has read neither document.
What she asked for
Where the two stop agreeing, and what that changes for the group starting next month.
What she has
A meeting with the head of studies right after yours.
What you want by the end
Her to know exactly what has to be decided, and by whom.

What does the coordinator need from you by the end of the meeting?

2 · Before you read

A plan and a syllabus for the same course

Answer with what you expect. You check your answers when you read the two documents.

Where do a lesson plan and a course syllabus usually stop agreeing?
Which of the two is a promise to someone outside the school?

3 · Read them once

Read both, for what they are

Read both documents once. What is each one for, and who is each one written for?

Lesson plan — Unit 7, Reporting what happened

Sample document.

Unit 7 runs over 18 teaching hours, from week 5 to week 7. The unit closes with a written report of 180–220 words.

The written report is corrected by a classmate against the checklist on page 4, and then rewritten once. The teacher reads the rewritten version.

The unit assessment is in week 7, in the last session, and covers the reporting language of the unit.

There is a listening task in session 4 built on a recorded staff meeting. It is not assessed.

Course syllabus — General English B1, second semester

Sample document.

The semester is divided into four units. Unit 7 is allocated 20 teaching hours, and no unit may run beyond its allocation without approval from the coordination.

Written work produced at the end of a unit is corrected by the teacher, with written feedback returned to the student within one week.

Each unit is assessed in the week after it ends. For Unit 7 this is week 8.

This syllabus states what the course commits to. It does not list every task a teacher may use.

4 · Where it says so

Six statements, and where each one comes from

Say which document supports each statement. Open the documents to find the line.

Lesson plan — Unit 7

18 teaching hours, weeks 5–7 · report corrected by a classmate, then rewritten once · assessment in week 7 · listening task in session 4, built on a recorded staff meeting, not assessed.

Course syllabus — B1, second semester

Unit 7 allocated 20 teaching hours; no unit may run beyond its allocation without approval from the coordination · end-of-unit written work corrected by the teacher, feedback within one week · assessment in the week after the unit ends, week 8 · does not list every task a teacher may use.

5 · What kind of claim

What the document says, and what you concluded

Six sentences someone might say about these two documents. Is each one in the document, a conclusion, or a recommendation?

5 · What gives it away

The words look the same, so what is different?

Two of the sentences on the last screen were conclusions. They sound as sure as the others. How can you tell they are conclusions?

“The syllabus allocates 20 hours to Unit 7.”
“The course was shortened at some point.”
The test Could someone with the document in front of them disagree with the sentence? If not, it is in the document. If they could, it is your conclusion, and the English says so.
“The syllabus says twenty hours.” — in the document. Nobody can argue with it.
“My reading is that the course was shortened.” — a conclusion, marked as yours.
“I would fix the week before the group starts.” — a recommendation. It asks for a decision.
6 · Say where it came from

Three differences, each with its document

Say each of the three differences out loud, one at a time. Start the sentence with the document it comes from.

“The syllabus says twenty, and the plan has eighteen.”
“These two do not line up on who corrects the writing.”
“According to the plan, the assessment is in week seven.”
“My reading is that nobody reread the syllabus.”
“That comes from the plan, not from the syllabus.”

A model briefing

The two documents disagree on three things. The syllabus says twenty teaching hours and the plan has eighteen. They do not line up on who corrects the written work either — the syllabus says the teacher, the plan says a classmate. And according to the plan the assessment is in week seven, which is one week before the syllabus puts it. My reading is that the plan was written from an older version, but neither document actually says that.
