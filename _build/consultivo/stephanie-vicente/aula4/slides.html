Lesson 04

The Feedback
You Have to Give

In this lesson you give feedback to a teacher you observed,
in a way he can answer — and you keep it when he disagrees.

When did you last give feedback to a teacher after watching a class?

1 · What you have to do

The conversation, and he taught the class

You observed a B1 group. The teacher is new to the school and works in English only.

Your role
Pedagogical support. Not his manager, and not his assessor.
His position
He is not obliged to accept anything you say. He can disagree, and he might.
What you saw
The instruction for the next task arrived while half the group was still working on the previous one. Three times.
What you want by the end
One thing he will try this week, that he chose to try.

What do you want him to leave the conversation with?

2 · Say it now

Before anything else, say it as you would today

Your teacher is the teacher you observed. Give him your feedback now.

What did you see?
What do you make of it?
What do you want him to do?
3 · How someone else says it

The same feedback, from someone else

Listen to the same feedback from someone else. At which point could the teacher say no, and still be talking about the same thing?

“What I noticed was the instruction coming while they were still working. It happened around three times.”
“What were you trying to do at that point?”
“That makes sense. The part I would still look at is what the two slower pairs do with that.”
“One thing you might try is giving the instruction before they start, and then just repeating it.”
“I might be wrong about the reason. What I saw was the pattern.”
4 · What makes it answerable

Six recommendations, and who decides

All six say roughly the same thing. After each one, can the teacher answer it, or can he only accept it?

4 · Polite is not the same

A polite order is still an order

This one is very polite, and the teacher can still only accept it. Why?

“Please give the instruction first next time.”
Polite is not the same as open Please makes the order softer. It does not give him anything to answer. What leaves room for an answer is where the decision is left.
“One thing you might try…” — one option among others, and he chooses.
“Have you thought about…?” — a question, so an answer is possible.
“It might be worth… — what do you think?” — the decision handed to him in words.
5 · Saying it out loud

The same point, four ways he can answer

Complete the four sentences, check, and then say each one out loud to your teacher.

Where the decision sits All four say the same thing about the class. What changes is who decides what happens next — and in all four, it is him.
6 · The conversation

Give it to him, for real this time

Choose option A or option B. Your teacher is the teacher, and he wants to know how the class went. Give him the feedback.

Option A — The instructions

The instruction for the next task came while the pairs were still working. It happened three times.

Option B — The checking question

He asked a checking question, took the first answer and moved on while three hands were still up.

Open with what you saw, not with how it was.
Ask what he was trying to do, and listen to the answer.
Recommend one thing, in a form he could refuse.
Say where you might be wrong.
What I noticed was…
What were you trying to do at that point?
One thing you might try is…
I might be wrong about…
6 · His questions

He wants more detail

The teacher asks you two questions about what you saw. Answer each one, and then ask him one question about his next class.

Answer with what you saw in the room.
Keep your recommendation open for him to decide.
Ask him one question, and agree on one thing he will try this week.
It was when the pairs were…
Is there anything you would like me to look at next time?
So this week you are going to try…?
7 · What just happened

Your two versions, side by side

The recommendation you started the lesson with, and the one you just used.

One sentence Take one sentence from the fourth box into the next conversation.
8 · Again, and he pushes back

Same conversation, and he does not agree

Give the same feedback again. This time the teacher disagrees, and part of what he says is right. What is still true after what he said?

Give the feedback with your sentence from the fourth box.
When he disagrees, say what is right in what he said.
Keep the part you saw yourself.
Finish where the two of you actually are.
That is fair — and the part I would still look at is…
I might be wrong about the reason. What I saw was…
So we are looking at the same thing differently.
