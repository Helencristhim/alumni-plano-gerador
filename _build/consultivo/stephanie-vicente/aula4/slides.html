Lesson 04

The Feedback
You Have to Give

He taught the class. You watched it.
Now he is sitting in front of you, and he speaks no Portuguese.

1 · What you have to do

Twenty minutes, and he taught the class

You observed fifty minutes of a B1 group. The teacher is new to the school and works in English only.

Your role
Pedagogical support. Not his manager, and not his assessor.
His position
He is not obliged to accept anything you say. He can disagree, and he might.
What you saw
The instruction for the next task arrived while half the group was still working on the previous one. Three times.
What you want by the end
One thing he will try this week, that he chose to try.

The teacher and the class in this lesson are invented. Nothing here is a record of any real school.

2 · Say it now

Before anything else, say it as you would today

Your teacher is the teacher. The conversation starts now.

What did you see?
What do you make of it?
What do you want him to do?

No preparation, no notes. This is the version you already have.

3 · How someone else says it

The same feedback, from someone else

“What I noticed was the instruction coming while they were still working. It happened around three times.”
“What were you trying to do at that point?”
“That makes sense. The part I would still look at is what the two slower pairs do with that.”
“One thing you might try is giving the instruction before they start, and then just repeating it.”
“I might be wrong about the reason. What I saw was the pattern.”

At which point in this could the teacher say no — and still be talking about the same thing?

4 · What makes it answerable

Six recommendations, three he can refuse

All six say roughly the same thing.

Which ones leave him a way to say no?

“You have to give the instruction before they start.”
“Please give the instruction first next time.”
“One thing you might try is giving it before they start.”
“The instruction needs to come first.”
“Have you thought about giving it before they start?”
“It might be worth trying it before they start — what do you think?”

Number 3 is the politest of the six. Why is it in the same group as number 1?

Because politeness is not the same as leaving room. Please softens the order; it does not make it refusable.

What actually opens the door is where the decision is left:

“One thing you might try…” — one option among others, and he picks.
“Have you thought about…?” — a question, so an answer is possible.
“It might be worth… — what do you think?” — the decision handed over explicitly.
5 · Saying it out loud

The same point, four ways he can answer

“One thing you is giving it before they start.”

“ repeating it after they begin?”

“It checking with the two slower pairs.”

“I about the reason — what I saw was the pattern.”

Where the decision sits All four say the same thing about the class. What changes is who decides what happens next — and in all four, it is him.
6 · The conversation

Give it to him, for real this time

Your teacher is the teacher.
He taught the class an hour ago and he wants to know how it went.

What did you see, and what could he do about it?

Open with what you saw. Not with how it was.
Ask what he was trying to do — and wait for the answer.
Recommend one thing, in a form he could refuse.
Say where you might be wrong.
Close on one thing he will try this week.
What I noticed was…  ·  What were you trying to…?  ·  One thing you might try…  ·  It might be worth…  ·  I might be wrong about…
7 · What just happened

Your two versions, side by side

The one you started the lesson with, and the one you just used.

One sentence Round two is not a second lesson. Take one sentence into it.
8 · Again, and he pushes back

Same conversation, and he does not agree

Everything as before.
This time he has an answer, and part of it is right.

What is still true, after what he just said?

Give the feedback as you rebuilt it.
When he disagrees, take what is right in it.
Keep the part you saw with your own eyes.
Do not turn it into a rule, and do not drop it.
Close where the two of you actually are.
That is fair — and the part I would still look at is…  ·  I might be wrong about the reason. What I saw was…  ·  So we are looking at the same thing differently.
