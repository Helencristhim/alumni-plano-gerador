Lesson 03

The Scene
and the Verdict

In this lesson you tell a colleague what happened in a class you watched,
and you keep what you saw apart from what you think of it.

When did you last tell someone about a class you observed?

1 · Tell me what you saw

One moment, as you would tell a colleague

Think of a class you watched, and one moment in it. Tell your teacher about that moment.

What was going on in the room at that point?
What happened?
What did you make of it?
2 · How they wrote it

A class, written by someone who watched it

Read the note. Where was this person when they wrote it, and what are they careful not to claim?

Observation note — B1 group, Tuesday

Sample document.

The students were working in pairs on the error-correction task when the teacher gave the next instruction. Two of them were still finishing the previous one.

The back table went quiet as soon as he got to the third example. They were copying it down while he was explaining it.

He asked a checking question and took the first answer. Four hands were still up when he moved on.

What I noticed was that the instruction came while they were still working. I might be wrong about the reason, but the pattern happened three times.

3 · What each form does

Six sentences, three different jobs

Six sentences about the class in the note. Is each one the scene, the event, or a reading of it?

3 · The one that is different

Three sentences, and one is different

One of these three sentences is not like the other two, and it is not about the verb. Which one, and why?

“Four hands were still up.”
“The instruction was confusing.”
“He gave the next instruction.”
Could the teacher disagree? The teacher who gave the instruction could disagree with “The instruction was confusing.” He could not disagree with the others. A report of a class holds three different things, and each one has to sound like itself.
“They were working in pairs…” — the scene. Already going on.
“…when he gave the instruction.” — the event. It happened at that point.
“What I noticed was that it came too early.” — your reading, marked as yours.
4 · The rule you were using

Two pasts, one moment

Read the table. Then look at your sentence from the beginning of the lesson: which part of it was the scene?

The sceneThe event
What it doesSays what was already going on, and does not say it endedSays what happened at that point, and cuts into the scene
Formwas / were + -ingpast simple
TogetherThey were working in pairs when he gave the instruction.
Soundwere is weak: /wə/. Said strong, it sounds like you are correcting somebody.
Not about time Both of them are past, and both of them finished. The difference is where you are standing: inside the scene, or at the moment it was cut.

7 · Five moments

Tell it to someone who was not there

Your teacher is a colleague who did not see the class. Your notes stay in front of you.

Tell your colleague what happened in the class, one moment at a time, and what you make of it.

Tell the five moments in the order they happened.
For each one, say what was going on and what happened.
Say what you make of it after the scene, and say that it is your reading.
They were… when…
What I noticed was…
The way I read it…
I might be wrong about this, but…
7 · Your colleague asks

After the story, two questions

Your colleague asks you two questions about the class. Answer each one, and then ask your colleague one question back.

Answer with the scene first, and then what happened.
If your answer is a reading, say that it is yours.
Ask your colleague one question about the class or the teacher.
While that was happening, the others were…
That is what I saw. What I concluded is…
Have you seen this teacher’s classes?
