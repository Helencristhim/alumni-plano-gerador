2 · How they wrote it

The same fifty minutes, written by someone else

Observation note — B1 group, Tuesday

Sample document.

The students were working in pairs on the error-correction task when the teacher gave the next instruction. Two of them were still finishing the previous one.

The back table went quiet as soon as he got to the third example. They were copying it down while he was explaining it.

He asked a checking question and took the first answer. Four hands were still up when he moved on.

What I noticed was that the instruction came while they were still working. I might be wrong about the reason, but the pattern happened three times.

Where was this person when they wrote it, and what are they careful not to claim?

3 · What each form does

Six sentences, three different jobs

All six are about the same fifty minutes.

Classify each one, then check.

“They were working in pairs.”
“He gave the next instruction.”
“The instruction was confusing.”
“Four hands were still up.”
“The back table went quiet.”
“Two of them were still finishing.”

One of the six is not like the other five, and it is not about the verb. Which one, and why?

“The instruction was confusing.” — the teacher who gave it could disagree with this sentence. He could not disagree with the others.

So a report of a class holds three different things, and each one has to look like itself:

“They were working in pairs…” — the scene. Already true before your moment.
“…when he gave the instruction.” — the event. It happened at that point, and it cut into the scene.
“What I noticed was that it came too early.” — your reading. Marked, so he can argue with it.
4 · The rule you were using

Two pasts, one moment

The sceneThe event
What it doesSays what was already going on, and does not say it endedSays what happened at that point, and cuts into the scene
Formwas / were + -ingpast simple
TogetherThey were working in pairs when he gave the instruction.
Soundwere is weak: /wə/. Say it strong and it sounds like you are correcting somebody.
Not about time Both of them are past, and both of them finished. The difference is where you are standing: inside the scene, or at the moment it was cut.
