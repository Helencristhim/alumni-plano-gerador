Lesson recap

A quick reference to what you did and the language you used.

SituationReporting five moments from a class you observed, to someone who was not in the room.
The sceneThey were working in pairs… · Two of them were still finishing…
The event…when he gave the instruction. · …and the back table went quiet.
Marking your readingWhat I noticed was… · The way I read it… · I might be wrong about this, but…
Key vocabularyto work in pairs · a checking question · to move on · to go quiet · the pace
The test that settles it Could the teacher who taught the class disagree with your sentence?
If no, it is what happened — say it flat. If yes, it is your reading, and it has to say so.

Optional practice

Speak More

About 60 to 90 seconds

Record a short voice message, if you would like to.

The situation Think of a class you watched recently — yours or someone else’s.

What you are trying to do: describe two minutes of it so precisely that the listener could draw the room. Then, and only then, say what you made of it.

You could cover:

  • what was already going on when your two minutes start;
  • what happened, and what it cut into;
  • what the others were doing while that happened;
  • your reading of it, marked as yours.

One rule for this one Nothing in it should be arguable. If a sentence could be answered with that is not how it was, it belongs in Section B.

Language Reference

The scene, the event, the reading

Use this whenever you want to review what you did in class.

What you are doingHow it tends to soundWhat goes wrong without it
Setting the sceneThey were working… · Two were still finishing…A list of events, and the listener cannot see the room.
Placing the event…when he gave the instruction.Everything sounds equally important, including what was only background.
Marking the readingWhat I noticed was… · I might be wrong, but…Your conclusion is heard as what happened, and the teacher has nothing to answer.
