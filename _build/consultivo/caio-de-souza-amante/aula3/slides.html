Lesson 03

He Wants,
She Needs

Eight people, ninety seconds.
The hard part is not who they are.

1 · Say it once

Four people around you

Thirty seconds. No notes.

Who are the four people closest to you at work, and what does each one decide?

2 · Both sides of the table

Eight roles, one line each

Your side

Head of Delivery — runs the delivery teams and decides who goes on which account.
CFO — owns the numbers the buyer asked for, and signs off what leaves the company.
Head of People — knows who on the team already works in English, and who does not.
You — decide whether this conversation happens at all.

Their side

Mark — runs finance, and wants the recurring revenue number confirmed.
Peter — advises them, and keeps coming back to delivery.
Diane — leads corporate development, and writes the emails.
Their board — meets monthly, and approves the structure.

Which two of their questions could nobody but you answer?

3 · Four of these are right

Two of them are not

4 · The form under it

One line, and you already know it

he · she · it → the verb takes -s
He runs delivery. She reports to me. It depends on the board.

I · you · we · they → nothing
I decide. They handle three markets. We hold the majority.

In American English, the team, the board and the company are one thing — so they take the -s too. The two irregular ones that come up constantly: has and does.

Does the team take it? Does my partners and I?

5 · Twelve, fast

Same information, different subject

Your teacher gives you the new subject. You say the sentence again, immediately.

6 · Your two teams

Eight sentences, one at a time

The chart is still on screen. Use it.

One sentence per person: what does each of them decide?

He runs…  ·  She reports to…  ·  Their CFO wants…
The team handles…  ·  Their board approves…  ·  My partners and I hold…
7 · Ninety seconds

Both sides, nothing on screen

Your teacher is a partner who has never met any of these people.

Who is on each side of this table, and what does each one decide?

Start with your side, and say what each person decides — not what they are called.
Move to theirs, and say what each one wants from you.
Finish with the two questions only you can answer.
If the form slips, correct it and keep going. Do not restart the sentence.
8 · Forty-five seconds

Same eight, half the time

Now your partner only wants to know one thing about each of them.

What does each person decide? Forty-five seconds.

What the clock does to the form
Faster is where the ending goes missing — not because you forget the rule, but because there is no attention left over for it. Holding it here is the whole difference between knowing a form and having one.
