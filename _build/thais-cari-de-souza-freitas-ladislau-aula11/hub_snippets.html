Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An application -- a formal request for credit, with the customer's information
"Every application is received through the app, day and night."
A credit score -- a number that shows how likely a person is to pay back
"A credit score is calculated for every customer in under a second."
A credit bureau -- a company outside the bank that collects people's payment history
"Payment history is collected from two credit bureaus every night."
Income -- the money a person receives every month, from work or other sources
"Income is not declared by the customer. It is estimated by the model."
A variable -- one piece of information that the model uses
"About forty variables are used, and none of them is the customer's name."
A criterion -- a rule or standard you use to judge something (plural: criteria)
"Each criterion is written down in the credit policy."
To assess -- to look at something carefully and decide its value or its risk
"Risk is assessed in two steps: first by the model, then by the policy."
To flag -- to mark something so that a person looks at it again
"Unusual cases are flagged and sent to an analyst."
To weigh -- to decide how important each thing is, compared with the others
"Recent payments are weighed more heavily than old ones."
To feed into -- to go into a system or a decision as information
"The bureau data feeds into the score, and the score feeds into the limit."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

When you explain a method, the steps matter more than the people. That is why method documents are written in the passive. Compare: "An analyst reviews the case" and "The case is reviewed." The second one does not say who does it, because in a process that is often not the point. The form is simple: is or are, then the past participle. One thing takes is: "The score is calculated every night." Two or more take are: "Unusual cases are flagged."

The negative puts not after is or are: "The final limit is not set by the model." The question moves is or are to the front: "Is income checked?" and "How is the score calculated?" Use by only when it matters who does the action: "It is set by the policy, not by the model." Most regular participles end in -ed, but some important ones do not: set stays set, send becomes sent, write becomes written, and make becomes made.

And the sound. The -ed ending has three sounds. In checked it sounds like a T, in flagged like a D, and only in words like calculated, where the verb already ends in T or D, does it add a new syllable. So it is never "check-ED". Get this right, and a method in English sounds as clear as it does in Portuguese.

1. Why are method documents often written in the passive?
A Because the steps matter more than the people.

2. Which sentence is correct?

3. How does the -ed sound in the word checked?
A Like a new syllable at the end, the same as in the word calculated.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Present Simple Passive

GRAMMAR

Is or are, then the past participle. By only when the who matters.

JobFormExample
One thingis + past participleThe score is calculated every night.
Two or moreare + past participleUnusual cases are flagged.
Negativeis not / are not + past participleThe limit is not set by the model.
QuestionIs / Are + subject + past participleIs income checked? · How is the score calculated?
by + whoonly when it matters who does itIt is set by the policy.
Irregularlearn the participle with the verbset → set · send → sent · write → written · make → made
The sound-ed = T, D, or a new syllablechecked (T) · flagged (D) · calculated (new syllable)

Never: the score is calculate · unusual cases flagged (no are) · the limit is setted · is checked the income? · check-ED
