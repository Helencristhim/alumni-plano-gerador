Grammar Discovery

Who Does the Action?

"Every application is received through the app."

"Payment history is collected from two credit bureaus."

"Unusual cases are flagged and sent to an analyst."

"The final limit is not set by the model. It is set by the policy."

Who receives the application? Who collects the history? The sentences do not say. Every blue phrase has two parts. And the red part appears only once — when does it appear?

JobFormExample
One thingis + past participleThe score is calculated every night.
Two or moreare + past participleUnusual cases are flagged.
Negativeis not / are not + past participleThe limit is not set by the model.
QuestionIs / Are + subject + past participleIs income checked? · How is the score calculated?
by + whoonly when it matters who does itIt is set by the policy, not by the model.
Irregularlearn the participle with the verbset → set · send → sent · write → written · make → made

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Walk Me Through It

One conversation, two recordings, one process document

Dialogue

How Is a Decision Made?

M
I am building a dashboard for the VP. Can you walk me through how a credit decision is made?
T
Sure. First, the application is received through the app. Then payment history is collected from two credit bureaus, and it feeds into the model.
M
And the score? Is it calculated by the model?
T
Yes. About forty variables are used, and recent payments are weighed more heavily. Income is estimated, not declared.
M
What happens to the strange cases?
T
They are flagged and sent to an analyst. Nothing unusual is approved automatically.
M
And who sets the limit, the model or your team?
T
The final limit is not set by the model. It is set by the policy, and every criterion is written down. That is the part I own.

Listening 1 · The Score

How a Score Is Built

A head of models explaining the method, step by step. Sound first — no text.

1. When is payment history collected, and from where?
Every night, from the credit bureaus.
2. Which behavior is weighed more, and why?
Recent behavior, because people change.
3. What happens if the results of the model start to get worse?
It is retrained, and the old version is kept, so every decision can be explained later.
Listening 2 · The Customer

When a Customer Asks Why

A specialist on explaining a decline. Sound first — no text.

1. What right does a declined customer have?
The right to ask why.
2. What is the customer given, and what is the customer not shown?
The two or three variables that weighed most against them, in plain language. The customer is not shown the model.
3. What do the good teams do differently?
They write the explanation before the model is built, not after.
