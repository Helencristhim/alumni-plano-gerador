The Oscar of Design -- The iF Design Award

You have entered three projects in an international design award. Key words: an award, an entry, to submit, a jury, a category, to be shortlisted, a ceremony, a winner, to stand out, an achievement. Structure: present perfect for experience and results.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An award -- a prize that people get for very good work
"The iF Design Award is like the Oscar of design."
An entry -- a project or a piece of work that you send to a competition
"I have sent three entries this year."
To submit -- to send a document or a project so that other people can evaluate it
"We submitted the texts in English."
A jury -- a group of experts who decide who wins
"The jury has members from many countries."
A category -- a group of things of the same type in a competition
"Our project is in the digital product category."
To be shortlisted -- to be chosen for the small final list before the winners
"If we are shortlisted, we hear in November."
A ceremony -- a formal event to celebrate something, like giving prizes
"The ceremony is in Berlin, in April."
A winner -- the person or project that wins a competition
"Every winner goes up to the stage."
To stand out -- to be easy to notice because it is better or different
"A simple story helps a project stand out."
An achievement -- something good that you did with effort
"Three entries in one year is already an achievement."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Juliane works in product design, and this year she has entered three projects in an international design award. She has written all the texts in English, and the team has already submitted every entry.

The jury is international, and it has seen thousands of projects. Juliane knows that a simple story helps a project stand out.

She has never been to an award ceremony, and the team hasn’t heard from the jury yet. But for her, three entries in one year is already an achievement.

1. What has the team already done?
A They have won the award.
B They have been to the ceremony.
C They have submitted every entry.
2. Why does Juliane want a simple story?
A Because the texts must be in Portuguese.
B Because the jury has seen thousands of projects, and a simple story helps a project stand out.
C Because the ceremony is short.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “She has never been to an award ceremony.”
B “She never has been to an award ceremony.”
C “She has never went to an award ceremony.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect -- Experience and Results

GRAMMAR

Use have / has + the third form of the verb for experiences and results when the date is not important. With a date, use the past simple.

JobFormExample
Experience, no datehave / has + third formI have entered three projects.
Zero timeshave never + third formI have never been to a ceremony.
QuestionsHave you ever + third form?Have you ever entered an award?
Done / not donealready / not ... yetWe have already submitted it. We haven’t heard yet.
Still true todayfor + a period / since + a startI have worked here for five years.
The trapwith a date, use the past simpleNot I have submitted it in May. Say I submitted it in May.

Never: I have submitted it last week · Have you ever went · I never have entered · The award exists since 1953
