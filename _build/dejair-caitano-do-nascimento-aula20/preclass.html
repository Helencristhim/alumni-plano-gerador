Lesson 20 -- Pre-class

Bangkok Ready -- One Congress Day, Five Scenes

A full congress day in one lesson: small talk, the Q&A, the hard question, the poster and the goodbye. Key words: plenary, breakout session, name badge, business card, small talk, jet lag, to run late, to wrap up. Structure: review -- the right tool for each scene.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. Four words are places and things of a congress; four are moments of the day.

Plenary -- a session for everybody at the congress, usually in the biggest room
"The plenary starts at nine, so let's get a good seat."
Breakout session -- a smaller session in a separate room, after a big session
"My poster is in breakout session B, after the plenary."
Name badge -- a small card with your name that you wear at an event
"I saw your name badge in the elevator."
Business card -- a small card with your name, your job and your email
"Here is my business card. My email is on the back."
Small talk -- a short, friendly conversation about easy topics, like the weather
"Small talk in the coffee line is a good way to meet people."
Jet lag -- the tired feeling after a long flight to a different time zone
"I arrived at six this morning, so my jet lag is terrible."
To run late -- to start or finish later than planned
"The session is running late, so please keep your answer short."
To wrap up -- to finish something, for example a talk or a meeting
"Before we wrap up, could I have your email?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Paulo is a researcher from Recife. Last year, at his first congress in Lisbon, he was waiting for the plenary when a woman from Chile started some small talk. Later, in his breakout session, somebody asked a hard question about his sample. Paulo said: "That's a fair point. The effect might be smaller in a bigger group. So far, we have tested forty patients." At lunch, he met the woman from Chile again. She asked: "Could you tell me where your poster is?" Before they wrapped up, Paulo gave her his business card and said: "If you send me your data, I'll compare it with ours." Today they write papers together.
Paulo says "So far, we have tested forty patients". What does this tell you?
A The study finished many years ago, and Paulo does not work on this project or with these patients any more
B The study is not finished: forty is the result until now
C Paulo wants to test forty patients next year
Why does Paulo say "the effect might be smaller", not "the effect is smaller"?
A Because he is not sure, so he uses hedging
B Because "might" is the past form of "may", and he is talking about something that happened at the congress
C Because he wants to end the conversation

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Your Toolbox for the Congress Day

GRAMMAR

No new grammar today: this is your toolbox. Each scene of a congress day needs one tool you already know.

small talkpast continuous + past simpleI was waiting for the elevator when I saw your badge.
the Q&Aindirect question: subject + verbCould you tell me what you mean? (not what do you mean)
a hard questionhedging: might / may / could + verbIt might be related to the dose.
the posterpresent perfect: have + past participleSo far, we have tested sixty patients. We haven't finished yet.
the goodbyeif + present, will + verbIf you send me your protocol, I'll share our data.

Questions with the toolbox: "Have you been to the plenary yet?" / "Do you know if the session is running late?" / "Will you send me the paper if I give you my card?"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
In the elevator, you see a congress name badge. What is a good first sentence?
A "Are you here for the congress too?"
B "Please tell me your full name, your university and the title of your poster before we arrive."
C "Your badge is wrong."
A researcher says your sample is too small. What is the best answer?
A "No. My sample is perfect, and everybody in my lab agrees that it is big enough for our study."
B "That's a fair point. So far, we have tested sixty patients, and we want to test more."
C "Next question."
The session is running late. The moderator asks you for one last sentence. What do you say?
A "I'll keep it short: so far, the pattern is clear."
B "I have eleven more slides, so please give me fifteen more minutes to finish everything."
C "I don't know."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Two minutes. Imagine you are at the congress lunch in Bangkok. A researcher sits next to you. Start some small talk, tell the story of one finding from your research, and wrap up with a follow-up promise: "If you send me..., I'll..." No script -- just speak.
