Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

The Night the Lights Went Out -- Telling a Story With Two Layers

Saying what happened is a list. Saying what was already going on while it happened is a story. Key words: to wait, suddenly, to notice, a moment, to realize, to happen, a line, meanwhile, to look for something, on time. Structure: past continuous for the background (was / were + -ing), past simple for the moment that breaks it, and when and while to join them.

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To wait -- to stay where you are until the thing you came for arrives
"While everybody was waiting for the bus, four people were still arguing."
Suddenly -- very fast, and with no warning at all
"Then a woman laughed, and suddenly everybody was talking at once."
To notice -- to see or hear something you were not looking for
"Nobody noticed the small green lights above the doors until they went out."
A moment -- a very short piece of time, a few seconds
"He carried on from the line he had reached a moment before."
To realize -- to understand something you did not understand a second earlier
"For four seconds nobody moved, because nobody realized what was happening."
To happen -- to take place, with nobody planning it
"What were you doing when it happened?"
A line -- people standing one behind the other, all wanting the same thing
"There was a line outside, and it was not moving."
Meanwhile -- at the same time, but somewhere else
"He was performing with one light. Meanwhile, the street outside was completely dark."
To look for something -- to try to find something, moving your eyes or your hands
"Its owner was looking for it in a bag and pretending it belonged to somebody else."
On time -- not late and not early -- exactly when you said
"The play started on time. It did not finish on time."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Every story has two layers, and English marks them with two different verb forms. The first layer is what was already going on — the room, the weather, the people, the thing nobody was paying attention to. That layer takes was or were plus the -ing form: "Two hundred people were sitting still." "An actor was walking towards the front." "A phone was ringing somewhere in the third row." Nothing in those sentences begins or ends. They simply describe a room that was already like that when you arrived.

The second layer is the moment that cuts through the first one. That layer takes the plain past you already know: "The lights went out." "The phone rang." "A woman laughed." Short, finished, one point in time. And the word that stitches the two layers together is when: "He was standing in the same place when the emergency lights came on." Put the long thing before when, and the short thing after it. If both halves are long — two things running side by side — use while instead: "While everybody was waiting for the bus, four people were arguing about the play."

The negative contracts the helper, never the -ing: "I wasn't expecting that." "They weren't listening." The question moves the helper to the front: "What were you doing when it happened?" — which is, by some distance, the most useful sentence in this lesson, because it hands the story straight back to the other person. And the trap is the one you have now met three times: the helper never disappears. Not I sitting, not she cooking — exactly as the s lands once and the did lands once. The other half of the trap runs the other way: the short moment never takes -ing. "The lights were going out" describes something slow and gradual, which is not what suddenly means at all.

1. According to the text, what does the was or were plus -ing layer describe?
A Something that started and finished at one point in time.
B What was already going on, with no beginning and no end in the sentence.
C Something that happens every week.
2. What is the difference between when and while, according to the text?
A When puts a long thing before it and a short one after; while joins two long things running side by side.
B When is for the past and while is for the future.
C They are the same word and you can use either one anywhere.
3. The text says the trap is one you have already met three times. What is it?
A Forgetting to put the in front of a superlative.
B Using much with a countable noun.
C Dropping the helper — the was or were here, the s in lesson 2, the did in lesson 3.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Continuous, Past Simple, and the Word That Joins Them

GRAMMAR

The long thing takes was or were plus -ing. The short thing takes the plain past. When joins them.

JobFormExample
The background -- already going onwas / were + verb + -ingTwo hundred people were sitting still.
Which helperwas: I, he, she, it · were: you, we, theyI was waiting. They were waiting.
The moment that cuts itpast simple — no helper, no -ingThe lights went out. The phone rang.
Both halves in one sentencebackground + when + past simpleHe was standing there when the lights came on.
Two long things at oncewhile + both halves in the -ing formWhile everybody was waiting, four people were arguing.
Negativewasn't / weren't + -ingI wasn't expecting that. They weren't listening.
QuestionWas / Were + person + -ingWhat were you doing when it happened?
Short answerYes, I was. / No, we weren't.Were you working at that time? — No, I wasn't.
Setting a second sceneMeanwhile, + backgroundMeanwhile, the street outside was completely dark.
The trapthe helper never disappears, and the short moment never takes -ingNot I sitting, not she cooking, not the lights were going out.

Never: I sitting · she cooking when · I was do my homework · What you were doing? · the lights were going out at eight

Stage 2: Put the Story in Order

Order

This is how a story is built: background first, then the moment, then what people did. Put it in order.

?"The play started on time, and the room was completely quiet."
?"Two hundred people were sitting still, and an actor was walking towards the front."
?"At quarter past eight the lights suddenly went out."
?"For four seconds nobody moved, because nobody realized what was happening."
?"He was standing in the same place when the emergency lights came on, and he carried on."
?"And you? What were you doing when it happened?"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to say what you were in the middle of when the power stopped. You say:
A "I cooking dinner when the power stopped."
B "I was cooking dinner when the power stopped."
C "I was cook dinner when the power stopped."
You want to ask a friend what she was in the middle of at that time. You say:
A "What you were doing at that time?"
B "What did you doing at that time?"
C "What were you doing at that time?"
You want to say two long things were happening side by side. You say:
A "While I was waiting outside, my sister was looking for the tickets."
B "While I waited outside, my sister looked for the tickets suddenly."
C "When I was waiting outside, my sister was look for the tickets."
Somebody finishes a story and you want to keep the conversation going. You say:
A "Yes."
B "That happened to me once too. And you, what were you doing when you found out?"
C "It is very interesting your story."
