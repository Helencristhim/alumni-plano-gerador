Series
Midnight Mass -- Episode 1, "Book I: Genesis"

A small island, a new priest, and something that nobody can explain. It is slow on purpose, and that is why it belongs to this lesson: almost every scene sets a background before anything happens in it. Suspense with a religious core — two things you already told us you are drawn to.

Tip: English subtitles on. Stop the episode once, at any point, and describe out loud what three different characters were doing at that moment. That is today’s grammar, and the show hands it to you on a plate.

Video
BBC Learning English -- "Past simple and past continuous" (6 Minute Grammar)

Six minutes on exactly the split you worked on today: the long thing underneath and the short thing that cuts through it. They use the same when and while examples, in two British voices, at a speed you can follow.

Tip: pause every time they give an example and say it back before they explain it. If your version and theirs are the same, you already have the rule.

Talk
Elizabeth Loftus -- "How reliable is your memory?" (TED)

She is the researcher behind the second recording of today’s lesson. Her subject is what your memory does to a story between the night it happened and the day you tell it — and her examples are uncomfortable in the best way. Seventeen minutes, very clear speech.

Tip: English subtitles on. Afterwards, tell somebody about one memory of your own in six sentences, and count how many of them describe the background rather than the event.
