1. What was happening in the theater when the power went out?
The play was running. It went out at 20:15, fifteen minutes after it started, so the actors were already on stage and the audience was watching.
2. How long was the room without electricity, and how late did the play finish?
Twenty-five minutes without electricity. The play ended at 22:10, forty minutes late.
3. What does the last line say people wrote to the theater, and what is the theater's answer?

Chapter 4: The Code

Two Layers, and the Word That Joins Them

The long thing underneath, and the short thing that cuts it

Grammar Discovery

Three Were Already Going On. One Arrived.

"Two hundred people were sitting still."

"An actor was walking towards the front of the stage."

"At quarter past eight the lights went out."

"He was standing in the same place when the lights came on."

Three of them were already running when the night arrived: a blue helper plus a red tail. The green ones are the moments that cut through — and they have no helper and no tail.

JobFormExample
The background — already going onwas / were + verb + -ingTwo hundred people were sitting still.
Which helperwas: I, he, she, it · were: you, we, theyI was waiting. They were waiting.
The moment that cuts itpast simple — no helper, no -ingThe lights went out. The phone rang.
Both halves in one sentencebackground + when + past simpleHe was standing there when the lights came on.
Two long things at oncewhile + both halves in the -ing formWhile everybody was waiting, four people were arguing.
Negativewasn't / weren't + -ingI wasn't expecting that. They weren't listening.
Question and short answerWas / Were + person + -ingWhat were you doing? — Yes, I was. / No, we weren't.
The trapthe helper never disappears, and the short moment never takes -ingNot I sitting, not she cooking, and not the lights were going out. Same helper logic as the did.

In one line: the long thing takes was or were plus -ing, the short thing takes the plain past, and when joins them.

Listening 1 · The Room, Not the News

Nobody Remembers the Same Thing

A man remembering one afternoon when he was nine. Sound first — no text.

1. What does each person say they were doing?
His mother says she was cooking. His brother says she was on the phone and the pan was burning. He was lying on the floor, not really doing his homework, with the television on and the sound down.
2. What happened, and how did the two children know something was wrong?
The phone rang and his mother went very quiet. He says children always know before anybody tells them.
3. What does he remember thirty years later, and what does he not remember?
He remembers the fan turning, somebody outside cutting the grass, and his pencil rolling under the sofa. He could draw the room, but he could not tell you a single word anybody said.
Listening 2 · The Last Frame of the Ordinary

Why You Remember the Background

A woman explaining what your brain saves, and when. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does your brain save almost nothing while ordinary time is going on?
Because it is running on the idea that today is the same as yesterday, so there is nothing to save.
2. What happens for about ninety seconds when something breaks the pattern?
The brain writes down everything, including the parts that do not matter at all: the smell, the shoes, what was playing.
3. What does she say about the sentence "I remember exactly what I was doing"?
She calls it the strangest sentence in the language, because people remember not what happened, but what they were doing.
Chapter 6: Say It Back

Set the Scene, Then Break It

Guided > Semi-free > Free

Role-Play 1 of 3 — Guided

Tell Me About That Night

Scenario: I was not at the theater that night and somebody told me about it. Tell me the whole thing: two sentences of background, one of what happened, and one of what the actor did next.

Keyword chips:

were sitting was walking suddenly went out nobody realized
Role-Play 2 of 3 — Semi-free

Ask Me What Was Going On

Scenario: I start telling you about a day something went wrong, and I stop after one sentence. Ask me at least three questions — two of them about what was going on at the time — until you can retell the whole scene back to me.

Keywords:

What were you doing? Was it raining? Who were you waiting for? And then what happened?
Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

One Day That Did Not Go as Planned

Scenario: Tell me about one day that did not go as planned. Start with what was already going on, then the thing that arrived and changed it, then what you did. Use when or while at least twice. Finish by asking me the same question.

No keywords, no notes, two minutes. Nobody will interrupt you.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I can describe what was already going on, with was or were plus the -ing form.
I can join the two halves with when, and two long ones with while.
I never drop the was or were, and I never put -ing on the short moment.
I can ask what were you doing when it happened, and hand the story back.
I know the words: to wait, suddenly, to notice, a moment, to realize, to happen, a line, meanwhile, to look for something, on time.
