When the Word Disappears

Eight words for the moment you get stuck (get stuck, hold on, on the tip of my tongue, look up, paraphrase, sort of, basically, what I mean is) and the grammar that lets you describe any word you forget: who, which, that, where. Around 30 minutes, in one sitting.

Stage 1.1: Words for When You Get Stuck

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say each word out loud. Eight words, three minutes.

Get stuck — to stop because you cannot find the next word
"I got stuck on one word in the middle of the case."
Hold on — wait a moment — you need a few seconds
"Hold on a second, let me find the right word."
On the tip of my tongue — you know the word, but you cannot say it right now
"It is on the tip of my tongue — give me a second."
Look up — to search for a word in a dictionary or online
"I will look it up after the session."
Paraphrase — to say the same idea with different words
"If you forget the word, paraphrase it."
Sort of — almost, but not exactly
"She is sort of like a project manager."
Basically — in simple words, the main idea is
"Basically, my team makes sure the clients get paid."
What I mean is — you are going to say it again, more clearly
"What I mean is, we need more time, not more people."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the text. Then answer the questions.

On the second day of the program, I was explaining our credit process to my group, and I got stuck. The word was on the tip of my tongue, but it did not come. In August I would stop and say it in Portuguese. This time I said, "Hold on," and I paraphrased: "Basically, it is the money that the client cannot pay back." A woman from Toronto said, "The default rate," and I said, "Yes, exactly." Then I explained the committee: "It is sort of like a board — the people who approve the big loans." I also described the office where my team works, and the report which we send to the directors every month. When one sentence came out badly, I said, "What I mean is, we need more time, not more people." That night I looked up three words. Nobody in the group remembered that I forgot them.
What did he do this time when the word did not come?
A He stopped and said the word in Portuguese, like he did in August.
B He said "hold on" and described it.
Why is it "the people who approve" and not "the people which approve"?
A Because who is for people.
B Because which is only used in questions about the future.
What did nobody in the group remember?
A His name.
B That he forgot the words.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Describe any word you forget

General word + who / which / that / where + the information that makes it specific.

who — people

the person who approves the budget
the people who work on my team

which / that — things

a report which shows the numbers
the money that a company makes

where — places

the office where my team works
the city where I was born

questions

Is it the report which goes to the board?
Who is the person who decides?

× "the report which it shows" — say the thing once: the report which shows
× "the person which approves" — people take who (or that)
× "the office where my team works there" — where already means "there"

Not sure which one? That works for people and things. Start with it.

Stage 4: In the room

Quiz
Which sentence is correct?
A "It is a report which shows the numbers."
B "It is a report which it shows the numbers every month."
You forget a word in the middle of a session. What is the best first thing to say?
A "Sorry, sorry, my English is very bad today, I am sorry."
B "Hold on — it is sort of like ..."
You want to describe the place where your clients sign contracts.
A "It is the office where clients sign."
B "It is the office who the clients sign the contracts there."

Stage 5: Five dangerous words

Reflection
Think of five words from your job that disappear when you speak English. Record one minute in English. For each word, do not say it: describe it with who, which, that or where. Start each one with "Hold on" or "Basically". No Portuguese, and no silence longer than three seconds.
