Explaining a Problem on Site

A burst water main at Station 1, and how to report what happened and what was done. Key words: leak, crack, burst, flood, damage, shut down, root cause, incident report. Structure: the past passive (was / were + past participle).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Leak -- a small hole where water or gas escapes
"There is a leak in the main pipe."
Crack -- a thin line where something is broken, but not in pieces
"The inspector found a crack in the wall."
Burst -- to break open suddenly, because of pressure
"A water main burst near Station 1."
Flood -- to cover an area with water
"The rain can flood the excavation."
Damage -- harm that makes something broken or less useful
"The water caused damage to two pumps."
Shut down -- to stop a machine or a place from working
"We had to shut down the station."
Root cause -- the main reason why a problem happened
"We are still looking for the root cause."
Incident report -- a document that describes a problem and what was done
"The incident report is due tomorrow."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At two in the morning, a water main burst near Station 1. The excavation was flooded, and two of the new pumps were damaged. Luckily, nobody was hurt: the crew was not on site at night.

The main valve was closed by the utility's team at three, and the station was shut down. In the morning the water was pumped out, and the area was inspected. There was no leak in the other pipes, and no crack in the walls.

Thiago thinks the pipe was hit by an excavator, but the root cause is still not confirmed, so he does not write it as a fact in the incident report. Notice the verbs: was or were plus the past participle, because what happened matters more than who did it.

1. Why was nobody hurt?
A Because the station was shut down by the utility before the water main burst at two in the morning.
B Because the crew was not on site at night.
C Because the excavation was not flooded.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A Two of the new pumps was damaged.
B Two of the new pumps damaged by the water.
C Two of the new pumps were damaged.
3. Why does Thiago not write the root cause as a fact?
A Because it is still not confirmed.
B Because the client asked him not to write anything about the excavator in the incident report.
C Because there was no damage.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Past Passive

GRAMMAR

In a report, what happened matters more than who did it. Use was or were plus the past participle.

FormExampleRule
Present passive (lesson 14)The flow rate is measured every hour.is / are + past participle
Past passiveThe excavation was flooded last night.was / were + past participle
Past passive + byThe pipe was hit by an excavator.by: only when who did it matters
NegativeThe workers weren't hurt.wasn't / weren't + past participle
QuestionWas the area inspected?Was / Were + subject + participle
