Lesson 16 -- Pre-class

The Miami Conference

A new event, nobody you know, and a plan for the first ninety minutes. Key words: side event, conference app, networking lounge, shuttle, track, to check in, to get your bearings, to find your footing, to make the rounds, to work the room. Structure: present perfect continuous for ongoing and recent activity.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A side event -- a smaller meeting, breakfast or reception organized around a conference, often by invitation
"The best conversation of the day happened at a side event, not in the main hall."
A conference app -- the phone application with the agenda, the floor plan and the list of attendees
"I've been using the conference app all morning to find the right rooms."
A networking lounge -- a quiet area with sofas and coffee where attendees sit down and meet people
"Let's meet in the networking lounge at eleven. It's quieter there."
A shuttle -- a bus that takes people between the hotels and the conference center on a fixed schedule
"The first shuttle leaves the hotel at seven thirty, so I'll get a coffee on the way."
A track -- a series of sessions on the same topic that runs through the whole conference
"I'm following the payments track, so I'll skip the sessions on digital assets."
To check in -- to arrive at an event or a hotel and register, usually to collect a badge or a key
"I checked in at the registration desk and had my badge in five minutes."
To get your bearings -- to understand where you are and how a new place is organized
"Give me ten minutes to get my bearings, and then I'll find the meeting room."
To find your footing -- to start feeling confident in a new situation after a slow beginning
"I just got in this morning, so I'm still finding my footing."
To make the rounds -- to walk around and talk briefly to several people, or visit several booths, one after another
"After lunch, I made the rounds of the payment booths and came back with six cards."
To work the room -- to move around an event and talk to many people in a confident, purposeful way
"She worked the room at the reception and spoke to everybody who mattered."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At any conference, the first question people ask each other is not Who are you? It is How's it going? or What have you been doing? And the natural answer is an activity that started earlier and has not stopped: I've been walking the floor since eight, or We've been talking to vendors all morning.

That is the present perfect continuous: have or has, then been, then the verb with -ing. It is the right answer when the activity is still going on, or has just stopped and you can still see it: You look tired. — I've been running between sessions. The question form moves have to the front: Have you been following the payments track?, How long have you been waiting?

Brazilian speakers usually make one mistake here, because Portuguese uses the present tense for this idea. In English, I am waiting since eight is wrong. With since, for and how long, the answer is always I've been waiting.

Two small rules finish the picture. When you give a result or a number, use the simple form: I've been meeting vendors all morning. I've met six. And verbs like know, understand and belong do not take -ing: I've known her for years, never I've been knowing her.

1. According to the text, when is the present perfect continuous the right answer?
A When the activity finished a long time ago.
B When the activity started earlier and is still going on, or has just stopped.
C When you want to give an exact number.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A I am waiting for the shuttle since eight.
B I wait for the shuttle since eight.
C I've been waiting for the shuttle since eight.
3. Why does the text say "I've met six" and not "I've been meeting six"?
A Because six is a result, and results take the simple form.
B Because meet never takes -ing.
C Because the activity is still going on.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect Continuous

GRAMMAR

Started earlier, still going: have been + -ing.

FormStructureExample
Affirmativesubject + have / has been + verb-ingI've been walking the floor since eight.
Negativesubject + haven't / hasn't been + verb-ingI haven't been following the crypto track.
QuestionHave / Has + subject + been + verb-ing?Have you been following the payments track?
How long?How long have / has + subject + been + verb-ing?How long have you been waiting?
Activity vs resultcontinuous for the activity · simple for the result or numberI've been meeting vendors. I've met six.
State verbsknow, understand, belong: simple onlyI've known her for years.

Never: I am waiting since eight · How long are you waiting? · I've been knowing him · I've been meeting six vendors
