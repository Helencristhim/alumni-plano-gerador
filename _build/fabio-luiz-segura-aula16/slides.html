Chapter 4: What You've Been Doing

Since Eight, and Still Going

The structure for an activity that started earlier and has not stopped

Grammar Discovery

Four Answers to “How's It Going?”

"I have been walking the floor since eight."

"Have you been following the payments track?"

"We've been talking to vendors all morning."

"How long have you been waiting for the shuttle?"

What do the red words have in common? Did the activity start today or earlier, and is it finished?

UseFormExample
Activity that started earlier and continues nowhave / has + been + verb-ingI have been walking the floor since eight.
Recent activity you can still seesame form, often with all morning, all dayYou look tired. — I've been running between sessions.
QuestionHave / Has + subject + been + -ing? · How long have you been ...?How long have you been waiting?
Negativehaven't / hasn't + been + -ingI haven't been following the crypto track.
Activity vs resultcontinuous = the activity · simple = the result or the numberI've been meeting vendors all morning. I've met six.
The trapno present simple or continuous with since / for · no -ing with know, understand, belongNot I am walking since eight. Not I've been knowing him. Say I've known him.

In one line: when the activity started earlier and is still going, the answer is have been + -ing — never the present.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Three Voices, Day One

An announcement, a coffee station, and somebody in a hurry

Listening 1 · The Announcement

Welcome to Miami

The organizers speak before the doors open. Sound first — no text.

1. Where is the registration desk, and when does it close?
At the Hall A entrance. It closes at eight thirty.
2. What can you find in the conference app?
The floor plan, the full agenda and the four tracks: payments, digital assets, regulation and small business.
3. What should you do before six, and why?
Check the app for invitations, because the side events this evening are by invitation only.
Listening 2 · The Coffee Station

My Third Time Here

A European banker explains how she spends the first morning. Sound first — no text.

1. How many times has she been to this conference?
This is her third time.
2. Where has she been spending most of the morning, and why?
In the networking lounge, because that is where people actually stop and talk.
3. Why does she say not to make the rounds of the booths before lunch?
In the morning everybody is fresh and busy, and the vendors only want the big names. After lunch they are tired and happy to talk to anybody.
Listening 3 · The Hallway

Two Minutes, One Card

An executive between sessions, in a hurry. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he do, and where?
He works in compliance for a bank in Charlotte.
2. How long has his bank been looking at Brazil, and what is its problem?
For about a year, because of instant payments. The problem is not the technology but knowing who is on the other side of the payment.
3. What exactly does he ask Fabio to do?
Send him an email today, not next week, with the word Brazil in the subject line.
