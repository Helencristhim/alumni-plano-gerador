Where Authority Is Lost

Three Ways an Email Loses Its Weight

The buried ask

context, background, justification — and the request in the last line, where nobody reaches it

Borrowed courtesy

three lines of apology before anything happens — it reads as hesitation, not as warmth

The open date

"as soon as possible" is not a date, and nobody has ever put it in a calendar

None of the three is a mistake in English. All three are what a perfectly good Portuguese email looks like once you translate it word for word.

Chapter 3: The Code

Two Futures, Two Effects

Future perfect and future continuous — the tenses an inbox runs on

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"By Thursday we will have closed the Colombian renewal."

"Next week I will be presenting the figures in Milan."

"By the time you land, the wording will have been signed off."

"Will you be sending the Mexican numbers before Friday?"

Two of them stand at a future moment and look back at something already finished. Two of them stand in the middle of something that is running. Which is which — and which of the four asks for something without giving an order?

FormWhat it does in an emailExample
will have + past participlecloses a commitment against a date: at that moment, this is doneBy Thursday we will have closed the Colombian renewal.
will have been + past participlesame deadline, but the doer does not matter — the register of governanceBy the time you land, the wording will have been signed off.
will be + -ingstates an arrangement already in place. Neutral: no promise extracted, no favor askedNext week I will be presenting the figures in Milan.
Will you be + -ing?asks without giving an order. "Send me the numbers" commands; this one enquiresWill you be sending the Mexican numbers before Friday?
by / by the timethe anchor the future perfect needs. by Thursday = no later than Thursdayby Wednesday evening · by the end of the quarter · by the time you land
Trap 1 — by, not untilby Thursday = at any point up to Thursday, and then it is done. until Thursday = the whole time continuing up to Thursday. A deadline is always by.
Trap 2 — no will after when / by the time"By the time you land..." — the time clause stays in the present, and the will lives in the other half of the sentence.

The Executive Point

The Same Email, Written Twice

A — courtesy first, ask last

Dear Lars,
I hope this message finds you well. Following our conversation last week regarding the Colombian renewal, and considering the amendments requested by the broker, we have prepared a revised version of the wording, which our legal team has already reviewed. Two clauses were amended. Given the timeline of the process and the fact that the market closes at the end of the month, it would be very important to have your position on this matter.
Best regards,
Denise

Ninety words. The ask arrives in line seven, and it never names a date.

B — ask first, courtesy last

Lars,
I need your sign-off on two clauses by Wednesday, 18:00. By Thursday we will have closed the Colombian renewal.
The two changes are in the attached one-pager; the rest is the boilerplate we have used since March. I have looped in Mexico as a heads-up, because the same wording will be going to them in April.
If I have not heard from you by Wednesday evening, I will chase once and then proceed as drafted.
Thank you for turning this around in what I know is a full week.
Denise

Eighty words. The ask and the date are in line one, and the warmth is still there.

Nothing in A is wrong English. A is a Portuguese email translated with great care. The only thing that changed in B is the order — and the order is what carries authority in an inbox.

Chapter 4: The Inbox

Lars, Group Operations

Two hundred emails a day, and two lines of attention for each one.

Dialogue

Under Forty Other Emails

L
Denise, I found your note about Colombia this morning, under about forty others. I nearly missed it. Tell me on this call what you need, and then we will fix the email.
D
Your sign-off on two clauses. By Thursday we will have closed the renewal, so Wednesday evening is the real deadline, not Thursday.
L
Then that sentence should have been your subject line. Yours says Colombia, update. What was I supposed to do with an update?
D
Nothing, which is why it sat there. I wrote it the way I would write it in Portuguese. Context first, the request at the end, and a paragraph of courtesy before either of them.
L
Put the ask at the top and the date next to it. Anything I open after nine at night, I read two lines of. That is not attitude, Denise. That is bandwidth.
D
Understood. The thread below the ask has the two clauses that changed, and the rest is the boilerplate we have used since March. I have also looped in Mexico, because the same wording will be going to them in April.
L
Good, and put exactly that in writing, not only on this call. I want a written trail and so do you. What is your turnaround if I come back with questions on Wednesday?
D
Same day. I will acknowledge within the hour even if the answer takes until the evening. And if I have not heard from you by Wednesday evening, I will chase once and then proceed as drafted.
L
That last sentence is the whole email. Lead with it next time and I will be answering you before lunch.
Comprehension

About Lars

1. What does he say happened to her email that morning?
He found it under about forty others and nearly missed it.
2. What does he say he does with anything he opens after nine at night?
He reads two lines of it — and he calls that bandwidth, not attitude.
3. Why does he want the same information in writing, and not only on the call?
He wants a written trail, and he says she wants one too.
Listening

The First Two Lines

A group chief of staff on what actually happens to your message. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does he say he actually reads before deciding what to do with a message?
The subject line and the first two lines — and that is the whole decision.
2. What does he say a subject line has to carry?
The ask and the date, not the topic. Not "Colombia update", but "Colombia renewal, decision needed by Thursday".
3. What happens to a message whose ask he cannot see straight away?
It goes into the pile he will get to on Sunday, and Sunday is where good work goes to die.
4. What is his rule when you have three things to ask for?
Three emails, one ask each — and then you will get three answers.
Listening 2

Cold, or Just Clear

A regional director on the warmth that was not landing as warmth. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What did she used to put at the beginning of every email, and why did she stop?
Two or three lines of warmth. She stopped because they were landing as hesitation, and hesitation from a director reads as weakness.
2. What does she say actually sounds childish — and what does not?
The pile of apologies around the ask sounds childish. Directness does not.
3. Where does the warm sentence live in her emails now?
At the end, and it is one sentence rather than a paragraph.
4. What is her position on apologizing for a deadline?
She never does it. A deadline is a fact of the business and not a favor, so she states it and says what happens if it moves.
Chapter 5: Practice

The Lines You Will Actually Type

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "Until Thursday we will have closed the Colombian renewal."
"By Thursday we will have closed the Colombian renewal." (by = no later than that point. until = the whole time continuing up to it)
2. "By the time you will land, the wording will have been signed off."
"By the time you land, the wording will have been signed off." (after by the time, when or as soon as, the verb stays in the present)
3. "I remain at your disposal for any clarification."
"Let me know if anything here needs unpacking." (a word-for-word translation of a Portuguese closing; in English it sounds like a supplier waiting outside the door)
4. "Any doubt, contact me."
"If you have any questions, come straight to me." (a doubt is a suspicion about somebody's honesty; the word you want is question)
The Real Thing

The Executive Email

Monday 07:42
Colombia renewal — sign-off needed by Wed 18:00
From  D. Gadelha Rocha — Commercial Director, Brazil
To  L. Nyberg — Group Chief Operating Officer
Cc  Underwriting Mexico · Regional Finance

Lars,

I need your sign-off on clauses 4 and 11 by Wednesday, 18:00. By Thursday we will have closed the Colombian renewal, and the wording travels to the broker the same morning.

Action items
Sign off clauses 4 and 11Lars · Wed 18:00
Send the final wording to the brokerDenise · Thu 12:00
Brief the Mexican underwriting teamMexico · Fri

The thread below has both clauses; the rest is the boilerplate we have used since March. Mexico is on copy as a heads-up, because the same wording will be going to them in April.

Write your own closing line here. One sentence of warmth — and no apology for the deadline.

Denise

1. Why does the subject line carry a time and not only a day?
Because Thursday is when the renewal closes, so Wednesday evening is the moment his answer stops being useful. A day is a wish; a time is something he can put in a calendar.
2. The email says what will happen if the deadline passes. Why does that not read as a threat?
Because it names her own next step rather than his failure, and it gives him the choice while there is still time to make one. A surprise in April would be the threat.
