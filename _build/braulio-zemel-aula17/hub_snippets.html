Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. None of these is a feeling -- they all describe something that happens between people. These twelve stay here in your material.

Bond -- a tie between people that survives long silences
"The bond between them survived eleven years and about four conversations."
To nurture -- to look after something so that it grows
"You cannot nurture a friendship at Christmas and expect it in March."
Dynamic -- the way a particular group of people behave towards each other
"The dynamic changed the year my father stopped driving."
Generation -- everybody born in roughly the same stretch of years
"My generation was taught to provide and never once taught to explain."
Expectation -- what somebody assumes you are going to do
"Half of every family argument is an expectation nobody ever said out loud."
Distance -- the space between two people who live close together
"There was no fight. There was distance, and it took about six years."
Presence -- being fully there, not simply in the room
"He was in the house every evening. Presence is a different thing, and he knows it."
Meaningful -- carrying real weight rather than good manners
"Twenty minutes on a Sunday is more meaningful than a week in December."
Devoted -- committed to somebody over a long stretch of time
"She is devoted to her mother in a way that costs her something every week."
To grow apart -- to become slowly less close, without any argument
"Two people can grow apart without anybody doing anything wrong at all."
Estranged -- no longer in contact, after something broke
"They have been estranged since the funeral and neither of them can say why."
Kinship -- the feeling of family, including people who are not
"There is a kinship there that has nothing to do with blood."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Ask a man of sixty-six about the economy and you get three clauses, a qualification and a counterargument. Ask him about his son and you get four words. He is a good boy. The vocabulary is not the problem, and neither is the feeling. What is missing is a habit nobody ever taught him: describing a person the way you would describe a country.

The method is small and it is not emotional. One specific habit. One specific moment. And what those two things tell you. He answers every message within a minute and has never once asked me for anything, which tells you most of what you need to know about him. That is a portrait. He is a good boy is a certificate, and nobody can answer a certificate.

The second half of the equipment is a tense. Family regret almost never sits in one time. Something happened in 1995 and you are living with the result on a Tuesday in 2026. English has a shape for exactly that. If I had retired five years earlier, I would be much further along with the grandchildren. The if half is the past. The other half is now. If I were the kind of man who says these things, I would have said it at his wedding. Here it runs the other way: a permanent condition about who you are, with a result back in the past.

There is one more thing worth knowing about this vocabulary. Most of it describes things that happen without anybody deciding them. Two people grow apart and nobody did anything wrong. A dynamic changes the year somebody stops driving. Distance arrives over six years and there is nobody to forgive. Having the words for that is not sentimental. It is the difference between something you can talk about and something you cannot.

1. According to the text, what is missing when somebody says he is a good boy?
A The vocabulary and the feeling.
B The habit of describing a person with specific facts.
C The correct verb tense.
2. In If I had retired earlier, I would be closer to them now, which half is in the past?

B The second half -- and the if half is now.
C Both halves are in the past.
3. What does the text say about growing apart?
A It always follows an argument.
B It happens without anybody doing anything wrong, so there is nobody to forgive.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Mixed Conditionals

GRAMMAR

A conditional whose two halves are in different times. It is the natural shape of family regret, and it is the one conditional that most advanced speakers never quite build correctly.

FormWhat it doesExample
If + past perfect, would + bare verbCondition in the past, result you are living with now."If I had retired earlier, I would be closer to them now."
If + were, would have + past participleA permanent condition about who you are, result back in the past."If I were that kind of man, I would have said it then."
Which half carries which timeThe if half carries the CONDITION, the other half carries the RESULT. They do not have to be in the same time."If I had not worked those hours, I would have a different relationship today."
Were, never wasIn this conditional English uses were for every person, including I and she."If I were you..."
NOT "If I was you..."
The trapsWould never appears inside the if half, and what follows would is always bare.NOT "If I would have retired"
NOT "I would had"

A practical note. When you build one of these out loud, decide the RESULT half first. That is the half you actually care about, it is almost always in the present, and once it is fixed the if half has only one possible shape.

Quiz

A He is a very good boy and I am proud of him.
B He answers every message within a minute and has never once asked me for anything.
C He is fine, thank you.
Which sentence is correct?
A If I would have retired earlier, I would be closer to them.
B If I had retired earlier, I would be closer to them.
C If I had retired earlier, I would had been closer to them.
You want to say that a permanent fact about you explains something you did years ago:
A If I was that kind of man, I would say it then.
B If I were that kind of man, I would have said it then.
C If I had been that kind of man, I would say it now.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer, and nobody is scoring it.

Record about ninety seconds. Then finish with one mixed conditional -- something that would be different now if something had gone differently then.
