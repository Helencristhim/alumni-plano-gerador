Chapter 3: The Code

Must, Has To, Should

The grammar of a recommendation

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Under this clause, the supplier must notify us within ten days."

"In Brazil, the company has to keep the records for five years."

"In my view, we should replace this wording before signature."

"We should not accept an indemnity with no limit."

Why blue when the contract or the law decides, but purple when you decide?

FormUseExample
must + verbAn obligation written in the contract itselfThe supplier must notify us.
have to / has to + verbAn obligation that comes from outside — the law, the regulatorThe company has to keep the records.
should + verbYour professional recommendation — the client can still say noWe should replace this wording.
must not / cannotProhibition — it is forbiddenYou must not disclose the terms.

No to after must and should: must sign, never must to sign. And no -s in the third person: she should review, never she shoulds.

The Structure

Three Lines, Every Time

Never start from nothing again. Every contract problem fits this shape.

1 · The problem

"My concern is clause fourteen."

2 · The consequence

"As it is written, the indemnity has no limit, so our exposure is unlimited."

3 · The recommendation

"We should cap it at the annual contract value."

Problem → consequence → recommendation. Thirty seconds, and the room already knows what to do.

Chapter 4: The Feedback Call

Meet David

Global contracts manager · a review call

Dialogue

A Contract Review Call

D
Hi Ana, thanks for turning this around so fast. I have the clause list in front of me. Where do you want to start?
A
Hi David. My main concern is clause fourteen, the indemnity. Everything else is minor.
D
Fourteen. Right. What is the issue with it?
A
As it is written, the indemnity has no cap. If a claim comes from a third party, our liability is unlimited, and under Brazilian law that part is probably not enforceable anyway.
D
So it is bad for us and it does not even work. What do you recommend?
A
We should cap it at the annual contract value, and we should add local wording. It is a short amendment — two sentences, not a new contract.
D
That is clear. Send me those two sentences today and I will take them to the other side. Thank you, Ana — that was the fastest review I have had this month.

The Real Thing

Walk Me Through This Clause

Master Services Agreement
Clause 14 — Indemnity
Reviewed by A. Valdivia
Confidential

"14.1 The Supplier shall indemnify and hold harmless the Client against any and all losses, damages, claims and expenses of any nature whatsoever arising out of or in connection with this Agreement, without limitation as to amount or duration."

Governing law: England and Wales · Supplier incorporated in Brazil

1. What exactly is the problem with 14.1?
There is no cap: "without limitation as to amount or duration" means the supplier's liability is unlimited and never expires.
2. Why does the governing law line matter here?
The supplier is Brazilian but the clause is read under English law, so a limit that would be implied in Brazil is not implied here.
3. What would you recommend, in one sentence?
We should cap the indemnity at the annual contract value and add a time limit for claims.
