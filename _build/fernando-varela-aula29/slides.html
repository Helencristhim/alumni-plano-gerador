Chapter 3: The Code

Too Far or Close Enough?

too · too much · too many · enough

Grammar Discovery

What Do the Orange Words Say?

"Building A is too far from the hospital."

"There is too much traffic on that street."

"The kitchen isn't big enough for two families."

"We have enough money for four apartments."

Look at the orange words. Is too far a good thing or a problem? Where is enough: before or after big? Before or after money?

FormUseExample
too + adjectiveMore than we want: it is a problem.too far · too noisy · too expensive
too many + pluralThings you can count: more than we want.too many stairs · too many cars
too much + nounThings you can't count: more than we want.too much traffic · too much noise
adjective + enoughThe right amount. With not: less than we need.close enough · It isn't big enough.
enough + nounEnough comes BEFORE the noun.enough money · enough rooms
The trapToo is not very. It's very big can be good; it's too big is always a problem. And big enough — never enough big; too expensive — never too much expensive.

Too is MORE than we want. Not enough is LESS than we need. Enough is just right.

Chapter 4: The Call

Three Buildings, One Choice

Fernando talks to Sarah on a video call

Dialogue

The Video Call

S
So, you visited three buildings this week. Which one is the best?
F
Building A is beautiful, but it's too far from the hospital. It takes forty minutes by car.
S
That's too long for the families. What about Building B?
F
It's very close, but it's too noisy. There's too much traffic on that street.
S
OK. And the last one, Building C?
F
Building C is my favorite. The apartments are bright, and the rent is affordable.
S
Great. Is there an elevator? A lot of our patients can't use the stairs.
F
Yes, there is. The only downside is the kitchen. It isn't big enough for two families.
S
That's OK. Do you have enough money for six apartments there?
F
Not for six. We have enough money for four. The landlord is very flexible, so maybe more next year.
