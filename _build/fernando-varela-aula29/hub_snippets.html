Vocabulary

Landlord -- the person who owns a house or an apartment and rents it to other people
"The landlord wants to meet us on Tuesday."
Noisy -- with a lot of loud sounds
"The street is noisy at night."
Affordable -- not expensive, so you can pay for it
"The rent in Building C is affordable."
Convenient -- easy and useful for you, for example close to the places you need
"The building is convenient: the hospital is ten minutes away."
Elevator -- a machine that carries people up and down in a building
"There is an elevator, so the patients don't need the stairs."
Downside -- the bad part of something that is mostly good
"The only downside of this apartment is the small kitchen."
Crowded -- full of people, with very little free space
"The bus stop is crowded in the morning."
Bright -- with a lot of light from the sun
"The bedrooms are big and bright."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The housing pilot needs a new building, so Fernando visited three. Building A is beautiful, but it is too far from the hospital: forty minutes by car. Building B is very close, but it is too noisy. There is too much traffic, and there are too many buses on the street.

Building C is bright and affordable, and it has an elevator. The only downside is the kitchen: it isn't big enough for two families. Fernando calls Sarah. "We don't have enough money for six apartments," he says, "but we have enough for four." Sarah smiles: "Four is a good start."

1. "Building A is too far from the hospital." Is the distance a problem?
A No. Too far means very far, and it is only a description, so the families can live there without any problem.
B Yes. It is more than we want, so it is a problem.
C We don't know.
2. "The kitchen isn't big enough for two families." What does it mean?
A The kitchen is too small for two families.
B The kitchen is very big, so two families can cook there together at the same time every day.
C The kitchen is new.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A We don't have money enough for six apartments, but we have for four in Building C.
B We don't have too much money enough for six apartments.
C We don't have enough money for six apartments.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Too and Enough

GRAMMAR

Too means more than we want: a problem. Not enough means less than we need.

PartRuleExample
too + adjectivea problem: more than we wantIt's too far. It's too noisy.
too many + pluralthings you can counttoo many stairs · too many cars
too much + nounthings you can't counttoo much traffic · too much noise
adjective + enoughthe right amount; with not = less than we needIt's close enough. It isn't big enough.
enough + nounenough comes before the nounWe have enough money for four apartments.

Too is not very: it is very big can be good, but it is too big is always a problem. Say big enough (not enough big) and too expensive (not too much expensive).

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think of a place you know well: your department at Einstein, your office or your home. Take about ninety seconds: what is the best thing about it, what is too... or isn't ... enough, and what one change do you want to make? Use at least two sentences with too, one with too much or too many, and two with enough. Do not stop to correct yourself.
