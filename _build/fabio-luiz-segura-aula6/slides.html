Chapter 4: We Used To

How It Was, and Is Not Any More

The structure that carries a whole decade in three words

Grammar Discovery

Which Two Have Stopped?

"Banks used to send short coded messages with very little room for information."

"Last year we tested two providers and chose neither of them."

"Compliance teams used to check everything by telephone, and now they do not."

"I am used to meetings in English now, although the first year was hard."

The red lines describe a habit that has stopped, without anybody having to say when. The last blue line looks almost identical and means something completely different.

JobFormExample
A habit or a state that has stoppedused to + bare verbBanks used to send short coded messages.
One finished event, with a timepast simpleLast year we tested two providers.
Negativedid not use to + verb (no D)We did not use to publish those figures.
QuestionDid + subject + use to + verbDid you use to run both systems in parallel?
The lookalike: being accustomedbe used to + noun or verb-ingI am used to meetings in English. Not I am used to meet.
The contrast pair, in one breathused to ... , now ...We used to reconcile by hand. Now the platform does it.
The trapthe D disappears after didNot did not used to. Say did not use to.

In one line: "used to" carries a whole decade in three words, and it says the habit has ended without anybody having to give a date.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Three People Who Build These Things

And none of them selling you anything

Listening 1 · Three Phrases

The Words That Commit Nobody

Somebody who designs these systems, on the vocabulary of the floor. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say "API first" actually describes?
How they built their own product, which tells you nothing about how hard it will be to connect to yours.
2. What is his objection to the word friction?
It covers everything from two days of settlement delay to one extra click, and those are not the same problem or the same price.
3. What does he say about "enterprise grade", and what does he recommend doing?
It has no definition and nobody has ever failed an enterprise grade test, because there is no test. He recommends asking them to say the sentence again without the phrase.
Listening 2 · The Demo

Everything You Are Watching Was Chosen

Somebody who has built plenty of demos, on what a demo can and cannot show. Sound first — no text.

1. What three things does she say about the conditions of a demo?
The volume is small, the data is clean, and the two systems were built by the same team in the same week.
2. Which question does she say a demo answers, and which ones does it not?
It answers whether the idea works at all. It answers nothing about your volume, your data or your regulator.
3. What is the question she would ask at the end of every demo?
What did you take out of this demo to make it run smoothly, and what would I see if you put it back in?
Listening 3 · How Recent This Is

The Gap Everybody Is Selling Against

A short piece on what changed, and why that change created a market. Sound first — no text.

1. How does she describe the way banks used to send payment instructions?
As short coded messages with very little room for information, and everybody accepted that a cross-border payment took days because there was no alternative.
2. Why does she say so much compliance checking used to happen by telephone?
Because compliance teams were working from those same thin messages.
3. What does she say made the old situation impossible to defend?
The gap between what a customer experienced at home, with instant domestic systems, and what they experienced abroad.
