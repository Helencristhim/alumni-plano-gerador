Lesson 10 -- The Book You Are Reading

Podcast
Hidden Brain (NPR) -- "Did That Really Happen? How Our Memories Betray Us"

The research behind the reading from class: memory as reconstruction rather than storage, and what that does to eyewitness testimony. The host speaks slowly and rephrases every technical term, which makes it an unusually comfortable hour of dense English.

Tip: the page carries the transcript. Listen first, then find three sentences where the speaker uses the past perfect to place an earlier event, and read them aloud.

Talk
Andrew Stanton -- "The clues to a great story" (TED, 19 min)

The writer of Toy Story and WALL-E on why a story withholds its backstory, how a scene earns its ending, and why the audience has to be made to want the explanation before you give it. It is the same argument the novelist makes in the first listening from class, from inside the trade.

Tip: note the moment he tells his own childhood story. Write down, in one sentence each, what was going on and what had already happened before it -- the two tenses from this lesson.

Watch on TED ↗
Series
Unbelievable (Netflix) -- limited series, eight episodes

A young woman gives an account of an assault, changes a detail, and is charged with lying. The series is built entirely on the question this lesson raises: what a statement is worth when memory is reconstructive, and what happens when an investigator treats an inconsistency as proof rather than as information.

Tip: watch the first episode with English subtitles and the second without. In episode two, count how often the detectives ask what somebody was doing rather than what happened.
