Grammar Review

What Does Each Sentence Do?

We are going to plant two new farms next year.

We need the first shipment by March.

To begin with, thank you for your trust.

It rained a lot. However, the farms are on track.

We have worked together since 2025.

A plan, a deadline, a signpost, a link, or how long?

ToolStructureExample
Plans (L25)going to + verb (plan) · will + verb (decision now, promise)We are going to plant. I’ll send it.
Deadlines (L26)by (not later than) · until (all the time up to)Send it by Friday.
Signposts (L27)to begin with · moving on to · finally · to sum upTo begin with, thank you.
Links (L28)so / as a result (result) · however / although (contrast)However, we are on track.
How long (L29)have/has + participle + for / sinceWe have worked together since 2025.

Chapter 4: The Contract Meeting

Three Years, One Plan

The meeting, Claire’s update and news from the farms

Dialogue

Closing the Details

C
Good morning, Rafael. So, are you ready to talk about the three-year contract?
R
Yes. To begin with, thank you for your trust. We have worked together since 2025, and we want to continue.
C
Great. Our target for 2027 is six hundred tons of mangoes. Is that possible?
R
Yes. We are going to plant two new farms, so we can deliver more.
C
Good. And the timeline? We need the first shipment by March.
R
The new farms are on track. However, the rainy season is always a risk.
C
I understand. What happens if there’s a delay?
R
I’ll call you the same day, and we’ll send fruit from the other warehouse. That’s a promise.
C
Perfect. You have always been reliable. So the key takeaway for my directors is simple: Picunha is ready.
R
Exactly. I’ll send you the draft contract by Friday.
