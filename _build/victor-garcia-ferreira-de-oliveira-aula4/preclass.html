Lesson 04 -- Pre-class

What Would You Do? -- The Decisions You Have Not Made Yet

If the offer arrives, you will decide. If the offer arrived tomorrow, you would decide differently. English keeps those two apart, and so should you. Key words: a deadline, tuition, a scholarship, eligible, affordable, to turn something down, to weigh something up, a backup plan, a long shot, to be worth the risk. Structure: first and second conditionals.

"He turned down the first offer and waited three weeks for the second one."
To weigh something up -- to compare the good side and the bad side before you decide
"I spent a week weighing it up, and the week was not wasted."
A backup plan -- the plan you use if the first one does not work out
"If Canada does not happen, the backup plan is not nothing. It is a different door."
A long shot -- something with a small chance of working, which you try anyway
"The full scholarship is a long shot, and a long shot you do not take is a no."
To be worth the risk -- giving back more than it might cost you if it goes wrong
"One extra year of preparation would be worth the risk if the offer were the right one."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

An application year is made of sentences that begin with if, and there are two kinds. The first kind is a plan. If I get the offer in March, I will accept it the same week. If I miss the deadline, there will not be a second chance this year. Both of those could really happen, so the verb after if is the present simple and the other half takes will. Notice that the present simple is doing the work of the future here, which is exactly what confuses a Portuguese speaker.

The second kind is a thought experiment. If the tuition were free, I would go tomorrow. If I had another year, I would apply to six places instead of two. Nothing there is a plan: the tuition is not free and there is no extra year. So the verb after if steps back into the past simple, and the other half takes would. With the verb to be, careful English uses were for every person: if I were you, never if I was you.

One rule fixes almost every mistake at this level, and it has no exceptions worth learning yet: never put will or would immediately after if. Not if I will get the offer, and not if I would have the money. The other half of the sentence carries those words; the if half never does. And do not mix the two kinds: if I have another year, I would apply is half a plan and half a daydream, and it leaves the listener unsure which one you meant.

1. "If I get the offer in March, I will accept it." Why is the verb after "if" in the present?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- First and Second Conditionals

GRAMMAR

Present after "if" means it might really happen. Past after "if" means you are only imagining it.

JobFormExample
A real, likely futureIf + present simple, + will + base formIf I get the offer, I will accept it.
A real, likely future (negative)If + present simple, + will not + base formIf I miss the deadline, there will not be a second chance.
An imagined or unlikely situationIf + past simple, + would + base formIf I had another year, I would apply to six places.
The verb to be, imaginedIf + were, for every personIf the tuition were free, I would go tomorrow.
AdviceIf I were you, I would + base formIf I were you, I would apply to both.
QuestionsWhat will you do if...? · What would you do if...?What would you do if the answer were no?
The rule that fixes everythingnever will or would immediately after ifNot if I would have the money. Say if I had the money.

Never: if I would have the money · if I will get the offer · if I have another year, I would apply · if I was you.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The offer might really arrive in March. You are describing your plan. You say:
A "If I will get the offer, I will accept it."
B "If I get the offer in March, I will accept it the same week."
C "If I would get the offer, I accept it."
There is no extra year and there never will be. You are imagining. You say:
A "If I had another year, I would apply to six places."
B "If I have another year, I would apply to six places."
C "If I would have another year, I applied to six places."
A friend asks for your advice about an offer. You say:
A "If I was you, I would send both."
B "If I were you, I would find out what the number covers first."
C "If I would be you, I send both."
You want to hand the question back at the end of your answer. You say:
A "And you, what you would do?"
B "And you, what will you do if the answer would arrive tomorrow?"
C "And you, what would you do if the answer arrived tomorrow?"
