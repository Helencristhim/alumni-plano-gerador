Lesson 14 -- The Memo That Changed the Deal

Grammar Video
"Beware of nominalizations (AKA zombie nouns)" -- TED-Ed

Five minutes on the exact structure of this lesson, from the opposite side: why a sentence made only of nouns -- the review, the failure, the implementation -- loses the people who did things. Connection to Lesson 14: it is the danger the memo had to avoid, and the reason you switch back to verbs in the meeting.

Tip: pause at each zombie sentence and do the reverse of the video once -- take the lively version and write it for a board with nouns. Doing it in both directions is the skill.

Podcast
"Corporate Finance Explained | Mastering Mergers & Acquisitions" -- FinPod, Corporate Finance Institute

Twenty-three minutes on the life of a deal: valuation, financing, the risks found too late and what happens after the closing, with two real acquisitions as cases. Connection to Lesson 14: it is the world of the memo -- valuation, exposure, the closing -- told by people who model it.

Tip: every time the hosts describe a step, say it back in one written-register sentence -- following the valuation, the financing of the deal. Five of those and you have the first paragraph of a memo.

Reading
"Writing: hidden verbs" -- Plain Language Guide, Digital.gov

The United States government guide to plain writing, with a short section on hidden verbs -- nominalizations -- and how to turn them back into verbs, with before-and-after examples. Connection to Lesson 14: it is the meeting half of the lesson, written down as an official rule.

Tip: for each before-and-after example, decide which version you would put in a memo to a board and which you would say out loud. They are not always the same answer, and that judgement is the point.
