Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

Sorry, Could You Say That Again? -- Could You Tell Me Why...?

Asking for clarification in a fast international call. Key words: to clarify, to follow, to rephrase, to slow down, a misunderstanding, to catch, to recap, a follow-up, an agenda item, a lead time, a shipment, to confirm. Structure: indirect questions.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To clarify -- to make something easier to understand by explaining it
"Could you clarify the new delivery date?"
To follow -- to understand what someone is saying or explaining
"Sorry, I’m not sure I follow you."
To rephrase -- to say something again, with different words
"Could you rephrase the question, please?"
To slow down -- to speak or move at a lower speed
"Could you slow down a little? The connection is bad."
A misunderstanding -- a situation where two people understand something in different ways
"There was a misunderstanding about the price."
To catch -- to hear or understand a word or a sentence
"I didn’t catch your name."
To recap -- to repeat the main points of something, briefly
"Let me recap the three decisions."
A follow-up -- a message or an action that continues something done before
"I’ll send a follow-up email with the dates."
An agenda item -- one of the topics to discuss in a meeting
"Let’s move to the next agenda item: the prices."
A lead time -- the time between an order and its delivery
"The lead time for this part is six weeks."
A shipment -- a group of products that are sent together
"The first shipment left the port on Monday."
To confirm -- to say that something is true or that it will happen
"Can you confirm the dates by Friday?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Julius is on a video call with a supplier in India. The connection is bad, and the man speaks very fast. Julius does not catch the date of the next shipment, so he says: “Sorry, could you tell me when the shipment arrives?”

The supplier explains again, but he uses a lot of technical words. Julius asks him to slow down and to rephrase the last part. Then he asks: “Do you know whether the second shipment is also late?” and “Can you clarify what that means for the lead time?”

At the end, Julius does not say “OK, OK”. He recaps the dates, and the supplier promises a follow-up email to confirm them. There is no misunderstanding this time.

1. Why does Julius say “Could you tell me when the shipment arrives?” and not “Could you tell me when does the shipment arrive?”
A Because after the polite opening, the question becomes a normal sentence: subject + verb.
B Because does is only used with I and you, and never with the word shipment or with other nouns.
C Because the sentence is in the past.
2. What does Julius ask the supplier to do?
A To send the shipment by plane, because the ship is too slow and the customer is waiting in Brazil.
B To call again tomorrow.
C To slow down and rephrase the last part.
3. How does the call end?
A Julius says “OK, OK” and closes the call quickly because the connection is very bad.
B Julius recaps the dates, and the supplier promises a follow-up email.
C There is a misunderstanding.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Indirect Questions

GRAMMAR

Start with a polite opening: Could you tell me... / Do you know... / Can you explain... / I was wondering... Then use a normal sentence: question word (or if / whether) + subject + verb. Do not use do, does or did after the opening.

JobFormExample
Polite openingsCould you tell me... / Do you know... / Can you explain... / I was wondering...“Could you tell me when it arrives?”
Wh- question insideopening + wh- word + subject + verb“Could you tell me why the shipment is late?”
Yes / no question insideopening + if / whether + subject + verb“Do you know whether it is delayed?”
No do / does / didthe auxiliary disappears“Can you explain what you mean?”
Clarifying phrasesSorry, I didn’t catch... / Could you rephrase that? / Just to confirm...“Sorry, I didn’t catch the last part.”
The trapdo not keep the question word order after the openingNot “Could you tell me where is the plant?” Say Could you tell me where the plant is?

Never: Could you tell me where is the plant? · Do you know when does it arrive? · Can you explain what do you mean? · I was wondering could you...

Stage 2: Put the Call in Order

Order

A fast call with a supplier. Put the conversation in a logical order.

?"The ship is held at the port, so the lead time is longer."
?"Sorry, I didn’t catch that. Could you tell me why the shipment is late?"
?"Customs wants more documents."
?"Do you know whether the second shipment is also delayed?"
?"No, the second one is on time."
?"Let me recap: the first shipment is late, and the second one is on time."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A supplier says a date, but you did not hear it. You say:
A "Sorry, I didn’t catch the date. Could you say it again?"
B "What? Repeat, please. I don’t understand anything that you are saying today."
C "Sorry, I didn’t caught the date."
You want to know if the price includes transport. You ask:
A "Do you know if does the price include transport and the documents for customs?"
B "Do you know if the price includes transport?"
C "You know the price includes transport?"
A director asks a very long question with two parts. You say:
A "Just to confirm: you’re asking about the price and the lead time, right?"
B "Just to confirm: you are asking about the price, the lead time, the shipment and the documents for customs, is it?"
C "Confirm: price and lead time?"
At the end of a call, you want to repeat the main decisions. You say:
A "Let me rephrase your question in different words, because I want to be sure about it."
B "Let me recap."
C "Let me catch."
