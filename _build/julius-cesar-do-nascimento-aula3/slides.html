Chapter 3: The Code

Could You Tell Me... Do You Know Whether...

A polite opening, and then a normal sentence

Grammar Discovery

A Polite Opening, Then a Normal Sentence

"Could you tell me why the shipment is late?"

"Do you know whether the second shipment is also delayed?"

"I was wondering if you could slow down a little."

"Can you explain what that means for the lead time?"

Compare: “Why is the shipment late?” and “Could you tell me why the shipment is late?” After the polite opening, the red part is a normal sentence: subject first, then the verb. There is no do, does or did. When there is no question word, we use if or whether.

JobFormExample
Polite openingsCould you tell me... / Do you know... / Can you explain... / I was wondering...“Could you tell me when it arrives?”
Wh- question insideopening + wh- word + subject + verb“Could you tell me why the shipment is late?”
Yes / no question insideopening + if / whether + subject + verb“Do you know whether it is delayed?”
No do / does / didthe auxiliary disappears“Can you explain what you mean?”
Clarifying phrasesSorry, I didn’t catch... / Could you rephrase that? / Just to confirm...“Sorry, I didn’t catch the last part.”
The trapdo not keep the question word order after the openingNot “Could you tell me where is the plant?” Say Could you tell me where the plant is?

In one line: polite opening + question word (or if / whether) + subject + verb: Could you tell me where the plant is?

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Call at Real Speed

One video call, three recordings, one page of notes

Dialogue

A Fast Call from Chennai

R
Good afternoon, Julius. So, about the new order, the lead time is going to be a bit longer, because the ship is held at the port and we need to change the plan for the line.
J
Sorry, Rajesh, I didn’t catch the last part. Could you tell me why the shipment is late?
R
Of course. The ship is waiting at the port in Chennai, because customs wants more documents.
J
I see. And could you clarify what that means for the lead time?
R
About two more weeks. So the first castings arrive in March, not in February.
J
Just to avoid a misunderstanding: do you know whether the second shipment is also delayed?
R
No, the second one is fine. Shall I send you a follow-up email to confirm the dates?
J
Yes, please. Let me recap: the first shipment arrives in March, and the second one is on time. Is that right?
Comprehension

About Rajesh

1. Why is the first shipment late, according to Rajesh?
The ship is waiting at the port in Chennai, because customs wants more documents.
2. When will the first castings arrive now?
In March, not in February: about two weeks later.
3. What does Rajesh offer to do after the call?
To send a follow-up email to confirm the dates.
Listening 1 · A Logistics Manager

Why Shipments Are Late

An Australian logistics manager explains where most delays really come from. Sound first — no text.

1. According to him, what usually causes a late shipment?
Not one big problem, but five small ones that each add a few days.
2. What three things should you ask your logistics team?
Where the shipment is right now, what the next step is, and who is responsible for it.
3. Where do most delays come from, in his opinion?
From a misunderstanding between two people who were too polite to ask a second question.
Listening 2 · A German Engineer

English Is Not My First Language Either

An engineer from Stuttgart remembers the day she stopped pretending to understand. Sound first — no text.

1. What did she do in meetings when she did not understand?
She smiled and nodded, and later she asked a colleague what the decision was.
2. What was her expensive mistake?
She nodded at the wrong moment and agreed to an impossible delivery date.
3. How do native speakers usually react when she asks?
They slow down and thank her, because other people did not understand either.
Listening 3 · A Communication Coach

The Native Speaker’s Job

An American coach explains what native speakers should do in international meetings. Sound first — no text.

1. Who does she train?
American managers who work with international teams.
2. What are her three habits for native speakers?
Slow down, avoid idioms, and check understanding.
3. Why is “Do you understand?” a bad question, in her opinion?
Because nobody says no to it.
