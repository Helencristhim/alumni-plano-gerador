Chapter 3: The Conditional Family

Real, Imaginary, Past

first · second · third conditional

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"If the board approves the budget, we will sign on Friday."

"If I were the president of the club, I would publish the audit."

"If we had read the clause, we would have avoided the dispute."

"If the sponsor calls tomorrow, I will explain the new terms."

Look at the orange words. Which sentence is a real future possibility? Which one is imaginary now? Which one is about the past?

ConditionalFormUse
FirstIf + present, will + verba real possibility in the future
SecondIf + past, would / could + verban imaginary or unlikely situation now
ThirdIf + had + past participle, would have + past participlethe past: it did not happen, so it cannot change
Polite questionCould you tell me + where / when / if + subject + verbno question order after the opening
Reported speechShe said that + one tense back"We will sign" becomes "she said that they would sign"

Ask yourself one question: is it real, imaginary, or past? Then choose the conditional. Never use would in the if-clause: "If I had known", not "If I would have known".
