Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Follow-up -- an action or a meeting that continues something you started before
"We scheduled a follow-up for next Tuesday."
Compromise -- an agreement where each side accepts less than it wanted
"We found a compromise: three years, not five."
Trade-off -- losing one thing to get another thing that you need more
"A lower fee for a longer contract is a fair trade-off."
Binding -- official, so the people who sign it must do what it says
"Once both parties sign, the agreement is binding."
Renewal -- making a contract continue for a new period of time
"The sponsor asked for a renewal before the season."
Unanimous -- when every person in a group agrees or votes yes
"The vote was unanimous: nine votes out of nine."
Track record -- the good or bad results that a person or company had in the past
"This sponsor has a strong track record with clubs."
Turning point -- the moment when a situation starts to change in an important way
"The new compliance policy was a turning point for the club."
Allegation -- a statement that someone did something wrong, without proof yet
"An allegation is not a fact until somebody proves it."
Etiquette -- the polite rules of behavior in a social or business situation
"Business etiquette in Miami is a little more formal."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Before the Miami module, Sofia asked Nilo if he was ready for a short debrief of Block 2. Nilo said that the negotiation lessons were the turning point for him. "If I had not practiced them, I would have felt nervous at the last board meeting," he explained. In the renewal case, the club and the sponsor found a compromise, and the vote was unanimous. The contract is binding now, so the trade-off was important. Then Sofia asked about the business etiquette in Miami. "If the meeting starts at nine, I will be there at ten to nine," Nilo answered. "And if I were the coordinator, I would add one more session on small talk!"

1. What did Nilo say about the negotiation lessons?
A "They were the turning point for him."
B "They were difficult, so he did not practice them before the last board meeting of the year."
C "He will forget them."
2. Which sentence is about the past, and cannot change?
A "If the meeting starts at nine, I will be there."
B "If I were the coordinator, I would add a session."
C "If I had not practiced them, I would have felt nervous."
3. Which reported question is correct?
A "Sofia asked him if was he ready."
B "Sofia asked him if he was ready."
C "Sofia asked to him if he is ready for the business etiquette in Miami next month."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Conditional Family

GRAMMAR

Real, imaginary, or past? Choose the conditional. Then ask and report politely.

FormUseExample
First: If + present, will + verba real possibility in the futureIf they accept, we will sign.
Second: If + past, would + verban imaginary or unlikely situation nowIf I were the president, I would publish the audit.
Third: If + had + participle, would have + participlethe past: it did not happenIf we had read the clause, we would have avoided the dispute.
Could you tell me + wh- / if + subject + verba polite indirect questionCould you tell me when the vote is?
said that / told me that + one tense backreported speech"We will sign." → She said that they would sign.
RuleNever use would in the if-clause: "If I had known", not "If I would have known". After "Could you tell me", use normal sentence order. Say "said that" or "told me that", never "said me".

Quiz
