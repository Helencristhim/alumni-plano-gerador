Chapter 4: The Code

Softer, Stronger, and Why

The words that say how sure you are

Grammar Discovery

Stronger or Softer?

“There is no question that patients want certainty.”

“The evidence seems to suggest that patients trust doctors more.”

“I am convinced that patients deserve the truth.”

“I may be wrong about how that works in Brazil.”

Two colored parts make the claim stronger, and two make it softer. Look at what each claim is about: a value the speaker holds, or a fact nobody can fully prove.

JobWordsExample
Hedge — modalmight · may · couldI may be wrong about that.
Hedge — adverbarguably · apparently · presumably · probablySport arguably teaches discipline.
Hedge — verbit seems that · it appears that · I tend to think thatThe evidence seems to suggest that...
Hedge — noun / impersonalsome evidence suggests · it is generally accepted that · research suggests thatIt is generally accepted that exercise reduces stress.
Boosterclearly · certainly · undoubtedly · I am convinced that · I firmly believe · there is no question thatI am convinced that patients deserve the truth.
Stance adverbfortunately · interestingly · surprisingly · worryinglySurprisingly, the second year was easier.
The ruleboost what you believe · hedge what you cannot proveone hedge per claim is enough
The trapstacking: two hedges, or a hedge and a booster, on one claimNot obviously, maybe it could be true.

Listening 1 · A Voice Note

What Overstating Costs

A doctor on the day she changed how she talks to patients. Sound first — no text.

1. What did she believe about patients when she started working?
That they wanted certainty, so she spoke as if she had it.
2. After her mistake, what did the patient stop trusting?
Not the diagnosis, but the doctor herself.
3. How do patients hear her hedge today, as far as she can tell?
As honesty, not as weakness.
Listening 2 · A Podcast

Certain Opinion, Careful Facts

A TOEFL tutor on the two mistakes in opinion tasks. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does the first group hedge everything?
Because they think it sounds academic.
2. Where does he say you should use a booster?
In your first sentence, on your clear position.
3. Why should you hedge your evidence?
Because evidence is rarely perfect.
