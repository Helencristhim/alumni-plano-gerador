Talk
Brian Goldman — “Doctors make mistakes. Can we talk about that?” (TED)

A Canadian emergency doctor on the culture of medicine that expects doctors never to be wrong, and on what that certainty costs patients. It is the first listening from class, told from inside a hospital.

Tip: write down every time he softens or strengthens a claim. Which ones are about facts, and which ones are about what he believes?

Talk
Kathryn Schulz — “On being wrong” (TED)

A writer on why being wrong feels exactly like being right, until the moment we find out. It is the best argument you will hear for leaving room for doubt, and it is full of careful, well-built opinions.

Tip: after watching, say three sentences about her idea: one with a booster, one with a hedge, and one that starts with a stance adverb.

Talk
Julia Galef — “Why you think you’re right — even if you’re wrong” (TED)

A short talk about two ways of thinking: defending an opinion like a soldier, or checking it like a scout. Scouts are the people who hedge facts honestly and still hold firm values, exactly like the third candidate in the text.

Tip: listen for how she signals certainty. Then answer out loud: are you more of a soldier or a scout when you talk about the vestibular?
