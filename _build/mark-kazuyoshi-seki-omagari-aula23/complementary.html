Lesson 23 -- What Will Happen Next?

Series
Wednesday -- Season 1, Episode 1 ("Wednesday's Child Is Full of Woe")

A mystery built so that the audience is always one step behind, which makes it the perfect place to practise predicting out loud. Every episode drops evidence you can point at and red herrings you cannot, and separating the two is exactly what this lesson is about.

Tip: pause at the end of the first episode and write three sentences before you watch the second -- one with "will" for a pure guess, one with "going to" for something you saw on screen, and say which one you trust more.

Podcast
BBC Learning English -- Learning English Grammar: Predicting the future

Two presenters say what they think the future holds, and then a grammar specialist takes their sentences apart and explains why each one took the form it took. It is the same content as today's lesson, said by different voices and at a slightly slower speed.

Tip: the transcript is on the same page. Listen first with it covered, then read it and underline every "will", every "going to" and every present continuous.

BBC Learning English -- Future tenses: present continuous, "be going to", "will"

Four minutes on exactly the choice you practised in class, with the present continuous treated as a real future and not as an exception. Watch it after the lesson, not before -- it will confirm the rule you already worked out yourself.

Tip: after watching, take the four lines of your prediction card and check each one against the video. If any line changes, that is the one worth remembering.
