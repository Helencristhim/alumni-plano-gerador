Why One Future Is Not Enough

You can point at it

Chapter 3: The Three Futures

Same Friday, Three Sentences

The form is easy. Choosing is the lesson.

Grammar Discovery

Four Futures, Four Reasons

I think they will delay it again.

Look at the last frame. It is going to be a prequel.

I am going to watch it on Friday. I decided last week.

We are watching it together at seven on Friday.

All four are about the future and all four are correct. What is different is where each one comes from: an opinion, something on the screen, an old intention, or a time and a person.

FormUse it whenExample
will + bare verbYou are predicting or deciding right now, with nothing but your opinion behind it."I think they will delay it." / "Fine, I will watch the recording."
going to (evidence)You can point at the reason: a teaser, a leak, the clouds."Look at that frame. It is going to be a prequel."
going to (intention)The plan was already in your head before this conversation started."I am going to watch it on Friday. I decided last week."
present continuousIt is an arrangement: fixed time, and usually another person involved."We are watching it at seven." / "I am playing football on Saturday."
The testIs the plan only in your head, or already in your calendar? In your head is going to. In the calendar is the present continuous.
Two trapsNever I will go to the cinema tomorrow, I already have the ticket — that is an arrangement. And after when, if, as soon as, English drops the future: "when it comes out", never "when it will come out".
Practice

Choose the Future

Say it first, then click to check

"Look at the sky. It ___is going to rain before the match." (rain — you can point at the evidence)
"I have not decided yet. Fine, I ___will watch the recording on Sunday." (watch — decided in this second)
"I ___am playing football on Saturday morning, so I cannot stay up." (play — fixed with other people)
"I ___am going to finish the season this week. That was the plan before you asked." (finish — an old intention)
"I will tell you as soon as it ___comes out." (come — after "as soon as", English drops the future)
"The odds are that they ___will delay it at least once." (delay — a prediction, and only an opinion behind it)
The Tool

Head or Calendar?

Question 1

Am I deciding right now, while I speak?

Yes: will. This also covers a pure opinion about the future.

Question 2

Can I point at the evidence, or was the plan already in my head?

Either one: going to. The two uses look different and take the same form.

Question 3

Is there a time, and is somebody else involved?

Then it is in the calendar, not in your head: present continuous.

When two forms still look possible, choose the present continuous. It is the one you use least and the one that sounds most like a person talking.

N

N

M
I cannot. I am playing football on Saturday morning and my mother is driving me at seven. I will watch the recording on Sunday.
N
Then you are going to be the last person in the group chat to find out. Again.
Comprehension

About Nina

1. What is her first prediction after watching the teaser?
That they will cancel the whole thing — based on nothing except how short the teaser was.
2. What does she say about the theories she read that morning?
That they were all far-fetched, unlike his, which she calls plausible.
3. What does she tell him at the end, and why?
That he is going to be the last person in the group chat to find out, again, because he is not staying up for the stream.

Chapter 5: Practice

Choosing Under Pressure

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "I will play football on Saturday. My mother is driving me at seven."
"I am playing football on Saturday." (a fixed arrangement with a time and another person takes the present continuous, not "will")
2. "I will tell you as soon as it will come out."
"...as soon as it comes out." (after when, if and as soon as, English drops the future and uses the present)
3. "Look at those clouds. It will rain before the match."
"It is going to rain before the match." (you are pointing at the evidence, so "going to")
4. "I am going to attend the phone if my brother calls."
"I am going to answer the phone..." (in English "attend" means to go to an event, never to answer a call)

Your cardFour lines about something you are waiting for. One opinion, one from evidence, one intention, one arrangement.
1. Why do lines two and three use the same form for different things?
Because "going to" covers both evidence you can point at and an intention you already had. The form is the same; only the reason changes.

Because it has a time and another person: it is an arrangement, and arrangements take the present continuous.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I use "will" for a prediction or a decision I am making right now.
I use "going to" when I can point at evidence or when the plan was already in my head.
I use the present continuous for arrangements with a time and another person.
I drop the future after "when", "if" and "as soon as".
I can separate what is confirmed from what is speculation, and say so.
