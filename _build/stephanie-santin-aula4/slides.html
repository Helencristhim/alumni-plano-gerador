Chapter 3: The Code

The Auxiliary Goes First

What turns a sentence into a question, in every tense

Discover the Rule

What Do These Have in Common?

Are you here for the whole symposium?
Do you work in trauma as well?
How long have you been in this field?
What did you think of the panel this morning?
Click to reveal the rule
In all four, the auxiliary comes before the subject: are you, do you, have you, did you. That inversion is what makes a question in English — "you are here" becomes "are you here?". With a wh- word (what, how long, where, why) the auxiliary still comes before the subject: What did you think?, never What you thought?. And after do / does / did the verb stays in the base form: Did you go?, never Did you went?

Chapter 6: Your Turn

Three Times, Less Help Each Time

From the opening line on the screen to three minutes with a stranger

Guided

Opening the Conversation

Your teacher is an attendee you have never met, sitting alone at the coffee break. Open the conversation and ask the first three questions, using the words on the screen. Full questions, with the auxiliary in front.

Is this seat free?I am ... from ...Is this your first time...?Are you here for...?
Semi-free

Keeping It Alive

The conversation has started and your teacher gives short answers on purpose. Keep it alive for two minutes with follow-up questions. Every time he stops, you ask.

What did you think of...?How long have you...?That is interestingto collaborate
Free

Three Minutes at the Coffee Break

Open the conversation, keep it alive, and close it asking to stay in contact. Three minutes, no words on the screen. Your teacher will not help you restart it if it dies.
