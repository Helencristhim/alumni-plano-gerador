Lesson 16 -- What Television Gets Wrong About Law

Grammar Video
"Participle clauses" -- BBC English Class, BBC Learning English

A short class on exactly the structure of this lesson: -ing clauses, -ed clauses and having + past participle, with examples read out and explained. Connection to Lesson 16: it is the Code chapter again, from a second teacher, in a different voice.

Tip: pause before each explanation and rebuild the long version of the example yourself -- which is, who was, after he had. Then check if the video agrees.

"Real Lawyer Reacts to Suits (full episode)" -- LegalEagle

An American trial lawyer watches the first episode of Suits and stops it every few minutes to say what is accurate and what is far-fetched: the interview, the mock trial, the objections. Connection to Lesson 16: it is the article you read, said out loud by somebody who has stood in a real courtroom.

Tip: every time he says that something is wrong, write one sentence about it with a participle clause -- Asked about the interview, he says... It is your homework, and it is easier with the video paused.

"Corporate Lawyer Reacts to Suits (S1, Ep 3)" -- Julian Sarafian

A corporate lawyer, not a litigator, reacts to an early episode of Suits: billable hours, junior associates, how a firm really treats a mistake. Connection to Lesson 16: it is the behind-the-scenes half of the lesson, closer to your own work than any courtroom.

Tip: note every word from the lesson you hear -- billable hour, paralegal, deposition -- and the sentence it was in. Hearing the word in a real lawyer's mouth is what makes it yours.
