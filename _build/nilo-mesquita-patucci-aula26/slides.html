Chapter 3: The Particle Code

Where Does It Go?

set it up · pass it on · run into him · catch up

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I'll set a call up. Don't worry, I'll set it up."

"Can you pass my card on? Please pass it on to Sofia."

"I ran into Daniel at the hotel. I ran into him at the bar."

"We haven't talked for months. Let's catch up."

Look at the orange words. Where do it and him go? Which verb has no object?

TypeRuleExample
Separable: set up, pass on, hand out, look upa noun can go before or after the particle; a pronoun goes in the MIDDLEset up a call / set a call up / set it up
Inseparable: run intothe object always goes AFTER the particlerun into Daniel / run into him
No object: catch up, get together, come across (as)no object; add a person with withLet's catch up. / Let's catch up with Daniel.
Fixed: hit it off, keep in touchthe words never change place; add a person with withWe hit it off. / Let's keep in touch.

These are phrasal verbs: a verb + a particle (up, on, out, into). With a separable verb, say set it up, never "set up it". With an inseparable verb, say run into him, never "run him into".

Five Habits of Great Networkers

Networking at a Leadership Program: Five Habits That Work

At the first module of a leadership program, the welcome reception is not a party. It is the first networking event of the year. In one evening, you can meet forty people who work in football all over the world. But many participants make the same mistake: they hand out fifty business cards, and the next day they remember nobody.

The first habit of great networkers starts before the event. They look up the other participants online, and they choose three or four people that they really want to meet. When they run into one of them at the bar, they already have a good question.

Second, they ask questions and listen. People who talk only about themselves come across as arrogant. People who ask good questions come across as interesting. Very often, two people hit it off in the first five minutes.

Third, they offer help. A good networker passes on a contact, an article or an idea, and asks for nothing in return.

Finally, they follow up. Within forty-eight hours, they send a short message, and they set up a call or a coffee. Networking is not one evening. It is the decision to keep in touch, and to get together again at the next module.
