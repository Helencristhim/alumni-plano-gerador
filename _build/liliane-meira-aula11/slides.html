Chapter 3: The Code

Precise Without Being Exact

roughly · just under · a few · hardly any · the bulk of

Grammar Discovery

How Much, Roughly?

"It costs in the region of forty thousand dollars."

"Just under half goes to equipment, and very little to materials."

"We need a few more weeks, but hardly any more money."

"The bulk of the budget is sound."

No sentence here gives an exact number. The red words tell the listener how much, and how sure you are.

RuleFormExample
Approximation before a numberroughly / about / around / in the region of + numberroughly five months, around forty thousand
Just above or belowjust under / just over / well over + number or fractionjust under half, well over a year
Things you counta few (some) · few (not enough)a few weeks, few technicians
Almost nonehardly any + either kind of nounhardly any days, hardly any money
Things you do not counta little (some) · little / very little (not enough)a little slack, very little money
The largest partthe bulk of / most of + the + noun; the verb agrees with the nounmost of the budget goes, most of the technicians are
The trapno of after around; no s on thousand after a numberaround forty thousand, not around of forty thousands

In one line: round the number with a word in front of it, and choose few or little by whether you can count the thing.

Chapter 4: Real Conversations

The Producer Has to Say Yes

One call, two recordings, one budget summary

Dialogue

A Ballpark Figure, Then a Breakdown

O
Before we commit, I need a ballpark figure. Roughly what would the installation cost?
L
In the region of forty thousand dollars. The bulk of it is sound, and very little is materials.
O
That is tight for us. Could you break it down?
L
Of course. Just under half goes to equipment, around a quarter to the technicians, and a few thousand to printing. The hospital gives us the furniture in-kind.
O
And the timeline? Our board meets in March.
L
Recording takes roughly five months. The first milestone is the end of the recordings, in May, and hardly any of that can be rushed.
O
What happens if the recordings slip?
L
Then we lose a few weeks, not the opening. I would rather have a little slack now than an overrun later.
Comprehension

About Owen

1. What does Owen ask for before the foundation commits?
A ballpark figure: roughly what the installation would cost.
2. How does Owen react to the figure, and what does he ask for next?
He says it is tight for them, and he asks her to break it down.
3. When does Owen’s board meet, and what risk does he ask about?
In March. He asks what happens if the recordings slip.
Listening 1 · A Producer

Nobody Believes an Exact Number

Somebody who turns artists’ ideas into timelines and budgets, on what he wants in the first meeting. Sound first — no text.

1. What is wrong with the numbers artists usually give him?
They are almost always too precise. Nobody believes a number like thirty-seven thousand four hundred at the start, and it makes the conversation brittle.
2. What does he want in the first meeting, and what example does he give?
A ballpark figure and a breakdown: in the region of forty thousand, just under half on equipment, around a quarter on people, and a little on everything else.
3. What does he want to hear about time, and what makes him worry?
Which milestone is most likely to slip, so he can protect the opening. He starts worrying if somebody tells him nothing will slip.
Listening 2 · A Grants Officer

In-Kind Is Not Nothing

Somebody who reads grant applications for art made in hospitals, on what applicants leave out. Sound first — no text.

1. What do applicants almost always undervalue, and what example does she give?
In-kind support. A hospital lending a room for six months might be worth several thousand dollars, and it should appear in the budget with an estimate.
2. What happens when artists put hardly anything down for their own time?
The project runs over and there is no money left to pay them.
3. What kind of budget does the foundation prefer, and what does she ask applicants to do with their figures?
A realistic budget with a small shortfall they can discuss, rather than a perfect one nobody believes. She asks them to round their figures: roughly, about, just over, just under.

1. Why does the summary give the total as in the region of forty thousand instead of an exact figure?
Because at this stage an exact number would not be believed. A rounded figure with a breakdown is what a producer can work with.
2. Why does the in-kind support appear with an estimate next to it?
Because it is worth real money, about five thousand dollars, and leaving it out undervalues what the hospital gives.
3. Which stage does the summary say is most likely to slip, and why is saying so a good idea?
The recordings. Naming it lets the producer protect the opening, instead of worrying that nobody has thought about risk.
