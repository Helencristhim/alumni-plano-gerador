Lesson 11 -- Pre-class

Talking About Time and Money -- Deadlines and Budgets

A call with a producer about a new installation, and the grammar of saying how much without an exact number. Key words: a timeline, a milestone, an overrun, to allocate, a shortfall, a grant, in-kind, tight, to slip, upfront, a ballpark figure, to break down. Structure: quantifiers and approximation.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A timeline -- a plan that shows when each stage of a project will happen
"The timeline has three stages and one fixed date: the opening."
A milestone -- an important point where one stage of a project is finished
"The first milestone is the end of the recordings, in May."
An overrun -- when a project costs more, or takes longer, than planned
"A small overrun on sound is normal; a large one is a problem."
To allocate -- to decide how much money or time goes to each part
"We allocate a quarter of the budget to the technicians."
A shortfall -- the amount by which the money is less than what is needed
"The grant leaves a shortfall of about six thousand dollars."
A grant -- money an institution gives for a project, which is not paid back
"The foundation grant covers the recordings, not the printing."
In-kind -- given as goods or services instead of money
"The hospital supports the project in-kind, with furniture and space."
Tight -- with very little room, in money or in time
"Forty thousand is a tight budget for eight speakers."
To slip -- (of a deadline) to move later than planned
"If the recordings slip, the opening does not have to."
Upfront -- paid or given before the work starts
"The foundation pays a third upfront and the rest on delivery."
A ballpark figure -- a rough estimate, not an exact number
"I only need a ballpark figure before the board meets."
To break down -- to divide a total into its separate parts
"Could you break down the budget for the board?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The producer asked for a ballpark figure, so Liliane did not give an exact number. The installation would cost in the region of forty thousand dollars. The bulk of it was sound, and very little was materials.

When he said the budget was tight, she broke it down: just under half for equipment, around a quarter for the technicians and a few thousand for printing. The hospital would give the furniture in-kind.

The timeline was roughly five months of recording, with the first milestone in May. Hardly any of that could be rushed, she said, but if the recordings slipped, they would lose a few weeks, not the opening.

1. Why did Liliane say in the region of forty thousand instead of an exact number?
A Because she did not know anything about the budget.
B Because a rough estimate is what a producer expects at the start of a project.
C Because forty thousand is a lucky number.
2. "Very little was materials." Why little and not few?
A Because you cannot count the word materials here: it means the money spent on them.
B Because little is always more formal than few.
C Because the number is small.
3. "Hardly any of that could be rushed." What does hardly any mean?
A All of it.
B Exactly half of it.
C Almost none of it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Quantifiers and Approximation

GRAMMAR

How to be precise without an exact number.

RuleFormExample
Approximation before a numberroughly / about / around / in the region ofroughly five months, around forty thousand
Just above or belowjust under / just over / well overjust under half, well over a year
Things you counta few (some) · few (not enough)a few weeks, few technicians
Almost nonehardly any + either kind of nounhardly any days, hardly any money
Things you do not counta little (some) · little / very little (not enough)a little slack, very little money
The largest partthe bulk of / most of + the + nounmost of the budget goes to sound
Numbersno of after around; no s on thousand after a numberaround forty thousand dollars

Never: a few money · most of the budget go · around of forty · forty thousands dollars.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the sentence you would actually say.

A producer asks what the project will cost. It is still early. You say:
A "Exactly thirty-seven thousand four hundred and twelve dollars."
B "In the region of forty thousand dollars, and I can break it down."
C "Around of forty thousands."
You want to say there is almost no money left for printing. You say:
A "There is hardly any money left for printing."
B "There is a few money left for printing."
C "There are few money left for printing."
The hospital lends you a room for six months. In the budget you write:
A "Nothing, because they did not pay us."
B "Hospital room, in-kind, about five thousand dollars."
C "A grant from the hospital."
A client asks if the date of the trip can move. You say:
A "The date never slips."
B "The date must to be fixed."
C "The hotel is the stage most likely to slip, so we confirm it by the end of March."
