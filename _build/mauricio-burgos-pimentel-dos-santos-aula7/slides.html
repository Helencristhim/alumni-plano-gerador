The Pattern

What happens to the word order?

Direct: Where did you get that number?  →  Could you tell me where you got that number?
Direct: Why is it slower in Europe?  →  I was wondering why it is slower in Europe.
Direct: Does that include tax?  →  Can I ask whether that includes tax?
Direct: What do you mean?  →  Would you mind clarifying what you mean?

After the opener, the question becomes a normal sentence

Put a polite opener in front (Could you tell me … / I was wondering … / Can I ask …) and the rest goes back to subject + verb. No did, no inversion. It is longer, it is more polite, and — the part that matters for you — the opener buys you three seconds to build the rest.

Did You Catch It?

About the exchange

1. What was David’s first question about, and how did he open it?
The timeline — he opened with "Can I raise a question about…"
2. What assumption had Claire’s team made?
That there would be no regulatory delay.
3. What does David add from his own perspective, and what does Claire propose?
That the delay would be the base case in Brazil, not the risk — and she proposes following up at the break.
Listening 1

Why the good questions come from the floor

1. Where does she say the best questions come from?
From the people who are quiet for forty minutes and then ask what nobody else noticed.
2. What does she say about grammar?
Nobody is grading it — they are listening to the question.
3. What is her practical advice before raising your hand?
Write the question down first — the confident-looking people are the ones who prepared the sentence.
Listening 2

Following up at the break

1. What point does he want to follow up on?
Freezing hiring while keeping the growth target.
2. What did the other person actually do?
They split the target by quarter instead of by year.
3. What does he ask for at the end?
Permission to email them about it next week.
