Asking Without Freezing

Stage 1.1: Words for a Discussion

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say each word out loud with your own numbers. Eight words, three minutes.

Clarify — to make something clearer, because it was not clear
"Could you clarify the second point?"
Elaborate — to say more about something you only mentioned
"Would you elaborate on the cost side?"
Perspective — the way one person sees a situation
"From a Brazilian perspective, it looks different."
Assumption — something you believe is true without checking
"What assumption is that number based on?"
Rationale — the reasoning behind a decision
"I did not follow the rationale for that."
Follow up — to come back to something later, to finish it
"Can I follow up on that at the break?"
Make a point — to say something that adds to the argument
"She made a very good point about scale."
Raise a question — to bring a question to the room
"I would like to raise a question about risk."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the text. Then answer the questions.

In the first session I did not raise a question at all. Somebody presented a plan and said it would take eighteen months, and I wanted to ask what assumption that was based on, but by the time I had built the sentence, the room had moved on. On day three I started writing the question down before putting my hand up. It changed everything. Now I open with "I was wondering…" or "Could you tell me…", and that opener gives me three seconds to finish the rest. Yesterday I asked someone to clarify a number, then to elaborate on the rationale behind it, and she said it was a good point. From my perspective the risk was obvious; from hers it was not. We agreed to follow up at the break.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

The opener buys you three seconds

Put a polite opener in front, and the question goes back to subject + verb. No did. No inversion.

Direct

Where did you get it?
Why is it slower?
Does it include tax?

Indirect

Could you tell me where you got it?
I was wondering why it is slower.
Can I ask whether it includes tax?

× "Could you tell me where did you get it?" — the did is already used up by the opener
× "Would you mind to clarify?" — after mind the verb takes -ing: mind clarifying
Yes/no question? Use whether or if: "Can I ask whether that includes tax?"

Two you will use daily: "Sorry, could you say that again?" and "Just to be clear — do you mean…?"

Stage 3: Say it out loud

Speaking

Tap Listen, then Record. Say it SLOWLY. A slow question in careful English lands better than a fast one that says nothing.

Stage 4: In the room

Quiz
Which question is correctly built?
A "Could you tell me where did you find that?"
B "Could you tell me where you found that?"
You did not understand one sentence. What is the best thing to say?
A Nothing — wait and hope it comes back.
B "Sorry, could you say that again?"
Should you apologise for your English before asking?
A No — ask the question. Nobody is grading the grammar.
B Yes — it is polite to warn them first.

Stage 5: Three questions, out loud

Reflection
Think of the last presentation you sat through, in any language. Record ninety seconds in English with three questions you would have asked: one to clarify something, one about an assumption, and one that adds your own perspective. Build each one with a polite opener. Say them slowly, as if forty people were listening.
