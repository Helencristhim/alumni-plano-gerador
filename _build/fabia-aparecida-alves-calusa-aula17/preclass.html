Lesson 17 — Pre-class

Four Percent in January

Four new words for the yearly fee change — adjustment, index, notice, cap. And one thing only: telling a client a plan your firm already decided. Around ten minutes.
0%
▼

Stage 1: Four new words

Vocabulary

You explain all four ideas to clients every year. What is new is only the word and the sound. Read each one and say it out loud.

Adjustment — a change to a price, usually once a year
"Our fee has an adjustment every January."
Index — a number that shows how prices change over time
"The adjustment follows the inflation index."
Notice — a message that tells someone about a change before it happens
"We send our clients notice thirty days before."
Cap — the highest number something can go up to
"The increase has a cap of six percent."

Stage 3: A plan you already decided

Grammar

One structure this week. When your firm already has a plan for the future, use am / is / are + going to + the verb: we are going to adjust the fee in January; the adjustment is going to follow the index. For the negative, put not after is or are: it is not going to go above six percent. Never "we going to" — the are stays.

"In January, we ______ adjust the fee."

B going to
"The adjustment is going to ______ the inflation index."

B follows
Which sentence is correct?
A The increase not going to go above six percent next year.
B The increase is not going to go above six percent.
The client asks about next year. Which answer can she plan her budget with?
A We are going to adjust the fee in January, about four percent.
B The price changes a little every year, I think, it depends on inflation, more or less.

Stage 5: Say it out loud

Speaking

Say "we are going to" as one smooth block, then the plan. That small start is what makes the answer sound decided.

In January, we are going to adjust the fee.
The adjustment is going to follow the inflation index.
It is not going to go above our cap of six percent.

Stage 6: Before class

Speaking
Think of how your firm changes its fees. Record three sentences to a client: when you are going to adjust the fee (we are going to...), what the adjustment is going to follow (it is going to...), and one limit (it is not going to...). One sentence each — no more.
