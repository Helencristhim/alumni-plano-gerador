Then and Now

Eight words for a town that changed (farm, forest, winery, tourist, quiet, famous, campfire, nowadays) and used to for things that were true in the past but are not true now.

Stage 1.1: A Town That Changed

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes. "Nowadays" is the opposite of "in the past".

Farm — land where people grow food or keep cows and sheep
"His grandpa had a farm near the river."
Forest — a big area with a lot of tall trees
"The road goes through a forest."
Winery — a place where people make wine from grapes
"There are a lot of wineries here now."
Tourist — a person who visits a place for fun, on vacation
"In summer the town is full of tourists."
Quiet — with very little noise and not many people
"It used to be a quiet town."
Famous — known by a lot of people
"The wave here is famous."
Campfire — a fire you make outside when you go camping
"We sat around the campfire."
Nowadays — at the present time, not in the past
"Nowadays the town is really busy."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read Renato's message to his parents from the campsite. Then answer the questions.

We're in Margaret River! It rained all the way, but the town is beautiful. Dana told us its story. Fifty years ago it used to be a quiet town. Most people used to work on a farm or in the forest, and there didn't use to be any wineries. Surfers from Perth used to sleep in their cars near the beach. Nowadays it's really busy: there are cafes, wineries and a lot of tourists, and the wave is famous all over the world. Tonight we're sitting around a campfire. Dad, did you use to camp when you were young? I didn't use to like camping, but I love it now!
"It used to be a quiet town." Is the town quiet now?
A No. It was quiet in the past, but it is busy now.
B Yes. It is quiet now, and it was also quiet fifty years ago, in the past.
"There didn't use to be any wineries." Why "use" and not "used"?
A Because the sentence is about wine and grapes, so the verb changes.
B After didn't, the verb goes back to the base form: use.
Which sentence is correct?
A Did you use to camp when you were young?
B Did you used to camp when you were young?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Used to: true then, not true now

Use used to + verb for habits (things you did many times) and states (be, have, like) that are different now.

Three forms

I used to surf every day.
I didn't use to like cold water.
Did you use to camp?

Never

I use to surf there.
She used to lives here.
Did you used to camp?

Then and now: It used to be quiet, but nowadays it's busy.  ·  Short answer: Yes, I did. / No, I didn't.
Your voice: "used to" sounds like one word, YOOS-tuh. You can't hear the d.
