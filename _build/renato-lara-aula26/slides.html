Grammar Discovery

Is it true now?

Margaret River used to be a quiet farm town.
Surfers used to sleep in their cars near the beach.
Dana's family didn't use to have a van.
Did you use to camp with your family?

What do the orange words have in common?

used to + verb = true in the past, not true now

Use it for habits (things you did many times) and states (be, have, like) that changed. Negative and question use did, so the d goes away: didn't use to, Did you use to...?

The Code

Then, not now

Three forms

+ I used to surf every day.
− I didn't use to like cold water.
? Did you use to camp?
Answer: Yes, I did. / No, I didn't.

Never

I use to surf there.
past, with d: I used to surf
She used to lives in Perth.
used to + base verb: live
Did you used to camp?
did takes the d: Did you use to

Then: used to · Now: nowadays + present simple — the perfect pair.

Sound Check

One word: YOOS-tuh

It used to be a quiet town.

"used to" sounds like YOOS-tuh: an s, not a z

I didn't use to like cold water.

"use to" sounds the same: you can't hear the d

Did you use to camp with your family?

"Did you" is one block: DID-ja

Listening 1

Dana at the campfire

1. How did Dana's family use to travel to Margaret River?
In a tiny old car, with the boards on top and the tent in the back.
2. Where did they use to sleep, and what did they use to eat?
In a tent in the forest, and they cooked fish on a campfire.
3. What is different nowadays, according to Dana?
There are cafes, wineries and a lot of tourists, and the best surfers in the world come for a big contest.
Listening 2

Kai, before he was captain

1. What did Kai use to be scared of?
Big waves. He used to sit on the sand and watch.
2. Who did he use to surf with, and what board did he use to ride?
With his older brother, on an old longboard.
3. What does Kai do before a heat nowadays?
He doesn't eat a lot, he stretches and he listens to music.
