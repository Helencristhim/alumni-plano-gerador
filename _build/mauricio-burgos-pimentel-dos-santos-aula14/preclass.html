Stage 1.1: Words for Group Work

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say each word out loud. Eight words, three minutes.

Divide up — to separate a job into parts for different people
"Let’s divide up the work before dinner."
Take turns — to do something one person after the other
"We take turns, so everybody presents once."
Draft — a first version, not the final one
"Ana will send the draft of the slides tonight."
Rehearse — to practice a presentation before the real one
"We rehearse at seven, before the session."
Volunteer — to offer to do something without being asked
"I volunteered to present the numbers."
On the same page — to agree, and understand the same thing
"Before we present, we must be on the same page."
Wrap up — to finish, with a short summary
"Let me wrap up in thirty seconds."
Contribute — to give your part, your ideas or your work
"In a good group, everybody contributes."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the text. Then answer the questions.

Our study group has to prepare a new case every evening. On Monday night we divided up the work. Kenji had to check the numbers, and Ana wrote the draft of the slides. I volunteered to present the figures, because I know banking. Claire said, "You don’t have to present on the first day," but I wanted to contribute early. Before we left, we had to agree on the recommendation — we must be on the same page before we present. On Tuesday we rehearsed at seven, and Claire wrapped up in thirty seconds. Next time we will take turns, and I will present the problem.
What did he volunteer to do?
A Present the figures.
B Write the draft of the slides and send it to the group before midnight.
Claire said, "You don’t have to present on the first day." What does she mean?
A It is not necessary — he can choose.
B It is forbidden, and the professor will not let him present on Monday.
Why is it "Kenji had to check" and not "Kenji must check"?
A Because must is only used with I and we, never with a person’s name.
B It is in the past.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Rules and choices

Have to / must = no choice. Don’t have to = not necessary, you can choose. Had to = no choice, in the past.

have / has to

We have to prepare one case.
Kenji has to check the figures.

must

We must agree before we leave.
Everybody must say something.

don’t have to

You don’t have to be perfect.
She doesn’t have to present today.

had to — past

We had to rehearse at seven.
Did you have to present?

× "Kenji have to check" — he / she: Kenji has to check
× "We must to agree" — bare verb: we must agree

Don’t have to never means "it is forbidden". It means "it is your choice".

Stage 4: In the room

Quiz
Which sentence is correct?
A "Ana have to send the draft of the slides before eleven tonight."
B "Ana has to send the draft before eleven."
A colleague is afraid of making mistakes in the presentation. What do you say?
A "You don’t have to be perfect."
B "You mustn’t make any mistakes, because the professor is watching all of us."
It is your second day and you have said nothing in the group. What is the best thing to do?
A Wait until Friday, because the others are better at presenting than you.
B Volunteer for one part today.

Stage 5: Lead the meeting

Reflection
Record one minute in English. Imagine your study group has a real case from your bank for tomorrow morning. Open the meeting and divide up the work: say what each person has to do, one thing you don’t have to do tonight, and what you must agree on. Volunteer for one part yourself, and wrap up with the time to rehearse.
