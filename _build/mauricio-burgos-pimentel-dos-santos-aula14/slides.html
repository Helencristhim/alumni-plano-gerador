Grammar Discovery

Is there a choice?

The group has to prepare one case every evening.
Everybody must say something in every meeting.
You don’t have to present on the first day.
We had to rehearse at seven in the morning.

Have to / must = no choice. Don’t have to = it is not necessary — you can choose.

Have to is the everyday word for rules and obligations. Must is stronger, for rules you feel are important. For the past, use had to — there is no past of must. Don’t have to does NOT mean "it is forbidden".

The Pattern

Rules, choices, and the past

He / she has to — never "he have to". Must + bare verb — never "must to".

Practice

Have to, must, or don’t have to?

(rule) Kenji ___ check the figures before nine.
Kenji has to check the figures before nine.
(important, I feel it) We ___ agree on the recommendation tonight.
We must agree on the recommendation tonight.
(not necessary) We ___ finish the slides tonight.
We don’t have to finish the slides tonight.
(past) Yesterday we ___ rehearse at seven in the morning.
Yesterday we had to rehearse at seven in the morning.

The quiet one

1. Why did he stay quiet for two days?
He thought he didn’t have to speak, because the others were better.
2. What did the woman from Singapore tell him?
That he had to tell them what he thought, because he was the only banker.
3. What is his advice?
You don’t have to speak a lot, but you must speak early.
