Chapter 5: Real Speed

Before You Testify

A summary, a chair and a veteran

Real Document

The Executive Summary

TEN YEARS OF PORT CONCESSIONS — EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
report to the infrastructure committee · example prepared for the lesson
1Private investment in the ports rose by 85% in the ten years after the adoption of the concession model.
2Tariffs fell by 12% in real terms over the same period (source: agency data).
3Three of the twenty contracts were renegotiated; two of them after delays in the right of way.
4Recommendation: share the demand risk in the first five years, and publish every renegotiation.
Full report: 214 pages. Opening statement: 3 minutes.
1. What happened to private investment, and after what?
It rose by eighty-five percent in the ten years after the adoption of the concession model.
2. Where does the tariff number come from?
From the agency's data.
3. Say the recommendation in plain English.
In the first five years, the state and the operator should share the demand risk, and every renegotiation has to be public.
Listening 1 · The Chair

The Rules of the Session

The chair of the committee opens the session. Sound first — no text.

1. How long is each opening statement, and what happens if a witness goes over?
Three minutes. If they go over, the chair will stop them, politely but firmly.
2. How long does each member of the committee have for questions?
Five minutes each.
3. What does she ask witnesses to do with every number?
To say where it comes from.
Listening 2 · The Veteran

Forty Times in the Chair

An economist who has testified forty times. Sound first — no text.

1. What is his first rule about numbers?
Never guess a number. If you do not know it, say you will send it in writing.
2. What does he do when he says something wrong?
He corrects it immediately, in one sentence, and continues. It makes him more credible, not less.
3. How does he answer a loaded question?
He does not say yes or no. He names the assumption and answers the real issue.
