Chapter 3: The Reception

A Reception in Atlanta

Thursday evening, nobody has an agenda, and everybody is working

Dialogue

Nobody Here Is Talking About Work

D
You must be Patricia. Dan Brooks, I run the Marietta plant. How was the flight?
P
Long. Ten hours, and I still do not know what time my body thinks it is. Have you ever been down to São Paulo?
D
Twice, years ago. Enormous city. My wife still talks about a churrascaria we went to and I still cannot pronounce the name of it.
P
Then you have eaten better than most people here. Can I ask you something? Do you know whether the Marietta team is on the monthly people call?
D
Two of them are. I am not, which is probably a mistake. You are the one who wrote the note about the first ninety days, aren't you?
P
I am. I was wondering if you would have twenty minutes while I am here. Marietta is the site I understand least.
D
Sure. Swing by tomorrow after ten, I am in the building all day. Fair warning, I will tell you that most of it is the shift pattern.
P
Then we already agree on something. Thanks for the heads-up -- I will see you tomorrow.
Comprehension

About Dan

1. How does Dan open the conversation, and what does he tell her about himself?
He identifies her by name, gives his own name, says he runs the Marietta plant, and asks about the flight.
2. What does Dan say about Brazil, and about the monthly people call?
He has been to São Paulo twice, years ago, and his wife still talks about a churrascaria whose name he cannot pronounce. Two of his team are on the people call and he is not, which he says is probably a mistake.
3. What does Dan offer, and what does he warn her about?
He offers twenty minutes if she swings by after ten the next day, and warns her that he will tell her most of it is the shift pattern.

Chapter 4: The Code

Asking Without Interrogating

The shapes that let you ask a stranger anything

Grammar Discovery

Do You Know Whether...

"Do you know whether the Marietta team is on the monthly call?"

"Could you tell me how long you have been with the company?"

"You have been to São Paulo before, haven't you?"

"I was wondering if you would have twenty minutes tomorrow."

Three of these are questions inside another question. Look at the word order INSIDE. What has happened to it?

FormWhat it doesExample
Do you know if / whether + statement ordersoftens a direct question, and the word order inside stays a STATEMENTDo you know whether the Texas team joins the call?
Could you tell me + wh- + statement orderthe most useful shape for asking a stranger anythingCould you tell me how long you have been here?
I was wondering if ...the softest way to ask for somebody time or a favourI was wondering if you would have twenty minutes.
question taginvites agreement and hands the turn over -- the engine of small talkYou have been to Brazil before, haven't you?
the trapkeeping question order INSIDE: do you know where is the room? The inversion belongs to the outer question only. Say where the room is.

The tag at the end is not decoration. It hands the turn to the other person, which is what keeps a conversation with a stranger alive past the second exchange.

Chapter 5: The Ear

What Small Talk Is Actually For

An American on the point of it, and a Brazilian on what he got wrong

Listening 1

It Is Not Small and It Is Not Talk

An American manager explains the four minutes before a meeting. Sound first.

0:000:00
1. What does she say those four minutes are actually for?
Finding out how to talk to you -- how direct she can be, whether you want to be asked about your family, whether you answer questions with a joke, and roughly how senior you feel.
2. What does she think when somebody skips small talk?
Not that they are efficient, but that she has no idea who she is talking to, so she becomes more careful rather than less.
3. What does she say about the answer to how was your weekend?
Nobody expects it to be interesting. It is a handshake with words, and the only wrong answer is silence.
Listening 2

What I Got Wrong in My First Year

A Brazilian director who moved to a US company. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What was his first mistake, and what did people actually want?
He answered questions completely -- four minutes when they wanted twenty seconds and a door to walk through.
2. What did he wait for, and what does he say you should do instead?
He waited to be invited, as somebody senior would do in Brazil. He says that if you want twenty minutes with the plant director, you ask the plant director yourself, and nobody finds it strange.
3. What does he say we should grab lunch sometime means, and what do you do about it?
It is not a plan, it is a friendly noise. If you want the lunch, you name a day -- they will say yes or offer another one.

Guided

Turn It Into Twenty Minutes

Support:

Task: Dinner in Atlanta, six people, no agenda, and you are the only person who is not American. Talk for three minutes about anything except HR. Your teacher will ask you the questions Americans actually ask, including the ones nobody prepares for.

No support. Target: three questions asked, not just answered, and one moment where you name a day for something.
