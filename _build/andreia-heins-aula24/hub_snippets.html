At an International Congress -- Networking

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Networking -- meeting people at events to build professional relationships
"I go to congresses for the talks and for networking."
Keynote -- the main talk of a congress, often by a famous speaker
"The keynote was about the future of robotic surgery."
Booth -- a small stand where a company shows its products at an event
"I tried the new console at the supplier's booth."
Name badge -- a small card you wear with your name and company
"I read her name badge before I said hello."
Icebreaker -- a question or comment that starts a conversation with someone new
"'How did you like the keynote?' is a good icebreaker."
Mingle -- to move around and talk to different people at an event
"At the reception, we mingled for two hours."
Small talk -- light, friendly conversation about simple topics
"Small talk about the city is a safe start."
Exchange contacts -- to give each other your phone number, email or social media
"Let's exchange contacts before the next session."
Keep in touch -- to continue to talk or write to someone after you meet
"We keep in touch by email after every congress."
Reach out -- to contact someone, often for the first time
"Feel free to reach out if you have questions."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

It is the first coffee break of the congress in Orlando. Andreia reads the name badge of the nurse next to her: Chicago. She uses an icebreaker: "Excuse me, do you know if the keynote starts at nine thirty?"

They start some small talk. Then Andreia asks: "Could you tell me which hospital you work at?" and "I was wondering if you work with robots too." Notice the order: which hospital you work at, not which hospital do you work at. These polite questions have no do, does or did, and yes / no questions use if.

After ten minutes, they exchange contacts with a QR code. Andreia promises to reach out next week, so they can keep in touch.

1. How does Andreia start the conversation?
A With a polite question about the keynote.
B By giving her business card first.
C She waits for the other nurse to speak.
2. Which polite question is correct?
A "Could you tell me which hospital do you work at?"
B "Could you tell me which hospital work you at?"
C "Could you tell me which hospital you work at?"
3. What will Andreia do next week?
A Go to another congress.
B Reach out to her new contact.
C Visit a booth.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Indirect Questions

GRAMMAR

Polite question = normal sentence order. No do, does or did. Yes / no questions use if.

DirectIndirect (polite)
Where is the booth?Do you know where the booth is?
What time does it start?Could you tell me what time it starts?
Which hospital do you work at?Could you tell me which hospital you work at?
Do you work with robots?I was wondering if you work with robots.
Has the keynote started?Do you know if the keynote has started?

Never: Do you know where is the booth? · Could you tell me what time does it start? · I was wondering you work here.
