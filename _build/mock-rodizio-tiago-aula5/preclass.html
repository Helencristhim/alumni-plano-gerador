Lesson 05 — Before class

The Meeting That Ends On Time

Warm up your mouth and bring your own agenda. In class you will run ten minutes of a meeting with someone who goes off-topic — and you will have to stop her.
0%

Before class 1: The lines of whoever is running it

Vocabulary

Eight lines said by the person who runs the meeting — which, next Monday, is you. Tap Listen once for each.

We have ten minutes — sets the size of the meeting before it starts
"We have ten minutes — let's take the first item."
Can I stop you there? — interrupts without a long apology
Five words. In Portuguese we would use twenty, and lose the turn.
Let's park that for now — moves a real subject out of this meeting without killing it
"Let's park that for now — I'll put it on Thursday's list."
That's a separate conversation — names the off-topic without saying no to the person
Firmer than parking it. Use it when the subject keeps coming back.
Who owns this? — gives the task a name, not a department
"Somebody should call maintenance" is not an action item.
Let's put a date on that — turns an intention into something you can check later
"Soon" is not a date. "Friday" is.
To recap — makes sure everyone leaves with the same version
"To recap: three things."
We're at time — ends the meeting on the minute you promised
"We're at time — let's stop there." It is why people come back.

Before class 2: Until they are automatic

Speaking

Interrupting is the hard one, and under pressure you only reach for what your mouth already knows. Record each line until it comes out without thinking — especially the short ones.

We have ten minutes, so let's take the first item.
Can I stop you there?
Let's park that for now.
Who owns this, and by when?
To recap: three things, and I'll send the notes today.

Before class 3: Bring your own agenda

Reflection

The meeting in class is ours. The one after it is yours — come with it ready.

Think of a real meeting you have this month. Record 60 seconds: the three items you would put on it, in the order you would take them, and what each one has to end with. If you cannot name three, one is enough — but say why the meeting exists.
Now the person. Record 40 seconds about someone who talks too long in your meetings: what they talk about, and what you usually do about it. You will meet a version of that person in class.
