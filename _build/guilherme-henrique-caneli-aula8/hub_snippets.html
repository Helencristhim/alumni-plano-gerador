Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

Full Circle -- Commitments With a Date

The last five minutes of the summit decide whether anything agreed on stage will ever happen. Key words: A benchmark, A milestone, A retrospective, To take stock, To recap, A call to action, An action item, To follow through on, A roadmap, A scorecard, To circle back, A debrief, A closing address, A through line. Structure: future perfect and future continuous for commitments in a closing address.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A benchmark -- a standard or point of reference used to measure progress
"Last year’s summit is our benchmark for this one."
A milestone -- an important stage in the progress of a project
"The first auction under the new model is the next milestone."
A retrospective -- a review that looks back at what worked and what did not
"We ran a retrospective after every roundtable."
To take stock -- to stop and assess a situation before deciding what to do next
"Before the next cycle, let us take stock of what the funds actually signed."
To recap -- to repeat the main points of what has been said
"Let me recap the three decisions before we close."
A call to action -- a clear request that tells the audience what to do next
"The closing address ended with a call to action for every fund."
An action item -- a specific task that someone agrees to do after a meeting
"Each action item has an owner and a date."
To follow through on -- to complete something you promised to do
"The ministry followed through on its promise to publish the draft."
A roadmap -- a plan that shows the steps toward a goal and their order
"The roadmap has three dates, and the first is in March."
A scorecard -- a simple record that measures performance against goals
"The scorecard shows who delivered and who did not."
To circle back -- to return to a topic later
"We will circle back to arbitration in June."
A debrief -- a meeting after an event to discuss how it went
"The team’s debrief lasted longer than the panel."
A closing address -- the final speech of an event
"His closing address did not start with thank you."
A through line -- the idea that connects all the parts of a talk, an event or a program
"The through line of the summit was trust, not money."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

An agreement made on stage is a hope. It becomes a commitment when it has three things: a name, a date and a public way to check it. The language of a good closing address is built to add those three things, and English has two future forms that do most of the work.

The first is the future perfect: will have + past participle. It describes something that will be finished before a moment in the future: “By the end of March, the ministry will have published its draft.” Put a name and a date in the sentence and it becomes an action item. Notice the time word: by March, not until March, and by the time we meet, not by the time we will meet.

The second is the future continuous: will be + -ing. It places the listener inside a moment in the future: “This time next year, we will be discussing projects, not frameworks.” A host who says this is not predicting. He is creating a meeting that everyone in the room now has a reason to attend.

Together with a one-sentence recap of the through line and a single call to action, these two forms turn applause into a roadmap. And the public scorecard at the next roundtable will show who decided to follow through on what they said.

1. According to the text, when does an agreement become a commitment?
A When everybody applauds.
B When it has a name, a date and a public way to check it.
C When it is written in the press release.
2. What does “By March, the ministry will have published its draft” describe?

3. Which sentence does the text say is wrong?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Future Perfect and Future Continuous

GRAMMAR

Will have done closes a promise before a date; will be doing puts the room inside the future.

JobFormExample
Finished before a future momentwill have + past participleBy March, the ministry will have published the draft.
In progress at a future momentwill be + -ingNext year, we will be discussing projects.
Deadlineby + dateBy 31 March, the draft will be out.
By the timeby the time + present simpleBy the time we meet, three funds will have signed.
QuestionWill + subject + have + participle?Will the agency have published by then?
Softer expectationshould have + participle + byThe funds should have decided by June.

Never: by the time we will meet · until March the ministry will have published · next year we will discussing · let me do a resume of the summit

Stage 2: Put the Closing Address in Order

Order

Put the moves of a strong closing address in a logical order.

?"If there is one idea to take home from these two days, it is that the region lacks trusted contracts, not capital."
?"So let me turn our agreements into commitments."
?"By the end of March, the ministry will have published its draft concession model."
?"This time next year, we will be discussing projects under construction, not frameworks on paper."
?"My call to action is simple: follow through on what you said on this stage."
?"And now, with real gratitude, thank you to our sponsors and to all of you."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to end a summit well. You start with:
A "Thank you to our twenty sponsors, one by one."
B "The one idea that connected these two days."
C "That is all for today."
You want to turn an agreement into a commitment. You say:
A "By the end of March, the ministry will have published its draft."
B "Until March the ministry publishes the draft maybe."
C "The ministry will publish sometime."
You want to put the room in the future. You say:
A "Next year we discuss projects."
B "Next year we will discussing projects."
C "This time next year, we will be discussing projects, not frameworks."
A topic was not resolved. You say:
A "We will circle back to arbitration in June."
B "We will do the arbitration again in June."
C "We will turn around to arbitration in June."
