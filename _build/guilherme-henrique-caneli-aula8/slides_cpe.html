You cannot set your own benchmark. A benchmark is external by definition: it is somebody else’s performance, and that is the whole reason it is worth measuring against. The Text

Paragraphs One and Two

The Year Between Two Conferences

The interesting part of an annual event is not the three days. It is the three hundred and sixty-two that follow, and almost nobody measures them.1

A closing address is where that year is supposed to be set in motion, and it is usually where it quietly is not. A speaker who lists the panels has produced a receipt. One who names a through line — the argument that ran underneath the whole programme — has at least told the room what it had been arguing about.2

The Text

Paragraphs Three and Four

The Year Between Two Conferences

What changes Monday is a date. “We will continue the conversation” is a call to action in roughly the sense that a shrug is an answer. By this time next year we will have published the framework, and between now and March the working group will be meeting monthly: those are sentences somebody can be held to.3

The apparatus is dull. An action item has an owner and a date and is otherwise a wish. A roadmap puts the items in sequence so that a room can see which one is blocking the rest. A scorecard is what you open at the next event.4 A debrief within the week, while people still remember what was said rather than what they wish had been said, costs an hour and saves the year.

The Text

Paragraphs Five and Six

The Year Between Two Conferences

Two words get confused, and the confusion is expensive. A benchmark is a standard you are measured against, usually somebody else’s performance. A milestone is a point on your own route.5 A room that has not grasped the difference will applaud a chart showing every milestone met by an organisation falling further behind the benchmark each quarter.

The honest instrument is the least popular. A retrospective that says what did not work is read by almost nobody and changes almost everything, because it is the only document in the cycle that is not selling something. Why this matters today
The writer ends on what the room EXPOSES, not on what it decides. That is the bridge into the grammar: an inversion does the same job in one sentence -- it puts the thing you want noticed where it cannot be skipped.

Why the answer is (b)
The continuous reassures: it says work is under way across a period. That is its job, and it is the right form when you are telling a room that something continues. It is the wrong form for a commitment, because nothing in it lands on a day anybody can hold you to. A closing address needs both, and needs to know which is which.

Collaborative Task

Fourteen Down to Three

Four minutes, together

Collaborative task — four minutes. You have the last ten minutes of a forum and fourteen suggested action items on a flipchart. Together, cut them to three, decide who owns each one, and agree the date you will circle back. You have to say all three out loud, as complete sentences, before the four minutes are up.

Long Turn

Two Minutes, Uninterrupted

No notes. No apology at the start.

Long turn — two minutes, uninterrupted. You are closing a three-day forum. Answer this: “What will be different a year from now, and how will we know?” Name the through line, then give exactly three commitments. Every one of them has to carry a month, and at least one has to be a future continuous.

Follow-up

Four Questions, No Restarting

Four examiner questions, straight after the two minutes. No restarting, and no preparation between them.
1Name the owner of your first commitment. Out loud, now.
2Say your second commitment again using the other future form, and tell me what changed.
3Somebody says three is too few and the room expected fourteen. Concede, then hold.
4One sentence: what will your retrospective admit next year?
Debate One

Defend the Side You Disagree With

This house believes that a closing address should contain no thanks at all, only commitments.
1Ninety seconds each, alternating. No notes.
2You choose your side, and you open with the strongest argument against yourself.
3Every commitment you mention has to carry a tense that lands on a date.
Debate Two

The Side You Were Given

This house believes that an organisation should publish its retrospective in full, including what failed.
1The teacher gives you the side. You do not choose it.
2Before you answer, restate the strongest point the other side has made, in your own words, so that they accept your version of it.
3Only then do you respond. A turn that skips the concession does not count.
