Chapter 4: The Code

Putting the Room in the Future

One form closes a promise before a date; the other puts the room inside it

Grammar Discovery

Finished by Then, or Happening Then?

"By the time we meet in March, the ministry will have published its draft."

"Next year, we will be discussing projects, not frameworks."

"By 2030, the region will have attracted the capital it needs, or it will not."

"This time next week, the funds will be reading our consensus statement."

Two sentences describe something finished before a moment in the future. Two describe something in progress at a moment in the future. Which are which, and why would a closing address need both?

JobFormExample
Finished before a future momentwill have + past participleBy March, the ministry will have published the draft.
In progress at a future momentwill be + -ingThis time next year, we will be discussing projects.
The deadline markerby + date; by the time + present simpleBy the time we meet again, three funds will have signed.
A softer expectationshould have + past participle + byThe funds should have decided by June.
Questions and negativesWill ... have + participle? / will not haveWill the agency have published by then?
Make it a commitmentname + will have + dateThe ministry will have published it by 31 March.
The trapnever by the time we will meet; by is not untilNot until March the ministry will have. Say by March.

In one line: Will have done closes a promise before a date; will be doing puts the room inside the future.

Listening 1 · An Executive Coach

Never End With That Is All

A coach on the last three minutes of any event. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the coach say most speakers do at the end?
They relax, say that is all or thank twenty people by name, and the energy of the room drains away.
2. What four steps does she recommend, in order, before saying thank you?
A one-sentence recap of the connecting idea, commitments with who and by when, a picture of the future, and one call to action.
3. Why does she say the ending matters so much?
Because the last thing the room hears is the first thing it remembers.
Listening 2 · The Closing Address

By the Time We Meet Again

A host closes a two-day infrastructure summit. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the host say the region lacks, and what does it not lack?
It does not lack capital; it lacks contracts that people trust.
2. What will the ministry and the development bank have done, and by when?
By the end of March, the ministry will have published its draft concession model; by June, the development bank will have shared a term sheet for a first-loss guarantee.
3. What does he hope they will be discussing next year, and what is his call to action?
Projects under construction, not frameworks on paper. His call to action is to follow through on what was said on stage.
