Lesson 09 — Pre-class

Twelve Point Five

Eight words from the management report — revenue, margin, forecast, variance, breakdown, benchmark, quarter, overhead — and the comparison that turns a figure into analysis. Around fifteen minutes.

Stage 1.1: Words from the report

Vocabulary

You know all eight concepts better than most native speakers. What is new is only the word and the sound. Tap Listen and say each one out loud.

Revenue — all the money a business takes in, before costs
"Revenue was up nine percent on last quarter."
Margin — what is left after you take the costs away
"The margin is thinner than last year."
Forecast — what you expect the numbers to be, before they happen
"We are ahead of forecast for the third month running."
Variance — the difference between the plan and what really happened
"There is a variance of four percent on payroll."
Breakdown — the detail behind a total, line by line
"I have sent you the breakdown by department."
Benchmark — the standard number you compare yourself against
"Twelve days is the benchmark in our sector."
Quarter — a three-month block of the financial year
"The third quarter closed better than the second."
Overhead — the cost of running the business, whatever you sell
"Overhead is the highest it has been since 2023."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Three minutes of a monthly review call. Read it once, then answer.

Let me take the four lines in order.

Revenue closed at one point four eight million, which is nine percent higher than the second quarter, and about eighty thousand ahead of forecast.

The margin is where it gets interesting: twenty-two percent, three points lower than a year ago — and that is on higher revenue, which is unusual.

The reason is the third line. Overhead is up twelve and a half percent, the highest it has been since twenty twenty-three, and two thirds of that increase is the new warehouse alone.

Against the sector benchmark we are still slightly better than average, but not by much. So the line to watch next quarter is the warehouse, not the margin.
Why does she call the margin line "interesting" rather than "bad"?
A Because twenty-two percent is a good margin in any sector.
B Because it fell while revenue rose, and that combination points at a specific line.
C Because the client had already seen the figure before the call.
Every figure in this report arrives with something next to it. What is it, and why?
A A comparison — without one, a number tells the client nothing about whether it is good or bad.
B A percentage, because percentages sound more professional than absolute figures.
C A date, so that the client can find the figure in the system afterwards.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Two things, or all of them.

Two things → comparative. Short word, add -er: higher, lower, thinner, stronger. Long word, use more: more profitable, more consistent. Then than: "revenue is higher than last quarter".

All of them → superlative. the highest, the lowest, the strongest · the most profitable. Almost always followed by a limit: "the highest since 2023", "the strongest quarter of the year".

Never both. "more higher" does not exist. To intensify, use much or slightly: "much higher", "slightly better than average".

And the one that is not grammar: the decimal separator. On the page it is 12.5; out loud it is "twelve point five". Never "comma".

Stage 3: Say it out loud

Speaking

Say the whole figure, slowly, before the comparison. The number is the part that trips people, not the grammar.

Revenue closed at one point four eight million.
That is nine percent higher than last quarter.
Overhead is the highest it has been since twenty twenty-three.

Stage 4: On the review call

Quiz
The client asks whether 22% is a good margin. What do you answer?
A "It is twenty-two percent." The figure speaks for itself.
B "It is three points lower than a year ago, and slightly better than the sector benchmark."
C "I would need to check the breakdown before I can say anything."
You have five minutes and twenty lines of figures. What do you present?
A All twenty, quickly, so nothing is left out.
B Four lines, each with a comparison, and the one line to watch.
C Only the good lines — the rest is in the file if they want it.
Reading 12.5% out loud on a call with an American client:
A "twelve point five percent"
B "twelve comma five percent"
C "twelve and five percent"

Stage 5: Present four lines

Reflection
Take a real report of one of your own clients. Cover the analysis and present four lines out loud, in English — each figure with a comparison next to it — and finish by naming the one line you would tell them to watch. Three minutes maximum, one take.
