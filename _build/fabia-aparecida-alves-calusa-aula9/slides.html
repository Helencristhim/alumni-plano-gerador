The Pattern

A number needs a neighbour

"Revenue is higher than last quarter."
"The margin is thinner than it was in March."
"This was the strongest quarter of the year."
"Overhead is the highest it has been since 2023."

Two things: -er / more ... than. Everything: the -est / the most

Short word: higher, thinner, lower. Long word: more profitable, more consistent. And a figure on its own says nothing — "twenty-two percent" is data; "thinner than last year" is the analysis the client is paying you for.

Did You Catch It?

Four lines, one conclusion

1. What is the revenue figure, and how does it compare?
1.48 million — nine percent higher than Q2, and about eighty thousand ahead of forecast.
2. Why does David say he could explain lower revenue but not a thinner margin?
Because the margin fell while revenue rose — that combination has no easy explanation for a board.
3. Which line explains the margin, and by how much did it move?
Overhead, up twelve and a half percent — the highest since 2023.
4. What does he ask for at the end, and why that?
The warehouse line against the sector benchmark — because the problem is that line, not the margin.
Listening 1

All the numbers, none of the meaning

1. How many figures does he say, and how many comparisons?
Five figures and no comparison at all.
2. What does the listener still not know at the end?
Whether any of it is good or bad — there is nothing to compare the numbers with.
3. Which single sentence would have saved that whole report?
Any comparison: "which is higher than last quarter", "ahead of forecast", "the highest since 2023".
Listening 2

Same page, told properly

1. How many comparisons can you count?
Six — against last quarter, against forecast, against a year ago, against 2023, against the benchmark, and warehouse against the rest.
2. Which line does she flag as the one to watch, and how?
Overhead — "the highest it has been since 2023", and she names the warehouse as two thirds of it.
3. The figures are identical to the first recording. What changed?
Every figure now has a neighbour to compare it with — that is what turns data into analysis.

Four broken sentences

"The margin is twenty-two comma five percent."
"twenty-two point five percent" — English reads the decimal as a point.
"Revenue is higher of last quarter."
"higher than last quarter"
"Overhead is more higher than the forecast."
"much higher than the forecast" — never "more" with an -er word.
"It was the most strong quarter of the year."
