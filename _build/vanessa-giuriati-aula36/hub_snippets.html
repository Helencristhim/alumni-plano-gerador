Lesson 36 -- Pre-class

A Place You Can See

Describe places so people can picture them: relative clauses with where, which and whose (a coffee shop where the owner knows your name / a house whose windows are always open). Key words: vibrant, spacious, laid-back, fast-paced, well-connected, underrated, off the beaten path, cozy.

Vocabulary

Vibrant -- Full of energy, color and life

Spacious -- With a lot of space inside
"The living room is bright and spacious."
Laid-back -- Relaxed and calm, not stressful
"Life in a small town is more laid-back."
Fast-paced -- Where things happen quickly and people are always busy
"New York is a fast-paced city. Everybody is in a hurry."
Well-connected -- Easy to reach by bus, train or metro
"The hotel is well-connected. The metro is two minutes away."
Underrated -- Better or more interesting than most people think
"This beach is really underrated. Nobody talks about it."
Off the beaten path -- Far from the places where tourists usually go
"We found a little restaurant off the beaten path."
Cozy -- Small, warm and comfortable
"It's a cozy coffee shop with old sofas and good coffee."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Last month I spent a week in Lisbon, which was my first time in Portugal. My hotel was in a well-connected area where the metro was two minutes away, but the city center was too fast-paced for me. On the third day, I found a small neighborhood off the beaten path, where old men played cards in the square. It was much more laid-back. I had lunch in a cozy restaurant whose owner sang while he cooked. The dining room wasn't spacious (there were only six tables), but it was vibrant and full of laughter. Everybody told me to visit the famous tower, which was nice, but for me that little neighborhood was the most underrated part of the trip.

1. Why didn't the writer like the city center very much?
A Because it was far from her hotel and the metro station was closed all week.
B Because it was too fast-paced for her.
C Because it was too quiet.
2. What was special about the restaurant?
A The owner sang while he cooked.
B It had a very big dining room with a view of the famous tower.
C It was in the city center.
3. For the writer, what was the most underrated part of the trip?
A The famous tower.
B The hotel near the metro, because it was cheap and very comfortable.
C The little neighborhood off the beaten path.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Relative clauses for places

GRAMMAR

Use where, which and whose to add a detail to a place without starting a new sentence.

WordUseExample
whereA place + what happens thereIt's a coffee shop where the owner knows your name.
which / thatA thing: a street, a building, a cityI live on a street which goes down to the river.
whose + nounPossession (its / their)It's a house whose windows are always open.
, which ...,Extra information, with commasSalvador, which I visited last year, is vibrant.

Don't repeat the place: 'the coffee shop where I work', not 'the coffee shop where I work there'. Use 'which' (not 'who') for places and things.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

You want to describe the office where you work. Best choice:
A "This is the building where I work there, on the fifth floor near the window."
B "This is the building where I work."
C "This is the building who I work."
A hotel has rooms with an ocean view. Best choice:
A "It's a hotel who's rooms have a beautiful view of the ocean and the beach."
B "It's a hotel which rooms see the ocean."
C "It's a hotel whose rooms face the ocean."
A friend asks about a calm, relaxed beach town. Best choice:
A "It's very laid-back."
B "It's very fast-paced, so you can rest all day on the beach."
C "It's very laid-backed."
A museum is much better than people think. Best choice:
A "It's overrated."
B "It's really underrated."
C "It's really well-connected, so nobody goes there on the weekend."
