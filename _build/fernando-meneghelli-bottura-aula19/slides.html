Chapter 3: The Code

What They Said, One Step Back

will → would · can → could · may → might · must → had to

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

“Your chief people officer told me the role would include IT.”

“The headhunter said you might want the new CFO to lead the integration.”

“She told me she couldn’t give me a date.”

“Your CFO said the role had to report directly to you.”

“The panel asked whether I could give an example from outside advertising.”

What did each person actually say? What happened to will, may, can and must?

The modalWhat was saidWhat you report
will → would“The role will include IT.”She told me the role would include IT.
can → could“I can’t give you a date.”She said she couldn’t give me a date.
may → might“They may want the CFO to lead it.”He said you might want the CFO to lead it.
must → had to“The role must report to the CEO.”He said the role had to report to you.
Questions: asked + if / whether“Can you give an example?”They asked whether I could give an example.
In an interviewwould, could, might and should do not change. If it is still true today, you can keep the present: she said the board will decide by June. Use told me, never said me. In a question, the order is normal: whether I could, not whether could I.

The Move

Report, Source, Check

1. What you heard

“In the first round, your chief people officer told me the role would include IT.”

2. Who said it

“I heard about the integration secondhand, from the headhunter.”

3. The check

“Is that still the case, or has it changed since then?”

Secondhand information is fine. Taking it into the room without checking it is not.

What Did You Take Away?

R
Thanks for coming back, Fernando. Before we start, what did you take away from the first round?
F
Your chief people officer told me the role would include IT, and that the finance team had grown fast. I took that on board.
R
That is right. Did anybody mention the integration?
F
Only secondhand. The headhunter said you might want the new CFO to lead it, but she told me she couldn't give me a date.
R

F

R

F
I appreciate that. The panel asked whether I could give an example from outside advertising, and I am happy to walk you through one.
R
Please do. Then we can iron out the details.
F
One last check, then. Your CFO said the role had to report directly to you. Does that still hold?

About the Conversation

That the board will decide on the acquisition by June.

3. What does she want to do after his example?
Iron out the details.
Listening

The Call After Round One

A headhunter on how she relays the feedback. Sound first — no text.

1. How does she relay the panel’s feedback?
She tells the candidate what the panel said, but never verbatim.
2. What do the weak candidates do?
They get defensive, or they ask who said it.
3. What do the strong candidates ask?
What the panel might want to see in round two, and whether they could send anything before it.
