Lesson 19 -- Pre-class

The Second Interview -- What You Were Told in Round One

Reporting back what the panel and the headhunter said, saying where it came from, and checking it with the chief executive. Key words: to relay, secondhand, verbatim, to take on board, a reservation, to read between the lines, to put in a good word, a frontrunner, to iron out, to touch base, to set expectations, to gloss over. Structure: reported speech with modal backshift.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These twelve are for the moment between round one and round two: what you heard, who said it, and what you do with it.

To relay -- to pass on a message from one person to another
“The headhunter relayed the panel’s feedback the same evening.”
Secondhand -- heard from somebody else, not from the source
“I only heard it secondhand, so I wanted to check it with you.”
Verbatim -- in exactly the same words
“I wrote down the chair’s question verbatim after the call.”
To take on board -- to accept feedback and act on it
“I took the panel’s feedback on board and prepared two examples.”
A reservation -- a doubt that stops you agreeing completely
“One director had a reservation about my sector experience.”
To read between the lines -- to understand what is meant but not said
“Reading between the lines, the board wanted a faster close.”
To put in a good word -- to say something positive about somebody to help them
“The headhunter said she would put in a good word with the chief executive.”
A frontrunner -- the candidate who is most likely to win
“She told me I was one of two frontrunners.”
To iron out -- to solve the last small problems
“We still have to iron out the reporting line.”
To touch base -- to contact somebody briefly to check something
“She said she would touch base with me after the second round.”
To set expectations -- to say clearly what will happen, and when
“The chief executive set expectations: a decision within three weeks.”
To gloss over -- to mention a problem quickly so nobody looks at it closely
“Do not gloss over the reservation; answer it directly.”

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The second interview was with the chief executive, and her first question was what I had taken away from round one. I said her chief people officer had told me the role would include IT, and that I had taken it on board.

About the integration, I was honest: I had only heard it secondhand. The headhunter said she might want the new CFO to lead it, but told me she couldn’t give me a date. The chief executive confirmed that the board would decide by June.

Then she said one director had a reservation about my sector experience. I did not gloss over it. I said the panel had asked whether I could give an example from outside advertising, and I gave her one. At the end I checked one last thing: the outgoing CFO had said the role had to report directly to her. It still did.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- One Step Back

GRAMMAR

When you report what somebody said, the modal usually moves one step back: will becomes would, can becomes could, may becomes might, and must becomes had to. Would, could, might and should do not change.

What was saidWhat you report
“The role will include IT.”She told me the role would include IT.
“I can’t give you a date.”She said she couldn’t give me a date.
“They may want the CFO to lead it.”He said they might want the CFO to lead it.
“The role must report to the CEO.”He said the role had to report to the CEO.
“Can you give an example?”They asked whether I could give an example.
The trapstold me, never said me. In a reported question, normal order: whether I could, not whether could I. If it is still true today, you can keep the present: she said the board will decide by June.

In a second interview, the modal tells the listener how sure the original speaker was. Might is not would: report it accurately, and check it if it matters.

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen, then record yourself. In natural speech would is short and unstressed, and had to sounds like hadta. Put the stress on the information you are reporting, not on the modal.

Quiz

C "I cannot remember."

A "I heard it secondhand. Is it still on the table?"

Which sentence is correct?
A "The panel asked whether could I give an example from outside advertising."
B "She said me the board would decide."

The chief executive says one director had a reservation. You:
A Say it is not a problem and move on to your next point quickly.

Before the interview ends, you want to confirm the reporting line. You say:
A Nothing; it is better to ask the headhunter by email later, when the process is over.
