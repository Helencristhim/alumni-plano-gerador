Wishing It Were Different -- Regret, Tone, and What Money Does Not Reach

She has been offered more money than she asked for and she is still not signing. What she wants cannot be paid. Key words: to acknowledge, remorse, a grievance, to defuse, resentment, conciliatory, to walk it back, begrudging, to talk past somebody, to make amends, to save face, a climbdown. Structure: wish structures.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To acknowledge -- to say openly that something is true, especially something uncomfortable for you
"Acknowledging that it happened is not the same as accepting the claim, and every mediator knows it."
Remorse -- real regret for harm you caused, which has to be shown rather than announced
"You cannot claim remorse. The room decides whether it heard any."
A grievance -- a complaint somebody has carried for a long time, whether or not anybody acted on it
"The grievance is six years old. The invoice is four months old. Only one of them is on the table."
To defuse -- to take the danger or the anger out of a situation before it goes further
"One sentence defused the whole afternoon, and it was not about money."
Resentment -- anger that stays in the room after the argument itself is over
"They settled the figure in an hour and the resentment stayed for two years."
Conciliatory -- meant to calm the other side and make agreement easier, in tone as much as in content
"The letter was conciliatory without giving away a single point of the case."
To walk it back -- to soften or partly withdraw something you said, without admitting you were wrong
"He walked it back in the afternoon and pretended the morning had not happened."
Begrudging -- given unwillingly, and everybody in the room can hear that it was
"A begrudging apology is worse than none. It says the words and takes them back with the tone."
To talk past somebody -- to speak without either side actually answering what the other one said
"They talked past each other for forty minutes. Both of them were right about different questions."
To make amends -- to do something concrete to repair harm you caused, not just to say sorry
"She was not asking for an apology. She was asking them to make amends in a way she could point at."
To save face -- to avoid public humiliation, and the useful version is doing it for the other side
"Give them a way to save face and you will get the clause you actually wanted."
A climbdown -- a public retreat from a position somebody had defended loudly
"Nobody signs an agreement that looks like a climbdown, however good the figure is."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The other side has offered more money than the claim asked for, and the client is not signing. This happens more often than people outside these rooms imagine, and it almost never means that the figure is too low. It means that the thing she is asking for cannot be paid. What she wants is for somebody to acknowledge that it happened. In the corridor her lawyer says the sentence that turns the afternoon around: she wishes somebody had acknowledged it two years ago, and nobody ever has.

Look at the verb. The regret is about the past, so the verb after wish goes one step further back: had acknowledged, not acknowledged. The same step happens with the present. I wish this meeting were less formal describes a meeting that is formal right now, and the verb still moves backwards. There is a third form for a different job: I wish they would stop treating an acknowledgement as an admission — that one is a complaint about what somebody else keeps doing, and it can never be used about yourself.

Tone is doing real work here. A begrudging apology is worse than none, because it says the words and takes them back with the voice. A conciliatory letter can give away no point of the case at all. And nobody signs anything that looks in public like a climbdown, which is why an experienced mediator spends the last twenty minutes giving the losing side a way to save face. One warning for a Portuguese speaker: never I wish I would have said it. Say I wish I had said it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Wish Structures

GRAMMAR

Step the verb one tense back, and use would only for what somebody else keeps doing.

JobFormExample
Dissatisfaction with nowwish + past simpleI wish this meeting were less formal.
Regret about the pastwish + past perfectI wish I had said it differently.
A complaint about somebody elsewish + would + base verbI wish they would stop repeating it.
Something you are not able to dowish + could + base verbI wish I could give you an answer today.
Were, not wascareful English keeps were for every personI wish I were in a position to agree.
Stronger, more emotionalif only + the same formsIf only somebody had told her in March.
The two trapsnever wish I would about yourself; never wish I would haveNot I wish I would have said it. Say I wish I had said it.

Never: I wish I would have said it · I wish I would be more direct · I wish this meeting is less formal · she wishes somebody has acknowledged it · I wish that they stop.

Stage 2: Put the Apology in Order

Order

Six moves that make an apology work in a room. Put them in a logical order.

?"We accept that the photograph appeared on two editions that were not covered."
?"We should have checked the licence before the paperback went to print."
?"We wish we had told you in March instead of waiting for you to find it."
?"From now on every cover licence will carry its limits on the front page of the file."
?"Your name will appear on every edition from the next printing."
?"We would like to agree the rest of it with you today, if you are willing."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to say that nobody ever admitted it, and that your client minds. You say:
A "She wishes somebody would have acknowledged it two years ago."
B "She wishes somebody had acknowledged it two years ago."
C "She wishes somebody has acknowledged it two years ago."
The other side keeps returning to the same figure and you want it to stop. You say:
A "I wish they would stop repeating the same number."
B "I wish they stopped repeating the same number."
C "I wish that they will stop to repeat the same number."
The other side will not put the acknowledgement in writing. You want to keep it alive. You say:
A "Then there is nothing more to discuss."
B "Then let them say it in the room. Nobody signs something that looks like a climbdown."
C "Then they are admitting it anyway by refusing."
You are drafting the first line of an apology. You say:
A "We are sorry that you feel our handling of this matter caused you distress."
B "We accept that it happened, and we wish we had told you in March."
C "We trust this draws a line under the matter."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Rewrite the apology letter out loud, in two minutes. Say what happened, what you wish had been done differently, what your side is offering that is not money, and what you wish were true about the way the two sides have been talking to each other. Do not use the word sorry more than once. If a sentence comes out wrong, finish it anyway.
