Today’s Goal

One Verb, One Step Back

1. The Words

to acknowledge, remorse, a grievance, conciliatory, to defuse, resentment, to make amends...

2. One Structure

what you wish were true, and what you wish you had done

3. Your Repair

acknowledge without conceding the case

English says regret by stepping the verb one tense back. Present dissatisfaction takes a past form; past regret takes a past perfect. It feels wrong, and it is what a room listens for.

Say it out loud, in two sentences: one about what you wish were true in that room right now, and one about what you wish you had said this morning.

Chapter 3: The Code

One Step Back

The tense that says this is not how things are

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"I wish this meeting were less formal." (it is formal, now)

"I wish I had said it differently." (I said it, this morning)

"I wish they would stop repeating the same number." (they keep doing it)

"I wish I could give you an answer today." (I cannot)

Read the blue notes first: they tell you when each sentence lives. Now look at the red verbs. Every one of them has moved one step further back than the time it is describing.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Room, at Full Speed

One conversation, three recordings, one draft letter

Dialogue

The Money Is Not the Problem

M
They have gone above what you asked for and your client has not moved. So let me ask the useful question. What would have to be in that room for her to sign?
A
Someone saying that it happened. Not that they are sorry she feels this way. That it happened. She wishes somebody had acknowledged it two years ago, and nobody ever has.
M
You know they will not put that in writing. Their lawyer will read it as an admission and they will refuse.
A
Then let us not write it. Let them say it in the room, with you present, and let the agreement say only what the agreement has to say. That way nobody has to sign anything that looks like a climbdown.
M
That could work. I wish you had said that before lunch, because we spent two hours on a number that was never the difficulty.
A
So do I. I wish I had said it this morning, and I did not, because I thought the money would be enough. It never was.
M
Nobody in these rooms works that out on the first attempt. I will go and give them a way to save face, because they will need one.
A
Thank you. And Marcus, before you go — how often does the thing that unlocks a room turn out not to be money?
Comprehension

About Marcus

1. What question does Marcus ask instead of asking whether the offer is accepted?
He asks what would have to be in the room for her client to sign.
2. Why does Marcus say the other side will refuse to put the acknowledgement in writing?
Because their lawyer will read it as an admission.
3. What does Marcus say he is going to do before he goes back to the other side, and why?
He is going to give them a way to save face, because he says they will need one.
Listening 1 · The Mediator

The Six Words That Ruin an Apology

A mediator on what makes a room accept an apology and what makes it worse. Sound first — no text.

1. Which six words does he say ruin an apology, and what does he say they actually do?
I am sorry you feel that way. He says the sentence moves the problem into the other person, so what the room hears is that the speaker thinks the feeling is the mistake.
2. What three parts does he say a working apology has?
Naming what happened, saying what should have happened instead, and one concrete thing that will be different from now on.
3. What does he say about lawyers who tell their clients never to apologise?
That they are protecting against a risk that is smaller than they think, and that in a mediation the words cannot be used later anyway, which is a fact half the room does not know.
Listening 2 · The Client

Nobody Ever Asked Me

A party to a dispute on what she was actually asking for. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say everybody assumed she wanted, and what did she want?
Everybody assumed she wanted money, and every meeting was built around a figure. What she wanted was for one person to say out loud that it had happened.
2. What does she say about the two years, and what does she wish had been different?
She says the two years cost her more than the money ever would have. She wishes somebody had asked her, in the first month, what she was actually asking for.
3. What does she say about her own lawyer?
That he was very good and that he never asked her either, because nobody had taught him that it was a question. She says she does not blame him for it.
Listening 3 · Radio Report

The Law That Lets You Say Sorry

A radio report on apology laws and what happened where they exist. Sound first — no text.

1. What problem do apology laws exist to solve?
That the people best placed to apologise are told by their own lawyers not to, because anything they say could be used against them later. The law makes the apology inadmissible so that it can be given.
2. What does the report say happened to the number of claims where these laws were introduced?
It fell, and the researchers were careful about why. The most convincing explanation is that many claims were never about money in the first place.
3. What limit does the report describe at the end?
That a protected apology only works if it is believed. A begrudging one, given because a lawyer said it was now safe, makes things measurably worse.
Real Document

The Letter She Would Not Accept

DRAFT LETTER — NOT SENT
annotated by Ana Ladeia Tavares before the session

Dear Ms Braga,

We are sorry that you feel our handling of this matter has caused you distress.

While we do not accept that any error occurred, we are willing, purely in the interest of bringing this to a close, to make a payment in the sum discussed.

We trust this draws a line under the matter.

Margin notes: line 1 makes the feeling the problem. Line 2 says the money is not for the harm but for the silence. Line 3 tells her when she is allowed to stop being upset. Nothing here acknowledges anything, and my client will read all three lines in under ten seconds.
1. According to the margin notes, what is wrong with the first line?
It makes the feeling the problem rather than the thing that happened. It apologises for her reaction, not for the event.
2. What does the note say the payment is being offered for, in the way the letter is written?
Not for the harm, but for the silence: the money is offered in the interest of closing the matter, while denying that anything went wrong.
3. Rewrite the first line out loud so that it acknowledges without admitting the legal claim.
Something like: we accept that the photograph appeared on two editions that were not covered, and we wish we had told you in March rather than waiting for you to find it.
