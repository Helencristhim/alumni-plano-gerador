Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Ouça cada palavra e leia o exemplo. Toque em Listen para ouvir. / Listen to each word and read the example.

Avenue -- a wide, long street in a city, usually with a lot of traffic
"There is a big avenue near my house." (Avenida)
Square -- an open public space in a city, with streets and buildings around it
"There is a small square with big trees." (Praça)
Market -- a place with many small shops or stands where people sell food
"My grandmother goes to the market every Saturday." (Mercado / feira)
Crowded -- full of people, so there is very little space to walk
"The subway is crowded in the morning." (Cheio / lotado)
Noisy -- with a lot of loud sound, like cars, buses and people
"My street is noisy, but I like it." (Barulhento)
Street art -- big colorful pictures painted on the walls of buildings
"There is incredible street art on this wall." (Arte de rua / grafite)
Rooftop -- the flat top of a building, where people go to look at the city
"We go up to a rooftop and look at the lights." (Terraço / cobertura)
Bike lane -- the part of the street that is only for bicycles, not for cars
"There is a bike lane on this avenue." (Ciclovia)
Food truck -- a truck that stops on the street and sells hot food
"There is a food truck outside the market." (Food truck / trailer de comida)

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- There is / There are

GRAMMAR

Para dizer o que EXISTE num lugar, o inglês usa "there is" (uma coisa) e "there are" (duas ou mais). Nunca use "have" para isso. Na pergunta, o verbo vem primeiro: "Is there...?" / "Are there...?" / For saying what exists in a place, English uses there is (one thing) and there are (two or more).

Form / FormaStructure / EstruturaExample / Exemplo
One thing (+)There is + a/an + nounThere is a food truck outside.
Many things (+)There are + plural nounThere are three museums here.
QuestionIs there...? / Are there...?Is there a market near here?
NegativeThere is not / There are notThere are not any cars on Sunday.
Short formThere's / There aren'tThere's a square near my house.

Key point / Ponto-chave: olhe a palavra DEPOIS. Uma coisa pede there is; duas ou mais pedem there are. E NUNCA diga "Have a square near my house" — em inglês é There is a square near my house.
