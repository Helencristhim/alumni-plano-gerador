Lesson 15 -- Pre-class

If I Could Change One Thing -- Opinion and Hypothesis

The words of a change: approval, paperwork, to streamline, to empower, feasible, pilot, buy-in, drawback. Structure: the second conditional, with if + past, would, could and if I were you. Mission: defend one change you would make in your company.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Approval -- official permission to do something
"Every small repair in the stores needs three approvals."
Paperwork -- the forms and documents you have to fill in for a process
"If we had less paperwork, the repairs would be faster."
To streamline -- to make a process simpler and faster
"I would streamline the approval process for small repairs."
To empower -- to give people the authority to make decisions themselves
"If we empowered store managers, they would solve small problems in a day."
Feasible -- possible and practical to do
"The idea is feasible, and it would not cost anything."
Pilot -- a small test of a new idea before you use it everywhere
"We could run a pilot in twenty stores for three months."
Buy-in -- agreement and support for an idea from the people involved
"Without buy-in from finance, the change would never happen."
Drawback -- a disadvantage, or a problem that comes with something good
"The main drawback is that we would have less control over spending."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

If I could change one thing in the company, it would be the approvals for small repairs. Today a broken door needs three signatures and nine days. If store managers approved repairs below two thousand reais, they would solve most problems in a day. It wouldn't cost anything. If finance were worried about control, we could start with a pilot in twenty stores.

Look at the verbs after if: approved, were. They look like the past, but they are not about the past. They show that the situation is not real now: it is an idea. The result takes would, could or might. And to give advice, we say If I were you, never if I would be you.

1. In If store managers approved repairs, is the writer talking about the past?
A Yes, because approved is the past simple of approve, so it happened before.
B No. It is an imaginary situation now.
C Yes, because the managers approved repairs last year.
2. How much would the change cost, according to the text?
A Two thousand reais for every store in the pilot, every single month.
B Nothing.
C Nine days.
3. What could they do if finance were worried about control?
A Start with a pilot in twenty stores.
B Cancel the whole idea and keep the three approvals and the paperwork.
C Ask the store managers for more signatures.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Second Conditional

GRAMMAR

A situation that is not real now, and what would happen if it were. The condition takes the past; the result takes would, could or might.

FormUse it forExample
If + past, would + verbCondition first, with a comma.If I had the budget, I would change the process.
would + verb if + pastResult first, no comma.Repairs would be faster if we cut approvals.
If I were you, I would...Advice. Use were with every person.If I were you, I would talk to finance.
could / mightA possible result, less sure than would.We could start with a pilot.
First or second?Real possibility: if + present, will. Imagined: if + past, would.If it arrives, we will call. / If I were CEO, I would change it.
NegativeIf we didn't have three approvals, it wouldn't take nine days.

The most common mistake is would in both halves. Only the result takes would: if I had, never if I would have.

A If I was you, I will talk to finance first, before the meeting starts.

C If I am you, I talk to finance.
You want to offer a small test. You say:

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think about one real thing you would change in your four areas if you had the power. Record about ninety seconds. Say what you would change and why, what would happen after the change, and what it would cost. Then say the main drawback, and how you would control it. Finish with one piece of advice that starts with if I were you. Use at least four words from this lesson. Do not stop to correct yourself.
