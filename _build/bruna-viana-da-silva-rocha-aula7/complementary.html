Film
Chef (2014) — Jon Favreau

A chef with a good job and a dead career walks out of the restaurant and buys a food truck. It is the same story Bruna tells about herself, with knives: who he used to be, what he grew out of, and the thing that was still there underneath. American English, fast, funny, and every kitchen scene is conversation at real speed.

Tip: write down five sentences about who the character used to be, before the truck. Then write five about who you used to be, in the same shape.

Podcast
NPR Life Kit — "6 tips for making a career change, from someone who has done it"

An American producer who was a nurse for fifteen years, and a career strategist who was a public defender, on what a pivot actually costs and what you take with you. Normal speed, full transcript on the same page.

Tip: listen once with the transcript closed. Then find three sentences where somebody talks about what they used to do, and read them out loud with the d silent: yoosta.

Video
Rachel's English — "How to Pronounce USED TO in American English"

Two words that come out as one, with a letter that vanishes. She shows exactly why used to becomes yoosta and why writing it the way it sounds is what stops you from hearing it in a real conversation.

Tip: after the video, say "I used to be a dietitian" five times, faster each time, until the d is gone and the two words are one.
