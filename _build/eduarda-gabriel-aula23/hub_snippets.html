Persuasion Techniques in Business English

Lane Packaging: Eduarda pitches to Victor Lane, a hesitant founder who built his company over thirty years. Key words: pitch, compelling, value proposition, credibility, competitive edge, buy-in, win over, hesitant. Structure: rhetorical questions to persuade (Wouldn't you agree...? Isn't it time...? Why not...? What if...?).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Pitch -- a short presentation to convince someone to buy or agree
"We have twenty minutes for the pitch."
Compelling -- so strong or interesting that people pay attention and believe it
"She made a compelling case for selling now."
Value proposition -- the main reason a client should choose you and not a competitor
"Our value proposition fits in one sentence."
Credibility -- the quality that makes people trust and believe you
"Two recent deals give us credibility in packaging."
Competitive edge -- an advantage that makes you better than your competitors
"Our network of buyers is our competitive edge."
Buy-in -- agreement and support for a plan from the people involved
"We need the family's buy-in before we start."
Win over -- to convince someone who did not agree with you at first
"It took two meetings to win him over."
Hesitant -- not sure, and slow to decide or act
"The founder is still hesitant about selling."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Victor Lane founded Lane Packaging thirty years ago. Now he is sixty-five, and three banks want the mandate to sell his company. He is hesitant: the company is his life's work. Eduarda has twenty minutes for her pitch.

She does not start with numbers. She starts with a question: What would happen to the company if you retired tomorrow? Then she gives her value proposition in one sentence and builds credibility with two packaging deals her team closed last year. That is her competitive edge.

When Victor worries about his employees, she asks: Isn't that exactly why the buyer matters? She knows she needs the family's buy-in, so she closes with one more question: Why not meet them together next week? Her argument is compelling, and in the end she wins him over.

1. Why does Eduarda start the pitch with a question and not with numbers?
A Because she does not know the numbers of Lane Packaging and wants Victor to give them to her first.
B Because the other banks started with questions.
C To make Victor think about the future of his company.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "Why not meet them together next week?"
B "Why not to meet your family together next week, with no commitment?"
C "You don't agree that timing is everything?"
3. What gives Eduarda's team credibility?
A The fact that her bank is the biggest of the three banks that visited Victor this week.
B Two packaging deals her team closed last year.
C A lower fee.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Rhetorical Questions

GRAMMAR

A rhetorical question does not ask for information. It makes the listener think and agree.

JobFormExample
Get a silent yesWouldn't / Don't + you + verb?Wouldn't you agree that timing matters?
Show it is obviousIsn't it / Aren't we + ...?Isn't it time to plan your exit?
Suggest an actionWhy not + base verb?Why not test the market now?
Open a possibilityWhat if + past simple?What if a buyer paid more?
Open a possibility (real)What if + present simple?What if we start with two buyers?
Persuasive openerImagine + clauseImagine the company in ten years.

Never: Why not to wait? · You don't agree? (as a rhetorical question) · What if a buyer would pay more?

Order

?"In the end, she won him over and got the mandate."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose what a persuasive advisor would say.

You want the founder to agree that now is a good moment. You say:
A "Wouldn't you agree that this is the right moment?"
B "You agree this is the right moment, yes or no? I need an answer before the end of this meeting."
C "Not would you agree that this is the moment?"
You want to suggest a short meeting with the family. You say:
A "Why not to meet your family next week and talk about the future of the company together?"
B "Why don't meet your family?"
C "Why not meet your family next week?"
The client asks about your competitive edge. You say:
A "We have no edge, but we are very nice."
B "Our network of buyers in packaging is our competitive edge."
C "Every bank is the same, so the choice is really up to you and your family in the end."
You want the founder to imagine a better price. You say:
A "What if a buyer paid twenty percent more?"
B "What if a buyer would pay twenty percent more than you expect for the company?"
C "What if a buyer pays more, it would be good."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Think of a real client, project or idea at work that you want someone to accept. Record one minute of a short pitch: give your value proposition in one sentence, and use three rhetorical questions: "Wouldn't you agree...?", "What if...?" and "Why not...?".
