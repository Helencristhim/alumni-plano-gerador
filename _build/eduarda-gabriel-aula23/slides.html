Chapter 3 -- The Code

Wouldn’t You Agree?

Questions that persuade, not questions that ask

Grammar Discovery

What Do the Purple Words Have in Common?

"Wouldn't you agree that timing is everything in a sale?"

"Isn't it time to find out what your company is really worth?"

"Why not test the market now, while prices are high?"

"What if a buyer paid twenty percent more than you expect?"

Does Eduarda really want an answer? How is each question built?

UseFormExample
Get a silent yesauxiliary + n't + subject + verb?"Wouldn't you agree that timing matters?"
Show it is obviousIsn't it / Aren't we / Don't you + ...?"Isn't it time to act?"
Suggest an actionWhy not + base verb?"Why not test the market now?"
Open a possibilityWhat if + clause?"What if a buyer paid more?"
Neverwhy not + to / statement orderNot "Why not to wait?" / "You don't agree?"
Grammar Rule

Statement or Question?

StatementRhetorical question (same idea)
"Timing is important.""Wouldn't you agree that timing is important?"
"You should sell now.""Isn't it time to sell?"
"You should test the market.""Why not test the market?"
"A buyer might pay more.""What if a buyer paid more?"
The client agrees or disagrees.The client thinks, and says yes to himself.

Make It a Question

Say it first, then click to check

Obvious: "___Isn't it (be / it / not) time to plan your exit?"

A possibility: "What if your son ___didn't want (not / want) to run the company?"
A silent yes: "___Don't you (do / you / not) want the best price for thirty years of work?"
Obvious: "___Aren't we (be / we / not) here because the timing is right?"
Chapter 4 -- Getting There

Lane Packaging: Twenty Minutes

One pitch, two recordings, one follow-up

Dialogue

Eduarda and Victor

V
I'll be honest, Eduarda. I'm hesitant. Two other banks were here this week.
E
I understand. So let me start with a question. What would happen to Lane Packaging if you retired tomorrow?
V
Honestly? I don't know. My son works in tech. He doesn't want to run a factory.
E
Then wouldn't you agree that this is the right moment to plan? Our value proposition is simple: the right buyer, not just the highest price.
V
Every bank says that. What is your competitive edge?
E
Last year we closed two packaging deals in Latin America. That gives us credibility with the buyers who matter to you.
V
That's a compelling point. But my employees worry me. Many of them have been here for twenty years.
E
Isn't that exactly why the buyer matters? What if we only talked to buyers who keep the team?
V
I would need my family's buy-in first.
E
Of course. So why not meet them together next week? No commitment, just a conversation. I think we can win them over.
Comprehension

About Victor

1. Why doesn't Victor's son want to run the company?
He works in tech and doesn't want to run a factory.
2. What worries Victor about the sale?
His employees, because many of them have been there for twenty years.
3. Whose buy-in does Victor need first?
His family's.

Listening 2 -- The Coach

Why Questions?

A pitch coach explains why questions persuade, and when to stop. Sound first -- no text.

1. What does a question do that a statement does not?
It makes the listener think.
2. What builds buy-in, according to the coach?
The small yes the client says in their head.
3. What happens if you use too many rhetorical questions?
You come across as a salesperson, not an advisor.
Real Document

The Follow-up Email

From: Eduarda Gabriel, Houlihan Lokey
To: Victor Lane, Founder, Lane Packaging
Re: Next Steps -- Planning the Future of Lane Packaging

Dear Victor,

Thank you for your time today. You built Lane Packaging over thirty years, and the decision about its future deserves the same care.

Wouldn't you agree that the best time to plan a sale is before you need one? Our team closed two packaging deals in Latin America last year, and we know the buyers who value a loyal team like yours.

Why not bring your family to a short meeting next Tuesday? There is no commitment: just a clear picture of your options.

Best regards,
Eduarda

1. According to the email, when is the best time to plan a sale?
Before you need one.
2. What proof of credibility does Eduarda give?
Her team closed two packaging deals in Latin America last year.
3. What does she propose as the next step?
A short meeting with Victor's family next Tuesday, with no commitment.
