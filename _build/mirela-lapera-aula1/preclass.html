Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

Edinburgh, Take Two -- I’ve Been / I Went

Planning a spring trip to Edinburgh and talking about the places you have been to. Key words: a blizzard, closed to visitors, a guided tour, to book in advance, the off-season, crowded, breathtaking, a highlight, to make the most of, a bucket list. Structure: present perfect x simple past.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A blizzard -- a very bad snowstorm with strong wind
"We couldn’t go out because of the blizzard."
Closed to visitors -- not open for people to come in and see it
"The castle was closed to visitors that day."
A guided tour -- a visit where an expert shows you a place and explains it
"We took a guided tour of the old town."
To book in advance -- to reserve something some time before, not at the last minute
"In the summer, you need to book in advance."
The off-season -- the time of the year when few tourists visit a place
"Hotels are cheaper in the off-season."
Crowded -- full of people
"The streets were very crowded at New Year."
Breathtaking -- extremely beautiful; so beautiful that it surprises you
"The view from the hill was breathtaking."
A highlight -- the best or most exciting part of something
"The highlight of the trip was the concert."
To make the most of -- to use a situation or a time as well as you can
"We only had two days, so we made the most of them."
A bucket list -- a list of things you want to do or see in your life
"Machu Picchu was on my bucket list for years."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Mirela has been to many places: New York, Paris, London, Amsterdam and Machu Picchu, which was on her bucket list for years. But one trip was different. She went to Edinburgh for New Year, and there was a blizzard. The castle was closed to visitors, and she saw almost nothing.

Now she wants to go back in the spring. It is the off-season, so the city is not crowded, and the parks are green. She has never taken a guided tour there, so this time she wants to book one in advance.

Her friend Ana went to Edinburgh last April. “The view from the castle is breathtaking,” she says. “It was the highlight of my trip.”

1. Why does the text say “She went to Edinburgh for New Year” and not “She has been”?
A Because the sentence tells a specific time and a specific story.
B Because she went with her family, and has been is only for trips that you take alone.
C Because the trip was bad, and we use has been only for good trips.
2. What does “She has never taken a guided tour there” mean?
A She took a guided tour last year, but she did not like it very much.
B She does not like guided tours.
C Until now, in her whole life, she has not taken a guided tour in Edinburgh.
3. Why is the spring a good time for her trip?
A Because the castle is free for visitors in the spring.
B Because it is the off-season: the city is not crowded and the parks are green.
C Because there are no tourists at all in Edinburgh in April and May.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect x Simple Past

GRAMMAR

Use have / has + past participle for your life until now, without a date. Use the simple past when you say when, or when you tell one specific story.

Life experience, no datehave / has + past participle“I’ve been to Peru.”
Asking about experienceHave you ever + past participle?“Have you ever been to Scotland?”
Never, until nowhave never + past participle“I’ve never seen the castle.”
A specific time or storysimple past“I went there in 2019. It snowed.”
Asking for detailsWhen / Where / How did you...?“When did you go?”

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

You call a tour company in Edinburgh. Put the conversation in a logical order.

?"Good afternoon, Old Town Tours. How can I help you?"
?"Hi, I’d like to book a guided tour for April."
?"Of course. Have you ever been to Edinburgh?"
?"Yes, I have. I went for New Year, but there was a blizzard."
?"Oh no! Well, April is much better. It’s not crowded."
?"Great. Can I book the castle tickets in advance?"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A friend asks about your experience in Peru. You say:
A "I went to Machu Picchu. It was breathtaking."
B "I have gone to Machu Picchu in 2015, and it has been breathtaking."
C "I go to Machu Picchu. It is breathtaking."
You want to know if your colleague knows Scotland. You ask:
A "Did you ever go to Scotland in your life before now?"
B "Have you ever been to Scotland?"
C "Are you ever in Scotland?"
The museum is famous and has a limited number of visitors. You say:
A "We can buy the tickets at the door on the day, it is always easy."
B "We need to book the tickets in advance."
C "We need to make the most of the tickets."
You only have two days in a city. You say:
A "Let’s make the most of these two days."
B "Let’s take the off-season of these two days and see everything we can."
C "Let’s book in advance these two days."
