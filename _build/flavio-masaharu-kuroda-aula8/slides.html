Today’s Goal

Read It, Compare It, Defend It

1. The Words

a quote, a licence fee, a term, upfront, a margin...

2. One Structure

cheaper than, the lowest, twice as much as, up to

3. Your Defence

two options compared, and the dearer one defended

A price is never defended with an adjective. It is defended with a comparison — against the other option, against what they pay today, or against what the problem costs them.

One Question

Compare Two Options, Out Loud

One option costs 40,000 a year for one year. The other costs 30,000 a year for three years. Compare them in one sentence.

Then the harder one: say why the more expensive one is the one you would take.

Chapter 4: The Code

Cheaper Than What?

A price only means something next to another number

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Do?

"Our licence fee is lower than theirs, but the setup costs more."

"Option A is the cheapest of the three, and the shortest."

"The setup costs twice as much as one year of licence."

"That price covers up to fifty users and at least two integrations."

Not one of them says a price. Every one of them puts a number next to another number — the other option, the total, or the limit. The blue words are the ones that set a boundary.

In one line: a price alone means nothing. Always say cheaper than what, and up to how many.

COMMERCIAL SUMMARY
valid for 30 days · all figures per year unless stated
Licence, up to 50 users28,000 per year
Setup, one-off56,000
Option A — one-year term84,000 in year one
Option B — three-year term19,000 per year, setup waived
Paid upfrontnothing — first payment at milestone one
Above 50 usersunit price falls by roughly a fifth
Option A is the cheapest to leave and the most expensive to stay in: eighty-four thousand in year one, and twenty-eight thousand every year after that. Option B is a little over half of that in year one, because the setup is waived, and about a third less per year across the three years. The line they will attack is the setup, because it is the only number here that is bigger than the annual fee — and the answer is not a discount, it is the nine days their closing takes today.
1. Which option costs less in year one, and by roughly how much?
Option B. Eighty-four thousand against nineteen thousand in year one, because the setup of fifty-six thousand is waived. Option B is a little over half of Option A in year one when the whole three-year commitment is taken into account.
2. The note calls Option A the cheapest to leave and the most expensive to stay in. What does that mean?
It has no long commitment, so leaving after one year costs nothing extra. But every year in it costs the full annual fee, with no discount, so staying is dearer.
3. Why does the note say the answer to the setup objection is not a discount?
Because a discount lowers the price without changing the comparison. The nine days their closing takes today gives the setup something to be measured against.
Chapter 6: Your Turn

Two Recordings, Then You

Listening 1 · Presenting the Price

Never Say the Number First

A sales director on the sixty seconds around the price. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say he always puts before the number, and why?
The comparison. He says a price with nothing beside it is just a big number, and the customer will find their own comparison if you do not give them one.
2. What does he say about breaking the price down?
That it almost always reduces the objection, because most of what looks expensive turns out to be two or three things, and only one of them is being argued about.
3. What does he say happens when a salesperson hesitates while saying a number?
The customer hears doubt about the price rather than about the language, and starts negotiating a number the vendor has already agreed is too high.
Listening 2 · From Procurement

What She Compares It Against

A procurement manager on how three quotes get compared. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say her real benchmark is?
What they pay today, including the people. Not the other vendors, and not the price the vendor thinks is competitive.
2. What does she do with a quote that has one number and nothing else?
She sends it back and asks for a breakdown, and she says the ones that cannot break it down usually cannot defend it either.
3. What does she say about the vendor who offers a discount too quickly?
That the first price was not real, and that everything else in the proposal now has to be checked twice.
