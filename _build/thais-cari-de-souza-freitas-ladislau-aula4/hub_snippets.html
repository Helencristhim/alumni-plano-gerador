Vocabulary

A loss -- money the company does not get back

To launch -- to put a new product in front of customers for the first time
"In April they launched a product for people who had never borrowed."
A cohort -- a group of customers who started in the same month
"The cohort that started in April reached its third payment in August."
A spike -- a sudden rise in a number
"A spike appeared in the default rate, and it did not fall back."
To drop -- to go down
"Revenue dropped in September for the first time in two years."
A write-off -- money the bank decides it will never receive
"The write-off landed in the same week as the quarterly review."
To tighten -- to make a rule stricter, so fewer people get in
"They tightened the policy in October and they lost some growth."
To loosen -- to make a rule less strict, so more people get in
"Somebody loosened a rule in March, and nobody wrote down why."
A milestone -- a point in a project that shows real progress
"The real milestone was the habit they kept, not the number."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Until now, everything you said in English happened every week. This one happened once, and it is over. Most verbs take a single ending: "In April they launched a new product." "They tightened the policy in October." The ending is -ed, and it never changes with the subject: I launched, she launched, they launched. There is no third-person -s in the past, ever.

A short list of common verbs refuses the ending and changes shape instead: rise becomes rose, write becomes wrote, go becomes went, find becomes found, keep becomes kept, lose becomes lost. "Revenue rose in May and the team kept the habit." Never rised, never keeped. These are learned one by one, and about twenty of them cover most of what a business person says.

To say no and to ask, the past moves onto did, and the verb goes back to its simplest form: "They did not argue about the model." "Did anybody write down the reason?" Never did not argued, never did anybody wrote. One past per sentence, and did takes it. And one last thing, which matters more than the forms: once a story is in the past, it stays there to the end. "They tightened it and nobody remembered why" — not remembers.

1. "She launched" and "they launched" have the same shape. Why?
A Because launch is an irregular verb.
B Because the past simple never changes with the subject. There is no third-person -s in the past.
C Because both sentences are about a company.
2. Why is "they did not argued" wrong?
A Because did already carries the past, so the verb goes back to its base form.
B Because argue is irregular and has another form.
C Because a negative sentence needs no verb at all.
3. The text warns against "they tightened it and nobody remembers why". What is the problem?
A Remember is an irregular verb and needs another shape.
B Nobody is plural, so the verb is wrong.
C The story started in the past, so it has to stay in the past to the end.
