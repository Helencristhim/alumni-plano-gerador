Chapter 4: The Code

Everything That Points Backwards

One ending, one exception, and one word that carries them both

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"In April they launched a new product."

"Revenue rose in May, and the team read the dashboard every day."

"They did not argue about the model."

"Did anybody write down the reason?"

Two of the red verbs take the same ending and two of them change shape completely. Now look at the last two sentences: the blue word turned up, and the red verb went back to its simplest form.

JobFormExample
Regular verb — positiveverb + -ed, for every subjectThey launched it. · She tightened it.
Irregular verb — positivea new shape you learn one by onerise → rose · write → wrote · read → read · go → went
Negativedid not + base verbThey did not argue. Not did not argued.
QuestionDid + subject + base verbDid anybody write down the reason?
Short answerYes, they did. / No, we did not."Did the rate fall back?" — "No, it did not."
The sound of -edthree sounds, one spellinglaunched = T · tightened = D · landed = ID
The trapa finished story stays in the past to the endNot they tightened it and nobody remembers why. Say nobody remembered why.

In one line: -ed or a new shape for yes, and did for no and for the question — and once the story is in the past, it stays there.

Listening 1 · A Manager Tells the Story

The Month Everything Turned

A credit manager describing a quarter that went wrong. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the team do in the first two months, and how did they feel about it?
They launched the product and watched revenue rise twice. She says everybody was happy and nobody asked a single question.
2. What did she notice in August, and what did she do first?
She noticed the default rate climb, and the first thing she did was open the policy, not the model.
3. What does she say she regrets, and what does she say she does not regret?
She regrets that nobody wrote down the reason for the March change. She does not regret the launch itself.
Listening 2 · A Podcast

Why Nobody Remembers

A risk consultant on decisions that nobody wrote down. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say he asks first when a company calls him?
He asks who changed the rule and why, and he says almost nobody can answer the second half.
2. What example does he give of a change that looked small at the time?
A team that let customers with a shorter credit history in, to reach a target that quarter.
3. What are the three things he says every change should carry?
A name, a date and a reason.

Survival Card

Five Phrases for a Quarterly Review

We launched the product in April, and revenue rose in May and in June.
In August a spike appeared in the default rate, and it did not fall back.
We did not change the model. We read the policy line by line.
Did anybody write down the reason for that change?
We tightened the policy in October, we lost some growth, and we kept the habit.

What I Can Do Now

I can tell the story of one quarter from beginning to end, and it stays in the past.
I use did not for a negative and Did for a question, and the verb goes back to its base form.
I never say did not argued or rised. I say did not argue and rose.
I can describe what a number did last quarter and say what my team decided because of it.
I know the words: revenue, a loss, to launch, a cohort, a spike, to drop, a write-off, to tighten, to loosen, a milestone.
