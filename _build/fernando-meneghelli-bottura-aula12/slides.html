Chapter 3: The Code

What They Are Actually Scoring

Four lines, and none of them is your accent

The Move

The Sheet, In Three Lines

1. What they score

“Structure, evidence, and how you recover. Never the accent.”

2. What raises a flag

“Blaming somebody else, a story that changes, and answers that never end.”

3. What you ask at the end

“How will you compare the final two, and what would make you say no to me?”

Nobody prepares for the third one. Watching a board try to answer it in front of you is the most useful five minutes of the whole process.

Chapter 5: The Conversation

The Call Before the Call

And the risk is not a hard question

Dialogue

The Risk Is Not the Question

C
The second round is with somebody you have not met, and I cannot tell you who.
F
That is fine. Could I ask what they will be scoring?
C
Structure, evidence, and consistency with the first round. They have the notes.
F
So the risk is not a hard question. The risk is a different version of the same answer.
C
That is exactly the risk, and it is the most common red flag in my files.
F
Then I will give the same figures I gave you, even when they sound rehearsed.
C
Say the numbers slowly, and let them write. One more thing: they will ask what would make you say no to us.
F
I have an honest answer to that, and I would rather give it than a polite one.
C
Good. The ones who hold up are the ones who are the same person twice.

Listening 2

The Question He Asks Last

A chairman on the final five minutes. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he ask every candidate at the end?
What would make them say no to the company.
2. Why does "there is nothing" cost the candidate?
It means they have not thought about it, or they would say anything to get the offer.
3. What did the man who was hired say?
That he would say no if the reporting line to the board went through somebody else.

Simulation

The Second Interview

Scenario: Final round for the CFO role, forty minutes compressed into fifteen. The evaluator has the notes from the first simulation, asks two questions you already answered, one hostile block of three, and finishes with what would make you say no to us.

No keywords, no notes on screen, no help. If you go blank, run the protocol out loud. If you miss the end of a question, ask for it again. Finish with your three questions.

Debrief

Five Numbers, No Adjectives

Blanks

How many, and how long each one lasted in seconds

Paraphrases

How many times you said the question back before answering

Headlines

How many answers opened with the conclusion

Questions

How many you asked at the end, and how many were about their problem

These four numbers are the measurement of the first half of the programme. Nothing here is a judgement about whether you are good enough for the role.
