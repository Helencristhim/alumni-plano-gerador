Lesson 12 -- Midpoint Checkpoint

Grammar Box Set
"Used to and would" -- BBC Learning English, five lessons in 28 minutes

A consolidation set rather than a new lesson, which is exactly what this week is for. Connection to Lesson 12: this is the family from Lesson 9, revisited from five angles, and it is the one advanced speakers keep half-learning for years.

Tip: do not watch it in one sitting. Four sessions of seven minutes across the week, and after each one produce three sentences about your own career out loud. Spacing is what makes review stick; volume is not.

Talk with transcript
"The power of believing that you can improve" -- Carol Dweck, TED (10 min)

Ten minutes on the difference between "I am not good at this" and "I am not good at this yet". Connection to Lesson 12: you described ten years of English as the same fight. The research says the sentence you use about yourself changes what you do next, and that is not a motivational claim — it is measured.

Tip: watch once, then write down the sentence you have been using about your own English for ten years. Rewrite it with a number in it. Say the new version out loud — it is also a better interview answer.

Long-form Interview
"Ethan Mollick" -- In Good Company (45 min)

A Wharton professor talking, at speed and without simplification, about how people actually get better at things. Useful here for two reasons at once: the content matches the lesson, and the pace is the pace of a real board conversation rather than a teaching recording.

Tip: this is the week to practise listening without the safety net. One pass, no transcript, no pausing. Afterwards write three sentences about what he argued. If you got the argument, the missing words did not matter — which is the point.
