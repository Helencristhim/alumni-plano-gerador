Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These twelve are for talking about your own progress — useful today, and useful in any performance conversation.

A baseline -- the measurement you took at the start, to compare against later
"The recording from lesson one is the baseline. That is the only reason it exists."
To plateau -- to stop improving after a period of visible progress
"People conclude they have plateaued at exactly the point where the gains turn invisible."
A blind spot -- something about your own performance that you cannot see
"A blind spot is not a weakness. It is a weakness only somebody else can name."
Incremental -- improving in small steps rather than in jumps
"Incremental gains are invisible weekly and obvious across three months."
A benchmark -- the standard you measure yourself against
"The benchmark is not a native speaker. It is the other three people on the shortlist."
To gauge -- to judge something you cannot measure exactly
"You cannot measure confidence, but you can gauge it from the first two seconds."
Tangible -- real enough that you can point at it
"Four seconds instead of eleven is tangible. Feeling more confident is not."
To fall short -- to not reach a standard you had set
"Falling short of a target you set is not the same thing as failing."
To build on -- to use what already works as the base for the next step
"Build on the recovery lines. They are the part that already works under pressure."
To sharpen -- to make something already good more precise
"We are not fixing your English any more. We are sharpening it."
Deliberate practice -- repeating only the hard part, with feedback that arrives at once
"Deliberate practice is not more conversation. It is the same four seconds, twenty times."
To keep pace -- to improve fast enough to stay level with a moving standard
"Your ear is improving too, so keeping pace with your own standard is the hard part."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions. Every structure from the first eleven lessons appears in it at least once.

Twelve lessons in, this is where I actually am. I have worked on interview English for three months, and I used to think the problem was vocabulary. I am used to hearing a hard question as information now, and it took one lost process to get used to that. By the time the simulation came round in lesson ten, I had already built three protocols and had been practising the recovery lines for about a month.

The baseline says eleven seconds to the first word. Last week it said three. What I would underscore is that I did not feel any of that, because my own benchmark kept rising — so I was gauging myself against something that moved. That is not a plateau. Not only did the numbers improve, they improved in the two places I never noticed: recovery and speed.

The blind spot is the first sentence of an answer. Only two in eight opened with a conclusion, which is tangible and easy to train. By the end of the next block I will have practised that first sentence about a hundred and forty times, and this time next month I will be opening with a conclusion without deciding to. If I had measured earlier, I would have found it earlier. Could I ask to be interrupted more often in the meantime? That is the part I still fall short on.

1. "By the time the simulation came round, I had already built three protocols." Which happened first?
A Building the protocols. The past perfect marks the earlier moment.
B The simulation, because it is the main clause.
C They happened simultaneously.
2. "I used to think the problem was vocabulary" and "I am used to hearing a hard question as information". What is the difference?
A The first is a belief that ended; the second is a state that is normal now — and the second takes the -ing form.
B They are the same structure with different verbs.
C The first is informal and the second is formal.
3. Why does the text say the improvement was not felt?
A Because the improvement was too small to matter.
B Because the writer's own benchmark kept rising, so he was measuring himself against a moving standard.
C Because the baseline recording was inaccurate.
4. "This time next month I will be opening with a conclusion without deciding to." What does that form say?
A That it will be the normal state at that point — something in progress, not something finished.
B That it will be completed and behind him by then.
C That it is a hope rather than a plan.

Reflection
