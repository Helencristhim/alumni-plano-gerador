Grammar Discovery

What Decides the Ending?

"We ended up doing it twice, which nobody had budgeted for."

"We managed to confirm it before six, but only just."

"I am looking forward to meeting them in person."

"They are reluctant to commit before the numbers are confirmed."

Nothing about the second verb changes: it is the SAME verb doing the same job. What changes is the expression in front of it. The red ones demand -ing and the blue ones demand to, and there is no logic to learn — only a list, and the list is short. Look at the third sentence again, though: it already has a to, and it still takes -ing. That one is not on any list. It follows a rule.

JobFormExample
After these verbs, always -ingenjoy, avoid, suggest, consider, keep on, end up, give up, mind, finishWe ended up doing it twice.
After these verbs, always to + basedecide, agree, manage, offer, refuse, promise, hope, plan, expect, be reluctant, botherWe managed to confirm it.
After ANY preposition, always -ingto (as a preposition), in, about, of, before, afterI look forward to meeting them. Before sending it.
The famous traplook forward to ends in a prepositionNot look forward to meet. Say to meeting.
The other famous trapsuggest takes -ing, never toNot I suggest to move it. Say I suggest moving it.
Both, with a change of meaning: stopstop doing = end it / stop to do = pause in order toI stopped reading. I stopped to read.
Both, with a change of meaning: rememberremember doing = a memory / remember to do = do not forgetI remember sending it. Remember to send it.
Both, with a change of meaning: trytry doing = experiment / try to do = attemptTry calling instead. Try to call before six.

In one line: there is no logic here, only the verb in front. The one real rule is that a preposition is always followed by -ing.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Two Recordings, One Plan

Nothing here is slowed down for you

Listening 1 · Business Radio

The Part Nobody Warns You About

A short radio piece on the moment progress stops being visible. Sound first — no text.

1. What happens in the first few months, according to her?
Everything you learn is visible: you could not say a thing on Monday and by Friday you can, and the line goes up in a way you can feel.
2. Why does the feeling of progress stop, and what is actually improving?
Because what improves next is invisible from the inside: the same sentence half a second faster, and the same idea with one fewer pause in the middle.
3. What does she say about that moment?
It is where most people give up, and it is the single worst place to stop, because everything on the other side of it is the part that sounds like fluency.
Listening 2 · A Voice Note

A Year of the Thing She Was Already Good At

A voice note about a colleague who studied every morning for a year. Sound first — no text.

1. What was her routine, and what could she do after a year?
Forty minutes every morning: vocabulary lists, grammar exercises and a podcast on the way to work. After a year she could read almost anything and still could not hold a conversation for five minutes.
2. What one thing did she change?
She kept everything she was doing and added two conversations a week with a person who let her finish her own sentences.
3. What does he say she had noticed?
That she had spent a year practising the thing she was already good at, which is what almost everybody does when nobody is watching.
Real Document

The Plan for the Next Ten

Block two — what changes
Agreed at the end of block one · three lines, one owner

1. What stays. We keep on working the same way: eight words a lesson, one structure, and ninety seconds of free speech at the end that nobody interrupts.

2. What changes. From lesson eleven the subject takes over. We stop building structures and start using them on news, politics and culture — which is what you asked for in the first place.

3. What you agree to do. You promise to bring one story to every lesson, in English, that you actually read that week. Not a summary. A story you have an opinion about.

4. What nobody has to do. You do not have to prepare it in writing, and you should not. The point is speaking about something you have not rehearsed.

Sample plan written for this lesson, following the programme brief.
1. Line 1 and line 2 use the same structure differently. Find both and say what changes.
keep on working takes -ing because keep on demands it. stop building and start using also take -ing here, and stop would mean something else entirely with to.
2. Line 3 uses promise to. What would change if it said you agree to consider?
Everything about whether it happens. Promise to bring sets an expectation somebody can hold you to. Agree to consider commits to thinking about it.
3. Line 4 uses two structures from earlier lessons. Which, and why here?
do not have to from lesson five, and should not from the same one. A plan works better when it also says clearly what carries no obligation and what is actively discouraged.
