To keep on -- to continue doing something, often for longer than you should
"She kept on sending emails when a call would have taken four minutes."
To manage to -- to succeed in doing something difficult
"We managed to confirm it before six, but only just."
To be reluctant to -- to be unwilling to do something, and to hesitate about it
"They are reluctant to commit before the numbers are confirmed."

"Nobody bothered to ask, which is why it took three weeks."
To be about to -- to be on the point of doing something, right now
"I was about to call you when your email arrived."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

When one verb is followed by another, English makes you choose between two endings, and the choice has nothing to do with meaning. It belongs to the verb in front. We ended up doing it twice. We managed to confirm it before six. The second verb is doing exactly the same job in both sentences; only the expression before it changed. There is no logic to work out here and there is nothing to feel: there is a short list, and the list is worth ten minutes of your life.

One group always takes -ing: enjoy, avoid, suggest, consider, mind, finish, admit, deny, keep on, end up, give up. Another always takes to plus the base verb: decide, agree, manage, offer, refuse, promise, hope, plan, expect, be reluctant, bother. The two commonest mistakes a Brazilian speaker makes here are both in the first group, because Portuguese uses one infinitive for everything: I suggest to move the call should be I suggest moving the call.

There is exactly one real rule, and it rescues the other classic error. After a preposition, always -ing. Before sending it, interested in hearing from you, thank you for sending it so quickly. And the trap: in look forward to, that little to is a preposition and not an infinitive, which is why it is I am looking forward to meeting them, and never to meet. Finally, three verbs take both and change meaning: stop doing ends the activity while stop to do pauses in order to start it; remember doing is a memory while remember to do is a reminder; try doing is an experiment while try to do is an attempt.

1. According to the text, what decides whether the second verb takes -ing or to?
A The meaning of the second verb.
B The expression in front of it.
C Whether the sentence is formal.
2. Why is it “looking forward to meeting” and not “to meet”?
A Because meeting is a noun here.
B Because that to is a preposition, and a preposition is always followed by -ing.
C Because the sentence is in the continuous.
3. What is the difference between “he stopped reading” and “he stopped to read”?
A There is none; both mean he is no longer reading.
B The first is British and the second is American.
C The first means he ended the activity. The second means he paused in order to start it.
