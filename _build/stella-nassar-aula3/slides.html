The Sentence That Points

Discover the Style

Where Is the Writer Pointing?

"It is the light, not the gesture, that decides the meaning of the scene."

"What distinguishes tenebrism from ordinary shadow is the violence of the contrast."

"It was not beauty that Caravaggio pursued, but a kind of truth."

"All he needed was a window, a table and a handful of ordinary faces."

Rewrite each sentence without the colored frame. What exactly does the frame add?

FormWhat it doesExample
It is / was + focus + that / whoputs one element under a spotlight, often in contrast with anotherIt is the light that calls Matthew.
What + clause + is / was + focusbuilds suspense and delivers the focus at the endWhat unsettled his critics was the dirty feet.
It is not X but Y thatcorrects an expectation the reader probably hasIt was not piety but naturalism that provoked them.
All / The only thing + clause + isemphasizes how little was neededAll he needed was a single window.
The C2 trappointing at nothing. A cleft on a vague focus ("What I think is that art is important") is filler with good posture. Use it once or twice per paragraph, on the claim that matters.

Chapter 5: Voices in the Chapel

The Painting Out Loud

A seminar call, a chapel guide and a lecture on Caravaggio's followers

Dialogue

An Online Seminar, One Contested Figure

O
Before we go on, I want everyone to commit to an answer. Which figure at the table is Matthew?
S
The bearded man pointing at himself. He looks as if he is asking, "Who, me?"
O
That is the traditional reading. Some historians argue it is actually the young man at the end of the table, the one who will not look up.
S
Really? But then the gesture would mean something completely different.
O
Exactly. If the young man is Matthew, the bearded man is pointing at him, not at himself.
S
Then it is the young man who is refusing the call, at least for a second. That makes it much more disquieting.
O
Does the light help you decide?
S
Not really. What the light does is connect the hand in the doorway to the whole table. It illuminates everyone.
O
I have taught this painting for three years and I still change my mind about it every semester.
S
So the right answer on the assignment is not a name. It is an argument.
Comprehension

About Oliver

1. What does Oliver ask the group to do before the seminar continues?
To commit to an answer about which figure at the table is Matthew.
2. What alternative reading does Oliver mention, and how would it change the gesture?
That Matthew is the young man at the end of the table who does not look up. The bearded man would then be pointing at him, not at himself.
3. What does Oliver admit about his own view of the painting?
He has taught it for three years and still changes his mind every semester.
Listening 1

Inside the Chapel

A guide speaks to visitors in a church in Rome. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. What does the guide ask visitors to do before looking at the paintings, and why?
To put a coin in the box so the lights come on for a few minutes, because the chapel is otherwise too dark to see the canvases.
2. According to the guide, what is surprising about the direction of the painted light?
It seems to come from the direction of the real window of the chapel, as if daylight from the church entered the scene.
3. Why does the guide say the setting shocked some viewers at the time?
Because a sacred moment is shown in a smoky room full of tax collectors in fashionable clothes, as if it were happening in an ordinary tavern.
Listening 2

After Caravaggio

A professor lectures on the painters who followed him. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. What does the professor say happened to Caravaggio's reputation after his death?
It faded for a long time, even though his influence spread quickly across Europe.
2. Which followers does she name, and what does she say about Artemisia Gentileschi?
She names Artemisia Gentileschi, Georges de La Tour and the young Rembrandt. Gentileschi used the same dramatic light but told stories from a perspective few painters had given them.
3. What point does she make about the word Caravaggisti?
That it is useful but misleading, because it suggests copying, when the best of them used his light to say something of their own.
