Chapter 4: The Used To Code

Then and Now

I used to ... but now I ...

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I used to skip my workout on trips, but now I go to the gym."

"She used to work late every night, but now she stops at seven."

"We didn't use to stretch, but now we stretch every day."

"Did you use to run?"

What do the orange words have in common? Is each habit true now, or only in the past?

FormStructureExample
Affirmativeused to + base verbI used to skip my workout.
Negativedidn't use to + base verbI didn't use to stretch.
QuestionDid + subject + use to + base verb?Did you use to run?
Nowbut now + present simplebut now I go to the gym.

Use used to for a past habit or situation that is not true now. It is the same for all people: I / she / we used to. With did, write use to (no "d").

Chapter 5: Small Talk at Dinner

The Reception Dinner

Personal topics, relaxed English

Dialogue

Dinner in Milan

M
So, Flavia, how are you finding Milan so far?
F
Honestly, I love it. But the days are long, so I try to keep my routine.
M
Your routine? Do you exercise when you travel?
F
Yes. I used to skip my workout on trips, but now I go to the hotel gym every morning.
M
That is real discipline! I used to run, but now I only walk to the office.
F
Walking is great for your well-being. For me, the most important thing is a good mindset.
M
I agree. And how do you recharge after a week like this?
F
On Saturdays, I do volunteer work. Speaking of discipline, that is how I lead projects at Sebrae, too.
