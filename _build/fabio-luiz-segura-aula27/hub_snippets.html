The Regulatory Conversation

A call with a policy advisor about a cross-border pilot: gray areas, sandboxes, and careful claims that keep trust. Key words: regulator, regulatory sandbox, gray area, consultation paper, grace period, audit trail, reporting obligation, to comply with, to be subject to, to err on the side of caution. Structure: hedging with seem, appear, tend to and be likely to.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A regulator -- an official organization that controls an industry and makes sure companies follow the rules
"Before we launch the pilot, we need to talk to the regulator."
A regulatory sandbox -- a controlled test area where a company can try a new product with real clients, under lighter rules and for a limited time
"A regulatory sandbox lets us test the new payment flow with ten clients first."
A gray area -- a situation where the rules are not clear, so it is difficult to say what is allowed
"Cross-border settlement in real time is still a gray area in many countries."
A consultation paper -- a document in which a regulator proposes new rules and asks the industry for comments
"The central bank published a consultation paper on instant cross-border payments."
A grace period -- extra time you get after a rule starts, before you must follow it completely
"Banks will have a grace period of six months to adapt their systems."
An audit trail -- a complete record of every step of a process, so that somebody can check it later
"Every payment in the pilot has a full audit trail, from the client to the settlement."
A reporting obligation -- something a company must send to the authorities regularly, by law
"Inside the sandbox, the reporting obligations are lighter for the first year."
To comply with -- to follow a rule, a law or an official request
"Our new system has to comply with the data protection rules in both countries."
To be subject to -- to be controlled or affected by a rule, a law or a decision
"Projects in the sandbox are still subject to the main anti-money laundering rules."
To err on the side of caution -- to choose the safer option when you are not sure
"We decided to err on the side of caution and start with only two currencies."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A meeting with a regulator is not a sales pitch. A regulator's job is to protect the system, so every sentence you say is, in a way, on the record. If you say "our solution is completely safe," you invite the regulator to look for the problem.

Experienced bankers use hedging instead. They separate what they know from what they think. The technology seems to be ready. It appears that the rules were written before real-time payments existed. These sentences are careful, but they are not weak.

Two more tools are very useful. Tend to describes a general pattern: Regulators tend to move slowly when two countries are involved. And be likely to describes a probable future: A regulatory sandbox is likely to be the safest first step. After all four structures, the verb comes with to.

Hedging is not about avoiding the truth. It is about honesty. When a topic is a gray area, a careful claim shows the regulator that you err on the side of caution, and that is exactly what builds trust.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Hedging with Seem, Appear, Tend to and Be Likely to

GRAMMAR

Separate what you know from what you think.

UseFormExample
Your reading of somethingseem / appear + to + verbThe rules seem to favor larger banks.
Your reading of a whole situationIt seems / It appears + that + clauseIt appears that the grace period will be extended.
A general patterntend to + verbRegulators tend to move slowly.
A probable futurebe likely / unlikely to + verbThe paper is likely to be published in March.
Negative and questiondoesn't seem to · Is it likely to...?It doesn't seem to be a problem. · Is there likely to be a grace period?

Never: The rules are seem to... · It is likely publish · Banks tend underestimate · It appears the regulator to be...

Stage 2: A Careful Call

Order

A call between a banker and a policy advisor. Put it in a logical order, from the first sentence to the next step.

?"That's fair. Regulators tend to want a full audit trail first."
?"Probably. But first, read the new consultation paper."
?"I understand. It seems that the technology is ready, but the rules are not. Is that a fair reading?"
?"I'll come back to you with our comments before the deadline."
?"Your pilot looks interesting, but cross-border settlement is still a gray area."
?"So a sandbox is likely to be the safest first step for us?"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that works best in a conversation with a regulator.

The regulator asks: "Is your system completely safe?" You say:
A It seems to be very stable in the tests so far. I can share the data.
B Yes, it is one hundred percent safe, there is absolutely no risk at all, I can promise you that.
C I don't know.
Your director asks if the grace period is guaranteed. It is probable, but not certain. You say:
A Yes, it is guaranteed.
B A grace period is likely, but it depends on the results.
C A grace period is likely happen after the test, so we don't need to worry about the full framework now.
You want to describe a general pattern in the industry. You say:
A Banks are tend to underestimating the reporting obligations in the first year of a new rule.
B Every bank underestimates everything.
C Banks tend to underestimate the reporting obligations.
The rules are not clear, and you are not sure what to do. You say:
A Nobody understands these rules, so we will just launch the product and see what the regulator says later.
B It's a gray area, so we'd rather err on the side of caution.
C Let's wait.
