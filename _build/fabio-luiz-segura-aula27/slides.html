Chapter 3: Careful Claims

It Seems That...

Saying what you think without promising what you cannot control

Grammar Discovery

Fact or Reading?

"The new rules seem to favor the larger banks."

"It appears that the grace period will be extended."

"Regulators tend to move slowly when two countries are involved."

"The consultation paper is likely to be published in March."

Is the speaker 100% sure in these sentences? What comes after the red words?

UseFormExample
Your reading of a situationseem / appear + to + verbThe rules seem to favor larger banks.
A reading about a whole situationIt seems / It appears + that + clauseIt appears that the grace period will be extended.
A general pattern, not alwaystend to + verbRegulators tend to move slowly.
A probable futurebe likely / unlikely to + verbThe paper is likely to be published in March.
The trapno be before seem; likely needs toNot The rules are seem... · Not is likely publish

In one line: hedging separates what you know from what you think, and that is exactly what makes a regulator trust you.

Grammar Practice

Make It Careful

Say the full sentence first, then click to check

“The technology is ready. (seem) ___The technology seems to be ready.”
“The regulator is worried about data protection. (appear) ___The regulator appears to be worried about data protection.”
“Banks always underestimate the reporting obligations. (tend to) ___Banks tend to underestimate the reporting obligations.”
“The rules will change next year. (likely) ___The rules are likely to change next year.”
“The sandbox has room for only ten projects. (it seems that) ___It seems that the sandbox has room for only ten projects.”
“They will not approve the pilot this year. (unlikely) ___They are unlikely to approve the pilot this year.”
Chapter 4: Real Speed

A Call on the Record

A policy advisor, a compliance consultant, and a sandbox update

Dialogue

A Fair Reading

C
Thanks for making time, Fabio. Let me be direct. Your pilot with Clearline looks interesting, but cross-border settlement is still a gray area for most regulators.
F
I understand. From our side, it seems that the technology is ready. What appears to be missing is a clear set of rules. Is that a fair reading?
C
That's fair. Regulators tend to move slowly when two countries are involved. Each side wants a full audit trail before it says yes.
F
That makes sense. So a regulatory sandbox is likely to be the safest first step for us?
C
Probably, yes. Inside a sandbox, you're still subject to the main rules, but some reporting obligations are lighter for twelve months.
F
Twelve months sounds reasonable. And what happens when the test ends? Is there likely to be a grace period?
C
Usually, yes, about six months, so you have time to comply with the full framework. But I can't promise anything. It depends on the results.
F
Of course. We'd rather err on the side of caution. We don't want to look like we're asking for special treatment.
C
Good. Then I'd suggest you read the new consultation paper first. It seems to answer most of your questions, and the comment period closes in May.
F
That's very helpful. I'll share it with our compliance team this week, and I'll come back to you with our comments before the deadline.
Comprehension

About Claire

1. What does Claire say about cross-border settlement at the start of the call?
It is still a gray area for most regulators, even though the pilot looks interesting.
2. Why do regulators move slowly, according to her?
Because two countries are involved, and each side wants a full audit trail before it says yes.
3. What does she suggest Fabio reads first, and when does the comment period close?
The new consultation paper. The comment period closes in May.
Listening 1 · The Consultant

Not a Sales Pitch

A compliance consultant explains how to talk to a regulator. Sound first — no text.

1. What mistake does he see bankers make again and again?
They go into a meeting with a regulator as if it were a sales pitch.
2. What do regulators tend to listen for, and why?
For risk, not for opportunity, because their job is to protect the system.
3. What are his three pieces of advice?
Don't overstate (say it seems to work and show the data), bring the audit trail, and ask questions about what worries them.
Listening 2 · The Sandbox Update

Nine Out of Forty-Two

A sandbox program lead opens a roundtable with a short update. Sound first — no text.

1. How many applications did the sandbox receive, and how many did it accept?
Forty-two applications. Nine were accepted.
2. Why were most of the other projects rejected?
They seemed to be ready technically, but they did not have a clear plan for data protection.
3. When is the next application window, and how often do sandbox projects report?
From the first of March to the end of April. They report every quarter instead of every month.
Real Document

The Internal Briefing

Subject: Call with Claire Whitmore · real-time settlement pilot
From: Fabio Luiz Segura · To: Compliance team · Internal

What we know: cross-border settlement is still a gray area. A regulatory sandbox gives lighter reporting obligations for 12 months.

Our reading: a sandbox seems to be the safest first step. A grace period of about six months is likely, but not guaranteed.

Risk: regulators tend to ask for a full audit trail from day one. We should prepare it now.

Next step: read the consultation paper and send our comments before the deadline in May.

"A grace period of about six months is likely, but it depends on the results of the test."
