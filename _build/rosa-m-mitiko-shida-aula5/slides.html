Chapter 3: The Code

Two Halves, One Sentence

The condition, and what follows from it

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"If the board approves the plan, we will open two more offices."

"If the deal falls through, we will go back to the March proposal."

"Unless we have a contingency plan, we do not really have a plan."

"As long as you send the figures on Wednesday, I will have the range by Friday."

Every sentence has two halves. In which half does will appear, and in which half does it never appear?

FormWhat it saysExample
If + present simple,
will + base verb		A real, possible future. The condition takes the present, the result takes will. Never both."If the board approves it, we will open two offices."
UnlessMeans if not. It is already negative, so the verb after it stays positive."Unless we have a second version, we do not have a plan."
As long as · provided thatSets your condition politely. Very useful when you agree to something but need something first."As long as you send the figures on Wednesday, I will have the range on Friday."
In caseNot the same as if. It means because it might happen, so you prepare before, not after."I will book the room in case the meeting runs long."
Order and commaYou can start with either half. The comma appears only when the if half comes first."If we go ahead, I will need a number."
"I will need a number if we go ahead."

One rule carries all of it: no will after if. The condition stays in the present, however far in the future it is.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"If the board will approve the plan, we will open two offices."
"If the board approves the plan, we will open two offices."
"If the client confirm tomorrow, I send the contract."
"If the client confirms tomorrow, I will send the contract."
"Unless we do not have a contingency plan, the project will fail."
"Unless we have a contingency plan, the project will fail."

Will never appears after if, however far in the future the condition is. And unless already means if not, so adding another negative reverses the sentence and says the opposite of what you mean. Present after if, will after the comma.

Chapter 4: The Planning Meeting

A Number Before Friday

He needs an answer you do not have yet

Dialogue

The Call from Planning

V
Rosa, Victor from regional planning. Two minutes, and then I have to be somewhere. The board votes on the second phase next Thursday.
R
Go ahead. If they approve it, what changes for my portfolio?
V
Two more offices in the first half of next year, and one of them will probably be Lima. If we go ahead, some of your accounts will need a second contact person.
R
How many is some? That is the part that decides my whole quarter.
V
That is exactly what I am asking you. I need a range by Friday, not a perfect answer. If I do not have your number, I will put an estimate in the file, and you will not like my estimate.
R
As long as I get the target figures on Wednesday, you will have the range on Friday morning. Without them I would be guessing.
V
You will have them Tuesday night. One more thing: build a second version. Unless we have a contingency plan for January, nobody upstairs will take the November date seriously.
R
Understood. Two versions, one number, Friday morning. And if the vote falls through on Thursday, call me before I send anything.
Comprehension

About Victor

1. What is happening next Thursday, and who decides?
The board votes on the second phase of the expansion.
2. What exactly does he need from her, and by when?
A range, not a perfect answer, by Friday.
3. What will he do if he does not get her number?
He will put his own estimate in the file.
4. Why does he insist on a second version of the plan?
Because without a contingency plan for January, nobody upstairs will take the November date seriously.

Listening 2

What We Know and What We Do Not

The opening of a planning session. Sound first — no text.

1. What do they know already, and what do they not know?
They know the budget. They do not know the timing, and the timing decides everything else.
2. What happens if the approval slips to January?
The office opens in June, which is the worst month in this business to start anything.
3. What rule does she give for leaving the room?
Nobody leaves with a plan that only works if everything goes right — without a second version, there is no plan.
