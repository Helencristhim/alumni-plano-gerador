Lesson 05 -- Pre-class

If the Expansion Happens -- Planning Out Loud

The first conditional, in the meeting where nobody knows yet what the company will decide. Key expressions: forecast, risk, to approve, budget, headcount, to fall through, contingency plan, target, to scale, trade-off, to go ahead, lead time. Structure: if plus present simple, will plus base verb -- and unless, as long as and in case.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. These twelve stay here in your material -- they do not disappear after the class.

Forecast -- a statement about what is likely to happen, based on the numbers you have now
"If the forecast is right, we will need two more people."
Risk -- the chance that something goes wrong
"The risk here is the timing, not the money."
To approve -- to say officially that a plan or an amount can go ahead
"If the board approves the second phase, two offices will open."
Budget -- the amount of money available for a plan or a period
"We know the budget. What we do not know is the timing."
Headcount -- the number of people working in a team or a company
"The plan does not change the headcount in Brazil."
To fall through -- when a plan or a deal fails before it is finished
"If the deal falls through, we will go back to the March proposal."
Contingency plan -- a plan you prepare in case the first plan does not work
"We plan for November and we build a contingency plan for January."
Target -- the result you have to reach, usually a number
"If we hit the target this quarter, the budget stays."
To scale -- to grow something in a controlled way, keeping it working
"We cannot scale the operation with the same team."
Trade-off -- a situation where you accept something worse in one area to get something better in another
"The trade-off is speed against price, and the client has to choose."
To go ahead -- to start or continue with a plan, as decided
"If we go ahead, I will need a number by Friday."
Lead time -- the time between deciding something and it actually happening
"If the approval comes in November, the lead time is comfortable."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Nobody in the room knows what the board will decide on Thursday, and everybody has to plan anyway. That is the normal condition of this industry, and the people who are good at it have stopped waiting for certainty before they open their mouths.

What they know is the budget. What they do not know is the timing, and the timing decides everything else. If the approval comes in November, the first office will open in March and the lead time is comfortable. If it slips to January, the same office will open in June, which is the worst month in this business to start anything.

So the meeting produces two versions. The main plan assumes November. The contingency plan assumes January, a smaller headcount and a later target. Unless there is a second version, nobody upstairs takes the first one seriously, and everybody in this room has watched a plan fall through for exactly that reason.

Rosa is asked for one number: how many of her accounts will need a second contact person if the company goes ahead. She does not have it yet, and she does not pretend to. As long as she gets the forecast and the target figures on Wednesday, she will send the range on Friday morning. She also says the part nobody enjoys saying: the real risk is not the money, it is that the team cannot scale at this speed, and the trade-off is either a later date or fewer accounts per person.

1. Why is "if the approval comes in November" in the present, when it is clearly about the future?
A Because English never uses will after if in this structure.
B Because November has already happened.
C Because it is a habit that repeats every year.
2. "Unless there is a second version, nobody takes the first one seriously." What does unless mean here?
A Even if there is a second version.
B If there is not a second version.
C Because there is a second version.
3. Rosa says she will send the range "as long as she gets the figures on Wednesday". What is she doing?
A Refusing the task.
B Promising something she cannot deliver.
C Accepting the task and naming the condition she needs.
4. According to the text, what is the real risk?
A That the budget will be cut.
B That the team cannot scale at this speed.
C That November is the worst month to start.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The First Conditional

GRAMMAR

How English talks about something real that has not happened yet. The whole structure rests on one rule, and almost every mistake at this level is the same one.

FormWhat it saysExample
If + present simple,
will + base verb		A real, possible future. The condition takes the present and the result takes will. Never both."If the board approves it, we will open two offices."
UnlessMeans if not. It is already negative, so the verb after it stays positive."Unless we have a second version, the plan will fail."
As long as · provided thatSets your condition politely. This is how you accept a deadline without promising the impossible."As long as you send the figures on Wednesday, I will have the range on Friday."
In caseNot the same as if. It means because it might happen, so you prepare before, not after."I will book a bigger room in case the meeting runs long."
Order and commaYou can start with either half. The comma appears only when the if half comes first."If we go ahead, I will need a number."
"I will need a number if we go ahead."

One rule carries all of it: no will after if. The condition stays in the present, however far in the future it is. And remember the third person: if the client confirms, not if the client confirm.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are in the planning meeting and on the phone with a worried client. Choose the best thing to say.

1. Your director asks what happens if the second phase is approved. You say:
A If the board will approve it, we will open two offices.
B If the board approves it, we will open two more offices.
C If the board approve it, we open two more offices.
2. He asks for a number by Friday and you need the figures first. You say:
A I cannot do it. It is impossible before Friday.
B Maybe I will try, I am not sure, it depends on many things.
C As long as I get the target figures on Wednesday, you will have the range on Friday.
3. You want to say that a plan with no second version is not a plan. You say:
A Unless we do not have a contingency plan, we do not have a plan.
B Unless we have a contingency plan, we do not really have a plan.
C Unless we will have a contingency plan, we do not have a plan.
4. A worried client asks what the restructuring means for him. The best opening is:
A Nothing changes for you, and if it does, I will call you first.
B I do not know anything about that, sorry.
C If everything will change, I will tell you.
5. You want to book a bigger room because the meeting might run long. You say:
A I will book a bigger room if the meeting will run long.
B I will book a bigger room in case the meeting runs long.
C I book a bigger room in case the meeting will run long.
