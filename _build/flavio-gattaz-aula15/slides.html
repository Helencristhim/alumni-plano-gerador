Chapter 3: The Code

How English Says Regret

Wish, if only, and the two faces of regret

Grammar Discovery

What Happens After Wish and Regret?

"I did not ask him first." → "I wish I had asked him first."

"I did not wait three months." → "If only I had waited three months."

"I did not train him before the move." → "I regret not training him before the move."

"We are sorry, the answer is no." → "We regret to inform you that your application was unsuccessful."

Everything in blue is the plain fact. Everything in red is the regret. After wish and if only, the past moves one step further back. After regret, the thing you are sorry you did takes -ing; regret to is only for bad news you are giving now.

ToolFormExample
Wish (past)wish + past perfect for a past you cannot changeI wish I had asked him first.
If only (past)if only + past perfect: the same idea, but stronger and more emotionalIf only I had waited three months.
Wish (now)wish + past simple for a present you would like to be differentI wish I had more engineers today.
Regret + -ingthe action you are sorry about; the negative is regret not + -ingI regret not training him before the move.
Regret toformal: bad news you are giving now (inform, say, tell)We regret to inform you that...
The pivotone regret sentence, then what you do nowI wish I had asked him. Now nobody is promoted without a conversation first.

In one line: after wish and if only, go one tense back; after regret, use -ing — and in an interview, say it once.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Question, at Full Speed

One conversation, two recordings, one email

Dialogue

The Promotion That Failed

H
I would like to ask about something that did not go well. Tell me about a time you failed, and please make it a real one.
F
In 2022 I promoted my best field engineer to run two sites at once. Six months later he resigned, and one of the sites was eleven weeks late.
H
Whose decision was the promotion?
F
Mine, entirely. I misjudged what he wanted. I saw a great engineer and assumed he wanted to manage people. I wish I had asked him.
H
Why do you think it went wrong? I am asking for the root cause, not the symptoms.
F
The root cause was that I gave him the title and none of the tools. I regret not training him before the move. He did not fall short. I did.
H
And what is different on your sites today because of it?
F
It was a wake-up call. Nobody is promoted now without a conversation first and a ninety-day handover. Two engineers have been promoted that way since, and both are still with us.
Comprehension

About Hannah

1. What condition does Hannah add when she asks for a failure?
She asks him to make it a real one, not a small or safe failure.
2. When she asks why it went wrong, what does she say she does not want?
The symptoms. She wants the root cause.
3. What is her last question about?
What is different on his sites today because of the failure — the change, not the feeling.
Listening 1 · An Interviewer

What the Question Is Testing

An admissions interviewer on the three things she listens for. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say a small failure is not a safe choice?
Because a failure too small to hurt anybody tells her that nobody ever trusted the candidate with anything that could go wrong.
2. What does she listen for when she tests ownership?
The first time the candidate says they, the client or the market. One sentence of context is fine, but once the candidate starts shifting the blame, the answer is over.
3. What is the third thing, and what does she say about a lesson with no change in behavior?
Evidence: what the candidate does differently now, on an ordinary Tuesday. A lesson with no change in behavior is a story, not a lesson.
Listening 2 · A Candidate

A Strength Wearing a Failure’s Clothes

A candidate on the same question, answered two different ways. Sound first — no text.

1. What did he answer the first time, and how did the interviewer react?
He said he worked too hard and trusted people too much, and the interviewer stopped writing.
2. What was the real failure, and what does he say its root cause was?
A product launched in a market they did not understand, which fell short of the target by more than half. The root cause was that he had never spoken to a single customer before they built it.
3. What does he say was the real answer?
The rule they have now: nobody signs off on a new product until they have interviewed ten customers personally.

The Email They Send Before the Day

Your Admissions Interview — What to Expect
From: Executive MBA Admissions Office · To: Flavio Gattaz

Dear Flavio,

Your interview will last 45 minutes, on video. One question will ask about a professional failure. Please prepare for it the same way you prepare for the others.

1. Choose a failure that mattered: one with a real cost to the business or to people.

2. Tell us what your part in it was. Context is useful; it should take no more than a sentence or two.

3. Spend most of your time on what you changed afterwards, and how we would see that change today.

Please note that answers such as "I am a perfectionist" or "I care too much" are not treated as failures by our interviewers.

Admissions Office · Interview Round 1 · October

Regret, Then the Pivot

Say the regret and what changed, then click to compare

1. "I did not visit the second site for a month." (wish + now)
"I wish I had visited the second site sooner. Now I visit every site every week."
2. "I accepted the client's schedule without questions." (regret + now)
"I regret accepting that schedule without questions. Now every schedule is checked by the site engineer first."
3. "We did not write down the handover." (if only + now)
"If only we had written the handover down. Now it is a two-page document, and it is signed."
4. "I did not tell my uncle for two weeks." (regret not + now)
"I regret not telling my uncle for two weeks. Now bad news reaches him the same day."

Role-Play 1 of 3 — Guided

Own It in the First Sentence

Scenario: The interviewer asks for a time you failed. Give ninety seconds: the failure and its cost first, your part in it second, one regret third, and what you do differently now for the rest of the time.

Keyword chips:

eleven weeks I misjudged I wish I had a ninety-day handover
Role-Play 2 of 3 — Semi-free

A Different Failure

Scenario: Same question, different failure — a budget, a lost client, a decision at the club, anything that is not the promotion. The interviewer will interrupt you twice and offer you an easy way out. Do not take it.

Keywords:

the root cause was I regret not we course-corrected I learned the hard way
Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

A Panel That Keeps Asking Why

Scenario: Two minutes with an interviewer who answers everything you say with another "why?". Get to the real root cause, say the regret only once, and finish on what is different today.

No keywords, no notes, two minutes.
