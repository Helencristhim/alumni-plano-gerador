Aced (formerly Exponent) -- "Tell me about a time you failed - Behavioral Interview Question"

An interview coach and a candidate working through the exact question you practiced today: how to pick a failure that is real, how to own it, and how to finish on what changed. American English at a natural pace, with the structure explained as they go.

Tip: pause after the sample answer and count how many sentences are about the regret and how many are about the change. Then record your own version and do the same count.

Podcast
Freakonomics Radio -- "How to Succeed at Failing, Part 1: The Chain of Events"

Harvard Business School professor Amy Edmondson on the difference between failures that teach you something and failures that are only expensive, and why the root cause is almost never the first thing anybody sees. Free, clear American speech, and full of examples from engineering and hospitals.

Tip: listen for the phrase "root cause" and write down each time you hear it. Then explain, in two sentences, the root cause of your own failure story.

TED -- Astro Teller, "The unexpected benefit of celebrating failure"

The head of Google's X on why his teams are rewarded for killing their own projects early. It is the organizational version of today's lesson: a failure is only useful when somebody owns it quickly and course-corrects, instead of dwelling on it.

Tip: watch it once with subtitles off. Then say two sentences about a project you should have stopped earlier, one with "I wish I had" and one with "I regret not".
