Chapter 3: Closed, Open, or a Good Idea

Must, Have To, Should

Obligation, prohibition, advice, and the freedom of don't have to

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Every visitor has to sign in at the gate."

"You mustn't take photos near the docks."

"You don't have to wear boots in the office."

"You should keep your badge where people can see it."

FormUse it forExample
have to / has toA rule that comes from outside: the company, the law, the policy.Every visitor has to sign in.
mustA strong obligation, often written, or one the speaker feels personally.I must give him the right answer.
mustn't / not allowed toForbidden. The door is closed.You mustn't take photos near the docks.
don't have toNot necessary. You are free to do it or not.You don't have to wear boots in the office.
shouldAdvice. A good idea, not a rule.You should keep your badge visible.
Past and questionsMust has no past: use had to. Do I have to sign? Yesterday I had to stay late.

Mustn't and don't have to look like twins and mean almost the opposite. One closes the door, the other opens it.

Chapter 4: The Tour

From the Gate to the Docks

Walking a visitor through your own building

Dialogue

From the Gate to the Docks

F
Welcome, Giulia. Before we go in, every visitor has to sign in here at the gate.
G
Of course. Do I have to show my passport?
F
Just an ID. And inside the warehouse you have to wear this vest. It is our policy for everybody, including me.
G
No problem. It is a very bright yellow.
F
That is the idea. The drivers have to see you from far away.
G
And the shoes? Must I wear safety boots?
F
In the office, you don't have to. In the warehouse, yes, and we have boots at the gate.
G
Good. Can I take some photos for my director?
F
Not near the docks, I'm afraid. Visitors aren't allowed to take photos in a restricted area.
G
I understand. Why is it so strict?
F
Because most of our shrinkage happens between the truck and the shelf, not in the stores.
G
That is the same in Milan. We just never wrote the rule down.
