Lesson 11 -- Pre-class

Rules and Policies -- Explaining the Rules

The words of loss prevention: policy, shrinkage, shoplifting, restricted area, to comply with, spot check, to enforce, to be allowed to. Structure: must, have to, should, mustn't and don't have to. Mission: walk a visitor through your building and explain every rule on the way.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Policy -- a set of rules that a company follows and expects everybody to follow
"Our policy is simple: every visitor has to sign in at the gate."
Shrinkage -- the stock a retailer loses through theft, damage or mistakes
"Shrinkage cost us almost two percent of sales last year."
Shoplifting -- stealing goods from a store while it is open
"Most shoplifting happens in the busiest hour of the day."
Restricted area -- a place where only authorized people can go
"You mustn't enter the restricted area without an escort."
To comply with -- to follow a rule, a standard or an instruction
"Every supplier has to comply with our safety standards."
Spot check -- a quick inspection done at random, without warning
"We do a spot check on two trucks every week."
To enforce -- to make sure that people really obey a rule
"A rule nobody enforces isn't a rule. It's a suggestion."
To be allowed to -- to have permission to do something
"Visitors aren't allowed to take photos inside the warehouse."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Welcome to the distribution center. Every visitor has to sign in at the gate, and inside the warehouse you have to wear a yellow vest. Those are not my rules. They are the policy, and they apply to everybody, including me. You mustn't take photos near the docks, and you mustn't stand behind a truck. Those two are closed doors.

Other things are open. In the office area you don't have to wear safety boots: you can, but nobody will ask you to. And some things are only a good idea. You should keep your badge where people can see it, because the security team will stop you if they cannot. One more thing: must has no past. Last week a driver refused a spot check, and we had to stop his truck for two hours.

1. What does you don't have to wear safety boots mean?
A Nobody is allowed to wear safety boots in the office area, not even visitors.
B Boots are not necessary in the office, but you are free to wear them.
C You should wear boots in the office.
2. Which two things are forbidden near the docks?
A Taking photos and standing behind a truck.
B Wearing a vest and showing an ID.
C Signing in and keeping the badge visible.
3. Why does the text say we had to stop his truck and not we must stop?
A Because had to sounds more polite and more friendly when you talk to a visitor.
B Because it was not really necessary.
C Because must has no past form, so a past obligation takes had to.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Modals of Obligation

GRAMMAR

Four kinds of door: closed, necessary, open, and only a good idea. Each one has its own word, and visitors listen for the difference.

FormUse it forExample
have to / has toA rule from outside: the company, the policy, the law.Every visitor has to sign in.
mustA strong obligation, often written, or one the speaker feels personally.I must call him back today.
mustn't / not allowed toForbidden. The door is closed.You mustn't take photos near the docks.
don't have toNot necessary. You are free to do it or not.You don't have to wear boots in the office.
shouldAdvice. A good idea, not a rule.You should keep your badge visible.
had toThe past of both must and have to.Last week we had to stop a truck.

No to after must, mustn't or should: you must wear, never you must to wear. And in questions, use have to: Do I have to sign?

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are explaining the rules of your building to a visitor. Choose the best thing to say.

A visitor asks if she needs safety boots in the office. She does not. You say:
A You mustn't wear safety boots in the office, only in the warehouse area.
B You must to wear safety boots only in the warehouse.
C You don't have to wear them in the office, only in the warehouse.
A visitor starts taking photos of the docks. You say:
A I'm afraid you aren't allowed to take photos here.
B You don't have to take photos here, it is the policy of the company.
C You should not to take photos here.
You want to talk about a rule you followed last week. You say:
A Last week we must stop a truck.
B Last week we had to stop a truck.
C Last week we have to stop a truck.
A colleague asks for your opinion, not a rule. You say:
A You have to talk to the supervisor.
B You mustn't talk to the supervisor before you talk to me first.
C You should talk to the supervisor first.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think about one real building you are responsible for: a store, the distribution center or the head office. Record about ninety seconds. Imagine a visitor who has never been there. Explain what they have to do when they arrive, two things they mustn't do, one thing they don't have to worry about, and one piece of advice. Then tell them one rule you had to enforce in the past, and why. Use at least four words from this lesson. Do not stop to correct yourself.
