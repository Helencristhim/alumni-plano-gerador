Grammar Discovery

Strong or soft?

She should buy the ticket early.
She could stay in Cambridge.
You could take a short walk at lunch.
Why don't you talk to your boss?

What do the orange words have in common?

should = strong · could and why don't you = soft

Should: I think this is the right thing. Could: this is one idea — you can choose. Why don't you ...?: a friendly idea, not a real question. After all three: the simple verb, no to.

The Code

Three ways to help

How it works

You should + verb — strong
You shouldn't + verb — a bad idea
You could + verb — one idea
Why don't you + verb? — friendly
Answer: That's a good idea!

Never

She should to buy the ticket.
no to: should buy
You could takes the subway.
simple verb: could take
Why you don't talk to him?
Why don't you talk ...?

Question: What should I do? — You could take the subway.

Sound Check

"could" has no L

You could take the subway.

"could" sounds like "good" with a K

You should buy the ticket early.

"should": no L here too

Why don't you talk to your boss?

"don't you" together, like "DON-chu"

Ask Mark

A trip to Boston

E
Érica: Mark, can I ask you something? My daughter wants to visit Boston in December.
M
Mark: Great idea! But December is very cold. She should take a big coat.
E
Érica: Ha, yes! And hotels in Boston are expensive. That's a problem.
M
Mark: She could stay in Cambridge. It's near Boston, and it's cheaper.
E
Érica: Good idea. And the plane ticket?
M
Mark: She should buy it early. In October, tickets are cheaper.
E
Érica: Okay. She wants to save money.
M
Mark: Then she shouldn't take taxis. Why don't you send her my number? I can help.
E
Érica: Thank you, Mark! That's great advice.
M
Mark: You're welcome. And you could come too!
Did You Understand?

Mark's advice

1. For Mark, what is Boston like in December?
It's very cold. She should take a big coat.
2. Where could the daughter stay? Why?
In Cambridge. It's near Boston, and it's cheaper.
3. When are the tickets cheaper, for Mark?
In October. She should buy the ticket early.
