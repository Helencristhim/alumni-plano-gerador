Lesson 13 -- Pre-class

The Verbs That Run the Close

The two-part verbs of every closing call, and where the object goes. Key words: carry out, hand over, bring up, look into, get back to, go over, put together, point out, sort out, come up with. Structure: phrasal verbs -- separable (hand it over) and inseparable (look into it).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To carry out -- to do a planned piece of work, like a review or a test, from start to finish
"We carry out a full review of the accruals before every sign-off."
To hand over -- to give a document or a piece of work to the person who will take it from now on
"We hand over the working papers to the auditors on Friday."
To bring up -- to start talking about a subject in a meeting, so that people discuss it
"I would like to bring up a concern about the timeline."
To look into -- to try to find out why something happened or what is wrong
"I am looking into the difference in account 4100."
To get back to -- to answer somebody later, when you have the information they asked for
"I will get back to you with the numbers by Thursday."
To go over -- to look at something again, carefully, usually with another person
"Can we go over the figures before the call?"
To put together -- to prepare something by collecting and organizing different parts
"I put together the talking points for the director this morning."
To point out -- to tell somebody about a fact or a problem they may not have noticed
"The auditor pointed out a missing signature on the reconciliation."
To sort out -- to solve a problem or put a situation in order
"The BPO sorted out the duplicate entries in one afternoon."
To come up with -- to think of an idea, a plan or a solution
"We came up with a workaround for the late tax file."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Two updates on the same closing call. The first: An error was identified by the auditors. It is being investigated. A response will be provided on Thursday. All correct, and all of it sounds like a report somebody is reading. The second: The auditors pointed out an error. I am looking into it, and I will get back to you on Thursday. Same facts, but now there is a person speaking.

The difference is the phrasal verb: a verb plus a small word, like out, over or into, that together mean one thing. Some of them can open in the middle. You can say hand over the papers or hand the papers over. And when the object is it or them, it has to go in the middle: hand them over, point it out. Never hand over them.

Others never open. Look into, go over and get back to keep their pieces together, so the object always comes after: I am looking into it, can we go over them? And in a formal report to the auditors, a single verb is often better: investigate, submit, raise. In the call, the two-part verb is what makes you sound like a person.

1. Why does the text say the first update sounds like a report?
A Because it has grammar mistakes that the director would notice immediately and correct in the call.
B Because it uses formal verbs and nobody is speaking in it.
C Because it is too short.
2. Where does it go with a verb that can open in the middle?
A After the whole verb, as in hand over it, because the object always comes last in English.

3. Which of these verbs never opens in the middle?
A Look into

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Where the Object Goes

GRAMMAR

It and them go in the middle -- but only if the verb opens.

TypeThe ruleExample
separable: hand over, point out, carry out, put together, sort out, bring upa noun can go after the verb or in the middlehand over the papers / hand the papers over
separable + it / themthe pronoun must go in the middlehand them over · point it out
inseparable: look into, go overthe object always comes after the whole verblook into it · go over them
three parts: get back to, come up withnever split; the object goes at the endget back to you · come up with a plan
passivethe particle stays with the participleThe review was carried out on Friday.
speaking vs. formal writingtwo-part verb in the call; one formal verb in the reportlook into = investigate · hand over = submit · bring up = raise

Never: hand over them · point out it · look the variance into · get back you
