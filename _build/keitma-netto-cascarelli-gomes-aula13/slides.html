Chapter 3: Where the Object Goes

Hand It Over, Look Into It

Two families, and one small word that moves

Grammar Discovery

Where Does It Go?

"I will hand over the working papers." → "I will hand them over."

"The auditor pointed out the error." → "The auditor pointed it out."

"I am looking into the variance." → "I am looking into it."

"Can we go over the figures?" → "Can we go over them?" — "I will get back to you."

Look at it and them. Where do they sit in the orange verbs? And in the blue ones?

TypeThe ruleExample
separable: hand over, point out, carry out, put together, sort out, bring upa noun can go after the verb or in the middlehand over the papers / hand the papers over
separable + it / themthe pronoun MUST go in the middlehand them over · point it out
inseparable: look into, go overthe object always goes after the whole verblook into it · go over them
three parts: get back to, come up withnever split; the object goes at the endget back to you · come up with a plan
in the call (speaking)the two-part verb sounds natural and directI looked into it.
in the report (formal writing)one formal verb is often betterlook into = investigate · hand over = submit · bring up = raise

In one line: with it and them, ask one question — can the verb open in the middle? If yes, the pronoun goes inside.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Thirty Minutes Before the Call

One rehearsal, two voices, and one Teams message

Dialogue

The Plan First, the Problem Second

M
The Latam call starts in thirty minutes. Can we go over the agenda before it starts?
K
Sure. There are three items, and at the end I would like to bring up a concern about the BPO timeline.
M
Good. The director will ask what the concern is, exactly. What do you tell her?
K
They have not handed over the intercompany entries yet. I asked them to hand them over by Thursday.
M
And the variance in the accruals? The auditors pointed it out last week, and she will remember.
K
I am looking into it. I will get back to you before the call with the numbers by entity.
M
Perfect. Can you put together three talking points, so she hears the plan first and the problem second?
K
I already put them together this morning. The first one is the plan: we carry out the review on Friday.
Comprehension

About Mark

1. What does Mark want to do before the call starts?
Go over the agenda with Keitma.
2. Why does Mark think the director will ask about the variance in the accruals?
Because the auditors pointed it out last week, and she will remember.
3. What does Mark ask Keitma to put together, and in what order?
Three talking points: the plan first, and the problem second.
Listening 1 · A Regional Director

A Person, Not a Report

A regional director on the updates he remembers. Sound first — no text.

1. What three things does he know from the short update?
What happened, what comes next, and when.
2. What does the other kind of update sound like?
Like a report being read out loud.
3. What is his advice about writing and speaking?
Write like a report when you write to the auditors; speak like a person when you speak to him.
Listening 2 · An Audit Senior

It Is About the Handover

An audit senior on the teams that finish on time.

1. How many clients does she visit every year?
About fifteen.
2. What do the good teams do on day one?
They put together a list of every working paper and hand it over with the dates already filled in.
3. Why do the quiet teams worry her?
They do not bring anything up, and on the last day she discovers three problems at once.
Real Document

The Teams Message

KEITMA GOMES → BPO BUENOS AIRES
Teams · Closing · Day 3 · 9:12 AM

Hi Lucia, quick update before the regional call.

The auditors pointed out a difference in the accruals, and I am looking into it this morning. I will get back to you by noon if I need anything from your side.

The intercompany entries are still open. Could you hand them over by Thursday at 6 PM? The review is carried out on Friday, so Thursday is the real deadline.

If something is stuck, please bring it up today, not on Thursday night. We can sort it out together.

Note: four short paragraphs, six two-part verbs, one passive.
1. What is Keitma looking into this morning?
The difference in the accruals that the auditors pointed out.
2. Why is Thursday the real deadline?
Because the review is carried out on Friday.
3. What does she ask Lucia to do if something is stuck?
To bring it up today, not on Thursday night, so they can sort it out together.
