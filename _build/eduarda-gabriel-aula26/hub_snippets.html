Lesson 26 -- Pre-class

Valuation Language -- Multiples, DCF, Comparables

The valuation call: Eduarda explains to Victor why Mark's offer of ninety million is too low. Key words: valuation, multiple, comparables, cash flow, discount rate, premium, undervalued, ballpark figure. Structure: defining relative clauses (a number that... / a buyer who... / a company whose... / the market where...).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Valuation -- an estimate of how much a company is worth
"Our valuation of Lane Packaging is close to a hundred million dollars."
Multiple -- a number that shows how many times its yearly profit a company is worth
"Similar companies sold at a multiple of nine times their profit."
Comparables -- similar companies that you use to check a price
"The comparables tell us what buyers paid for companies like Lane."
Cash flow -- the money that comes into and goes out of a business
"Lane has a strong and stable cash flow."
Discount rate -- the rate you use to turn future money into its value today
"A higher discount rate gives a lower valuation."
Premium -- an extra amount that a buyer pays above the normal price
"A strategic buyer usually pays a premium for control."
Undervalued -- worth more than the price that someone is offering
"At ninety million, we think the company is undervalued."
Ballpark figure -- a rough number, not an exact one
"Can you give me a ballpark figure before Friday?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Friday, Victor Lane calls Eduarda. He has Mark's offer of ninety million on his desk, and forty pages from his accountant. He wants to understand the valuation before he answers.

Eduarda starts with the comparables: three packaging companies that were sold last year. Buyers paid nine to ten times their profit. That number is the multiple. Lane makes eleven million a year, so the range is ninety-nine to one hundred and ten million. Then she explains the cash flow model, the one that looks at the money Lane will earn in the future. With a discount rate of ten percent, it gives a ballpark figure of about one hundred million.

Victor understands: at ninety million, his company is undervalued. And NorthPack is a buyer whose factories are close to Lane's. A buyer who needs your factories usually pays a premium.

1. What are the comparables in Eduarda's valuation?
A Lane's own sales numbers for the last three years, together with the forty pages from Victor's accountant.
B Three packaging companies that were sold last year.
C Mark's offer.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "NorthPack is a buyer whose factories are close to Lane's."
B "NorthPack is a buyer who its factories are close to the factories of Lane."
C "NorthPack is a buyer what factories are close."
3. Why should NorthPack pay a premium?
A Because Victor's accountant sent forty pages and asked for more money than the comparables show.
B Because the discount rate is ten percent.
C Because it needs Lane's factories.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Defining Relative Clauses

GRAMMAR

Use a relative clause to say exactly which person or thing you mean, or to define a term. No commas: the information is necessary.

ForUseExample
Thingsthat / whichA multiple is a number that compares price and profit.
Peoplewho / thatA buyer who needs your factories pays more.
Possessionwhose + nounA company whose factories are near Lane's.
PlaceswhereThe market where Lane sells its boxes.
Object: you can drop that(that) + subject + verbThe rate (that) we used was ten percent.
Subject: you cannot drop itthat / who + verbA number that shows... (not "a number shows")

Never: a number what compares · the buyer which pays · the comparables that we used them
