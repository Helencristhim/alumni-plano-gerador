Chapter 3: The Code

From the Cause to the Consequence

Because of, due to, which is why — and which way the arrow points

Grammar Discovery

A Noun or a Sentence?

"Our schedules were late due to weak planning."

"The foremen trusted her because she spent eight weeks on their sites."

"Two foremen resigned, which is why I sat in on her meetings."

"The Monday review led to fewer delays."

Look at what comes after each word in red. After some of them there is only a noun. After others there is a full sentence, with its own subject and verb. Which is which — and in the last one, which comes first, the cause or the consequence?

ToolWhat followsExample
because of / due toa noun or -ing — never a full sentenceThe delays were due to weak planning.
because / since / asa full clause (subject + verb)Since the foremen resisted, I sat in on her meetings.
which is whya clause, after a comma; it points back to the whole sentence before itTwo foremen resigned, which is why onboarding takes eight weeks.
as a result / consequentlya new sentence, with a comma after itAs a result, turnover dropped.
lead to / result incause first, then the consequence (noun or -ing)The review led to fewer delays.
result fromconsequence first, then the causeThe delays resulted from weak planning.

In one line: because of and due to take a noun, because takes a sentence; lead to and result in go forward, result from goes back.

Grammar Practice

Link the Chain

Say the sentence first, then click to compare

"Weak planning caused the delays." (due to) ___The delays were due to weak planning.
"The foremen resisted. I sat in on her meetings." (which is why) ___The foremen resisted, which is why I sat in on her meetings.
"The Monday review reduced our delays." (lead to) ___The Monday review led to fewer delays.
"Turnover dropped. Our site knowledge stayed in the company." (as a result) ___Turnover dropped. As a result, our site knowledge stayed in the company.
"A bad cultural fit caused the two resignations." (result from) ___The two resignations resulted from a bad cultural fit.
Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Question, at Full Speed

One conversation, two recordings, one memo

Dialogue

The Hire From Outside the Family

L
Your file says you rebuilt the planning side of the company. Tell me about the team you built, and start with the hardest decision.
F
The hardest decision was a hire. In 2019 I brought in a planning engineer from outside the family, and I put her above four foremen who had been with us for twenty years.
L
Why her, and not one of them?
F
None of them had the skill set. Our schedules were always late, and the delays were due to planning, not to the work on site.
L
How did the foremen react?
F
Badly, at first. Two of them stopped talking to her, which is why I onboarded her myself and sat in on her meetings for two months.
L
And what did that hire lead to, in the end?
F
Our delays fell by a third in the first year. As a result, the foremen started asking her for the schedule, and staff turnover on the sites dropped too.
Comprehension

About Laura

1. What does Laura ask him to start with?
The hardest decision he made while building the team.
2. After he names the hire, what does she want to know?
Why he chose that engineer and not one of the foremen who were already there.
3. What is her last question about?
What the hire led to in the end — the consequence, not the decision itself.
Listening 1 · An Interviewer

Not an Organization Chart

An admissions interviewer on what the team question is really asking. Sound first — no text.

1. What do most candidates give her, and why is it not enough?
An organization chart. It tells her the size of the team, and nothing else.
2. What does she think when a candidate says nobody ever left his team?
She does not believe him, or she suspects that he never asked anybody to change.
3. What detail does she ask for, and what does it tell her?
One person by name and what that person is doing today. It tells her whether the candidate built the team or only inherited it.
Listening 2 · A Director

The Perfect Hire Who Did Not Fit

A construction director on a hire he was proud of, and what it cost him. Sound first — no text.

1. Where did he find the engineer, and what did he pay him?
He poached him from their biggest competitor and paid him almost twice what his other engineers earned.
2. What happened because of the way the engineer worked?
Two younger engineers resigned in the same month, and the team lost more knowledge than he had brought in.
3. What does every senior candidate do now, and what has it led to?
A full day on a site with the team before the offer. In five years they have not had to replace a single senior hire.
Real Document

The Memo to the Partners

MEMO — The Planning Team, Twelve Months On
From: Flavio Gattaz, Managing Partner · To: The Partners

Where we started. Our sites were late on seven of nine projects in 2018. The delays were due to planning, not to the work on site.

What we changed. We hired a head of planning from outside the company and moved the schedule review to every Monday morning.

What it led to. Three of nine projects were late in 2020. Two foremen resigned in the first months, which is why onboarding now takes eight weeks, not two.

Next step. Groom one of our site engineers to run the Monday review, so that the result no longer depends on one person.

Internal memo · Partners’ meeting · March
1. According to the memo, what were the delays due to?
Planning — not the work on site.
2. What happened in the first months, and what did it lead to?
Two foremen resigned, which is why onboarding now takes eight weeks instead of two.
3. Why does he want to groom a site engineer?
So that the result no longer depends on one person — so the head of planning does not become a bottleneck.
