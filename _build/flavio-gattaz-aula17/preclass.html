Lesson 17 -- Pre-class

The Team You Built -- Decisions and What They Led To

The panel does not want an organization chart. It wants the chain: why you chose each person, and what each choice led to. Key words: a hire, to onboard somebody, staff turnover, a skill set, to retain somebody, a cultural fit, to groom somebody for something, to pull your weight, cohesion, to poach somebody, a bottleneck, to bring out the best in somebody. Structure: cause and consequence -- because of and due to with a noun, because with a sentence, as a result, which is why, lead to, result in and result from.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A hire -- a person who has just joined a company, or the decision to employ that person
"Our best hire that year was a planning engineer from outside the family."
To onboard somebody -- to help a new employee learn the job and fit in during the first weeks
"I onboarded her myself, one site at a time, for eight weeks."
Staff turnover -- the rate at which people leave a company and have to be replaced
"Staff turnover on our sites fell by half in two years."
A skill set -- the particular group of abilities that a person has
"None of the foremen had the skill set to plan a whole project."
To retain somebody -- to keep an employee and stop that person from leaving
"Paying more did not retain our best engineers. Giving them real projects did."
A cultural fit -- a person whose values and way of working match those of the company
"He was brilliant on paper, but he was not a cultural fit."
To groom somebody for something -- to prepare somebody over a long time for a bigger role
"I spent two years grooming her for the partners' meeting."
To pull your weight -- to do your fair share of the work in a group
"On a small team, everybody can see who is not pulling his weight."
Cohesion -- the quality of a group whose members work well together and support each other
"The cohesion of that team came from one bad year we got through together."
To poach somebody -- to persuade somebody who works for another company to come and work for you
"A competitor tried to poach our head of planning twice."
A bottleneck -- a point where work slows down because too much depends on one person or one step
"When every schedule waited for one man, he became a bottleneck."
To bring out the best in somebody -- to help somebody show the best of his or her abilities
"A good foreman brings out the best in the young engineers."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Ask a director about a team he built and most of them describe an organization chart. The panel is not interested in the chart. It wants the chain behind it: why you made each decision, and what each decision led to. English gives you two kinds of tools for that chain, and Brazilian speakers often mix them up. Because of and due to are followed by a noun: the delays were due to weak planning. Because, since and as are followed by a full sentence: because the planning was weak. Never "because of the planning was weak".

For the consequence, as a result starts a new sentence: Our delays fell by a third. As a result, the foremen started to trust her. Which is why does the same job inside one sentence, after a comma, and it points back to everything before it: two foremen resigned, which is why onboarding now takes eight weeks. Be careful with the verbs. Lead to and result in go from the cause to the consequence: the Monday review led to fewer delays. Result from goes the other way, from the consequence back to the cause: the resignations resulted from a bad cultural fit.

The most important rule is not grammatical. Every decision in your answer should be followed by what it led to. A candidate who lists names sounds like a man who inherited a team. A candidate who connects each decision to its consequence sounds like a man who built one.

1. According to the text, what follows "because of" and "due to"?
A A full sentence, with a subject and a verb.
B A noun, never a full sentence.
C The phrase "which is why".
2. What is the difference between "lead to" and "result from"?
A There is no difference; they mean the same thing.
B "Lead to" goes from the cause to the consequence; "result from" goes from the consequence back to the cause.
C "Result from" is only used in writing.
3. According to the last paragraph, what makes a candidate sound like he built the team?
A Connecting each decision to its consequence.
B Listing the name of every person on the team.
C Describing the organization chart in detail.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Cause and Consequence

GRAMMAR

Because of and due to take a noun. Because takes a sentence. Lead to goes forward; result from goes back.

ToolWhat followsExample
because of / due toa noun or -ingThe delays were due to weak planning.
because / since / asa full clauseBecause the planning was weak, we were late.
which is whyafter a comma; points back to the whole sentenceTwo foremen resigned, which is why onboarding takes eight weeks.
as a result / consequentlystarts a new sentenceAs a result, turnover dropped.
lead to / result incause → consequenceThe review led to fewer delays.
result fromconsequence ← causeThe resignations resulted from a bad fit.

Never: because of the planning was weak · due to we hired her · what is why · resulted to fewer delays · the delays resulted in weak planning (wrong direction)

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The panel asks about the team you built. Which opening is the strongest?
A "I had twelve engineers and three foremen."
B "In 2019 I hired a head of planning from outside the family, because our delays were due to planning."
C "I have always been a people person."
You want to link a cause, a noun, to a result. You say:
A "The delays were because of the planning was weak."
B "The delays were due to weak planning."
C "The delays were due to the planning was weak."
The interviewer asks: "Did anybody leave?" You say:
A "No, nobody ever leaves my teams."
B "Two foremen resigned in the first months, which is why onboarding now takes eight weeks."
C "Some people left, but that was their problem."
She asks what the hire led to. Which answer gives her a consequence?
A "It was a very good decision, I think."
B "Our delays fell by a third, and the foremen now ask her for the schedule."
C "I felt that the team was happier."
