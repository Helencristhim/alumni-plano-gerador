Chapter 3: A, An and Some

Can You Count It?

a · an · some

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I would like a salad."

"I would like an orange juice."

"Can we have some bread?"

"Can we have some water?"

WordWhenExample
a + one thing you can countthe word starts with a consonantI would like a salad.
an + one thing you can countthe word starts with a vowel soundI would like an orange juice.
some + things you cannot countbread, water, rice, coffeeCan we have some bread?
some + two or more you can countplural, no number givenCan we have some napkins?

Ask yourself: can I count it? One = a / an. Cannot count, or plural with no number = some. And it is an only before a vowel SOUND.

Dialogue

She Orders for Everybody

S
Good evening. Do you have a reservation?
P
Good evening. Yes. A table for four, in the name of Shimada.
S
Perfect, table nine. Here is the menu. Can I get you anything to drink?
P
Some water for the table, please. And two orange juices for the children.
S
Lovely. Are you ready to order, or shall I give you a few more minutes?
P
Sorry, I don't understand. Can you speak more slowly, please?
S
Of course. Do you want to order now, or later?
P
Now, please. One salad, two pasta and one fish. And can we have some bread?
S
Of course, the bread is free. Anything else?
P
Yes. My son is allergic to nuts. Does the pasta have nuts in it?
S
The pasta is fine. Only the chocolate cake has nuts, so no dessert from that one.
P
Thank you. Later, two coffees and the check, please.
S
Certainly. I bring the drinks now, and the food in fifteen minutes.
Comprehension

What Did Sam Say?

1. Which table, and what is free?
Table nine. The bread (and the water for the table) is free.
2. What does Sam say about the nuts?
The pasta is fine. Only the chocolate cake has nuts.
3. When does the food arrive?
In fifteen minutes. The drinks come now.
