Lesson 28 -- Pre-class

Term Sheets and LOIs -- Reading and Discussing

NorthPack sends the letter of intent, and Eduarda walks Victor through the term sheet, clause by clause. Key words: letter of intent, term sheet, purchase price, escrow, warranty, indemnity, cap, closing date. Structure: legal modals of obligation and permission (shall / may not / is entitled to / is required to).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Letter of intent -- a letter in which a buyer says that it plans to buy a company, and on what terms
"NorthPack sent the letter of intent on Monday morning."
Term sheet -- a short list of the main terms of a deal, before the final contract
"The term sheet is only two pages long."
Purchase price -- the total amount that the buyer agrees to pay for the company
"The purchase price is one hundred and five million dollars."
Escrow -- money that a third party holds until certain conditions are met
"Ten million will stay in escrow for eighteen months."
Warranty -- a promise from the seller that certain facts about the company are true
"Victor gives a warranty that the accounts are correct."
Indemnity -- a promise to pay for a loss if a certain problem happens
"If the supplier wins the case, the indemnity covers the cost."
Cap -- the maximum amount that someone has to pay
"The cap on the indemnity is twenty percent of the price."
Closing date -- the day when the deal is completed and the money is paid
"The closing date is the thirty-first of March."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

After two weeks in the data room, NorthPack sends Victor a letter of intent with a two-page term sheet. The first clause says: the buyer shall pay a purchase price of one hundred and five million on the closing date, March thirty-first.

Victor is worried about the second clause. Ten million shall be held in escrow for eighteen months. If Lane loses the supplier case, NorthPack is entitled to take the cost from that money. Victor is required to give a warranty about the accounts, and the indemnity has a cap of twenty percent of the price.

Eduarda explains that most of the term sheet is not binding. Only one clause is binding now: Victor may not talk to other buyers for sixty days.

1. How long will the ten million stay in escrow?
A Eighteen months.
B Until the closing date at the end of March, when NorthPack pays the full price.
C Sixty days.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "NorthPack is entitled take the cost from the money in escrow if Lane loses the case."
B "NorthPack is entitled to take the cost from the escrow."
C "NorthPack shall to take the cost."
3. Which clause is binding now?
A The purchase price.
B The cap on the indemnity.
C The clause that says Victor may not talk to other buyers.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Legal Modals: Obligation, Right, Prohibition

GRAMMAR

Contracts use a few small words to say who has to do what. Shall, may and must take the base verb, with no "to". Is entitled and is required take "to" + verb.

MeaningIn the contractIn plain English
ObligationThe Buyer shall pay the price at closing.NorthPack has to pay.
ObligationThe Seller is required to give warranties.Victor has to promise that the facts are true.
RightThe Buyer is entitled to claim under the indemnity.NorthPack has the right to ask for money.
PermissionThe Seller may stay as chairman for one year.Victor can stay, if he wants to.
ProhibitionThe Seller may not talk to other buyers.Victor can't talk to other buyers.

Never: shall to pay · is entitled take · doesn't may talk
