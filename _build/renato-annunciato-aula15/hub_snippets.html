Lesson 15 -- Pre-class

Reporting Progress -- Weekly and Monthly Updates

Give a weekly update in four lines and report what clients and partners told you. Key words: status update, on track, behind schedule, overdue, highlight, blocker, to flag, action item, to keep someone posted, to touch base, to report back, next steps. Structures: reported speech (statements) with say and tell (she said she was happy, he told me it would arrive late), and the four lines of an update.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the definition. Four say where the work is, four say what you report, four keep the conversation going.

Status update -- a short report on where a project or task is now
"I send my status update every Monday at nine."
On track -- moving forward as planned, with no big problems
"The Curitiba project is on track this week."
Behind schedule -- later than the plan says it should be
"The training is two weeks behind schedule."
Overdue -- not done or not paid by the date it was needed
"The visit report from Joinville is overdue."
Highlight -- the best or most important part of something
"The highlight of the week was the visit to Doctor Costa."
Blocker -- a problem that stops the work from moving forward
"The late shipment is our only blocker."
To flag -- to point out a problem so other people notice it
"I want to flag a risk with the distributor."
Action item -- a task that one person agreed to do, often by a date
"The action item is on me, by Friday."
To keep someone posted -- to give someone regular news about something
"I will keep you posted on the shipment."
To touch base -- to make short contact with someone to check how things are
"Let us touch base again next Monday."
To report back -- to come back later with the information someone asked for
"I will call the supply team and report back by Friday."
Next steps -- the actions that will happen after this point
"The next steps are a call on Tuesday and a visit on Thursday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read Renato's Monday update and answer the questions. Notice the words in bold: they report what other people said.

Every Monday, Renato sends a short status update to Megan Ross, from global commercial operations. This week, the Porto Alegre project is mostly on track. The highlight was a joint visit to Doctor Costa: she said she was happy with the visit, but she told us she needed a month to finish her stock from the competitor.

There is one blocker, and Renato flags it early. The distributor told him the November shipment would arrive two weeks late. The second clinic is fine: the owner said they had a training day the following week, and he invited one of Renato's consultants.

At the end, Renato writes the next steps, with a name and a date: he will call the supply team today and report back by Friday. Megan answers in two minutes. She says it is exactly what she needs.

1. Doctor Costa said: "I am happy with the visit." How does Renato report it?
A "She said she was happy with the visit."
B "She said me that she is happy with the visit, and that she wants to see us again soon."
C "She told that she was happy."
2. Why does Renato write "the distributor told me" and not "the distributor said me"?
A Because said is only used for good news, and told is used when somebody gives you bad news about a delivery.
B Because the distributor is a company.
C Because tell is followed by a person, and say is not.
3. The distributor said: "The shipment will arrive late." In the update, the verb will becomes:
A was going to be arriving, because a report always needs the continuous form of the verb
B would
C wills
4. What is the main problem in Renato's week?
A Doctor Costa was not happy with the joint visit.
B The second clinic cancelled its training day, so the consultant cannot go there next week.
C The November shipment will arrive two weeks late.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech (Statements)

GRAMMAR

When you report what someone said in the past, the verb usually moves one step back in time. Say is followed by what was said; tell is followed by a person.

They saidYou reportExample
"I am happy."am / is → wasShe said she was happy.
"I need a month."present → pastShe told us she needed a month.
"It will arrive late."will → would · can → couldHe told me it would arrive late.
"We have finished."have done / did → had doneHe said they had finished.
Negativethe same step backShe said she did not need more samples.
say or tell?say (that) + sentence · tell + person (that) + sentence: She told me that the plan was ready.
Two trapsNever she said me — it is she told me. And never she told that — tell needs a person: she told us that.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

Megan writes: "Give me your update." Your first line is:
A "On Monday I had some calls, and on Tuesday I visited a clinic, and on Wednesday I wrote a report."
B "This week, the Porto Alegre project is on track."
C "Nothing special."
A doctor told you she was happy with your visit. In the update, you write:
A "Doctor Costa said she was happy with the visit."
B "The visit went well, I think, and in my opinion the doctor liked it very much."
C "Doctor Costa said me she is happy."
The shipment will be two weeks late. When do you tell Megan?
A At the end of the month, when you are sure about the exact date and you have a full plan.
B Never.
C Today, in the first half of the update.

A on track
B overdue
C a highlight of the week, because it shows that the team is busy with many other important things
Megan asks: "Who is the action item on?" You say:
A "It is on me. I will report back by Friday."

A "Goodbye forever."
