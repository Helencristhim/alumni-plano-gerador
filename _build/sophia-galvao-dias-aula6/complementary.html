Film
Diary of a Wimpy Kid (2010)

Greg spends the whole film comparing his new school with everything he expected it to be, and he is wrong about almost all of it. Short scenes, clear American English, and the exact situation you read about in this lesson.

Tip: every time Greg compares two things, say his sentence again with a size word in it — far, slightly, considerably. Ten of those and the structure is yours.

Podcast
But Why: A Podcast for Curious Kids — "Why Do We Have To Go To School?"

Twenty-five minutes in which a sociologist and a teacher of the year answer questions sent in by students: why school is compulsory, why there are twelve grades, why teachers are called by their last name. Slow, clear American English written for your age.

Tip: the answers compare school systems all the way through. Write down three comparisons you hear, then say each one out loud with a different size word in front.

YouTube
BBC Learning English — 6 Minute English: "Which country has the best schools?"

Six minutes of two presenters comparing school systems in different countries — how long the day is, how much homework there is, how strict the rules are. Everything they say is on the screen while they say it.

Tip: count how many times they put a word in front of a comparative instead of leaving it alone. Then answer their opening question yourself, in four sentences.
