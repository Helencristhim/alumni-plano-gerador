Lesson 17 -- What Might Have Happened

Talk
Julia Galef -- "Why you think you're right -- even if you're wrong" (TED, 12 min)

She opens with the Dreyfus affair: a whole army that jumped to conclusions about one man, and the officer who finally followed the evidence. Then she explains the difference between the soldier mindset and the scout mindset. It is this lesson in one talk.

Tip: as she tells the Dreyfus story, pause and say what the officers must have believed, and what they can't have checked.

Talk
Scott Fraser -- "Why eyewitnesses get it wrong" (TED, 19 min)

A forensic psychologist rebuilds a shooting in Los Angeles from the evidence and shows how two honest witnesses sent an innocent young man to prison. Clear, careful English from a courtroom expert.

Tip: each time he shows a new piece of evidence, say one sentence with must have, might have or can't have before he explains it.

Podcast
What It's Like to Be... with Dan Heath -- "A Forensic Accountant"

A real forensic accountant on how he catches executives who steal from their own companies: fake invoices, missing money and the paper trail that gives people away. The real version of the second listening in this lesson.

Tip: the page has a full transcript. Find three moments where he forms a theory before he has proof, and say each one with might have.
