Chapter 3: The Code

Two Little Words: For and Since

The structure behind every answer to “how long?”

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Answers Share?

"We have been here for two months."

"I have lived on this block since February."

"Greg has known this street since 1998."

"I have not met the family next door yet."

The red part is always the same machine: have or has plus a past participle. The blue words are not the same, and the difference is the whole point.

UseFormExample
Started before, still truehave / has + past participleWe have been here two months.
for+ a length of timefor two months, for twenty years, for a week
since+ a point in timesince February, since 1998, since we arrived
QuestionHow long + have / has + subject + participleHow long have you lived here?
Negativehave / has not + participle (+ yet)I have not met them yet.
The trapNever the simple presentNot I am here since February.

In one line: have plus participle, then FOR a length and SINCE a starting point.

Grammar Practice

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Street, at Full Speed

One neighbor, three recordings, one flyer

Dialogue

Walking the Dog, Eight Thirty

G
Morning. You folks just moved in, right? I am Greg, I am right across the street.
G
Hi, I am Gisele. Yes, we are the newcomers. We have been here for about two months.
G
Two months already. How are you settling in?
G
Slowly. I am still getting my bearings, honestly. Everything takes me twice as long.
G
That is normal. I have lived on this block since 1998 and the new grocery store still beats me. So what brought you here?
G
My husband was transferred. He works for Amazon, and the whole relocation happened in six weeks.
G
Six weeks. No wonder you are swamped. Listen, if you ever run into me out here, stop and talk. Nobody on this street should keep to themselves.
G
I would like that. I have not met many people yet, and small talk is exactly what I need to practice.
Comprehension

About Greg

1. How long has Greg lived on the block, and where exactly is his house?
Since 1998, and his house is right across the street.
2. What does Greg admit still gives him trouble after all those years?
The new grocery store. He says it still beats him.
3. What does Greg ask her to do, and what is his opinion about the street?
To stop and talk if she runs into him outside. He thinks nobody on the street should keep to themselves.
Listening 1 · A Neighbor

The Mistake She Made in Month One

A woman who moved here from another state, and what finally turned it around. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What mistake does she say she made at the beginning?
She kept to herself. She stayed inside, unpacked boxes, and told herself she was too swamped to go out and meet anybody.
2. What finally helped her, and why did it work?
The dog. Walking it, she ran into the same four people every morning, and after a while the small talk turned into a real conversation.
3. How long did it take her to get her bearings, and how long before it felt like home?
About six months to get her bearings, and probably a year before it felt like home.

Listening 3 · An Opinion

Why the First Three Months Are the Worst

Somebody who has done this before, answering a question she gets all the time. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is the answer she always gives to the question?
Longer than you want, and less time than you think.
2. Why are the first three months the worst, in her opinion? Give two reasons.
Everything automatic suddenly costs energy — buying shampoo or making a phone call becomes a project. And you have no small talk yet, because small talk runs on shared references you do not have.
3. What changes it, and what is her example of the moment you notice?
Not the language, but repetition. One day you run into somebody you know at the store, have a boring conversation about the weather, and realize you have started to settle in.
