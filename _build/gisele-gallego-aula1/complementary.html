Complementary Activities

Optional extras to enjoy outside class. Mark each one as done after you watch or listen to it.

Lesson 1 -- Who I Am and Why I Am Here

Film
Brooklyn (2015), directed by John Crowley

A young woman leaves a small town in Ireland for New York, and spends the whole film in the exact place you are in right now: half here, half there, learning a street she did not choose. The dialogue is slow, clear and almost entirely about the things this lesson is about — who you are, how long you have been here, and why you came.

Tip: watch the first thirty minutes with English subtitles and count how many times somebody asks her a how long question. You will hear the present perfect in real speech before you notice you are studying.

Podcast
NPR Life Kit -- "How to make friends after a move"

Twenty minutes of American English on the one problem you have this month: you live on a street full of people and you do not know any of them. Two guests explain how long it normally takes, why the first months feel wrong, and what actually works. The page has the audio and the full transcript side by side.

Tip: listen once without the transcript, then read the transcript while it plays a second time. Write down three expressions you would use on your own sidewalk.

Talk
Pico Iyer -- "Where is home?" (TEDGlobal 2013)

A writer who has three or four origins asks the question every neighbor on your block will ask you: where are you from? Fourteen minutes, a calm and very clear speaker, and an answer to what home means when you live in a country that is not the one you grew up in.

Tip: turn the subtitles on and set the speed to 0.75x the first time. Then decide how you would answer his question in one sentence, and say it out loud.
