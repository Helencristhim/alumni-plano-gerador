Lesson 15 -- Pre-class

Presenting Dataside’s Future -- The Five-Part Pitch

Investors buy the future. A pitch that spends four minutes on the past and thirty seconds on the plan sounds like a goodbye. Key words: a value proposition, a growth driver, a target market, to outline, to position, going forward, to expand into, a long-term goal. Structure: will, be going to and the present continuous for the future.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Many founders pitch like historians. They spend four minutes on the past (fifteen years, three hundred people, the first client) and thirty seconds on the future. But an investor is not buying the past. She is buying what the company is going to become, and she wants to hear it in a clear structure.

A good pitch has five parts: who we are, what we have done, where we are going, why this deal, and your questions. The first two parts use tenses you already know: Dataside is a data company, we have grown every year since 2015. The third part is where the future lives, and English has three ways to talk about it.

Use be going to for a plan you have already decided: we are going to expand into Mexico next year. Use the present continuous for something already arranged, with a date or a contract: we are opening an office in Austin in March. Use will for a prediction or a promise: this market will double, I will stay as CEO for three years.

The difference matters to a buyer. We are opening an office sounds signed. We are going to open an office sounds decided. We will open an office sounds like a promise, so be ready to back it up. Choose the form that says exactly how sure you are.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Talking About the Future

GRAMMAR

Three forms, three levels of certainty. Choose the one that says how sure you are.

UseFormExample
A plan you have decidedbe going to + verbWe are going to expand into Mexico next year.
Something already arrangedpresent continuous + timeWe are opening an office in Austin in March.
A predictionwill + verbThis market will double in five years.
A promise or offerwill + verbI will stay as CEO for three years.
A decision made nowwill (often ’ll)Good question. I’ll send you the numbers today.
Negativewon’t · am not going toWe won’t raise prices. We aren’t going to hire this year.
QuestionWill…? · Are you going to…?Are you going to keep the team? Will you stay?

Remember: after will, the base verb (it will grow, never it will grows). After going to, the base verb too (we are going to expand).
