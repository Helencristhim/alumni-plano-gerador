The Pattern

How sure is the person speaking?

You should talk to Marta about that. (I am recommending it)
It might be worth asking her. (maybe — I am not sure)
I will send you the deck tonight. (certain — I am promising)
This might sound simple, but it worked. (softening my own idea)

Should = I recommend it. Might = maybe.

Both are followed by the bare verb — no to. Should gives advice without giving an order, which is how peers talk to peers. Might lets you offer an idea without claiming it is right — and it is the safest way to disagree with someone senior at a lunch table.

Did You Catch It?

About the table

1. Why had David been avoiding the table?
Because small talk in English is harder for him than the sessions.
2. What do he and Claire discover they have in common?
They both did the same migration, and it went badly for both.
3. What two things does Claire offer?
To introduce him to Marta after lunch, and the advice that he should talk to her.
Listening 1

What the programme was actually for

1. What does she say she has forgotten?
Most of the sessions.
2. What does she still have four years later, and where did she meet them?
Six people — and she met all of them at lunch, not in a session.
3. What is her advice, and what does she admit about it?
Sit somewhere different every day — and she admits it might feel uncomfortable on day one.
Listening 2

How not to be the person with forty cards

1. What is the difference between the two people he describes?
One collects forty cards and goes home with nothing; the other has three real conversations and three people who will answer an email.
2. What should you say when a conversation ends?
Something specific about what you will do next — not "let’s keep in touch".
3. What does he say makes people good at this?
Not charm — they follow up.
