Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Gravitas -- a serious, calm authority that makes people respect you
"She spoke slowly, with real gravitas."
Poise -- calm and confident control of yourself, even under pressure
"Even when the video froze, she kept her poise."
Command the room -- to hold the attention of everyone who is listening
"You do not need to shout to command the room."
Conviction -- a strong belief that people can hear in the way you speak
"Say the last line with conviction, then stop."
Filler words -- words like um, like and you know that add nothing to a message
"Three filler words in one sentence weaken the message."
Resonate -- to feel meaningful and important to the people listening
"A real story will resonate more than a chart."
Soundbite -- a short, memorable sentence that people repeat later
"That line is a soundbite people will quote."
Rule of three -- the habit of grouping ideas in threes so they are easy to remember
"Map, diversify, rehearse: the rule of three."
Call to action -- a clear request that tells the audience what to do next
"End with a call to action for every team."
Pause for effect -- to stop speaking for a moment to make a point stronger
"After the first line, pause for effect."
Land -- to be understood and remembered in the way you wanted (a message or a point)
"Give the message a second to land."
Hook -- an opening line or image that catches attention immediately
"Your hook is the fire, not the numbers."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The board approved the dual-supplier contract, and now Priya wants Fabiana on a bigger stage: five minutes at the global operations town hall, in front of about three hundred people. Marcus helps her rehearse. Her hook is simple: last July, a fire in Penang stopped the keyboard supply for twelve days. Then she will pause for effect and let it land.

Her key line is a conclusion: "The longer we depended on one supplier, the bigger the risk became." The structure has two parts, and each part starts with the and a comparative. When the first part changes, the second changes too. It works in the present ("The more connected we are, the more fragile we become") and in a short form with no verb: "The sooner, the better."

Marcus has three notes. Watch the filler words. Use the rule of three for the close. And say the call to action with conviction: "The sooner every team maps its suppliers, the safer we all are." A talk like that will resonate, and a line like that becomes a soundbite.

1. What is the structure of "The longer we depended on one supplier, the bigger the risk became"?
A the + comparative, then the + comparative.
B a negative question.
C a conditional with if.
2. "The sooner, the better." What is special about this sentence?
A It is a question.
B It is the short form, with no verb.
C It is in the past.
3. What does Marcus tell Fabiana to use for the close?
A A long list of numbers.
B A joke.
C The rule of three and a call to action.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The More..., The More...

GRAMMAR

Two things change together. The sentence sounds like a conclusion, so it sounds like a leader.

StructureWhat it saysExample
the + comparative, the + comparativetwo things change togetherThe longer we wait, the higher the cost.
the more / the less + clausemore or less of an actionThe more we rehearse, the less we worry.
Long adjectivesthe more + adjectiveThe more connected we are, the more fragile we become.
Negative directionthe less / the fewerThe fewer filler words, the stronger the message.
Short formno verbThe sooner, the better.
Questionthe structure stays the same inside a questionIs it true that the longer we wait, the more it costs?

Never: More we wait, more it costs · The more faster we move · The sooner, better · the most confident (in the second part)

Stage 2: Put the Talk in Order

Order

Fabiana plans her five-minute town hall talk. Put her plan in order.

?"She opens with one image: last July, a fire in Penang stopped our keyboard supply for twelve days."
?"She pauses for effect and lets the image land."
?"She tells one story with three lessons: map, diversify, rehearse."
?"She gives the soundbite: the longer we depended on one supplier, the bigger the risk became."
?"She closes with a call to action: the sooner every team maps its suppliers, the safer we all are."
