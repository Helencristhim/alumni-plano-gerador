Chapter 3: The Code of Conclusions

The Code of Conclusions

One structure that makes a sentence sound final

Grammar Discovery

Two Changes, One Sentence

"The longer we depended on one supplier, the bigger the risk became."

"The more connected we are, the more fragile we become."

"The sooner every team maps its suppliers, the safer we all are."

"The fewer surprises, the better."

What do the purple words have in common? When the first part changes, what happens to the second part?

StructureWhat it saysExample
the + comparative ..., the + comparative ...two things change togetherThe longer we wait, the higher the cost.
the more / the less + clausemore or less of an actionThe more we rehearse, the less we worry.
the more + long adjectivelong adjectives use more, not -erThe more connected we are, the more fragile we become.
the fewer + plural nounfewer things you can countThe fewer suppliers, the bigger the risk.
Short formno verb, very common in speechesThe sooner, the better.
The trapalways two the; never a double comparativeNot More we wait, more it costs, not the more faster.

In one line: it sounds like a conclusion, so it sounds like a leader.

Listening 2 · A Model Speech

The Speech People Remember

Tom Becker, the COO, at last year's town hall. Sound first — no text.

1. What story does Tom Becker open with?
A container ship that got stuck in a canal, when the world's supply chains held their breath for six days.
2. How does he describe resilience?
It is not a project and not a department. It is a habit, and habits are built in quiet weeks, not in crisis weeks.
3. What is his call to action?
Before the end of the quarter, every team names one single point of failure, one person who owns it, and one plan to remove it.
