Lesson 1 -- First Words: Breaking the Silence on Day One

Series
Modern Family — season 1, episode 1 (“Pilot”) · Disney+

This is the series for lesson 1 for a technical reason: Modern Family is filmed like a documentary. Every few minutes a character looks into the camera and does exactly what you practiced today — says their name, where they are from, what they do and who they live with. They are short introductions, in everyday American English, with the face on screen (which helps a lot when you are starting to listen). In episode 1, watch Gloria in particular: she is Colombian, she speaks English with a strong accent, she gets words wrong — and she is the most confident character in the show. Nobody laughs at her English.

Tip: watch it with English subtitles. Do one thing only: every time a character introduces themselves to the camera, hit pause and write the sentence down. You will hear “My name is...”, “I'm from...” and “I work in/at...” — the three blocks from the lesson, in the mouths of real people. 20 minutes is enough.

Podcast
Espresso English Podcast — “A SUPER-easy way to introduce yourself professionally in English” (with Shayna Oliveira, ~10 min, free)

This is your lesson 1, told by another teacher, in simple, slow English. Shayna opens the episode with a line that could have come from your first meeting with us: “one of the situations that makes people most nervous is having to introduce yourself” — and she talks about the job interview, when the interviewer says “So, tell me about yourself!”. Her method is a three-part skeleton (present → past → future) that fits perfectly on top of the blocks you learned today. You do not have to understand everything — listen again, and recognize the sentences you already know.

Tip: listen ONCE without pausing, just to let your ear get used to it — and do not worry if you only catch 30%. Then listen again with your finger on the pause button, and count how many times you hear “My name is”, “I'm from” and “I work”. If the pace is too fast, use the 0.8x speed in the player. Listen in the car, at the gym, doing the dishes: the ear works on its own.

YouTube
English Greetings and Introductions — Oxford Online English (~13 min)

This video is exactly your lesson 1, with two real teachers on screen and English subtitles. They show the different ways of greeting people and introducing yourself and — the part that matters most for you — the difference between the formal introduction (the Zurich interview) and the informal one (coffee with a colleague). The pace is slow on purpose, and you can see their mouths moving, which makes a big difference at the start. Oxford Online English is one of the most serious channels online for adults: no cartoons, no school tone.

Tip: watch it once all the way through, just listening. The second time, STOP the video after every sentence and repeat it out loud — copying the intonation, not only the words. It feels strange at first and it works: you are training your mouth, and the mouth learns by repetition, not by explanation. 10 minutes a day, three days a week.
