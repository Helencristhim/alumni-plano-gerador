Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

First Words -- Breaking the Silence on Day One

Lesson 1 is a check-up and an unlock: seven words (name, city, job, English, please, thank you, slowly), three ready-made blocks to introduce yourself (My name is... / I am from... / I work in...) and the rescue kit that stops the silence (Sorry, I don't understand / Can you repeat that, please? / Can you speak slowly, please?). No new grammar to memorize today — the goal is to speak, even with mistakes. This Pre-class is only a first contact: come to class with the words in your ear, not on the tip of your tongue.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions. The words in bold are the blocks you will use in class.

Monday, two o'clock. Paula meets David. He works at Zurich.

David says: "Hello. I am David. I work here, at Zurich." Paula answers: "My name is Paula." David asks: "Where are you from?" Paula answers: "I am from Osasco, near São Paulo." Then David asks: "And what do you do?"

Paula does not understand. She does not stop. She does not speak Portuguese. She says: "Sorry? Can you repeat that, please?" David repeats: "Of course. What is your job?" Now Paula understands. She says: "Ah! I work in insurance." David smiles: "Me too. Welcome to the team, Paula."

Paula did not understand one question. Nobody laughed. Nobody was angry. The conversation continued — because Paula asked for help in English, instead of giving up.

1. Paula does not understand the question "And what do you do?". What does she do?
A She stays silent and waits for the moment to pass.
B She asks for help IN ENGLISH: "Sorry? Can you repeat that, please?" — and the conversation continues.
C She answers in Portuguese.
2. What does David do when Paula asks him to repeat?
A He gets annoyed and changes the subject.
B He repeats exactly the same sentence, but faster.
C He says "Of course" and repeats it with easier words: "What is your job?"
3. To say her name, which block does Paula use?
A "My name is..." — a ready-made block. She changes nothing: she only adds "Paula" at the end.
B "I have the name..." — a word-for-word copy of the Portuguese.
C "My name Paula" — with no verb, the way people speak in Portuguese.
4. Which sentence is correct?
A "I am work in insurance."
B "I work in insurance."
C "I working in insurance."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Ready-made blocks (chunks)

GRAMMAR

Today you change nothing. Every sentence starts with a ready-made block — you only add your information at the end. The full verb to be comes in lesson 2.

Ready-made blockFull exampleWhen you use it
My name is + your name"My name is Paula."The first thing you say to someone new.
I am from + your city"I am from Osasco, in Brazil."To answer "Where are you from?"
I work in + your field"I work in insurance."To answer "What do you do?"
I speak a little + language"I speak a little English."To warn people, before they speak too fast.
I am + age + years old"I am 43 years old."Your age — with am, never with have.
Can you + verb + please?"Can you repeat that, please?"To ask for anything politely.

Careful (the two mistakes almost everyone makes): (1) "I have 43 years" — in English your age is something you are, not something you have: "I am 43 years old"; (2) "My name Paula" — the verb is can never disappear: "My name is Paula".

The rescue kit (learn these by heart — they are the alternative to silence): "Sorry, I don't understand." · "Can you repeat that, please?" · "Can you speak slowly, please?" · "How do you say this in English?" · "I speak a little English."

Why this matters: asking someone to repeat is not failure — it is what everybody does in a conversation, in any language. If you ask, you are talking. If you stay silent, you are not.

Speaking

Listen to each sentence, then record your voice. Nobody is grading you — it is only you and your microphone. If it comes out wrong, record it again. Careful: “job” ends on the B, with no vowel after it, and the TH in “thank you” is said with the tongue between the teeth.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record your answer. Speak for 30 to 60 seconds, with no script. Do not redo it more than twice — this recording is your starting point, and in a few lessons you will listen to it again and not believe it.

Introduce yourself in English: your name, your city, your job and one sentence about your English (for example: "I want to speak English for my new job"). It does not have to be perfect. Do not stop to correct yourself. If you get stuck, use the rescue kit and keep going — getting stuck and carrying on is also speaking English.
