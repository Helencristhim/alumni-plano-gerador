Chapter 3: Bigger, Better

This One or That One?

warmer · more modern · better than

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Copenhagen is warmer than Oslo."

"The Garden Hotel is cozier than the Harbor."

"The Harbor Hotel is more modern."

"The food here is better than at home."

Look at the blue words. When do you add -er? When do you say more?

AdjectiveCompare
Short wordwarm, cheap, cleanwarmer, cheaper, cleaner
Ends in -ycozy, friendly, livelycozier, friendlier, livelier
Long wordmodern, expensivemore modern, more expensive
Differentgood, bad, farbetter, worse, farther
Two thingsCopenhagen is warmer than Oslo. — than, not that

Short word: -er. Long word: more. And between the two things: than.

The Sound

How It Sounds

"warmer than"

than is short and weak: "WARMer th'n"

"cheaper than the Harbor"

the stress goes on the adjective: CHEAper

"more modern"

more is short, MOdern is strong

"Which one is better?"

the question that starts every choice

C
So, next weekend: Copenhagen or Oslo?
L
I think Copenhagen. It is warmer than Oslo in May.
C
That's true. But Oslo is more beautiful. The mountains, the fjords...
L
Maybe. But Oslo is more expensive. A coffee there is twice the price.
C
OK, that's a good point. And Copenhagen is livelier at night.
L
Yes. Now the hotel. The Harbor or the Garden?
C

L

C
Hmm. And the Garden is seventy euros cheaper.
L
And cozier. Small hotels are friendlier, too.
C
OK, you win. The Garden Hotel. But next time, Oslo!
L
Deal. I'll book it tonight.
