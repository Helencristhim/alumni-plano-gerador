Lesson 10 -- Pre-class

Executive Currency -- Phrasal Verbs at Work

The two-word verbs native executives use in meetings. Key words: to rule out, to narrow down, to scale up, to phase out, to follow up on, to push back on, to bring up, to report back. Structure: phrasal verbs and separability -- where the object goes.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each verb and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To rule out -- to decide that something is not possible and remove it
"We ruled out the three categories where the data was too thin."
To narrow down -- to reduce a long list to a few real options
"We narrowed the list down from forty categories to six."
To scale up -- to make something much bigger after it works in one place
"We are scaling the pilot up to two more regions."
To phase out -- to stop using something slowly, over a period of time
"We are phasing out the old spreadsheet at the end of the month."
To follow up on -- to check again after a first contact, so nothing is forgotten
"She asked me to follow up on it with finance."
To push back on -- to disagree with something and ask for it to change
"I would push back on the timeline, and here is why."
To bring up -- to introduce a subject into a conversation
"I brought the sign-off delay up with the commercial director."
To report back -- to come back with the information you were asked for
"I will report back on Thursday with a date."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Team, a short update. We have narrowed the list down from forty categories to six, and we have ruled out the three where the data was too thin. Nobody should read that as a failure: ruling something out early is a result. The pilot in the south is holding, and we are scaling it up to two more regions in March. We are phasing out the old spreadsheet at the end of the month, so please stop using it now. And if you disagree with any of the six categories, bring it up this week, in the meeting. Last point: I brought the sign-off delay up with the commercial director, and she asked me to follow up on it with finance. I will report back on Thursday.

1. Why is it "we ruled it out" and never "we ruled out it"?
A A pronoun always goes in the middle of a two-word phrasal verb.
B Because "rule out" is a three-word phrasal verb.
C Because the sentence is in the past.
2. Why can we not say "follow it up on"?
A "Follow up on" has a preposition, so it never separates.
B Because the object is a pronoun, and pronouns always go last.
C Because "follow" is an irregular verb.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "We are scaling up it to two more regions."
B "We are scaling it up to two more regions."
C "We are scaling it to up two more regions."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Phrasal Verbs and Separability

GRAMMAR

One small rule, and getting it wrong is the fastest way to sound foreign.

TypeRuleExample
Separable (two words)A noun can go after the verb or in the middle.rule out the category / rule the category out
With a pronounit, them and him always go in the middle.rule it out — never "rule out it"
Three words (with a preposition)Never separate. The object comes at the end.push back on it / follow up on it
RegisterIn a written report, prefer the formal twin (eliminate, reduce, expand, raise). In a meeting, the phrasal verb is what people actually say. Both are correct.
