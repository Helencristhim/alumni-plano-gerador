Lesson 13 -- Talking About the Future: Plans and Intentions with “Going To”

Street interviews
Easy English · “Talking about the future” · Easy Languages channel (YouTube) — free · ~7 min

It is not a course: it is real people stopped in the street answering “What are you going to do?”. And it is exactly today's lesson in real life — every person answers with going to, without noticing, because that is how people speak. You will hear “I'm going to…” about twenty times in seven minutes, from different mouths and different accents. Best of all: there are English subtitles on screen the whole time, so you READ and HEAR the same sentence at once — the fastest way for the structure to stick.

Tip: watch it once only and do one thing: every time somebody says “going to”, repeat the whole sentence out loud along with them, tripping over it if you must. You do not have to understand everything (it is fast British street English) — the goal is to get your mouth through the going to without freezing. Then answer the video's question yourself — “What are you going to do this weekend?” — out loud, with a real sentence of your own. Free, no sign-up.

Espresso English — Episode 075: “Phrases for Talking about Plans and Goals” (with Shayna, American teacher, ~10 min, free)

Ten minutes, and they are exactly the sentences of today's lesson: talking about plans and goals with going to. Look at the title: phrases, not rules. Shayna does not stand there explaining grammar — she gives you ready-made “I'm going to…” sentences to use about real plans. And it is the same argument you heard from David and Sofia: people who freeze do not freeze for lack of a rule; they freeze because the sentence does not come. So here are the sentences already built for you to steal.

Tip: listen twice — it is ten minutes, it fits in the coffee queue. The first time, just listen. The second time, pause at each sentence and say your version out loud, with a real plan of your own (“I'm going to learn the new system”, “I'm not going to change jobs”). A borrowed sentence only becomes yours when you put your own plan inside it. Open and free, no sign-up: the player is on the page itself.

Six minutes from the BBC, with two presenters who speak slowly and on purpose — made for learners. You need nothing beyond what you have already seen today — and hearing the same rule in a calm British voice helps it settle. It is the safety net: if going to still feels too good to be true, here is the confirmation.

Tip: watch it with English subtitles (the video's own CC) — you already understand the subject, so use the six minutes to train your ear. Do one thing only: every time they give an example with going to, pause and say a sentence of YOUR own in the same mould. By the end of the video you should have said about five future sentences of your own out loud — those five are worth more than the whole video. Open channel, no sign-up.
