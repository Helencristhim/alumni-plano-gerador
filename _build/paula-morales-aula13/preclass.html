Lesson 13 -- Pre-class

Talking About the Future -- Plans and Intentions with “Going To”

This is the lesson where English, for the first time in thirteen lessons, plays on your side. You are going to talk about the future — about your plans — and the English future is the one structure that works exactly like your own language: “I am going to travel” maps word for word onto what you already say every day. Nothing new to fear. You take six planning words (plan, future, next, soon, learn, change) and the full going to structure: affirmative, negative and question. This Pre-class is a first contact: come to class with going to in your ear, not on the tip of your tongue.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Six words — and all of them belong to the future, to the plan. Listen to each one and repeat it out loud. The sound traps of the day: FYOO-cher · cheynj · suun (rhymes with moon) · lern (with a long R). Tap the table on the strong syllable while you repeat.

Plan -- what you are going to do
"I have a plan for next month." Say plaen, one syllable, with the open A of cat.
Future -- the time that has not come yet
"In the future, I'm going to travel more." Say FYOO-cher, stress on the first syllable — never “fu-TU-re”.
Next -- the one that comes after this one
"Next month I'm going to visit the client." Say nekst — k-s-t all stuck together at the end, with no vowel in between.
Soon -- in a short time from now
"I'm going to speak English soon." Say suun, the long U of moon — it is not “son”.
Learn -- to get new knowledge or a new skill
"I'm going to learn the new system." Say lern, with a long R — never a short clipped one.
Change -- to make something different
"I'm not going to change jobs." Say cheynj, a “ch” at the front and a soft “j” at the end — never “chan-ge”.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions. The sentences in bold are today's future — notice that they all follow the same mould: going to + bare verb.

David, from Miami, left Paula a message before the call with Greg. He said: “Today English is on your side. You are going to learn the future — and you already use it every day. I'm going to visit São Paulo is exactly the sentence you would say at home, word for word.”

He told her his plan: in April he is going to bring the new system and is going to train the whole team. And he said something Paula needed to hear: “On the call, I am not going to speak fast, and I am not going to use difficult words. I am going to talk like a normal person.”

Then Sofia sent hers: “Next month I am going to recommend you for the international desk. Not because your English is perfect — but because you ask when you do not understand. Today, think about it: where are you going to be in a year? What are you going to learn?”

1. The sentence “I'm going to visit” is the future. Where does that structure come from?
A It is a new and difficult rule, only in English.
B From the way you already speak — going to + a bare verb, exactly as you say it at home.
C From lesson 8 — the question with does.
2. How does David say he is NOT going to speak fast?
A "I'm not going to speak fast." — the not after am.
B "I don't going to speak fast."
C "I no speak fast."
3. Why is Sofia going to recommend Paula for the international desk?
A Because Paula's English is perfect.
B Because Paula asks when she does not understand — and she does not have to be perfect.
C Because Paula speaks very fast.
4. The question “Where are you going to be in one year?” throws the are to the front. Why?
A Because Sofia was in a hurry.
B Because in a question am/is/are jumps to the front — just like “Are you busy?”, from lesson 2.
C Because be is an irregular verb.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- going to is the future you already speak

GRAMMAR

Today the grammar is a gift: the English future works the same way as the one you already speak. going to + a bare verb. The only extra piece is the am / is / are in front — which you have had since lesson 2.

FormIn EnglishWhen you use it
Affirmative (I)I'm going to travel.Eu vou viajar.
Affirmative (she)She's going to learn.Ela vai aprender.
NegativeI'm not going to change.The negative — not after am.
QuestionAre you going to travel?Você vai viajar?
Wh- questionWhat are you going to do?The question — are jumps to the front.

The two mistakes that feel natural to make, and the fix for each one:

1) “She going to study.” — you swallowed the is. Your first language has no helper here, so the hand forgets it. In English every sentence needs the am/is/are: “She's going to study.” — the same is as “She is busy”, from lesson 2.

2) “I'm going to changed / learning.” — the verb has to stay bare after going to: change, never changed or changing.

Why this matters: for twelve lessons English worked differently from the language you think in — the does, the could, the how much. Today it works the same. You already know how to talk about the future; the English version was the only thing missing — and it is the same shape.

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen to each sentence, then record your voice. Nobody is grading you — it is only you and your microphone. Careful: in spoken English the two words in going to stick together into a quick “gonna” — and that is correct. The goal here is for the sentence to come out whole, without building it piece by piece, and without swallowing the am/is/are at the front.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best option for each real situation — the call, the colleague, your plan.

You want to say that you are travelling next month. You say:
A "I go travel next month."
B "I'm going to travel next month."
C "I going to travel next month."
Greg asks if you are going to help. You are NOT going to translate everything. You say:
A "I'm not going to translate everything."
B "I don't going to translate everything."
C "I no translate everything."
You want to ask Greg about his plans for April. The best sentence:
A "You going to do what in April?"
B "What are you going to do in April?"
C "What you go to do in April?"
Paula wants to talk about her colleague's plan. The colleague is “she”. You say:
A "She going to learn the system."
B "She's going to learn the system."
C "She's going to learned the system."
Sofia asks where you are going to be in a year. You answer:
A "In one year, I'm going to be on the international desk."
B "In one year, I go to be on the international desk."
C "In one year, I'm going to being on the international desk."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record your answer. Speak for 60 to 90 seconds, with no script. Do not redo it more than twice. This is exactly the homework audio — start building it here.

Sofia asked you two questions: “Where are you going to be in one year? What are you going to learn?” Answer out loud, talking about your own future — at Zurich and in life. Use at least one affirmative sentence (“I'm going to…”), one negative (“I'm not going to…”) and one that starts with a time word (Next year… / Soon… / Next month…). The only rule of this recording is: do not switch language. It does not have to be a perfect plan — it has to be yours, and in English.
