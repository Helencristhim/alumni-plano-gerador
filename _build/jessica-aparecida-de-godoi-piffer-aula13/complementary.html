Talk
Julia Galef — “Why you think you’re right — even if you’re wrong” (TED)

Twelve minutes on two ways of holding an opinion: defending it at any cost, or looking at the evidence with curiosity. It is the attitude behind a good hedge — a clear stance that stays open to nuance.

Tip: write down every marker she uses to soften, turn or conclude. Then give your own opinion on the talk in four sentences: one soft start, one turn, one point and a bottom line.

Podcast
Freakonomics Radio — “How to Change Your Mind”

Economists, psychologists and a former politician talk about why it is so hard to change an opinion, and what makes people do it. Real experts giving careful opinions, full of the markers from this lesson.

Tip: listen for “I tend to think”, “that said” and “the bottom line”. Pause after each one and repeat the sentence with the same rhythm.

Video
BBC Learning English — “Being polite: how to soften your English”

A short English Masterclass on how to make an opinion or a request sound softer without losing the message. Clear examples, natural speed, and useful for emails to head office too.

Tip: take one direct sentence from a real email of yours and rewrite it in two softer versions. Say both out loud.
