Chapter 3: The Words Between Ideas

The Words That Are Not Facts

They carry no information, and they change everything about how you sound

Grammar Discovery

What Do the Coloured Words Do?

"Let me think about that for a second. The risk is in the timing."

"I tend to think the exposure is manageable."

"The rate is stable. That said, the deadline worries me."

"The bottom line is, we need one more analyst."

Cover the coloured words. The information is still there. So what are they doing?

The jobMarkersExample
1. Buy timeLet me think about that for a second · That's a good question — I'd say... · My initial thought is...That's a good question. I'd say the timing.
2. Soften an opinionI tend to think · From where I stand · It seems to me that · I might be wrong, but · There's a case to be made forFrom where I stand, the risk is low.
3. Change directionThat said · That being said · Even so · Having said that · On the other handIt is stable. Even so, I would watch it.
4. Add and organizeTo start with · On top of that · What's more · All things consideredOn top of that, the team is small.
5. ConcludeThe bottom line is · What it comes down to is · In a nutshell · Long story shortThe bottom line is, we are within the limit.
the trapNot In my opinion, I think... — choose one. And a hedge is not a fog: after the soft start, the opinion still has to come.

In one line: the markers carry no facts. They tell the listener what you are doing with the facts — thinking, softening, turning, adding or concluding.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

After the Slides, the Real Questions

One call, two recordings, one follow-up email

Dialogue

What's Your Personal Take?

M
Thanks, Jessica, that was very clear. Before we wrap up, what's your personal take on the exposure level we're carrying?
J
That's a good question. I tend to think it's manageable, but the timing worries me more than the amount.
M
Could you elaborate on that? The number looks stable to me.
J
Sure. Initially, the reform only changes the rate. That said, the new filing rules start in the same quarter, and my team is already stretched.
M
I see. Would you say your team is well-positioned for what's coming?
J
I might be wrong, but I'd say we're about seventy percent there. On top of that, we need one more analyst before the first filing.
M
Fair enough. What would you say to someone who thinks you're being too cautious?
J
There's a case to be made for both views. But the bottom line is, a late filing costs more than one salary.
Comprehension

About Mark

1. What does Mark want to know before they wrap up?
Jessica's personal take on the exposure level they are carrying.
2. What does Mark think about the number?
He thinks it looks stable.
3. What is Mark's last question?
What she would say to someone who thinks she is being too cautious.
Listening 1 · A Communication Coach

Three Seconds of Silence

On what fluent speakers do while they think. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say most people do during a three-second silence?
They panic and start a sentence they cannot finish.
2. According to him, what do native speakers do differently?
They do not think faster; they fill the gap with phrases like that's a good question.
3. How will the phrases feel at first, and after a month?
Artificial at first; after a month nobody notices them, not even you.
Listening 2 · A Finance Director

Clear About How Certain You Are

On giving an opinion to people more senior than you. Sound first — no text.

1. What are the two ways of losing credibility she describes?
Being too certain and wrong, or being so vague that you never give an answer.
2. Where does she say the risk is?
In the timing, not in the amount.
3. What does the most senior person in the room need from you?
For you to be clear about how certain you are, not to be certain.
Real Document

The Follow-Up Email

Re: Q3 tax risk indicators — your question on exposure
From: Jessica Piffer · To: Mark · VP of Finance, Chicago

Hi Mark,

Thanks for the question at the end of today's call. I have thought it through, and here is my view in writing.

To start with, the exposure itself is stable. It seems to me that the amount is not the problem. Having said that, the new filing rules begin in the same quarter as the rate change, and my team is already working at full capacity. What's more, two of our five analysts are new.

What it comes down to is timing. I suggest hiring one more analyst before the first filing, not after it.

Happy to elaborate on any of these points.

Best,
Jessica

1. According to the email, is the amount of the exposure the problem?
No. The exposure itself is stable; the problem is the timing.
2. Which marker turns the direction of the argument?
Having said that.
3. What does she suggest, and when?
Hiring one more analyst, before the first filing and not after it.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I buy a few seconds with a phrase, not with silence: "That's a good question. Let me think about that."
I soften an opinion and still give it: "I tend to think...", "From where I stand...".
I turn an argument with "that said", "even so" or "having said that".
I finish with a clear conclusion: "The bottom line is...", "What it comes down to is...".
I know the words: a stance, nuance, to elaborate, initially, ultimately, arguably, to weigh, to think something through, to hedge, measured.
