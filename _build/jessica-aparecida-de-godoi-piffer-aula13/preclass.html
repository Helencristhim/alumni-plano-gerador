Lesson 13 -- Pre-class

Thinking Out Loud in English -- discourse markers

Giving an opinion that sounds careful, clear and senior: buying time, softening, changing direction and concluding. Key words: a stance, nuance, to elaborate, initially, ultimately, arguably, to weigh, to think something through, to hedge, measured. Structure: discourse markers and hedging language.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A stance -- a public opinion or position on a question
"Headquarters wants us to take a clear stance on the new tax rules."
Nuance -- a small difference in meaning, feeling or opinion
"Her answer was correct, but it needed a little more nuance."
To elaborate -- to explain something in more detail
"Could you elaborate on the risk you mentioned in the second slide?"
Initially -- at the beginning, before things changed
"Initially, the project looked simple. Then the new rules arrived."
Ultimately -- in the end, after everything else is considered
"Ultimately, the decision belongs to the VP, not to my team."
Arguably -- used to say something is probably true, although some people may disagree
"The reform is arguably the biggest change in twenty years."
To weigh -- to think carefully about the good and bad sides before deciding
"We need to weigh the options before we reply to Chicago."
To think something through -- to consider something carefully, step by step, before you decide
"I want to think it through before I give you a number."
To hedge -- to avoid giving a completely direct answer, so that you are not fully committed
"She hedged her answer because the numbers were not final yet."
Measured -- careful and calm, not extreme or emotional
"The VP liked her measured answer more than a confident guess."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

When a senior person asks for your opinion, the facts are only half of the answer. The other half is a group of small words that carry no information at all. That's a good question. I tend to think. That said. The bottom line is. Take them out and the facts are still there — but the answer suddenly sounds rushed, or too sure of itself.

These words, called discourse markers, do five different jobs. Some buy time while you organize the answer: Let me think about that for a second. Some soften an opinion without hiding it: From where I stand, It seems to me that, I might be wrong, but. Some change direction: That said, Even so, Having said that. Some add and organize: To start with, On top of that. And some conclude: The bottom line is, What it comes down to is, Long story short.

Softening an opinion is called hedging, and it is not the same as being vague. A good hedge is followed by a clear opinion: I tend to think the exposure is manageable. A bad hedge is a fog with no opinion inside: I don't know, maybe, it depends. The first sounds careful and senior. The second sounds as if you have not thought about the question.

There is one trap. Some learners put two markers that do the same job side by side: In my opinion, I think the risk is low. One is enough. And the conclusion matters most of all: an answer that starts softly and never lands leaves the listener with nothing to remember.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- five jobs for small words

GRAMMAR

The markers carry no facts. They tell the listener what you are doing with the facts.

The jobMarkersExample
Buy timeLet me think about that for a second · That's a good question · My initial thought isThat's a good question. I'd say the timing.
Soften (hedge)I tend to think · From where I stand · It seems to me that · I might be wrong, butI tend to think it is manageable.
Change directionThat said · That being said · Even so · Having said that · On the other handIt is stable. That said, I would watch it.
Add and organizeTo start with · On top of that · What's more · All things consideredOn top of that, the team is small.
ConcludeThe bottom line is · What it comes down to is · In a nutshell · Long story shortThe bottom line is, we are within the limit.

Never: In my opinion, I think · In the other hand · a hedge with no opinion after it · an answer with no conclusion

Stage 2: Put the Answer in Order

Order

The VP asks for your personal take on the exposure. Put your answer in a logical order.

?"That's a good question. Let me think about that for a second."
?"I tend to think the exposure itself is manageable."
?"That said, the new filing rules start in the same quarter."
?"On top of that, two of our five analysts are new."
?"I might be wrong, but I would say we are about seventy percent ready."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the sentence a careful speaker would use.

The VP asks a question you did not expect, and you need a few seconds. You say:
A I don't know, maybe, it depends on a lot of different things that are happening right now.
B That's a good question. Let me think for a second.
C Wait.
You want to give your opinion about the risk, but you are not completely sure. You say:
A From where I stand, the risk is in the timing.
B In my opinion, I think that maybe the risk is probably in the timing of the filing.
C The risk is in the timing. Period.
You agreed that the rate is stable, but you want to raise a problem. You say:
A The rate is stable. In the other hand, the deadline is a problem.
B The rate is stable. Resuming, the deadline is a problem for the whole team this quarter.
C The rate is stable. That said, the deadline is a problem.
You have explained everything and want to finish your answer clearly. You say:
A So... yes. That is more or less it, I think, but we can talk about it again some other time.
B The bottom line is, we need one more analyst.
C Actually, we need one more analyst.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Ninety seconds, no notes. A colleague from Chicago asks: if you could change one thing about how your team communicates with headquarters, what would it be? Buy a few seconds, give a soft opinion, turn once with that said, add one more point and finish with the bottom line is.
