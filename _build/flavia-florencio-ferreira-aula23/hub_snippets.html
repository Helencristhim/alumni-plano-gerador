Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Proposal -- a plan or idea that you offer to someone for a decision
"We sent our proposal to the consortium on Monday."
Agreement -- a decision that two or more people accept together
"We have an agreement: the visit is in March."
Condition -- something that must happen before another thing can happen
"Our only condition is a written agreement."
Terms -- the rules and details of an agreement
"Let us read the terms together before we sign."
Offer -- something you say you are ready to give or do
"Our offer is a free training for the producers."
Feasible -- possible to do in a practical way
"A visit in March is feasible for our team."
Mutual -- felt or shared by both sides
"This partnership is for our mutual benefit."
Compromise -- an agreement where each side accepts a little less
"March is too early for them, so the compromise is April."
Next step -- the thing you do after this, to move forward
"The next step is a call with the producers."
To negotiate -- to talk with someone to reach an agreement
"We need to negotiate the dates of the visit."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Flavia is in Milan for a meeting with Paolo, from a food certification consortium in Parma. She presents her proposal: "If you confirm the partnership this week, we will organize a visit to our producers in March." Paolo says March is difficult. Flavia asks: "Is April feasible?" They agree on April. It is a good compromise. Paolo has one condition: the terms must be in English and Italian. Flavia explains a simple fact about the program: "If a product has a geographic indication, it gets more protection in the market." At the end, she says: "If you send us your model, I can prepare the document. The next step is a short call on Tuesday."

1. What will Flavia organize if Paolo confirms the partnership this week?
A A visit to the producers.
B A dinner with all the members of the consortium in Parma.
C A new fact sheet with numbers.
2. Which sentence is a fact that is always true?
A "If you confirm this week, we will organize a visit to the producers."
B "If you send us your model, I can prepare the document."
C "If a product has a geographic indication, it gets more protection."
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "If you will send us the model, I can prepare the document next week."
B "If you send us the model, I can prepare the document."
C "If you sent us the model, I can to prepare it."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- If + Present for Conditions

GRAMMAR

How to put a condition in a proposal, and how to say a fact with "if".

PatternMeaningExample
If + present, presentalways true (a fact)If a product has a GI, it gets more protection.
If + present, will + verba real possibility in the futureIf you confirm this week, we will organize the visit.
If + present, can + verban offer that depends on a conditionIf you send the model, I can prepare the document.
Polite phrasesto propose and to offerWould it be possible to sign in May? / We would be happy to help.
RuleAfter if, use the present, never "will": "If you confirm" (not "If you will confirm"). Put a comma when "if" comes first: "If you confirm, we will start." You can also start with the result: "We will start if you confirm." To close a proposal, say: "If that works for you, ..."
