Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A learning curve -- the speed at which you learn something new; at first it is fast, later it often feels slower
"The learning curve was steep at the beginning, and now it feels flatter."
A turning point -- a moment when a situation starts to change in an important way
"The bilateral meeting in Frankfurt was a turning point for my confidence."
A track record -- what a person or a company has done well or badly in the past, which shows what to expect
"The vendor has a good track record with banks in Latin America."
A focus area -- one specific skill or topic that you decide to work on more than the others
"My first focus area for the next block is speaking in long, formal meetings."
Room for improvement -- the space a skill still has to get better
"My presentations are good, but there is still room for improvement in the Q&A."
A wake-up call -- an event that makes you suddenly understand that you need to change something
"Losing the thread in that panel was a wake-up call for me."
To raise the bar -- to set a higher standard than before
"For the second half of the program, I want to raise the bar."
To fall back on -- to use something you know well when other things do not work
"When I am tired, I can always fall back on a few simple phrases."
To step up -- to start doing more, or doing something at a higher level, when it is needed
"In the next block, I want to step up and lead the meeting in English."
To get the hang of -- to learn how to do something after some practice
"At first the cleft sentences were strange, but I have got the hang of them now."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Halfway through a program, many learners feel that nothing is changing. In reality, the learning curve is only getting flatter. The best way to see real progress is to measure situations, not words. Can you run a meeting? Can you disagree politely? Can you report what a partner said?

Each of those situations has a grammar tool. To report, use reported speech: You mentioned that the platform would be ready in Q1. To disagree politely, use a modal: That timeline might be a little ambitious. To put the spotlight on one point, use an it-cleft: It's the timeline that worries us. To present data, use the passive: Two million payments were processed last year.

To talk about change, use the present perfect for the result today and the past simple for the moment it happened: I was nervous at Sibos, but I've become much more confident since then. And to compare old habits, use used to: I used to wait for the perfect sentence.

At this point, you do not need more structures. You need to choose the right one, fast, and to know which phrases you can fall back on when you are tired. That is the focus area of the second half.

1. According to the text, what is the best way to see real progress at the halfway point?

C Learn many new structures.
2. You want to disagree politely with a vendor. Which sentence uses the right tool?

3. Which sentence is correct?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Your Grammar Toolkit

GRAMMAR

No new structure. Choose the right tool for the job.

UseFormExample
Report what somebody saidreported speech: said / mentioned that + backshiftYou mentioned that it would be ready in Q1.
Disagree politelymodal hedging: might, could, mayThat timeline might be a little ambitious.
Put the spotlight on one pointit-cleft: It's... that...It's the timeline that worries us.
Present datapassive voice: be + past participleTwo million payments were processed.
Talk about changepresent perfect (result) + past simple (moment)I was nervous at Sibos. I've become more confident.
