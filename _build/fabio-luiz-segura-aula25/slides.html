Grammar Discovery

Which Tool Does the Job?

"You mentioned that the platform would be ready in Q1."

"That timeline might be a little ambitious for Brazil."

"It's the exclusivity clause that concerns us, not the price."

"Last year, forty percent of the volume was processed in real time."

What is the job of each sentence? Report, disagree, emphasize, or present data?

UseFormExample
Report what somebody saidreported speechYou mentioned that it would be ready in Q1.
Disagree politelymodal hedging: might, could, mayThat timeline might be a little ambitious.
Put the spotlight on one pointit-cleft: It's... that...It's the clause that concerns us.
Present data and institutional factspassive voiceForty percent was processed in real time.
Talk about experience and changepresent perfect vs past simpleI've become more confident. · I was nervous at Sibos.
Compare old and new habitsused to + base verbI used to wait for the perfect sentence.

In one line: the grammar you already know is enough for a senior meeting. The skill now is choosing the right tool, fast.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Six Weeks Later

A call with Dana, a coach, and a banker in Frankfurt

Dialogue

Six Weeks After Las Vegas

D
Fabio! Good to see you again. So, how have things been since Las Vegas?
F
Busy, but good. Since the conference, I've had three follow-up meetings with vendors, and one with a partner bank in Frankfurt.
D
Wow, that's a lot. On our side, we've finished the pilot with the bank in Mexico. The results were even better than we expected.
F
Congratulations. You mentioned in Las Vegas that the main problem was the testing time. Has that changed?
D
It has. We used to need eight weeks of tests. Now we need five, because the translation layer was rebuilt in the summer.
F
That's impressive. Honestly, it's the testing time that worries my team most, so five weeks might change the conversation.
D
And you? You sound different. More relaxed. When we met, you said you had literally just landed.
F
I did. I used to wait for the perfect sentence. Now I start and adjust. The suite meeting in Las Vegas was a turning point.
D
I can tell. So, what's the next step for us? Should we bring our product team in?
F
Yes. Let's set up a call next week, and by the end of the month we'll have decided on a focus area for a pilot.
Comprehension

About Dana

1. What has Dana's company finished since Las Vegas?
The pilot with the bank in Mexico. The results were even better than they expected.
2. How has their testing time changed, and why?
They used to need eight weeks of tests. Now they need five, because the translation layer was rebuilt in the summer.
3. What does Dana notice about Fabio, and what does she suggest at the end?
She says he sounds different, more relaxed. She suggests bringing her product team in.
Listening 1 · The Coach

The Wall in the Middle

An English coach for executives talks about the halfway point. Sound first — no text.

1. What happens around the halfway point of a program, according to him?
It feels like nothing is changing. It is the learning curve getting flatter, not the learner getting worse.
2. What should you measure instead of words?
Situations: can you run a meeting, can you disagree politely.
3. How many focus areas does he recommend, and what should you do with a recording today?
Three, not ten. Record yourself today, keep the file, and listen to it in six months.
Listening 2 · The Banker

Visible in the Room

A banker in Frankfurt talks about how his English changed. Sound first — no text.

1. What did he use to do in meetings, and why was it a problem?
He used to stay quiet and wait for the perfect sentence. By then, the conversation had moved on.
2. What was his turning point?
A project review. His director asked why he never pushed back. It was a wake-up call.
3. What two things has he changed since then, and what has improved most?
He has stopped waiting for the perfect sentence and started using simple phrases he can fall back on. He has become much more visible in the room.
