Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Case -- one patient's illness and treatment, told as a story
"Today I would like to present a difficult case."
Symptom -- a sign of an illness that the patient feels or notices
"Her first symptom was a strong headache."
Admit -- to accept a patient into the hospital for care
"We admitted her on Sunday night."
Complication -- a new problem that makes a situation more difficult
"There were no complications after the surgery."
Vital signs -- the basic body numbers: heart rate, blood pressure, breathing and temperature
"I check the vital signs every hour."
Deteriorate -- to become worse
"His condition deteriorated very fast."
Stable -- not changing, and not in danger
"The patient is stable now."
Recover -- to get well again after an illness or an operation
"She recovered well and went back to work."
Discharge -- to let a patient leave the hospital
"We usually discharge these patients on day three."
Postoperative -- happening after a surgery
"Postoperative care is a big part of my job."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last year, we admitted a 72-year-old woman for a robotic surgery. She had had stomach pain for three months, and another hospital had sent her to us.

On the day of the operation, her vital signs were normal. The surgeon was working at the console and I was checking the monitors when her heart rate suddenly went up. It was a small complication. The anesthesiologist gave her a medication, and in a few minutes she was stable again.

In the postoperative period, she did not deteriorate. She recovered well, and we discharged her after three days. Past simple tells the events, past continuous tells the scene, and past perfect tells what happened before.

1. What happened BEFORE the patient arrived at the hospital?
A Her heart rate went up.
B She had had stomach pain for three months.
C She recovered well.
2. "The surgeon was working at the console" is...
A something that happened before the story (past perfect).
B a plan for the future.
C the scene: an action in progress (past continuous).
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "I was checking the monitors when her heart rate went up."
B "I checked the monitors when her heart rate was going up."
C "I was check the monitors when her heart rate went up."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Narrative Tenses

GRAMMAR

Events: past simple. The scene: past continuous. Before the story: past perfect.

TenseFormExample
Past simple (events)verb + -ed / irregularHis pressure dropped.
Past continuous (scene)was / were + -ingThe surgeon was working.
Past perfect (before)had + past participleHe had had pain for months.
Negativewasn't + -ing / hadn't + participleWe hadn't expected so much blood.
QuestionWas ... + -ing? / Had ... + participle?Had he had surgery before?

Never: The patient arrive last March · He had have pain · I checked the monitors when it was dropping
