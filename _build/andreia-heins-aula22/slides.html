The Target

Three Parts of a Case

1. The patient: who, and what had happened before.

2. The event: what was happening, and what suddenly happened.

3. The outcome: how the team solved it and how the patient did.

Chapter 4: The Code

Three Pasts in One Story

Narrative tenses

Grammar Discovery

Background, Event, Before

"We admitted Mr. Ribeiro last March."

"The surgeon was working at the console."

"I was watching the monitors when his blood pressure dropped."

"He had lost more blood than we had expected."

Which color tells the events? The scene? What happened before?

TenseUse it forExample
Past simplethe main events, in orderHis pressure dropped. We acted fast.
Past continuous
was / were + -ing		the background: an action in progressThe surgeon was working at the console.
Past perfect
had + past participle		something that happened BEFORE the storyHe had had pain for six months.
when + past simplea short event interrupts the backgroundI was watching the monitors when it dropped.
while + past continuoustwo long actions at the same timeWhile he was giving fluids, the surgeon closed the vessel.

Events: past simple. The scene: past continuous. Before the story: past perfect.

Grammar Practice

Scene, Event or Before?

Say it first, then click to check

"The team ___was preparing the robot when the patient arrived." (prepare -- the scene)
"A colonoscopy ___had found a small tumor before the surgery." (find -- before)
"After two hours, his blood pressure suddenly ___dropped." (drop -- the event)
"While the anesthesiologist ___was giving fluids, the surgeon closed the vessel." (give -- long action)
"Nobody panicked, because we ___had practiced this in simulation." (practice -- before)
"He ___recovered quickly and went home on day four." (recover -- the event)
Detective

Spot the Error

0 / 4 errors found

"Last March, the patient arrive with pain."
"Last March, the patient arrived with pain." — a finished event in the past: past simple
"The surgeon worked at the console when the pressure was dropping."
"The surgeon was working at the console when the pressure dropped." — long scene: continuous; short event: simple
"He had have pain for six months."
"He had had pain for six months." — had + past participle
"We knew what to do because we practiced it before the surgery started."
"We knew what to do because we had practiced it." — the earlier event: past perfect

Listening 1 · Case Conference

The Robot Stopped

A surgeon presents a case at a monthly case conference. Sound first — no text.

1. What surgery was the patient admitted for?
A robotic gallbladder surgery.
2. What happened while they were working on the second port?
The robot showed an error message and the arm stopped.
3. Why could the first assistant fix it so fast?
She had seen this error before.
Listening 2 · The Case, Spoken

Andreia Presents the Case

Andreia tells the case at the hospital meeting. Sound first — no text.

1. What were the surgeon and Andreia doing when the pressure dropped?
The surgeon was working at the console and Andreia was watching the monitors.
2. Why did the pressure drop?
He had lost more blood than they had expected.
3. What does she say worked?
Their simulation training.
