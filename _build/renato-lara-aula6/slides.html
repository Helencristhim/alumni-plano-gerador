Grammar Discovery

Four rules, four meanings

You can use the kitchen at any time.
You can't take the car.
You have to be home by nine thirty.
You don't have to cook. But you can.

The trap is the fourth one

can't = it is forbidden. don't have to = it is NOT obligatory, but you are free to do it. They look similar and they mean opposite things.

The Code

Free, forbidden, obligatory

canit is allowed — You can surf before school.
can'tit is forbidden — You can't take the car.
have toit is obligatory — You have to lock up.
don't have toit is not necessary, but you may — You don't have to cook.

After can and after to, the verb is bare: can go, have to go. And in the third person: he has to go — but he can go, never "cans".

Sound Check

Can and can't sound nothing alike

I can surf before school.

the can is weak and fast, almost swallowed

I can't take the car.

the can't is strong and stops short

I have to lock up.

"have to" becomes one word, with an f sound

The tour of the house

1. What are the two things he has to do every week?
Put the bins out on Tuesday night and do his own laundry on Sunday.
2. What is he free to do without asking?
Help himself to anything in the kitchen, and use the bikes in the garage.
3. What is the one thing he can't do?
He can't leave the back door open, because of the dog.
Listening 2

The unofficial version

1. Which rule does she say is not really a rule?
The curfew at the weekend. If you send a message, it is fine.
2. Which rule does she say you should never break?
The bins. If you forget the bins, everybody is angry for a week.
3. What does she offer to share?
Her old board, until his boards arrive.
