Chapter 3: The Code

Four Words That Hold an Answer Together

The reason, the result, and the two ways to disagree politely

Discovery

Which Two Disagree?

"The order arrived late because the invoice was incomplete."

"The template was wrong, so we have changed it."

"Although the delay was our responsibility, the product was not damaged."

"We will credit the freight. However, we cannot credit the product."

The blue words connect a reason and a result. The red ones disagree — and they do it in two different shapes: one holds two halves in a single sentence, the other starts a new one.

UseFormExample
The reason... because + subject + verbIt arrived late because the invoice was incomplete.
The result..., so + subject + verbThe template was wrong, so we have changed it.
Admit and still holdAlthough + subject + verb, ...Although the delay was ours, the product was fine.
Disagree in a new sentence. However, + subject + verbWe will credit the freight. However, we cannot credit the product.
The shapes are differentalthough joins · however startsOne sentence with although. Two sentences with however.
The trapboth need subject + verb after themNot although the delay, we... Not because of the invoice was.

In one line: because gives the reason, so gives the result, although joins two halves, and however opens a new sentence with a comma.

Listening 1 · The Customer

The Voicemail He Left at Seven Thirty

Before the e-mail, he called. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What happened to him because of the delay?
He had to tell four of his own stores that the product was not coming, and one of them had a promotion running that week.
2. What does he say he is not asking for?
Money. He says he is not calling about a refund.
3. What does he say he needs before he can commit to the next order?
To know what actually went wrong and what has changed, so that he can tell his own team it will not happen again.
Listening 2 · How to Answer

The Answer That Keeps the Account

Somebody who has written a thousand of these. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she say you must do within twenty-four hours, and what does she say it is not?
Acknowledge the complaint. She says acknowledging is not agreeing and not apologizing: it is saying you received it and you are working on it.
2. What are the three things a complete answer must contain?
The root cause, the corrective action, and what you are giving, if anything.
3. What does she say about a goodwill gesture that is not named as one?
If you give something without saying it was goodwill, the customer thinks you owed it, and next time he will expect it as a right.
Real Document

The Answer You Send Back

COMPLAINT C-1188 — RESPONSE
Shipment BS-4471 · signed by Roberto Saraiva
Acknowledgedsame day, 8:10 a.m.
What happeneddelivered 9 days after the promised date
Root causeproduct code missing on the invoice
Corrective actiontemplate fixed · every invoice checked before loading
Credit note issuedfreight only · named as goodwill
Not creditedthe product · delivered and undamaged
Next stepcall on Monday, not another e-mail
Nothing in this document says sorry twice. It says it once, and then it says what changed.
1. Join the root cause and the corrective action in one sentence, twice: once with because and once with so.
The shipment was held because the product code was missing on the invoice. The template was wrong, so we have changed it and every invoice is now checked before loading.
2. Now join the two credit lines, using although in one version and however in the other.
Although the delay was our responsibility, the product was delivered undamaged, so we are crediting the freight only. Or: we will credit the freight. However, we cannot credit the product.
3. Read the last line. Why a call and not another e-mail?
Because the customer said he needs to be able to tell his own team it will not happen again, and that is a conversation, not a document.
