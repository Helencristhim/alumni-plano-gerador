Lesson 10 -- Pre-class

When It Goes Wrong -- The Complaint That Arrives From the Customer

A customer is angry, in writing, and copying people. Key words: a complaint, to acknowledge, root cause, to escalate, a refund, a credit note, a goodwill gesture, a corrective action. Structure: because, so, although and however -- the four words that hold a difficult answer together.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A complaint -- a formal statement from a customer saying that something was wrong
"The complaint arrived by e-mail, with four people in copy."
To acknowledge -- to say officially that you received something and that you have read it
"Acknowledge the e-mail the same day, even if you have no answer yet."
Root cause -- the real reason behind a problem, not the first one you see
"The late truck was not the root cause. The invoice was."
To escalate -- to take a problem to a level above you, on purpose and openly
"He escalated it to my director before he called me."
A refund -- money given back to the customer, because he paid for something he did not get
"He is not asking for a refund. He is asking for an explanation."
A credit note -- a document that reduces what the customer owes you, instead of returning cash
"We issued a credit note for the freight, not for the product."
A goodwill gesture -- something you give that you do not owe, to keep the relationship
"The freight credit was a goodwill gesture, and we said so."
A corrective action -- the change you make in the process, so that the same thing cannot happen again
"Every complaint closes with one corrective action, or it is not closed."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The complaint arrived at eight in the morning with four people in copy. Roberto acknowledged it at ten past eight, because the first rule is that you answer the same day even when you have nothing yet.

The root cause was not the truck. The shipment was held because the product code was missing on the invoice. The template was wrong, so it has been changed, and every invoice is now checked before a container is loaded. That is the corrective action, and without it the answer would be an apology.

Then the difficult part, in two shapes. Although the delay was the company’s responsibility, the product arrived undamaged, so a credit note was issued for the freight only. And the letter says it plainly: we will credit the freight. However, we cannot credit the product. The credit is named as what it is, a goodwill gesture — because a customer who is not told will assume he was owed it. He had already escalated the case to a director, and he never asked for a refund at all.

1. "Although the delay was our responsibility, the product arrived undamaged." What does although do here?
A It admits one thing and holds a different position, inside one sentence.
B It gives the reason for the delay.
C It gives the result of the delay.
2. "We will credit the freight. However, we cannot credit the product." Why not use although here?
A Because although cannot be used with money.
B Because these are two separate sentences. However starts a new one and takes a comma; although joins two halves of one.
C Because however is stronger than although in every case.
3. "The template was wrong, so it has been changed." What is so doing?
A Giving the reason why the template was wrong.
B Giving the result: because of the first half, the second half happened.
C Disagreeing with the first half.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Because, So, Although and However

GRAMMAR

Four short words that hold a difficult answer together. Two connect cause and result; two let you disagree without fighting.

UseFormExample
The reason... because + subject + verbIt arrived late because the invoice was incomplete.
The result..., so + subject + verbThe template was wrong, so we have changed it.
Admit and still holdAlthough + subject + verb, ...Although the delay was ours, the product was fine.
Disagree in a new sentence. However, + subject + verbWe will credit the freight. However, we cannot credit the product.
Different shapesalthough joins · however startsOne sentence with although. Two sentences with however.
The trapall four need subject + verb after themNot although the delay, we... Not because of the invoice was...

Never: Although the delay, the product was fine · We will credit the freight however we cannot credit the product · because of the invoice was incomplete · He escalated it so I called him (missing the comma).

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

A complaint arrives and you still have no explanation. The same day, you write:
A Nothing. You wait until you have the full answer.
B "I received your e-mail this morning and I am looking into it today."
C "I am very sorry, it will never happen again."
You want to admit the fault and still refuse to credit the product. You say:
A "Although the delay, the product was not damaged."
B "Although the delay was our responsibility, the product was not damaged."
C "Because the delay was our responsibility, the product was not damaged."
You are writing two separate sentences, and the second one disagrees with the first. You write:
A "We will credit the freight. However, we cannot credit the product."
B "We will credit the freight however we cannot credit the product."
C "We will credit the freight, however we cannot credit the product, although."
You are giving something you do not owe. The professional way to say it is:
A Say nothing about it and hope he notices.
B "The freight credit is a goodwill gesture, and I want to be clear that it is."
C "You are right, we owe you this and much more."
