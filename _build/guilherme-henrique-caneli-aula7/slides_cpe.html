Why C is the one
A lightning round is an instrument for FINDING OUT where a room is: one question, everybody, sixty seconds. Parting thoughts are the opposite — they ask for nothing and produce nothing. Welding the two together sounds efficient and delivers the weakest possible ending. The collocation exists; the purpose cancels itself.

The Text

Paragraphs One and Two

The Eight-Second Delay

Nobody who has not worked in one quite believes how much a room changes once it is running with simultaneous interpretation. The words are the same. Everything else is different.1

Begin with the obvious. A joke lands twice, which kills it. An interruption arrives on top of somebody else’s sentence, because the interrupter has heard the end of a thought the rest of the room has not reached.2 A chair who has not planned for this spends the session apologising for collisions that nobody actually caused.

The Text

Paragraphs Three and Four

The Eight-Second Delay

The structural answers are dull and they work. Ground rules stated in the first two minutes — who may be quoted, whether a follow-up question comes from the floor or only from the chair, how long a speaking slot runs — save the hour that is otherwise spent negotiating them one intervention at a time. To time-box an intervention and say the number out loud is worth more than any appeal for brevity.3

Format does the rest. A breakout session moves twenty people into a room small enough that silence becomes uncomfortable, which is the only reliable way of making the quiet ones speak. A lightning round puts one question to everybody in turn, sixty seconds each.4 A rapporteur writes down what was said, and it is a defined job rather than a favour asked of the youngest person present.

Adapted from a comment essay on multilingual meetings · ~600 words · gaps 3 and 4
The Text

Paragraphs Five and Six

The Eight-Second Delay

What none of this solves is the ending. A closing round in which everybody offers a parting thought is pleasant and produces nothing that can be written down afterwards.5 If the session was convened to produce a consensus statement, somebody has to put a draft sentence on the screen before people begin gathering their coats, and the chair has to wrap up by reading it aloud.

Composition matters more than content. A lineup is not a list of the most senior people available; it is a list built so that the disagreements already in the room are represented on the floor.6 A multilateral development bank will send four people to a session like this, and either all four leave with the same sentence or none of them does.

Collaborative Task

One Sentence, Ten Minutes Left

Four minutes, together

Collaborative task — four minutes. You are co-chairing a closed session of sixty people in three languages, and you have ten minutes left. Together, decide: the one sentence you will put on the screen, who reads it aloud, and what you will do if two delegates object. You have to agree on the sentence.

Long Turn

Two Minutes, Uninterrupted

No notes. No apology at the start.

Long turn — two minutes, uninterrupted. You are closing a session of sixty people in three languages. Answer this: “What did this room actually agree?” Give the sentence first, then the evidence. Short sentences, slow numbers, and at least three complex noun phrases with two or more adjectives in front of the noun.

Follow-up

Four Questions, No Restarting

Four examiner questions, straight after the two minutes. No restarting, and no preparation between them.
1Say your consensus sentence again, slowly enough to be interpreted.
2You used two adjectives before a noun. Say that phrase again and tell me why that order.
3Somebody in the room says you have overstated the agreement. Concede, then hold.
4One sentence: who in this room would have written it differently, and how?
Debate One

Defend the Side You Disagree With

This house believes that the plenary session should be abolished and replaced entirely by breakout groups.
1Ninety seconds each, alternating. No notes.
2You choose your side, and you open with the strongest argument against yourself.
3Short sentences only. If a sentence could not be interpreted live, it does not count.
Debate Two

The Side You Were Given

This house believes that a chair should choose a lineup for disagreement even when the sponsors want seniority.
1The teacher gives you the side. You do not choose it.
2Before you answer, restate the strongest point the other side has made, in your own words, so that they accept your version of it.
3Only then do you respond. A turn that skips the concession does not count.

The Proposal, For Next Time

Paper 2, Part 2

Paper 2, Part 2 — a report, 280 to 320 words, for the next lesson. You chaired a closed session of sixty people last week. Write the report your organisation will circulate: the sentence the room agreed, the two positions that did not converge, and what you would change about the format next time. Assume the reader was not there and will not read a second page.

What the teacher will be reading for
Three things are being read for. The agreed sentence, quoted exactly and early. An honest account of what did NOT converge, named rather than softened. And one concrete change to the format, not to the people. A report that says the discussion was rich and wide-ranging has told the reader nothing they could act on.
