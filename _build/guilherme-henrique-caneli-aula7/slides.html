Lesson 7

The Full Room

Four nationalities, three interpretation booths and one moderator keeping time

Warm-up

What the Moderator Did Right

Think of the best multilateral panel you have attended. What did the moderator do that you already do, and what would you do differently? Use two words from last lesson: a lede, on background, a key message, a holding statement.

The Target

Run a Room That Speaks Four Languages

1. Set the ground rules and the clock before the first answer, not after the third.

2. Describe people and projects precisely, with words in the order a native listener expects.

3. Speak for the interpreters as well as the room: noun early, pause at the end of the idea.

Diagnostic

Introduce the Lineup

Four panelists: a director from a multilateral development bank, a Chinese policy bank, a Canadian pension fund and the Brazilian ministry.

Introduce all four in under sixty seconds, with one precise description for each. Do it now.

Chapter 3: The Code

The Order Nobody Teaches

Every word is correct, and one phrase still sounds wrong

Grammar Discovery

Three Sound Natural. One Does Not.

"a robust long-term bilateral infrastructure framework"

"an ambitious new Brazilian rail concession"

"two experienced Canadian pension funds"

"a bilateral long-term robust infrastructure framework"

Every word in the last phrase is correct English, and a native listener would still stop you. Look at the colors in the first three: which kind of word always comes first, which kind always sits next to the noun, and what comes in between?

PositionKind of wordExample
1. Opinionrobust, ambitious, credible, experienceda credible plan
2. Size, age or timelarge, new, long-term, early-stagea credible long-term plan
3. Origin or scopeBrazilian, regional, bilateral, multilaterala credible long-term regional plan
4. Type or purpose (closest to the noun)infrastructure, rail, pension, risk-mitigationa credible long-term regional infrastructure plan
Hyphens before the nounlong-term, early-stage, risk-mitigationa long-term plan, but the plan is long term
Know when to stopthree or four before the noun, then use a clausea regional infrastructure plan that is credible and long term
The trapthe word next to the noun is never pluralNot a pensions fund. Say a pension fund.

In one line: Opinion, then time, then origin, then the word that says what kind -- and the more technical the word, the closer it sits to the noun.

Chapter 4: On Stage

The Multilateral Panel

On stage with a development bank, two recordings and the run sheet

Dialogue

Ninety Seconds per Answer

G
Welcome back. Two ground rules: ninety seconds per answer, and everyone gets a closing round. Amara, you lead infrastructure at a multilateral development bank. Why is your bank in this room?
A
Because we are the ones who can take the first loss. A robust long-term guarantee from us changes how every pension fund reads a Brazilian rail concession.
G
Let me ask a follow-up question on that. First loss for how long, and on which risks?
A
The first seven years, on construction and currency. After that, the project has to stand on its own.
G
That is the key message for the funds here tonight. I am going to time-box the next part with a lightning round: ten seconds each. Amara, one word for the biggest obstacle.
A
Licensing.
G
And one word for what would unlock it?
A
Predictability. Although I suspect that is two ideas hiding in one word.
G
It counts. We have a rapporteur taking notes and simultaneous interpretation for our colleagues from Beijing, so let me summarize for the booth: the bank takes early risk, and licensing is the bottleneck. Amara, your parting thought.
A
Stop asking whether Brazil is ready. Ask which projects are ready, and fund those first.
Comprehension

About Amara

1. What does Amara lead, and why does she say her bank is in the room?
Infrastructure at a multilateral development bank. Her bank is there because it can take the first loss.
2. For how long, and on which risks, does her bank take the first loss?
For the first seven years, on construction and currency.
3. What are her two answers in the lightning round?
Licensing is the biggest obstacle, and predictability would unlock it.
4. What is her parting thought?
Stop asking whether Brazil is ready; ask which projects are ready, and fund those first.
Listening 1 · An Interpreter

A Few Words Behind You

A conference interpreter on what speakers do to the booth. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does the interpreter say long strings of adjectives are a problem?
Because interpreters must wait for the noun to know what the adjectives describe, and by then the speaker is in the next sentence.
2. What happens when a speaker tells a joke based on a play on words?
The interpreters cannot translate it, and half the room laughs ten seconds after the other half.
3. What three things do the speakers interpreters love most do?
They put the noun early, pause at the end of each idea, and send their notes the night before.
Listening 2 · The Rapporteur

Three Conclusions and One Disagreement

The rapporteur closes a multilateral panel. Sound first — no text.

1. What are the three conclusions of the panel?
A credible long-term guarantee from a multilateral development bank brings pension funds into early-stage projects; licensing is now the main bottleneck; and foreign investors want a neutral arbitration mechanism.
2. What was the one disagreement?
Whether the guarantee should cover currency risk, or construction risk only.
3. What will be published by Friday, and where will the licensing discussion continue?
A short consensus statement. The licensing discussion continues in the breakout session that afternoon.
