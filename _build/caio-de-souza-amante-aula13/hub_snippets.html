Lesson 13 -- Pre-class

Handling the Hard Questions -- The Short Verbs That Keep You in Control

Under pressure, a phrasal verb is shorter than an explanation, and it says that something was already done. Key words: to bring up, to back up, to rule out, to look into, to follow up, to figure out, to hold off, to walk someone through. Structure: business phrasal verbs and where the object goes.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To bring up -- to start talking about a subject in a conversation
“Thank you for bringing it up. It is a fair question.”
To back up -- to support a claim with evidence
“We can back it up with the audit report from March.”
To rule out -- to decide that something is not possible
“We have ruled out a second office this year.”
To look into -- to investigate or check something carefully
“We looked into it last month and found the cause.”
To follow up -- to contact someone again to continue a conversation
“I will follow up with your lawyer on Friday.”
To figure out -- to find the answer to a problem by thinking about it
“We figured out why the margin fell in the third quarter.”
To hold off -- to wait before you do something
“We are holding off on hiring until the deal closes.”
To walk someone through -- to explain something to a person step by step
“Let me walk you through the numbers.”

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Every due diligence has one meeting where the questions get harder. A buyer brings up the client you lost last year, or a number that does not match. Founders who handle this well do not have better answers; they have better verbs. They say we looked into it, we can back it up, we have ruled that out. Short verbs, and every one of them says that something was already done.

These are phrasal verbs: a verb plus a small word, with a meaning you cannot always guess from the parts. Bring up a subject is to start talking about it. Back up a claim is to give the evidence. Rule out an option is to decide it is not possible. Look into a problem is to investigate it. Follow up is to come back to it later, figure out is to find the answer, hold off is to wait before acting, and walk somebody through something is to explain it step by step.

The grammar is in where the object goes. With most of them, a noun can go before or after the small word: back up the claim or back the claim up. A pronoun can only go in the middle: back it up, rule it out, bring it up. A few never separate: you look into it, never look it into. Walk through takes the person in the middle: let me walk you through the numbers. And when hold off is followed by an activity, Lesson 11 comes back: we are holding off on signing.

Under pressure, the temptation is to answer with a long explanation. A phrasal verb is shorter, and it sounds like the person in control. The buyer asks about the lost client; you say we looked into it, here is what we figured out, and I can walk you through it. Three verbs, and the meeting moves on.

1. According to the text, what do founders who handle hard questions well have?
A Better answers, which their lawyers prepared for them in the weeks before the meeting.
B Better verbs, which say that something was already done.
C Fewer hard questions from the buyer.
2. Where does a pronoun go with back up?
A After the small word: back up it.
B Before the verb, as in it back up, which is the order used in formal written English.
C In the middle: back it up.
3. What does the text say about look into?
A It never separates: you look into it.
B It works like back up, so look it into is also correct when you are speaking.
C It is always followed by the -ing form.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Business Phrasal Verbs and the Object

GRAMMAR

A noun can go on either side. A pronoun goes in the middle. Look into never separates.

JobFormExample
Starting a subjectbring upThank you for bringing it up.
Supporting a claimback upWe can back it up with the audit.
Deciding something is not possiblerule outWe have ruled out a second office.
Investigatinglook into (never separates)We looked into it last month.
Coming back to it laterfollow up (on / with)I will follow up with your lawyer on Friday.
Finding the answerfigure outWe figured out why the margin fell.
Waiting before actinghold off (on + -ing)We are holding off on hiring.
Explaining step by stepwalk + person + throughLet me walk you through the numbers.
The object rulepronoun in the middleback it up · rule it out · bring it up

Never: back up it · look it into · rule out it · walk through you the numbers
