Grammar Discovery

Sure, or possible?

Dana: "The wind might change after two."
"There may be a long lull in the middle of the heat."
Renato: "I might not get a good wave in the first five minutes."
Kai: "Finn may be tired after his last heat."

What do the orange words have in common? Are the speakers sure?

possible: might / may + verb

It's possible, but we are not certain. The verb after might and may is the base verb: no s, no to. Negative: might not / may not.

The Code

One word, no promises

Positive

The peak might move.
I may need one more wave.
It might be choppy.
Finn may win again.

Negative

The wind might not stop.
I may not get a good wave.
It might not be enough.
Kai may not come.
Watch out: same form for every person: I might, he might (never "mights"). No to after it: it might rain. And maybe (one word) goes at the start: Maybe the wind will change.
The Code

How sure are you?

Certain: will or won't. Possible, not certain: might or may.

100% · Certain

"The heat will start at two."

50% · Possible

"The wind might change."
"I may advance."

0% · Certain

"The horn won't wait for you."
Anything might happen.

The best line for a close heat, a test, or a new week.

Sound Check

Short and soft

The wind might change.

"might" sounds like MITE — the GH is silent

I might not get a wave.

"might not" sounds like MY-NOT — the first T is almost silent

Maybe the wind will stop.

"maybe" is strong at the start: MAY-be

Practice

Maybe, maybe not

Maybe the peak will move. → The peak ___ (might / move).
The peak might move.
Maybe there will be a lull. → There ___ (may / be) a lull.
There may be a lull.
Maybe I won't get a good wave. → I ___ (might not / get) a good wave.
I might not get a good wave.
Maybe Finn will be tired. → Finn ___ (may / be) tired.
Finn may be tired.
