Lesson 29 — Pre-class

Anything Might Happen

Eight words for a close heat (opponent, underdog, in the lead, comeback, lull, gust, unpredictable, certain) and how to talk about what is possible: The wind might change. I may not get a wave.

Stage 1.1: A Close Heat

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes. "In the lead" is one expression: learn it together.

Opponent — a person you compete against
"My opponent is a local surfer."
Underdog — the person that people think will probably lose
"I'm the underdog in this heat."
In the lead — in first place at the moment
"Finn is in the lead after five minutes."
Comeback — when you start winning again after you were losing
"There's still time for a comeback."
Lull — a quiet time when no waves come
"There might be a long lull."
Gust — a sudden, strong wind that comes and goes
"A strong gust might push you off the wave."
Unpredictable — when you can't know what is going to happen
"The ocean is unpredictable this afternoon."
Certain — completely sure about something
"I'm not certain. It might rain."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read Renato's message to his friend Lucas in Brazil, fifteen minutes before the quarterfinal. Then answer the questions.

Lucas, I advanced! I'm in the quarterfinal, at two o'clock. My opponents are three local guys, and one of them, Finn, won this contest last year. So I'm the underdog. The conditions are unpredictable this afternoon. The wind might change, and there may be some strong gusts. Kai says there might be a long lull in the middle of the heat. Finn might be in the lead after five minutes, but that's okay — there's always time for a comeback. Honestly, I'm not certain about anything. I may not get a good wave at all. But I might surprise everybody. Anything might happen!
"The wind might change." Is Renato sure about the wind?
A Yes. Dana checked the forecast, and the wind is going to change at two.
B No. It's possible, but he isn't sure.
Why is Renato the underdog?
A Because Finn won this contest last year.
B Because he got knocked out in the first round this morning in Margaret River.
"I may not get a good wave at all." What does Renato mean?
A He knows there will be no waves.
B It's possible that no good wave comes to him.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Possibility: might / may + verb

Use might or may when something is possible, but you are not certain. After them, use the base verb: no s, no to. Negative: might not / may not.

Possible

The wind might change.
There may be a lull.
Finn might win again.

Possible: not

I might not get a wave.
It may not be enough.
Kai might not come.

Positive: It might rain.  ·  Negative: It might not rain.  ·  Question: Do you think it might rain?
Remember: same form for every person: he might (never "mights"). Certain? Use will. Maybe (one word) goes at the start: Maybe the wind will stop.
Your voice: "might" sounds like MITE: the GH is silent, like in "night".

Stage 4: On the sand

Quiz
Tom asks: "Is the wind going to change?" You're not sure. Answer him.
A It might change. I'm not certain.
B It might to change after two o'clock, so watch the flags on the beach.
Nobody thinks you can win the heat. What are you?
A I'm in the lead, and I'm going to win this heat easily.
B I'm the underdog.
No waves for five minutes. Kai is worried. Calm him down.
A It's just a lull. A set may come soon.
B It's a lull. A big set mights come soon, so be ready.
Finn is in the lead. There are ten minutes left. What do you say?
A Finn is going to win for certain. I'm knocked out.
B There's still time for a comeback.
Dave asks about your plans for Saturday. You have no plans yet.
A I might surf, or I may stay home.
B May be I surf, or may be I stay home with the family.
