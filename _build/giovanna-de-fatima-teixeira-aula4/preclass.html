Lesson 04 -- Pre-class

Under the City -- Where Everything Is

Say what is on your site and where each thing is: there is, there are, and the prepositions you called difficult -- through, along, across, into, under. Take your time, and repeat as often as you like.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen as many times as you want.

Entrance -- the place where you go into a building or a site
"There is one entrance for people, on the west side."
Gate -- a big door in a fence, wide enough for trucks and machines
"The trucks come in through the main gate."
Fence -- a wall of metal or wood around a site, to keep people out
"There is a blue fence around the whole site."
Ramp -- a road that goes up or down, instead of steps
"We walk down the ramp into the tunnel."
Walkway -- a narrow safe path for people, never for machines
"Stay on the walkway, never on the road."
Container -- a big metal box used as an office or a store on a site
"My office is a container next to the gate."
Ground level -- the floor of a building that is level with the street
"The meeting room is at ground level."
Underground -- below the surface of the street, under the city
"Twenty meters underground, the crew is working."
Area -- one part of a site, used for one thing
"The north area is closed all week."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the text out loud, then answer the questions below. Read slowly -- nobody is timing you.

From the tenth floor, the site is a grey rectangle. There is a blue fence around it, and there is only one way in for people: a small entrance on the west side. The trucks do not use it. They come in through the big gate, fifty meters away. Inside the fence there are two areas: the office area, with four containers, and the work area, with the machines. A yellow walkway goes across the site, from the entrance to the ramp. People walk along the yellow line, machines use the road. The meeting room is at ground level. And the ramp goes down, under the road, into the tunnel: twenty meters underground, the crew works all day.

1. Why does the text say "there are two areas" and not "there is two areas"?
A Because the areas are far from the entrance.
B Because there are two of them, and two or more things take "there are".
C Because an area is a big thing.
2. The trucks come in "through" the gate. Why not "under" the gate?
A Because "under" is only used for people.
B Because the gate is very high.
C Because they go in one side and out the other side -- that is "through". "Under" only says where something is.
3. Where does the yellow walkway go?
A Around the fence, outside the site.
B Across the site, from the entrance to the ramp.
C Under the road, into the tunnel.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Where, and Which Way

Grammar

Two families of words, and two different questions. The verb tells you which family you need.

FormHow it worksExample
There isone thing (singular)There is one entrance for people.
There aretwo or more things (plural)There are three containers next to the gate.
Negativethere is no / there are noThere is no ramp on this side.
QuestionIs there...? / Are there...?Is there a gate on the north side?
WordThe pictureExample
under / abovebelow it / over it — answers where?The tunnel is under the street.
betweenwith one thing on each side (two things)The walkway is between the fence and the containers.
throughin one side, out the other — answers which way?The trucks come in through the gate.
alongfollowing a line, end to endWe walk along the fence.
acrossfrom one side to the other sideThe walkway goes across the site.
intofrom outside to insideThe crew goes into the tunnel.
The one rulePlace words answer where? Movement words answer which way? Look at the verb: is asks for where, go asks for which way.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation. Wrong? Try again -- nobody is watching.

A visitor asks how the trucks get onto your site. You say:
A "The trucks enter in the gate of the site."
B "They come in through the main gate, on the north side."
C "The trucks come in under the gate of north."
Somebody asks how many offices there are on your site. You have four. You say:
A "There is four containers for the offices."
B "Have four containers for offices."
C "There are four containers, and we use them as offices."
A new colleague walks on the road, not on the yellow line. You say:
A "Stay on the walkway, please. Walk along the yellow line."
B "Stay in the walkway, please. Walk across the yellow line."
C "Stay under the walkway, please. Walk into the yellow line."
