Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

The Question You Did Not Expect -- One Second, One Point, Stop

What to do in the three seconds after a question you did not prepare for: buy one second, give one point and one reason, and stop; or say you do not know yet and give a time. Key words: to be put on the spot, a tough question, to think on your feet, off the top of my head, to get back to someone, a ballpark figure, to buy time, a follow-up, hypothetical, to go blank. Structure: if for a real situation, and if with the past form plus would for an imagined one.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To be put on the spot -- to be asked a difficult question suddenly, in front of other people
"I was put on the spot when the CFO asked for the 2027 number."
A tough question -- a question that is difficult to answer, or uncomfortable
"That is a tough question, and I want to answer it properly."
To think on your feet -- to think and answer quickly, without time to prepare
"In a board meeting you have to think on your feet."
Off the top of my head -- from memory, without checking, so maybe not exact
"Off the top of my head, the rate is around twenty-six percent."
To get back to someone -- to answer somebody later, after you check the information
"I will get back to you by tomorrow morning."
A ballpark figure -- a number that is roughly right, not exact
"Can you give me a ballpark figure for the cost?"
To buy time -- to do or say something that gives you a few seconds to think
"I repeated the question to buy time."
A follow-up -- a second question or action that comes after the first one
"He had a follow-up question about the transition."
Hypothetical -- about a situation that is imagined, not real
"It is a hypothetical question, but let me try."
To go blank -- to suddenly not be able to remember or think of anything
"My mind went blank for two seconds, and then the answer came."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Sofia, a finance director in Madrid, used to freeze in board meetings. The chairman would ask something she had not prepared for, her mind went blank, and she started to talk before she knew her point. After one bad meeting she decided to prepare for the unprepared, and she found that two kinds of if did most of the work.

The first kind is about something that can really happen: “If the rate goes up, our cost goes up too.” “If the bank is late, I will call you.” The verb after if stays in the present, even when she talks about tomorrow. She never says if the bank will be late.

The second kind is for hypothetical questions, the ones that start with what if: “If we moved the team to Lisbon, we would save money, but we would lose people.” The past form after if does not mean the past. It means: this is imagined. And when she really does not know, she no longer invents: “I do not have that number off the top of my head. I will get back to you by noon.”

Why does Sofia say "If the bank is late, I will call you" and not "If the bank will be late"?
A Because it is a hypothetical question.
B Because the bank is in Spain.
C Because after if, the verb stays in the present for a real future situation.
In "If we moved the team to Lisbon, we would save money", does "moved" mean the past?
A Yes, they moved the team last year.
B No, it means the situation is imagined.
C Yes, it is a past story.
What does Sofia do now when she does not know a number?
A She changes the topic.
B She says she does not have it off the top of her head and gives a time to get back.
C She gives a number anyway.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Two Kinds of If

GRAMMAR

Real and possible: if plus the present. Imagined: if plus the past form, and would.

JobFormExample
Real, always trueif + present, presentIf the rate goes up, our cost goes up.
Real, the futureif + present, willIf the bank is late, I will call you.
Imaginedif + past form, wouldIf we moved the team, we would save money.
Imagined, with beif I were…, I would…If I were the CFO, I would wait.
Buy one secondone phrase onlyThat is a fair question.
Do not knownot yet + a timeI do not know yet. I will get back to you by noon.

Never: if the bank will be late · if we would move the team · If I was the CFO, I will wait · three buying-time phrases in a row
