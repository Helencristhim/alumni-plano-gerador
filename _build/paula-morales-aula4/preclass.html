Lesson 04 -- Pre-class

Telling People What I Do -- My Role, My Team

In lesson 3 you learned to say what you do (I work, I start, I travel). In this lesson you learn to say what other people do: seven words about roles (cover, manage, handle, visit, report, team, office), the present simple in the third person — she works / she doesn't work / Does she work? — and the possessives (my, your, her, his). With them you describe your role, your team and your manager. This Pre-class is only a first contact: come to class with the S in your ear, not on the tip of your tongue.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Seven words — and they cover your whole role. Listen to each one and repeat it out loud, alone. Watch the stress: MA-nage (at the start) but re-PORT (at the end). And team has a long I: say “teem”, never “Tim”.

Cover -- to pay for a risk in a policy
"Our insurance covers big risks." (O nosso seguro cobre grandes riscos.)
Manage -- to be in charge of a team or a project
"Sofia manages the commercial team."
Handle -- to take care of a problem or a task
"My team handles big companies."
Visit -- to go and see a client or a place
"I visit brokers every month."
Report -- a document with the numbers of the month
"David writes the report every Monday."
Team -- the people who work with you
"My team handles large risks."
Office -- the place where you work every day
"My office is in São Paulo."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions. The words in bold are the grammar of this lesson — look at the S and at who is speaking.

Monday, ten o'clock. The Zurich intranet, commercial team page.

"Sofia Reyes manages the commercial team. She covers the south region, and she visits the brokers every month. She doesn't stay in the office all week."

"David is the analyst. He handles the numbers, and his report comes to Sofia every Monday. Does he visit the brokers? No, he doesn't. That is not his job."

Paula reads the page and thinks: "So my job is the client, her job is the team, and his job is the report." (Notice two things: with she and he, the verb takes an S — manages, covers, visits, handles. And when doesn't or does comes in, the S leaves the verb: he doesn't stay, never "he doesn't stays".)

1. Why does "manages" take an S and "I manage" does not?
A Because "manages" is plural.
B Because the person who manages is she — with he, she and it the verb takes an S. With I, it does not.
C Because the sentence is in the past.
2. What does David do?
A He manages the team and visits the brokers.
B He handles the numbers and writes the report — the one that reaches Sofia every Monday.
C He covers the south region.
3. The page says "he doesn't stay", not "he doesn't stays". Why?
A Because the S is already inside doesn't — one S per sentence, never two.
B Because it is a typo on the intranet.
C Because after "doesn't" the verb always goes plural.
4. "His report" — whose report is it?
A Sofia's — she is the one who reads it.
B David's — his agrees with the OWNER, not with the thing.
C It is his because a report is a masculine word.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple na 3ª pessoa (she works / Does she work?) + possessivos

GRAMMAR

Lesson 3 gave you I. This lesson gives you she, he and it — and the difference between them is ONE letter. In English the verb changes for one person only.

FormInglêsWhen you use it
I / you (lesson 3)I cover large risks.Eu cubro grandes riscos.
She / he / itShe manages the team. / He handles the numbers. / It covers fire.To say what another person does.
NegativeShe doesn't visit on Mondays. (= does not)To say what another person does not do.
QuestionDoes she visit the brokers?Ela visita os corretores?
Short answer (yes)Yes, she does.Sim, visita.
Short answer (no)No, she doesn't.The short way to say no.

The fair trade (the rule that solves half of the mistakes): the S appears in one place only. In the affirmative it sits on the verb: "She visits". But when does or doesn't comes in, the S leaves the verb — because does already carries the S inside it: "Does she visit?" and "She doesn't visit". Never both: "Does she visits?" has one S too many.

The possessives (my / your / her / his): two facts that save you time. (1) They never change in the plural: my client, my clients — the same my. (2) Her and his agree with the OWNER, never with the thing: Sofia's report is her report, and David's team is his team. This is the most common mistake here, because in your first language the possessive agrees with the thing.

Why this matters: at Zurich nobody will ask about you only. They will ask who handles what, who your manager is, who writes the report. Without the S and the possessives you can talk about yourself; with them, you can talk about the team — and that is a conversation.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each real situation — the Zurich coffee break, the talk with HR, the broker on the phone.

A new colleague asks: "What do you do here?" The best answer is:
A "I am responsible for large risks. I cover big companies."
B "I am responsible for large risks. I covers big companies."
C "Large risks." (nothing more, with no sentence)
HR asks: "What does your manager do?" You answer:
A "She manage the commercial team."
B "She manages the commercial team. She covers the south region."
C "Her manage the commercial team."
"Does Sofia visit the brokers?" She does. The right answer is:
A "Yes, she visits." (as a short answer)
B "Yes, she does. She visits the brokers every month."
C "Yes, she is."
You want to ask if David handles the report. You say:
A "Does he handles the report?"
B "Does he handle the report?"
C "He handle the report?"
Sofia is your manager. How do you talk about her team?
A "His team handles large risks."
B "Her team handles large risks." (the owner is Sofia)
C "Her team handle large risks."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record your answer. Speak for 60 seconds, with no script. Do not redo it more than twice. Then compare it with your lesson 3 recording: it is the same person, one week later.

Describe your team in English: what YOU are responsible for, what your manager does, and what another person on the team does. It can be your real team today or the one you imagine at Zurich. Use at least one sentence with "I am responsible for...", one with "She manages..." (or "He handles...") and one with a possessive (my, her, his). It does not have to be perfect — if the S escapes, keep going. If you get stuck, use the rescue kit from lesson 1 and carry on.
