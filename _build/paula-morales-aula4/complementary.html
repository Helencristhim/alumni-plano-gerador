Lesson 4 -- Telling People What I Do: My Role, My Team

Film
The Intern (2015) · HBO Max — included in the subscription

This film is here for two reasons, and the second is the one that matters. The first: the opening twenty minutes are people introducing themselves and explaining their own role — the whole company is a parade of “she runs the company”, “he handles the site”, “my team does...”. It is the grammar of this lesson, loose, in the middle of an office conversation. The second reason: Ben is seventy and joins a company where he does not understand half the words — and he does not hide, he asks. You will recognize the scene. It is yours, in different clothes.

Tip: watch it with English subtitles. Do ONE thing only: in the first 20 minutes, every time someone explains what ANOTHER person does (“she manages...”, “he handles...”), hit pause and repeat the sentence out loud. You do not have to finish the film today — if you only watch up to the moment Ben meets the team, the task is done. Then try to describe Jules (the boss) in a sentence of your own: “She manages...”

Podcast
VOA Everyday Grammar — “Use Possessive Pronouns to Talk about Friends, Family” (~4 min, free, with transcript)

Four minutes, American English, and the subject is the second half of this lesson: my, your, her, his. They go straight to the point you saw in the Grammar Tip — that the English possessive agrees with the owner, and never changes in the plural. And the example they use is the one from your own day: how to talk about your boss, your colleague, your team. VOA is an American public broadcaster: clear delivery, a pace slowed down on purpose for learners, and the full free transcript on the same page — no sign-up, no login, no catch.

Tip: listen the first time without reading anything, just to let your ear catch the rhythm (you understand more than you think — listening is your strength). The second time, open the transcript and follow along. The episode asks you to pause and build sentences — take the pauses for real and answer out loud with YOUR life: “My manager is Sofia. Her team handles large risks. His report comes on Monday.”

YouTube
English Grammar — Present Simple: YES/NO Questions · Alex, from engVid (~6 min)

It is the same teacher as the lesson 3 video — on purpose: you already know his voice and his pace, so your ear is free for the content. Before you press play, look at the thumbnail: everything is written on the whiteboard by hand, and it is exactly today's lesson — “He works downtown” / “Does he work downtown?” / “Yes, he does.” The three lines show the fair trade you studied in the Grammar Tip: the S is on the verb in the affirmative, and jumps to does in the question. Never both.

Tip: watch it once all the way through. The second time, pause after each sentence on the board and say YOUR version out loud, swapping his example for yours: “She works in São Paulo. Does she work in São Paulo? Yes, she does.” Then do the same with Sofia and David — three sentences each. The channel is free and open, with no sign-up to watch.
