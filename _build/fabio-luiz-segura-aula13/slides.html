Chapter 3: Softer, Not Weaker

Four Ways to Say No

And the small word that leaves the door open

Grammar Discovery

Four Sentences, All of Them No

"That might not be viable with our compliance calendar."

"We would need to revisit that assumption before we commit."

"I am not sure that would scale in the Brazilian market."

"This could have been caught at the proof-of-concept stage."

Every one of these sentences is a no. None of them says you are wrong. What do the red words have in common, and which one is about the past?

What you want to doFormExample
Doubt a claim without calling it wrongmight / may not + verbThat might not be viable for a first corridor.
Set a condition before you agreewould need to / would have to + verbWe would need to see the numbers first.
Question a resultI am not sure that + would + verbI am not sure that would scale here.
Offer an alternativecould + verb · Wouldn't it be better to...?We could start with one corridor. Wouldn't it be safer to test it first?
Criticize the past, softlycould have / should have + past participleThis could have been tested earlier. We should have asked sooner.
The form trapmodal + have + past participle, alwaysNot could been, not could had. Say could have been.
The tone trapyou should have accuses a personNot you should have told us. Say this could have been flagged earlier.

In one line: the modal does not change your position. It removes the accusation, so the other side can move without losing face.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Same Plan, Again

One video call, three recordings, and the email that follows

Dialogue

Twelve Months, or Four

A
Thanks for making the time, Fabio. So, twelve months for the full integration. Honestly, that is standard for a bank of your size.
F
I take your point, but I would push back a little on that assumption. Twelve months might be standard for a full rollout. It would not be viable for a first corridor.
A
With respect, every bank tells us that at the start, and every bank ends up needing twelve months.
F
That is a fair observation. However, the delay usually comes from the scope, not from the bank. If we reduced it to one corridor, we would need four months, not twelve.
A
Four months could be possible. But then settlement stays on our standard stablecoin model. That part is not flexible.
F
That is our one real constraint. Stablecoin settlement might not be approved by our compliance team this year. Honestly, that could have been tested at the proof-of-concept stage, and it was not.
A
So is that a deal-breaker for you?
F
Not necessarily. Here is a counterproposal: one corridor, conventional settlement for the first four months, and we revisit the stablecoin question in phase two.
A
That is a concession on our side. What do we get in return?
F
A reference case with our name on it, and a decision date in writing. I would say that is worth more than a twelve-month contract that nobody signs.
Comprehension

About Anand

1. How does Anand justify the twelve months, twice?
First he says twelve months is standard for a bank of that size. Then he says every bank asks for less at the start and ends up needing twelve months anyway.
2. What does Anand accept, and what does he say is not flexible?
He accepts that four months could be possible for one corridor, but he says settlement has to stay on their standard stablecoin model.
3. How does Anand react to the counterproposal?
He does not reject it. He calls it a concession on their side and asks what they get in return.
Listening 1 · The Melody

A Door, Not a Wall

A negotiation trainer on why disagreement sounds rude. Sound first — no text.

1. According to her, what is usually the real problem when people disagree in a second language?
It is almost never vocabulary. The problem is that the sentence arrives with no cushion.
2. What does the rise in the voice tell the other person, and what does the fall tell them?
The rise tells them you heard them. The fall tells them you are not moving.
3. What does she say the modal verbs do?
They are just as firm as saying no, but they leave the other person a door to walk through — and people with a door move faster than people facing a wall.
Listening 2 · From the Other Side

The Pushback That Works on Me

A vendor's delivery lead, two hundred negotiations later. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say "your timeline is too long" usually means?
That the bank has not looked at its own approval process.
2. What are the three things that pushback needs to work on him?
A constraint he can see, a number, and a counterproposal.
3. What does he do when a bank only rejects his plan?
He sends the same plan again the next month with a new cover page.
Listening 3 · The Two Rules

Nothing Given for Free

A procurement lead on concessions and on the past. Sound first — no text.

1. What is her first rule, and what example does she give?
Never give a concession without getting one back: if you move on the price, they move on the scope.
2. What happens when you give something away for free?
You teach the other side that pushing works, and they push harder in the next meeting.
3. What does she say instead of "you should have told us", and why?
"This could have been caught earlier, and here is how we make sure it is caught next time." It is the same criticism, but nobody has to defend themselves, so everybody can start fixing it.
Real Document

The Email After the Call

Re: Implementation plan — a counterproposal
From: Fabio Luiz Segura · To: Anand · Cc: trade finance innovation team

Anand, thank you for the call on Tuesday. I take your point that twelve months is common for a full rollout, and I understand why you plan for it.

For a first phase, however, twelve months might not be viable for us. Our compliance review alone takes ninety days, and we would need to see a first result inside the current budget cycle.

Settlement is our one real constraint. I am not sure a stablecoin model would be approved this year, and I think that question could have been tested at the proof-of-concept stage.

Our counterproposal: one corridor, conventional settlement, four months. In return, you would get a reference case with our name on it and a decision date in writing.

Could we agree on a thirty-minute call next week to confirm the scope?

Fabio

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I can disagree with a vendor without saying "no" or "you are wrong", and without moving from my position.
I use might, would need to and could to doubt, to set conditions and to offer alternatives.
I criticize the past with "could have been" and never say "could been".
I never give a concession without asking for something in return.
I know the words: to push back on, counterargument, assumption, viable, scope, constraint, concession, deal-breaker, counterproposal, to hold your ground.
