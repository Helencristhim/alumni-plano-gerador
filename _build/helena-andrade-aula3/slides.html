Chapter 4: The Code

Who Said It, and Does the Writer Believe It?

Told, confirmed, stated, denied -- four verbs, four positions

Grammar Discovery

The Verb Before the That

"The officer told investigators that he had seen the echo forty minutes before the encounter."

"The master confirmed that he had read it during his last certificate renewal."

"He stated that the lookout had been stood down at midnight."

"Rule 19 states that a vessel shall determine whether a close-quarters situation is developing."

Three of these report a person and one reports a document. Which is which -- and why does only one of the four keep its verb in the present?

FormWhat it doesExample
said / told + that + backshiftthe reporting verb is past, so the verb inside moves one step backHe said that the lookout had been stood down.
past perfect insidewhat is reported had already happened before he spokeHe told investigators that he had seen the echo.
states / requires + that, no backshifta regulation is true now, so the verb inside stays in the presentRule 19 states that a vessel shall determine...
shall, in a regulationnot the future. It is an obligation: read it as mustshe shall take avoiding action = she must take avoiding action
The reading trapthe reporting verb carries the writer's own position. Confirmed, stated, claimed, alleged and denied are not interchangeable: swap one for another and you change who the text believes. That swap is a whole question.

In an investigation everything a person says arrives inside a reporting verb. The clause after that tells you WHAT was said; the verb before it tells you what the writer thinks of it. Read the verb first.

Chapter 6: Say It Back

Say It Back

Two recordings, then the case in your own words

Listening 1

The Officer of the Watch

The man who saw the echo, in his own words. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What did he see, when, and what did he do about it?
He had a steady radar echo at about two o'clock, fine on the starboard bow. He plotted it twice, judged that the other vessel had to keep out of his way, and held his course.
2. What does he now accept was wrong?
That in fog the crossing rules do not apply at all. Both vessels should have slowed down and both should have been sounding.
3. What is the one point he refuses to accept?
The suggestion that there was nobody on lookout. He says the lookout was stood down at midnight because the master told him to send the man to the forward store.
Listening 2

What the Rule Actually Says

An examiner reads Rule 19 the way it has to be read. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What is the mistake candidates make with Rule 19?
They read it as if it were the crossing rule with fog added. It is not: Rule 19 replaces the steering rules entirely, and there is no give-way vessel and no stand-on vessel.
2. What are the two duties she names for every vessel?
Every vessel shall proceed at a safe speed adapted to the circumstances, and every vessel that detects another by radar alone shall determine whether a close-quarters situation is developing and take avoiding action in ample time.
3. What does she say about the word shall?
In this document shall is not the future. It is an obligation, and there is no version of the rule in which it is optional.
