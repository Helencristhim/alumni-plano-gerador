Rules of the Sea -- COLREGS and the Language a Regulation Uses

The words a collision regulation is written in and the reported speech an investigation uses. Key words: collision, visibility, give-way vessel, stand-on vessel, restricted waters, lookout, sound signal, distress, anchor watch, traffic separation scheme. Structure: reported speech in technical documentation.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read it inside a real sentence. These are the words the exam text will use.

Collision -- an impact between two vessels, or between a vessel and a fixed object
"There was no collision and no damage."
Visibility -- how far a lookout can see, in the conditions of the moment
"Visibility at the time was reported as half a mile in fog."
Give-way vessel -- the vessel required by the rules to keep out of the way of another
"He expected her to keep clear, because she was the give-way vessel."
Stand-on vessel -- the vessel required to hold her course and speed while the other keeps clear
"In restricted visibility there is no stand-on vessel."
Restricted waters -- narrow or shallow areas where a ship has little room to manoeuvre
"The schedule was never amended after the vessel entered restricted waters."
Lookout -- the crew member posted to watch and listen for danger, by sight and by hearing
"The report concludes that a proper lookout was not maintained."
Sound signal -- a prescribed blast on the whistle that tells other vessels what you are doing
"No sound signal was made at the prescribed intervals."
Distress -- the state of a vessel in grave and imminent danger, needing immediate assistance
"No distress call was transmitted."
Anchor watch -- the duty of monitoring a ship's position while she lies at anchor
"The anchor watch schedule still in use on board had never been amended."
Traffic separation scheme -- a set of one-way lanes that keeps opposing streams of shipping apart
"The two vessels passed inside a traffic separation scheme."

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

The officer of the watch told investigators that he had seen the echo forty minutes before the encounter and that he had expected the other vessel to keep clear. The master confirmed that he had read the manual during his last certificate renewal.

The officer also stated that the lookout had been stood down at midnight, which the master denied. Rule 19 states that a vessel which detects another by radar alone shall determine whether a close-quarters situation is developing. The report observes that a rule read once every five years is not a rule that is known.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech in an Investigation

GRAMMAR

Everything a person says arrives inside a reporting verb. The clause after that tells you what was said; the verb before it tells you what the writer thinks of it. Read the verb first.

FormWhat it doesExample
said / told + that + backshiftthe reporting verb is past, so the verb inside moves one step backHe said that the lookout had been stood down.
past perfect insidewhat is reported had already happened before he spokeHe told investigators that he had seen the echo.
states / requires + that, no backshifta regulation is true now, so the verb inside stays in the presentRule 19 states that a vessel shall determine...
shall, in a regulationnot the future. It is an obligation: read it as mustshe shall take avoiding action = she must take avoiding action
The reading trapthe reporting verb carries the writer's own position. Confirmed, stated, claimed, alleged and denied are not interchangeable: swap one for another and you change who the text believes. That swap is a whole question.
