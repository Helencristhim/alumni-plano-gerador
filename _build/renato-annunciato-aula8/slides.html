Chapter 3: The Code

Will, Going To, or Already Happening

Three futures, and the one your plan needs

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"In October I am presenting to the global team."

"I am going to apply for a national role next year."

"I will send you the timeline tonight."

"When I finish the programme, I will apply."

All four are about the future. Which one is already in a calendar? Which was decided before today? Which is being decided right now?

FormWhen you use itExample
am / is / are + verb-ingA fixed arrangement. There is a date, and usually another person involved.In October I am presenting to the global team.
going to + verbAn intention decided before now, or a prediction with evidence in front of you.I am going to apply next year. Look at the numbers — we are going to miss the target.
will + verbA decision made at the moment of speaking, an offer, a promise, or an opinion about the future.I will send you the timeline tonight. I think the position will open in June.
After when, as soon as, before, untilNo will in that half of the sentence. "When I finish the programme, I will apply."
Negative and questionwill not (won't) · am not going to · Are you presenting...?I am not going to wait for the committee. Will you speak to the plan?
Three trapsNever I go to the congress in November — a future arrangement is never the present simple. Never When I will finish. And a prediction you can SEE takes going to, not will.

The Structure

Three Layers, Thirty Seconds

This is the answer to "where do you see yourself?" — prepared once, and used for the rest of your career.

1 · The aspiration, with a horizon

"My aspiration is a national commercial role, within the next two or three years."

2 · The gap, named by you first

"I know the gap already, and it is English. It is the declared prerequisite, so there is nothing to argue about."

3 · The action, with a date

"I started a programme in August, twice a week, and in October I am presenting to the global team."

Aspiration → gap → action with a date. Never an ambition without a horizon.

Good. Most people make me ask twice. And on readiness — what would the committee say about you today?

Listening

What Actually Decides a Promotion

A director on the room you are not in. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Where does he say promotion decisions are actually made?
Not in the room where they are announced — in ordinary weeks, by people who watched somebody work.
2. What is the first thing he weighs?
Whether the person has made anybody else better. A manager who grows nobody cannot be moved.
3. Which single habit tells him more than any result?
Naming your own gaps before he does.
4. What is he NOT looking for, and what is he looking for instead?
Not perfect English — whether you can hold a room that is not in your own language.
Listening 2

Why Development Plans Fail

Four hundred plans, one pattern. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What do all the failed development plans have in common?
They are honest and thoughtful, and they have no dates.
2. What is her phrase for a plan with no date?
A wish with better formatting.
3. What is the second, quieter failure?
Writing the plan about things you cannot control, such as whether a position opens next year.
4. Why should you tell your manager what you are working on before they ask?
Because the person who is going to speak about you in a committee needs sentences to use.
Chapter 5: Practice

Your Plan

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "Next month I will travel to Boston, the flight is already booked."
"Next month I am flying to Boston, the flight is already booked." (a fixed arrangement takes the present continuous)
2. "I go to the congress in November."
"I am going to the congress in November." (a future arrangement is never the present simple)
3. "When I will finish the course, I will apply for the position."
"When I finish the course, I will apply for the position." (no will after when)
4. "Look at these numbers — the region will miss the target."
"Look at these numbers — the region is going to miss the target." (a prediction with the evidence in front of you takes going to)
