Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A vineyard -- a piece of land where grapes grow for wine
"We walked through the vineyard before lunch."
A winery -- a place where wine is made, and often sold to visitors
"The winery is a family business, more than a hundred years old."
A wine tasting -- an event where you try small amounts of different wines
"The tour ends with a wine tasting of three wines."
A grape -- a small round fruit, green or purple, used to make wine
"This wine is made from only one grape."
A barrel -- a large round container made of wood, used to keep wine
"Each barrel holds about three hundred bottles of wine."
To age -- to keep wine for a long time so that it gets better
"They age this red wine for two years."
A tour guide -- a person who shows a place to visitors and explains it
"Our tour guide spoke English, Italian and French."
Dry -- not sweet (for wine)
"I prefer a dry white wine with fish."
A cellar -- a room under a building, cool and dark, where wine is kept
"The cellar is ten meters under the house."
A harvest -- the time when farmers pick the grapes, or the grapes that they pick
"The harvest usually starts in September."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last spring, Ana visited a winery in Italy. The tour guide spoke only English, and Ana understood almost everything. They walked through the vineyard, and the guide showed them the grapes. Ana wanted to ask something, but she didn’t. In her head, the question was not ready.

This time she has a plan. Before her next trip, she practiced a few openings. In the cellar, she will ask: “Could you tell me how long the wine ages in the barrel?” During the wine tasting, she will ask: “Do you know which grape this is?”

And at the end, she has one more question: “I was wondering if I could buy a bottle of the dry white.” The answer, of course, will be yes. She just needs to ask.

1. Why does the text say “how long the wine ages” and not “how long does the wine age”?
A Because the sentence is about the past, and in the past we never use do or does in a question about wine.
B Because age is an irregular verb.
C Because after Could you tell me, the rest of the question has the order of a normal sentence.
2. What happened on her first visit to the winery?
A She understood almost everything, but she did not ask her question.
B She asked the guide many questions about the grapes, the cellar and the barrels in English.
C She did not understand the guide.
3. Why does she use “I was wondering if” for her last question?
A Because it is a question about a place, and questions about a place always start with I was wondering.
B Because it is a yes / no question.
C Because it is a question about the past.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Indirect Questions

GRAMMAR

To sound polite, start with an opening: Could you tell me...? / Do you know...? Then use the order of a normal sentence: wh-word + subject + verb, with no do / does / did. For yes / no questions, use if: I was wondering if...

JobFormExample
Wh- questionsCould you tell me / Do you know + wh-word + subject + verb“Could you tell me where the cellar is?”
Yes / no questionsI was wondering if / Do you know if + subject + verb“Do you know if the winery is open on Sunday?”
No do / does / didthe verb takes its normal form: starts, ages, opened“Could you tell me when the harvest starts?”
With bethe subject goes before is / are“Do you know how old the barrels are?”
The punctuationCould you tell me / Do you know ends with ?; I was wondering if ends with a period“I was wondering if we could visit the cellar.”
The trapafter the opening, never question orderNot Could you tell me where is the cellar? Say where the cellar is.

Never: Could you tell me where is...? · Do you know when does...? · I was wondering if could we... · Could you tell me what means...?

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to know where the cellar is. You ask the guide:
A "Could you tell me where is the cellar, please, before the tasting starts?"
B "Could you tell me where the cellar?"
C "Could you tell me where the cellar is?"
You want to know the date of the harvest. You ask:
A "Do you know when the harvest starts?"
B "Do you know when does the harvest start this year in this region of Italy?"
C "Do you know when starts the harvest?"
You want to visit the vineyard after lunch. You say:
A "I was wondering if could we visit the vineyard after lunch with the guide."
B "I was wondering if we could visit the vineyard."
C "I was wondering we visit the vineyard."
The guide uses a word you do not know. You say:
A "Sorry, could you tell me what that word means?"
B "Sorry, could you tell me what does that word mean in English, please?"
C "Sorry, what that word means?"
