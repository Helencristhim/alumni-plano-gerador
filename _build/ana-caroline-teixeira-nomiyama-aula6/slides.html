Chapter 4: The Code

Could You Tell Me Where It Is?

A friendly opening, then the order of a normal sentence

Grammar Discovery

Where Is It, or Where It Is?

"Could you tell me how long the wine ages in the barrel?"

"Do you know where the cellar is?"

"I was wondering if we could try the dry white."

"Could you tell me when the harvest starts?"

Look at the red words: they are the friendly opening. Now look at the blue words: the subject comes first, then the verb, like in a normal sentence. There is no do or does in the blue part. And when the question is a yes / no question, the word if joins the two parts.

JobFormExample
Wh- questionsCould you tell me / Do you know + wh-word + subject + verb“Could you tell me where the cellar is?”
Yes / no questionsI was wondering if / Do you know if + subject + verb“Do you know if the winery is open on Sunday?”
No do / does / didthe verb takes its normal form: starts, ages, opened“Could you tell me when the harvest starts?”
With bethe subject goes before is / are“Do you know how old the barrels are?”
The punctuationCould you tell me / Do you know ends with ?; I was wondering if ends with a period“I was wondering if we could visit the cellar.”
The trapafter the opening, never question orderNot Could you tell me where is the cellar? Say where the cellar is.

In one line: friendly opening + subject + verb: Could you tell me where the cellar is?

Listening 1 · A Tour Guide

The Guide Who Loves Questions

An English tour guide who lives in Tuscany talks about her visitors. Sound first — no text.

1. Why did she move to Tuscany?
She came for a summer job at a winery, and she never left.
2. What do many visitors do at the end of her tour?
They say nothing, but later they ask her their questions in private.
3. What is her favorite question from visitors, and why?
How the harvest works, because she can tell the story of the grapes.
Listening 2 · A Winemaker

Harvest Time Down Under

An Australian winemaker explains how the harvest works on his family vineyard. Sound first — no text.

1. When does the harvest happen in Australia, and why?
In February and March, because the seasons are opposite to Europe.
2. Why do they pick the grapes at night?
Because it is cooler, and the grapes stay fresh.
3. How long do they age their red wine?
About eighteen months, in old barrels.
Listening 3 · A Traveler

The Day I Finally Asked

A traveler from the Netherlands remembers the first time he asked a question on a tour. Sound first — no text.

1. What was his problem on tours in English?
He understood almost everything, but he never asked a question.
2. What did a friend teach him?
To start with a short opening, like Could you tell me, to get a second to think.
3. What happened when he asked his first question?
The guide was happy, and she opened a special old wine for the group.
