Chapter 3: The Tense With No Date

Experience, Without a Calendar

The form English uses when the point is that it happened, not when

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Do?

"I have worked with teams in Germany, in the Netherlands and in India."

"I moved to that company in 2020 and I stayed for two years."

"Have you ever been to Tennessee?"

"I spent three months there last year, and I did not understand a word for the first week."

The blue lines tell you WHEN. The red ones do not, and they are not being vague by accident.

the words that pull the perfectever, never, already, beforeWe have met before.
the trapa date kills the perfect, alwaysNot I have moved in 2020. Say I moved in 2020.

In one line: if you say when, use the past simple. If you refuse to say when, use have plus the participle.

Dialogue

Two Coffees at the Back of the Room

G
Is this seat taken? Greg Sullivan, finance, out of the Chicago office. I think you presented right before lunch.
J
I did, yes. Jessica Piffer, tax. Please, sit down — the coffee here is the only reason anybody stays for the afternoon.
G
Good to know. So how long have you been with the company?
J
Two years here. Before that I have worked with a German team and then a Dutch one, so I have spent most of my career on calls at strange hours.
G
Then we have something in common. I did four years in Amsterdam. Have you ever been to the Chicago office?
J
Not yet. I have been to Tennessee, though — three months, a few years ago. I understood almost nothing for the first week.
G
Nobody understands Tennessee in the first week. My wife is from Memphis and I still ask her to repeat things. Small world — what took you there?
J
A project with the American side of a Brazilian group. I learned more in those three months than in two years of classes. And you — how often do you come down here?
Comprehension

About Greg

1. How does Greg introduce himself, and where does he work?
He says he is Greg Sullivan, from finance, out of the Chicago office, and he mentions that he saw her present before lunch.
2. What experience does Greg discover that they share?
He spent four years in Amsterdam, so he has also worked with a Dutch team.
3. What does Greg say about understanding people in Tennessee?
He says nobody understands Tennessee in the first week, and that his wife is from Memphis and he still asks her to repeat things.
Listening 1 · A Voice Note

What the Host Remembers on the Way Home

An American manager recording a note to herself after three days of hosting a visiting team. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say she cannot remember about the visit?
She cannot remember a single slide from the four presentations, and she says she was in all of them.
2. What does she remember instead, and about whom?
She remembers the woman from tax who told her about a case she had read about, and the man who talked about running.
3. What does she say about the people who only spoke in the meetings?
She says she could not name them a week later, and that it had nothing to do with how good their English was.
Listening 2 · Business Radio

The Detail That Makes You Memorable

A short radio piece on what people actually remember after a conference. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the researchers ask people to do, and how long after the event?
They asked people to name everybody they had spoken to, six weeks after the event.
2. What was the one thing the remembered people had in common?
Every one of them had said something about their life outside work, and it did not matter what it was.
3. What does the piece say about people who only talked about their job?
They were described by their job title instead of their name, and often nobody could remember the name at all.
