Lesson 22 -- Pre-class

Learning from the Incident

Reading what a cancer hospital learned after six hours without a system, and the new protocol of the Oncology Network. Key words: backup, prevent, protocol, drill, staff, mistake, required, lesson learned. Structure: have to, has to, had to and don't have to -- That night, we had to use paper. Now, every team has to do a drill.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Backup -- an extra copy or system you can use when the first one fails
"We keep a backup of every patient file in another city."
Prevent -- to stop something bad before it happens
"A good plan can prevent a long crash."
Protocol -- the official list of steps for a situation
"The new protocol has only five steps."
Drill -- a practice exercise for an emergency
"We have a fire drill twice a year."
Staff -- all the people who work in a place
"All the staff have to know the plan."
Mistake -- something you do wrong, without wanting to
"Everybody makes mistakes. Good teams learn from them."
Required -- necessary, because a rule says so
"The training is required for all new nurses."
Lesson learned -- something useful you understand after a problem
"Our biggest lesson learned: always have a plan B."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On the night of the crash, Fernando's team had to finish the call with Boston by phone, and the doctors had to use paper forms. The next morning, Fernando wrote a new protocol for the Oncology Network.

Now the backup server has to be in a second building, and every team has to do a drill twice a year. New staff have to do the training in their first month. But nobody has to read twenty pages: the protocol is one page, so the staff don't have to remember it. Sarah read it and said: "This is a real lesson learned."

1. "The doctors had to use paper forms." When was this necessary?
A Every day, as a normal rule of the hospital for all the doctors and nurses of the network.
B On the night of the crash, in the past.
C Next year, in the future.
2. Why does the text say "every team has to" and not "every team have to"?
A Because has to is more formal, and this text is a formal document for the staff of the hospital.
B Because has to is the past form of have to.
C Because "every team" is singular, like it.
3. "The staff don't have to remember it." What does this mean?
A It is not necessary for them to remember it.
B They cannot remember it, because the hospital says it is a secret document that only the managers can read.
C They forgot it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Have to, Has to, Had to

GRAMMAR

Have to tells the rule. Had to tells the story. Don't have to gives freedom.

FormRuleExample
Affirmative (now)I / you / we / they have to + verb · he / she / it has to + verbEvery team has to do a drill.
Pasthad to + verb (the same for all people)That night, we had to use paper.
Negativedon't / doesn't have to + verb = it is not necessaryYou don't have to print it.
QuestionDo / Does + subject + have to + verb? · Did + subject + have to + verb?Do we have to do the drill today?
The trapAfter have to, use the base form of the verbhad to write (not had to wrote)

Use have to for rules and necessary actions. Use don't have to when there is no rule: you can do it, but you do not need to.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Rules after a problem. Choose the best thing to say.

A new nurse asks if the training is optional. You say:
A "Yes, it is optional, but the director has to do it first, and then everybody can decide."
B "No, you have to do it in your first month. It is required."
C "No, you has to do it."
Sarah asks what happened on the night of the crash. You say:
A "We had to finish the call by phone."

C "We had to finished the call."
