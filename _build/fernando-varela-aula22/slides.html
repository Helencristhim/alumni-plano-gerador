Chapter 4: The Code

Rules Now, Rules Then

have to · has to · had to · don't have to

Grammar Discovery

What Do the Orange Words Say?

"That night, the staff had to work with paper and pens."

"Now, all the staff have to do a drill twice a year."

"A new nurse has to finish the training in the first month."

"They don't have to remember the whole protocol."

Look at the orange words. Which one is about the past? Which one is for he or she? And which one means it is not necessary?

FormUseExample
I / you / we / they + have to + verbA rule or a necessary action now.We have to do a drill twice a year.
he / she / it + has to + verbThe same, with he, she or it.A new nurse has to do the training.
had to + verbA necessary action in the past. The same for all people.That night, we had to use paper.
don't / doesn't have toIt is not necessary. You can, but you do not need to.You don't have to print it.
QuestionDo / Does + subject + have to + verb? · Did + subject + have to + verb?Do we have to do the drill today?
The trapAfter have to, the verb is ALWAYS the base form: had to write — never had to wrote.

Have to tells the rule. Had to tells the story. Don't have to gives freedom.
