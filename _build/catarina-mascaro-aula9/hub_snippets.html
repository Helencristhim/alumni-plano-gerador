Stage 1.1: Story Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. These are the words that give a story its rhythm.

Suddenly — it happened fast and nobody expected it
"Suddenly the lights went out."
While — at the same time as something else
"While I was cooking, she was on the phone."
Right then — exactly at that second, not before or after
"Right then, my phone died."
Out of nowhere — with no warning at all
"And then, out of nowhere, he started laughing."
Run into — to meet someone by accident, with no plan
"I ran into my old teacher at the mall."
Realize — the moment you finally understand something
"Then I realized I was on the wrong bus."
Freeze — to stop moving completely, usually from surprise
"Everyone looked at me and I just froze."
Crash — when two things hit each other, or two plans clash
"My laptop crashed while I was writing it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the story. Then answer the questions.

Yesterday I was listening to music on the bus and I was reading something at the same time, so I was not looking outside. Then I realized nothing looked familiar. Right then, my phone died. Zero percent. I got off at the next stop and I froze — I had no map, no music and no idea where I was. While I was standing there, a car stopped, and out of nowhere I ran into my cousin. She was going to the same place. So, technically, the wrong bus was the right bus.
Why "was listening" but "realized"?
A Listening took time; realizing happened in one second.
B Because "realize" is an irregular verb.
What was she doing when the car stopped?
A She was reading.
B She was standing at the stop with no idea where she was.
Why does the story use "while" before "I was standing there"?
A Because "while" goes before the long action.
B Because "while" always starts a sentence.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

One long action. One short one that cuts it.

Background → was / were + -ing. The event → the past simple from lesson 3.

The long one

I was watching
she was talking
they were waiting

The short one

my phone died
I walked in
it started to rain

I was watching a video when my phone died. (when + SHORT)
While I was watching a video, my phone died. (while + LONG)
? What were you doing when it happened? (not "What you were doing")
✗ They was waiting · ✓ They were waiting

In a story, never stress was. The strong word is the -ing one.

Stage 4: In the conversation

Quiz
Somebody starts a story and stops. What do you ask to keep it going?
A "Ok."
B "Wait — what were you doing when it happened?"
Two things happened at the same time, and both took a while. Which is correct?
A "While I was cooking, she was talking on the phone."
B "While I cooked, she talked on the phone at the same time."
Which sentence is correct?
A "What you were doing when I called?"
B "What were you doing when I called?"

Stage 5: A real story

Reflection
Record 45 seconds in English. Tell one story that really happened to you, with a surprise in the middle. Start with what was already going on ("I was..."), then say what cut in ("...when..."), and use "while" at least once. Use three words from Stage 1.1. Finish with a question for your teacher.
