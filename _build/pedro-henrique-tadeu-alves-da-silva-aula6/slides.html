Note: any workaround used during a live week must be written in the handover notes the same day. Nothing in the Expected section is enforced, and everything in it is noticed.
1. Two lines on this page are both Required, but they come from different places. What is the difference?
The pass and the food rule were decided by the studio itself. The Thursday delivery was decided by the broadcaster, outside the building. In English the first takes must and the second usually takes have to.
2. The note says nothing in the Expected section is enforced, and everything in it is noticed. What does that mean in practice?
Nobody will stop you or punish you for breaking those rules, but they decide whether you are asked back. They are the should rules: advice with consequences.
3. What does the page require if somebody uses a workaround during a live week?
It must be written in the handover notes the same day.

Grammar Discovery

Who Decided Each of These?

"You must not bring food into the control room."

"We have to deliver on Thursday, because the broadcaster has the slot."

"You should flag a problem the same day."

"Can I use studio two after eleven?"

The red ones are not optional, and they do not come from the same place. The blue ones are optional in completely different ways.

JobFormExample
Obligation from insidemust + base verbYou must wear the pass. We must write it in the handover notes.
Obligation from outsidehave to / has to + base verbWe have to deliver on Thursday. He has to be on call one weekend in four.
Prohibitionmust not / cannot + base verbYou must not bring food in. You cannot enter studio two after eleven.
No obligation (NOT prohibition)do not have to + base verbYou do not have to stay tonight. It is allowed, it is just not required.
Adviceshould / should not + base verbYou should flag it the same day. You should not cut in during playback.
Permissioncan (normal) · may (formal)Can I use studio two? May I take five minutes of your time?
The trapmust not and do not have to are OPPOSITESYou must not stay = forbidden. You do not have to stay = not necessary.

In one line: must came from inside, have to came from outside, should came from nobody — and do not have to is not a prohibition at all.

Listening 1 · The Three Things

Nobody Is Sent Home for Forgetting a Lanyard

A first-day briefing that goes past the printed rules. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say about the two printed pages?
Read them and follow them, but understand that they are not what anyone will judge you on. Nobody has ever been asked to leave a studio for forgetting a lanyard.
2. What are the first two things people are actually asked to leave for?
Talking during playback, and discovering a problem at nine in the morning but mentioning it at six in the evening, when it costs four times as much to solve.
3. What is the third one, and what is the only rule she would put on the wall?
Being right in the wrong tone of voice: you can be the most technically correct person in the room and still be the reason the session slowed down. Her rule: if you have to choose between being right and being useful, be useful first, and be right about thirty seconds later.
Listening 2 · Two Kinds of Deadline

Is This Date Fixed, or Is This Date a Preference?

A producer on the word that means two different things and is never separated out loud. Sound first — no text.

1. What makes a date a real deadline, in his definition?
It exists because of physics or a contract: the show goes out on Sunday at nine and the broadcaster has the slot. Nothing you do on Saturday night changes it, and if you miss it there is no show.
2. What is the other kind of date, and what does he say about wanting one?
A date that exists because somebody wanted a comfortable margin. He says that is a perfectly reasonable thing to want, but it is not the same thing at all.
3. What did he do for the first four years, and what does he do now?
He treated both kinds identically and stayed until four in the morning for dates that moved the following week. Now he asks one question early, in a friendly voice: is this date fixed, or is this date a preference?
