Lesson 7 -- Going Deeper

Grammar Video
"Discourse Markers: Sound Fluent & Natural in English Conversations" — mmmEnglish

How the little words that link ideas actually work in speech — where they go, what each one promises the listener, and why leaving them out makes correct English sound abrupt. Connection to Lesson 7: this is the toolbox for the second and third sentence.

Tip: watch it once, then pick three markers you do not currently use and say one sentence with each about your own week. Three is the right number — a marker you never say is one you will never find in time.

Podcast
"How to be a supercommunicator" — Life Kit, NPR

Charles Duhigg on what separates people who are good at conversation from everybody else: mostly asking the next question and building on what the other person just said. Connection to Lesson 7: it is the second listening of this lesson, from the other side of the table.

Tip: listen for the moments where the host does NOT stop after one sentence. Count them. Then try the same thing in your next call and notice how much longer the conversation lasts.

Reading
"Transitions and Transitional Devices" — Purdue OWL

The reference list American universities send their own students to: transitional words and phrases sorted by the job they do — adding, contrasting, exemplifying, concluding. Connection to Lesson 7: it is the four moves of this lesson, written out in full.

Tip: do not read the whole page. Take the "contrast" and the "conclusion" columns only, and pick two from each. Those four will cover most of what you need for a TOEFL essay and for a meeting.
