Lesson 05 — Pre-class

What’s the Plan?

Eight words for what has not happened yet (plan, decide, happen, probably, maybe, hope, look forward to, I’d rather) and the difference between "I’m going to" (decided before) and "I’ll" (deciding right now).

Stage 1.1: Future Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Plan — something you decide to do before you do it
"We have a plan for Saturday."
Decide — to choose one thing and stop thinking about the others
"I decided to stay home."
Happen — to take place, often without anyone planning it
"Something is going to happen next week."
Probably — you are almost sure it is true
"I am probably going to be late."
Maybe — you are not sure at all
"Maybe I will go. I have not decided."
Hope — to want something good to happen
"I hope it does not rain on Saturday."
Look forward to — to feel happy about something that is coming
"I am looking forward to the holidays."
I’d rather — to prefer one option to another
"I would rather stay home than go to a party."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. Then answer the questions.

Next month is going to be busy. On the third I have an English test, so I am probably going to study the night before. On the eighth it is my cousin’s birthday and the whole family is going. On the fourteenth we are going to visit the science museum with school — I am really looking forward to that one. On the twenty-first the new season drops, so I am not going to sleep that night. And on the twenty-ninth, maybe the beach. Nobody has decided. Honestly, I would rather stay home that weekend than travel two hours in a car.
Which date is NOT decided yet?
A The fourteenth.
B The twenty-ninth.
Why does she use "going to" and not "will" for the museum?
A Because it was decided before — the school already organised it.
B Because it is far away in the future.
What does she prefer for the twenty-ninth?
A To travel to the beach.
B To stay home, rather than sit two hours in a car.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

The future: it is about when you decided

Decided before you spoke → going to. Deciding while you speak → will.

Going to

I am going to study tonight.
She is going to call you.
We are not going to stay home.

Will

Your bag is heavy — I’ll help.
Okay, I’ll text you later.
I’ll have the same, thanks.

+ I am going to see my friends on Saturday.
− She is not going to come. (after "going to", the verb stays normal)
? Are you going to watch it? (not "Do you going to")

In speech you will hear gonna. Understand it — but always write going to.

Stage 3: Say it out loud

Speaking

Tap Listen, then Record. Say "I’m going to" fast, as one block — that is how it sounds in real speech.

I’m going to see my friends on Saturday.
I’d rather stay home than go out.
And you? What are you going to do?

Stage 4: In the conversation

Quiz
A friend invites you somewhere and you already have plans. What do you say?
A "No."
B "I can’t — I’m going to my cousin’s birthday. What about Sunday?"
Someone drops their books in front of you. What do you say?
A "I’ll help you."
B "I’m going to help you."
You prefer one option to the other. How do you say it?
A "I’d rather to stay home."
B "I’d rather stay home than go out."

Stage 5: Your next month

Reflection
