Grammar Discovery

Always true, or true right now?

I play volleyball on Saturdays.
I am learning two languages this year.
She lives in Manchester.
She is staying with her cousin this week.

One tense for your life, one for your week

The present simple is for things that are true in general: routines, facts, the person you are. The present continuous (am / is / are + verb-ing) is for what is happening now, or around now, and will probably change.

The Code

Two forms, and the words that give it away

Present simple

I play volleyball.
She plays volleyball.
I don't play in a team.
Do you play every week?

Present continuous

I am learning English.
She is learning Spanish.
I am not learning French.
Are you learning it now?
These pull the simple:
every week, on Saturdays, usually, most of the time, never
These pull the continuous:
at the moment, right now, this week, this year, today

Did You Catch It?

About Ruby

1. Where does Ruby live, and what does she do in her spare time?
She lives in Manchester, and she draws.
2. What is she learning at the moment, and how is it going?
She is learning Spanish, and it is going badly.
3. Why does she say her cousin is basically her sister?
Because she is an only child, and her cousin lives on the same street and goes to the same school.
Listening 1

Ruby's voice message

1. What two things is she into?
Drawing, mostly people, and playing the drums.
2. What is she doing at the moment, and what does she ask for?
She is learning Spanish at school, it is going terribly, and she asks for help from anyone who speaks Spanish.
3. Who does she hang out with most of the time, and why?
Her cousin, because she lives on the same street.
Listening 2

How the club works

1. When does the club meet, and for how long?
Every Wednesday at five, always for one hour.
2. What does everyone always do at the start?
Say three things about themselves and ask one question back.
3. What is different this month?
They are pairing every new member with somebody from a different country, so this week you talk to one person, not the whole group.
