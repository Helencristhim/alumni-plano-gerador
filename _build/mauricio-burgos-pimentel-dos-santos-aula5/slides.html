The Pattern

What do these eight have in common?

To be honest, the quarter is going badly.
In short, we are thirty-one against forty.
That said, the migration finishes in December.
The point is, we need two temporary people.

They buy you time — and they hold the floor

None of them adds information. Each one gives you two seconds to build the next sentence, and tells the listener what is coming: a summary, an objection, an opinion, the main idea. This is what fluent people actually use — not bigger words.

Did You Catch It?

About David’s pitch

1. Which four things did he cover, in order?
The role, the career, the current quarter, and November.
2. What does he admit with "to be honest"?
That he never planned to stay twenty years.
3. What does he say the point of presenting is?
Getting feedback from people outside banking.
Listening 1

A pitch that works

1. What does he run, and how big is it?
Supply chain for a food company in Chile — about ninety people across four sites.
2. What is happening at the moment, and how does he describe the year?
They are closing one of the sites — not the calmest year of his career.
3. Why is he going to present the site closure?
Because it is the hardest thing he has ever managed, and he would rather talk about the difficult one.
Listening 2

What the room actually remembers

1. What does the room NOT remember by Friday?
Job titles — who was a director and who was a head of something.
2. What does the room remember instead?
One number and one problem.
3. What is the advice for planning the two minutes?
Do not polish the title — pick your number and your problem, and say them slowly.
