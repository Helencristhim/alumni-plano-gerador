Stage 1.1: Words for a Case

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say each word out loud with your own numbers. Eight words, three minutes.

Summarize — to make something long short, keeping only what matters
"Let me summarize in three sentences."
Analysis — looking at the parts of a problem to understand it
"Our analysis showed the opposite."
Findings — what you discovered when you looked
"The findings surprised everybody."
Recommendation — what you say should be done
"My recommendation was to stop and rebuild."
Impact — the effect something has, in real terms
"The impact was two lost quarters."
Implement — to actually put a decision into practice
"Deciding is easy. Implementing is not."
Evaluate — to judge how good or bad something turned out
"We evaluated it six months later."
Root cause — the real reason underneath the obvious one
"The root cause was not the system."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the text. Then answer the questions.

Let me summarize before I start. Initially, we were running two systems in parallel, which everybody said was impossible and which we did anyway. At that point the regulator changed the deadline, and while we were rebuilding the plan, two of my best people resigned. Our analysis showed something uncomfortable: the delay was not technical. The findings pointed at us. As a result, we lost two quarters and closed the year at thirty-one deals against a target of forty. In the end, the root cause was that nobody wanted to tell the board it was late. My recommendation is that bad news should reach the board in the same week it is known. We implemented that in January and we evaluated it in June: it works, and it is uncomfortable. That is the case. Happy to take questions.
What was going on when the regulator changed the deadline?
A They were running two systems in parallel.
B They were closing the year.
Why is it "we were rebuilding… when two people resigned"?
A The rebuilding was the long situation; the resignations happened inside it.
B Because both things took a long time.
What was the root cause?
A The system was too old.
B Nobody wanted to tell the board it was late.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Two layers in every story

The situation already going on → was / were + -ing. The event that lands in the middle → past simple.

The background

We were migrating the system…
I was preparing the budget…
While we were restructuring…

The event

…when the regulator changed the rules.
…when the invitation arrived.
…two people resigned.

Initially, … → where the story starts
At that point, … → the thing that changed everything
As a result, … → the impact, with a number
In the end, … → the root cause and the recommendation

Summarize — not "resume". Resume in English means to start again after a pause.

Stage 4: In the room

Quiz
Which sentence puts the background and the event in the right shapes?
A "We migrated the system when the regulator changed the rules."
B "We were migrating the system when the regulator changed the rules."
You want to say the long thing short. Which verb?
A "Let me resume the case."
B "Let me summarize the case."
Which opening has the room after thirty seconds?
A "I am going to talk about a project we carried out over an eighteen-month period…"
B "We closed a system, it went badly, and the reason was not the one in the report."

Stage 5: Your case in four minutes

Reflection
Record four minutes in English. Present one real case from your desk, in this order: the background (was/were + -ing), the event that landed in it, what your analysis showed, the impact with one number, the root cause, and your recommendation. Use all four carrier words: initially, at that point, as a result, in the end. Finish with "Happy to take questions" — and mean it.
