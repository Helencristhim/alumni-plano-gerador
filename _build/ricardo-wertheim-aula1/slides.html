Chapter 3: The Code

Who You Are, Every Day

I live · she speaks · do you travel?

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I live in Sao Paulo."

"I work as an architect."

"My wife speaks English very well."

"Dubrovnik has a beautiful old town."

Look at the orange verbs. Why do two of them end in -s?

FormUseExample
I / you / we / they + verbFacts about you and things you do regularlyI live in Sao Paulo.
he / she / it + verb + sThe same idea, but for one other person or thingShe speaks English. It has an old town.
Negative
don't / doesn't + verb		The -s moves to does, so the verb goes back to plain formI don't like tourist menus. He doesn't travel much.
Question
Do / Does + person + verb		Same rule: does carries the -sDo you travel often? Does she live here?

One rule, four shapes: the -s belongs to he, she and it — and when does appears, it takes the -s for itself.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I have 73 years."
"I am 73 years old."
"My wife speak three languages."
"My wife speaks three languages."
"I no like crowded places."
"I don't like crowded places."

Age uses to be, not to have. The -s belongs to he, she, it. And English never negates with a bare no before a verb — it uses don't and doesn't.

Chapter 4: The Hotel Lobby

Checking In at the Guesthouse

Dubrovnik, four in the afternoon

Dialogue

Welcome to Dubrovnik

E
Good afternoon, and welcome to the Old Town Guesthouse. Are you checking in?
R
Yes, good afternoon. My name is Ricardo Wertheim, and I have a reservation for four nights.
E
Perfect. I'm Elena, and I work here at the front desk. Where are you from?
R
I'm from Sao Paulo, in Brazil. I'm an architect, so I'm really here for the architecture.
E
Then you're in the right place. I'm from Ljubljana, but I live here now, and I still wander through these streets every morning.
R
That's exactly why I came. Where do you usually eat? I want the local cuisine, not a tourist menu.
E
I always eat at a small place behind the cathedral. The family cooks fish the old way, and they don't have a menu in English.
