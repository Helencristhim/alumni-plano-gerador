Brand Representation -- Have To and Don't Have To

A foreign brand is looking for a representative in Brazil. Read the rules of the job and say what is necessary with have to (She has to visit the buyers) and what is not necessary with don't have to (You don't have to pay to start). New words: representative, distributor, agreement, exclusive, territory, commission, catalog, shelf.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Learn the eight new words for brand representation. Tap Listen to hear each one.

Representative -- a person who sells and speaks for a company in a place
"NatureCare wants a representative in Brazil." (representante)
Distributor -- a company that buys products and takes them to the stores
"The distributor buys the products from the brand." (distribuidor)
Agreement -- an official plan that two sides accept and sign
"The agreement is for two years." (contrato, acordo)
Exclusive -- only for one person or company, not for others
"The agreement is exclusive." (exclusivo)
Territory -- the area where you work, for example a state or a country
"Her territory is the state of Sao Paulo." (território)
Commission -- money you receive as a part of every sale you make
"She receives a commission of eight percent." (comissão)
Catalog -- a book or file with all the products of a company
"Show the catalog to the pharmacy buyer." (catálogo)
Shelf -- the long flat place in a store where products stay
"Our sunscreen has to be on the shelf at eye level." (prateleira)

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text about Gleice and answer the questions.

"Gleice reads the agreement from NatureCare. As the representative, she has to visit twenty buyers every month, and she has to show the catalog to every pharmacy chain. She doesn't have to buy the products, because the distributor buys them. She doesn't have to pay for the trip to Boston, either. Her territory is the state of Sao Paulo, and the agreement is exclusive. She receives a commission of eight percent. Her first goal? To put the sunscreen on the shelf of fifty pharmacies."

1. What does Gleice have to do every month?
A She has to buy all the products from NatureCare first.
B She has to travel to Boston.
C She has to visit twenty buyers.
2. Why doesn't Gleice have to buy the products?
A Because the distributor buys them.
B Because the products are free.
C Because she works in Boston for the brand every month.
3. Which sentence is true?
A She has to pay for the trip to Boston.
B The agreement is exclusive.
C Her territory is all of Brazil and Mexico in year one.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Have To and Don't Have To

GRAMMAR

Read the explanation and the examples.

Use have to + verbo para o que é NECESSÁRIO (I have to visit the buyers = Eu tenho que visitar os compradores). Com he / she / it, use has to (She has to visit). Use don't / doesn't have to para o que NÃO é necessário -- você pode escolher (You don't have to pay = Você não precisa pagar). Atenção: don't have to NÃO significa proibido. / Use have to for what is necessary, and don't have to for what is not necessary.

Use / UsoStructure / EstruturaExample / Exemplo
Necessary / NecessárioI / you / we / they + have to + verbI have to visit the buyers. (Eu tenho que visitar.)
Necessary / Necessário (3a pessoa)he / she / it + has to + verbShe has to send a report. (Ela tem que mandar.)
Not necessary / Não precisadon't / doesn't have to + verbYou don't have to pay. (Você não precisa pagar.)
Question / PerguntaDo / Does + subject + have to + verb?Do I have to buy the products?
Short answer / Resposta curtaYes, you do. / No, you don't.Does she have to speak Spanish? No, she doesn't.
