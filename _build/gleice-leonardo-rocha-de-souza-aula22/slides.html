Chapter 4 -- The Rules

Necessary or Not Necessary?

have to · has to · don’t have to

Grammar Discovery

What Is Necessary?

"The representative has to visit the buyers."

"You have to send your plan by June thirtieth."

"You do not have to pay anything to start."

"Do you have to speak Spanish? No, you don't."

Three things are necessary. One is not. Which one? And why has in the first sentence?

UseFormExample
Necessary (I, you, we, they)have to + verbYou have to send your plan.
Necessary (he, she, it)has to + verbShe has to visit the buyers.
Not necessary (you can choose)don't / doesn't have to + verbYou don't have to pay to start.
QuestionDo / Does + subject + have to + verb?Do I have to buy the products?
Short answerYes, you do. / No, you don't.Does she have to speak Spanish? No, she doesn't.
Neverhave to + -s or -ing / don't mustNot she has to sends. Not you don't must pay.

In one line: have to = necessary; don’t have to = not necessary, you can choose.

Listening 1 -- The Sales Director

A Message from Jennifer

Jennifer Lowe, NatureCare Skin, explains the job. Sound first -- no text.

1. Does the representative have to be an employee of NatureCare?
No, she doesn't. She works for herself.
2. How often does NatureCare pay the commission?
Every three months.
3. What does the representative have to send every Friday?
A short report about her visits.
Listening 2 -- An Experienced Representative

Before You Sign

A representative with fifteen years in pharmacies gives advice. Sound first -- no text.

1. What three things do you have to check in the agreement?
The territory, the commission and if it is exclusive.
2. Do you have to accept the first offer?
No, you don't. You can ask for a bigger territory or a better commission.
3. Why is the shelf so important, for him?
Because if the product is not on the shelf at eye level, nobody buys it.
