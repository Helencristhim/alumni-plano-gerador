Chapter 3: The Code

Always True, or Right Now

Two forms, and the time word decides which one

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"We integrate with SAP and Oracle. That is what we do."

"We are rolling out a new connector this quarter."

"I report to the CEO and I run the sales team. Every week."

"At the moment I am covering the international pipeline myself."

Look at the blue words. Two of them describe something permanent. Two of them name a period that is happening around now. The red verb follows.

JobFormExample
What the company always doespresent simpleWe integrate with SAP.
What is happening around nowam / is / are + verb-ingWe are rolling out a connector.
Routine, every weekpresent simpleI run the weekly forecast.
Temporary, only for nowam / is / are + verb-ingI am covering the region myself.
He / she / itthe simple form takes -sThe platform connects both systems.
Questions and negativesdo / does + verbWhat does your company do?
Time words: simpleevery week, usually, normally, alwaysWe normally close in sixty days.
Time words: -ingright now, at the moment, this quarter, currentlyWe are hiring this quarter.
Verbs that never take -ingknow, need, want, mean, cost, understandI know this market. Not I am knowing.

In one line: the business takes the simple form; the quarter takes -ing.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The First Call, at Full Speed

One call, two recordings, one company page

Dialogue

First Call with a Partner

S
Thanks for making the time. I read your one-pager this morning, but tell me in your own words: what does Invent actually do?
F
We build software that sits between a customer's ERP and everything else they use. In practice, we integrate with SAP, Oracle and the local systems.
S
And who buys it? Large corporations?
F
Mostly mid-market companies. Four people handle thousands of invoices a month there, and that is the use case we solve.
S
That is clearer than the one-pager, honestly. And you? What is your role there?
F
I am Head of Sales. I report to the CEO, and at the moment I am covering the international pipeline myself, while we hire a director.
S
So you are the person I should be talking to. Are you rolling out anything new this quarter?
F
We are rolling out a connector for a European ERP right now, and that is why I wanted this call. And you? How does your team usually choose the vendors you work with?
Comprehension

About Sarah

1. What had Sarah already done before the call, and what did she ask for anyway?
She had read the one-pager that morning, and she still asked him to say what the company does in his own words.
2. In what order does Sarah ask her questions, and what does that order tell you about a first call?
First what the company does, then who buys it, and only after that what his role is. On a first call the business comes before the person.
3. Her last question is are you rolling out anything new this quarter. Why not do you roll out anything new?
Because she is asking about this quarter, a period that is happening around now. A routine or a permanent fact would take the simple form instead.
Listening 1 · A Sales Director

What We Actually Sell

A sales director answering the question she gets on every first call. Sound first — no text.

1. She says she stopped opening a call with a certain kind of sentence. What kind, and what does she open with now?
She stopped opening with a sentence about the platform and the technology. She opens with the customer’s Monday morning instead: the invoices nobody can find.
2. Who does she say buys the product, and what does she say about the size of those companies?
Mid-market companies. They are big enough to have a real problem and small enough that one person still knows everybody’s name.
3. What is the team doing this quarter, and why does she mention it on a first call?
They are rolling out a new integration to a small group of customers first. She mentions it because a partner wants to know what is moving now, not only what the company always does.
Listening 2 · A Partner Manager

How We Choose Who to Work With

The other side of the table, on what makes a first call work. Sound first — no text.

1. How many calls does he say he takes, and how quickly does he decide?
About six a week, and he says the decision is usually made in the first ten minutes.
2. What does he say is the difference between the vendors he calls back and the ones he does not?
The ones he calls back describe a customer he recognises. The others describe a product he cannot place.
3. What does he say about people whose English is not perfect?
That it has never been the reason he said no. What loses the call is a long answer with no customer in it.

1. Two lines on this page use different verb forms in the paragraph below. Which line becomes a simple form, and which becomes am/is/are + -ing?
What we do becomes the simple form, because it is permanent: Kestrel implements ERP systems. This year: opening an office becomes is opening, because it is happening around now.
2. According to the paragraph, what does Kestrel not do, and what does it do instead?
It does not build software. It chooses the vendors its customers connect to.
3. What two things does the paragraph say Kestrel looks for in a first call?
A customer it recognises, and a partner who answers the phone.
