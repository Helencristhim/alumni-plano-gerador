Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

Thirty Seconds on a Call -- Saying What Your Company Does

A partner joins the call and asks what your company does. The answer has to fit in three sentences before it fits in three minutes. Key words: a lead, a pipeline, to roll out, an integration, a stakeholder, to report to, mid-market, to scale, a use case, to handle. Structure: present simple and present continuous.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A lead -- a company that might become a customer, but has not bought anything yet
"We got forty leads from the event in Orlando."
A pipeline -- all the deals a salesperson is working on at the same time
"My pipeline is bigger this quarter than it was last year."
To roll out -- to make something available step by step, not to everybody at once
"We are rolling out the new connector to ten customers first."
An integration -- a connection that lets two different systems send data to each other
"The integration with SAP takes about three weeks."
A stakeholder -- anybody a decision affects, and whose opinion counts in the room
"There are four stakeholders on their side, and only one of them signs."
To report to -- to have somebody as your direct manager
"I report to the CEO, so decisions do not take three weeks."
Mid-market -- companies that are bigger than a small business and smaller than a corporation
"We sell to mid-market companies, not to the top ten banks."
To scale -- to grow bigger without the team or the system breaking
"The process works with ten customers. The question is whether it scales."
A use case -- one real situation in which a product solves a real problem
"Their strongest use case is closing the month in two days instead of ten."
To handle -- to be responsible for something and deal with it yourself
"Four people handle thousands of invoices a month, and that is the problem."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A partner manager in Rotterdam takes a first call with a software company she has never met. She asks the question she always asks: so, what does your company do? The answer she likes is short. The company builds software that sits between an ERP and everything else a business uses. In practice, it integrates with SAP, Oracle and the local systems. That is what the company does, every day of the year, and it takes the simple form.

Then she asks who buys it. Mostly mid-market companies: big enough to have a real problem, small enough that four people still handle thousands of invoices a month. That is the use case. She writes it down, because a lead she can picture is a lead she calls back.

Her last question is different. She is not asking what the company always does — she wants to know what is moving right now. This quarter the team is rolling out a new integration for a European ERP, and it is hiring two salespeople for the region, because the pipeline has grown faster than the team. The Head of Sales reports to the CEO and, at the moment, he is covering the international accounts himself. He says one more thing before the call ends: the process works with ten customers, and the real question is whether it scales.

1. "It integrates with SAP, Oracle and the local systems." Why not "It is integrating"?
A Because the sentence has three things in a list.
B Because it is permanent: that is what the company does every day, not something happening only now.
C Because software companies never use am/is/are + -ing.
2. "This quarter the team is rolling out a new integration." Why not "the team rolls out"?
A Because this quarter names a period that is happening around now, and it takes am/is/are + -ing.
B Because rolling out is always a slow action.
C Because the team is more than one person.
3. The Head of Sales "reports to the CEO" but "is covering the international accounts himself". Why do the two verbs take different forms in the same sentence?
A Because the second verb is longer than the first.
B Because you cannot use the same form twice in one sentence.
C Because reporting to the CEO is his permanent position, and covering the accounts is temporary: only until they hire a director.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple and Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

One decision, made by the time expression: is it always true, or is it happening around now?

JobFormExample
What the company always doespresent simpleWe integrate with SAP.
What is happening around nowam / is / are + verb-ingWe are rolling out a connector.
Routine, every weekpresent simpleI run the weekly forecast.
Temporary, only for nowam / is / are + verb-ingI am covering the region myself.
He / she / itthe simple form takes -sThe platform connects both systems.
Questions and negativesdo / does + verbWhat does your company do? We do not sell hardware.
Time words that take the simple formevery week, usually, normally, always, neverWe normally close in sixty days.
Time words that take -ingright now, at the moment, this quarter, currently, these daysWe are hiring this quarter.
Verbs that never take -ingknow, need, want, mean, cost, understand, belongI know this market.

Never: We are integrate with SAP · I am knowing this market · At the moment we hire two people · What means a use case? · He report to the CEO

Stage 2: Put the First Call in Order

Order

A partner has just asked what your company does. Put your side of the call in a logical order.

?"We build software that sits between an ERP and everything else."
?"In practice, we integrate with SAP, Oracle and the local systems."
?"Our customers are mid-market companies, not the top ten banks."
?"Four people handle thousands of invoices a month, and that is the use case."
?"I am Head of Sales and I report to the CEO."
?"And you? How does your team usually choose the vendors you work with?"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A partner asks what your company does. You answer:
A "We are integrate with SAP and Oracle."
B "We build software that sits between an ERP and everything else. We integrate with SAP and Oracle."
C "Our software is very good and very complete, you will like it."
The partner asks who buys the product. The answer that keeps her listening is:
A "Mid-market companies. Four people handle thousands of invoices a month there, and that is the use case."
B "Everybody. Any company can use it."
C "We have many customers in many segments and many countries."
She asks what is new this quarter. You say:
A "This quarter we roll out a new integration."
B "This quarter we are rolling out a new integration for a European ERP."
C "This quarter we are roll out a new integration."
You have answered three questions and the call is becoming a monologue. The natural repair is:
A "Sorry, my English is not very good."
B "That is everything about us. Any questions?"
C "And you? How does your team usually choose the vendors you work with?"
