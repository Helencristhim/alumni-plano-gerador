Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

Two Proposals -- Comparing Partnership Offers

Two companies want to sponsor the same event, and you have to recommend one. Key words: a proposal, a sponsor, a benefit, affordable, reliable, cost-effective, visibility, a package, a drawback, a budget. Structure: comparatives, superlatives and not as ... as.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A proposal -- a document or a plan that offers something and asks for a decision
"We received two proposals for the same event."
A sponsor -- a company that pays part of an event in exchange for publicity
"The main sponsor is a Canadian bank."
A benefit -- something good that you receive from a deal or an action
"The biggest benefit is contact with new partners."
Affordable -- not too expensive; at a price people can pay
"The second package is more affordable for small companies."
Reliable -- that you can trust to do what it promises
"They are the most reliable supplier we know."
Cost-effective -- that gives good results for the money you spend
"Online events are more cost-effective than dinners."
Visibility -- how much people see and notice a company or a brand
"The gold package gives more visibility at the event."
A package -- a group of services or benefits sold together at one price
"The silver package includes a booth and two tickets."
A drawback -- a disadvantage, or a problem in a plan
"The only drawback is the date."
A budget -- the amount of money you can spend on something
"Our budget for the event is fifty thousand reais."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Two companies sent a proposal for the business breakfast. The bank offers more money than the logistics company, and it is the most famous name, so it brings more visibility.

But the logistics company is more reliable: it has always paid on time. Its package is smaller, but it is cheaper for the chamber and more flexible. The bank is not as flexible as the logistics company, because it wants a long presentation.

Notice the pattern. Short adjectives take -er (cheaper). Long adjectives take more (more reliable). To compare with all the others, use the most or -est.

1. Which company offers more money?
A The bank.
B The logistics company.
C They offer the same amount.
2. Why is the logistics company more reliable?
A Because it is more famous.
B Because it has always paid on time.
C Because it wants a long presentation.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “The bank is not as flexible than the other company.”
B “The bank is not as flexible as the other company.”
C “The bank is not flexible as the other company.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Comparatives and Superlatives

GRAMMAR

Use -er / more ... than to compare two things, the -est / the most to compare one with all the others, and not as ... as to compare more softly.

JobFormExample
Short adjective, two thingsadjective + -er + thancheaper than, bigger than
Long adjective, two thingsmore + adjective + thanmore reliable than, more flexible than
One against all the othersthe + -est / the most + adjectivethe biggest, the most reliable
Irregulargood / better / the best · bad / worse / the worstThis offer is better. It is the best.
A softer comparisonnot as + adjective + asThe bank is not as flexible as the other one.
The trapnever more + -erNot more cheaper or more better. Say cheaper, better.

Never: more cheap · more better · not as flexible than · the more reliable sponsor we have
