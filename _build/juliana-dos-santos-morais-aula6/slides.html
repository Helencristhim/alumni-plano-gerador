Chapter 4: The Code

Better, the Best, Not as Good As

Three ways to compare

Grammar Discovery

Four Sentences, One Comparison

"The logistics offer is cheaper than the bank offer."

"But the bank is more famous than the logistics company."

"It is the most reliable partner we have."

"The bank is not as flexible as the logistics company."

Look at the blue words. Short adjectives take -er; long adjectives take more. When you compare one thing with all the others, you use the most or -est. The red words connect the two sides.

JobFormExample
Short adjective, two thingsadjective + -er + thancheaper than, bigger than
Long adjective, two thingsmore + adjective + thanmore reliable than, more flexible than
One against all the othersthe + -est / the most + adjectivethe biggest, the most reliable
Irregulargood / better / the best · bad / worse / the worstThis offer is better. It is the best.
A softer comparisonnot as + adjective + asThe bank is not as flexible as the other one.
The trapnever more + -erNot more cheaper or more better. Say cheaper, better.

In one line: short -er, long more, all of them the -est / the most, softer not as ... as.

A comparison table has no verbs. When you present it, you turn each line into a comparative sentence: the bank offers more money, the logistics company is more flexible.
1. Turn the Amount line into a comparative sentence.
The bank offers more money than the logistics company.
2. Which offer is more flexible, and why?
The logistics company, because it only asks for its logo, not a presentation.
3. Give one reason for the recommendation with a superlative.
The logistics company is the most reliable sponsor: it has always paid on time.
Listening 1 · The Event Manager

How I Choose a Sponsor

An event manager explains the three questions she asks about every offer. Sound first — no text.

1. What is her first question about a sponsor?
Is the company a good fit for our members?
2. Why is the biggest offer not always the best one?
Because some sponsors ask for too much in return, like long presentations.
3. What does she say about reliability?
A reliable sponsor that pays on time is more valuable than a famous one that pays late.
Listening 2 · The Sponsor

Why We Chose This Chamber

A marketing director explains why his company sponsors events at a chamber of commerce. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the company do before?
It sponsored big international conferences.
2. Why did it change to smaller events?
Because the big conferences were more expensive and less effective for finding partners.
3. What is the best benefit for his company?
Direct contact with decision makers from member companies.
