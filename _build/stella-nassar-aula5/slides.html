Chapter 4: The Style Code

Confidence, Calibrated

Hedging and concession: the grammar of a trustworthy argument

Discover the Style

Why Does Careful Sound Stronger?

"While the museum has cared for the sculptures, their removal remains contested."

"It is arguably the most famous restitution case in the world."

"Granted, the sculptures survived in London; that alone does not settle who should own them."

"The evidence suggests that the original permit was far narrower than Elgin claimed."

Remove each colored expression. Does the argument become stronger, or only louder?

FormWhat it doesExample
hedging adverbslower certainty to what the evidence supportsarguably, ostensibly, purportedly
hedging framesattribute a claim to evidence or to othersThe evidence suggests that... It has been argued that...
concession + contrastadmits the other side before answering itWhile X, Y. Although X, the fact remains that Y.
concession markerssignal a point granted, and prepare a replyGranted, X; yet Y. To be sure, X. However, Y.
The C2 traphedging everything. A writer who writes "it could perhaps possibly be argued" has no position at all. Hedge the claim that is uncertain, and state the one you can defend.

Build

From Blunt to Calibrated

Say the full sentence, then click to compare

1. The museum stole the marbles. (It has been argued that / contested)
"It has been argued that the removal was, at the very least, legally contested."
2. They were saved in London, so they should stay. (Granted... yet)
"Granted, they survived in London; yet survival alone does not establish ownership."
3. The permit was fake. (purportedly)
"The permit, purportedly issued in 1801, has never been found."
4. Returning them will empty every museum. (While... it does not necessarily follow)
"While one return might encourage others, it does not necessarily follow that museums would be emptied."
Chapter 5: Voices in the Debate

The Question, Out Loud

A seminar debate, a museum director and an archaeologist

Dialogue

The Devil's Advocate in the Seminar

O
I am going to argue for the museum today, and I want you to make it hard for me. Why shouldn't the sculptures stay in London?
S
Because they were removed from a monument that still exists, in a city that has built a museum to receive them.
O
Granted, the Acropolis Museum is extraordinary. But millions of people see the sculptures in London every year, for free, next to objects from the rest of the world.
S
That is a real advantage, I concede that. It does not explain why the owner of the monument should need permission to see its own frieze together.
O
The museum would say it acquired them legally, with a permit from the authorities of the time.
S
A permit that purportedly exists only in a translation, issued by an empire that did not represent the Greeks.
O
Arguably, by that logic, half the objects in every major museum should be sent somewhere else.
S
Perhaps some should. But it does not necessarily follow that every case is identical. This one has a building waiting.
O
That is the most persuasive thing you have said, and you said it quietly.
S
I learned that from you. You conceded twice before you disagreed.
Comprehension

About Oliver

1. Which side does Oliver say he will argue, and what does he ask Stella to do?
He will argue for the museum, and he asks Stella to make it hard for him.
2. What advantage of the London museum does Oliver point out?
Millions of people see the sculptures there every year, for free, next to objects from the rest of the world.
3. What does Oliver say about Stella's point that the case has a building waiting?
It is the most persuasive thing she has said, and she said it quietly.
Listening 1

The Museum Director

The director of a large encyclopedic museum speaks at a public forum. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. How does the director describe the purpose of an encyclopedic museum?
To let visitors see the cultures of the world side by side, so that no single culture is seen in isolation.
2. What does the director concede about the history of some collections?
That some objects came to museums in ways that would not be acceptable today, and that this history has to be faced honestly.
3. What does the director propose instead of permanent returns?
Long-term loans, shared research and partnerships that keep objects moving between institutions.
Listening 2

The Archaeologist

An archaeologist speaks at the same forum. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. Why does the archaeologist say the sculptures are different from a painting or a coin?
Because they were carved as parts of a single building that still stands, so their meaning depends on being seen together with it.
2. What does she concede about the museum in London?
That the sculptures were cared for there and seen by enormous numbers of people.
3. What does she say the new museum in Athens was designed to do?
To display the surviving sculptures in sight of the Parthenon, with empty spaces left for the pieces that are missing.
