"Present perfect and past simple" -- BBC Learning English, 6 Minute Grammar

Six minutes on the exact decision you practiced today: the date is in the sentence, or the date does not matter. Connection to Lesson 2: this is the difference between "I went to New York in January" and "I have been to New York twice".

Tip: after each example, pause and say two sentences about your own life out loud — one with a date, one without. Two sentences, then stop.

Podcast
"Airbnb's Joe Gebbia at the How I Built This Summit" -- NPR, with Guy Raz

A live conversation where a founder is asked, for almost an hour, one version of the same question: what have you built, and how. Slow American English, a real audience, and a speaker who tells stories instead of listing facts. Connection to Lesson 2: it is a whole hour of somebody framing a career with have and has, and then dropping into the past simple for each specific story.

Tip: listen for the moment he switches from "we have always been" to "then in 2008 we decided". Write down two examples of each and copy the pattern for your own career.

Talk
"Try something new for 30 days" -- Matt Cutts, TED (3 minutes)

Three minutes about doing things you have never done before, from someone who tested it on himself for a year. Short, clear and slow enough to follow without subtitles on the second listen. Connection to Lesson 2: the whole talk lives in "I have never" and "I have already", and it is about exactly what you are doing with painting.

Tip: turn the English transcript on for the first listen and off for the second. Then answer out loud: what is one thing you have never done that you could start in 30 days?
