Looking Back -- What You Would Change and What You Would Not

A flat tire, a piano that was sold and a train that was cancelled: the third conditional for a past that did not happen. Key words: a regret, hindsight, in retrospect, to turn out, a close call, fortunate, to dwell on something, to take something for granted, to make the most of something, a blessing in disguise, water under the bridge, to have second thoughts. Structure: third conditional for unreal past situations.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A regret -- a feeling of sadness about something you did or did not do
"My only regret is that I did not learn to play the piano."
Hindsight -- understanding a situation only after it has happened
"With hindsight, I should have asked more questions before I bought the car."
In retrospect -- when you think about it now, after it happened
"In retrospect, moving in the winter was not a good idea."
To turn out -- to happen in a particular way, often a surprising one
"The trip I almost cancelled turned out to be the best one of my life."
A close call -- a situation where something bad very nearly happened
"We missed the car by one meter. It was a close call."
Fortunate -- lucky, especially when things could have gone wrong
"We were fortunate that the storm started after we got home."
To dwell on something -- to keep thinking about something bad for too long
"There is no point in dwelling on it. It is finished."
To take something for granted -- to stop noticing how valuable something is because you always have it
"I took my grandmother’s stories for granted until she was gone."
To make the most of something -- to use an opportunity as well as you can
"We only had two days in Lisbon, so we made the most of them."
A blessing in disguise -- something that seems bad at first but turns out to be good
"Losing that apartment was a blessing in disguise. I found a much better one."
Water under the bridge -- something from the past that is no longer important
"We had a big argument years ago, but it is water under the bridge now."
To have second thoughts -- to start to doubt a decision you have already made
"I had second thoughts the night before the move."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Twelve years ago, Bram was cycling to the station in Utrecht when his tire went flat. He missed his train and went to a small party instead, where he met his future wife. If his tire had not gone flat, he would have gone to the concert and he would never have met her. For Bram, it was a blessing in disguise.

Megan, from Ohio, has a different story. Her family sold her grandmother’s piano because nobody played. Now her daughter loves the piano. If they had kept it, her daughter could have learned on it. Megan does not dwell on it — it is water under the bridge — but in retrospect, they took it for granted.

Ana thinks about her own decisions too. If she had stayed in Sao Paulo, she would have saved more money. But she would not have found her house. With hindsight, she would not change a thing.

1. "If his tire had not gone flat, he would have gone to the concert." Did Bram go to the concert?
A Yes, he went to the concert.
B No. The sentence imagines the opposite of what happened.
C We do not know.
2. "If they had kept it, her daughter could have learned on it." What does could have show?
A A possibility in the past that did not happen.
B Something her daughter does every day.
C A plan for next year.
3. Why does Ana not regret leaving Sao Paulo?
A Because she saved more money.
B Because she found her house.
C Because she never lived there.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Third Conditional

GRAMMAR

For a past that did not happen — and it is too late to change it.

FormWhat it doesExample
if + had + past participleAn imagined pastIf I had known...
would have + past participleThe imagined result...I would have come.
could have / might haveA possible resultWe might have caught the train.
negativeshad not / would not haveIf it had not rained, we would not have stayed.
short formsVery common in speechIf I’d known, I’d have come.
questionWhat would you have done?What would you have done if you had stayed?

Never: If I would have known · if I had knew · we would caught the train · if I had studied, I would pass last year. Both halves need their full form: had + past participle, and would have + past participle.
