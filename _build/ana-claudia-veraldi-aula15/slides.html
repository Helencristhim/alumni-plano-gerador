Chapter 3: The Code

The Past That Did Not Happen

if + had done · would have done · and the short forms you really hear

Grammar Discovery

It Did Not Happen, and It Is Too Late

"If my bike had not had a flat tire, I would have missed the party."

"If I had known about the storm, I would not have driven to the coast."

"If we had left ten minutes earlier, we might have caught the train."

"If she had kept the piano, her kids could have learned to play."

All four sentences are about the PAST. Did any of the colored things really happen? Look at the two halves. What comes after if? And what comes in the other half?

FormWhat it doesExample
if + had + past participleAn imagined past — the opposite of what happened.If I had known... (I did not know)
would have + past participleThe imagined result in the past.I would have come earlier.
could have / might haveA possible result, less certain.We might have caught the train.
negativeshad not / would not have.If it had not rained, we would not have stayed.
what you really hearIf I’d known, I’d have come. would have = /WOULD-uv/.If I’d known, I’d have come.
never would after ifThe same rule as the other two conditionals.never: if I would have known

In one line: if + had done, would have done — for a past that did not happen.

Chapter 4: Many Englishes

A Dutchman and an American Look Back

Dutch and American English — and two ways to talk about regret

Dialogue

Would You Change It?

G
So, Ana, do you ever think about Sao Paulo? If you had stayed, where would you be now?
A
Sometimes. If I had stayed, I would have saved more money. But I would not have found this house.
G
I know the feeling. I almost did not move to Portugal. I had second thoughts the week before.
A
Really? What would you have done in Chicago?
G
The same job, the same apartment. In retrospect, it was the best decision I ever made.
A
For me, the first year was hard. I took the city for granted. I missed the cinemas and the restaurants.
G
Sure, but you do not dwell on it, right? That is the American way. No regrets, move on.
A
Not really. And honestly? If I had not left, I would never have learned to restore anything.
Comprehension

About Greg

1. Where did Greg move, and where did he live before?
He moved to Portugal. Before that he lived in Chicago.
2. What happened the week before Greg moved?
He had second thoughts — he almost did not move.
3. What does Greg call the American way of looking back?
No regrets, move on.
