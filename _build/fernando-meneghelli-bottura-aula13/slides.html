Chapter 3: The Code

One Letter of Difference

The structure that turns an opinion into a recommendation

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I recommend that the audit committee review the policy before the year end."

"It is imperative that the migration cost be presented first."

"We stipulated that the controller sign the reconciliation, not the CFO."

"I would caution against deciding before we have the number."

Three of these break a rule you learned twenty years ago. Which rule, and why is nobody correcting it?

TriggerWhat followsExample
recommend / propose / suggest thatThe base form of the verb, with no s and no toI recommend that the committee review it.
insist / request / demand thatThe same base formI insisted that the controller sign it.
It is imperative / essential / advisable thatThe same, and with be it is very visibleIt is imperative that the cost be presented.
NegativeNo auxiliary: I recommend that the board not decide this quarter. Never does not decide.
The alternativeBritish English often uses should: I recommend that the committee should review it. Both are correct. The bare form is the standard in written American business English.
Register warningThis is board-paper English. It belongs in a formal recommendation, in a written proposal, and in the moment in an interview when you say what you would actually do. In casual conversation it sounds pompous, and knowing that is part of knowing the structure.

It is the only place in modern English where the third person loses its s on purpose — which is exactly why it marks the register so clearly.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I recommend that the committee reviews the policy."
"I recommend that the committee review the policy."
"I recommend the board to decide this quarter."
"I recommend that the board decide this quarter."
"It is imperative that the cost is presented first."
"It is imperative that the cost be presented first."

After the trigger comes the base form: review, decide, be, sign. No s, no to, and no does in the negative.

The Move

Name Them, Then Choose the Order

1. Name the parts

"There are three parts to that."

2. Choose the order

"I will take the middle one first, because the other two depend on it."

3. Flag the weakest

"The second is the one I am least certain about."

Naming the structure buys a second chance: if one part is thin, they will ask for it instead of writing down that you missed it.

Chapter 4: In the Room

She Asks Everything in Threes

Governance, systems and structure, in a single question

Dialogue

Three Parts, Named Out Loud

B
Three things, and take them however you like. What you would do about governance, whether you would replace the systems this year, and whether you would keep the current controller structure.
F
There are three parts to that, and I will take the middle one first, because the other two depend on it.
B
Go ahead.
F
On systems, it is not imperative that we decide this quarter, and I would caution against deciding before we have the migration cost.
B
And governance?
F
My recommendation would be that the audit committee review the reconciliation policy before the year end, on the grounds that the same exception has appeared twice.
B
And the third, on the controllers?
F
No, I would not keep it, and I would put forward a single shared function. I should add that the systems question is the one I am least certain about, and if you wanted to defer it a quarter I would not argue.
Comprehension

About the Question

1. What are the three things Beatrice asks about?
Governance, whether to replace the systems this year, and whether to keep the current controller structure.
2. Does she impose an order, and what does she say instead?
No — she says to take them however he likes, which is itself part of the test.
3. What does she do between the parts of his answer?
Almost nothing — two words each time, "go ahead" and "and governance?" — leaving the structure entirely to him.
Listening

Why They Ask Three at Once

It is not disorganisation. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the three-part question actually test?
Whether you can hold a structure in your head while you are also producing content.
2. What do most candidates do, and what do a few do?
Most answer the last part, because it is still in short-term memory, and then trail off. A few answer all three in a row and sound like a list.
3. What second advantage does naming the structure give you?
If you name three parts and only answer two well, the interviewer asks for the third instead of writing down that you missed it.
Listening 2

The Whole Answer, In Order

Three parts, one recommendation, one caution. Sound first — no text.

1. Which part does he take first, and why?
The middle one, because the other two depend on it.
2. What is his recommendation on governance, and on what grounds?
That the audit committee review the reconciliation policy before the year end, on the grounds that the same exception has appeared twice.
3. How does he close, and what does that add?
By saying which of the three he is least certain about, and that he would not argue if the board deferred it — which makes the other two more credible.

The Paper

Where This Grammar Actually Lives

Recommendation to the Board
Finance — Three Items
From: CFO
Decision required on 1 of 3

1. Governance — imperative

It is imperative that the audit committee review the reconciliation policy before the year end, on the grounds that the same exception has appeared twice.

2. Systems — advisable, not urgent

I would caution against a decision this quarter, and I recommend that the board not commit before the migration cost is on the table.

3. Structure — proposal

I put forward a single shared function, and I stipulate that the transition be completed before the next audit cycle.

Least certain: item 2. If the board prefers to defer it by a quarter, I would not argue.

1. Which of the three items is marked as unavoidable, and how do you know?
Item 1 — the word imperative, and the grounds are stated in the same sentence.
2. How is the negative recommendation written, and what is unusual about it?
"I recommend that the board not commit" — the negative takes no auxiliary at all.
3. Why does the paper name the least certain item at the bottom?
Because flagging the weakest item is what makes the other two credible.
