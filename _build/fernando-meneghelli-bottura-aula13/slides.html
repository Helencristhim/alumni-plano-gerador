Chapter 3: The Code

Asking Without Demanding

The same request, in a form nobody has to refuse

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

“I would be looking for something in the region of that range.”

“Could I ask what the role was signed off at?”

“I would be comfortable with more variable pay if the targets were agreed in writing.”

“That would work for me, provided the reporting line is to the board.”

Which of these are still true today, and what comes after each expression?

FormMeaningExample
would + be + -ing, to name a number without demanding itI would be looking for something in the region of four hundred.I used to report to New York.
Could I ask..., to get information without pressingCould I ask what range the role was signed off at?I am used to reporting in English.
would + be comfortable with, to accept a conditionI would be comfortable with a larger variable part.I got used to being challenged.
provided / as long as, to attach your conditionThat would work for me, provided the reporting line is to the board.

The -ing after be used to is the single most common advanced mistake in this family, and it survives for years because it never blocks understanding.

Practice

Ask, Name, Attach

Say it first, then click to check

"I ___would be looking for something in the region of that range." (be)
"___Could I ask what the role was signed off at?" (can, polite)
"I would be ___comfortable with more variable pay." (comfortable)
"That would work for me, ___provided the reporting line is to the board." (condition)
The Move

Ask, Region, Condition

1. Ask before you name a number

“Could I ask what range the role was signed off at?”

2. Name the number as a region

“I would be looking for something in the region of that upper half.”

3. Attach the condition, not the complaint

“That would work for me, provided the scope stays as we described it.”

Never blame the market, the parent company or the previous CFO. Every one of those may be true, and every one of them costs you the room.

Chapter 4: In the Room

The Offer Call

Dialogue

The Range Before the Number

C
They want to make you an offer. Base is four hundred and twenty, with a bonus of up to thirty per cent.
F
Thank you. Could I ask what range the role was signed off at?
C
Four hundred to four hundred and sixty, and they have gone to the middle.
F
That is useful. I would be looking for something in the upper half of that range, and I can say why.
C
Go on.
F
The scope includes the shared service centre, which was not in the original description. I would be comfortable with the base at four hundred and fifty, provided the bonus targets are agreed in writing.
C
And if they hold at four hundred and thirty?
F
Then I would want the sign-on bonus to cover what I am leaving behind, and I would still say yes.
C
That is a clean answer. Most candidates either take the first number or make it personal.
F
I would rather be specific than difficult. The scope matters more to me than the last ten thousand.

Listening

Where Candidates Lose Money

A search consultant on the last ten minutes of a process. Sound first — no text.

1. What are the two ways candidates lose money?
Naming a number before knowing the range, and making it personal.
2. Why is naming a number early a problem?
Once it is said it becomes a ceiling, and no client offers more than the candidate asked for.
3. What kind of reason can she take to the client?
One that lives in the job, like the scope growing, not one from the candidate's personal life.
Listening 2

The Thing He Negotiated Instead

A CFO on what he asked for when the money would not move. Sound first — no text.

1. What did he negotiate when the base would not move?
The reporting line, in the contract, and a review of the scope at six months.
2. Why did he want the reporting line to the board?
It cost the company nothing and changed everything about the job.
3. What does he say scope creep does?
It makes people leave inside two years.
