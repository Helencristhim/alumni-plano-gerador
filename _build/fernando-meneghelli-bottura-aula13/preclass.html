Grammar

Stage 1.3 -- Grammar in Context

Read the text, then answer the questions. Notice what happens to the verb after every underlined trigger.

The paper went to the board on a Tuesday and it was one page long. The first item said that it is imperative that the audit committee review the reconciliation policy before the year end — not reviews, review — and it gave the reason in the same sentence, on the grounds that the same exception had appeared twice in six months.

The second item was weaker on purpose. It said the systems replacement was advisable rather than imperative, and it cautioned against a decision before the migration cost was on the table. One word carried that whole difference. A director who reads advisable knows she has a quarter; a director who reads imperative knows she does not.

The third item put forward a single shared finance function, and it stipulated that the transition be completed before the next audit cycle. At the bottom, in two lines, the CFO wrote which of the three items he was least certain about. That last paragraph is the reason the board approved the first two in ten minutes: a person who tells you where he is weak has already told you the truth about where he is strong.

1. Why does the first item say "review" and not "reviews"?
A Because the committee is plural.
B Because after "it is imperative that" the verb takes its base form.
C Because it refers to the future.
2. What is the practical difference between "advisable" and "imperative" for the director reading the paper?
A There is no difference; they are formal synonyms.
B "Advisable" gives her time to decide; "imperative" does not.
C "Advisable" means the CFO disagrees with the item.
3. In "it stipulated that the transition be completed", why is it "be" and not "is"?
A Because the transition has not started yet.
B Because "transition" is an uncountable noun.
C Because "stipulate that" is followed by the base form, and with "be" the change is very visible.
4. Why did naming the weakest item help the CFO?
A It made the other two items credible, so the board approved them quickly.
B It let him avoid responsibility for the third item.
C It shortened the paper to one page.
Grammar

Stage 1.4 -- Grammar Tip: The Subjunctive of Recommendation

After certain triggers, English uses the base form of the verb — no s in the third person, no to, and no auxiliary in the negative. It is the one place in modern English where the third person deliberately loses its s, which is exactly why it marks a formal register so clearly.

TriggerExample
recommend / propose / suggest thatI recommend that the committee review the policy.
insist / request / demand thatI insisted that the controller sign it.
stipulate thatWe stipulated that the cost be presented first.
It is imperative / essential / advisable thatIt is imperative that the audit committee see this.
Negative (no auxiliary)I recommend that the board not decide this quarter.
British alternative with shouldI recommend that the committee should review it. Also correct.

Register warning: this is the English of a board paper, a written proposal, and the interview moment when you say what you would actually do. In casual conversation it sounds pompous — knowing that is part of knowing the structure.

The strength scale: advisable → prudent → imperative. Changing one word changes the weight of the whole recommendation, and it is the cheapest precision tool you have.

Quiz

Stage 4 -- Situational Quiz

1. A panel asks you three things at once. What is the first sentence out of your mouth?
A "Sorry, could you repeat the whole question?"
B "There are three parts to that, and I will take the middle one first."
C "Let me answer the last one, which is the one I remember."
2. You want the board to act now, with no room to postpone. Which word do you choose?
A "It is advisable that the board decide this quarter."
B "It is imperative that the board decide this quarter."
C "It might be a good idea for the board to decide this quarter."
3. You disagree with a decision but you do not want to block it. What do you say?
A "I would caution against deciding before we have the migration cost."
B "I refuse to support this decision."
C "I am not sure, it is difficult to say."
4. You have to give the formal reason behind a recommendation, in the same sentence. Which one is it?
A "I recommended the review, and also there was a problem before."
B "I recommended the review on the grounds that the same exception had appeared twice."
C "I recommended the review because I felt it was the moment."
5. Of your three answers, one is thinner than the others. What do you do?
A Say nothing and hope they do not come back to it.
B Spend the most time on it, to cover the gap.
C Say which one it is: "The second is the one I am least certain about."
