Grammar Lesson
"How to use SUGGEST and RECOMMEND" -- engVid (13 min)

The structure from this lesson, taught by someone who spends the whole video on the exact point that trips people up: what happens to the verb after "recommend that". Connection to Lesson 13: it drills the one letter of difference between "the committee reviews" and "the committee review".

Tip: pause after each example and say the sentence with your own company in it before he moves on. The structure only becomes yours once it has your numbers inside it.

Talk
"The art of persuasive storytelling" -- Kelly D. Parker, TED (11 min)

A communications strategist on why a proposal that carries a short story gets funded and the same proposal without one does not. Connection to Lesson 13: you now have the grammar of a recommendation. This is about the thirty seconds of narrative you put around it so a board can repeat it to somebody who was not in the room.

Tip: turn on English captions and watch for her structure, not her words. Then take the recommendation you wrote for homework and add one sentence of story in front of it.

Long-form Interview
"Andrea Orcel, CEO of UniCredit" -- In Good Company (40 min)

A European bank CEO answering questions that arrive in threes, from an interviewer who does not slow down for anyone. Neither man is a native speaker of the other's English, which is exactly the room you are training for.

Tip: this week, listen for the question rather than the answer. Every time Tangen asks two or three things at once, pause and count the parts out loud. You are training the ear that failed you in the Michael Page call.
