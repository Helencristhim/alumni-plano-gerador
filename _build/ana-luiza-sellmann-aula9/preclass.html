Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

She Said, He Told Me -- Repeating What Somebody Else Said

Repeating what other people said, and making clear whose opinion is whose. Key words: a rumor, to bring up, word for word, to get the wrong idea, nonsense, to be in touch, the minutes, a follow-up. Structure: reported speech -- every verb takes one step back, plus the difference between say, tell and ask.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. Do not translate — the example sentence is the meaning.

A rumor -- something people repeat that may not be true
"There's a rumor that she is leaving in September."
To bring up -- to start talking about a subject
"Marcos brought it up in front of six people."
Word for word -- exactly as it was said, with nothing changed
"He repeated the whole thing word for word."
Nonsense -- something that is simply not true
"That's complete nonsense. She never said that."
To get the wrong idea -- to understand something in the wrong way
"I don't want him to get the wrong idea."
To be in touch -- to have contact with somebody over time
"We're still in touch, ten years later."
The minutes -- the written record of what was said in a meeting
"Please read the minutes before you reply to anyone."
A follow-up -- a message or meeting that comes after the first one
"I said we would send a follow-up by Wednesday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Saturday Julia told me that she was leaving the country in September. She said she had already handed in her notice, and she asked me not to tell anybody until she had spoken to her parents. On Sunday Marcos brought it up in front of six people and said she was moving because of a man, which is complete nonsense. She never said that. She told me it was about the job and nothing else, and now half the room is going to get the wrong idea.

The same thing happens at work, only with minutes. The client said they loved the concept. They explained that the budget would be reviewed in October and asked whether we could hold the launch until November. Laura told them we could, which I am not sure was true. They also mentioned that a new marketing director had started in March. Daniel said he would send a follow-up by Wednesday. When I repeat all of this to somebody else, I try to be careful: I say what each person said, not what I think they meant.

1. Julia's exact words were "I am leaving in September". Why is it "she was leaving" in the text?
A Because when you report what somebody said, every verb takes one step back.
B Because she changed her mind and is not leaving anymore.
C Because September is in the past.
2. Why is it "she told me" but "she said she had handed in her notice"?
A Because "told" is for good news and "said" is for bad news.
B Because "tell" always needs a person right after it, and "say" does not.
C Because they are two different people speaking.
3. The client asked "Can you hold the launch?" Why does the text say "asked whether we could hold"?
A Because the answer was no.
B Because reported questions always keep the question word order.
C Because a reported question uses normal word order, no question mark, and can becomes could.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- One Step Back

GRAMMAR

The same move you already used in Lesson 5: the past form means distance, not past time.

They actually saidYou reportExample
presentpast"I am tired." → She said she was tired.
will / can / maywould / could / might"We will review it." → They said they would review it.
have done / didhad done"I have sent it." → He said he had sent it.
say or telltell takes a person immediately: "She told me that..." say does not: "She said that..." Never "she told that" and never "she said me".
QuestionsNormal word order, no question mark: "He asked me what the timeline was." For yes or no questions, use if or whether: "They asked whether we could wait."
Still true now?If the information has not changed, you may keep the present: "She just said the concept is approved." Both versions are correct, so this is one decision you do not have to agonise over.

Beyond "said": mention, explain, confirm, suggest, ask. Using these makes a recap sound precise instead of repetitive, and it also makes clear whose opinion each sentence is.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are repeating what other people said. Choose the best thing to say.

Julia's words were "I am leaving in September". You repeat them to a friend:
A "She told me she was leaving in September."
B "She told that she was leaving in September."
C "She said me she is leaving in September."
The client asked "What is the timeline?" You report it:
A "He asked me what was the timeline?"
B "He asked me what the timeline was."
C "He asked me what is the timeline."
They said "We will review the budget in October". You report it:
A "They said they will review the budget in October."
B "They said they would review the budget in October."
C "They said they reviewed the budget in October."
You want to make clear that the opinion is the client's, not yours. You say:
A "Just to be clear, that's what they said, not what I think."
B "Just to be clear, I think that they told."
C "Just to be clear, that is what I said them."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer, and nobody is correcting this one.

Your manager asks for a recap of a client meeting you attended and she did not. Speak for about ninety seconds. Report what the client said about the concept, what they explained about the budget, what they asked for, what your colleague answered and what was promised. Use said, told, asked and explained at least once each, and make clear whose opinion each sentence is. If a sentence breaks in the middle, say "let me start that again" out loud and keep going.
