Grammar Video
"How to use reported speech" -- BBC Learning English, 6 Minute Grammar

Six minutes on the one rule you practiced today: every verb takes one step back. It also covers the reported question, which is where almost everybody keeps the wrong word order. Connection to Lesson 9: this is "she said she was leaving" and "he asked me what the timeline was".

Tip: after each example, repeat the original sentence out loud and then your reported version. Two sentences, then play again.

Podcast
"Retraction" (episode 460) -- This American Life

A radio show discovers that a story it broadcast was not true, and spends a whole hour going back over who said what, and when. Almost every sentence in it is reported speech, because that is the only way to tell this kind of story. Slow American English, with the full transcript on the page.

Tip: read along with the transcript for the first fifteen minutes. Highlight five sentences with "he said", "she told me" or "they asked", and read those five out loud.

"10 ways to have a better conversation" -- Celeste Headlee, TED (11 minutes)

A radio interviewer explains what she does to make people talk, and what most of us do instead. Eleven minutes, fast but very clear American English, and funny. Connection to Lesson 9: half the talk is about listening properly so that what you repeat afterwards is actually what the person said.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Then pick the two rules you break most often and say them out loud as advice: "She said you should..." and "She asked whether..."
