Chapter 3: The Code

Small Words, Big Jobs

well · I mean · actually · by the way · speaking of · anyway

Grammar Discovery

What Does Each Little Word Do?

"Well, let me think... I’d say Lisbon."

"By the way, where are you from?"

"Speaking of food, have you tried the custard tarts?"

"Anyway, as I was saying, the class starts at seven."

Look at the colored words. Which one gives you a second to think? Which one opens a completely new topic? Which one opens a new topic that is connected to the last one? And which one goes back to the main topic?

FormWhat it doesExample
well / let me seeBuys time. You need a second to think.Well, let me see... about ten years.
I meanExplains or corrects what you just said.It’s small. I mean, it’s not tiny, but...
actuallyAdds a surprise, or politely says the opposite.Actually, I’m from São Paulo, not Rio.
by the wayOpens a new topic with no link to the last one.By the way, do you like football?
speaking of + nounOpens a new topic linked to the last word.Speaking of coffee, have you been to Brazil?
anywayGoes back to the main topic, or starts to close the chat.Anyway, it was nice talking to you.

In one line: well = wait, I’m thinking; I mean = let me explain; actually = a surprise; by the way = new topic; speaking of = new topic, same thread; anyway = back, or goodbye.

Chapter 4: Many Englishes

A Dutchman, an Indian and an American

Dutch, Indian and American English — and three ideas of what is safe to ask

Dialogue

Before the Class Starts

M
Hi! Is this the pastry class? I think I'm a little early.
A
Me too. Well, at least we get the best seats. I'm Ana, from Brazil.
M
Mike, from Ohio. So, what brings you to Lisbon?
A
A short vacation. I mean, it's mostly an excuse to eat. By the way, have you done a class like this before?
M
Never. My wife booked it. Speaking of food, have you tried the custard tarts yet?
A
Three times! We have something similar in Brazil, so we already have some common ground.
M
Ha! That's a nice way to break the ice. Anyway, I think the chef is here.
A
Perfect timing. It was nice talking to you, Mike. Let's sit together.
Comprehension

About Mike

1. Where is Mike from, and why is he at the class?
He is from Ohio. His wife booked the class.
2. Which word does Mike use to change the topic to the custard tarts?
Speaking of — “Speaking of food, have you tried the custard tarts yet?”
3. How does Mike close the small talk?
With anyway: “Anyway, I think the chef is here.”
Listening 1 · The Netherlands

How Are You? An Honest Answer

A Dutchman on why his small talk is short and very direct. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What questions might Pieter ask at a party, and why does he think they are okay?
How much you paid for your house, or why you don’t have children. For the Dutch, they are harmless — people are just curious.
2. What happened at the supermarket in Texas?
A woman asked how he was doing, and he told her everything. She just smiled and walked away: it was only hello.
3. Which topic is safe everywhere, according to Pieter, and why does he joke about Holland?
The weather. In Holland it is safe because it is always bad.
Listening 2 · India

The Auntie at the Wedding

An Indian woman in London on personal questions, the weather and the quiet moments in a conversation. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does an auntie at an Indian wedding ask, and what does it mean?
Are you married? Why not? How much does your husband earn? It means: I care about you.
2. Which topics are off-limits in London?
Money, age and religion.
3. What does Priya do now when there is a lull in the conversation?
She talks about the weather: “Lovely day, isn’t it?” — even when it isn’t.
