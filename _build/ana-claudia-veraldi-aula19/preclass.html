Lesson 19 -- Pre-class

Small Talk -- What's Safe to Ask Where

A Dutchman, an Indian auntie and an American in Lisbon: the small words that keep a conversation going, and the topics that are safe in each culture. Key words: small talk, off-limits, intrusive, harmless, to pry, common ground, rapport, a lull, to mingle, to break the ice, to keep the ball rolling, to touch a nerve. Structure: connectors and fluency fillers for small talk: well, I mean, actually, by the way, speaking of and anyway.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Small talk -- light, friendly conversation about easy topics, often with people you do not know well
"We made small talk about the weather while we waited for the bus."
Off-limits -- not allowed; a topic you should not talk about
"In many countries, money is off-limits when you meet someone new."
Intrusive -- asking about things that are too private
"Asking a stranger about her age can feel intrusive."
Harmless -- not able to hurt or offend anybody
"A question about the weather is completely harmless."
To pry -- to try to find out about somebody’s private life
"I do not want to pry, but are you two related?"
Common ground -- interests or experiences that two people share
"We found some common ground: we both love old houses."
Rapport -- a friendly feeling of understanding between two people
"After ten minutes of chatting, there was a nice rapport between us."
A lull -- a short, quiet moment when nobody is talking
"When there was a lull in the conversation, she asked about the food."
To mingle -- to move around a party and talk to different people
"Before dinner, the guests had time to mingle in the garden."
To break the ice -- to say or do something that makes people feel relaxed at the start
"A joke about the rain helped to break the ice."
To keep the ball rolling -- to keep a conversation or an activity going
"She asked another question to keep the ball rolling."
To touch a nerve -- to talk about something that upsets a person
"I asked about his job, and I think I touched a nerve."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Pieter, from Utrecht, thinks small talk is a little strange. At a Dutch party he might ask how much you paid for your house. For him, it is a harmless question. Well, we are just curious, he says. I mean, why not ask?

Priya grew up in Chennai, where an auntie at a wedding asks about your salary. In London, she learned that money is off-limits. Actually, she now loves British small talk: the weather is common ground, and nobody gets hurt.

In a cooking class in Lisbon, Ana meets Mike from Ohio. They break the ice with the custard tarts. By the way, have you done a class like this before? she asks. Speaking of food..., he answers. When the chef arrives, Mike says, Anyway, I think we are starting.

1. Why does Pieter ask about the price of a house?
A Because in the Netherlands he thinks it is a harmless, curious question.
B Because he wants to buy the house himself.
C Because he does not like small talk about the weather and wants to be rude.
2. What did Priya learn in London?
A That the British never talk about the weather at all.
B That money is off-limits, and the weather is common ground.
C That aunties ask about salaries.
3. What does Mike’s “Anyway, I think we are starting” mean?
A He wants to change the topic to the chef’s life story.
B He did not understand Ana.
C The small talk is ending.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Small Words, Big Jobs

GRAMMAR

Buy time, explain, surprise, change topic, link, close — six different jobs.

FormWhat it doesExample
well / let me seeBuy time to thinkWell, let me see... ten years.
I meanExplain or correctIt’s small. I mean, not tiny.
actuallyA surprise, or a polite noActually, I’m from the interior.
by the wayA new topic, no linkBy the way, where are you from?
speaking of + nounA new topic, linked to the last oneSpeaking of food...
anywayBack to the topic, or goodbyeAnyway, nice talking to you.

Never: By the way of food · speaking about of · five connectors in one sentence. Speaking of is followed by a noun. One or two small words per sentence sounds natural; more sounds nervous. And remember: weather and food are safe almost everywhere, money and age are not.

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

Ana meets Mike before a cooking class in Lisbon. Put the conversation in order.

?"Hi! Is this the pastry class? I think I'm a little early."
?"Me too. Well, at least we get the best seats. I'm Ana, from Brazil."
?"Mike, from Ohio. So, what brings you to Lisbon?"
?"A short vacation. By the way, have you done a class like this before?"
?"Never. Speaking of food, have you tried the custard tarts yet?"
?"Three times! Anyway, I think the chef is here."
