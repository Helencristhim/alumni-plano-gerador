Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

The Language of Negotiation -- Reporting What Was Said and What Was Agreed

In a negotiation summary the verb you choose already says whether a point is closed, on the table or dead. Key words: concession, leverage, deal-breaker, trade-off, compromise, proposal, counteroffer, deadlock, bargaining, walk-away point, goodwill, settlement. Structures: the reporting verbs of negotiation and the pattern each one needs -- offer and refuse with to, suggest and propose with -ing or that, insist on and object to with a noun -- plus the difference between agreed, agreed in principle and did not respond.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the definition. These are the pieces on the table in any negotiation -- the goal is to say them out loud.

Concession -- something you give up to move the deal forward
"That was the only concession we made."
Leverage -- the advantage that makes the other side listen to you
"We have leverage while they need the deadline."
Deal-breaker -- the one point that ends the deal if it is not accepted
"The exclusivity clause is a deal-breaker for them."
Trade-off -- giving up one thing to get another
"It was a trade-off: a shorter term for a lower penalty."
Compromise -- an agreement where each side gives up part of what it wanted
"We reached a compromise on the volume."
Proposal -- a formal suggestion put on the table
"Their proposal is on the table until Friday."
Counteroffer -- a new offer made in reply to their offer
"We sent a counteroffer the same afternoon."
Deadlock -- the point where neither side will move
"Pricing is still in deadlock."
Bargaining -- the back-and-forth of offers before an agreement
"The bargaining took three rounds."
Walk-away point -- the limit beyond which you leave the negotiation
"Below that number we hit our walk-away point."
Goodwill -- the trust between the two sides that makes a deal easier
"They moved first, as a sign of goodwill."
Settlement -- an agreement that ends a dispute
"The settlement ended the dispute in March."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Ana has to tell a manager what happened in yesterday's round, and she writes it in three groups. First, what is closed. The other side agreed to extend the term by sixty days, and both parties agreed on the delivery windows. That was a real compromise, and it goes into the draft. Second, what is only on the table. They proposed a five percent cap and offered to review it after the first year; they also suggested capping the penalty instead of raising the price. None of that is signed, so Ana calls it a proposal, never an agreement, and she plans a counteroffer for the next round. Third, what is dead. They refused to discuss exclusivity, because it is a deal-breaker for them, and they insisted on keeping the clause exactly as it is. That point is in deadlock. One line at the end belongs to none of the three: nobody answered on indexation. Silence is not a yes, so Ana writes that they did not respond, and leaves the point open.

1. Why does Ana write "they proposed a five percent cap" and not "they agreed to a five percent cap"?
A Because the cap is on the table and nothing is signed, so nobody has committed to it.
B Because the cap is not an important point in this deal.
C Because "agree" cannot be used with a number.
2. The text says "they suggested capping the penalty". Why not "they suggested us to cap the penalty"?
A Because "us" is the wrong pronoun in that sentence.
B Because suggest never takes a person plus "to"; it takes an -ing form or a "that" clause.
C Because suggest is only used in writing, never in speech.
3. What does "they insisted on keeping the clause" tell the reader?
A That they would not move on that point, which is why it is in deadlock.
B That they accepted the clause the way Ana wanted it.
C That they asked for more time before deciding.
4. Nobody answered on indexation. How does Ana report that line?
A She writes that they rejected it, because they said nothing.
B She writes that they did not respond, and leaves the point open.
C She writes that they agreed, because nobody objected to it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reporting Verbs of Negotiation and Their Patterns

GRAMMAR

The verb says what the point is. The pattern says whether the sentence is English.

FormUseExample
offer / agree / refuse / promise + to + verbA commitment or a refusal made by somebody. This is the agreed and the rejected column.They agreed to extend the term by sixty days.
insist / agree / propose / recommend + that + clauseThe content of what was said, in a full sentence. The verb after "that" stays in the base form.They insisted that the cap stay at five percent.
suggest / propose / recommend + -ingThe same idea, shorter, with no person after the verb. This is the proposed column.They suggested capping the penalty instead.
insist on / agree on / object to + noun or -ingA position held on one point. Useful when nobody moved.They objected to paying the registration fee.
QuestionWhat did they agree to? · What did they propose? · What did they refuse to do?What did they agree to in the end?
Three trapsNever "they suggested me to sign" or "they proposed me a new price" -- suggest, propose and recommend never take a person plus "to". Never "they insisted to keep it" -- it is insisted on keeping. And agree with is agreeing with a person or an opinion, while agree to is committing to an action: they agreed to extend, not they agreed with extending.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

On the call the other side said they were open to sixty days. In your summary you write:
A "They proposed sixty days. It is not agreed yet."
B "They accepted sixty days."
C "Sixty days is confirmed."
You want to report that they would not move on exclusivity. Which is correct?
A "They insisted to keep exclusivity."
B "They insisted on keeping exclusivity."
C "They insisted keeping exclusivity."
They put forward the idea of capping the penalty. You report:
A "They suggested us to cap the penalty."
B "They proposed me a cap on the penalty."
C "They suggested capping the penalty."
Nobody on their side answered when the indexation clause was raised. Your line says:
A "They rejected indexation."
B "We proposed indexation and they did not respond. The point is still open."
C "They did not object, so indexation is agreed."
The manager calls the price cap agreed when it was only agreed in principle. You reply:
A "Yes, more or less, I think so."
B "Let me be precise. They agreed in principle and offered to review it after year one. Nothing is signed yet."
C "No, you are wrong, that is not what happened."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Take the last negotiation or contract discussion you were part of. Debrief it in three blocks: what was agreed, what was only proposed, and what was refused. Start every line with a reporting verb, and say out loud which of the three states each line belongs to. Finish with the one point that is still open. Take your time.
