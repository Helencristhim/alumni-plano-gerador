Lesson 6 -- The Language of Negotiation

Series
Better Call Saul -- Season 1, Episode 1 ("Uno")

A lawyer spends the whole season doing what you did today: putting an offer on the table, refusing one, and reporting to somebody who was not in the room what the other side actually said. The word settlement is on screen every other scene, and it never means the same as an agreement in principle.

Tip: watch with English subtitles and write down five sentences that report an offer, a refusal or a proposal. Then label each one agreed, proposed or rejected.

Podcast
Life Kit (NPR) -- "How to use classic negotiation tactics in everyday life"

A negotiation expert walks through concessions, leverage and walk-away points in plain American English, using situations that have nothing to do with contracts. Exactly the vocabulary of this lesson, heard outside the courtroom, which is the fastest way to make it stick.

Tip: there is a full transcript on the page. Listen first with the transcript closed and note every time the speakers report an offer -- then open it and check which verb they chose.

YouTube
British Council -- "How to negotiate in English" (Business English)

Short and practical: how to open a position, make a proposal, push back and close, with the fixed phrases that native speakers use at the table. Watch it for the moment somebody makes an offer and somebody else only says they will consider it -- those are your two different verbs.

Tip: after the video, take three lines from your own last negotiation and report them out loud. Then check the pattern: to, -ing, or a preposition?
