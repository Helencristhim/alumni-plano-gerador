The Language of Negotiation

The verb you choose decides whether something was agreed, offered, or refused. Choose it badly and you invent a deal.

The Problem

One Verb Changes the Legal Status

What the summary said

"They accepted the five percent cap."

What happened in the room

"They proposed a five percent cap and offered to review it after year one."

Nobody lied. One verb turned a proposal into a commitment, and three weeks later somebody has to explain it.

Chapter 3: The Code

Every Verb Wants Something Different

offer to · insist on · suggest -ing

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"They offered to extend the term by sixty days."

"They refused to move on exclusivity."

"They suggested capping the penalty instead."

"They insisted on a shorter notice period."

Look at what comes straight after each verb. to + verb, -ing, preposition + noun. Which verb takes which?

PatternVerbsExample
+ to + verboffer · agree · refuse · promise · threatenThey agreed to extend the term.
+ that + clauseinsist · agree · propose · recommend · suggestThey insisted that the cap stay at five percent.
+ -ingsuggest · propose · recommendThey suggested capping the penalty.
+ preposition
+ noun or -ing		insist on · agree on · object toThey objected to paying the fee.
Three trapsNever "they suggested me to sign" or "they proposed me a price" — suggest, propose and recommend never take a person plus to. Never "they insisted to keep it" — it is insisted on keeping. And agree with is agreeing with a person; agree to is committing to an action.

The question that saves you: is this closed, on the table, or dead? Pick the verb from the answer.

The Habit

Three States, Always

Decide which of the three a line belongs to. Then, and only then, choose the verb.

Agreed — it binds

agreed to · agreed on · accepted
"They agreed to extend the term."

Proposed — on the table

proposed · offered to · suggested · recommended
"They proposed a five percent cap."

Rejected — it is dead

refused to · rejected · objected to
"They refused to discuss exclusivity."

And a fourth line that is none of the three: they did not respond. Silence is not a yes, and it is not a no. Report it as silence.

Chapter 4: The Debrief

Where Are We After the Round?

Three states, kept apart

Dialogue

What Was Actually Agreed

D
Ana, I only saw the headline. Where are we after yesterday's round?
A
Three groups. Agreed: they agreed to extend the term by sixty days, and we agreed on the delivery windows. That was the compromise of the round, and it is closed.
D
Good. And the price cap? I understood that was agreed as well.
A
Agreed in principle only. They proposed five percent and offered to review it after year one. It is a proposal, not a commitment — nothing is signed.
D
So we cannot plan on five percent.
A
Not yet. And exclusivity is a deal-breaker for them: they refused to discuss it at all, so that point is in deadlock. I would send a counteroffer on the cap and leave exclusivity for the next round.
D
Understood. Write it exactly like that — agreed, proposed, rejected. That is what I am taking to the board on Monday.
Comprehension

About David

1. What did David think had already been agreed?
The price cap. He had understood it was closed, and Ana corrects him: it was agreed in principle only.
2. Why can he not plan on five percent?
Because it was proposed and offered for review, not committed to. Nothing is signed.
3. What does he ask for at the end, and why?
The summary written exactly as agreed, proposed and rejected — because he is taking it to the board on Monday.
Listening

The Damage Happens Afterwards

A negotiator on the verbs he uses and the two he banned. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Where does he say his worst problems came from?
Not from the table itself, but from the summary somebody wrote afterwards.
2. What happens to "they are open to sixty days"?
By the time it reaches an email it reads "they accepted sixty days", and nobody finds it strange.
3. Which verbs are on the list he gave his team?
Agreed for a closed point, offered and proposed for what is on the table, refused and rejected for what is dead, and insisted on when they would not move.
4. Which two words did he ban, and why?
"Accepted", unless he can point at the moment they accepted it, and "confirmed", which people use when they only mean nobody said no.
Listening 2

Silence Is Not a Yes

Agreed, agreed in principle, did not object. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she say "agreed in principle" does not do?
It does not bind anybody. They like the direction, and it can disappear in the next round.
2. What actually happened with the indexation clause?
The counterparty said nothing when it was mentioned, and the summary recorded it as an acceptance.
3. How did that file end?
Six weeks and a lot of money later they said they had never agreed to it — and she says they were right.
4. What is her habit now?
Every line starts with a verb, and she chooses the verb before writing the sentence. If she cannot pick one honestly, the line is not ready.

The Real Thing

Two Badges Are Wrong

Internal — Negotiation Summary
Master Supply Agreement — Round 3
PointStatusNote
Term lengthAgreedThey agreed to extend the term by sixty days.
Price capAgreedThey said they were open to a five percent cap.
Delivery windowsAgreedBoth sides agreed on October and January.
ExclusivityRejectedThey refused to discuss it in this round.
IndexationRejectedRaised at the end of the round; nobody on their side responded.
1. Which line has the wrong status, and why?
The price cap. "They were open to it" is not a commitment — nobody agreed to anything. The badge should be Proposed.
2. Which verb should that note use instead?
"They proposed a five percent cap" — or "they offered to consider a five percent cap". Never "agreed".
3. Nobody answered on indexation. How do you report that line out loud?
"We proposed indexation and they did not respond. The point is still open." Silence is not a rejection, and it is not an acceptance either.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I decide whether a point is agreed, proposed or rejected before I choose the verb.
I use offer, agree, refuse and promise with to and a verb.
I use suggest, propose and recommend with -ing or that, never with a person plus to.
I use insist on, agree on and object to with a noun or an -ing form.
I keep agreed, agreed in principle and did not respond apart when I report a round.
