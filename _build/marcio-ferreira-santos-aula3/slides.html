Chapter 3: The Code

Present Perfect Continuous

I have been working · we have been testing

Grammar Discovery

Read and Notice

"I have been working on this pricing model since 2019."

"We have been testing the new price for three months."

"My team has been studying demand at SKU level."

"They have been losing customers to a competitor."

All four use have / has been + -ing. What do the four situations have in common?

FormUseExample
have / has been + verb-ingWork that started in the past and is still going. The focus is the activity, not the result.I have been working on the model.
for / sinceThe same partners as the present perfect: for + a period, since + a starting point.for three months / since 2019
Activity vs resultContinuous = the work in progress. Simple = the finished result.I have been building it. / I have built three.
Verbs that do not take itState verbs stay simple: know, understand, believe, need, want. Say I have known this market for ten years, never I have been knowing.

Ask yourself: is the work still going on? If yes, and you want to show it, use the continuous.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I work on this model since 2019."
"I have been working on this model since 2019."
"I have been knowing this market for ten years."
"I have known this market for ten years."
"We have testing the new price for three months."
"We have been testing the new price for three months."

The form is always have / has + been + -ing, and state verbs (know, understand, believe) never take it.

"The board ___has approved the rollout." (approve, finished)
"Since March, demand ___has been growing every month." (grow, in progress)
Chapter 4: The Call

Explain It to Ruth

A category director asks how you price

Dialogue

The Video Call

R
Thanks for the time, Marcio. My team wants to understand your method. How long have you been doing this?
M
I have been working in pricing for more than ten years, and on this model since 2019.
R
Good. And where do you start? Most teams start with the competitor.
M
We start with demand. We forecast it for every SKU, and then we look at the price elasticity.
R
That is not what I expected. Honestly, we have been doing the opposite for years, and it has not worked well.
M
The competitor is only one input. The real decision is the trade-off between volume and margin.
R
And how do you explain that to a board? That is where I always lose the room.
M
With one number and one sentence. I show the breakeven point, and I explain our value proposition in one line.
Comprehension

They start with the competitor.
2. What has her own team been doing for years?
The opposite, and she says it has not worked well.
3. Where does Ruth say she always loses the room?
When she explains the trade-off to a board.
