Explaining your own method to an international audience. Key words: price elasticity, demand, SKU, trade-off, value proposition, breakeven, to forecast, competitor. Structure: the present perfect continuous for work that started in the past and is still going.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Price elasticity -- how much demand changes when the price changes
"Coffee has low price elasticity: people buy it anyway."
Demand -- how much of a product customers want to buy
"Demand for fresh food goes up at the weekend."
SKU -- the code for one exact product on the shelf
"We price this category at SKU level, not as a block."
Trade-off -- a situation where you gain one thing and lose another
"There is always a trade-off between volume and margin."
Value proposition -- the reason a customer chooses you and not a competitor
"Our value proposition is price and volume, not service."
Breakeven -- the point where the money in equals the money out
"Below this price we are under breakeven."
To forecast -- to say, with numbers, what you think will happen
"We forecast demand before we set the price."
Competitor -- another company that sells the same thing to the same customer
"We check every competitor in the region twice a week."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

A supermarket group in England has been testing a new pricing model since January. The team has been studying the demand for two hundred SKUs, and it has been measuring the price elasticity of each one. They have not been cutting every price: they chose the products customers remember. Since March, the group has been gaining customers from two larger competitors. The chief executive has known this market for twenty years, and he says the hard part was the trade-off, not the model.

1. Why do we say "the group has been testing a new model since January"?
A The test started in the past and it is still going on.
B The test finished in January and nobody continued it.
C The test is a routine that happens every January.
2. Why is "he has known this market for twenty years" not continuous?
A "Know" is a state verb, so it never takes the continuous form.
B Twenty years is too long for the continuous form.
C The sentence is about a competitor, so the form changes.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "We have testing the price for three months."
B "We have been testing the price for three months."
C "We are testing the price since three months."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect Continuous

GRAMMAR

For work that started in the past and is still going on today.

FormUseExample
have / has been + verb-ingWork that started in the past and is still going on. The focus is the activity, not the result.I have been working on the model.
for / sinceThe same partners as the present perfect: for takes a period, since takes a starting point.for three months / since 2019
Activity or result?Continuous shows the work in progress. The simple form shows the finished result.I have been building it. / I have built three.
State verbsVerbs like know, understand, believe, need, want never take the continuous. Say "I have known this market for ten years", never "I have been knowing".
