Chapter 3 -- The Code

When One Goes Up, the Other Moves

The higher..., the higher... The more..., the lower...

Discovery

Four Sentences, Two Halves

Read the highlighted words. What do the two halves of each sentence have in common?

"The higher the return, the higher the risk."

"The more collateral you offer, the lower the interest rate."

"The longer we wait, the fewer options we have."

"The sooner we decide, the better."

PatternFormExample
two adjectivesthe + comparative + noun, the + comparative + noun"The higher the return, the higher the risk."
with a nounthe more / the less + noun + clause, the + comparative"The more collateral you offer, the lower the rate."
with a verbthe + comparative + subject + verb, the + comparative + subject + verb"The longer we wait, the fewer options we have."
short fixed formthe + comparative, the + comparative"The sooner, the better."

Key idea: both halves start with the + a comparative, with a comma in the middle -- the second half changes because of the first. Two traps: never drop "the" ("More the risk, more the return"), and never double the comparative ("the more higher").

The Structure

Three Jobs at the Bank

The same pattern, doing three different jobs in a meeting with a banker.

1 · Show you understand the trade-off

"The higher the return, the higher the risk -- so what is the downside?"

The banker sees you will not buy a promise.

2 · Negotiate a condition

"The more collateral we offer, the lower the rate should be."

You link what you give to what you want.

3 · Push for a decision, politely

"The sooner we sign, the better for both sides."

A deadline without an order.

Notice the rhythm: two short halves, a small pause at the comma.
