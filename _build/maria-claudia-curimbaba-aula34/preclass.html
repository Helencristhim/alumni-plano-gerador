Lesson 34 -- Pre-class

Banking English -- Global Financial Partners

Talk to a private bank with confidence: say how risk and return move together with the + comparative ("the higher the return, the higher the risk", "the more collateral, the lower the rate", "the sooner, the better"). New vocabulary: portfolio, diversify, return, risk appetite, liquidity, equity, bond, interest rate, collateral, credit line.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Portfolio -- the group of investments that a person or company owns
"The family portfolio is mostly in Brazilian real estate."
Diversify -- to spread your money across different types of investment to reduce risk
"We want to diversify outside Brazil."
Return -- the money you make on an investment, usually shown as a percentage
"The bank expects a return of seven percent a year."
Risk appetite -- how much risk an investor is willing to accept
"Our risk appetite is low, because this is family money."
Liquidity -- how quickly and easily an investment can be sold for cash
"Real estate has low liquidity. It can take months to sell."
Equity -- shares in companies; money invested in the stock market
"Thirty percent of the portfolio is in equity."
Bond -- an investment that pays fixed interest and returns your money on a set date
"A US government bond is a safe place for part of the cash."
Interest rate -- the percentage a bank charges for a loan or pays on savings
"The interest rate on the credit line is six percent a year."
Collateral -- something valuable that you promise to a lender in case you cannot pay a loan back
"We offered part of the bond portfolio as collateral."
Credit line -- an agreement that lets a company borrow up to a fixed amount when it needs to
"The joint venture has a credit line of two million dollars."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

On Monday Maria Claudia meets Linda, a relationship manager at a private bank in New York. Before the meeting, she writes down what the family needs. Their risk appetite is moderate, and they need liquidity: one and a half million dollars in cash within twelve months. She also wants to diversify the family portfolio outside Brazil. Linda explains the basic rule: "The higher the return, the higher the risk." For the Houston joint venture, they also need a credit line. Linda says that the more collateral they offer, the lower the interest rate will be. Maria Claudia agrees to offer part of the bond portfolio as collateral, and she tells Linda that the sooner they sign, the better.

1. What does Linda say about return and risk?
A A higher return means the investment is safer for a family holding like hers.
B They go up together.
C Risk does not matter for bonds.
2. How can they get a lower interest rate?
A By offering more collateral.
B By keeping all the family money in Brazilian real estate for ten more years.
C By signing later.
3. Why does the family need liquidity?
A To buy more equity.
B Because the bank requires every client to keep half of the portfolio in cash at all times.
C They need cash within twelve months.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The More..., the More...

GRAMMAR

When two things change together, use the + comparative in BOTH halves of the sentence, with a comma in the middle. The second half changes because of the first.

PatternFormExample
two adjectivesthe + comparative + noun, the + comparative + nounThe higher the return, the higher the risk.
with a nounthe more / the less + noun + clause, the + comparativeThe more collateral you offer, the lower the rate.
with a verbthe + comparative + subject + verb, the + comparative + subject + verbThe longer we wait, the fewer options we have.
short fixed formthe + comparative, the + comparativeThe sooner, the better.

Key point: never drop "the": "More the risk, more the return". Never double the comparative: "the more higher", "the more better".
