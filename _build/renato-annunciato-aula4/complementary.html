Lesson 4 -- The Client Is a Doctor

Series
Painkiller (Imperio da Dor) -- Episode 1

A series about pharmaceutical sales representatives and the doctors they visit. It is the closest thing on television to the room you walk into every week, and the visit scenes are a full course in the language of access: the waiting room, the twenty minutes, the request, the follow-up.

Tip: watch it for the LANGUAGE of the visit, not for the argument of the story — this is a dramatised case, not a picture of your industry. Write down every sentence a representative uses to open a conversation with a doctor, and mark the ones you would never say.

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart -- Talk Less, Say More: How to Kick The Habit of Over-talking

A free episode from the Stanford business school on the exact problem of a twenty-minute visit: how much of the room you take, and what you lose by taking it. The doctor in today's listening said the same thing in different words — a representative who needs fifteen minutes to arrive at the point does not get booked again.

Tip: listen once without the transcript, then open the transcript and listen again. After that, time yourself saying your opening question out loud — if it is longer than fifteen seconds, cut it.

Listen on Stanford GSB ↗
YouTube
Ernesto Sirolli -- Want to Help Someone? Shut Up and Listen! (TED)

Seventeen minutes on what happens when you arrive somewhere with a solution before you have asked a single question. He is talking about development projects, but the mechanism is the one in today's second listening: the visit where you learn nothing is the visit you will have to repeat.

Tip: watch once with English subtitles for the argument. Then, before your next real visit, write down the three questions you would ask if you had no message to deliver at all.
