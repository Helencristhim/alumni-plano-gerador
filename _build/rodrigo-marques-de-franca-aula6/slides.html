Chapter 4: Who Is Saying It

An Opinion With Nobody Behind It

The passive, and the structures that report what people think

Grammar Discovery

Who Holds These Opinions?

"The staging was described as atmospheric by almost everybody."

It is often said that critics only argue about the mediocre productions."

"That performance is now thought to be the best of her career."

"The revival has been reviewed twice by the same newspaper."

Point at the person who holds each opinion. You cannot: in three of the four there is nobody there at all, and in the fourth the newspaper arrives only at the end. The red part is how the sentence does that on purpose.

JobFormExample
The passivebe + past participleThe play was reviewed twice.
Naming who, if it mattersby + agent, at the end...twice by the same newspaper.
Reporting what people thinkit is said / believed / thought thatIt is often said that critics agree.
The shorter versionsubject + is thought / said to beIt is thought to be a masterpiece.
Describingbe described / regarded asIt is widely regarded as her best.
Why you would use itto report an opinion without adopting itIt is thought to be brilliant. I disagree.
The trapthe passive always needs beNot the play described as atmospheric.

In one line: this is how you put an opinion on the table without standing behind it — and you already do it every day in Portuguese.

Listening 1 · A Critic

Why I Stopped Giving Stars

A theatre critic on the part of his job that has any value. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the argument FOR stars, and what is his argument against them?
For: readers are busy, which he admits is true. Against: a star rating is a verdict with the reasons removed, and the reasons are the only part of his job with any value.
2. He gives an example of a useful review. What does it tell the reader?
That the first act is twenty minutes too long and the second is the best thing the company has done in a decade — so the reader can decide whether that trade is worth their Saturday.
3. What did he learn against his own instincts, and what is his reason?
Not to review anything on the opening night. The room is full of people who are there to be seen having watched it, and that changes what happens on stage.
Listening 2 · Ten Years Apart

The Same Ending, Twice

Somebody who saw the same play at thirty and at forty. Sound first — no text.

1. What did she think of the ending the first time, and how did she feel about her own reaction?
She thought it was sentimental and unearned, and she remembers being quite pleased with herself for noticing.
2. What was different the second time, according to her?
Nothing in the staging. What was different is that by then she had been through the thing the play is about, and at thirty she simply had not.
3. What does she conclude about what a review is?
That it is a record of what one person could see on one evening — worth reading, and not the same thing as a measurement.

Five Phrases for Any Verdict

It is widely regarded as a masterpiece, and I found it uneven.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I can report what people think without saying that I agree with it.
I build the passive with be plus the participle, and I use by for the agent.
I never start a sentence with Is often said that.
I give a reason with every verdict, and never a verdict on its own.
I know the words: understated, gripping, uneven, a slow burn, to fall flat, to be panned.
