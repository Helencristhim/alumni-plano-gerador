Lesson 5 -- Holding the Room

Series
Suits -- Season 2, Episode 1

Back to the firm, this time for one thing only: watch who concedes first. In almost every confrontation the character who gives one point away early is the one still standing at the end of the scene. The English is fast, but the pattern is visible even when the words are not.

Tip: pick one confrontation and write down the first sentence each character says. Which of them conceded anything? What happened to the other one two minutes later?

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart -- episodes on handling difficult questions

The Stanford podcast has several episodes on exactly today's problem: answering under pressure, managing a hostile room, and speaking when you have not prepared. The advice lines up almost word for word with the board chair in the first listening, which is itself worth noticing.

Tip: find one phrase you would actually use with your global head, write it on the anticipated-questions sheet, and use it within two weeks.

YouTube
Celeste Headlee -- 10 Ways to Have a Better Conversation (TED)

Worth a second visit, for a different reason than in lesson three. Her rules about not equating your experience with someone else's, and about admitting when you do not know, are the whole second half of today's lesson stated as conversation rules rather than as executive technique.

Tip: this time watch only for the rule about saying "I do not know". Compare it with what the director in the second listening says about parking a question with a date.
