Chapter 4: If We Were

The Shape That Describes What Is Not True

A past tense that is not about the past

Grammar Discovery

Which One Is an Actual Plan?

"If we opened an office in New York, we would need a different kind of leader."

"If I were you, I would not raise before the platform is profitable."

"If we spun off the analytics unit, headcount would fall by forty."

"If we open an office in New York, we will need a different kind of leader."

Look at the red half of the first three: every verb is in a past form, and not one sentence is about the past. The green half is where would lives, never the red half. The orange pair is the only one describing something that is actually going to be decided.

JobFormExample
A future you are imagining, not planningIf + past simple, would + bare verbIf we opened in New York, we would need a leader there.
Advice, without giving an orderIf I were you, I would …If I were you, I would wait one more year.
The verb be, at this registerwere for every person, including I and itIf the market were stronger, we would raise now.
A result you are less sure aboutcould or might instead of wouldIf we spun it off, we might lose the platform team.
A real plan, not a pictureif + present, will (lesson 4)If we open in New York, we will need a leader there.
The trapwould never follows ifNot if we would open. Say if we opened.
The permanent targetafter would, the verb takes no ending at allNot he would needs. Say he would need.

In one line: the past form is not time. It is distance from the truth.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

The Question, From Both Sides

Two recordings and one scenario page

Listening 1 · A Growth Investor

Why We Ask the Five-Year Question

A growth investor on what she is really listening for. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say the answer is really testing?
Whether the founder has ever seriously considered a version of the company other than the one he already chose.
2. What answer does she say makes her stop listening?
Bigger, more clients, more countries — because it is a description of the same company with larger numbers, not a choice.
3. What does she want to hear instead?
Two futures and the trade-off between them, and which one the founder would take and why.
Listening 2 · A Founder Who Raised

What Dilution Actually Costs

A founder on the number he stopped looking at. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say founders count, and what do they stop counting?
They count the percentage they still own, and they stop counting how many decisions they can still make alone.
2. What happened to him in the second year after raising?
He still owned a majority, but he had to explain a hiring decision to three people before making it.
3. What does he say he would do differently?
He would have written down which decisions he wanted to keep, before the money arrived rather than after.

Three futures, one page. The page does not recommend anything. It only makes the board argue about the last row instead of about the first.
1. Which column costs you the most control, and what do you get for it?
Column B. Ownership falls from seventy-one percent to around forty, and in return headcount doubles and there are four years of runway.
2. Which column is the most dangerous, and why?
Column C. Nothing is diluted, but fourteen months of runway leaves almost no room if the first year is slow.
3. If the board could only remove one column, which would you take off the page?
Any answer works if it names the trade-off. What does not work is naming a column without saying what would be lost with it.

Five Sentences for the Five-Year Question

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I can describe a version of my company that does not exist: if we opened, we would need.
I never put would after if. The past form goes there instead.
I can give advice with if I were you, and I say were, not was.
I can hear the difference between a plan and a picture: if we open, we will versus if we opened, we would.
I know the words: a moat, headcount, to reinvest, runway, to spin off, a trade-off, to double down, dilution.
