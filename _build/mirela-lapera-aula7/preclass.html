Machu Picchu Memories -- I Was Climbing When...

Telling a trip to the mountains moment by moment. Key words: the altitude, a trail, ruins, the summit, a viewpoint, out of breath, foggy, to set off, a landmark, unforgettable. Structure: past continuous x simple past.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

The altitude -- how high a place is above the level of the sea
"The altitude made me feel dizzy."
A trail -- a path for walking in the mountains or in a forest
"We followed a narrow trail up the mountain."
Ruins -- the parts of an old building or city that are still standing
"The ruins are more than five hundred years old."
The summit -- the highest point of a mountain
"We reached the summit at nine in the morning."
A viewpoint -- a place where you can see a beautiful view
"From the viewpoint, you can see the whole city."
Out of breath -- breathing fast because you made a big physical effort
"After the stairs, I was completely out of breath."
Foggy -- with a thick, low cloud that makes it hard to see
"It was foggy, and we couldn’t see anything."
To set off -- to start a trip
"We set off at five in the morning."
A landmark -- a famous building or place that is easy to recognize
"Machu Picchu is the most famous landmark in Peru."
Unforgettable -- so special that you will always remember it
"The sunrise was unforgettable."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Peter, a colleague of Mirela’s, has just come back from Peru. On the big day, his group set off at five in the morning. They were climbing the trail when the clouds came down. It was so foggy that they couldn’t see the ruins.

“Everybody was waiting at the viewpoint,” he says. “Some people were taking photos of the fog. Then, at nine, the sun came out, and the whole city appeared.”

Because of the altitude, he was out of breath on every staircase. But he says the most famous landmark in Peru was worth it. “It was unforgettable.”

1. Why does the text say “They were climbing the trail when the clouds came down”?
A Because climbing is the scene, and the clouds coming down is the event that happened.
B Because they climbed the trail after the clouds came down, at the end of the day, in the evening.
C Because both actions were very short.
2. What was happening at the viewpoint before the sun came out?
A Nothing: the viewpoint was closed because of the fog and the rain, so nobody was there.
B Everybody was waiting, and some people were taking photos of the fog.
C Peter was sleeping.
3. Why was Peter out of breath?
A Because of the altitude.
B Because he was running to catch the bus.
C Because the trail was flat and very long, and he walked for more than ten hours without stopping.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Continuous x Simple Past

GRAMMAR

Use was / were + verb-ing for the scene: what was happening. Use the simple past for the events: what happened. Connect them with when and while.

JobFormExample
The scene, the backgroundwas / were + verb-ingWe were climbing...
The events, the storysimple past...when the fog came.
An interruptionpast continuous + when + simple pastI was walking when it started to rain.
Two things at the same timewhile + past continuousWhile I was taking a photo...
A sequence of eventssimple past, simple past, simple pastWe arrived, looked and smiled.
The trapa short event does not take -ingNot When the fog was coming, we climbed. Say We were climbing when the fog came.

Never: When the fog was coming, we climbed · While I took a photo · We was walking
