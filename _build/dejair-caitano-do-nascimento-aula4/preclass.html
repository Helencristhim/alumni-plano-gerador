Lesson 04 -- Pre-class

Bangkok Is Coming -- Your Week, Hour by Hour

Saying what you are going to do in October, and deciding things on the spot. Key words: schedule, registration, workshop, deadline, networking, moderator, to take part in, to look forward to. Structure: going to for plans and will for decisions made in the moment.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. Six of these have a time and a place on them. Two are things you will feel.

Schedule -- the plan of what happens and at what time
"The schedule says the workshop starts at two."
Registration -- the step where you give your name and get your badge
"Registration opens at eight on the first morning."
Workshop -- a small session where people work, not only listen
"I am going to take part in a workshop on Wednesday."
Deadline -- the last day you can do something
"The deadline to sign up is Friday at midnight."
Networking -- meeting people who can become contacts for your work
"The Wednesday dinner is really a networking event."
Moderator -- the person who runs a session and gives people the floor
"The moderator is going to ask for questions at the end."
To take part in -- to join an activity and do it, not only watch
"I am going to take part in the workshop, not just watch."
To look forward to -- to feel happy about something that is going to happen
"I am looking forward to meeting the group from India."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Going to and Will

GRAMMAR

Both are the future. The difference is when you decided. Use going to for a plan you already had before you spoke. Use will (usually I'll) for a decision, an offer or a promise made at the moment of speaking.

plan you already hadam / is / are going to + verbI am going to take part in the Wednesday workshop.
negative planam / is / are not going to + verbWe are not going to stay for the closing dinner.
question about a planam / is / are + subject + going toAre you going to the dinner on Wednesday?
decision, offer or promise nowwill / I'll + verb"I do not have your paper." -- "I'll send it tonight."
fixed programme timepresent simpleThe congress starts on October 26th.

After look forward to comes a noun or an -ING verb: "I am looking forward to meeting you", never "to meet you".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
You signed up for the workshop last week. Someone asks about your plans. What do you say?
A "I will take part in the workshop on Wednesday."
B "I am going to take part in the workshop on Wednesday."
C "I take part in the workshop on Wednesday."
A colleague says she does not have your paper. You decide right now to send it. What do you say?
A "I'll send it to you tonight."
B "I am going to send it, I decided last month."
C "I send it tonight."
You want to tell a colleague you are happy about the congress. Which sentence is correct?
A "I am looking forward to meet you in Bangkok."
B "I look forward meeting you in Bangkok."
C "I am looking forward to meeting you in Bangkok."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
A colleague asks how your first day in Bangkok is going to work. Record two minutes: what time you are going to arrive at the venue, what you are going to do at registration, which sessions you are going to choose and why, who you are going to look for, and how the day is going to end. No script -- just speak.
