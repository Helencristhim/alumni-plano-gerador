Chapter 3: The Code

Ask Them Back

One shape. Every English question uses it.

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Where are you from?"

"How long are you staying?"

"Do you have any brothers or sisters?"

"What time does the bus leave?"

Look at the word right after the question word. What is it, every single time?

ShapeHow it worksExample
Question word + helper + person + verbThe helper always comes before the person. Always.Where do you live?
What time does it start?
Yes / no questionsSame shape, without the question word. Start with the helper.Do you have a locker?
Are you coming with us?
With to beThe verb to be is its own helper, so nothing is added.Where are you from?
Is she your sister?
The -s ruleWhen does is in the question, the main verb loses its -s: "What time does the bus leave?" — never leaves.
Who or what as subjectOnly exception: when you ask about the person doing the action, no helper is needed. "Who lives here?", "What happened?"

Four boxes, always in the same order: question word · helper · person · verb.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"Where you are from?"
"Where are you from?"
"You like Vancouver?"
"Do you like Vancouver?"
"What means homeroom?"
"What does homeroom mean?"

Portuguese lets you ask a question with the same word order as a statement. English does not: the helper has to jump in front of the person, and the main verb keeps its base form.

Chapter 4: At the Airport

The Sign With Your Name

Vancouver International, arrivals, Sunday evening

Dialogue

Forty Minutes to Burnaby

G
You must be Lais. I'm Greg. Welcome to Canada. How was the flight?
L
Long. Really long. Thank you so much for coming to pick me up. How far is your house from here?
G
About forty minutes, a bit more if the bridge is busy. My daughter Emma is in the car. She insisted on coming.
L
That's really nice of her. How old is she? And does she go to the same school?
G
She's fifteen, and yes, same school. She'll show you around on Monday, so you won't walk in alone.
L
That helps a lot. Honestly, the first day is the nerve-racking part. The rest I'm looking forward to.
G
Everyone says that, and by Wednesday nobody remembers it. Are you hungry? We can stop somewhere on the way.
L
I'd love that. What do people eat here that we don't have in Brazil?
Comprehension

About Greg and Emma

1. How long is the drive from the airport to Greg's house?
About forty minutes, or a little more if the bridge is busy.
2. Who came to the airport with Greg, and why?
His daughter Emma. She insisted on coming.
3. What is Emma going to do on Monday?
Show Lais around the school, so she does not walk in alone.
Listening

A Voice Message from Your Host Dad

Greg recorded this the night before your flight. Sound first — no text.

1. Where exactly will Greg be waiting, and how will you recognise him?
Right outside the doors at international arrivals, holding a sign with her name on it.
2. Who is coming to the airport with him?
Emma, his daughter, because she refuses to miss it.
3. What does he say about the weather in September?
It is not freezing, it is pretty mild, so a light jacket is enough for the first weeks.
Listening 2

The School Welcome Line

A recorded message for new international students. Sound first — no text.

1. When and where does orientation happen?
Monday at nine, in the main hall, not in the classroom building.
2. What two documents do you have to bring?
Her passport and the letter from her school in Brazil.
3. What does the message say about feeling lost in the first week?
That it is completely normal, everyone felt the same, and nobody minds explaining twice.
