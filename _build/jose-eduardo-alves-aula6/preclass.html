Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

How Much, How Many? -- Talking About Quantity

Everything a contract counts, and everything it only measures. Key words: an amount, a quantity, cargo, volume, a quota, tuition, a credit, a condition, information, an option. Structure: quantifiers with countable and uncountable nouns.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An amount -- how much there is of something you cannot count one by one, like money or sugar
"The amount is already written in the contract, months before the ship arrives."
A quantity -- how many there are of something you can count, like ships or clauses
"Almost every difficult call is about a quantity that does not match."
Cargo -- the goods that a ship, a truck or a plane is carrying
"We need some information about the cargo before we release it."
Volume -- how much space or how much material there is, once somebody measures it
"The volume on the documents was not the volume the buyer expected."
A quota -- the maximum a country or a company allows in a period
"The export quota changed on Friday, so do not use the old figure."
Tuition -- the money a university charges you to study there
"How much is the tuition, and how many payments can you make?"
A credit -- one unit of study that counts toward a degree
"How many credits does the program have, and how many are compulsory?"
A condition -- something in a contract that has to be true, or the deal does not hold
"Are there any conditions attached to the second year?"
Information -- facts about something — one word that never takes an s and never takes an a
"We need some information about the cargo." Never: "an information."
An option -- one of the things you are allowed to choose
"If you cannot start in September, how many options do you actually have?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

English divides every noun into two groups, and the test takes one second: can you say one, two, three of it? A credit, a condition, an option, a quota, a day — yes, you can count them. Tuition, information, cargo, volume, time, money — no, you can only measure them. Everything else in this lesson follows from that one test.

Asking: "How many credits does the program have?" but "How much tuition does it cost?" A small quantity: "a few difficult calls" but "a little time". Not enough: "not many options" but "not much information". A large quantity is the easy one, because a lot of works for both: a lot of options, a lot of cargo.

Two more words do a job that Portuguese does with nothing at all. Use some in a positive sentence and any in a question or a negative: "We need some information about the cargo." "Are there any conditions for the second year?" "There are not any options left."

And here is the trap that costs Brazilians the most, because Portuguese does the opposite. The measured nouns take no plural and no a. It is never an information and never two informations; it is some information, or a piece of information when you really need to count. The same goes for cargo and for tuition. The other side notices this one immediately, and it is the fastest way to sound less senior than you are.

1. What is the one-second test that decides everything?
A Whether the noun is long or short.
B Whether you can say one, two, three of it.
C Whether the noun is about business or about study.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "I sent them all the informations about the quota."
B "I sent them all the information about the quota."
C "I sent them an information about the quota."
3. When do you use any instead of some?
A In a question or a negative sentence.
B Only with things you can count.
C When the quantity is very large.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Count It, or Measure It
