Grammar Discovery

Both halves are the future. Why only one will?

If you go outside the flags, the patrol will not see you.
If a rip takes you, you will get tired in a minute.
If the wind stays offshore, it will be good all morning.
All three are about tomorrow, not about now.

The condition stays in the present

Real and possible, in the future: if + present simple, then will + verb. The if half never takes will, even though it is about the future. Say the condition, then say the result — that is the whole sentence.

The Code

Two halves, one order you choose

The form

If it rains, I will stay home.
I will stay home if it rains.
If you wait, you won't get a wave.
What will you do if it is flat?

two traps

Never if it will rain
after "if", present simple — always
The comma
only when "if" comes first

Same sentence, two orders: If it is flat, I will go to the gym = I will go to the gym if it is flat.

Sound Check

Two words that collapse

If it's flat tomorrow, I'll go to the gym.

"I will" becomes one short sound: I'll

You won't feel it until you're past your waist.

"won't" has the long o of "go" — not the o of "want"

What will you do if the flags are down?

the question comes out as one block: "whattlyoudo"

Listening 1

Tuesday report

1. What will happen if you get down there early?
You will have the best two hours of the week, because the wind is offshore until ten.
2. Why should you not plan a long session?
After ten the wind turns onshore and it will be a mess by lunchtime.
3. What is the warning about the beaches up north?
The patrol is only on from nine, so if you paddle out before nine, nobody will be watching you.
Listening 2

Voice message before the beach

1. Where is the sunscreen, and what will happen if he forgets it?
In the blue bag by the door. If he forgets it, he will regret it by two o'clock.
2. What should he do if he sees something like a plastic bag in the water?
Get out. It is a jellyfish, and it will hurt for hours.
3. What is the rule about the yellow sign?
If the yellow sign is up at the top of the steps, do not go in — come home and go tomorrow.
