Lesson 12 — Pre-class

Read the Beach

Eight beach words (flag, warning, patrol, signal, jellyfish, drift, sunscreen, hazard) and the first conditional: if plus present simple, then will — the condition and the result in one sentence.

Stage 1.1: Beach Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Flag — a piece of colored cloth on a pole that tells you something
"Do not swim outside the flags."
Warning — words that tell you something dangerous may happen
"A yellow sign is a warning: something is dangerous today."
Patrol — the lifeguards who watch a beach at fixed hours
"The patrol watches this beach from seven to seven."
Signal — a movement or a sound that sends a message
"Float, raise one arm and signal to the lifeguards."
Jellyfish — a soft sea animal that stings you
"If the water is warm, there will be jellyfish."
Drift — to move slowly in the water without control
"If you stop paddling here, you will drift down the beach."
Sunscreen — the cream you put on your skin against the sun
"Put sunscreen on before you leave the house."
Hazard — a thing that can hurt you -- the word the signs use
"The sign says there is a hazard in the water today."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the notice. Then answer the questions.

Swim between the red and yellow flags. If you go outside them, the patrol will not see you. Read the signs at the top of the steps: a yellow sign is a warning, and it means there is a hazard in the water today. If the sign says strong current, you will feel it as soon as you are past your waist, and if you stop paddling you will drift down the beach. If a rip takes you, do not swim against it. Float, raise one arm and signal to the tower. If the water is warm in January, there will be jellyfish. And put sunscreen on before you leave the house — if you wait, you will forget.
Is this notice about now or about the future?
A The future — every sentence says what will happen if something happens.
B Now — it describes what is happening on the beach today.
Why is it "if a rip takes you" and not "if a rip will take you"?
A Because the rip is happening right now.
B Because the verb after "if" stays in the present simple, even about the future.
Which half of "If you go outside them, the patrol will not see you" is the result?
A "the patrol will not see you" — the half with "will".
B "If you go outside them" — the half with "if".

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

First conditional — the condition and the result

Two halves: if + present simple, then will + verb. Both halves are about the future, but only the second one takes will.

The form

If it rains, I will stay home.
I will stay home if it rains.
If you wait, you won't get a wave.
What will you do if it is flat?

two traps

Never if it will rain
after "if", present simple
The comma
only when "if" comes first

Compare: "If it rains, I stay home" → what always happens. "If it rains, I will stay home" → tomorrow, one time.
Third person: the -s does not disappear inside the "if" half. "If a rip takes you", not "if a rip take you".
In speech: I'll, you'll, we'll — and "won't" has the long o of "go".

Stage 4: On an Australian beach

Quiz
You want to warn a friend about swimming outside the flags. What do you say?
A If you swim outside the flags, the patrol won't see you.
B If you will swim outside the flags, the patrol won't see you.
Tomorrow is forecast flat. What is your plan?
A If it will be flat, I go to the gym.
B If it's flat, I'll go to the gym.
You are asking a new friend about his plan for the weekend.
A What will you do if the surf is good on Saturday?
B What you will do if the surf is good on Saturday?
The yellow sign is up and there is a hazard in the water. What do you tell your host brother?
A If a rip take you, you get tired.
B If a rip takes you, you will get tired in under a minute.

Stage 5: Five rules, five results

Reflection
Walk a new exchange student around your home break, out loud, for 30 seconds. Give five rules, and after each one say what will happen if he does not follow it. Check one thing before you send it — there is no "will" after "if".
