Chapter 4: The Code

When, Why, Despite, What For

The clauses that live inside the sentence

Grammar Discovery

Which Question Does the Colored Part Answer?

"As soon as one block finished, she walked around the building."

"Since she had trained six days a week, the news felt like a door closing."

"Although she studied more than anyone, her results hardly moved."

"She divided the day into blocks so that every subject had an end."

Each colored part is a full clause with its own subject and verb, but it cannot stand alone. It answers one question about the main clause: when, why, in spite of what, or what for.

QuestionSubordinatorsExample
When?when · as soon as · once · by the time · until · while · the momentAs soon as one block finished, she walked.
Why?because · since · as · given thatSince I have already applied, there is nothing left to do.
In spite of what?although · even though · whereas · whileWhereas volleyball had a sequence, studying had none.
What for?so that + clause · in order to / so as to + verbI study in blocks so that every subject has an end.
The future ruleinside a time clause, present — never willBy the time the exam is over, I will have decided.
Condition with timeas long as = only while it stays trueI will keep going as long as I am improving.
The trapno will after when, as soon as, by the time; no for toNot when I will finish or for to have.

For recognition only, in formal writing: No sooner had she sat down than the exam began.

Listening 1 · A Voice Note

My Mind Needed a Whistle

A former swimmer on what she lost when she retired. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say was the hardest thing about retiring?
Not the training she had lost, but the shape of the day.
2. Why did she give every task a beginning and an end?
So that she always knew which part of the day she was in.
3. What did she do as soon as one task finished, and why?
She stood up, even for one minute, because her mind needed a whistle, the way it always had in the pool.
Listening 2 · A Podcast

What Will Have Happened by the Time?

A study coach on planning a year backwards. Sound first — no text.

1. What question does he ask instead of what will you do?
What will have happened by the time — for example, by the time the exam is over.
2. Why does he say the question works?
Because it makes you plan backwards: you start from the end and ask what has to come first.
3. What does he say the trouble always is?
Never the size of the plan, but the sequence, which almost nobody writes down.
