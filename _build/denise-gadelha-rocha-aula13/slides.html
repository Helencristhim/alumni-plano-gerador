Where the Message Is Lost

Three Ways a Relayed Decision Goes Wrong

Word for word

the sentence arrives intact and the reasoning stays in Milan

Softened

the call goes well, the team hears a suggestion, and the deadline does not move

Unattributed

nobody knows whose decision it is, so three regions negotiate it again

None of these is a translation problem. All three are choices, and today they become choices you make on purpose.

Chapter 3: The Code

One Step Back

Reported speech, in full — tenses, pronouns, time and questions

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"He said the pricing floor was final and that he would review it in March."

"She told me the decision had come from Milan, not from the region."

"He asked me what I thought of the Colombian forecast."

"They asked whether we had already briefed the country leads."

The last two were questions when somebody asked them. Nothing is inverted now, and there is no do, does or did anywhere. And in all four, a verb has moved. Moved where?

MoveWhat happensExample
Tenses go one step backis → was · decided → had decided · will → would · can → could · must → had to"The floor is final." → He said the floor was final.
Pronouns move with the speakerthe I in the room becomes he in your report; you becomes we or they"I will send you my numbers." → He said he would send us his numbers.
Time and place move tootoday → that day · tomorrow → the following day · next week → the following week · here → there · this → that"We review it next week." → He said they would review it the following week.
Indirect questions lose the inversionstatement word order, and no do / does / did. The wh- word stays at the front"What do you think?" → He asked me what I thought.
Yes-or-no questions take if / whetherwhether is the register of a board room; if is fine in speech"Have you briefed them?" → They asked whether we had briefed them.
say vs tellsay (that) — no person. tell somebody (that) — always a person. Never he said meHe said the floor was final. / He told me the floor was final.
No backshift when it is still truea standing rule may keep the present — but choose it, do not forget itHe said the mandate runs to December. (and it still does)
Reporting verbs carry judgementinsist / urge / warn / claim / deny / concede / acknowledge. Patterns: insist that we hold (base form) · urge somebody to hold · warn somebody that · deny holding · acknowledge that.

The Executive Point

One Sentence, Five Different Mondays

He said eleven words in Milan. You choose the verb. Only one of these is true to what happened.

"He said we should wait."

Information. The team files it and waits to be told again.

"He suggested we wait."

The door is ajar. Somebody pushes on it, and you spend Friday explaining why it did not move.

"He insisted we wait."

The door is closed. Nobody spends a day on it, and the energy goes somewhere useful.

"He conceded we should wait."

He was pushed into it. So the team learns the position can be pushed again, and next quarter it will be.

"He claimed we should wait."

You have just told three countries that you do not believe your own group. That travels back to Milan.

Same meeting, same words, five different teams by Monday. The reporting verb is not neutral and it is not grammar — it is an editorial decision, and it is yours.

Chapter 4: The Cascade Call

Rafael, Andean Region

He does not want the minutes. He wants to know how hard the door is closed.

Dialogue

What Milan Actually Said

R
Denise, before you start — is this a decision or a proposal? My team will read it completely differently depending on your answer.
D
It is a directive. Milan decided on Tuesday, and I am relaying it, not opening it.
R
Then give it to me the way they gave it to you. Do not water it down for me.
D
I will not. Cornelli said the pricing floor was final, and he insisted that we hold it through the renewal season. He did not suggest it. He insisted.
R
That is a different sentence from the one I got in April. Somebody told me then that the floor could be reviewed.
D
It could then, and it cannot now. He also acknowledged that the timeline was theirs and not ours, which is the part I want your leads to hear.
R
Can I attribute that to him? It lands very differently if it comes from Milan and not from you.
D
You can attribute the decision. My read-out is with you this afternoon and the minutes come on Thursday. What they did not decide is how the Chilean shortfall is treated, and I would rather you heard that from me than assumed it.
R
Understood. Then I brief my leads tomorrow morning, I quote the decision, and I name the gap. That is a much cleaner call than the one I was expecting.
Comprehension

About Rafael

1. What does he want to know before she starts, and why?
Whether it is a decision or a proposal — because his team will read it completely differently.
2. What does he ask her not to do?
Water the message down for him. He wants it the way Milan gave it to her.
3. What does he say he will do the next morning?
Brief his leads, quote the decision, and name the gap.
Listening

What Survives the Cascade

A group chief of staff on what arrives at the fourth layer. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does he say travels well, and what does not?
The decision travels well. The reason behind it does not — by the fourth layer the instruction is intact and the reasoning has evaporated.
2. What is his test for whether a cascade has actually worked?
He asks somebody two levels down to say, in their own words, why they are doing it. Repeating the instruction accurately is not enough.
3. Why does he say a team that cannot state the purpose is a problem?
Because it cannot adapt when the situation changes — and it always does.
4. What does he say happens to the gaps you leave?
Somebody's assumption fills them, and assumptions travel faster than minutes.
Listening 2

The Verb Is Never Neutral

A group communications director on the half of the job nobody teaches. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she say is never neutral when you report what somebody said?
The verb you choose. Same meeting, same sentence, four verbs, four different rooms afterwards.
2. What happens when the verb tells her team the door is ajar?
They push on it, and she spends Friday explaining why it did not move.
3. What rule does she hold herself to?
The verb has to be true to the force of what was said, not to how comfortable she is delivering it.
4. What does she say she has done the moment she softens a verb to make her own afternoon easier?
She has taken a decision that was not hers to make.
Chapter 5: Practice

The Sentences You Will Say on Thursday

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "When I called him in March, he said that the budget is frozen and that he will revisit it next quarter."
"When I called him in March, he said that the budget was frozen and that he would revisit it the following quarter." (after a past reporting verb, is becomes was, will becomes would — and next quarter moves with them)
2. "He asked me what did I think about the Colombian forecast."
"He asked me what I thought about the Colombian forecast." (a reported question is no longer a question — statement word order, and no do, does or did)
3. "He said me that the decision was final."
"He told me that the decision was final." (or: "He said that the decision was final.") (say never takes a person directly — you tell somebody, you say something)
4. "She commented something about the timing in the meeting."
"She said something about the timing in the meeting." (or: "She commented on the timing.") (comment is not transitive in English — you comment on something, or you simply say it)
The Real Thing

The Minutes You Have to Relay

Minutes — Group Commercial Committee
Milan, Tuesday 14:00 CET
Circulated to regional directors
Read-out same day

Decision 1 — Pricing floor

Held at the current level through the renewal season. Tabled by G. Cornelli, Group Underwriting, who would not accept a regional exception. Carried without objection.

Decision 2 — Review date

Moved from January to March, on the Group calendar and not the regional one. The chair accepted that this was a Group constraint, not a regional preference.

Not decided

The treatment of the Chilean shortfall. Deferred to March with no interim guidance to the region.

Actions

D. Gadelha Rocha to brief the three country leads before Thursday. Read-out same day; minutes on Thursday.

The verb is yours

Write the sentence you will actually say to Rafael about Decision 1. Which reporting verb is true to the force of what was said?

1. Which line will your team most want to negotiate, and what does that mean for the verb you choose?
The pricing floor. It is the only decision that limits what they can sell, so it is the line they will test — which is exactly why a soft verb there costs more than anywhere else in the document.
2. Why does "not decided" belong in a read-out at all?
Because every gap you leave gets filled by somebody's assumption. Naming it is what stops three countries from inventing three different answers and working from them for a month.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I move the tense, the pronoun and the time marker together, without thinking about it.
I report a question without inverting it and without do, does or did.
I choose the reporting verb on purpose, and I can say why I chose it.
I name what was not decided before anybody has to assume it.
I hold a message at full strength in English, even when softening it would make the call easier.
