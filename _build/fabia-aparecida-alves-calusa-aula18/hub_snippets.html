Review — Four Percent in January

Nothing new this week. The same four words — adjustment, index, notice, cap — and the same shape: telling a client a plan your firm already decided. A new client, new numbers. Around ten minutes.

Stage 1: The same four words

Vocabulary

Nothing new here. Read each one and say it out loud. If it comes back on its own, that word is yours.

Adjustment — a change to a price, usually once a year
"The adjustment is every March."
Index — a number that shows how prices change over time
"This year the index is about five percent."
Notice — a message that tells someone about a change before it happens
"He gets notice sixty days before."
Cap — the highest number something can go up to
"His contract has a cap of eight percent."

Stage 3: A plan you already decided — one more time

Grammar

No new rule this week. The same shape as lesson 17, with a new client: we are going to adjust the fee in March; it is going to follow the index; it is not going to go above eight percent. The are or is always stays, and the verb after to has no s.

"In March, we ______ adjust the fee."

B are going to
"We are going to ______ you notice sixty days before."

B sending
Which sentence is correct?
A It not is going to go above our cap of eight percent.
B It is not going to go above our cap.
The CFO asks about the fee. Which answer can he plan his budget with?
A We are going to adjust it in March, about five percent.
B Yes, it is going to change a little after the first year, more or less, it depends.

Stage 5: Say it out loud

Speaking

Three lines. Say "we are going to" as one block, then the plan. Listen first, then record yourself.

In March, we are going to adjust the fee.
This year, it is going to be about five percent.
It is not going to go above our cap of eight percent.

Stage 6: Before class

Speaking
Choose one real client of your firm (no name needed). Record three sentences as if you were talking to that client: when you are going to adjust the fee (we are going to...), how many days of notice they are going to get, and one limit (it is not going to...). One sentence each — no more. In class you will use this client.
