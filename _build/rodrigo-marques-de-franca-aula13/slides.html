Chapter 3: The Code

Soft Words, Hard Point

How English disagrees without raising its voice

Grammar Discovery

Take the Red Words Out. What Changes?

I'd say the deadline is a little optimistic.

It seems to me that we might be missing one fact.

I'm not entirely convinced the clause covers this.

Wouldn't it be safer to ask the client first?

In all four, the speaker disagrees. What do the red words change — the meaning, or the tone?

ToolFormExample
Distancing modalwould / might / could + verbI'd say it's too early. That might be a problem.
Tentative verbseem / appear / tend toIt seems to me that... Courts tend to read it narrowly.
Softened negativenot + entirely / quite / completely + positive wordI'm not entirely convinced. (not: That's wrong.)
Downtonera little / slightly / somewhatThe fee is slightly high.
Negative questionWouldn't it / Couldn't we / Isn't there... ?Couldn't we wait for the audit? ↘
Acknowledge firstfair point / I see where you are coming from + butThat's a fair point, but...
The limitone hedge per sentence, never threeNot: Maybe perhaps it could possibly...

In one line: keep the point hard and the words soft — one hedge is polite, three is vague.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Disagreement, at Full Speed

One call, two recordings, one email

Dialogue

Their Offer Is an Insult

M
Rodrigo, I'll be blunt. Their offer is an insult. I say we reject it today and go straight to the hearing.
R
I see where you're coming from, and it is a low number. But I'm not entirely convinced the hearing is our best option.
M
Really? Our case is strong. You said so yourself last week.
R
It is strong on liability. On damages, I'd say it's more of a gray area. The tribunal might not accept our numbers.
M
That's a fair point. But if we accept anything now, they'll think we're weak.
R
Wouldn't it make more sense to send a counteroffer? We keep the pressure on, and we start with the common ground.
M
Point taken. But let me play devil's advocate. What if they simply refuse?
R
Then we go to the hearing, and we have lost nothing. I just don't think we should sit on the fence for another week.
Comprehension

About Megan

1. How does Megan describe the offer, and what does she want to do?
She calls it an insult. She wants to reject it today and go straight to the hearing.
2. Why is Megan worried about accepting anything now?
She thinks the other side will see them as weak.
3. What question does Megan ask when she plays devil's advocate?
What happens if the other side simply refuses the counteroffer.
Listening 1 · The Chair

Behind a Closed Door

A tribunal chair on how she disagrees with senior colleagues. Sound first — no text.

1. What happened when she told colleagues directly that an argument was wrong?
It never worked. People defend a position harder when you attack it directly.
2. What does she do first, and how many points does she challenge?
She finds the common ground and says it out loud. Then she challenges only one point.
3. What did a colleague once tell her, and how did she take it?
That she disagrees more than anyone else on the tribunal and nobody notices. She took it as a compliment.
Listening 2 · The Trainer

Polite Is Not Vague

A negotiation trainer on the two mistakes he sees every year. Sound first — no text.

1. What happens when the first group is too direct?
The room goes cold.
2. Why is the second group's problem harder to fix?
Because it looks like good manners. They hedge everything, and nobody knows what they actually think.
3. What is the rule he gives them?
One hedge per sentence, never three. Keep the point hard and the words soft.
Real Document

Two Emails, Two Tones

RE: Their settlement offer
Confidential · privileged · attorney work product
FROM: Megan Hart (New York) · 7:42 a.m.
Their offer is a joke. We reject it today and go to the hearing. Agreed?
FROM: Rodrigo Marques de França (São Paulo) · 9:15 a.m.
Megan, I see where you are coming from, and the number is low. I would, however, take issue with going straight to the hearing: damages are a gray area, and the tribunal might not accept our figures. Wouldn't a counteroffer at 4.2 million keep the pressure on? Happy to agree to disagree on tone, but I think we need a decision by Friday.
Hearing scheduled for March 12 · offer valid until Friday, 6:00 p.m. (New York time)
1. Which word in Megan's email shows that she expects a yes, not a discussion?
"Agreed?" She does not ask what he thinks; she asks him to confirm.
2. Which point does Rodrigo take issue with, and why?
Going straight to the hearing, because damages are a gray area and the tribunal might not accept their figures.
3. Find three hedges in Rodrigo's reply.
"I would, however, take issue", "might not accept", "Wouldn't a counteroffer...?", "I think".

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I acknowledge the other person's point before I disagree.
I soften a disagreement with would, might, seem or not entirely, and my point stays clear.
I suggest an alternative with a negative question: Wouldn't it...?
I use one hedge per sentence, never three.
I know the words: to beg to differ, a gray area, common ground, to play devil's advocate, to agree to disagree.
