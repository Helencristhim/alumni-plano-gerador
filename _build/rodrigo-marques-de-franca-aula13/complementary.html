Lesson 13 -- I See It Differently

Podcast
NPR Up First (Short Wave) -- "Science-backed tips for difficult conversations"

Two friends who have disagreed about almost everything for forty-five years explain how they stay friends, and a scientist explains what happens in the brain when somebody contradicts us. The same moves as this lesson: find the common ground first, then disagree.

Tip: the page carries the full transcript. Listen once without reading and write down every phrase that softens a disagreement. Then check the text.

Talk
Julia Dhar -- "How to disagree productively and find common ground" (TED)

A world debating champion on why the best disagreements start from shared facts and attack the idea, not the person. Eleven minutes, very clear American English, and full of sentences you can reuse in a negotiation.

Tip: pause after each of her three ideas and say, in one sentence, how you would use it in your next call. Use one hedge in each sentence, never three.

Watch on TED ↗
Film
12 Angry Men (1957)

Twelve jurors, one room, and one man who is not sure. The whole film is a single disagreement, and Henry Fonda wins it without ever raising his voice. Available in Brazil to rent or stream (Prime Video, Apple TV).

Tip: watch the first twenty minutes twice. The second time, listen only to how Juror 8 disagrees: count how often he says "I don't know" or "maybe", and notice that his point never changes.
