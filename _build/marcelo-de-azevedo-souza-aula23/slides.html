Chapter 3: The Code

Saying Less

So do I · Neither can I · I hope not · Sounds good

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

Every highlighted answer is a full idea in two or three words. What is missing, and what stays?

A. "I love this coast road." — "So do I."

B. "I could not make out the names." — "Neither could I."

C. "Will it rain on the boat?" — "I hope not."

D. "Coffee before the tour?" — "Sounds good."

Grammar Rule

The Short Answers Natives Use

FormYou use it forExample
So + auxiliary + subjectagreeing with a positive sentence"I love this road." "So do I."
Neither / Nor + auxiliary + subjectagreeing with a negative sentence"I could not hear." "Neither could I."
think / hope / guess / be afraid + so / notanswering without repeating the whole sentence"Will it rain?" "I hope not."
dropped words at the startrelaxed, informal speech"(Do you want) Coffee?" "(That) Sounds good."

Key idea: natives say less than you expect, so the listener fills in the gaps. In So and Neither, the auxiliary copies the tense of the first sentence: "I went" → "So did I", "I have never been" → "Neither have I". And it is "I hope not", never "I hope no"; "I don't think so" is more natural than "I think not".

Grammar Practice

Answer in Three Words

Say your answer out loud first, then tap to check.

"I was exhausted after the ride." — "So _____was I." (copy the tense)
"I have never heard that accent." — "Neither _____have I." (copy the auxiliary)
"Is the tour still on?" — "I think _____so." (you believe yes)
"Did we miss the last boat?" — "I hope _____not." (you want the answer to be no)

Dialogue

After the Tour with Nora

N
So? Did you get any of that?
M
Some of it. He talks so fast. I could not make out the names of the islands at all.
N
Neither could I, and I grew up here. He rattles them off like a shopping list.
M
Really? I thought it was just me. I zoned out after the third island, I am afraid.
N
So did half the boat. The trick is to tune in again on the story, not the names. Did you get the gist of the shipwreck?
M
I think so. Two brothers, a storm, and only one boat came back. Tell me I got that right.
N
You did. See? You understand far more than you think.

Self-Assessment

What I Learned

I agree with a positive sentence: "So do I", "So did I".
I agree with a negative sentence: "Neither can I", "Neither have I".
I answer without repeating: "I think so", "I hope not", "I am afraid not".
I recognize dropped words in fast speech: "Coming?", "Sounds good."
I listen for the gist and fill in the gaps, instead of chasing every word.
