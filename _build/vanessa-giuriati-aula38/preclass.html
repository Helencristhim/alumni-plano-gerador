Lesson 38 -- Pre-class

How Do You Really Feel?

Talk about feelings with precise words: -ed adjectives for how a person feels (I'm frustrated) and -ing adjectives for what causes it (it's frustrating), plus make me feel. Key words: overwhelmed, relieved, frustrated, thrilled, let down, on edge, fed up, over the moon.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Overwhelmed -- Feeling that you have too much to do or to feel, and you can't manage it all
"I felt overwhelmed in my first week at the new job."
Relieved -- Calm and happy because something bad did not happen or is finished
"I was so relieved when the results came back fine."
Frustrated -- Upset and annoyed because something is not working or you can't change it
"I get frustrated when the internet is slow."
Thrilled -- Very happy and excited about something that happened
"She was thrilled with the surprise party."
Let down -- Disappointed because someone did not do what they promised
"I felt let down when he didn't come to my birthday."
On edge -- Nervous and worried, waiting for something to happen
"I was on edge all morning before the interview."
Fed up -- Annoyed and bored because something has continued for too long
"I'm fed up with this traffic every morning."
Over the moon -- Extremely happy, usually because of good news
"My mother was over the moon when I called her."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

What a week! On Monday, I was overwhelmed: forty-seven emails were waiting for me after the holiday. It was exhausting, and by lunchtime I was fed up. On Tuesday, a colleague forgot to send me an important file, and I felt a little let down. On Thursday, I was on edge all day, because my presentation was the next morning. But on Friday, everything changed. The presentation was a success, and the client said it was the most interesting proposal he had seen that year. I was relieved, and my manager was thrilled. When I told my mother, she was over the moon. It's funny how one good day makes a whole week feel different.

2. How did she feel on Thursday, and why?
A Over the moon, because her manager loved the proposal she had sent.
B On edge, because of the presentation.
C Let down, because of the client.
3. Who was over the moon?
A The client who said it was the most interesting proposal of the year.
B Her colleague.
C Her mother.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- -ed and -ing adjectives

GRAMMAR

Use -ed adjectives for how a person feels, and -ing adjectives for the thing or situation that causes the feeling. Use make + someone + feel to say what causes it.

StructureUseExample
-ed adjectiveHow a person feelsI'm frustrated.
-ing adjectiveWhat causes the feelingThe traffic is frustrating.
make + someone + feel + adjectiveThe cause of a feelingLong meetings make me feel tired.
It's + -ing adjective + to + verbA comment on an experienceIt's exhausting to commute two hours.

'I'm boring' means people find you boring! Say 'I'm bored'. No 'to' after make me feel: 'It makes me feel tired'.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.
