Chapter 3

The Feeling or the Cause?

Bored or boring? It changes everything

Grammar Discovery

Bored or Boring?

"The meeting was frustrating, so I was frustrated."

"The news was overwhelming. I felt overwhelmed for days."

"It's thrilling to see my progress. I'm thrilled!"

"Long meetings make me feel exhausted."

FormUseExample
-ed adjectiveHow a person feelsI'm frustrated.
-ing adjectiveWhat causes the feeling (a thing, a situation)The traffic is frustrating.
make + someone + feel + adjectiveSay what causes a feelingLong meetings make me feel tired.
It's + -ing adjective + to + verbComment on an experienceIt's exhausting to commute two hours.
⚠ Careful'I'm boring' means people find YOU boring! Say 'I'm bored'. And no 'to' after make me feel: 'It makes me feel tired', not 'It makes me to feel tired'.

Chapter 7

Lock It In

Five sentences for real feelings

Survival Card

Vanessa's Feelings Card

1Honestly, it's been a bit overwhelming.
2That sounds really frustrating.
3I felt let down, to be honest.
4I'm so relieved it's over!
5I'm thrilled for you!
I can say how I feel with precise words, not just "good" or "bad".
I know the difference: I'm bored (the feeling) / it's boring (the cause).
I can say what causes a feeling: "Long meetings make me feel tired."
I can use overwhelmed, relieved, frustrated, thrilled, let down, on edge, fed up and over the moon.
I can react to other people's feelings: "That sounds frustrating." / "I'm thrilled for you!"
