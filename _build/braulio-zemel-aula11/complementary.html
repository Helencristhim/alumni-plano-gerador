Talk
"What is a story?" -- Matthew Dicks, TED

Fifteen minutes from a man who has won more live storytelling competitions than almost anybody, on what actually makes a story a story. Connection to Lesson 11: his answer is the turn -- the moment something changes -- which is exactly what you practiced today. The page carries the full English transcript beside the video.

Tip: watch once with English subtitles and once without. Then take your own anecdote and write down, in one line, the exact moment it turns. If you cannot find it, the story is not ready.

"How to tell a good story" -- Life Kit, NPR

Twenty minutes of American English with the storytelling coaches from The Moth, who train people to hold a room for five minutes without notes. Connection to Lesson 11: it is the same three rules from today, with eight practical tips on top and real examples of stories being cut down. The full transcript sits under the player.

Tip: listen once without the text and write down only the tips you can remember. Then open the transcript and see which ones you missed. Those are the ones worth trying on your own anecdote.

Grammar Video
"I USED TO & I'M USED TO" -- engVid, free lesson with quiz

A free video lesson on the exact pair that trips up almost every Portuguese speaker, with a quiz underneath the video. Connection to Lesson 11: it is the difference between the habit that stopped and the thing that is normal now, which is half of your survival card.

Tip: do the quiz at the bottom of the page after the video. Then say three sentences about yourself out loud: one with used to, one with am used to, and one with am getting used to.
