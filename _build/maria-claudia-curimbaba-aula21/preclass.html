Maria Claudia in Charge -- Leading the Meeting

Run the quarterly board meeting yourself. Make polite requests with "Would you mind" + the -ing form and "Could you" + the base verb, and ask indirect questions in statement word order. New vocabulary: chair, convene, steer, digress, overrun, interject, chime in, show of hands, motion, sidebar.

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Chair -- to lead a meeting and keep it in order
"Maria Claudia will chair the quarterly board meeting."
Convene -- to call people together so a meeting can start
"The board convenes every quarter in Sao Paulo."
Steer -- to guide a discussion in the direction you need
"Let me steer us back to the budget for a moment."
Digress -- to move away from the subject you were discussing
"We are starting to digress, so let us come back to item three."
Overrun -- to continue past the time the meeting should end
"We cannot overrun: the Houston call starts at eleven."
Interject -- to add a short comment while someone else is speaking
"May I interject for one second before you move on?"
Chime in -- to join a discussion with a quick, welcome comment
"David, please chime in whenever the numbers look wrong."
Show of hands -- a quick, informal vote in which people raise a hand
"Can I get a show of hands on the Houston budget?"
Motion -- a formal proposal that the meeting votes on
"The motion to approve the Houston budget carries."
Sidebar -- a short side conversation away from the main topic
"That is a sidebar. Let us handle it after the meeting."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

On Thursday Maria Claudia will chair the quarterly board meeting for the first time. She convenes the room at nine and opens with a request, never with an order: "Would you mind taking us through the numbers?" For the shorter asks she uses the everyday form: "Could you send the minutes to the board tonight?" When she needs information that somebody may be nervous about, she makes the question indirect: "Could you tell me where we stand on the contract?" -- and she keeps the word order of a statement, with no inversion and no do or does. Halfway through, two directors open a sidebar about the port and the room begins to digress, so she steers it back: "That is a sidebar. Shall we come back to item three?" David asks whether he may interject one figure, and she invites the finance director to chime in as well. The meeting must not overrun, because counsel joins at ten, so she closes each item with a show of hands and puts the budget to the board as a formal motion.

1. Which form follows "Would you mind" in the text?
A The -ing form ("taking us through the numbers").
B "to" plus the base verb ("to take us through").
C The past form ("took us through").
2. What happens to the word order after "Could you tell me"?
A It becomes a question: "where do we stand".
B It keeps the order of a statement: "where we stand".
C It needs "does" before the verb.
3. Why does the chair close each item with a show of hands?
A Because the board prefers a written vote.
B Because the sidebar has to be recorded.
C Because the meeting must not overrun: counsel joins at ten.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Polite Requests & Indirect Questions

GRAMMAR

The chair of a meeting gives instructions all hour long and almost never uses the imperative. Three structures carry it. WOULD YOU MIND is followed by the -ing form, never by "to". COULD YOU is followed by the base verb. And an indirect question ("Could you tell me...") keeps the word order of a statement: no inversion, and no do or does.

FormUseExample
Would you mind + -ingthe most polite request"Would you mind repeating the figure?"
Could you + base verbpolite and quick, for most of the hour"Could you walk us through slide four?"
Could you tell me + statement orderindirect question: no inversion, no do / does"Could you tell me when the audit is ready?"
Shall we + base verbmove the whole room together"Shall we move on to the next item?"
I would like to hear from + personbring in someone who has not spoken"I would like to hear from David on this."

Key point: two traps to watch. After mind the verb takes -ing ("Would you mind to repeat" → "Would you mind repeating"). And an indirect question is not a question inside: "Could you tell me where do we stand" → "Could you tell me where we stand".
