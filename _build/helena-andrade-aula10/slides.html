Chapter 4: The Code

The Verb With No Ending

requires that he notify · recommends that she complete · it is essential that it stay

Grammar Discovery

Where Did the S Go?

"The convention requires that the master notify the authority immediately."

"The regime requires that every vessel calling at a member port be liable to inspection."

"It recommends that all crew members complete the relevant drill before departure."

"It is essential that it stay that way."

Three of these have a singular subject and a verb with no s, and one uses be where you would expect is. Say what the four have in common in the words that come BEFORE the that.

FormWhat it doesExample
require / recommend / demand / insist + thatthe verb in the that-clause goes into the BASE form, whatever the subjectThe Code requires that the master notify the authority.
it is essential / vital / imperative thatsame base form, in the impersonal construction regulations preferIt is essential that the standard be independent.
be, not is or arethe base form of be is be, in every personrequires that every vessel be liable to inspection
the negativenot goes straight in front of the base form, with no auxiliaryrequires that the master not sail without clearance
The reading trapthe base form looks like a plural, a typing error, or a missing auxiliary, and every one of those misreadings is offered to you as an option. Requires that the master notifies is not the same sentence: it is not regulatory English at all, and choosing it means you did not recognise the form.

The subjunctive survives in English almost only where somebody is being told what to do. That is why a regulation is full of it and a newspaper is not -- and why recognising it is worth more marks than it looks.

The Debrief

Listening 1

What an Inspector Actually Decides

A port state control officer on the only judgement that is his. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. How many vessels has he detained, and over how long?
Eleven vessels in nine years, and he says he did not enjoy any of them.
2. What does he record, and what does he not decide?
He records what he finds -- an unserviced lifeboat release gear is a deficiency whether the master agrees or not. He does not decide whether it matters.
3. Why does he say the review by somebody who never saw the ship is not a weakness?
Because it is the whole design: recording the deficiency and deciding whether it justifies detention are two different responsibilities.
Listening 2

Marks Lost in Block Two

The examiner returns, on the three mistakes of the harder texts. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What were the three mistakes of the first block, and what is the fourth?
Degree, agency and register. The fourth, which separates good candidates from very good ones, is structure.
2. What three structural mistakes does she name?
A relative clause attached to the wrong noun, a cleft read as an ordinary sentence, and a subjunctive taken for a typing error.
3. Where does she say a candidate should slow down?
After a comma that introduces a which, after the word that, before any verb with no ending, and at the first word of a sentence beginning with a negative adverb.
