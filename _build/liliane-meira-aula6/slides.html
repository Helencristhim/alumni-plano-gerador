Chapter 4: Stacking the Words

Four Words, One Noun

The shape every wall label is written in

Grammar Discovery

Where Is the Thing Itself?

"At the centre of the room is a large-scale site-specific sound installation."

"Around it hang four small untitled works on paper."

"The label for the four-channel sound piece runs to six lines."

"It was a 2019 museum acquisition, not a loan."

The blue word is the thing itself, and it always comes last. Everything in red is stacked in front of it.

RuleFormExample
The head noun comes lasteverything before it describes ita sound installation is an installation, not a sound
Stack, do not unpacksize + place/type + material + NOUNa large-scale site-specific sound installation
Hyphen before the nouna compound modifier takes a hyphen there, and loses it after the verba large-scale work / the work is large scale
A number in front stays singularnever an s on the premodifiera four-channel piece, not four-channels
A noun used as an adjective stays singular toonoun + noun, the first one flata research project, a museum acquisition
The trapdo not rebuild it with ofnot a work of sound of four channels

In one line: put the thing last and pile everything else in front of it, flat and singular.

Listening 1 · The Interpretation Desk

Half of It Is a Decision

The person who writes the wall, on how the wall gets written. Sound first — no text.

1. How many words does he have for a room text, and how many for a label?
About sixty for a room text and about twenty for a label, and he says every one of them is negotiated.
2. Why does he rewrite what the artist puts in the medium line?
Because a label describes what is physically in the room, not what the work is about. What it is about belongs in the room text, which he calls a different genre entirely.
3. What are the two things he advises an artist to do, and why the hurry?
Ask to see the label before it is printed, and read the medium line out loud. Say something that same week, because once it is on the wall it stays there for the whole run of the show.
Listening 2 · The Bottom Two Lines

What Visitors Skip and Researchers Read First

The collections side of the same label. Sound first — no text.

1. What do the three credit lines mean: courtesy of the artist, on loan, and acquisition?
Courtesy of the artist means it came straight from the studio and goes straight back. On loan with a date means it belongs to somebody else for a fixed period. Acquisition with a year means the museum owns it and it is not going anywhere.
2. When is the provenance usually incomplete, and why?
For older work, and for anything made inside an institution such as a hospital or a school, because the provenance is only ever as good as the paperwork.
3. What does the museum write when it cannot prove who made the work, and how does she describe the difference?
Attributed to rather than by. She calls it the difference between a certainty and a strong argument, and says it is not a small thing to put on a wall.
