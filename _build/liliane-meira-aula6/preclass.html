Reading the Wall Text -- How the Institution Writes About the Work

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A wall label -- the small printed card beside a work, with the facts and nothing else
"The wall label gives the title, the date and the medium, and it stops there."
A wall text -- the longer panel that explains a whole room, or a group of works together
"The wall text at the entrance tells you what the room is arguing."
The medium -- the material, or the materials, a work is physically made of
"The medium is listed first, before the dimensions."
Dimensions -- the measured size of a work, height given before width
"Dimensions are in centimeters here, height before width."
Provenance -- the recorded history of who has owned a work, from the artist onward
"A gap in the provenance is the first thing a researcher looks for."
On loan -- held for a fixed period from another collection, and going back after it
"Three of the drawings are on loan from a private collection."
Courtesy of -- naming who allowed the work to be shown, at the end of the label
"Courtesy of the artist and the gallery, with the city after the gallery name."
Site-specific -- made for one place, and losing part of its meaning anywhere else
"It is a site-specific installation, so it will not travel."
An acquisition -- a work the institution has bought, or been given, for its own collection
"The piece was a 2019 acquisition, and it is not going anywhere."
To attribute -- to say which artist made a work when there is no signature and no record
"The drawing is attributed to her, not signed by her."
Untitled -- given no title by the artist on purpose, and written with a capital letter
"Untitled, 2021, is not the same as a work nobody bothered to name."
Mixed media -- made with more than one material, named on the label as a single phrase
"Mixed media: four-channel sound, hospital furniture, printed transcripts."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At the centre of Room 4 stands a large-scale site-specific sound installation, built from recordings made in a public hospital. Around it hang four small untitled works on paper, lent by the artist. The wall text at the entrance explains what the room is arguing; the wall label beside each work gives the facts and stops there.

The label for the sound piece reads: Untitled (Ward 7), 2023. Mixed media: four-channel sound, hospital furniture, printed transcripts. Dimensions variable. Courtesy of the artist and a private collection. On loan until March. Nine facts in six lines, and not one adjective. The interpretation team writes the room text; the registrar writes the label; and the artist approves the medium line.

Two of the drawings were a 2019 museum acquisition. The other two are attributed to the same artist but not signed, because the provenance of work made inside a hospital is rarely complete. Everything here is written the same way: the thing itself comes last, and everything that describes it is stacked in front of it, flat and singular.

1. "A large-scale site-specific sound installation." What is the thing itself?
A The sound, because it is the most specific word in the phrase.
B The installation. The head noun comes last, and everything before it describes it.
C The scale, because it is the first thing named.
2. "Four-channel sound" has no s on channel. Why not?
A A modifier in front of the noun stays singular, even after a number.
B Because there is only one channel in the work.
C Because the word channel has no plural form in English.
3. "A 2019 museum acquisition." Why not "an acquisition of the museum of 2019"?
A Both are normal English, and the longer one is more formal.
B Because a year can never be used to describe a noun.
C Because English stacks the modifiers in front of the noun instead of unpacking them with of.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Complex Noun Phrases and Premodification

GRAMMAR

The shape every wall label is written in.

RuleFormExample
The head noun comes lasteverything before it describes ita sound installation is an installation, not a sound
Stack, do not unpacksize + place/type + material + NOUNa large-scale site-specific sound installation
Hyphen before the nounthe compound takes a hyphen there, and loses it after the verba large-scale work / the work is large scale
A number in front stays singularnever an s on the premodifiera four-channel piece, not four-channels
A noun used as an adjective stays singular toonoun + noun, the first one flata research project, a museum acquisition
Place names work the same wayplace + past participle, hyphenated, before the nouna Berlin-based artist

Never: an installation of sound with four channels · an artist Berlin-based · a large scale work (before the noun) · three-channels videos works.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the sentence you would actually say.

The gallery asks what the work is. You answer:
A "It is an installation of sound with four channels, in large scale."
B "It is a large-scale, site-specific, four-channel sound installation."
C "It is a sound installation four-channels and specific of the site."
The label is being printed tomorrow and you want to check it. You write:
A "Could you send me the label before it goes to print? I would like to read the medium line."
B "Send the label after the printing, I check then."
C "The label of medium of the work, I want to see."
Two drawings are not signed and there is no record. The label should say:
A "By the artist, probably."
B "Attributed to the artist, because the provenance is incomplete."
C "Courtesy of the artist, because nobody can prove anything."
A curator wants to move your piece to a different gallery. You say:
A "It is a specific work of the site, so no."
B "It is a site-specific piece, built for that stairwell. If it moves, the label has to say so."
C "The work is on loan of the site, it cannot go."
