Lesson 6 -- Reading the Wall Text

Podcast
The Allusionist 6 -- "The Writing on the Wall"

Helen Zaltzman and the museum researcher Rachel Souhami look closely at the small texts on museum walls, the ones most visitors walk straight past. Who actually writes a label, how much a museum decides to tell you, and how one artist turned label text itself into a criticism of the institution.

Tip: listen once without the transcript, then open the free transcript on the same site and count how many stacked noun phrases you had missed.

Talk
Thelma Golden -- "How art gives shape to cultural change" (TED, 12 min)

The director of the Studio Museum in Harlem on what a curator actually does: choosing the artists, building the exhibition, and finding the words that frame a whole moment in art. It is the institution's voice, spoken instead of printed.

Tip: listen for the long noun phrases she uses to describe shows and artists' work, and write down three of them exactly as she says them.

Watch on TED ↗
Reading
"Writing Gallery Text at the V&A: A Ten Point Guide" (Victoria and Albert Museum)

The in-house manual a major museum hands its own curators before they write a single wall label. It fixes the word count for every kind of text, from the room panel to the object label, and it shows real labels before and after editing.

Tip: read points one to five only, then rewrite the label of one of your own works against them. You do not need the whole document.
