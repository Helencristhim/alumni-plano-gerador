Listening 1 · Norway

The Man Who Started the Coffee

Eighteen years in Oslo, and what he misses is two minutes long. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. How long did Lars live in Oslo, and what did he say for a long time when people asked what he missed?
Eighteen years. For a long time he said he missed nothing, and he says that was not honest.
2. What were his two old habits, and did he plan either of them?
The same coffee place every morning before seven, where the man started making his coffee without asking; and meeting his brother at the station on Fridays for the last train. He calls the first one second nature and says they never planned the second one once.
3. What two things does he say are true at the same time?
That life is better now, and that he still misses the coffee man. He says nobody ever tells you that before you move.
Listening 2 · Britain

The Other Way Round

A village in Yorkshire, then London at twenty-six. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Which way did Ruth move, and at what age?
From a village in Yorkshire to London, at twenty-six. She calls it the other way round.
2. What did everybody expect her to talk about, and what did she notice instead?
Everybody expected the traffic. What she actually noticed was the loss of the boring parts of the day.
3. What did she dread before moving, and what did she actually lose?
She dreaded the noise. What she lost was the part of the day where she was not deciding anything.
Chapter 4: The Code

Used To, Would, and the Plain Past

Three forms, three different jobs

Grammar Discovery

Which One Happened Only Once?

"I used to order takeout three nights a week."

"Every Friday I would swing by the bakery on the way home."

"I used to live within walking distance of everything."

"In 2021 I moved to the countryside."

The orange ones say this was my life and it is not any more. The blue one replays a scene, and only works because we already know which years. The green one happened once.

FormIts jobExample
used to + base verbA habit or a state that was true then and is not true now.I used to order takeout.
would + base verbA repeated scene the listener can picture — only when they already know when.Every Friday I would swing by the bakery.
would + a state verbNot possible. be, have, live, know, like take used to only.I used to live in the city.
Negativedid not use to — no d on the end of use.I did not use to cook.
Questiondid you use to ...? — same rule, no d.Did you use to take the train?
Plain pastOne finished event, or a fact with a number on it.I moved in 2021. I lived there for forty years.

In one line: used to opens the routine, would replays a scene inside it, and the plain past is for the day it actually happened.

Dialogue

What Did You Do Every Single Day?

I
So you were in Sao Paulo for forty years. What did you do every single day there that you do not do now?
A
Takeout. I used to order takeout three nights a week, and I did not even look at the menu.
I
Because cooking after work was a hassle.
A
Exactly. And nothing here is within walking distance, so now I cook. I did not use to cook at all.
I
I had the same thing with the shop. In Oslo I would stop there on the way home every evening. Now I stock up once a week and I plan it.
A
That is the part nobody warns you about. An errand used to take ten minutes. Now it takes an afternoon.
I
And do you miss it, or do you only miss the convenience?
A
Neither, really. I miss being a creature of habit without organizing it. Even winding down is something I schedule now.
Comprehension

About Ingrid

1. Where did Ingrid live, and what did she do on the way home every evening?
In Oslo, and she would stop at the shop on the way home every single evening.
2. What has replaced that habit for her now?
She stocks up once a week, and she has to plan it.
3. What does she ask at the end, and how does she soften the question?
She asks whether Ana misses the habit itself or only the convenience. She does not soften it at all: there is no I suppose, no if you do not mind me asking. The question arrives bare.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I use used to for a habit or a state that is finished.
I use would to replay a repeated scene, once the listener knows when.
I never say I would live, I would be or I would have for the past.
I say did not use to and did you use to, with no d on use.
I know the words: a ritual, mundane, an errand, a hassle, to swing by, second nature, a creature of habit.
