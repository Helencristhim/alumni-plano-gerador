Chapter 4: The Code

Two Ways to Say Every Day

used to · would — and where one of them stops working

Grammar Discovery

Which Two Can Swap Form?

"I used to live ten minutes from downtown."

"Every morning I would leave the house before six."

"The square used to be full on Sunday evenings."

"The station master would shout the name of every stop."

The green ones are things somebody did, again and again. The blue ones are things that simply were.

FormIts jobExample
used to + verbPast states AND past repeated actions. Always safe.I used to live near downtown.
would + verbPast repeated actions only. Never a state.I would leave before six.
The trapA state with would is wrong.I would live near downtown.
Time framewould needs the past to be established first.Back then, I would walk everywhere.
NegativeThe -d moves to did, so it is use.I did not use to drive.
QuestionSame rule: Did you use to ...?Did you use to work there?
One event onlyOne finished action is past simple, not used to.I left the city in 2015.

In one line: if it is something you WERE, only used to works. If it is something you DID over and over, both work.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I use to live near downtown."
"I used to live near downtown."
"I did not used to drive to work."
"I did not use to drive to work."
"I would be really restless back then."
"I used to be really restless back then."
"Do you used to run errands on Saturdays?"
"Did you use to run errands on Saturdays?"

Three of these four are the same error in different clothes: the -d belongs to the past, and the past can only be marked once per sentence. The fourth one is the state that would cannot carry.

Dialogue

The Photo from the Christmas Party

P
Ana! I was going through some old photos and I found the one from the office Christmas party. Do you remember those?
A
Of course I do. I used to dread that party every single year.
P
You did? I always thought you loved it. You would stay until the very end.
A
I stayed because I was juggling two teams and I could not say no to anybody. I was a creature of habit, and the habit was saying yes.
P
Fair enough. Honestly, I have cut back on all of that. I do not even go into the office on Fridays anymore.
A
Good for you. And do you still run errands on Saturday mornings, the way you used to?
P
Every Saturday, same order, same streets. It is second nature by now. But I am restless, Ana. I keep thinking about doing what you did.
A
Then come and see it for yourself. I bump into more people here in one week than I did in a whole year down there.
Comprehension

About Paulo

1. What did Paulo always think about Ana and that party?
That she loved it, because she would stay until the very end.
2. What has Paulo cut back on?
All of it. He does not even go into the office on Fridays anymore.
3. What does he say about his Saturdays, and how does he feel now?
He runs errands every Saturday, same order, same streets — it is second nature. But he is restless and keeps thinking about moving.

1. How long did the trip to the office use to take?
Forty-five minutes. She would leave at five fifty-five and arrive at twenty to seven.
2. Which line needs "would", and which line needs "used to"?
"I would leave the house at five fifty-five" is a repeated action. "I used to have only one free evening a week" is a situation, so only used to works.
3. How many hours a day did she use to be out of the house?
Almost fourteen: from five fifty-five in the morning until ten to eight at night.
