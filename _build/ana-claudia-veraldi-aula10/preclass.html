Lesson 10 -- Pre-class

I Used to Live in the City

The habits that disappeared when your life changed. Key words: a creature of habit, an errand, to be stuck in a rut, hectic, to juggle, to dread, to bump into, to swap, second nature, to cut back on, restless, nowadays. Structure: used to and would for past habits and past states.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A creature of habit -- someone who always does the same things in the same order
"The whole town was a creature of habit."
An errand -- a short trip you make to do one small necessary job
"On Saturdays I used to run errands until two."
To be stuck in a rut -- to be trapped in a routine that never changes and never improves
"I was stuck in a rut and I did not even know it."
Hectic -- very busy and fast, in a way that feels out of control
"Mornings that used to be hectic became slow."
To juggle -- to handle two or more demanding things at the same time
"I was juggling two teams and I could not say no."
To dread -- to feel fear or heavy dislike about something before it happens
"I used to dread walking past the old station."
To bump into -- to meet someone by chance, with no plan at all
"I bump into half the town at the market."
To swap -- to give one thing up and take another in its place
"Families had to swap the train for a slow bus."
Second nature -- something you do so automatically that it takes no thought
"The work has become second nature to them."
To cut back on -- to reduce how much of something you do or use
"Most people cut back on their trips to the capital."
Restless -- unable to relax, sit still or stay in one place
"Nobody is restless anymore."
Nowadays -- in the present period of time, in contrast with the past
"Nowadays my whole morning takes twenty minutes."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Ana used to live ten minutes from Butantan, and her whole week ran on a timetable. She would get up at five, she would leave the house before six, and on Saturdays she would run errands until two in the afternoon. She was a creature of habit, and the habit was hectic. She did not use to notice it — she was juggling too much to notice anything at all. Nowadays she gets up when the dogs do. The mornings that used to be a race are slow, and walking down to the gate is second nature. She has cut back on almost everything, and she is not restless about it. "I used to dread Mondays," she says. "I do not dread anything now."

1. Which sentence describes a past SITUATION, so it cannot use "would"?
A "She would leave the house before six."
B "She used to live ten minutes from Butantan."
C "She would run errands until two."
2. Which negative form is correct?
A "She did not used to notice it."
B "She did not use to notice it."
C "She used not notice it."
3. According to the text, what changed most about her mornings?
A They became slow instead of a race.
B She started getting up even earlier than before.
C She stopped walking down to the gate.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used To and Would

GRAMMAR

Two ways to say "every day, back then" -- and the place where one of them stops working.

FormIts jobExample
used to + verbPast states and past repeated actions. Always safe.I used to live near downtown.
would + verbPast repeated actions only — never a state.I would leave before six.
The trapA state with would is wrong.I would live near downtown.
Time framewould needs the past to be established first.Back then, I would walk everywhere.
NegativeThe -d moves to did, so it is use.I did not use to drive.
QuestionSame rule: Did you use to ...?Did you use to work there?
In one lineIf it is something you WERE, only used to works. If it is something you DID over and over, both work.
