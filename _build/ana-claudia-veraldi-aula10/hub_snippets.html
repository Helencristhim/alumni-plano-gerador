Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To take something for granted -- to stop noticing something good because it is always there
"I took the bakery on the corner completely for granted."
A creature of habit -- somebody who does the same things in the same order, every time
"I am a creature of habit, and the move broke every routine I had."
To fall into a routine -- to start doing the same thing every day without ever deciding to
"Within a month I had fallen into a routine I never chose."
Second nature -- so familiar that you do it without thinking at all
"By the third year that drive was second nature."
On autopilot -- doing something without paying any attention, from sheer repetition
"I did that whole journey on autopilot, five days a week."
To break a habit -- to stop doing something you have done for years
"Moving house is the fastest way to break a habit."
Back then -- at that time in the past, when things were different
"Back then, nobody worked from home at all."
To miss out on something -- to lose the chance to have or do something good
"I missed out on eleven years of evenings, sitting in that car."
A regular -- somebody who goes to the same place so often that they are known there
"I was a regular at that place for nine years and I never asked his name."
To wind down -- to relax slowly at the end of a day or a period of work
"It took me two hours to wind down after that commute."
To dread -- to feel real fear about something that has not happened yet
"I used to dread Sunday evenings more than Monday mornings."
To look back on something -- to think about a period of your life that is over
"When I look back on those years I mostly remember being tired."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Ana used to live in Sao Paulo, and for eleven years her weekdays never changed. She would leave the flat at ten past seven, she would sit in the same traffic on the same avenue, and she would buy a coffee from the same man at the same kiosk. She never asked his name.

There used to be a bakery on her corner, and it closed while she was still living there. She barely noticed, because by then the whole journey was second nature and she did it on autopilot. She was a creature of habit who had fallen into a routine she never chose, and she took every part of it for granted.

She did not use to notice the noise. She used to dread Sunday evenings more than Monday mornings, and it took two hours to wind down after every commute. She moved to the interior in March 2024. When she looks back on those years now, what she mostly remembers is being tired.

1. Why "used to live" and not "would live" in the first line?
A Because live is a state, and states only take used to.
B Because used to is more formal than would.
C Because would is only used in questions.
2. "She would leave the flat at ten past seven." What does would tell you here?
A That she left once and it was memorable.
B That it was a repeated action, part of a remembered routine.
C That she was planning to leave but did not.
3. Why "She moved to the interior in March 2024" and not "she used to move"?
A Because it is one finished event with a date, which always takes past simple.
B Because moving is a state and states never take used to.
C Because the sentence is in the third person.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used To and Would

GRAMMAR

Portuguese does this with one tense. English built two structures and a rule between them.

FormWhat it doesExample
used to + verbA habit or a state that was true then and is not nowI used to live in Sao Paulo.
would + verbA repeated action, inside a memoryEvery Monday I would take the same train.
states take used to onlybe, have, know, like, live — would is impossibleThere used to be a bakery.
negativedid not use to — the d moves to didI did not use to notice it.
questiondid you use to...?Did you use to walk to work?
one finished eventPast simple. Never used to.I moved in March.
not be used tobe / get used to + -ing is a different structureI am used to the quiet now.

Never: I use to live · I did not used to notice · there would be a bakery · I used to move in March. Two of those put the d in the wrong place, one breaks the state rule, and one turns a single event into a habit.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

A colleague asks what your weekdays were like in the city. You answer:
A "I used to leave at ten past seven and I would sit in the same traffic."
B "I use to leave at ten past seven and I was sitting in the same traffic."
C "I would be leaving at ten past seven every day."
You want to say a bakery existed on your corner and does not now. The natural version is:
A "There would be a bakery on the corner."
B "There used to be a bakery on the corner."
C "There was being a bakery on the corner."
Somebody asks when you moved. The correct answer is:
A "I used to move in March 2024."
B "I moved in March 2024."
C "I would move in March 2024."
You want to say you never noticed the noise back then. The correct negative is:
A "I did not use to notice the noise."
B "I did not used to notice the noise."
C "I use not to notice the noise."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.
