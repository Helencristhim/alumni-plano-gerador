I Used to Live in the City -- The Habits That Stayed Behind

Nobody ever decided to have those habits, and then one day they were gone. Key words: a ritual, to dread, mundane, to stock up, an errand, takeout, a hassle, to swing by, within walking distance, to wind down, second nature, a creature of habit. Structure: used to and would for past habits, against the plain past.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A ritual -- a small thing you do the same way every time, because it matters to you
"Coffee before seven was a ritual, not a habit."
To dread -- to feel bad in advance about something that is coming
"I used to dread Sunday evenings for eleven years."
Mundane -- ordinary and repetitive, with nothing special about it
"For the first year the quiet felt mundane rather than peaceful."
To stock up -- to buy a large amount of something so that you do not run out
"Now I stock up once a week, because the shop is twenty minutes away."
An errand -- a short trip you make to do one small necessary job
"An errand that used to take four minutes now takes an afternoon."
Takeout -- restaurant food that you order and eat at home
"I used to order takeout three nights a week."
A hassle -- something small that takes far more effort than it should
"Cooking after eleven hours was a hassle, so I never did it."
To swing by -- to stop somewhere quickly on your way to somewhere else
"Every Friday I would swing by the bakery on the way home."
Within walking distance -- near enough that you can get there on foot
"Everything used to be within walking distance. Now nothing is."
To wind down -- to let yourself slow down at the end of the day
"I never had to plan how to wind down. It simply happened."
Second nature -- something you do without thinking, after years of doing it
"That was second nature. I did not choose it."
A creature of habit -- a person who does the same things in the same order every day
"I was a creature of habit and I did not even know it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Ana used to live within walking distance of everything. The pharmacy, the bank and three restaurants were all four minutes away, so an errand was never something she had to plan. She used to order takeout three nights a week, because cooking after eleven hours was a hassle.

Every Friday she would swing by the same bakery on the way home, and every Sunday evening she would dread the week ahead. Nobody taught her that route. After nine years it was second nature. She was a creature of habit and she did not even know it.

Then, in 2021, she moved to the countryside. She did not use to cook at all, and now she cooks every night. She stocks up once a week because the shop is twenty minutes away, and for the first year the quiet felt mundane rather than peaceful. Even winding down is something she has to schedule.

1. Why "she used to live within walking distance" and not "she would live within walking distance"?
A Because would is only used for the future.
B Because live is a state, and states take used to and never would.
C Because the sentence already has the word within.
2. "Every Friday she would swing by the same bakery." Why does would work here?
A Because it is a repeated scene and the reader already knows which period of her life we are in.
B Because bakeries always take would in English.
C Because the action was unpleasant.
3. "Then, in 2021, she moved to the countryside." Why the plain past and not used to?
A Because moving is a state verb.
B Because used to never appears in the same paragraph as a year.
C Because it happened once, on a date. Used to and would are for things that repeated.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used To, Would and the Plain Past

GRAMMAR

Three forms, three different jobs.

FormIts jobExample
used to + base verbA habit or a state that was true then and is not true nowI used to order takeout.
would + base verbA repeated scene, only when the listener already knows whenEvery Friday I would swing by the bakery.
would + a state verbNot possible: be, have, live, know, like take used to onlyI used to live in the city.
Negativedid not use to -- no d on the end of useI did not use to cook.
Questiondid you use to ...? -- same rule, no dDid you use to take the train?
Plain pastOne finished event, or a fact with a number on itI moved in 2021.

Never: I use to live in the city · I would live in the city · I did not used to cook · Did you used to take the train? · Last Tuesday I would swing by the pharmacy.
