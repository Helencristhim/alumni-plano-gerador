Lesson 04 -- Pre-class

How the Rule Was Made -- The Passive Voice in Regulation

How a rule is prepared, discussed, published and sometimes cancelled. Key words: a ruling, a framework, to enforce, to amend, a public consultation, to issue, an agency, a requirement, to overturn, a loophole. Structure: the passive voice in the present, past and future.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A ruling -- an official decision made by a court or an agency
"The ruling was published on Monday."
A framework -- the set of rules and principles that organize a sector
"The new framework for ports was approved last year."
To enforce -- to make sure that people follow a rule or a law
"The agency does not have enough staff to enforce the rules."
To amend -- to change a law, a rule or a contract officially
"The contract was amended twice in five years."
A public consultation -- a period when the public can send comments on a proposed rule
"The public consultation is open until the end of the month."
To issue -- to publish or give something officially, such as a license or a rule
"The license will be issued next week."
An agency -- a public organization responsible for a sector or an activity
"The agency regulates electricity in the whole country."
A requirement -- something that a rule says you must do or have
"One new requirement is an annual safety report."
To overturn -- to cancel an official decision, usually in a court
"The ruling was overturned by a higher court."
A loophole -- a weakness in a rule that allows people to avoid it legally
"The new rule closes an old loophole in the contract."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last year, a new framework for electricity was prepared by the agency. A public consultation was opened, and more than five hundred comments were received.

The text was amended in June, and the final rule was published in August. Today, tariffs are reviewed every year, and a new requirement will be enforced from January.

Not everybody is happy. The rule has been taken to court by two companies. If the judge agrees with them, the ruling will be overturned. Notice that the text almost never says who did each action: the decision is more important than the person.

1. Who prepared the new framework?
A The companies.
B The agency.
C The judge.
2. What happens to tariffs today?
A They are reviewed every year.
B They were reviewed only once, in August.
C They will never be reviewed.
3. Why does the text use the passive voice so much?
A Because the actions are not important.
B Because the decision is more important than the person who took it.
C Because the passive voice is only used in the past.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive Voice in Three Tenses

GRAMMAR

be in the right tense + past participle. Add by + the agent only when it matters.

JobFormExample
Present (a regular action)am / is / are + past participleTariffs are reviewed every year.
Past (a finished action)was / were + past participleThe rule was published in March.
Futurewill be + past participleThe framework will be approved next month.
Present perfecthas / have been + past participleThe contract has been amended twice.
When the agent matters... by + the agentIt was overturned by a higher court.
The trapthe verb be is never missingNot The rule published in March. Say The rule was published.

Never: The rule published in March · The framework will approved · The license was issue · Tariffs are reviewing every year
