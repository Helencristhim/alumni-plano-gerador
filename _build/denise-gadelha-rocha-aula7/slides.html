Where Credibility Is Lost

Three Ways to Lose an Uncertain Room

Vagueness

"it could go either way" — true, and useless to anyone deciding

False precision

a single number you cannot defend in March, given in October

Precision about the doubt

a range, a driver, and the level at which you would change your mind

Only the third one survives the next quarter. It also needs the most precise English of the three, which is what today is for.

Chapter 3: The Code

Which One Do You Mean?

Relative clauses, and the comma that changes the number

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The limits that we set in February were built for a season we no longer expect."

"The southern portfolio, which we stress-tested in June, is the one that worries me."

"This is the threshold at which we stop writing new business."

"The broker whose model we use has changed the return period."

One of the four carries commas. Remove that part and the sentence still stands. Remove any of the others and the sentence loses the thing it was about. What is the comma actually doing?

FormWhat it doesExample
that / which — no commastells the room which ones; take it out and the sentence loses its subjectThe limits that we set in February were built for another season.
, which ... , — with commasadds information about something already identified; removable. Never that hereThe southern portfolio, which we stress-tested in June, is the one that worries me.
who / whosepeople and organizations; whose = belonging to themThe broker whose model we use has changed the return period.
where / whenplace and time — shorter than in which / on whichThe season when the losses arrive has moved.
preposition + whichthe register of a board note: formal, exact, never casualthe threshold at which we stop; the basis on which we priced it
The comma is meaning, not style"The limits that were revised in February" = only those. "The limits, which were revised in February," = all of them, and by the way. In a risk note, that comma is a different decision.
Two trapsThe relative pronoun is the object — never repeat it (the portfolio which we tested it). And never that after a comma.

The Executive Point

One Comma, Two Different Decisions

Defining — some of them

"The limits that were revised in February no longer reflect the exposure."

Only the February ones. The rest are fine. The room revises one set.

Non-defining — all of them

"The limits, which were revised in February, no longer reflect the exposure."

Every limit we have. February is background. The room revises the whole book.

Same eleven words. Two different requests to the committee. This is why the comma is not a matter of taste in a risk note.

Chapter 4: The Risk Committee

Marco, Group Risk

He does not want a forecast. He wants to know what would change your mind.

Dialogue

The Number You Cannot Promise

M
Denise, I have your projection for next year. Before we discuss the number, tell me how much confidence sits behind it.
D
Less than the single figure suggests. Can I give you the range and the driver instead?
M
That is the answer I was hoping for. Go ahead.
D
Nine to thirteen percent, and eleven is the base case. Almost all of the volatility sits in one place: the agricultural book in the south, which is where a warm season stops being weather and becomes a loss.
M
And the downside of nine. Is that a bad season, or is that a bad season on top of something else?
D
One bad season. What we have not priced in is a year in which two events arrive together, at the limits that we set in February. That is the tail risk, and I would rather name it here than have it found.
M
Have you stress-tested that? And do not tell me the model output. Tell me what breaks first.
D
Capacity in the south breaks first, at around forty percent of the aggregate. So that is the threshold I want agreed on Thursday, rather than argued about in February. Broker submissions are our leading indicator, and they turned in July.
M
Then bring the threshold, not the projection. That is a shorter conversation and a better one.
Comprehension

About Marco

1. What does he ask for before he will discuss the number itself?
How much confidence sits behind it.
2. What does he refuse to be given when he asks about the stress test?
The model output — he wants to know what breaks first.
3. What does he ask her to bring instead of the projection?
The threshold.
Listening

A Model Is Not a Forecast

A catastrophe modeller on what the output actually says. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does he say the model actually gives you?
A distribution — a shape of what could happen — not a prediction of what will.
2. What happens to that distribution on its way to the board?
Somebody flattens it into a single number, and by the third slide it has become a promise nobody made.
3. What question does he say is worth more than the number?
What would have to happen for the number to be wrong.
4. What does he say about the years the model has never seen?
The record it is built on is short, so the year that costs you is often one it could not have known about.
Listening 2

Careful, or Just Vague

A chief underwriting officer on the difference, and what each one costs. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is her test for whether a careful sentence is doing any work?
Whether it tells you what the doubt is about — if it does not, it is vagueness wearing careful clothes.
2. What does she say a range must always come with?
A driver — the reason it is a range, and where the movement sits.
3. Why does she say vagueness is more expensive than being wrong?
A wrong number can be corrected once; a vague one is never corrected, because nobody can say what it claimed.
4. What does she recommend saying when you genuinely do not know?
Name the level at which you would change your mind, so the room gets a decision rule instead of a shrug.
Chapter 5: Practice

The Sentences You Will Actually Write

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "The limits, that were revised in February, no longer reflect the exposure."
"The limits that were revised in February no longer reflect the exposure." (a defining clause takes no commas — and never that after a comma)
2. "The southern portfolio, which we stress-tested it in June, is the one that worries me."
"The southern portfolio, which we stress-tested in June, is the one that worries me." (which already is the object — no second pronoun)
3. "We realized a stress test on the agricultural book in June."
"We ran a stress test on the agricultural book in June." (to realize means to become aware of something — realizar is to run or to carry out)
4. "At those limits the portfolio cannot support another bad season."
"At those limits the portfolio cannot withstand another bad season." (to support means to back or to fund — suportar is to withstand)

The One-Page Risk Note

Risk note — for the committee, Thursday
Climate exposure — southern agricultural book
D. Gadelha Rocha
One page only

Base case

Eleven percent growth, at a loss ratio in line with the plan.

Downside

Nine percent, driven almost entirely by the agricultural book, which sits in the region where the season has moved by three weeks.

Not priced in

A year in which two events arrive together, at the limits that we set in February.

Threshold requested

Capacity review at forty percent of the aggregate — the level at which the southern book stops absorbing.

Leading indicator to watch

Write your own. Which signal moves before the loss does?

1. Why does the note ask for a threshold rather than approval of the projection?
A projection cannot be approved — it can only be believed or doubted. A threshold is a decision the committee can actually take, and it takes it before the pressure arrives.
2. In "at the limits that we set in February", what would change if you added commas?
It would mean every limit we have, with February as background information — a request to review the whole book instead of one set.
