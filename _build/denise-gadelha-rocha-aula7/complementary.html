Lesson 7 -- Navigating Uncertainty

Documentary
Inside Bill's Brain -- Part 3

The third part is a documentary about climate risk told entirely in the language of this lesson: base cases, thresholds, what happens in the tail, and what is not priced in. Listen for how rarely anyone gives a single number, and for how often a range arrives with the driver attached to it.

Tip: write down three sentences where somebody describes a risk without claiming to know the outcome. Two of them will use a relative clause to say exactly which case they mean.

Podcast
WorkLife with Adam Grant -- episodes on decisions and rethinking

Several episodes deal with the thing that sits underneath today's grammar: how confident to sound when you are not certain, and what it costs a leader to change a stated view. Grant interviews people who have to commit to positions in public and then revise them, which is exactly the position you are in every time the season moves.

Tip: choose one episode and note every phrase used to signal doubt without sounding vague. Bring two of them to class and we will test whether they carry a driver.

YouTube
Hans Rosling -- The Best Stats You've Ever Seen (TED)

Rosling presents a moving distribution to a room that arrived expecting averages, and he never once flattens it into a single figure. Watch it as a masterclass in the thing Group Risk asked for in today's dialogue: showing where the movement sits, rather than reporting the middle of it.

Tip: count how many times he says which countries, which years or which group he means. That is a relative clause doing technical work, and it is why nobody in that room can misread him.
