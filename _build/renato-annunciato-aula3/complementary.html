Lesson 3 -- Reporting the Numbers

Series
Formula 1: Drive to Survive -- Season 1, Episode 1

Team principals reporting a bad result to an owner who does not want excuses. Every episode is somebody explaining a number they did not want to explain, and the ones who survive are the ones who name the problem first and bring a plan with it. It is your quarterly review, at three hundred kilometres an hour.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Every time a team principal explains a result, decide whether he gives the number first or the excuse first — and notice which of the two the owner listens to.

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart -- Strategic Success: How to Communicate Your Gameplan

A free episode from the Stanford business school on how to explain a plan so that senior people act on it. This is the third sentence of today's report, taken apart properly: what happens next, why you believe it, and what you need from the room in order to deliver it.

Tip: listen once at normal speed without stopping, then again with the transcript open. Write down two sentences you could say in your next review, exactly as he says them.

Listen on Stanford GSB ↗
YouTube
Hans Rosling -- The Best Stats You Have Ever Seen (TED)

Twenty minutes of a doctor making numbers tell a story, out loud, without a single boring slide. He is a physician talking to a room that is not made of physicians — which is exactly the translation you do every time you explain your market to somebody outside it.

Tip: he speaks with a Swedish accent, which is useful practice: most people on your international calls will not be native speakers either. Watch once with subtitles, once without.
