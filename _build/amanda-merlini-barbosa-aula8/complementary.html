Film
Dark Waters (2019)

A corporate lawyer spends years reading boxes of company records to prove what a chemical company knew. It is a film about documents: what was recorded, what was hidden, and what a report says when nobody wants to be named in it.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Every time somebody reads from a document, write down one passive sentence you hear, and who the hidden agent is.

Podcast
Learning English Grammar (BBC) -- Active and passive voice

A short episode from BBC Learning English on when to put the action first and when to put the person first. Six minutes, clear British and American examples, and the same choice you make every time you write a report.

Tip: listen once without pausing. Then listen again and pause after each example to say it in the other voice, active to passive or passive to active.

Video
David Detzler: Carbon Offsets — The Big Lie

A hard critique of carbon offsets, and of what happens when the verification is weak. You will not agree with all of it, and that is the point: it is the argument your reports have to answer.

Tip: after watching, write three sentences in the passive that a verification report would use to answer his main criticism.
