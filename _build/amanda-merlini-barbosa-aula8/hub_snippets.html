Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

What Was Done, Not Who Did It

The language of the regulatory record, and the call with the verification body that goes through it point by point. Key words: findings, submission, supporting evidence, discrepancy, to be granted, to be issued, to be audited, to be governed by. Structure: the passive voice -- putting the fact at the front of the sentence, and naming the agent only when it matters.

Stage 1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Four words for the document, four for what happens to it. Listen to each one and read it in context.

findings -- what an inspection concluded, written down
"The report has been submitted and we are waiting for the findings."
submission -- the act of sending a document to an authority, and the document itself
"The submission was made on the last working day of March."
supporting evidence -- the documents that prove a number in the report is true
"The figure was corrected last week, and the supporting evidence is attached."
discrepancy -- a difference between two numbers that should be the same
"There is a discrepancy of four hundred hectares between table two and table five."
to be granted -- to receive something an authority decided to give you
"The concession was granted in twenty twenty-one."
to be issued -- to be formally produced and put into circulation
"The credits were issued after the audit, in March."
to be audited -- to have your records checked by somebody from outside
"The project is audited every twelve months by an accredited body."
to be governed by -- to be under the rules of a particular law or country
"The agreement is governed by Brazilian law."

Stage 3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about what it says.

Open any regulatory document in English and you will notice something strange. Almost nobody does anything in it. The concession was granted. The report has been submitted. The credits were issued in March. The agreement is governed by Brazilian law. Page after page, the action is there and the author is not.

The structure behind this is the passive: a form of be plus a past participle. The tense lives in be, not in the participle, so the whole system is one small decision repeated: is granted for a rule that holds, was granted for a finished event with a date, has been granted for something done whose consequence is still open, and is being granted for what is happening while you speak.

The reason is not caution and it is not evasion. A report is a record of facts that has to stay true after everybody who wrote it has left the company. The sentence is therefore built around the action, which does not change, rather than around the author, who will. This is also why the findings of an inspection are written about the project and not about a person.

The author comes back when the identity is the point. "The study was verified by an accredited body" says something that "the study was verified" does not. So the test is simple: does the reader need to know who? If the answer is yes and the by is not there, that is a defect in the report, not a question of style.

And when a discrepancy appears, the same shape carries you through the hardest conversation of the job. You can say what was prepared, what is being checked against the supporting evidence, and what will be issued if the error is confirmed — all without naming a colleague before the facts are in, and without sounding as if you were hiding one.

1. According to the text, where does the tense of a passive sentence live?
A In the past participle.
B In the form of be that comes before the participle.
C In the word by, at the end of the sentence.
2. What reason does the text give for building the sentence around the action?
A It is more polite than naming a colleague.
B It makes the document shorter.
C The record has to stay true after the people who wrote it have gone.
3. When the reader needs to know who did the action and the by is missing, what is that?
A A defect in the report.
B A normal feature of formal writing.
C A sign that the sentence should be in the active voice.

Stage 4: Grammar Tip -- the passive voice

GRAMMAR

One structure, four tenses. Change the form of be and the sentence moves in time; the participle never moves.

FormWhen you use itExample
is / are + participlea permanent fact or a standing ruleThe agreement is governed by Brazilian law.
was / were + participlea finished event, usually with a dateThe concession was granted in 2021.
has / have been + participledone, with the consequence still openThe report has been submitted.
is / are being + participlehappening right nowIt is being reviewed by the team.
will be + participlea commitment about the futureA corrected version will be issued on Friday.
The agent, with byonly when the identity matters: "verified by an accredited body". Leave it out when the reader does not need it.
The three usual slipsforgetting be ("the report submitted"); using the past simple instead of the participle ("were issue"); and from instead of by ("verified from an accredited body").

Quick test before you speak: finished with a date, permanent, done with the result still open, or happening now?

Order

?Say what will be issued if the error is confirmed.

?Give the date by which the other side will have it.
?Name the point that is open, without calling it an error yet.
?Say what is being done about it right now.

Stage 8: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Real situations from your week. Only one option is both correct and natural.

1. The verifier asks when the concession came through. It was 2021. What do you say?
A The concession granted in twenty twenty-one.
B The concession was granted in twenty twenty-one.
C The concession has been granted in twenty twenty-one.
D The concession is granted in twenty twenty-one.
2. The team is checking the four hundred hectares at this moment. How do you report that?
A The discrepancy is reviewed by the technical team right now.
B The discrepancy is being reviewed by the technical team.
C The discrepancy has been reviewing by the technical team.
D The discrepancy was being reviewed by the technical team now.
3. You want to say which law the contract falls under. Which sentence is correct?
A The agreement is governing by Brazilian law.
B The agreement is governed from Brazilian law.
C The agreement is governed by Brazilian law.
D The agreement was governed by Brazilian law.
4. The verifier insists on knowing who made the mistake in table five. What is the professional answer?
A I do not know who prepared it, so I cannot help you.
B Table five was prepared by the project company and reviewed here. If the error is confirmed, a corrected version will be issued.
C The junior analyst made the mistake, and we are talking to him about it.
D Somebody made a mistake, but it was not our department.

Stage 9: Free Production

Reflection

Record your answer. This is the spoken draft of the call you will hold in class.

Record two minutes: a verification body has three open points about a report of yours -- a missing annex, a difference of four hundred hectares between two tables, and a study done before the current rules. Answer all three. State each fact with its date, say what is being done now, say what will be issued, and give a deadline for each point. Use four different passive forms, and name the agent only where the reader needs it.
