Lesson 02 -- Pre-class

Reporting What the Contract Says -- Quoting a Clause Without Reading It

Nobody can follow legal drafting by ear, so you never read the clause out loud -- you report it. Key words: party, obligation, deadline, notice, confidentiality, warranty, penalty, renewal, draft, wording, term, counterparty. Structures: reporting verbs for a document in force (states, requires, provides, specifies) and reported speech for what a person said, with the tense one step back -- plus say versus tell.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the definition. These are the moving parts of any agreement -- the goal is to say them out loud.

Party -- one of the companies or people who sign the contract
"Either party may terminate with notice."
Obligation -- something a party must do under the contract
"Our only obligation there is to give notice."
Deadline -- the date by which something has to be done
"The deadline for the notice is the tenth."
Notice -- a formal written message one party sends to the other
"We have to serve notice thirty days before."
Confidentiality -- the duty to keep the information of the deal secret
"Confidentiality survives the end of the contract."
Warranty -- a formal promise that something is true or will work
"The warranty lasts twelve months from delivery."
Penalty -- money a party pays when it breaks a rule of the contract
"The penalty is two percent per month of delay."
Renewal -- continuing the contract for a new period
"Renewal is automatic unless we object."
Draft -- a version of a document that is not final yet
"This is the third draft, and it is still wrong."
Wording -- the exact words chosen in a text
"The wording of that sentence is the whole problem."
Term -- how long the contract lasts
"The initial term is two years."
Counterparty -- the other company in the deal
"The counterparty has not signed yet."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Ana has to summarise a call for a manager who was not there. She does not read the document out loud. She reports it. The new draft states that the term is three years, and it requires ninety days of notice before renewal. Clause twelve also provides that confidentiality survives the end of the agreement. Then she moves to the people. The lawyer for the counterparty said that his client wanted a longer term, and he told her they could accept sixty days if the penalty came down. That second part is not an obligation yet: it is not in the wording. One column is the document, in the present. The other column is a conversation, one step back.

1. Why is "the draft states that the term is three years" in the present tense?
A The document is in force, so it still says that today.
B Because somebody said it during the call.
C Because the term has already finished.
2. The text says the lawyer "said that his client wanted a longer term". Why "wanted" and not "wants"?
A Because the client changed his mind afterwards.
B Because we are reporting what a person said, so the verb moves one step back.
C Because "want" has no present form after "said".
3. The text uses "he told her" and "he said that". What is the difference?
A "Tell" takes a person after it; "say" does not.
B "Tell" is formal and "say" is informal.
C They are interchangeable in every sentence.
4. Why does Ana say the sixty days is "not in the wording"?
A Because the counterparty never mentioned it.
B Because the manager refused it on the call.
C Because somebody said it in a conversation, but the document still requires ninety.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reporting a Document and Reporting a Person

GRAMMAR

Two columns that must never be mixed: what the contract says, and what somebody said.

FormUseExample
states / provides / specifies + thatA document in force. It stays in the present, because it still says it today.Clause five states that the term renews automatically.
requires / allows + object + toThe same idea with an obligation or a permission attached to a party.The agreement requires us to give thirty days notice.
said (that) + verb one step backA person, in a conversation that already happened. is → was · wants → wanted · can → could · will → would.He said the deadline was Friday.
told + person + (that)Tell needs a person straight after it. Say never takes one.She told me they would send a new draft.
QuestionWhat does clause X say? · What did they say?What does the draft state about the term? / What did their lawyer say?
Three trapsNever "he said me" -- it is "he told me". Never leave a person's words in the present ("he said the deadline is Friday") -- that reads like an agreed fact. And never put a contract in force in the past ("clause five said that") -- a living document speaks in the present.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

The manager asks what the renewal clause says. You answer:
A "Notwithstanding anything to the contrary herein, the Term shall automatically renew for successive periods..."
B "Clause five states that the contract renews automatically, unless we give thirty days notice."
C "It is written in the clause five, the renewal."
Three weeks ago the other side told you they can extend the deadline. In your summary today you write:
A "They said they could extend the deadline, if we paid the difference."
B "They said they can extend the deadline."
C "The contract says they can extend the deadline."
You want to report what the counterparty's lawyer communicated to you personally. Which is correct?
A "He said me the draft was ready."
B "He told me the draft was ready."
C "He told that the draft was ready."
The contract is in force today. How do you report clause twelve?
A "Clause twelve provides that confidentiality survives termination."
B "Clause twelve provided that confidentiality survived termination."
C "Clause twelve said me that confidentiality survives."
The manager treats something the other side only said as if it were agreed. You reply:
A "Yes, more or less, I think so."
B "Let me separate that. They said it on a call, with a condition. It is not in the wording yet."
C "No, this is wrong, you did not understand."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

A colleague missed the negotiation call and asks you what happened. Give the summary in two blocks: first what the document says (use states, requires or provides), then what the people said (move the tense one step back, and keep the condition). Finish with what is still open. Take your time.
