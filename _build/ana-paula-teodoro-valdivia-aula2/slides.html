Chapter 3: The Code

It States That, He Said That

Two ways of reporting, one big difference

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Clause five states that the term renews automatically."

"The agreement requires us to give thirty days notice."

"David said that the counterparty wanted a shorter term."

"He told me they could extend the deadline."

Two report a document, two report a person. What happens to the verb?

ReportingRuleExample
A document
(in force)		Stays in the present. The contract still says it today.Clause five states that the term renews.
Reporting verbs
for documents		states · requires · provides · sets out · specifies · allows — put one at the front and the rest is easy.
A person
(said before)		The verb moves one step back: is → was · wants → wanted · can → could · will → would.He said the deadline was Friday.
say vs telltell takes a person: he told me. say does not: he said that — never he said me.

The question that saves you: is this what the document says, or what somebody said?

The Habit

Two Columns, Always

Before you send any summary, split what you are reporting into two.

The document says

present tense · states, requires, provides
"Clause 5 states that the term renews."

Somebody said

one step back · said, told me
"He said they could extend it."

One is binding. The other is a conversation. Your reader cannot tell them apart unless your grammar does it for them.

Chapter 4: The Summary Call

David Was Not There

Tell him what the document says and what they said

Dialogue

What Happened on That Call

D
Ana, I could not join yesterday. Give me the short version — where are we?
A
Two things. First, the document. The new draft states that the term is three years, and it requires ninety days notice before renewal.
D
Ninety days? That is longer than before.
A
It is. And second, the people. Their lawyer said they wanted the longer term, and he told me they could accept sixty days if we moved on the penalty.
D
So sixty days is agreed?
A
No. That is why I am separating the two. The draft still says ninety. Sixty was something they said on a call, with a condition. It is not in the wording yet.
D
Understood. That distinction is exactly what I needed. Send me the two lines in writing and I will take it from here.
Comprehension

About David

1. Why does David need the summary?
He could not join the call the day before.
2. What does he get wrong in the middle of the call?
He assumes sixty days is agreed, when it was only something the other side said, with a condition.
3. What does he ask for at the end?
The two lines in writing, so he can take it from there.

The Real Thing

Find the Problem in This Summary

Internal email
Re: Supply agreement — call summary

Hi all,

Quick summary of yesterday's call:

1. The draft states that the term is three years.
2. It requires ninety days notice before renewal.
3. Their lawyer said they can accept sixty days.
4. So we have until the end of the quarter to serve notice.

Best,
A.

1. Which line is not safe, and why?
Line 3. "Said they can accept" keeps the present tense, so it reads like an agreed fact. It should be "said they could accept" — and the condition they attached is missing.
2. Why is line 4 dangerous?
It draws a conclusion from line 3 as if sixty days were binding. The document still requires ninety.
3. Rewrite line 3 out loud.
"Their lawyer said they could accept sixty days, if we moved on the penalty. This is not in the draft yet."
