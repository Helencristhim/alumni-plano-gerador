Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Survey -- a set of questions that a company sends to learn what customers think
"The quarterly survey goes to every premium customer."
Rating -- a score, for example from one to ten, that shows how good something is
"Brightwave gave us a rating of seven out of ten."
Praise -- words that say that something was done well
"The praise in the survey was for the support team."
Complaint -- saying or writing that you are not happy with something
"One complaint came up three times: the nightly sync."
Drawback -- a disadvantage: a part of something that is not good
"The only drawback is the price of the extra region."
Reliable -- that you can trust to work well every time
"They need a sync that is reliable every single night."
Frustrating -- making you feel annoyed because something does not work or does not change
"It is frustrating when the orders are late in the morning."
Overall -- thinking about everything together; in general
"Overall, the customer is happy with the platform."
Outweigh -- to be bigger or more important than something else
"For them, the benefits still outweigh the problems."
Genuine -- real and honest, not fake or polite only
"Thank you for the genuine feedback. It helps us improve."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Two weeks after the fix, Brightwave answers the quarterly survey. Daniel gives a rating of seven out of ten. Although the dashboards work well, the sync failed three times in August, and that was frustrating for the stores. However, the comment is not only a complaint. Despite the problems, Daniel has praise for Patrícia: even though nobody knew the cause, she called him the same day. His main complaint is that his team found the bug first. The only other drawback is the price of the extra region. In spite of all this, the sync is reliable again, and overall the benefits outweigh the problems. Brightwave is planning to renew, and Patrícia wants to answer this genuine feedback with balance.

1. "Although the dashboards work well, the sync failed." What comes after although?
A only a noun
B a subject and a verb
C an -ing verb only
2. "Despite the problems, Daniel has praise." What comes after despite?
A a noun
B a subject and a verb
C the word of
3. What is Daniel's main complaint?
A The price of the platform.
B That his team found the bug before Patrícia's team.
C That the support team was slow.
4. Which sentence is correct?
A "Despite of the problems, they are renewing."
B "Although the problems, they are renewing."
C "In spite of the problems, they are renewing."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Contrast Linkers: although / however / despite

GRAMMAR

Use these words to put a good idea and a bad idea together. What comes after each one is the important part.

StructureUseExample
although / even though + subject + verbTwo opposite ideas in one sentence.Although the fix was fast, the orders were late.
However, + new sentenceThe opposite idea in a new sentence.The dashboards are clear. However, the sync failed.
despite / in spite of + nounOnly a noun after it.Despite the problems, they renewed.
despite / in spite of + -ingOr an -ing verb.In spite of having problems, they stayed.
despite the fact that + subject + verbA full sentence after despite.Despite the fact that it failed, they renewed.
AvoidNever "despite of": not "despite of the delay" → "despite the delay". Never a noun after although: not "although the delay" → "despite the delay". And never a full sentence after despite: not "despite it failed" → "although it failed".

Order

?Call the customer before you write anything.
?Explain what you have changed since then.
?Thank the customer for the praise and follow up in writing.
?Accept the complaint in one sentence, without excuses.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each real work situation.

A customer writes that the product is good but the price is high. You say:
A "Although the price is high, I am glad the product works for you."
B "Although the high price, the product works."
C "Despite of the price, the product works."
The customer is angry about a delay. The fix was fast. You say:
A "Despite the fix was fast, I am sorry."
B "Despite the fast fix, I know the delay was frustrating."
C "The fix was fast, so there is no problem."
A customer gives you a seven and only praises your support. What is the best first step?
A Thank them and end the call.
B Find the complaint behind the seven and ask about it.
C Ask them to change the rating to a ten.
Which sentence is correct?
A "The report is useful, however it is late."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Imagine that one of your real customers gave you a seven out of ten this quarter, with good and bad comments. Record your answer on the phone in five or six sentences: accept the complaint, say what you have changed, reassure them, and thank them for the praise at the end. Use at least three contrast words -- although, even though, however, despite or in spite of. Take your time.
