Listening 1 · Nordic

Nineteen Years, Never Mistaken for Native

What a colleague from Lisbon said, and why it changed everything. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What did the colleague from Lisbon say to her?
That nobody in the room is grading her grammar — they are waiting to hear what she thinks.
2. In nineteen years, what has nobody ever said to her, and what have they said instead?
Nobody has ever said her English is not good enough. What they have said is: sorry, I did not follow that part.
3. What are her three pieces of advice?
Be concise, stop when the point is made, and when you lose the word, do not stop talking — go around it.
Listening 2 · France

The Three Minutes That Went By

Somebody who did exactly this exercise, and what happened in the second minute. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What went wrong in the first minute?
He was translating in his head, he was long-winded, and he said the same thing three times.
2. What changed in the second minute?
He stopped listening to himself. He was still making mistakes, and it did not matter, because she was following him.
3. Looking back on it, what does he say the exercise was?
The milestone — not a test he passed, but a thing he stopped being afraid of.

Language Review

Which Lesson Was This?

"I have lived here since 2021, and I lived in the city for forty years."

"It is far quieter and nowhere near as convenient."

"I have been restoring it since 2021 and I have finished the kitchen."

"There is a gruff old Italian cheese seller, and the market is fascinating."

"I was sitting in gridlock when I snapped."

"The plaster was removed by hand and the kitchen is being rewired."

Six sentences, six lessons, and the seventh is not a sentence at all — it is what you say when you miss one.

LessonStructureThe one-line test
1Present perfect vs past simpleDoes it still continue, or is it over?
2Comparatives with boostersfar, nowhere near as, by far — never the plain form alone.
3Present perfect continuous vs simpleHow long, or how much is done?
4Asking again, by distanceHow much distance does this person expect?
5Adjective order · -ed and -ingOpinion first · the thing or the person?
6Past simple vs past continuousDid it last, or did it happen once?
7The passiveIs the doer unknown, obvious or unimportant?

In one line: seven questions, and every one of them can be answered in the second before you speak.

Dialogue

So, How Is It Going?

P
We spoke a few weeks ago, and you had just moved. How is it going now?
A
Better than I expected. I have lived here since 2021, and it is far quieter than anywhere I lived before.
P
And the house? You said something about a kitchen.
A
I have been restoring it room by room. The kitchen is finished and it is being rewired at the moment. Sorry — let me backtrack. The kitchen is done. The wiring is not.
P
How long is that going to take, roughly?
A
Off the top of my head, two months. Come to think of it, everybody said two months about the roof as well, and that was a year ago.
P
Your English has changed since we last spoke. You know that, do you not?
A
To sum up eight weeks in one sentence: I still lose words, and now I keep talking. Looking back on it, that is the whole difference.
Comprehension

About Priya

1. When did Priya last speak to Ana, and what had just happened?
A few weeks earlier, and Ana had just moved.
2. What does Priya remember from that conversation?
Something about a kitchen — she brings it up herself.
3. What does Priya notice at the end, and how does she say it?
That Ana’s English has changed since they last spoke, and she asks whether Ana knows it.

Chapter 6: Benchmark

Now the Clock

Benchmark 1 of 3 — Guided

Three Questions, Thirty Seconds Each

Scenario: A new colleague on an international call asks three things: where you live and how long, what you do outside work, and how the house is going. Thirty seconds each, no stopping.

Keyword chips:

since 2021 far quieter is being rewired
Benchmark 2 of 3 — Semi-free

When You Lose the Thread

Scenario: I am going to speak faster than usual and change the subject once without warning. Your job is not to be correct. Your job is to ask again, get back to what you were saying, and finish the point you started.

Keywords:

Sorry, I did not catch that. bear with me to sum up
Benchmark 3 of 3 — Timed

Three Minutes, No Notes

Scenario: Three minutes on your own life, start to finish: where you were, what was going on, the morning you decided, the town and the people in it, and what has been done to the house since. Nobody will interrupt you.

No keywords, no notes, no stopping. When a word disappears, go around it and keep the turn.
