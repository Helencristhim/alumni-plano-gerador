The Real Question

What Happens When the Word Will Not Come?

Everybody loses a word mid-sentence, in every language, several times a day. Fluent speakers lose them too. The difference is what happens in the next second.

In Portuguese you have twenty ways to fill that second. Tonight you get them in English, because the silence is what makes you panic, not the missing word.

Dialogue

He Has Been Stuck Longer Than You

L
Ana, can I ask you something? You have been doing these lessons for a while. Do you still struggle with speaking, or is that over?
A
It is not over. But something has changed. In a nutshell, I stopped going silent when I lose a word.
L
That is exactly my problem. I draw a blank and then there is this terrible silence and everybody waits.
A
So fill it. Say bear with me, or off the top of my head, and keep the floor while you look for it.
L
Does that not sound like you are inventing time?
A
You are inventing time. So is everybody else. I went off on a tangent twice in this conversation and you did not notice.
L
I did not, actually. And your grammar was not perfect either, but I understood every word.
A
That is the whole point. I got my point across. Two years ago I would have said nothing and gone home furious with myself.
Comprehension

About Lars

1. What does Lars describe as his problem?
He draws a blank, and then there is a terrible silence while everybody waits for him.
2. What is Lars worried about when Ana gives him the phrases?
That using them sounds like inventing time — as though it were cheating rather than speaking.
3. What does Lars admit at the end, and why does it matter?
That he did not notice Ana going off on a tangent, and that her grammar was not perfect but he understood every word. It matters because it proves her point: getting the point across is not the same as being correct.
Listening

I Was Fluent Before I Was Correct

An Indian woman on the year she stopped apologising. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What did she believe for years, and what did it cost her?
That she had to be correct before she was allowed to be fluent. She reckons the belief cost her about six years.
2. Why did people think she was quiet?
Because she was preparing and checking every sentence in her head, and by the time it was ready the conversation had moved on. She was not quiet, she was editing.
3. What rule did she make, and what surprised her about the result?
To say the sentence first and repair it afterwards — to backtrack out loud. What surprised her is that her grammar improved too: you cannot correct a sentence you never said.
Chapter 4: The Seven Codes

The Seven Codes

One sentence per lesson — name the form before you look at the table

Grammar Discovery

Which Lesson Does Each One Come From?

"I have lived here for two years, and I moved in the March before that."

"It is far quieter here, and by far the best decision I have made."

"I have been restoring this house since the day I got the keys."

"The ground floor is being rewired, and nothing had been done since 1974."

Four sentences, four different structures, and every one of them came out of a lesson you have already had. Name the form in each before you open the table. If you can name three of the four, this block is done.

FormWhat it doesExample
Lesson 1 — present perfect vs past simpleExperience with no date, against a finished moment with one.I have lived here for two years. I moved in March.
Lesson 2 — comparatives and superlativesTwo things measured, or one thing at the extreme, with the intensifier doing the work.It is far quieter · by far the best decision.
Lesson 3 — present perfect continuousHow long something unfinished has been going on.I have been restoring it since March.
Lesson 4 — asking for clarificationThe register scale, from blunt to careful.Sorry, I did not catch that · Would you mind repeating?
Lesson 5 — adjective order and -ed/-ingOpinion before fact, and the difference between what you feel and what a thing is.A lovely old wooden door · I am exhausted, the walk is exhausting.
Lesson 6 — past simple vs past continuousThe scene that was already running, then the event that landed inside it.I was sitting in traffic when it happened.
Lesson 7 — the passiveThe work first, the worker last or nowhere at all.The floor is being rewired · nothing had been done.

In one line: seven forms, and you now use six of them without deciding to.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I am living here since two years."
"I have lived here for two years."
"It is more quiet than Sao Paulo and the most good decision."
"It is far quieter than Sao Paulo and by far the best decision."
"I was knowing it was the last straw."
"I knew it was the last straw."
"The kitchen is rewiring this week."
"The kitchen is being rewired this week."

One from lesson 1, one from lesson 2, one from lesson 6 and one from lesson 7. These four are the ones that came back most often across the block — and three of them are the same instinct: taking the Portuguese structure and putting English words in it.

Listening 2

The Meeting Where I Winged It

An Italian man on the day he had no notes at all. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What happened at the meeting he describes?
He was asked to present with about ninety seconds of warning — no slides, no notes — so he completely winged it, and it went better than his prepared meetings.
2. What did he notice when he listened back to himself?
That he used about fifteen phrases that were not content at all: let me put it another way, in a nutshell, bear with me, off the top of my head.
3. What is his advice, and what reason does he give for it?
Learn the connective phrases before learning more vocabulary, because you will sound twice as fluent with the same number of words. The prepared version of him sounds better and says less, because it is reading.

RuleNo notes. If a word disappears, use a phrase from tonight and keep talking.
1. Which structure does Part 1 almost force you to use, and why?
Comparatives and superlatives. The moment you compare where you live now with where you lived before, you need far quieter, much smaller, by far the best.
2. Part 3 needs two tenses, not one. Which two, and in what order?
Past continuous for the scene that was already running, then past simple for the event that landed inside it. Scene first, event second — the other order kills the story.
3. Part 4 asks how long. Which form, and what is the trap?
Present perfect continuous: I have been working on it since March. The trap is the Portuguese structure, which gives I am working on it since March.
