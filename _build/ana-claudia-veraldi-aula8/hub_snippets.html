Checkpoint -- Block 1: Everything from Lessons 1 to 7

Key words: a milestone, to digress, long-winded, concise, to backtrack, roughly, to sum up, to look back on something, off the top of my head, come to think of it, bear with me, to hold your own. Structure: review of the seven structures of block one, and what to do when a word disappears.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A milestone -- a point in a long process that marks real progress
"That exercise was the milestone, not a test I passed."
To digress -- to move away from the subject you were talking about
"Sorry, I am digressing. Back to the house."
Long-winded -- using far too many words to say something small
"The first minute I was long-winded and I said the same thing three times."
Concise -- saying a lot in very few words
"Be concise. Stop when the point is made."
To backtrack -- to go back to something you said and correct it
"Let me backtrack. I said 2021, and I meant 2022."
Roughly -- approximately, not exactly -- the number you give when you have not checked
"Roughly four years, maybe a little more."
To sum up -- to say the whole thing again in one short sentence at the end
"To sum up: I left, I do not regret it, and the roof is still not done."
To look back on something -- to think about something that happened long ago and judge it now
"Looking back on it, that exercise was the milestone."
Off the top of my head -- without checking anything, from memory, right now
"Off the top of my head, roughly four years."
Come to think of it -- used when something occurs to you in the middle of speaking
"Come to think of it, that was the first time I forgot it was English."
Bear with me -- asking somebody to wait a moment while you find the words
"Bear with me, I am looking for the word in English."
To hold your own -- to manage well in a hard conversation with people who are better than you
"I do not sound native, and I hold my own in any meeting."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Ana has lived in the countryside since 2021, and before that she lived in the city for forty years. It is far quieter here and nowhere near as convenient. She has been restoring the house since she arrived, and she has finished the kitchen and both bedrooms.

The town has a gruff old Italian cheese seller and a Saturday market that is fascinating. She was sitting in gridlock one Tuesday when she snapped and decided to leave the city for good. The plaster in the new house was removed by hand, and the kitchen is being rewired at the moment.

Seven structures, eight weeks. Looking back on it, she says the real change is smaller and stranger: she still loses words, and now she keeps talking. To sum up, in her own words, off the top of my head: concise is better than perfect, and bear with me buys more time than silence ever did.

1. "She has lived here since 2021, and before that she lived in the city for forty years." Why the two different tenses?
A Because the first one still continues and the second one is completely over.
B Because forty years is longer than four years.
C Because the city is a bigger place than the countryside.
2. "She was sitting in gridlock when she snapped." Which part is the background?
A The snapping, because it is the important one.
B The sitting, because it was already going on when the other thing cut it.
C Neither — both happened at exactly the same second.
3. "The plaster was removed by hand, and the kitchen is being rewired." Why does neither sentence name a person?
A Because the writer forgot to say who did it.
B Because the passive is not allowed to have a person in it.
C Because the work is what matters, and the doer is unknown or unimportant.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Block One on One Page

GRAMMAR

The seven structures, and the one question that decides each of them.

LessonStructureThe one-line test
1Present perfect vs past simpleDoes it still continue, or is it over?
2Comparatives with boostersfar, nowhere near as, by far
3Present perfect continuous vs simpleHow long, or how much is done?
4Asking again, by distanceHow much distance does this person expect?
5Adjective order · -ed and -ingOpinion first · the thing or the person?
6Past simple vs past continuousDid it last, or did it happen once?
7The passiveIs the doer unknown, obvious or unimportant?

Never: I live here since 2021 · more busy · How long are you restoring it? · a seller cheese Italian old · I sat in gridlock when I snapped · The kitchen is rewired at the moment.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

Somebody asks for a number you have not checked. You answer:
A "I do not know exactly, sorry, I cannot say."
B "Off the top of my head, roughly two months."
C "I will look back on it and tell you."
You have just said something that is not quite right, and you want to fix it without stopping:
A "Sorry, let me backtrack. The kitchen is done, the wiring is not."
B "Sorry, I am digressing. The kitchen is done."
C "To sum up, the kitchen is done."
The word has disappeared and you need two seconds. You say:
A Nothing, and you wait in silence.
B "Bear with me, I am looking for the word in English."
C "Sorry, my English is very bad."
Somebody says you must sound like a native speaker to be taken seriously. You answer:
A "You are right, and that is why I am long-winded."
B "I do not sound native, and I hold my own in any meeting."
C "I have been sounding native since 2021."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

This is the benchmark. Three minutes on your own life, start to finish, no notes and no stopping: where you were, what was going on, the morning you decided, the town and the people in it, and what has been done to the house since. When a word disappears, go around it and keep going.

Two and a half billion people speak English, and two billion of them learned it in a classroom. The whole talk is about which of those Englishes is actually the standard, and why the answer is not the one you were taught.
