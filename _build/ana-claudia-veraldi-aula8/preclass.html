Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Off the top of my head -- from memory, without checking, and not exactly
"Off the top of my head, I would say about eleven years."
To draw a blank -- to reach for something in your memory and find nothing there
"I asked her for the name and she completely drew a blank."
Bear with me -- a polite way of asking somebody to wait while you find the words
"Bear with me, I am trying to remember the English for this."
To put it another way -- to say the same thing again in different words, because the first way failed
"To put it another way, the house is older than the town."
To get your point across -- to make somebody understand what you actually mean
"My grammar was terrible and I still got my point across."
To go off on a tangent -- to start talking about something unrelated and never come back
"Sorry, I went off on a tangent. Where was I?"
In a nutshell -- the whole thing said in very few words
"In a nutshell, I left the city and I have not looked back."
It slipped my mind -- i forgot it completely, and I did not mean to
"I was going to call the builder and it completely slipped my mind."
To be at a loss for words -- to have nothing to say, because of surprise or emotion rather than language
"When they handed me the keys I was at a loss for words."
To struggle with something -- to find something hard and keep going at it anyway
"I still struggle with conditionals, and I use them every day."
To backtrack -- to go back and correct something you have just said
"Let me backtrack. I said Tuesday, and it was actually Wednesday."
To wing it -- to do something with no preparation at all and hope it works
"I had no notes for that meeting. I completely winged it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Ana has lived in the interior for two years, and she moved there in March. The town is far quieter than Sao Paulo and, in her words, by far the best decision she has made. She has been restoring the same house since the day she got the keys.

On the Tuesday it happened, she was sitting in traffic and nothing was moving, and then something snapped. Today the ground floor of the house is being rewired, and nothing had been done to the place since 1974.

What she notices is not the grammar. It is that she no longer stops. When a word disappears she says bear with me, or gives an approximate figure off the top of her head, or puts it another way. She still struggles with conditionals. She gets her point across anyway, which two years ago she did not.

1. Why "has lived" in the first sentence and "moved" in the second?
A Because the two years are unfinished, and March is a finished moment with a date.
B Because the present perfect is more formal than the past simple.
C Because English never uses the past simple with places.
2. "She has been restoring the same house since the day she got the keys." What does this form add that "she has restored" would not?
A That the restoring is finished and the house is ready.
B That it is still going on, and the focus is on how long.
C That she restored it once and then stopped.
3. What does the third paragraph say has actually changed for her?
A Her grammar became perfect.
B She stopped going silent when a word disappears, and gets her point across anyway.
C She no longer finds any part of English difficult.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Seven Structures of Block 1

GRAMMAR

Nothing new here. One line per lesson, so you can see the whole block at once.

FormWhat it doesExample
Lesson 1Present perfect (no date) vs past simple (with one)I have lived here for two years. I moved in March.
Lesson 2Comparatives and superlatives, with intensifiersIt is far quieter · by far the best decision.
Lesson 3Present perfect continuous: how long, still goingI have been restoring it since March.
Lesson 4Asking again, on a scale from blunt to carefulSorry, what? · Would you mind repeating that?
Lesson 5Adjective order, and -ed vs -ing adjectivesA lovely old wooden door · I am exhausted.
Lesson 6Past continuous for the scene, past simple for the eventI was sitting in traffic when it happened.
Lesson 7The passive: the work first, the worker last or nowhereThe floor is being rewired · nothing had been done.

Never: I am living here since two years · the most good decision · I was knowing · the kitchen is rewiring. All four take a Portuguese structure and put English words inside it.

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

Lars asks Ana whether speaking is still hard. Put the exchange in a logical order.

?"Do you still struggle with speaking, or is that over?"
?"It is not over. But I stopped going silent when I lose a word."
?"That is exactly my problem. There is this terrible silence and everybody waits."
?"So fill it. Say bear with me, and keep talking while you look for the word."
?"Does that not sound like you are inventing time?"
?"You are inventing time. So is everybody else, in every language."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

You are asked for a number you do not know exactly. The natural answer is:
A "Off the top of my head, about eleven years."
B "I am sorry, I cannot answer that question."
C "In my head from the top, eleven years more or less."
The word you need has disappeared mid-sentence. You:
A Stop talking and wait until it comes back.
B Say "bear with me a second" and keep the floor while you look for it.
C Apologise for your English and change the subject.
You have just said something that came out wrong. The natural repair is:
A "Sorry, my English is very bad, forget it."
B "Let me put it another way."
C "I am at a loss for words about this."
A colleague asks how long you have been restoring the house. You answer:
A "I have been restoring it since March."
B "I am restoring it since March."
C "I restore it since March."
