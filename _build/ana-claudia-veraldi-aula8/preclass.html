Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

Checkpoint -- Block 1: The Story So Far

A checkpoint on Lessons 1 to 7: telling the story of a life and choosing the right form for each part of it. Key words: to settle in, a fresh start, to end up, to take stock, to stick with, steady, to unwind, to get by, sense of purpose, to look forward to, worn out, on the whole. Structure: a review of the Block 1 forms -- present simple and continuous, past simple and past continuous, comparatives, present perfect, present perfect continuous and the passive voice -- and how to choose between them.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To settle in -- to slowly start to feel at home in a new place
"It took her almost two years to settle in."
A fresh start -- a chance to begin again, leaving the old life behind
"Everyone talks about the fresh start."
To end up -- to reach a place or a situation you did not plan
"She ended up in a town of eleven thousand people."
To take stock -- to stop and think carefully about where you are now
"I take stock every January."
To stick with -- to continue with something even when it gets hard or slow
"I started badly, and I stuck with it."
Steady -- regular and unhurried, without sudden changes
"Progress is slow but steady: one room a year."
To unwind -- to let go of stress and relax after a busy day
"In the evening I unwind on the porch with the dogs."
To get by -- to manage with the little money or help you have
"She gets by on far less money than she earned in the city."
Sense of purpose -- the feeling that what you do every day matters
"The house gave her a sense of purpose."
To look forward to -- to feel happy about something that is coming
"I look forward to the quiet part of the evening."
Worn out -- very tired, or old and damaged by long use
"I was worn out, and I was working every weekend."
On the whole -- when you consider everything together
"On the whole, it was the best decision I ever made."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Marina grew up in a crowded apartment in Sao Paulo. She left the city in 2015 and ended up in a small town, and while her friends were saying she would be bored, she was already packing. The town is much quieter than the city and life there is far cheaper, but it took her almost two years to settle in. She has been working on an old farmhouse for nine years now, and she has never hired a designer. The roof was replaced in the first winter, the wiring was redone by an electrician, and right now the kitchen is being rebuilt. Notice how each form has one job: left gives the finished moment, has been working gives the length of time, has never hired gives the life experience with no date, and was replaced puts the thing first because who did the work does not matter. She gets by on far less money than before, and on the whole she says it was the best decision she has ever made.

1. Why does the text say "she has been working on an old farmhouse for nine years" instead of "she worked on it"?
A Because the work finished nine years ago.
B Because the sentence gives the length of time and the work is still going on.
C Because the present perfect continuous is the polite form.
2. Why does "she has never hired a designer" use the present perfect and not the past simple?
A Because it happened yesterday.
B Because the past simple cannot be used with "never".
C Because it is a life experience up to now, with no time given.
3. What is the difference between "was replaced" and "is being rebuilt" in the text?
A "Was replaced" is finished; "is being rebuilt" is happening right now.
B "Was replaced" is formal and "is being rebuilt" is informal.
C There is no difference; both describe finished work.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Choosing the Right Form

GRAMMAR

Seven forms from Lessons 1 to 7, and the one job each of them does.

FormIts one jobExample
Present simple / continuousA fact or a routine that is true now, or something in progress at this moment.She gets by on very little. / I am fixing a chair.
Past simpleA finished moment, usually with a time expression.She left the city in 2015.
Past continuousThe background action around that finished moment.While they were saying that, she was packing.
Comparative / superlativeTwo things side by side, or one above all the others.The town is much quieter than the city.
Present perfectA life experience up to now. No time is given.She has never hired a designer.
Present perfect continuousHow long something has been going on, and it still is. Takes for or since.She has been working on it for nine years.
Passive: be + past participleThe thing matters more than the person who did it.The roof was replaced. / The kitchen is being rebuilt.
In one lineDecide what the sentence is FOR, then pick the form. When it happened, how long it has lasted, or what was done to the thing.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each real conversation.

Someone asks how long you have been restoring furniture. You answer:
A "I have been doing it for about six years."
B "I am doing it since about six years."
C "I do it for about six years."
A visitor asks who repaired the roof. It does not matter who did it. You say:
A "The roof replaced last winter."
B "The roof was replaced last winter."
C "The roof is replacing last winter."
You want to compare your town with the capital. You say:
A "My town is more quiet than Sao Paulo."
B "My town is quieter that Sao Paulo."
C "My town is much quieter than Sao Paulo."
A friend asks if you have ever hired a designer. You never have. You say:
A "No, I have never hired one."
B "No, I never hire one since years."
C "No, I am never hiring one."
