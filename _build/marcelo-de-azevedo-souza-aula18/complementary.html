Lesson 18 -- The Journey Home

Lesson theme: coming home from a long trip and getting things done (the causative have / get something done). Extra practice outside class -- tick each one when you finish it.

Film
Nomadland (2020) -- directed by Chloé Zhao

A woman loses her town and her job and starts living in a van, moving between temporary work and open road. Half the film is exactly tonight's language: she gets the van looked at, asks what the repair will cost, has parts replaced, and keeps asking herself what home even means. Slow, quiet dialogue at a comfortable pace, and a perfect companion to Lesson 18: the journey and the coming back are the same story.

Tip: after the garage scene, say out loud three things she had done to the van, using "she had ..." plus a past participle.

Podcast
99% Invisible -- Episode 513: The Safety Bicycle

The story of how the bicycle became the machine you ride today: pedals, the penny farthing, the chain drive, the diamond frame, and finally the e-bike. Free to stream, with a full transcript on the page, and about twenty-five minutes of natural American narration at real speed -- the listening practice you asked for. The object at the center of tonight's lesson, told properly.

Tip: read the transcript after the second listen, then describe three things you have had done to your own bike over the years.

How to use "have something done" -- Learners' Questions (BBC Learning English)

A short, reliable BBC explanation of exactly tonight's grammar: why "I had my bike serviced" is not the same as "I serviced my bike", and how the object slots in before the past participle. It turns your Grammar Tip into three or four clear examples you can repeat out loud.

Tip: pause after every example and rebuild it with something of yours -- the bike, the car, the house.
