Lesson 18 -- Pre-class

The Journey Home -- Getting Things Done After the Trip

Come home from a long trip and say what somebody else did for you: the causative have / get something done. New vocabulary: wear and tear, to overhaul, a tune-up, roadworthy, a quote, to drop something off, a backlog, to be swamped, to run errands, reverse culture shock, to readjust, to recharge your batteries.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Wear and tear -- the damage that normal use causes over time
"Three months of wear and tear is hard on a bike."
To overhaul -- to take apart and repair every part of something
"I had the whole bike overhauled."
A tune-up -- a small service that makes a machine run well again
"The bike needs more than a tune-up."
Roadworthy -- safe enough to be used on the road
"The car is finally roadworthy again."
A quote -- the price given before a job starts
"I asked the workshop for a quote."
To drop something off -- to leave something for someone to work on
"I dropped the bike off at the workshop."
A backlog -- work that has built up while you were away
"I came back to a backlog of four hundred emails."
To be swamped -- to have far too much to deal with
"I was swamped before I had even unpacked."
To run errands -- to do small practical jobs around town
"I spent Saturday running errands."
Reverse culture shock -- the strange feeling of returning to your own country
"Nobody warns you about reverse culture shock."
To readjust -- to get used to normal life again
"It took me a month to readjust."
To recharge your batteries -- to rest until you feel strong again
"First I need to recharge my batteries."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Kaia is back from Patagonia. "I had the van towed on Wednesday," she says, "because it would not start after six weeks in a cold garage." She dropped her camera off at a shop in town, and the quote they gave her was almost the price of a new one. The wear and tear on her gear is always worse than the wear and tear on her. The office was harder: a backlog of two hundred emails, and she was swamped for two weeks, so she got her brother to answer the easy ones. Notice the shape. When you name the THING, it comes first and the verb becomes a past participle: had the van towed, got the mail sorted. When you name the PERSON, you say had the mechanic look, or got her brother to answer.

1. Who towed the van?
A Somebody else did it for Kaia.
B Kaia towed it herself.
C Nobody. The van started fine.
2. Which sentence means a shop repaired the camera?
A "I had repaired my camera."
B "I had my camera repaired."
C "I had my camera repair."
3. After "get" plus a person, what comes next?
A "to" plus the base verb: got her brother to answer.
B The past participle: got her brother answered.
C Nothing at all: got her brother answer.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Causative

GRAMMAR

Use these structures when somebody else does the work for you.

You name...PatternExample
the thinghave + object + past participleI had my bike serviced.
the thing (informal)get + object + past participleI got the brakes checked.
the personhave + person + base formI had the mechanic look at it.
the person (informal)get + person + to + base formI got my sister to drive me.
bad newshave + object + past participleI had my wallet stolen.

Key point: the object always comes before the past participle. "I had my bike serviced" means a mechanic did the work. "I had serviced my bike" means you did it yourself, earlier. And after get plus a person you always need to: "I got my sister to drive me", never "I got my sister drive me".
