Chapter 3: The Code

Shorter, or More Flexible?

English counts the syllables before it compares

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Their bonus is bigger than ours."

"Our turnover is lower than the market."

"We are more flexible than most retail companies."

"Their career path is less clear than ours."

Two adjectives took -er. Two needed the word more or less. Count the syllables and you have the rule.

FormUseExample
short adjective + -erOne syllable, or two ending in -y (y becomes i).big → bigger · low → lower · easy → easier
more / less + long adjectiveTwo syllables or more: never add -er.more flexible · less expensive · more competitive
always with thanThe comparison needs than, never that.Our turnover is lower than the market.
irregular: good / bad / farThese three do not follow any rule.good → better · bad → worse · far → further
as ... asWhen the two things are equal.Our salary is as competitive as theirs.

The Structure

The Honest Comparison

This is what you say when a candidate asks why they should choose you and not the competitor.

1 · Where you are better

"We are more flexible than most retail companies, and our career path is clearer."

2 · The number

"Our store turnover is eleven points lower than the market average."

3 · Where you are worse

"Their bonus is bigger than ours. That is true, and I will not pretend it is not."

Better → a number → worse. The third sentence is the one that makes the first two believable.

Why You and Not Them?

N
Viviane, right? We met at the panel. I run HR for the group across the street.
V
Hello, Nick. I know your company. Your bonus is bigger than ours.
N
It is. And still, three of my store managers went to you last year. Why?
V
Because we are more flexible than you are. And our career path is clearer — we published it.
N
Is your turnover really lower, or is that a marketing number?
V
Sorry, could you repeat the question?
N
Your turnover. Is it really lower than the sector, or is it a nice number for the report?
V
It is real. Eleven points lower. But you pay better than we do, and I am not going to pretend that is not true.
N
That is the most honest answer I have heard today. Send me that benchmark, I will send you mine.
Comprehension

About Nick

1. How does Nick know Viviane?
They met at a panel at the conference — he runs HR for the group across the street.
2. What does he want to understand?
Why three of his store managers left his company and went to hers.
3. What does he propose at the end?
To exchange benchmarks — she sends hers, he sends his.
Listening

Two Offers, One Decision

A candidate explains why he chose the smaller company. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. How much higher was the salary in the bigger company?
About fifteen per cent higher.
2. What did the manager of the smaller company ask him?
What he was bad at — and he says nobody asks that.
3. Why was the career path better?
They showed him a real document with real steps; the bigger company only said we will see in a year.
4. What does he say about money at the end?
Money is easier to compare than everything else, so people compare it and forget the rest.
Listening 2

The Question You Will Always Get

A recruiter on how to answer why you and not them. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. When does this question come up?
In every process, on both sides of the table — hiring and being hired.
2. Why is the perfect answer the worst answer?
Because no company is better at everything, so nobody believes it.
3. What are the three parts of the answer that works?
One thing where you are clearly better (with a number), one where you are honestly worse, and a sentence that ties them together.
4. What does she say about the last sentence?
It sounds like you are losing the argument, but it makes you the only person in the room who sounds true.
