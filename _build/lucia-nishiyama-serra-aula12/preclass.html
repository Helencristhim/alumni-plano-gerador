Lesson 12 -- Pre-class

Present Perfect Continuous -- Reporting the Work That Is Still Running

Showing effort, duration and work in progress the way a senior leader reports it. Key words: to ramp up, workload, to pilot, adoption, to iterate, headcount, to keep up with, sustained. Structure: present perfect continuous (have or has + been + -ing) with for and since, and when the present perfect simple is the right choice instead.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To ramp up -- to increase something gradually, step by step
"The platform team has been ramping up capacity for two quarters."
Workload -- the total amount of work one person or one team has to do
"The workload has been growing faster than headcount."
To pilot -- to test something with a small group before using it everywhere
"We have been piloting the new review process since March."
Adoption -- how widely and how fast people start using a new tool or process
"Adoption has been climbing every week since the launch."
To iterate -- to improve something by repeating a cycle of small changes
"We have been iterating on the same design for three months."
Headcount -- the number of people employed on a team
"We have been growing the workload, but not the headcount."
To keep up with -- to move at the same speed as something that keeps changing
"I have been keeping up with the safety literature since I moved into this role."
Sustained -- continuing at the same strong level over a long period
"This is the first quarter of sustained growth in two years."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Lucia is writing the status page a board member will read before the meeting. She could list the projects, but a list has no clock in it. So she writes: "We have been piloting the new review process since March." Started, still running, and the reader knows how long. The next line adds the trend: "Adoption has been climbing every week since the launch." Then the pressure: "The platform group has been ramping up capacity for two quarters, because the workload has been growing faster than headcount." Two small words carry the whole clock -- for before a length of time, since before a starting point. Then she needs the score, not the story, and the form changes: "We have piloted four teams so far." Finally, a state verb, where the continuous is simply not allowed: "I have known this engineering group for twenty years."

1. Why does Lucia write "We have been piloting the new review process since March" instead of "We piloted the new review process"?
A Because the pilot started in the past and is still running now.
B Because the pilot finished last month.
C Because the sentence would be too short otherwise.
2. In "We have piloted four teams so far", why is the simple form used instead of the continuous?
A Because the sentence has the word "so far" in it.
B Because she is giving a number -- the result, not the duration.
C Because the continuous form does not exist with the verb "pilot".
3. In "I have known this engineering group for twenty years", why is there no continuous form?
A Because twenty years is too long a period for the continuous.
B Because "for" can never appear with the continuous form.
C Because "know" describes a state, and state verbs refuse the continuous.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect Continuous

GRAMMAR

How to say that the work started, is still running, and has lasted a measurable time.

FormUseExample
Affirmativehave / has + been + verb-ing.We have been piloting the process since March.
Negativehave not / has not + been + verb-ing.Headcount has not been growing with the workload.
QuestionHow long + have / has + subject + been + verb-ing?How long have you been running this program?
forA length of time -- the answer to "how long?".The team has been ramping up capacity for two quarters.
sinceA starting point: a date, a year, or a whole clause.Adoption has been climbing since the launch.
Simple, not continuousUse the present perfect simple for the number: "We have piloted four teams." And never use the continuous with state verbs: "I have known this group for twenty years" (never "I have been knowing").
