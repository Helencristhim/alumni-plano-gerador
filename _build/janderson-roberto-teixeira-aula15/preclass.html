Lesson 15 -- Pre-class

Could You Tell Me Where -- Asking Politely

How to ask a stranger for information so that the answer comes with a smile, at a hotel, an airport or a client's office. Key expressions: a front desk, to wonder, directions, to find out, a badge, to sign in, to mind, down the hall, out of order, complimentary. Structure: indirect questions.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. These ten stay here in your material -- they do not disappear after the class.

A front desk -- the place where you arrive and ask for help in a hotel or building
"The front desk is open twenty-four hours."
To wonder -- to want to know something
"I wonder if they are expecting me."
Directions -- instructions that tell you how to get to a place
"Could you give me directions to the bank?"
To find out -- to get information you did not have
"I need to find out what time the office opens."
A badge -- a small card with your name that lets you into a building
"Please wear your badge on the twelfth floor."
To sign in -- to write your name when you arrive at a building
"Visitors need to sign in at the front desk."
To mind -- to feel bothered or unhappy about something
"Do you mind if I wait here?"
Down the hall -- along the corridor, a short walk from here
"The meeting room is down the hall, on the right."
Out of order -- not working, usually a machine
"The elevator on the right is out of order."
Complimentary -- free, offered by a hotel or company
"There is complimentary coffee on every floor."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Janderson lands in Miami on a Tuesday night for his first visit to a client's family office. The client is a Brazilian family that has invested abroad with the bank for years, but he has never met them in person. The meeting is at ten the next morning, in a tall building in Brickell that he has only seen on a map.

At the hotel he goes to the front desk and asks two questions. He does not say what time is breakfast. He says, could you tell me what time breakfast starts?, and then, do you know if there is a taxi rank nearby? The receptionist answers both with a smile and gives him directions for the morning.

At a quarter to ten he walks into the lobby of the building. He wonders for a second where to go, then asks the woman at the desk which floor the office is on. She asks him to sign in, gives him a badge, and tells him the elevators are down the hall — but not the one on the right, which is out of order.

One detail in all these questions is worth noticing. After could you tell me or do you know, the words go in the order of a normal sentence: what time breakfast starts, not what time does breakfast start. The opening asks the question; the rest of the sentence simply states.

1. "Could you tell me what time breakfast starts?" Why is there no does in this question?
A Because it is a question about the morning, and those never need does.
B Because after the polite opening the words follow the order of a normal sentence.
C Because breakfast is a noun.
2. Why is Janderson in Miami?
A For his first in-person meeting with a client's family office.
B For a holiday with his family that he planned months ago.
C To open a new office for the bank.
3. Which question is correct?
A Do you know where is the elevator?
B Do you know where does the elevator go?
C Do you know where the elevator is?
4. What does the woman at the desk tell him about the elevators?
A That all the elevators in the building are working normally this morning.
B That they are down the hall, but the one on the right is out of order.
C That he should take the stairs.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Indirect Questions

GRAMMAR

A polite question is a normal question with an opening in front of it. The opening asks; the rest of the sentence keeps the order of a statement.

FormWhat it saysExample
opening + question word + subject + verbAfter could you tell me or do you know, the verb goes after the subject."Could you tell me where the elevators are?"
no do, does or didThe helper verb disappears. The main verb keeps its -s or its past form."Do you know what time the office opens?"
if for yes/no questionsWhen there is no question word, use if."Do you know if the flight is on time?"
I was wondering ifThe softest opening. It ends with a full stop, not a question mark."I was wondering if you could help me."
Direct and politeSame information, different temperature."Where is the bank?" (direct)
"Could you tell me where the bank is?" (polite)
The most common mistakeKeeping the question order after the opening."Could you tell me where the bank is?"
NOT "where is the bank"

There is a professional use for this beyond airports. When a foreign client visits the bank, could you tell me and I was wondering if are also the safest way to ask a client for information they may not want to give, because the question arrives softly.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are traveling for work and you need information from people you do not know. Choose the best thing to say.

At the hotel, you want the Wi-Fi password:
A Could you tell me what is the Wi-Fi password for the rooms on this floor?
B Could you tell me what the Wi-Fi password is?
C Tell me the Wi-Fi password.
At the airport, you are not sure your flight is on time:
A Do you know if my flight is on time?
B Do you know is my flight on time or is it delayed this afternoon?
C You know if my flight on time?
At a client's office, you want to wait somewhere:
A I was wondering do I can wait here until she finishes her meeting.
B Where I wait?
C I was wondering if I could wait here.
You want to know when the bank opens:
A Do you know what time does the bank open in the morning on weekdays?
B Do you know what time the bank opens?
C Do you know what time opens the bank?
The elevator is not working:
A The elevator is out of order.
B The elevator is in a bad order today, so please use the other one.
C The elevator is without order.
