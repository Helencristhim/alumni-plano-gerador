Chapter 4: The Code

Four Ways to Ask

Could I · Could you · I would like · Would it be possible to

Grammar Discovery

Who Does the Action?

"Could I check in a little later? My ferry is delayed."

"Could you check the name again, please?"

"I would like a room with a view, if one is available."

"Would it be possible to move the reservation to Thursday?"

In two of them you do the action. In one, the other person does it. And one of the four is far more careful than the rest — which one?

FormWhen you use itExample
Could I ...?you ask permission for something you doCould I have the receipt?
Could you ...?you ask the other person to do somethingCould you check the name?
I would like ...you say what you want, politely, with no questionI would like a table for two.
Would it be possible to ...?the most careful of the four — for a bigger favorWould it be possible to change the date?
The shapeAfter could, the bare verb: could I have, never could I to have. After would like and would it be possible, always to + verb: I would like to try, never I would like try.
And when you are not sureUse might: there might be a delay · I might need a taxi later. It is the honest way to say something you cannot promise.

All four say the same thing. They only change how much room you leave the other person to say no.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"Could you to check the name again?"
"Could you check the name again?"
"I would like try the local fish."
"I would like to try the local fish."
"Do you could give me the receipt?"
"Could you give me the receipt?"

One rule for the two families: could takes the bare verb and needs no do · would like and would it be possible always take to.

Listening

Ten Words, Not a Hundred

The woman on the night shift explains what actually works. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What happens at her front desk at around eleven at night?
The ferry passengers start arriving, all of them tired, and half of them with a reservation she cannot find on the first try.
2. Which guest gets a room faster, and why?
The guest who says "could you check the name again, please" — not because she likes him more, but because she can actually listen.
3. When the room really is gone, who does she offer the alternative to?
To the people who ask her a question, not to the people who bring her a complaint.
Listening 2

Three Things I Do Before Every Trip

The guest who nearly lost his room in Split. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What three things went wrong on his night in Split?
The ferry was two hours late, the evening shift had already gone home, and the man on duty said there was no reservation under his name.
2. What did he ask, and how long did the answer take?
He asked "could you check that number for me, please", and it took the man forty seconds. The reservation was there, spelled with a different letter.
3. What are the three things he now does before every trip?
He saves the receipt as a photograph, he writes the booking number on paper as well, and he learns how to say "would it be possible" in the local language.
