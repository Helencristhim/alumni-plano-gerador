Making It Happen -- Asking For What You Need

Reservations, requests and solving a problem at a front desk or a table. Key words: reservation, available, front desk, check-in, delay, receipt, complaint, alternative. Structure: could I, could you, I would like and would it be possible to for polite requests.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Reservation -- a room or a table kept in your name because you booked it before
"He could not find my reservation anywhere on the screen."
Available -- free to be used or taken at that moment
"I would like a room with a view, if one is available."
Front desk -- the counter in a hotel where you arrive and ask for help
"The man at the front desk was polite and very tired."
Check-in -- the moment you arrive and the hotel gives you your room
"My advice for a late check-in is short."
Delay -- extra waiting time, when something happens later than planned
"A delay is not the same thing as a disaster."
Receipt -- the paper or the file that proves you paid, with the amount on it
"I had the receipt on my phone, with the number on it."
Complaint -- what you say when something is wrong and you want somebody to fix it
"A complaint tells somebody that they have a problem."
Alternative -- a second option, for when the first one is not possible
"He had an alternative on the top floor, smaller and much quieter."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The ferry was two hours late, so I reached the front desk close to midnight. The man on duty could not find my reservation on the screen. There are two ways to spend the next five minutes, and that night I chose the quiet one. I said: "I am afraid there might be a problem with my booking. Could you check the number instead of the name?" I had the receipt on my phone.

It took him forty seconds. The reservation was there, spelled with one different letter, but my room was no longer available. So I asked one more thing: "Would it be possible to put me anywhere with a window?" He had an alternative on the top floor. Before I left the desk I said what I actually wanted for the morning: "I would like to leave at six. Could I have the receipt tonight?" Both answers were yes. A complaint would have taken longer, and a delay is not the same thing as a disaster.

1. In the text, the writer says "Could you check the number" and later "Could I have the receipt". What is the difference between the two?
A With "Could you" the other person does the action. With "Could I" the writer does it.
B "Could you" is polite and "Could I" is a direct order.
C "Could I" is only used in the past, and "Could you" only in the present.
2. Look at "Would it be possible to put me anywhere with a window?" and "I would like to leave at six". Why is there a "to" in both?
A Because both sentences are questions, and questions always need "to".
B Because "would like" and "would it be possible" are always followed by "to" + verb.
C Because the verb after them is in the past, and "to" marks the past.
3. The writer says "there might be a problem" instead of "there is a problem". Why?
A Because "might be" and "is" mean exactly the same thing in English.
B Because the problem happened in the past and "is" would be wrong.
C Because he is not certain whose mistake it is, and "might" leaves that open.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Four Ways to Ask

GRAMMAR

All four ask for the same thing. They change how much room you leave the other person to say no.

FormWhen you use itExample
Could I ...?you ask permission for something you doCould I have the receipt?
Could you ...?you ask the other person to do somethingCould you check the name?
I would like ...you say what you want, politely, with no questionI would like a table for two.
Would it be possible to ...?the most careful of the four, for a bigger favorWould it be possible to change the date?
The shape after themAfter could, the bare verb: could I have, never "could I to have". After would like and would it be possible, always to + verb: I would like to try, never "I would like try".
Question formNever add do: Could you give me the receipt? — not "Do you could give me the receipt?"
When you are not sureUse might: there might be a delay · I might need a taxi later. It is the honest way to say something you cannot promise.

A useful pair at a desk: "I would like a room with a view" states what you want, while "Would it be possible to move to a room with a view?" asks somebody to do work for you. The second one earns more help.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are travelling and something is not right. Choose the best thing to say.

It is almost midnight and the front desk cannot find your name. You want a room, not an argument. You say:
A "This is unacceptable. I paid in June and I want my room now."
B "I am afraid there might be a problem. Could you check the booking number?"
C "Do you could look for my reservation again, please?"
You want the waiter to bring the bill. The natural, polite question is:
A "Could you to bring the bill, please?"
B "Could you bring the bill, please?"
C "Would you possible bring the bill, please?"
You want to order the local fish, but you are not choosing for the table yet. You say:
A "I would like to try the local fish."
B "I would like try the local fish."
C "I would like that I try the local fish."
You need the hotel to change your booking from Wednesday to Thursday. That is a real favor, so you ask:
A "Would it be possible change the reservation to Thursday?"
B "Is possible to change the reservation to Thursday?"
C "Would it be possible to change the reservation to Thursday?"

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think of one real thing that went wrong on one of your trips: a reservation, a delay, a bill, a room. Take about ninety seconds. Set the scene, say what the problem was, say exactly what you asked for at the time, and say what you would ask for today instead. Use Could I, Could you, I would like and Would it be possible to at least once each, and four words from this lesson. Do not stop to correct yourself.
