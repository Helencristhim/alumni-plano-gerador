Chapter 3 -- The Probability Code

May · Might · Could · Is Likely To

How certain do you actually sound?

Discovery

Listen and Notice

Put these in order, from the most probable to the least. Which form of the verb follows each one?

"The property files are likely to delay the closing by two weeks."

"The regulator may ask for the full audit trail."

"The seller might refuse to extend the notice period."

"A hidden liability could cost us the whole transaction."

FormHow probableExample
be likely to + base verbhigh, with numbers behind it"The files are likely to delay the closing."
may + base verba real possibility"The regulator may ask for the audit trail."
might + base verba possibility, slightly weaker"The seller might refuse to extend."
could + base verbremote, but serious if it happens"A hidden liability could cost us the deal."
be unlikely to + base verbthe honest low end of the scale"The board is unlikely to approve it this quarter."

Key idea: every one of these takes the base verb -- "may to ask" → "may ask" -- and likely always keeps its to: "is likely to delay". Saying will about a risk turns a probability into a promise, and the committee will hold you to it.

The Structure

One Risk, Three Levels of Certainty

Same register, same committee. Only the modal moves.

Level 1 · Probable

"The property files are likely to delay the closing by two weeks."

Use it when you have a number behind the sentence. The committee will plan around it.

Level 2 · Possible

"The regulator may ask for the full audit trail."

A genuine maybe. Say it and prepare for it, without treating it as a plan.

Level 3 · Remote but severe

"A hidden liability could cost us the whole transaction."

Low likelihood, very high severity. This is the item that gets escalated anyway.

Say "will" about any of these and you have stopped presenting a risk. You have made a promise.

Legal Risk Register

LEGAL RISK REGISTER -- Q3

Presented to the Risk Committee · Simone Quiles · Thursday 14:00

Three open items. Board threshold: high severity, any likelihood.

1. Property files, Campinas target

Likelihood: high · Impact: moderate · Below threshold. Mitigated in the department; remediation owner and date recorded.

2. Undisclosed liability in the target group

Likelihood: low · Severity: very high · Exposure: full transaction value. ABOVE THRESHOLD -- escalated, contingency plan attached.

3. Late quarterly filing

Already in remediation. Safeguard in place since May. One line only.

4. For the committee, not for legal

Risk appetite on item 2: how much exposure the group is prepared to carry to close on time.

5. Next review

Exposure on all three items reassessed at the end of the quarter.

Your line: "Item two could cost us the full transaction value, so it is likely to need a board decision this week."

Dialogue

In Front of the Committee

R

"Three items on the register. Start with the one you want us to decide, not the one you find most interesting."

S

"Item two. The likelihood is low, but the severity is very high: an undisclosed liability could cost us the full exposure of the deal."

R

"Low likelihood. So why is it in front of us at all, and not handled in your department?"

S

"Because it sits above the board threshold. I am required to escalate it, and I have brought a contingency plan rather than an opinion."

R

"Fair. And in your view, should we carry that exposure in order to close on time?"

S

"That is risk appetite, and it is your decision. I can tell you the two numbers, and that item one is likely to delay closing while we mitigate it."
