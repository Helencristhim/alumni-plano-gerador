Lesson 3 -- What Exactly Do You Do?

Grammar Video
"Defining relative clauses" — 6 Minute Grammar, BBC Learning English

Six minutes on the clause that tells you WHICH one — who, that, which, where, and the moment the pronoun can be left out altogether. Connection to Lesson 3: this is the grammar of "the clause that protects us" and "the safeguard we rely on".

Tip: after each example, say the same sentence about your own work. If you can drop the pronoun and it still sounds right, you have understood the rule that matters most in speech.

Podcast
"Episode 916: The Whistleblower Whisperer" — Planet Money, NPR

A lawyer who represents Wall Street whistleblowers explains what he actually does all day, in ordinary American English, to an audience that is not legal at all. Connection to Lesson 3: it is a professional explaining a highly technical job without a single word of jargon.

Tip: listen once for the story, then once counting how many times he explains a legal idea by giving an example instead of a definition. That substitution is the whole skill of this lesson.

"Use simple words and phrases" — Federal Plain Language Guidelines

The list the United States government requires its own lawyers to use: the long word on the left, the word a reader actually knows on the right. Connection to Lesson 3: this is plain English as an official standard, not as a style preference.

Tip: find five words on that page that you used in something you wrote this month, and rewrite those sentences. Read both versions out loud — the shorter one is almost always the one you can say without preparing.
