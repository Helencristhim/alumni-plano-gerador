Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Lyrics -- the words of a song
"I looked up the lyrics after the concert."
A verse -- one of the parts of a song that tell the story; each one has different words
"In the second verse, he says he’s sorry."
The chorus -- the part of a song that repeats, usually after each verse
"Everybody sings the chorus at the concert."
Catchy -- easy to remember and pleasant to hear
"The melody is so catchy."
To make out -- to manage to hear or see something that is not clear
"I can’t make out the words in the chorus."
To mishear -- to hear a word wrongly and understand something different
"I misheard that line for years."
To sing along -- to sing together with a song or with other people
"Turn it up, I want to sing along."
To get stuck in your head -- when a song keeps playing in your mind and you cannot stop it
"That chorus got stuck in my head all day."
A cover -- a new recording of a song by a different artist
"I prefer the cover to the original."
Slang -- very informal words, often used by a particular group of people
"The lyrics are full of slang."
To look up -- to search for information in a dictionary, on the internet or in a book
"I always look up the lyrics I don’t understand."
To grow on someone -- to become more liked over time
"I didn’t like the song at first, but it grew on me."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Cauê and his friend Jess are in the car when a catchy song comes on. “What does he say in the chorus?” he asks. He can never make out the words. Jess explains that the singer says he is leaving the city.

Cauê laughs. For years he misheard that line. Jess tells him that, in the second verse, the singer admits he had made a mistake, and at the end the woman promises she would wait, but he tells her not to.

At home, Cauê looks up the lyrics. There is a lot of slang, but now he understands the story. The song has grown on him, and it is stuck in his head.

1. Why do we say “she promised she would wait” and not “she promised she will wait”?
A Because promise is always followed by would in every situation, even about the future.
B Because the woman in the song is not telling the truth, so we use would to show doubt.
C Because in reported speech, will moves one step back to would.
2. What did Cauê do for years?
A He sang the song every day in the car with Jess on the way to work.
B He misheard a line of the song.
C He looked up the lyrics every week but never understood the slang in them.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “He told me he was leaving.”
B “He said me he was leaving.”
C “He told that he is leaving the city and never coming back.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech -- He Said He Was Leaving

GRAMMAR

When you tell someone what another person said, the verbs usually take one step back in time, and words like tomorrow and today change too. Tell needs a person after it; say does not.

JobFormExample
Present → pastam / is leaving → was leaving · love → lovedHe said he was leaving.
Perfect or past → past perfecthave told / told → had toldShe admitted she had never told anyone.
Will → wouldwill wait → would waitHe promised he would wait.
Time words move tootomorrow → the next day · today → that daythe next day, that night
Promise, warn, tell + (not) tofor orders, advice and promisesShe warned him not to call.
The trapsay has no person after it; tell doesNot he said me. Say he told me or he said that.

Never: He said me · She promised she will wait · He admitted he has made a mistake · She told him to not call

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A friend said: “I’m going to the concert.” You tell someone else:
A "She said me she is going to go to the concert on that day."
B "She said she was going to the concert."
C "She told that she goes to the concert, I think."
The singer said: “I’ve never written a love song.” You report it:
A "He admitted he never writes any love songs, not even one."
B "He admitted me he has never written a love song before."
C "He admitted he had never written a love song."

A "I can’t make out the words."
B "I can’t grow on the words of the song, they are too fast."
