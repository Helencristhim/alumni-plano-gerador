Chapter 3: The Code

He Said He Was Leaving

One step back in time

Grammar Discovery

What Happens to the Verbs?

"I’m leaving tomorrow." → He said he was leaving the next day.

"I’ve never told anyone." → She admitted she had never told anyone.

"I’ll wait for you." → He promised he would wait for her.

"Don’t trust him." → Her mother warned her not to trust him.

Compare the words in quotation marks with the report after the arrow. Look at the red verbs: what happened to am leaving, have never told and will? Look at the blue words: tomorrow changed too. And in sentence 4, an order became warned her not to.

JobFormExample
Present → pastam / is leaving → was leaving · love → lovedHe said he was leaving.
Perfect or past → past perfecthave told / told → had toldShe admitted she had never told anyone.
Will → wouldwill wait → would waitHe promised he would wait.
Time words move tootomorrow → the next day · today → that daythe next day, that night
Promise, warn, tell + (not) tofor orders, advice and promisesShe warned him not to call.
The trapsay has no person after it; tell doesNot he said me. Say he told me or he said that.

In one line: in a report, the verbs take one step back in time: is > was, have > had, will > would.

Listening 1 · A Radio DJ

What the Singer Told Me

A British radio DJ introduces a song and reports what the singer told him in an interview. Sound first — no text.

1. When did the singer write the song, according to the DJ?
On a night bus, after a bad day at work.
2. What did the singer admit?
That he had never expected the song to be a hit.
3. What did he promise?
That he would play it at every concert.
Listening 2 · A Podcast About Lyrics

The Words We Mishear

An American podcast host talks about misheard lyrics. Sound first — no text.

1. What did she believe for ten years?
That a song said something about a bathroom, but it said something completely different.
2. Why do we mishear lyrics, according to her?
Because singers stretch the words, and our brain fills the gaps with words it knows.
3. What is her advice?
Look up the lyrics, then listen again and try to make out each word.
Listening 3 · A Music Teacher

Why Songs Work

An Australian music teacher explains why songs help people learn a language. Sound first — no text.

1. Why is the chorus useful for learners, according to him?
Because it repeats many times, so you hear the same words again and again.
2. What did one of his students tell him?
That she had learned more English from songs than from her grammar book.
3. What does he say about slang?
Do not worry about it at first; look it up later, and it will grow on you.
