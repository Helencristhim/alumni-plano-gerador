Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

Writing to the Court -- Although, However, Therefore

Preparing a motion for a U.S. court and explaining your argument. Key words: a motion, a complaint, a plaintiff, a defendant, a ruling, to appeal, a hearing, to request, a brief, to grant. Structure: although / despite / however for contrast; therefore / so for result.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A motion -- a formal written request to a judge to make a decision about something in a case
"We are filing a motion to freeze the debtor’s bank account."
A complaint -- the first document of a lawsuit, where the plaintiff explains the problem and what it wants
"The complaint was filed in March."
A plaintiff -- the person or the company that starts a lawsuit
"Our client, the bank, is the plaintiff."
A defendant -- the person or the company that a lawsuit is against
"The defendant did not answer the complaint."
A ruling -- an official decision of a judge
"We are waiting for the ruling."
To appeal -- to ask a higher court to change a decision
"If we lose, we will appeal."
A hearing -- a meeting in court where the judge listens to both sides
"The hearing is on November 12."
To request -- to ask for something in a formal way
"We request more time to submit the documents."
A brief -- a written document that explains the legal arguments of one side
"David is writing the brief this week."
To grant -- to say yes to a request, officially
"The judge granted our motion."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Southern Trust Bank lent two million dollars to Coastal Imports, a company in Florida. Although the company promised to pay in six months, it never paid. The bank became the plaintiff, and its lawyers filed a complaint against the company, the defendant.

Despite the deadline, the defendant did not answer. It had money in a Florida bank account. However, it started to move the money to other countries. The money could disappear soon. Therefore, the lawyers filed a motion and a brief, and they requested an urgent hearing.

At the hearing, the judge listened to both sides and granted the motion, so the account was frozen. The defendant did not agree with the ruling, and it decided to appeal.

1. Why does the text use “Although” in the first paragraph?
A Because the second idea is a surprise: the company promised to pay, but it never paid.
B Because the second idea is a result.
C Because although always starts the first sentence of a story.
2. Why did the lawyers request an urgent hearing?
A Because the judge asked them to write a brief.
B Because the defendant wanted to pay the debt quickly, before the end of the six months.
C Because the defendant was moving the money, and it could disappear.
3. What did the defendant do after the ruling?
A It paid the two million dollars to the bank and asked the judge to close the case for good.
B It decided to appeal.
C It filed a new complaint.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Linking Words: Contrast and Result

GRAMMAR

For a contrast, use although + subject + verb, despite + noun or -ing, or start a new sentence with However,. For a result, start a new sentence with Therefore, (formal, written) or use so inside one sentence (spoken). Never use although and but in the same sentence.

JobFormExample
Although + sentencecontrast inside one sentence: subject + verb after itAlthough he was served, he did not answer.
Despite + noun / -ingcontrast, but NO subject + verb after itDespite the deadline / Despite receiving the complaint, he did not answer.
However,contrast between two sentences: period, However, commaHe has assets. However, he is hiding them.
Therefore,result between two sentences, formal: perfect for a motion or a briefThe money can disappear. Therefore, we request a hearing.
soresult inside one sentence, less formal: good for meetingsHe did not answer, so we filed a motion.
The trapdespite never takes of, and although never takes butNot Despite of the deadline or Although he owes, but he.... Say Despite the deadline / Although he owes, he...

Never: Despite of the deadline · Although he owes, but he... · Despite he was served · We requested for a hearing

Stage 2: Put the Argument in Order

Order

A short argument for a judge. Put the sentences in a logical order: facts, problem, risk, request.

?"Our client lent two million dollars to the defendant."
?"Although the defendant promised to pay, it never paid."
?"Despite the deadline, it did not answer the complaint."
?"However, it is moving money out of its Florida account."
?"The money can disappear soon."
?"Therefore, we request an urgent hearing."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to say that the debtor did not pay, and this was a surprise. You say:
A "Although he promised to pay, he never paid."
B "Although he promised to pay our client in six months, but he never paid anything."
C "Despite he promised to pay, he never paid."
You are writing the last sentence of a motion. You write:
A "So that, we are requesting for an urgent hearing with the judge as soon as possible."
B "Therefore, we request an urgent hearing."
C "However, we request an urgent hearing."
The client asks what happens if the judge says no. You say:
A "If the judge doesn’t grant the motion, we can appeal."
B "If the judge grants it, we can appeal."
C "If the judge grants the motion, we appeal."
A colleague asks who the defendant is in your case. You say:
A "The defendant is the bank, because the bank started the lawsuit against the company in Florida."
B "The defendant is the bank."
C "The defendant is the company that didn’t pay."
