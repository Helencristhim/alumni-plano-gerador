Chapter 1: The Room

When Did You Last Disagree?

Think of the last time somebody said something about the world that you thought was wrong. What did you say, and what did you decide not to say?

Where You Start

Two Speeds, and the Missing Step

Say nothing

Safe, and the table never learns that you had an argument

Contradict flatly

Clear, and the other person stops listening at the first word

Concede, then push back

One true point given away, and the room turns towards you

In Portuguese you have the middle step and you use it without thinking. In English it disappears, and what is left are the two extremes. The middle step is a move with a shape, and the shape is what we are learning today.

Chapter 3: The Code

I Suggest That the Figure Be Checked

The form that challenges the claim and leaves the person out of it

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I suggest that the figure be checked before we go any further."

"It is essential that this be understood by anybody arguing in English."

"They recommended that he withdraw the claim."

"The only condition I insist on is that every objection be addressed to the argument."

Look at the verb straight after that. Something that English normally insists on is missing from it. What is missing, and why would a language drop it here?

TriggerWhat happens to the verbExample
suggest / recommend / propose thatBase form, always. No s in the third person, however singular the subject is."They recommended that he withdraw the claim."
insist / demand / ask thatBase form as well, and it carries more force than a modal would."I would ask that the vote be taken at the end."
It is essential / vital / important thatSame form. This is the impersonal opening, and it removes you from the sentence."It is essential that this be understood."
The negativenot goes straight in front of the verb. No does, no did, nothing."I suggest that he not answer that question."
Why it lands harderThe form takes the person out. You are not telling somebody they are wrong; you are putting a claim on the table for the room to check."I suggest that the figure be checked." (not: you are wrong)

This is standard American English, and it is everywhere in the press you read. British speakers often say should instead, and both are correct.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I suggest that he withdraws the claim."
"I suggest that he withdraw the claim."
"I suggest that he does not answer that question."
"I suggest that he not answer that question."
"Can I suggest you a different way of looking at it?"
"Can I suggest a different way of looking at it?"

Three rules, and the first two are the same rule. After that, the verb goes into its base form and stays there, whoever the subject is. Not goes straight in front of it, with no does. And suggest never takes a person after it in English — you suggest a thing, and the person is already in the room.

Comprehension

About Judith

1. What does Judith say happened in the debate?
That he was right and lost the room anyway.
2. Why does she say nobody argued with the figures?
Because nobody was listening by then -- he corrected the other speaker in his first sentence.
3. What exactly does she say he should have conceded?
One small true thing -- that the timing was never explained, which was true.
4. Why does she say nobody can be offended by that sentence?
Because there is no person in it -- it puts the claim on the table instead of the speaker.
Listening

The Rules Before the Debate

A moderator opening the evening. Sound first -- no text.

1. How long does each speaker have, and what happens at the end of it?
Four minutes, not five, and the moderator stops them at four.
2. What is the one condition the moderator insists on?
That every objection be addressed to the argument and not to the person who made it.
3. Why must nobody talk over anybody else?
Because the recording picks up nothing when two people speak at once, and half the room hears nothing either.
Listening 2

One True Thing, Then But

A short method clip. Sound first -- no text.

1. What do you do before you disagree, and what has to be true about it?
You concede one thing, and it has to be true.
2. Why does she say the move works, if it is not about politeness?
Because it proves you were listening, and a room gives its attention to somebody who has just shown that.
3. What are the two mistakes to avoid?
Conceding something nobody claimed, which is a straw man, and conceding everything, because then you have simply agreed.
