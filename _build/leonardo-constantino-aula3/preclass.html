Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

Speaking With Authority -- Describing Roles, Responsibilities, and Ongoing Projects at ALMA

Describing what you do, what your team has already delivered, and what the client must, should or can do next. Key words: tax liability, withholding tax, regulatory burden, tax base, cash flow impact, reform implementation, tax planning, financial holding, indirect tax. Expressions: assess the impact, file a return, mitigate risk, deal with, ahead of the curve, cut through the noise. Structures: modals must / have to / should / can, with present perfect for project updates.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Tax liability -- the total amount of tax a company owes at a given moment
"The reform reduces their tax liability by twelve percent."
Withholding tax -- tax taken out of a payment before the money reaches the receiver
"There is a withholding tax on every payment to the fund."
Regulatory burden -- the weight of all the rules a company has to follow
"Small banks feel the regulatory burden the most."
Tax base -- the amount that the tax is calculated on
"The new rule makes the tax base much wider."
Cash flow impact -- the effect of a decision on the money entering and leaving a company
"The board only asked about the cash flow impact."
Reform implementation -- the period when a new law starts to work in practice
"Reform implementation runs until the end of the decade."
Tax planning -- organising a company legally so that it pays the right amount, and no more
"Good tax planning starts before the deal, not after."
Financial holding -- a company whose business is owning other financial companies
"The group sits under a financial holding in Luxembourg."
Indirect tax -- a tax on goods and services, not on income or profit
"The whole reform is about indirect tax."
Assess the impact -- to measure how much a change will cost or save
"We have already assessed the impact for eleven clients."
File a return -- to send the official tax document to the authorities
"They must file a return before the deadline."
Mitigate risk -- to make a risk smaller before it becomes a problem
"We can mitigate risk before implementation starts."
Deal with -- to handle a problem, a client or a question
"I deal with the questions the board is afraid to ask."
Ahead of the curve -- prepared earlier than the rest of the market
"Our clients are ahead of the curve on the reform."
Cut through the noise -- to say the one thing that really matters
"A good advisor cuts through the noise in two minutes."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Leonardo is briefing a foreign partner on two live mandates. "My role involves indirect tax for financial institutions, and I am currently leading the reform implementation project," he says. "We have already assessed the impact for eleven clients. In four of them the cash flow impact was positive, because the new tax base works in their favour. The committee has not published the final rate yet, so nobody can promise a number." Then he moves to the advice. "Every financial holding must file a return under the new rules from January. That is the law, not my opinion. Before that, the client should review the structure, because that is where we can mitigate risk. They do not have to decide this week, but good tax planning keeps them ahead of the curve. What I recommend is one meeting with the CFO, so that we cut through the noise and deal with the tax liability question first."

1. Why does the text use "must file a return" and not "should file a return"?
A Because "must" is a politer way of giving advice.
B Because it is an obligation written in the law, not a personal recommendation.
C Because the action happens in the past.
2. "They do not have to decide this week." What does this mean?
A Deciding this week is prohibited.
B Deciding this week is not necessary, but it is allowed.
C They have already decided.
3. Why does he say "the committee has not published the final rate yet"?
A Because the committee published it a long time ago.
B Because "yet" always goes with the past simple.
C Because it has not happened up to now, and it is still expected.
4. "That is where we can mitigate risk." What does "can" express here?
A A legal obligation for the client.
B An option, and what his firm is able to do.
C Something that happened last year.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Must, Have to, Should, Can

GRAMMAR

One question decides the modal: is this the law, my advice, or an option? And remember the project update tense: what has already happened, and what has not happened yet.

FormUseExample
must + base verbA strong obligation, often written in the law itselfThey must file a return in January.
have to / has to + base verbAn obligation from outside: a rule, a client, a deadlineEvery holding has to report the tax base.
should + base verbYour professional recommendation, not an obligationThe board should review the structure now.
can + base verbPossibility, option, or what your firm is able to doWe can mitigate the risk before the vote.
Negativemust not = prohibited · do not have to = not necessary. They are opposites, not synonyms.
QuestionDo we have to file this quarter? · Should we wait for the final text? · Can you send the memo today?
No "to", no "-s""should to consider" and "he musts file" do not exist. Only have keeps the to.
Project updateWe have already assessed the impact · They have not approved it yet · I have just sent the model · for eight months · since the first draft.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each real conversation.

A CFO asks: "Is the January filing optional for us?" The deadline is in the law. You answer:
A "No, you should to file in January."
B "No, you must file in January. The deadline comes from the law."
C "No, you do not have to file in January."
You want to give a recommendation, not an order. You say:
A "What I recommend is a review of the holding structure this quarter."
B "You must to review the holding structure this quarter."
C "You reviewing the holding structure this quarter."
The client asks how long your firm has worked on the mandate. It started eight months ago and continues. You say:
A "We work on this mandate since eight months."
B "We have worked on this mandate for eight months."
C "We have worked on this mandate since eight months."
A partner asks what your role covers. The most professional answer is:
A "I make tax things for banks."
B "My role involves indirect tax for financial institutions."
C "My role is to treat about the indirect tax."
