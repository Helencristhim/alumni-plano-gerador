Chapter 4: The Code

The Word Before the Idea

Formal connectors, and three ways to say how firm a commitment is

Grammar Discovery

What Does Each Red Word Promise the Reader?

"Investment rose sharply, albeit from a very low base."

"Funds want arbitration abroad, whereas the ministry prefers local courts."

"In light of the floods, the agency is to publish new rules by March."

"The concession review was supposed to come out in June, but it was delayed."

The red words connect ideas. Which one introduces a contrast, which one limits what was just said, and which one gives a cause? Now the blue words: which describes an official plan, and which a plan that did not happen?

JobFormExample
Contrast two positionswhereas + clause; by contrast,Funds want arbitration abroad, whereas the ministry prefers local courts.
Limit what you just saidalbeit + short phrase; that said,Investment rose, albeit from a low base.
Concede, then continuenotwithstanding + noun / thisNotwithstanding the delay, the auction will proceed.
Give a causein light of / as a result of + noun; consequently,In light of the floods, the dates were revised.
An official planbe to + verbThe agency is to publish the rules by March.
A plan that failedwas / were supposed to + verbThe report was supposed to come out in June.
An obligationhave to + verbOperators have to comply within ninety days.

In one line: The connector tells the reader the relationship before the idea arrives; be to announces a plan, and was supposed to reports one that failed.

Quick Fire

One Question at a Time

Listening 1 · A Communications Director

The Six O’Clock Reader

A communications director on writing for international investors. Sound first — no text.

1. Who is the one reader the director asks her team to picture?
An investor in another time zone, reading the release at six in the morning, on a phone, in her second language.
2. What does she cut first, and why?
Adjectives such as distinguished, historic and landmark, because they tell the reader nothing she can use.
3. What do whereas, albeit and in light of tell the reader?
Whereas signals a contrast, albeit signals a limit on what was just said, and in light of gives the cause.
Listening 2 · A Journalist

What You Can Print

A financial journalist explains the rules to a room of executives. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the difference between on the record and on background, according to the journalist?
On the record, he can print it with the person’s name. On background, he can use the information but describes the source only as a person familiar with the matter.
2. When must an executive say they want nothing printed?
Before telling the journalist, not after.
3. What does he advise when something goes wrong, and why?
To send a short holding statement early, because silence makes a small story big.
