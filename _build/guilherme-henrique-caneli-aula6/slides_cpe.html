Why C is the one
Boilerplate is already public and already attributed: it is the fixed paragraph at the foot of every release. Putting it “on the record” is an offer of something the journalist already has. The Text

Paragraphs One and Two

Everything Leaks, and the Timing Is the Message

Every organisation eventually discovers that it does not control when its news becomes public. It controls, at best, the twenty minutes before.1 The instruments built around that gap are older than the internet, and they survive for a reason.

An embargo is the oldest of them: the material goes out in advance on the condition that nothing appears before a stated hour. It is held together by nothing except professional habit, which is precisely why a breach is punished socially rather than legally.2

Adapted from a comment essay on corporate communication · ~600 words · gaps 1 and 2
The Text

Paragraphs Three and Four

Everything Leaks, and the Timing Is the Message

Below the embargo sit two conventions that outsiders confuse constantly. A remark made on background may be used but not attributed; a remark made on the record belongs to whoever wrote it down.3 A spokesperson who is unclear about which is in play should not be in the conversation, and when it goes wrong the fault is almost never the journalist’s.

Then there is the law, which cares nothing for any of this. Disclosure obligations require a listed company to publish material information — anything a reasonable investor would want to know before dealing — promptly, and to everybody at the same moment.4 A selective briefing that would be ordinary practice in a private company is, in a listed one, a serious matter.

Adapted from a comment essay on corporate communication · ~600 words · gaps 3 and 4
The Text

Paragraphs Five and Six

Everything Leaks, and the Timing Is the Message

When something breaks before the organisation is ready, a holding statement buys the forty-eight hours in which the real one can be written. Where several parties are involved, a joint statement takes five times as long, because every clause has to survive stakeholder alignment before anybody outside reads a word of it.5

The craft itself is small and unforgiving. A lede that buries the development in the fourth line will be rewritten by somebody whose version you will not enjoy. Boilerplate at the foot is not filler; it is the paragraph the lawyers fought over. Forward guidance has to be specific enough to be useful and vague enough to survive a bad quarter.6

Adapted from a comment essay on corporate communication · ~600 words · gaps 5 and 6

Why D is the distractor
D is true, it is on topic, and it would sit comfortably in a conversation about this article. What it never does is answer a reference or complete an argument. That is the whole test: a gapped text is not asking what is true, it is asking what is cohesive.

The trap in (a) and (d)
Both are TRUE statements about corridors. They are still wrong, because the question asks what the sentence is for, not what it contains. In Part 5, at least one option in every item is true and irrelevant.
The distinction drawn in the final paragraph is between a statement that has been
aapproved and one that has been drafted.✓
bwritten and one that has merely been assembled.✓
cpublished and one that has been held.✓
dagreed by one party and one agreed by several.✓
Why this matters today
The writer ends on what the room EXPOSES, not on what it decides. That is the bridge into the grammar: an inversion does the same job in one sentence -- it puts the thing you want noticed where it cannot be skipped.

The Forms

Four That Look Alike

What each one commits you to, and who is imposing it
1Contrast two positions
whereas + clause; by contrast,

2Limit what you just said

3Concede, then continue

4Give a cause

In a release, which linker commits the writer to a causal claim?
aWhereas the first draft was cautious, ...✓
bGiven the revised guidance, ...✓
cNotwithstanding the revised guidance, ...✓
dAs regards the revised guidance, ...✓
Why the answer is (b)
GIVEN asserts a cause: it says the guidance is the reason. WHEREAS contrasts, NOTWITHSTANDING concedes, and AS REGARDS merely changes the subject. Only one of the four puts the writer behind a causal claim, and in a release that is where the exposure sits.

Collaborative Task

Three Sentences, Ten Minutes

Four minutes, together

Collaborative task — four minutes. You and your partner are the only two people available to sign off a holding statement in the next ten minutes. Together, agree the three sentences. One of you wants a fourth sentence; the other has to decide whether it survives. You have to agree on the final wording.

Long Turn

Two Minutes, Uninterrupted

No notes. No apology at the start.

Long turn — two minutes, uninterrupted. Something has gone wrong at your organisation and the first call has come in. Answer this: “What are we saying, and what are we not saying yet?” Give the holding statement out loud, then justify each thing you left out. Use at least three formal linkers and one be to construction.

Follow-up

Four Questions, No Restarting

Four examiner questions, straight after the two minutes. No restarting, and no preparation between them.
1Say your key message again, in one sentence, without the structure around it.
2You said something “is to” happen. Who arranged it, and would you write that down?
3A journalist tells you a rival has already been briefed. Respond, on the record.
4One sentence: what is the first thing you would put in writing tonight?
Debate One

Defend the Side You Disagree With

This house believes that a statement which commits to nothing does more damage than saying nothing at all.
1Ninety seconds each, alternating. No notes.
2You choose your side, and you open with the strongest argument against yourself.
3At least two formal linkers per turn, and no “however” twice.
Debate Two

The Side You Were Given

This house believes that forward guidance should be abolished, because precision and honesty pull in opposite directions.
1The teacher gives you the side. You do not choose it.
2Before you answer, restate the strongest point the other side has made, in your own words, so that they accept your version of it.
3Only then do you respond. A turn that skips the concession does not count.

What the teacher will be reading for
Three things are being read for. A first line that says what has happened, not what the organisation feels. A visible decision about what is being withheld, with a date attached to it. And a statement short enough that somebody could read it aloud from memory. A holding statement that reads like a press release has failed at its only job, which is to buy time without spending credibility.
