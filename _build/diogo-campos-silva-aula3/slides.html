Chapter 3: The Code

Can, Can’t

What users can do, and what you ask for

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Have in Common?

"The users can’t log in to the VPN."

"Linda can reset the VPN."

"Can you help me?"

"Can they access the internet?"

Look at the blue words. Does can change with Linda or the users? What comes after it?

JobFormExample
Affirmative (everybody)can + verbLinda can reset the VPN.
Negativecan’t (cannot) + verbThe users can’t log in.
Question / requestCan + person + verb?Can you help me?
Short answersYes, I can. / No, they can’t.Can they access the internet? Yes, they can.
The trapno -s, no “to”Not she cans or can to reset. Say she can reset.

In one line: can is the same for everybody. Can’t for the problem; Can you...? for the request.

Chapter 4: On the Phone

On the Phone

One conversation, two recordings, one support plan

Dialogue

Can You Help Me?

L
Good afternoon, SecureNet Support. This is Linda. How can I help you?
D
Hi, Linda. This is Diogo, from Brazil. I can’t open a ticket on your website. Can I open it by phone?
L
Of course. What is the problem?
D
Our users can’t log in to the VPN. They can’t access the server.
L
I see. Can they access the internet?
D
Yes, they can. But the VPN connection doesn’t work.
L
Okay. I can reset the VPN for you. Is it urgent?
D
Yes, it is. Twenty users can’t work from home.
L
Then it is a high priority. Your ticket number is six one nine.
D
Thank you, Linda. Can you send me an email, please?
Comprehension

About Linda

1. Where does Linda work?
At SecureNet Support.
2. What can Linda do for Diogo?
She can reset the VPN.
3. What ticket number does Linda give?
Six one nine.

Ravi Calls Back

The vendor’s engineer leaves a message about the ticket. Sound first — no text.

Tonight at ten.

No, they can’t. They can work from the office.
3. When can Ravi call Diogo?
Tomorrow at nine.
