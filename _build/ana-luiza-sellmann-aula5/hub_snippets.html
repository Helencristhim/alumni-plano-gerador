Lesson 05 -- Pre-class

If I Lived Somewhere Else -- The Life You Can Picture

Talking about imagined situations, giving advice and describing the job you would say yes to. Key words: tempted, a dealbreaker, to be based in, to afford, a long shot, to picture, a role, a portfolio. Structure: the second conditional (if plus the past simple, would plus the base verb), with could, might and "If I were you".

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. Do not translate — the example sentence is the meaning.

Tempted -- wanting to do something before you have decided
"I'd be tempted to say yes, honestly."
A dealbreaker -- the one thing that would make you say no
"The salary is fine. The time difference is the dealbreaker."
To be based in -- to have your home or your office in a place
"If I worked remotely, I'd still be based in Sao Paulo."
To afford -- to have enough money for something
"If we saved a bit more, we could afford a year abroad."
A long shot -- something with very little chance of working
"It's a long shot, but I'd apply anyway."
To picture -- to see something clearly in your head
"I can't picture myself living far from my family."
A role -- a job or a position inside a company
"If they offered me the role, I'd take it."
A portfolio -- the collection of work you show to get a job
"He got my portfolio from a colleague."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A friend of mine has been offered a job in Amsterdam and he keeps asking me what I would do. Honestly, I would be tempted. If I lived in another country for a year, I would learn the language faster than in any class, and I could picture the whole thing: a small flat, a new gym, no car. But if I were far from my family, I would miss them every single day. My mother is already in Shanghai, so that would be one continent too many. Being far from them would be the dealbreaker.

There is a version I would say yes to immediately. If a company offered me a role where I stayed based here and travelled four or five times a year, I would not think twice. It is a long shot, but a recruiter has my portfolio, so who knows. If I were my friend, I would go and look at the city before deciding anything. And if he asked me to visit him there, I would go in a second.

1. Why does the text say "If I lived in another country" and not "If I live"?
A Because she is imagining it. There is no plan and no offer for her.
B Because she lived in another country in the past.
C Because the sentence is about a year, and years take the past form.
2. What does "Being far from them would be the dealbreaker" mean?
A That she has already said no to a job abroad.
B That the distance from her family is the one thing that would make her refuse.
C That her family would move with her.
3. Why is it "If I were my friend" and not "If I was my friend"?
A Because the friend is a man.
B Because in this imagined structure careful English uses were for everybody.
C Because the sentence is in the plural.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Real or Imagined

GRAMMAR

The same two halves as last lesson, one step back in time.

HalfFormExample
The conditionif + past simple. The past form means distance from reality, not past time.If I lived abroad, ...
If they offered me the role, ...
The consequencewould + base verb. In speech, almost always the contraction: I'd, she'd, we'd...., I'd miss my family.
..., I'd take it.
First or second?If they offer me the role, I'll take it = real, it may happen. If they offered me the role, I'd take it = imagined, there is no offer.
Negative and question"If I didn't have a family here, I wouldn't think twice." / "What would you do if you had a free year?"
could and mightInstead of would: could = would be able to, might = maybe. "If we saved more, we could afford it."
If I were youWith the verb to be, careful English uses were for everybody: If I were you, I'd go. It is the politest way to give advice in English.

There is only one thing to remember: would never lives in the if half. And if you choose the wrong conditional, nothing breaks — the person understands you, and you can adjust in the next sentence.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

People are asking you hypothetical questions. Choose the best thing to say.

There is no offer on the table, but a friend asks what you would do about a job abroad. You say:
A "If they offered me the role, I'd probably take it."
B "If they would offer me the role, I would probably take it."
C "If they offer me the role, I would probably took it."
A friend cannot decide about moving to another country. You want to give advice politely. You say:
A "If I was you, I go and see the city."
B "If I were you, I'd go and see the city first."
C "You must go and see the city, obviously."
The interview is next week and it is a real possibility. You say:
A "If they called me next week, I would go."
B "If they call me next week, I'll go."
C "If they will call me next week, I go."
You want to say that money is what stops you, without sounding negative. You say:
A "If we saved a bit more, we could afford a year abroad."
B "If we would save a bit more, we can afford a year abroad."
C "We cannot afford nothing, so it is impossible."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer, and nobody is correcting this one.

A recruiter calls and asks: "If we designed the role around you, what would it look like?" Speak for about ninety seconds. Say where you would be based, how often you would travel, what kind of projects you would want, and what would make you say no. Use if at least four times, and start one sentence with "If I were". If a sentence breaks in the middle, say "let me start that again" out loud and keep going.
