Chapter 3: The Code

One Step Back

Same two halves. Different distance.

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"If I lived in Amsterdam, I would work remotely."

"If they offered me the role, I would take it."

"If we had more time, we could afford a year abroad."

"What would you do if you had a free year?"

The verbs look like the past. None of these sentences is about the past. What are they about?

HalfFormExample
The conditionif + past simple. The past form here means distance, not past time.If I lived there, ...
The consequencewould + base verb. In speech, almost always the contraction: I'd, she'd, we'd...., I'd work remotely.
First or second?If they offer me the role, I'll take it = real, it may happen. If they offered me the role, I'd take it = imagined, and no offer exists.
could and mightInstead of would: could = would be able to, might = maybe. "If we saved more, we could afford it."
If I were youWith the verb to be, careful English uses were for everybody: "If I were you, I would go." It is the most useful way to give advice without sounding bossy.

One shape, one step back: if + past, would + verb. And would never lives in the if half, exactly like will last week.

The Contrast

Real, or Imagined?

IT MAY REALLY HAPPEN

"If they offer me the role, I'll take it."

"If it rains, we'll stay home."

I AM ONLY IMAGINING

"If they offered me the role, I'd take it."

"If I lived abroad, I'd miss my family."

The past form is not about the past. It is the distance between you and the situation.

And if you pick the wrong one, nothing breaks. The person still understands you, and you can correct yourself in the next sentence.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"If I would live abroad, I would miss my family."
"If I lived abroad, I would miss my family."
"If they offered me the role, I will take it."
"If they offered me the role, I'd take it."
"If I was you, I would go and see the city first."
"If I were you, I'd go and see the city first."

The word would never goes inside the if half. And with the verb to be, careful English says were for everybody, which is why the advice phrase is always If I were you.

Chapter 4: The Offer

What Would You Do?

Sam has an offer and nobody to think out loud with

Dialogue

After the Tennis Class

S
Can I ask you something? They offered me a job in Amsterdam. I have two weeks to answer.
A
Two weeks is nothing. What would you do with your flat if you took it?
S
I'd keep it for a year, just in case. If I hated the city, I would at least have somewhere to come back to.
A
That is smart. Honestly, I'd be tempted. I can picture the whole thing.
S
So would you go, if it were you?
A
I don't know. Being far from my family would be the dealbreaker. My mother is already in Shanghai, so that is one continent too many.
S
If I were you, I would go and look at the city first. You can always say no after.
A
Fair. And if I found a role where I could stay based here and travel, I'd take it tomorrow.
Comprehension

About Sam

1. What has Sam been offered, and how long does he have to answer?
A job in Amsterdam, and he has two weeks.
2. What would he do with his flat, and why?
He would keep it for a year, so he would have somewhere to come back to if he hated the city.
3. What advice does he give at the end?
That if he were her, he would go and look at the city first, because you can always say no after.
Listening

A Year with No Job

She has been thinking about your question all week. Sound first — no text.

1. What would she NOT do with a free year?
Travel the world. She thinks it is a long shot that anyone really enjoys it.
2. Where would she live, and what would she learn?
In a small place near the sea, somewhere cheap, and she would finally learn to cook properly.
3. What would be her dealbreaker?
Being far from her sister. If her sister could not visit, she would not go.
Listening 2

A Question, Not an Offer

Michael, from a studio in Amsterdam. Sound first — no text.

1. How did Michael get her portfolio, and what is he offering?
From a colleague. He says it is a question, not an offer, so there is no pressure.
2. Where would she be based, and how often would she travel?
She would stay based in Sao Paulo and travel four or five times a year.
3. What does he say about the time difference?
If it were a problem, they could look at the hours, because half the team is in the Americas.
