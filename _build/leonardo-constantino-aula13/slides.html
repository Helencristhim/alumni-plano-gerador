Chapter 3: The Code

Who Moves the Conversation

Social register · technical register · and the sentence that crosses between them

Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I'm a tax lawyer." → "I'm a tax lawyer, and this year almost all of it has been foreign payment platforms."

"Yes, the reform is complicated." → "It is, and it's been a busy year because of it. Are you seeing it on your side?"

"We advise banks." → "Actually, we advise banks on exactly that. Speaking of which, what are your clients doing about it?"

"Goodbye, it was nice to meet you." → "It was good to meet you. Would you be open to a quick call next week? I'll send you something first."

Every pair carries the same information. So what does the right side add, and why does the conversation survive it, while the left side ends?

The moveWhat it doesWhat it sounds like
Add a second sentenceTurns a label into a conversation. The first sentence says the job; the second one is the interesting one."...and this year almost all of it has been foreign payment platforms."
Hand the ball backNever end on your own full stop. A short question keeps the other person in the conversation."Are you seeing it on your side?"
Signal the pivotTwo words that let you move from small talk to what you actually do, without it feeling like a sales call."Actually..." · "Speaking of which..."
Close with a reasonA goodbye ends the contact. An invitation keeps it, and it costs one sentence."Would you be open to a quick call next week?"
The forms underneathNothing new today. Present perfect for what has been happening this year, the present continuous for what is going on right now, a phrasal verb to reach out to somebody, and a question that hands the turn back. Every one of them is already yours.
RegisterContractions are correct here. "I'm", "it's been", "I'd love to" are what a native speaker uses at a coffee table. The full forms you write to a board sound cold in this room — and that is the only thing that changes.

The room is not testing your English. It is deciding whether you are worth twenty more minutes.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I am a lawyer. Tax."
"I'm a tax lawyer, and this year almost all of it has been foreign payment platforms. What about you?"
"Our firm was founded in 1998 and we have four offices and thirty partners and our practice covers..."
"We advise financial institutions on the reform. Are your clients running into it too?"
"It was very nice to meet you. Goodbye."
"It was good to meet you. Would you be open to a quick call next week? I'll send you a page first."

One word gives the silence back. A speech takes the room away. Two sentences and a question is the size that works — and a goodbye without an invitation is a conversation you will never finish.

Dialogue

Four Minutes at the Coffee Table

L
I don't think we've met. I'm Leonardo Constantino, a partner at ALMA in São Paulo.
E
Erin Delgado. Good to meet you. That last session was longer than my flight.
L
It was. What brings you to this conference?
E
Mergers and acquisitions, mostly. Half my deals this year have a Brazilian entity somewhere in them, and I don't understand what is happening to your tax rules.
L
Then we have common ground, because that is most of what I do. Tax, financial institutions, and this year almost all of it has been the reform.
E
So people keep telling me. Is it as bad as they say?
L
It's a lot of work, and it isn't a disaster. Speaking of which, we've been seeing exactly your problem with payment platforms coming into Brazil. They price a deal on the old rules and sign it under the new ones.
E
That is what happened to us in March. Which firm did you say you were with?
L
ALMA. Aguiar Lisboa Monteiro, in São Paulo. Actually, you may know Renata Sicoli. She was on the panel this morning.
E
Renata? She was my co-counsel on a deal in 2023. Small world.
L
It is. I'd love to continue this conversation. Would you be open to a quick call next week?
E
I would, but send me something first. If I get one page on what the reform does to a foreign buyer, I can put it in front of my partners.
L
You'll have it on Tuesday. Let me give you my details, and I'll send the follow-up with that page attached.
Comprehension

About Erin

1. Why is she at this conference?
Half of her deals this year have a Brazilian entity in them, and she does not understand what is happening to the Brazilian tax rules.
2. What happened to one of her deals in March?
Exactly what Leonardo describes: it was priced on the old rules and signed under the new ones.
3. How does she know Renata Sicoli?
Renata was her co-counsel on a deal in 2023.
4. What does she want before the call, and what will she do with it?
One page on what the reform does to a foreign buyer, so that she can put it in front of her partners.
Listening

The Call Before the Flight

A colleague tells you how the conference really works. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Where does she say the conference actually happens?
Not in the sessions: in the twenty minutes between them, at the coffee tables, and at the reception on the first evening, which she tells him not to skip.
2. What does she say you have to be, instead of interesting?
Curious. The best person she ever watched asked questions for forty minutes, gave one answer, and had three firms call him afterwards.
3. What is her rule about how many people to talk to, and what do you do that same night?
Four real conversations beat forty business cards; write one line about each of them on your phone the same night, and email them on the Tuesday.
Listening 2

The Second Sentence

Why nobody at a conference is judging your English. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does he say people at a conference are actually deciding?
Not whether your English is good: whether you are worth twenty more minutes of their afternoon.
2. What has to be in the second sentence about what you do?
The interesting part. Anybody can say "I am a tax lawyer"; the second sentence is the one that makes a stranger lean in.
3. What does he say the real point is, and what makes the difference?
The follow-up, not the conversation. A short email on the Tuesday with one line about what you discussed — the people who are good at this are just better at Tuesday.

The Real Thing

Read This Email

Tuesday, 9:12
Great to meet you at the conference
Leonardo Constantino
ALMA · Financial Institutions
ToErin Delgado, Mergers and Acquisitions, New York
WhereHi Erin, it was good to meet you at the coffee table on Monday, between the transfer pricing sessions.
Her lineYou mentioned the deal you priced on the old rules and signed under the new ones. That is the exact problem we have been working on all year.
AttachedOne page on what the reform does to a foreign buyer. Short enough for your partners to read before a meeting.
The askWould Thursday or Friday work for a short call? Renata sends her regards. Best, Leonardo
One page attached. One question asked. Sent two days after the conversation, not two weeks.
1. Why does the email repeat something Erin said, instead of describing the firm?
Because it proves he listened, and it puts her problem, not his profile, at the centre. A firm description could have been sent to anybody.
2. Why does it end with two dates instead of "let me know if you are interested"?
Because a specific question is easy to answer in one line, and "let me know" leaves all the work with her.

Chapter 6: Your Turn

A Stranger and a Coffee

From guided to free — the pitch, the approach, then the whole floor

Role-Play

So, What Do You Do?

Scenario: Somebody you have just met asks what you do. Give the whole pitch out loud in sixty seconds: who you are, what ALMA does, what makes it different, and one problem your clients are dealing with this year. Finish with a question back.

Keyword chips:

partner at ALMA banks and payment companies the tax reform What about you?
Role-Play

The Person Standing Alone

Scenario: A compliance director at a European bank is standing alone near the coffee. She gives short answers and she is not going to help you. Approach her, find the common ground, and leave with a reason to talk again.

Keywords:

I don't think we've met What brings you here? Speaking of which Would you be open to a quick call?
Free Practice

Four Approaches, No Script

Scenario: The reception on the first evening. You will meet four people, one after another. With each of them: open, ask what brought them there, find the overlap with your practice, and leave with a reason to send something. Use real files of yours, and do not name the clients.

No keywords. Two sentences and a question every time, and nobody leaves without a follow-up.
