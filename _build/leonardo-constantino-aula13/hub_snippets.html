Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Mingle -- to move around a room talking to several different people, one after another
"I gave myself twenty minutes to mingle before the first session started."
Approach -- to walk up to somebody you do not know and start talking to them
"She approached me at the coffee table and asked what had brought me to the conference."
Initiate -- to be the one who starts something, instead of waiting for the other person
"Somebody has to initiate the conversation, and it may as well be you."
Mutual connection -- a person that you and the other person both know
"It turned out we had a mutual connection at a firm in Lisbon."
Elevator pitch -- a short description of what you do, short enough to give while you walk
"My elevator pitch is two sentences: who we advise, and what is keeping us busy this year."
Common ground -- something two people share, which gives them a reason to keep talking
"We found common ground in about a minute: her clients have the same problem mine do."
Follow-up -- the message you send after a meeting to keep the contact alive
"The conversation was good, but the follow-up is what turns it into work."
Rapport -- an easy, comfortable relationship between people who have just met
"You build rapport by listening, not by talking about yourself."
Break the ice -- to say something that makes the first moments with a stranger less uncomfortable
"The panel ran late, which broke the ice for everybody in the queue."
Exchange contact details -- to give somebody your email or number and take theirs
"We exchanged contact details before the second session started."
Stay in touch -- to keep contact with somebody over time, long after you first met
"We met in Lisbon in 2022 and we have stayed in touch ever since."
Small world -- what you say when you find an unexpected connection between you and a stranger
"She was my co-counsel on a deal in 2023. Small world."
Touch base -- to make short contact with somebody, usually to keep the relationship warm
"I try to touch base with her twice a year, even when there is no deal."
Reach out to -- to contact somebody first, before they contact you
"I will reach out to her on Tuesday with one page attached."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

The sessions are not where anything happens. Everything happens in the twenty minutes between them, and Leonardo has twenty of them. He does not wait to be introduced, because nobody is going to introduce him. He approaches a woman standing alone at the coffee table and initiates the conversation himself: "I don't think we've met. I'm Leonardo Constantino, a partner at ALMA in São Paulo." Erin Delgado says her name and complains about the last session, and that complaint breaks the ice for both of them. Then he asks the only question that matters at this point: "What brings you to this conference?" She does mergers and acquisitions, half her deals this year have a Brazilian entity in them, and she does not understand what is happening to the tax rules. There is the common ground, and it arrived in under a minute, because he asked instead of talking. His answer is two sentences, not ten. "Tax, financial institutions, and this year almost all of it has been the reform." That second sentence is the elevator pitch, and it is the one that makes her lean in. When she asks whether it is as bad as people say, he does not lecture. He pivots: "Speaking of which, we've been seeing exactly your problem with payment platforms coming into Brazil." Two minutes later they discover a mutual connection, a lawyer who was her co-counsel in 2023 and who spoke on the panel that morning. "Small world," she says. It is, and it is also why the last move works. He does not say goodbye. He asks: "I'd love to continue this conversation. Would you be open to a quick call next week?" She agrees, but she wants one page first, and he gives her a date. On Tuesday he sends the follow-up: one line about what she said, one page attached, one question about two dates. The conversation was the easy part. The rapport was built by listening, and the work came from Tuesday.

1. "This year almost all of it has been the reform." Why does that sentence work better than "I am a tax lawyer"?
A Because it is longer, and longer answers always sound more professional.
B Because it says what is happening right now, which gives the other person something to react to.
C Because it avoids naming his profession, which is safer with a stranger.
2. Why does "Speaking of which" work at that moment in the conversation?
A Because it connects what she just said to what he does, so the move to his practice does not sound like a sales call.
B Because it is a polite way of changing the subject to something unrelated.
C Because it signals that the conversation is now over.
3. He asks "What brings you to this conference?" instead of talking about ALMA. What does that buy him?
A Time, because it delays the moment when he has to speak English.
B The common ground, because her answer tells him which part of his practice is relevant to her.
C Nothing in particular: it is only a polite formula.
4. Why is the Tuesday email the part that decides everything?
A Because a written message is always more professional than a spoken conversation.
B Because it lets him correct anything he said badly at the conference.
C Because a conversation with no follow-up is a nice afternoon, and the follow-up is what turns it into a relationship.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Discourse Management in Social Register

GRAMMAR

This lesson adds no new structure. Everything below is a form you already have, used in the one register the program has not drilled yet: unscripted, standing up, with a stranger. What is new is not the grammar. It is who moves the conversation, and how you keep it alive.

The moveWhat it doesWhat you say
OpenNames yourself and your firm before anybody has to ask."I don't think we've met. I'm Leonardo Constantino, a partner at ALMA."
Ask firstTheir answer tells you which part of your practice is worth mentioning."What brings you to this conference?"
The second sentenceThe first sentence is the job title. The second one is the one that starts a conversation."...and this year almost all of it has been foreign payment platforms."
Signal the pivotTwo words that let you move from small talk to your practice without it sounding like a sales call."Actually..." · "Speaking of which..."
Hand the ball backNever end on your own full stop. A short question keeps the other person in the room with you."What about you?" · "Are you seeing it on your side?"
Close with a reasonA goodbye ends the contact. An invitation keeps it, and it costs one sentence."Would you be open to a quick call next week?"
ContractionsIn this register they are correct, not careless. "I'm", "it's been", "I'd love to", "we've been seeing" are what a native speaker uses standing at a coffee table. The full forms you write to a board sound cold here.
The forms underneathNothing new. Present perfect for what has been happening this year ("it has been a busy year"), present continuous for what is going on now ("we are seeing a lot of that"), a phrasal verb to reach out to somebody, and a question that gives the turn back. All of them are already yours.
The follow-upThe conversation is not the point. One line about what they said, one page attached, one question with two dates in it, sent two days later. That is the whole difference between a nice afternoon and a client.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each real conversation.

Somebody at the coffee table asks: "So, what do you do?" The best answer is:
A "I am a lawyer. Tax."
B "I'm a tax lawyer, and this year almost all of it has been foreign payment platforms. What about you?"
C "Our firm was founded in 1998 and we have four offices and thirty partners, and our practice covers indirect tax, transfer pricing..."
She has just told you that half her deals involve a Brazilian entity. You want to move to what you do. You say:
A "Let me tell you about our firm and the services we offer."
B "Speaking of which, we've been seeing exactly that with payment platforms coming into Brazil."
C "That is interesting. Anyway, the weather in New York is better than I expected."
The conversation is ending and she has to get to the next session. What do you say?
A "It was very nice to meet you. Goodbye."
B "Let me know if you are ever interested in working with us."
C "It was good to meet you. Would you be open to a quick call next week? I'll send you a page first."
You are writing the follow-up email two days after the conference. Which opening line is the strongest?
A "It was good to meet you on Monday. You mentioned the deal you priced on the old rules and signed under the new ones."
B "Please find attached our firm profile for your consideration."
C "I am writing to introduce myself and my practice areas in some detail."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Somebody at an international conference has just asked you what you do. Answer now, in sixty seconds and without reading: who you are, what ALMA does, what makes it different, and one problem your clients are dealing with this year. Make the second sentence the interesting one, and finish by handing a question back to them.
