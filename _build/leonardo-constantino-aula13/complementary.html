Series
Inventing Anna -- Season 1, Episode 1 "Life of a VIP"

A woman with no track record walks into rooms full of bankers, lawyers and hotel managers, and each of them ends up believing she belongs there. Watch the mechanics, not the ethics: the approach, the mutual connection dropped at exactly the right second, the two-sentence pitch, and the question that hands the conversation back. Connection to Lesson 13: every move you practised in class, performed at professional speed by somebody whose whole plan depends on it.

Tip: pick one party scene and write down the first three sentences she says to a stranger. Then say your own version of each, at a tax conference, about ALMA.

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart -- "Quick Thinks: Don't Sweat the Small Talk"

A short Stanford episode on the part of professional life that has no agenda: the drinks reception, the queue for coffee, the two minutes before a meeting starts. It explains why small talk is a skill and not a personality trait, and it gives you the questions that keep a conversation alive when you have nothing in common yet. Connection to Lesson 13: this is the warm-up material for the twenty minutes between the sessions.

Tip: listen twice. The second time, write down every question the host suggests, and turn three of them into questions you could ask a stranger at a tax conference.

Derek Callan -- "How to Start a Conversation at a Conference"

Exactly the situation of this lesson, in about ten minutes: how to open with somebody you have never met, which questions work at an event and which ones close the conversation, and how to leave without it feeling abrupt. Every phrase is given at natural professional speed. Connection to Lesson 13: the same four moves you drilled in class, with a few extra openings you can steal.

Tip: pause after each phrase and repeat it out loud with your own details, so ALMA and the reform are already in the sentence before you need it.
