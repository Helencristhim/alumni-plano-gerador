1. Say the first two lines as one sentence each, with used to.

Chapter 4: The Code

Three Words, and the D That Disappears

What was true for years, and is not any more

Grammar Discovery

Which One Is Not Like the Others?

"He used to know every judge in the district by name."

"He did not use to read contracts in English."

"Did you use to travel as much as you do now?"

"In 2011 the mill signed its first export contract."

The red one keeps its D. In the blue ones the D is gone — something else is carrying the past. And the green one refuses the whole structure. Ask yourself why before you click.

JobFormExample
A habit or a state that endedused to + bare verbHe used to know every judge by name.

Verbs of state are welcome hereused to know, used to have, used to beThe firm used to be three rooms above a bakery.
The two trapsnever did you used to, and never used to for something that happened once

In one line: used to says it was true for years and is not any more — and the moment did turns up, the D goes away.

Listening 1 · The Calendar

One Diary That Fitted in a Pocket

A lawyer describing the change through her working day. Sound first — no text.

1. What did her working day use to look like?
One diary that fitted in her pocket: meetings in the morning, court in the afternoon, and nothing on a Sunday. She did not use to think about time zones at all.
2. When do her first and last messages arrive now, and from where?
The first at seven, from Rotterdam, where somebody has already had lunch; the last after ten, from Singapore.
3. What does she say is difficult, and what are the two feelings she allows herself?
The work is not more difficult — the difficult part is that it never stops arriving. She does not miss the old caseload, and she does miss the old calendar.
Listening 2 · The Profession

Learning the Words Is Finite

Thirty years of a profession, and who made the change well. Sound first — no text.

1. What did a commodity lawyer in this country use to be, thirty years ago?
A specialist in Brazilian law who occasionally saw a document from abroad. He says that job does not really exist any more.
2. What moved, and what does the same lawyer have to do today?
The contracts moved, the arbitration moved and the language of both moved with them. Today the lawyer has to keep up with three legal systems instead of one.
3. Who made the change well, and why?
Almost never the youngest. The ones who already knew the business by heart and only had to learn the words — because learning the words is slow but finite, and learning the business takes twenty years.
