Stage 1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Four words for the measurement, four for the market around it. Listen to each one and read it in context.

baseline -- the level of emissions there would be without the project
"If the baseline is wrong, every number after it is wrong too."
additionality -- the proof that the reduction would not have happened anyway
"Additionality is hard because you are proving something about a world that does not exist."
verification body -- the independent organisation that checks the project and signs the credits
"We may choose the verification body, but it must be accredited."
monitoring report -- the document that shows, year after year, what the project actually did
"Under the standard, we have to submit the monitoring report every twelve months."
emissions -- the gases a company releases into the atmosphere
"Companies above the threshold must report their emissions annually."
compliance threshold -- the number above which the rule starts to apply to you
"Below the compliance threshold, the obligation simply does not exist."
land use -- what a piece of land is used for: crops, cattle, forest or building
"A change of land use is what creates the credit, and also what creates the dispute."
carbon market -- the place where credits are bought and sold, with a price
"The carbon market moves with policy, not only with demand."

Stage 3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about what it says.

Somebody in a field asks you a question and a crew is waiting. What you say next has to do two things that most people do at once, and that a lawyer has to keep apart: say what the rule requires, and say what you would do.

English separates them with five small verbs. Must and have to carry obligation: the baseline must be established before any planting, and under the standard we have to submit the monitoring report every twelve months. The difference between the two is where the obligation comes from, and in practice it is small: have to usually points outside, to the standard or the law, and must often carries the speaker's own authority.

Must not and cannot carry prohibition. Existing forest must not be cleared, and no softer word will do, because a prohibition said softly is heard as advice. And here is the trap that matters most in a legal sentence: do not have to is not must not. "You do not have to send it monthly" means there is no obligation. "You must not send it monthly" means it is forbidden. Two opposite meanings, one small word apart.

May and can carry permission. The project may appoint any accredited verification body; the rule allows it and the choice is yours. And should carries recommendation: you should wait for the measurement. Nobody breaks a rule by ignoring a should — which is exactly why the word has to be used honestly, and never to dress a prohibition in polite clothes.

One more, for what you do not control. Might is possibility: the price on the carbon market might fall before the credits are sold. It is not a hedge and it is not weakness. It is precision about a thing nobody can promise.

Stage 4: Grammar Tip -- modals of obligation, permission and recommendation

GRAMMAR

Five small verbs, five different weights. In a legal answer, choosing the wrong one does not change the style: it changes the content.

VerbWeightExample
mustobligation, often the speaker's own authority or a written ruleThe baseline must be established first.
have toobligation that comes from outside: standard, law, clientWe have to submit the report every year.
must not / cannotprohibitionExisting forest must not be cleared.
may / canpermission: the rule allows it and the decision is yoursYou may appoint any accredited body.
shouldrecommendation: advice, and no rule is broken by ignoring itYou should wait for the measurement.
mightpossibility that nobody controlsThe price might fall before we sell.
The trapdo not have to is NOT must not. The first says there is no obligation; the second says it is forbidden.
The formno to after must, may, should or might, and no -s in the third person: "the company must report", never "musts to report". Only have to changes: he has to.

Quick test before you speak: obligation, prohibition, permission, recommendation or possibility?

Stage 8: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Real situations from your week. Only one option is both correct and natural.

A You should not clear the existing forest.
B You must not clear the existing forest.
C You do not have to clear the existing forest.
D You might not clear the existing forest.
2. There is no obligation to send the report monthly. How do you say it?
A You must not send the report monthly.
B You do not have to send the report monthly.
C You cannot send the report monthly.
D You should not send the report monthly.
3. Which sentence is correctly formed?
A The company musts report its emissions every year.
B The company must to report its emissions every year.
C The company must report its emissions every year.
D The company have to reports its emissions every year.
4. A manager asks whether the price of a credit will fall before the sale. What do you answer?
A It must fall, because the carbon market always moves.
B It should fall, because policy is changing.
