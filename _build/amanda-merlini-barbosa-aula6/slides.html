Chapter 3: The Question from the Field

A Crew Is Waiting

A phone call from the site, line by line

Dialogue

The Call from the Site

B
Amanda, it is Bruno. I am on the site and I have a crew waiting. Two questions, and I need the rule, not an opinion.
A
Go ahead. I will tell you what the rule says first, and what I recommend afterwards.
B
First one. Do we have to measure the area before we plant, or can that wait?
A
You have to measure first. The baseline must be established before any planting, and it must be done by the verification body.
B
Understood. Second one. The old trees at the edge. The crew says clearing them would save two days.
A
No. Existing forest must not be cleared, and the permit is explicit about it. That one is not a judgement call.
B
And the timing? The rain starts in six weeks and the certification people have not confirmed a date.
A
There the rule allows you to start. You may plant before the certificate arrives. But you should wait for the measurement, because without it the credits might not count.
B
So: measure first, do not touch the old trees, and after that it is my decision.
A
Exactly. And I will put those three lines in writing today, so that nobody has to remember this call in two years.
Comprehension

About the Call

1. What does Bruno say he needs, and what does he say he does not want?
He needs the rule, not an opinion, because he has a crew waiting.
2. What has to happen before any planting, and who has to do it?
The baseline has to be established, and the verification body has to do it.
3. On the timing, what does the rule allow and what is only advice?
The rule allows planting before the certificate arrives. Waiting for the measurement is advice, because without it the credits might not count.
Chapter 4: The Code

Five Small Verbs, Five Different Weights

must · have to · cannot · may · should

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The baseline must be established before any planting."

"Existing forest must not be cleared."

"You may plant before the certificate arrives."

"You should wait for the measurement."

In which of these does the listener still have a choice?

VerbWhat it doesExample
mustobligation, usually from a written rule or from the speaker's own authorityThe baseline must be established first.
have toobligation that comes from outside: the standard, the law, the clientWe have to submit the report every year.
must not / cannotprohibition: there is no choice, and the answer is noExisting forest must not be cleared.
may / canpermission: the rule allows it, and the decision is yoursYou may choose the verification body.
shouldrecommendation: it is advice, and nobody breaks a rule by ignoring itYou should wait for the measurement.
The trapdo not have to is NOT must not. "You do not have to send it monthly" means there is no obligation. "You must not send it monthly" means it is forbidden. A lawyer cannot confuse the two.

Sound Check

Say It Clearly

must, must not and have to · the sentences of this lesson

Why this one matters more than usual

The sound: before another consonant, the t of must almost disappears. "Must be" becomes one word, and "must not" becomes a single short block.

The risk: if the two forms sound the same in your mouth, the listener hears a prohibition where you meant an obligation, or the opposite. In a legal answer that is not an accent problem, it is a content problem.

The pair to keep: "you must not send it" and "you do not have to send it" mean opposite things, and they sound nothing alike. Say both, twice.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

The Field and the Room

A question with a crew waiting, and the three words that decide everything

Listening 1

A Voice Message from the Site

Bruno records his two questions before calling. Sound first, no text.

1. What does the planting crew want to do with the old trees, and why?
Clear them, because they are few and not healthy and it would be faster.
2. What is the timing problem, in one sentence?
The certification has no confirmed date and the rain starts in six weeks, so waiting costs a season and planting early may cost the credits.
3. What does Bruno say he is not asking for?
He is not asking her to guess. He wants what the rule says, and then her recommendation.
Listening 2

The Three Words That Decide Everything

The second part of the technical session. Sound first, no text.

1. Why does the speaker say a wrong baseline ruins everything?
Because everything the project claims is measured against it, so every number after it is wrong too.
2. Why is additionality called the hardest of the three?
Because you are proving something about a world that does not exist.
3. What is the part that surprises lawyers, and why should it not?
That none of the three is mainly a scientific question: all three are questions of evidence, which is a lawyer's job.
Real Document

An Extract from the Standard

CERTIFICATION STANDARD · EXTRACT
internal reading note · sample text
The baseline shall be established before any activity on the site.obligation
Existing native forest shall not be cleared at any stage.prohibition
The project may appoint any accredited verification body.permission
Planting is not required to wait for the certificate.no obligation
Projects are encouraged to align planting with the measurement.recommendation
A monitoring report shall be submitted every twelve months.obligation
Sample text written for this lesson. It is not an extract from a real standard.
1. Rewrite row one and row two with a modal, out loud.
"The baseline must be established before any activity on the site." "Existing native forest must not be cleared at any stage."
2. Row four and row five look similar. Why are they completely different in practice?
Row four says there is no obligation to wait, so planting early is allowed. Row five is only advice: ignoring it breaks no rule, but it may cost the credits.
3. Which of the six rows would you put first in your written note to Bruno, and why?
Row two, the prohibition, because it is the only one where a wrong decision cannot be repaired later.
