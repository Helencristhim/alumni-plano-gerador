Chapter 3: The Code

The Question Inside Another Sentence

Same information, different door

Grammar Discovery

Two Pairs, One Difference

When did they send the draft?

Could you tell me when they sent the draft?

Is the clause still in the final version?

I was wondering whether the clause is still in the final version.

Each pair asks for the same information. Look at what happens right after the question word: in one, the verb jumps in front of the subject. In the other, it does not.

JobFormExample
Direct questionverb before subjectWhen did they send it?
Indirect questionnormal order, no auxiliaryCould you tell me when they sent it?
Yes or no insideif / whetherI was wondering whether the clause is still there.
The openersCould you tell me... / Do you know... / I was wondering... / Would you mind...all take normal order after them
Would you mind+ verb-ingWould you mind repeating that?
Softer requestCould / Would, not CanCould you send it by Friday?
The trapnever keep the inversion insideNot Could you tell me when did they send it.

In one line: the moment the question goes inside another sentence, it stops looking like a question.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Call, at Full Speed

One bad line, two recordings, one calendar invitation

Dialogue

Thursday, Nine in the Morning

S
Morning. Before we start, let me run through the three points, because I have a hard stop at half past.
R
Please go ahead. Sorry, one thing first: your line is a little patchy here. If you disappear, I will call back.
S
Noted. So, the indemnity, the governing law, and the timetable. On the indemnity, our client will not accept the cap as drafted, and we sent a revised clause on Tuesday.
R
Sorry, you are breaking up a bit. Could you tell me when exactly you sent that revised clause?
S
Tuesday afternoon, to you and to the London team. Did it not arrive?
R
It may have. I was in a hearing all Tuesday. Let me check and get back to you before lunch. Would you mind copying our tax partner as well? I would like to loop him in on the indemnity.
S
Of course. And the timetable? We are still aiming to sign before the end of the month.
R
So, to confirm: I read the revised clause today, we loop in the tax partner, and the target is signature by month end. Have I got that right?
Comprehension

About Sarah

1. Why does Sarah want to go through the three points before anything else?
Because she has a hard stop at half past, so the whole agenda has to fit in the time she has.
2. What is her client refusing, and what did her side do about it?
Her client will not accept the indemnity cap as drafted. Her side sent a revised clause on Tuesday afternoon, to him and to the London team.
3. Sarah asks did it not arrive. What does that tell you about where she thinks the problem is?
That she thinks the email may have gone missing, not that he ignored it. She is offering him an explanation rather than asking why he did not reply.
Listening 1 · The Chair

Before We Start

Somebody opening a call with four time zones on the line. Sound first — no text.

1. How many items are on the agenda, and what does she say will happen to the third one?
Four items. The third one will not be solved today: it will only be scoped.
2. What is her first rule, and what does she say happens if you ignore it?
If the line breaks up, say so immediately. If you sit there hoping it improves, it never does, and the call loses ten minutes reconstructing what you missed.
3. What does she say she would rather have, and what is she trying to avoid?
She would rather have three people talking over each other than one person going quiet for an hour and then sending an email that reopens everything.
Listening 2 · Twenty Years of Calls

Asking Again Is Free

Somebody who has spent twenty years on calls in a second language. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does he say asking somebody to repeat costs nothing?
Because the other person has no idea whether you missed it because of your English or because their microphone is terrible, and nine times out of ten it is the microphone.
2. What does he call the expensive alternative, and what does it cost you later?
Nodding. The call ends and you are the one who has to reconstruct what was agreed from context, usually at eleven at night.
3. What is his rule, and where does he put the problem when he asks?
If he has not understood something twice, he stops and asks. He puts the problem on the line, not on the person: sorry, you are breaking up a bit, could you say that last part again.
Real Document

The Invitation That Arrives the Day Before

CALENDAR INVITATION
Project Meridian · contract review
WhenThursday, 9:00 – 9:40 (São Paulo)
Also13:00 London · 14:00 Milan · 08:00 New York
OrganizerSarah Whitfield
RequiredRodrigo Marques de França · 3 others
Agendaindemnity cap · governing law · timetable
Please review the revised indemnity clause beforehand; we will not have time to read it on the call. If you cannot make this slot, could you let me know by end of day today so that I can move it. Dial-in details below. Apologies for the short notice.
1. The note contains one indirect request. Which sentence is it, and what would the direct version sound like?
Could you let me know by end of day today. The direct version would be tell me by end of day today, which on a first call with four people would read as an instruction.
2. Why does the organizer ask everybody to read the clause before the call?
Because there will not be time to read it during the call: the slot is forty minutes for three items.
3. Look at the times. What is the practical problem this invitation is quietly solving?
Four time zones. Nine in São Paulo is eight in New York and two in the afternoon in Milan, so the slot is the only hour that works for everybody.

Chapter 6: Your Turn

The Call, Three Times

Guided > Semi-free > Free

Role-Play 1 of 3 — Guided

The Clause You Never Received

Scenario: The other side says they sent a revised clause and you never saw it. Ask when it was sent, ask them to copy your tax partner, say when you will come back to them, and confirm the whole thing at the end.

Keyword chips:

Could you tell me when... Would you mind get back to you to confirm
Role-Play 2 of 3 — Semi-free

Now You Are Running It

Scenario: You are chairing a forty-minute call and one participant has been speaking for three minutes about something that is not on the agenda. Open the call with the agenda, come in politely, bring the conversation back, and give the floor to somebody who has been silent.

Keywords:

let me run through May I chime in a hard stop
Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

Three Minutes, Bad Line

Scenario: A call about a deadline you are not going to meet. The line is bad, the other side speaks quickly, and one number matters. Get the number, explain the delay, propose a new date, and confirm what was agreed before you hang up.

No keywords, no notes, three minutes.
