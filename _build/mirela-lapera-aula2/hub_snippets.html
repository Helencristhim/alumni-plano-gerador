Just One More Episode -- Avoid Watching, Decide to Stop

Why series are so hard to stop, and how to talk about your viewing habits. Key words: to binge-watch, addictive, to get hooked on, a cliffhanger, a plot twist, a sitcom, a documentary, dubbed, subtitles, to rewatch. Structure: verb + -ing x verb + to.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To binge-watch -- to watch many episodes of a series one after another
"We binge-watched the whole season on Saturday."
Addictive -- so enjoyable that it is very hard to stop
"This documentary is really addictive."
To get hooked on -- to start liking something so much that you want more and more
"I got hooked on the series after one episode."
A cliffhanger -- an exciting end of an episode that makes you need to know what happens next
"Every episode ends with a cliffhanger."
A plot twist -- a surprising change in the story
"Nobody expected the plot twist in the last episode."
A sitcom -- a funny series about the same characters in everyday situations
"Friends is my favorite sitcom."
A documentary -- a film or series about real people and real events
"I’m watching a documentary about a famous family."
Dubbed -- with the voices changed into another language
"I usually watch series dubbed into Portuguese."
Subtitles -- the words of the dialogue written at the bottom of the screen
"Try English subtitles: you read and listen at the same time."
To rewatch -- to watch something again
"I never rewatch a series, except Friends."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Mirela loves series, but she avoids starting them during the week, because they are addictive. When she gets hooked on a story, she wants to finish it, and every episode ends with a cliffhanger. She only watches one series at a time, and she never rewatches anything.

Well, almost anything. She enjoys watching old episodes of two sitcoms: Friends and The Big Bang Theory. She can start any episode, and it always makes sense. But she stopped watching another famous series, because it was too long and the story stopped making sense.

Today she watches everything dubbed, with Portuguese subtitles. Her new plan? She has decided to try English audio with English subtitles, one episode at a time.

1. Why does the text say “she avoids starting” and not “she avoids to start”?
A Because the verb avoid is always followed by a verb with -ing.
B Because the sentence is in the past, and the past always takes the -ing form after a verb.
C Because starting is a noun here, and nouns never come after to.
2. What does “she stopped watching another famous series” mean?
A She paused for a short time so that she could watch that series.
B She watched the whole series again, from the first episode to the last one.
C She does not watch that series anymore.
3. What is her new plan?
A To stop watching series.
B To watch with English audio and English subtitles, one episode at a time.
C To rewatch all the sitcoms she knows, dubbed and with Portuguese subtitles, every night.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Verb + -ing x Verb + to

GRAMMAR

Some verbs are followed by another verb with -ing (enjoy, avoid, keep, finish, mind). Others are followed by to + verb (want, decide, need, plan, hope). After a preposition, always use -ing.

JobFormExample
Verb + -ingenjoy, avoid, finish, keep, mind + -ingI avoid watching series at night.
Verb + towant, decide, need, plan, hope, would like + toI decided to stop after one episode.
After a prepositionof, about, at, before, after + -ingI’m tired of waiting for the next season.
Stop + -ingstop doing something: you do not do it anymoreI stopped watching This Is Us.
Stop + tostop to do something: you pause in order to do itI stopped to make some popcorn.
The trapavoid and enjoy never take toNot I avoid to watch. Say I avoid watching.

Never: I avoid to watch · I enjoy to watch · I decided stop · I’m tired of watch
