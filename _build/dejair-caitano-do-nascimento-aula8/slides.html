Chapter 3: The Code

The Same Request, Four Levels

Can, could, should, might

Discover the Rule

Put Them in Order

Can you repeat that?
Could you repeat that, please?
I might have misunderstood — did you say the second group?
You might want to check the follow-up in that paper.
Click to reveal the rule
All four are modals, and after every one of them comes the plain verb: could you repeat, never could you to repeat. Can is direct and fine among colleagues. Could is the safe choice in a room full of strangers. Might takes the blame off the other person: I might have misunderstood means the problem could be mine. And you might want to is how you give advice to somebody more senior without telling them what to do.

Chapter 4: In the Room

The Two Worst Cases

A very long question, and a heavy accent with an offer inside it

Listening 1

One Very Long Question

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What is the core of her question, in one short sentence?
Whether the effect is really caused by the treatment, or whether it is just the natural course of the disease.
2. What happened in her own group at about eighteen months?
The difference tends to disappear at around eighteen months.
3. What is she really suggesting you should have done?
Continued the follow-up for longer, to see whether the same thing happens.

Sorry, I Did Not Catch That

O
Thanks for that. I wondered whether the effect you describe is driven by the treatment at all, or whether it might just be the natural course of the disease.
D
Sorry, I did not catch the second part. Could you say it again, a little more slowly?
O
Of course. Is it the treatment, or is it just the disease getting better on its own?
D
In other words, you are asking about the control group.
O
Exactly that.
D
Good. I might have misunderstood earlier, so let me be clear: we do have a control group, but it is small.
O
How small?
D
Twenty-two patients. So I would rather say the effect is probable than proven, and that is why we are extending the follow-up.
O
That is a fair answer. Thank you.
D
Thank you for the question. Could we talk after the session? I would like your view on the follow-up.
Comprehension

About Olivia

1. What is she really asking about?
The control group -- whether the effect comes from the treatment or from the disease getting better on its own.
2. How does she react when he asks her to repeat?
She says "of course" and says it again in a much shorter, simpler way.
3. What does she say about his answer at the end?
That it is a fair answer, and she thanks him.
Chapter 5: Practice

Not Fast. Heavy

And there is an offer hidden inside it

Listening 2

An Offer You Cannot Afford to Miss

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What did his centre compare instead of healthy people?
Patients from the same clinic who did not receive the treatment, because they arrived later.
2. What problem does that solve?
It removes the difference between the hospitals.
3. What is he offering you, and what does he say about the paper?
To send you the paper. It is in a small journal and not well known outside his country, but the method is simple and you could use it directly.

Free

Three Questions, No Pause

Question time after your talk. Three people ask you three questions, one after the other, at different speeds and with different accents. Answer all three. Repair whenever you need to, check the core before you answer, and never apologise for your English.
