Lesson 6 -- What Was Done, and In What Order

Documentary
Human Nature (2019)

Ninety minutes on gene editing, and the useful part for you is not the ethics. It is the number of scenes in which a working scientist stands at a bench and says out loud what was done, in what order, to what. The English is unscripted and the pace is normal, which is the point.

Tip: pick any one procedure somebody describes and write it down afterwards as four sentences of a methods section, with nobody named. Then check that a reader could tell what came first.

Podcast
Short Wave — A cooler of eggs and a bold plan to save leopard sharks

Thirteen minutes, and underneath the story there is a complete procedure: eggs are collected, transported, hatched, the pups are raised, and only then are they released. Everything you worked on today is in there, spoken instead of printed.

Tip: listen once for the story. Then listen again and write the steps in order, six lines, every one of them starting with the thing rather than the person.

Video
Veritasium — Is Most Published Research Wrong?

Twelve minutes on why results that look solid turn out not to repeat. It is the argument behind today’s lesson, told with examples, and it explains why a methods section is judged on whether somebody else can run it rather than on whether it sounds convincing.

Tip: watch with English subtitles on. Stop at any point where a study is described and say, out loud, what was done and in what order, before he tells you.
