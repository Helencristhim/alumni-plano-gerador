Chapter 4: The Code

Who, Which, Whose, Where

Two sentences, one smooth sentence

Grammar Discovery

Join the Ideas

"Marco is the winemaker who showed us the cellar."

"Barolo, which is made from Nebbiolo, ages for years."

"This is the room where we tasted the wine."

"Marco, whose grandfather planted the vineyard, opened a bottle."

Each red word starts a relative clause: a piece of information about the word just before it. Some clauses tell us which one we mean (no commas). Others only add extra information (between commas), and the sentence still works without them.

WordUseExample
whofor peopleThe winemaker who showed us the cellar.
whichfor thingsBarolo, which is made from Nebbiolo...
whosepossession: his, her, theirMarco, whose grandfather planted the vineyard...
wherefor placesThe room where we tasted the wine.
Defining (no commas)tells us which one; that is possible; the pronoun can go when it is the objectThe wine (that) we tasted was a 2016.
Non-defining (commas)extra information; never that; never omittedNot “Barolo, that is from Piedmont...” Say Barolo, which is from Piedmont...

In one line: who = people; which = things; whose = possession; where = places; commas = extra information, and no that.

Listening 1 · An Italian Winemaker

Four Generations, One Hill

An Italian winemaker talks about the vineyard that her great-grandfather planted in Piedmont. Sound first — no text.

1. Who planted the vineyard?
Her great-grandfather.
2. Why is the fog important?
It keeps the grapes cool, and the Nebbiolo grape needs a long, slow season.
3. What does she say a good Barolo needs?
Time: years in the cellar.
Listening 2 · A British Wine Writer

How to Read a Wine List

A British wine writer gives advice for choosing a wine at a business dinner. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the first thing she looks for on a wine list?
The region and the vintage.
2. What does she do when she doesn’t know a wine?
She asks the sommelier for a wine that pairs with the food.
3. What does she never do at a business dinner?
Choose the most expensive bottle.
Listening 3 · An Australian Winemaker

From the Barossa to the World

An Australian winemaker explains why the Barossa Valley is famous, and what he learned in Italy. Sound first — no text.

1. What grape is the Barossa Valley famous for?
Shiraz.
2. Where did he work for one year?
In a small winery in Piedmont, in Italy.
3. What did he learn there?
Patience: to let the wine age and not to sell it too early.
