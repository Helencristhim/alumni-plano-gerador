Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A vineyard -- a piece of land where grapes are grown to make wine
"The vineyard is on a hill that faces the south."
A grape -- a small round fruit that grows in groups and is used to make wine
"Barolo is made from only one grape: Nebbiolo."
A winemaker -- a person whose job is to make wine
"The winemaker, whose family has lived there for a century, showed us the cellar."
The harvest -- the time of the year when the grapes are picked
"The harvest usually starts in October."
A vintage -- the year in which the grapes for a wine were picked
"Which vintage would you recommend?"
A cellar -- a cool room under a building where wine is kept
"We went down to the cellar, where it was cold and quiet."
A barrel -- a large round wooden container used to keep and age wine
"The wine stays in the barrel for at least two years."
To age -- to keep a wine for a long time so that it becomes better
"Barolo must age for at least three years before it is sold."
Full-bodied -- strong and rich in taste, not light
"It is a full-bodied red, so it goes well with meat."
To pair -- to choose a food and a wine that taste good together
"We paired the Barolo with a white truffle risotto."
A label -- a piece of paper on a bottle with information about the wine
"The label shows the producer, the region and the vintage."
A sip -- a very small amount of a drink that you take into your mouth
"He took a sip and smiled."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last autumn, Julius visited a small winery in Piedmont which belongs to an old family. The winemaker, whose grandfather planted the first vineyard, met him at the door. It was the end of the harvest, and the air smelled of grapes.

They went down to the cellar, where hundreds of barrels were waiting in the dark. “This is the wine that we will sell in four years,” the winemaker said. “Barolo needs to age.”

At dinner, he opened a 2016 vintage, which was full-bodied and elegant. They paired it with a risotto. Julius took a sip, read the label and asked to buy six bottles.

1. Who planted the first vineyard?
A Julius, during his first business trip to the north of Italy many years before this visit.
B A Japanese company.
C The grandfather of the winemaker.
2. Why does the text say “the cellar, where hundreds of barrels were waiting”?
A Because where introduces a place, and the commas add extra information about the cellar.
B Because where is the only word that we can use after a noun in a story about wine or about food.
C Because the cellar is a person.
3. What did Julius do at the end?
A He went back to the cellar and stayed there with the winemaker until the end of the harvest.
B He asked to buy six bottles.
C He refused the wine.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Relative Clauses

GRAMMAR

Who is for people, which for things, whose for possession and where for places. A defining clause tells us which one (no commas; that is possible). A non-defining clause adds extra information (commas; never that).

WordUseExample
whofor peopleThe winemaker who showed us the cellar.
whichfor thingsBarolo, which is made from Nebbiolo...
whosepossession: his, her, theirMarco, whose grandfather planted the vineyard...
wherefor placesThe room where we tasted the wine.
Defining (no commas)tells us which one; that is possible; the pronoun can go when it is the objectThe wine (that) we tasted was a 2016.
Non-defining (commas)extra information; never that; never omittedNot “Barolo, that is from Piedmont...” Say Barolo, which is from Piedmont...

Never: Barolo, that is from Piedmont... · The winemaker which... · Marco, who’s grandfather... · The restaurant where we had dinner there...
