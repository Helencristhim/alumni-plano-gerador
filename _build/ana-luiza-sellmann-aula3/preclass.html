Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

What Have You Been Up To? -- The Story Still Running

Talking about what has been happening lately and what has not stopped yet. Key words: to catch up, for ages, exhausting, on and off, to keep up with, worth it, a deadline, to coordinate. Structure: present perfect continuous (have been plus the -ing form) against the present perfect simple, with for and since.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. Do not translate — the example sentence is the meaning.

To catch up -- to talk to someone after a long time apart
"We've been catching up for two hours and we are not done."
For ages -- for a very long time
"I haven't seen him for ages."
Exhausting -- so tiring that you have nothing left
"It's been an exhausting week, but a good one."
On and off -- sometimes yes, sometimes no, over a long period
"I've been painting on and off since January."
To keep up with -- to move at the same speed as something or someone
"I can't keep up with all the series everybody is watching."
Worth it -- good enough for the time or the money it costs
"It's been hard, but it's worth it."
A deadline -- the day something must be finished
"We've been chasing the same deadline all week."
To coordinate -- to organize different parts so they work together
"I've been coordinating with the venue team all week."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

It has been a strange month. I have been working on the same project since March and we have been chasing the same deadline the whole time. I have been coordinating with three different teams, which is exhausting, and the client has not answered a single email since Tuesday. Nobody has missed the deadline yet, and I would like to keep it that way.

Outside work, life has been quieter. I have been painting on and off since January and I have finished exactly two small pictures, which is not much, but it is worth it. I have not been sleeping very well, and I have not been keeping up with any of the series everybody talks about. My friend Marcus has been living in Berlin for two years and we have not spoken for ages. I have known him since university, so a phone call is long overdue.

1. Why does the text say "I have been working on the same project since March"?
A Because the work started in March and it is still going on now.
B Because the project finished in March.
C Because March is a month, and months always take been plus the -ing form.
2. The text says "I have finished exactly two small pictures" and not "I have been finishing". Why?
A Because painting is a hobby, and hobbies never take been plus -ing.
B Because the point is the result you can count: two finished pictures.
C Because the sentence has a number in it, and numbers block the continuous.
3. Why is it "I have known him since university" and never "I have been knowing him"?
A Because university was a long time ago.
B Because they have not spoken for ages.
C Because know is on the short list of verbs that never take the -ing form here.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Is It Over, or Still Going?

GRAMMAR

One question, two answers. That is the whole decision in this lesson.

FormUseExample
Present perfect continuous
have / has been + -ing		An action that started in the past and is still running now. The time and the effort are the point.I have been working here since March.
We have been waiting all week.
Present perfect simple
have / has + participle		A finished result you can count or show.I have written three emails.
We have finished the deck.
Negativehave not been or has not been plus the -ing form.I have not been sleeping well.
She hasn't been answering.
QuestionThe most natural way to ask about somebody's life right now.What have you been up to?
How long have you been living here?
for and sincefor plus a length of time: for two years, for ages, for a week. Since plus a starting point: since March, since Tuesday, since university. Never "since two years".
The short listThese verbs stay simple even when the situation continues: know, be, have, like, love, want, understand. Say "I have known him for years", never "I have been knowing".

If you are not sure, ask one thing: has it stopped? If it has not, add been and -ing. And if you pick the other one by mistake, the person still understands you perfectly — this is a choice about naturalness, not about being right.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Someone is catching up with you. Choose the best thing to say.

A friend you have not seen in two years asks how life has been. You answer:
A "I've been working a lot, honestly. It's been exhausting."
B "I am working a lot since two years. It is exhaustive."
C "I work a lot from long time ago."
You want to say the wait started on Tuesday and is not over. You say:
A "We are waiting for the client since Tuesday."
B "We've been waiting for the client since Tuesday."
C "We wait for the client from Tuesday."
Your manager asks how many emails you sent this morning. The best answer is:
A "I've been writing three emails."
B "I've written three emails so far."
C "I have wrote three emails."
You want to say you met Marcus at university and still know him. You say:
A "I've known Marcus since university."
B "I've been knowing Marcus since university."
C "I know Marcus since the university."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer, and nobody is correcting this one.

A friend you have not spoken to in two years calls you and asks: "So, what have you been up to?" Speak for about ninety seconds. Give three pieces of news: one about work, one about something you have been doing outside work, and one about your family. Use for and since at least twice. If a sentence breaks in the middle, say "let me start that again" out loud and keep going.
