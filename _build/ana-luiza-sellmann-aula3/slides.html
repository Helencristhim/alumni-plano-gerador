Chapter 3: The Code

Over, or Still Going?

Two options. That is the whole decision.

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I have been working on this since Monday."

"She has been living in Berlin for two years."

"We have been waiting for the client since Tuesday."

"What have you been doing all week?"

Four sentences about the past. Not one of these actions has stopped. How does English show that?

FormUseExample
have / has been + -ingAn action that started in the past and is still running nowI have been working here since March.
Negative
have not been + -ing		Something you have not been doing latelyI have not been sleeping well.
Question
Have you been + -ing		The most natural way to ask about somebody's life right nowWhat have you been up to?
The two friendsfor plus a length of time: for two years, for ages. Since plus a starting point: since Monday, since 2024.

One question: is it over, or is it still going? If it is still going, add been and -ing. Everything else stays the same.

The Contrast

The Result, or the Running

THE RESULT IS THE POINT

"I have written three emails."

"We have finished the presentation."

THE ACTIVITY IS THE POINT

"I have been writing emails all morning."

"We have been working on it since Monday."

If you can count it and it is done, use the simple. If it is the effort and the time, add been and -ing.

And a short list stays simple even when the situation continues: know, be, have, like, love, want. Say "I have known him for years", never "I have been knowing".

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I am working on this project since March."
"I have been working on this project since March."
"We have waiting for the client all week."
"We have been waiting for the client all week."
"I have been knowing Marcus for ten years."
"I have known Marcus for ten years."

With for and since, English uses have or has, never the plain present. The word been is not optional. And verbs like know and be never take -ing here.

Chapter 4: The Video Call

Two Years of News

Marcus is calling from Berlin

Dialogue

A Call from Berlin

M
There she is. We haven't spoken for ages. What have you been up to?
A
Honestly? I've been working like crazy. I've just finished a big project and I'm still recovering.
M
That sounds exhausting. Has it been like that all year, or just now?
A
On and off. We've been chasing the same deadline since March. But enough about me. You've been in Berlin for two years now?
M
Two years in September. I've been learning German the whole time and I still cannot follow a conversation in a bar.
A
Nobody can follow a conversation in a bar, in any language. Has it been worth it?
M
Completely. Hard, but worth it. My sister has been asking me to come home since day one and I keep saying next year.
A
I know that conversation. My mother has been having it with me since 2024. Send me your address, I've been saving up for a trip.
Comprehension

About Marcus

1. How long has Marcus been in Berlin?
Two years in September.
2. What has he been learning, and how is it going?
German, the whole time, and he still cannot follow a conversation in a bar.
3. What has his sister been asking him to do?
To come home, since day one.

Listening 2

Nobody Has Missed the Deadline Yet

Daniel from production, before your day starts. Sound first — no text.

1. What has Daniel's team been waiting for, and since when?
The client's feedback, since Tuesday. It still has not come.
2. Who has he been coordinating with all week?

3. What does he ask Ana to tell him?
Whether she has been talking to the client directly, so they do not send two different messages.
