Lesson 30 -- Pre-class

Telling the Week -- Three Pasts in One Story

Ten to twenty minutes before class. How to tell your week as a story: what happened, what was happening, and what had already happened. Key words: an update, to approve, a decision, to postpone, a draft, to follow up.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An update -- the new information about a project
"I sent my manager a short update on Friday."
To approve -- to say officially that something is accepted
"The client approved the proposal on Thursday."
A decision -- a choice that you make after you think about it
"We made a quick decision about the budget."
To postpone -- to move an event to a later time
"They postponed the meeting to next week."
A draft -- the first version of a document
"I was writing the draft when the client called."
To follow up -- to contact a person again about something
"I followed up with the client after the meeting."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last Wednesday, Laura was presenting the quarterly results when the projector stopped. She had prepared a printed version, so she continued with paper.

The director liked her calm reaction. By the end of the meeting, the team had already agreed on the new targets.

"She was presenting when the projector stopped." Which action was in progress?
A The presenting.
B Both actions finished at the same time.
C The stopping.
"She had prepared a printed version." When did she prepare it?
A During the meeting.
B Before the projector stopped.
C After the projector stopped.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Three Pasts

GRAMMAR

Past simple for the events, past continuous for the background, past perfect for what happened before.

JobFormExample
The events of the storypast simpleThe client called on Monday.
An action in progresswas / were + -ingWe were discussing the budget.
Before another past momenthad + third formWe had already decided.

Never: While we discussed, the client was calling · When I arrived, the meeting already started

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

Listen first, then put the lines in order. Put the story of the week in order.

Step 1: listen to the whole conversation. Step 2: put the lines in the order you heard.
?"On Thursday, we presented the first results."
?"On Tuesday, we were testing the model when the data stopped."
?"On Friday, the director approved the plan."
?"On Monday, we started the new project."
?"By Wednesday, the IT team had already fixed the problem."

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen, then record yourself. Say had already as one group of sounds.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You were in a meeting. Your phone rang in the middle. You say:
A "While I was in the meeting, my phone was ringing then stopped."
B "While I am in the meeting, my phone rang."
C "While I was in the meeting, my phone rang."
You arrived late. The meeting started before you arrived. You say:
A "When I arrived, the meeting had already started."
B "When I arrived, the meeting started already."
C "When I arrived, the meeting has already started."
Your manager asks: "How was your week?" You answer:
A "Week good, many meetings."
B "Busy. We made two big decisions and sent the report."
C "It is being a busy week."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Tell your week in about one minute: one meeting, something that happened while you were working on it, and one decision that your team had already made before Friday.
