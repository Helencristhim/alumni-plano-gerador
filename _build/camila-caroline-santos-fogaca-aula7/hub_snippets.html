From Portuguese to English: the Same Subject, Twice

Stage 1: Start with the Text

Reading

Start with the text. Read it once before you look at anything else: the language of this lesson comes out of it.

Here is a message written twice. The first version is a word-by-word translation from Portuguese: "Actually we have a situation with the credit team. They are with difficulty because the demand grew and they pretend to hire one collaborator. I will pass you the information after."

Every word in that paragraph exists in English. The paragraph, however, says something else. Actually means "in fact", not "currently". Pretend means to act as if something is true, not to intend. A collaborator, in English, is not an employee. And "I will pass you the information" is a structure that no manager uses; English says "I will send you the details".

The natural version is shorter, and it is organised. "Some background first: the credit team took on the Chile portfolio in March and the volume grew by a third. The key point is that they now ask for one replacement, not two new positions. My recommendation is to approve the replacement and review the second position in January. Next steps: finance confirms the budget on Friday and I speak to the manager the same day."

Four moves, ninety seconds, and every one of them answers a question the listener already has: where does this come from, what happened, what do you think, and what happens now. The order is not decoration. A report that starts with the recommendation and explains the background at the end forces the listener to hold an opinion without knowing why.

The expressions that hold the four moves together are few and always the same. In a nutshell compresses. The bottom line is concludes. On the same page says that two sides understand the same thing. From our side separates your part from everybody else's. Learn these four and the ninety seconds build themselves.

Stage 2: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

The words you have just met, one by one. Listen to each expression and read it in context: you already do all of this at work. Here it gets its English name.

summary -- a short version that keeps only what matters
"I send a summary of the meeting to my manager."
key point -- the most important idea of a discussion
"The key point is that the team agreed on two positions."
background -- the information the listener needs before the main point
"Some background first: this discussion started in March."
recommendation -- what you think should be done, with a reason
"My recommendation is to wait for the budget confirmation."
next steps -- what happens after this conversation, and who does it
"Next steps: finance confirms the budget and I speak to the manager."
in a nutshell -- in very few words
"In a nutshell: the team needs one replacement, not two new positions."
the bottom line is -- used to introduce the single most important conclusion
"The bottom line is that we cannot open new positions this quarter."

Stage 4: Grammar Tip -- The false friends of HR English

GRAMMAR

Six words that look like Portuguese and mean something else. Each one has a correct alternative, and the alternative is usually simpler.

FormWhat it doesExample
actuallyin fact, in realityFor the idea of "at the present time", say currently or at the moment.
eventuallyin the end, after some timeFor the idea of "perhaps, if needed", say possibly or if necessary.
attend / assistattend = to be present at; assist = to helpFor watching a video or a series, say watch.
pretend / collaboratorpretend = to act as if; collaborator is not a neutral wordSay intend or plan, and employee or team member.
Watch out"collaborator" for an employee. It is the most repeated false friend in Brazilian HR presentations, and in English it carries a very different history.

Before your next report, check three words: actually, eventually and collaborator. They are the ones that change your meaning without you noticing.

Stage 8: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Real sentences from a report to a global manager. Only one option is both correct and natural.

1. You want to say that today the company has three open positions. What do you say?
A Actually we have three open positions.
B At the moment we have three open positions.
C Eventually we have three open positions.
D In the actuality we have three open positions.
2. Which sentence is correct in English?
A We have two hundred collaborators in the finance company.
B We have two hundred employees in the finance company.
C We have two hundred collaborators employees in the finance company.
D We have two hundred of employees in the finance company.
3. You were present at the committee meeting. What do you say?
A I assisted the committee meeting on Thursday.
B I attended the committee meeting on Thursday.
C I participated the committee meeting on Thursday.
D I was present in the committee meeting on Thursday.
4. Which sentence introduces a short summary correctly?
A In a nutshell, the team asked for one replacement.
B In a nutshell that the team asked for one replacement.
C Resuming, the team asked for one replacement.
D In few words saying, the team asked for one replacement.
