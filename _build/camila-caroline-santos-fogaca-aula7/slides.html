Chapter 3: Reporting to the Manager

Ninety Seconds With Your Manager

A conversation about a discussion that happened in another language

Dialogue

The Report That Fits in a Corridor

M
Camila, I have four minutes before my next call. How was the meeting with the credit team?
C
Some background first: the team took on the Chile portfolio in March, and the volume grew.
M
I remember that part. What did they decide today?
C
In a nutshell, they asked for one replacement instead of two new positions.
M
Good. And what is your recommendation?
C
My recommendation is to approve the replacement and to review the second position in January.
M
Is finance on the same page?
C
From our side, yes. Finance confirms the budget on Friday, and then we have full alignment.
M
So the bottom line is one position now and one decision in January.
C
Exactly. Next steps: finance confirms on Friday, and I speak to the manager the same day.
Comprehension

About the Report

1. What does Mark already know, and what does he want to hear?
He already knows the background about the Chile portfolio; he wants to hear what the team decided.
2. What is her recommendation, and what does it depend on?
To approve the replacement and review the second position in January; it depends on the budget confirmation from finance on Friday.
3. How does Mark check that he understood correctly?
He repeats the conclusion: one position now and one decision in January.
Chapter 4: The Code

The False Friends of HR English

Words that translate themselves, and get it wrong

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Words Really Mean?

"Actually, the decision was taken last week."

"Eventually, the team agreed on one replacement."

"Fifteen managers attended the meeting."

"Could you assist me with the headcount file?"

Each of these four words looks like a Portuguese word and means something different. What is each one really saying?

FormWhat it doesExample
actuallyin fact, in reality — not "currently"For "currently", say at the moment or currently.
eventuallyin the end, after some time — not "possibly"For "possibly", say possibly or if necessary.
attend / assistattend = to be present; assist = to helpFor "to watch", say watch. For "to answer a client", say help or deal with.
pretend / collaboratorpretend = to act as if; collaborator is not an employeeFor "to intend", say intend or plan. For a person on your team, say employee, team member or colleague.
Watch outthe word collaborator for an employee. In English it suggests somebody who works with an enemy, and it is the most common false friend in Brazilian HR presentations.

Sound Check

Say It Clearly

Word stress · point 3 of 14

Why this one is hard for a Brazilian speaker

In Portuguese: The cognate keeps its Portuguese stress (contexto, sumário, recomendação).

In English: The same word has a different strong syllable in English, and the unstressed ones are reduced, not just quieter.

If it slips: You are understood, but it costs the listener the attention you need for the content itself.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

The Long Version and the Short One

A local discussion and the ninety-second report that follows it

Listening 1

The Long Version: the Whole Story

A manager tells the whole story of a request. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. What happened in March, according to the manager?
His team took on the Chile portfolio and the volume of files grew by about a third.
2. What did the team ask for at the end of the discussion?
One replacement instead of the two new positions they had discussed at first.
3. What does he say about the two candidates?
He has two good candidates and he is afraid of losing them if the decision waits until January.
Listening 2

The Short Version: Ninety Seconds

The same story, reported to a global manager. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. In what order does the speaker organise the report?
Background first, then the key point, then the recommendation, then the next steps.
2. What is the recommendation, and what does it depend on?
To approve one replacement now and review the second position in January, depending on the budget confirmation from finance.
3. What does the short version add that the long one did not have?
A clear recommendation and next steps with an owner and a date.
Real Document

The Ninety-Second Card

REPORTING CARD · 90 SECONDS
four moves · one minute and a half
1. Background (15s)Where this comes from, in one sentence.
2. Key point (30s)What was decided or asked, in a nutshell.
3. Recommendation (30s)What you think should happen, and why.
4. Next steps (15s)Who does what, and by when.
RuleNo names, no salaries. Roles and percentages only.
If interruptedAnswer the question, then return: going back to my recommendation...
Sample structure for this lesson. The real conversation stays confidential.
1. Which part of the card is the shortest, and why?
The background and the next steps, fifteen seconds each: the listener needs them, but the value is in the key point and the recommendation.
2. What do you do if the manager interrupts in the middle?
Answer the question, then return to the recommendation.
3. What never appears in this report?
Names of employees and salaries: only roles and percentages.
