Warm-up — From Lesson 24

Review

In Lesson 24 you presented your own numbers: sales increased by eighteen percent, there was a sharp drop in the checkout time, and you compared every result with the target. Today the numbers come from other people. Daniela watches a retail tech summit online, with a talk and a panel about the future of checkout. She cannot write every word, so she listens for three things: the main point, the evidence and the takeaway. Then she reports it to her team. (Good listeners do not catch every word. They catch the right words.)

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Panel -- a group of experts who discuss a topic in front of an audience
"The panel talked about the future of checkout."
Speaker -- a person who gives a talk at an event
"The first speaker was from a fashion retailer."
Moderator -- the person who leads a panel and asks the questions
"The moderator gave each speaker five minutes."
Audience -- the people who watch and listen to a talk
"The audience had many questions about self-checkout."
Argue -- to give reasons to show that an idea is right
"Tom argued that stores still need people."
Evidence -- facts or numbers that show something is true
"Her evidence was a survey of five thousand customers."
Takeaway -- the main idea you remember after a talk or a meeting
"My main takeaway from the panel is: start small."
Summarize -- to say the main points in a short way
"Can you summarize the panel in two minutes?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

On Tuesday, Daniela watched a panel at a retail tech summit in New York. The topic was the future of checkout. The first speaker, Karen Lopez, said that customers leave stores because of long lines, not because of high prices. According to her survey, forty percent of customers left a store without buying because the line was too long. The second speaker, Tom Bennett, argued that technology is not enough. He told the audience that many older customers did not like self-checkout at first. His main point was that stores still need people next to the machines. After the panel, Greg sent Daniela a message: he missed the talk and wanted a short summary for the US team.

1. Why do customers leave stores, according to Karen?
A Because of high prices in the fashion stores, especially in New York.
B Because the app is slow.
C Because of long lines.
2. What is Tom’s main point?
A Self-checkout is a bad idea, and stores should stop using machines.
B Stores still need people next to the machines.
C Prices are too high.
3. What does Greg want from Daniela?
A A short summary of the panel.
B The numbers of the Brazilian stores for the fourth quarter and a new target.
C A ticket to the summit.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip — Reporting What a Speaker Said

GRAMMAR

After a talk, you do not repeat every word. You report the idea: who said it, and what the idea was.

UseFormExample
Report an ideasaid / explained + that + sentenceKaren said that lines are the real problem.
Report an opinion with reasonsargued + that + sentenceTom argued that stores still need people.
Say who heard ittold + person + that + sentenceShe told us that sales increased by twelve percent.
Give the sourceaccording to + person, + sentenceAccording to Karen, speed is a customer project.
The big ideahis / her main point was that + sentenceHis main point was that you should start small.
RememberSay has no person after it: "she said that", never "she said me". Tell always has a person: "she told us that", never "she told that". And "according to Karen", never "according Karen".

Stage 2: Daniela’s Summary, in Order

Order

Listen first, then put the parts of Daniela's summary for Greg in the right order.

?Her evidence was a survey: forty percent of the customers said the line was too long.
?First, the panel was about the future of checkout, and the moderator was Linda Park.
?Finally, my main takeaway is that we should test the new system in a few stores first.

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen to each sentence, then record yourself saying it. Notice that "that" is short and weak in these sentences: said-that sounds like one word.

Her main point was that speed is a customer project.
According to Karen, customers leave because of long lines.
Tom argued that stores still need people.
The speaker told us that sales increased by twelve percent.
My main takeaway from the panel is to start small.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

Greg asks what Karen said about lines. Which sentence is correct?
A "She said me that long lines are the real problem for every retailer in the United States."
B "She told that long lines are the real problem."
C "She said that long lines are the real problem."
You want to give the source of a number in your report. What do you say?
A "According Karen, forty percent of customers left."
B "According to Karen, forty percent of customers left."
C "For the opinion of Karen, forty percent of the customers left the store without buying anything."
During the panel Q&A, you did not catch a number. What do you ask?
A "Sorry, could you repeat the percentage?"
B "Sorry, can you say me again the percentage that you said before about the customers?"
C "Repeat the number."
Your director asks for the main idea of Tom’s talk in one sentence. What do you say?
A "His main point it was that stores need people."
B "His main point was that stores still need people."
C "He talked about many things: machines, customers, tests, employees, stores and complaints."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think of a talk, webinar or panel you watched for work: an SAP event, a retail event or a video from a vendor. Record a one-minute summary for your team: who the speaker was, the main point, the evidence, and your own takeaway. Use "said that", "argued that" and "according to".
