Complementary Activities

Optional extras to enjoy outside class. Mark each one as done after you watch or listen to it.

Video
TED-Ed -- "How to spot a misleading graph" (Lea Gaslowitz)

Four minutes on the chart that puts somebody third without lying about a single number — the exact situation of today’s table. Short, slow and full of comparatives: higher, steeper, far bigger, the largest.

Tip: watch it twice. The second time, pause at each chart and say out loud what is being compared with what, and what is missing.

Podcast
Planet Money (NPR) -- Episode 522, "The Invention of the Economy"

The story of the single number that lets anybody rank countries against each other, and of the year Italy started counting differently and overtook the United Kingdom overnight without changing anything. It is your own argument about the missing column, told about whole countries.

Tip: the transcript is on the same page. Write down three comparative sentences from it and say them out loud with the than weak and fast.

Listen on NPR ↗
Talk
Malcolm Gladwell -- "Choice, happiness and spaghetti sauce" (TED)

Seventeen minutes about the moment an industry stopped looking for the one best product and started looking at groups instead. It is the argument of the last two lessons put together: the average describes nobody, and a ranking of one column answers a question nobody asked.

Tip: turn the English subtitles on. Write down five comparisons he makes, and mark which ones compare two things and which one thing against all.
