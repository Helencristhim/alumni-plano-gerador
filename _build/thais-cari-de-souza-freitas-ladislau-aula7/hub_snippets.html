Better, Worse, the Highest -- Defending a Comparison

Every conclusion you reach is a comparison. Key words: a benchmark, a peer, to outperform, a margin, to narrow, to widen, steady, volatile, a ranking, an outlier. Structure: comparatives and superlatives, regular and irregular.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A benchmark -- a number from outside, used to compare your own against
"Our rate is lower than the market benchmark, and that is the headline."
A peer -- a company or a person at the same level as you
"Three of my peers are American, and two of them run bigger books."
To outperform -- to do better than somebody else
"The new cohort outperformed the old one by half a point."
A margin -- what is left after the costs are paid
"The margin is thinner on that product than on the old one."
To narrow -- to make a difference between two numbers smaller
"The gap narrowed from one point one to zero point four."
To widen -- to make a difference between two numbers bigger
"The gap widened after we tightened the policy, and that was expected."
Steady -- not going up or down much, month after month
"The old portfolio is steadier than the new one, and half the size."
Volatile -- going up and down a lot, and hard to predict
"The first three months of any cohort are the most volatile."
A ranking -- a list in order, from best to worst
"Brazil is third in the ranking, behind Mexico and Colombia."
An outlier -- a number that sits very far from all the others
"One outlier moved the average, and the average moved the decision."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Every conclusion in your work is a comparison, and English has two shapes for it. When you compare exactly two things, short words grow by three letters and take than: "This cohort is riskier than the last one." "The margin is thinner than the old one." Long words do not grow; they take more in front instead: "The ranking is more volatile than it looks." Never both at once. More riskier does not exist, and neither does more high.

When you compare one thing against a whole group, the shape changes: the goes in front, and short words take -est. "April was the worst month of the year." "Peru has the highest rate of all five markets." Long words take the most: "The first three months are the most volatile of any cohort." The word the is not optional here. April was worst month is not English.

Three verbs refuse the pattern and you learn them once: good becomes better and the best; bad becomes worse and the worst; far becomes further and the furthest. And two extras make a comparison sound professional instead of flat. To say two things are the same, use as ... as: "This quarter is as volatile as the last one." To say how big the difference is, put it after the comparison: lower by half a point, far higher, slightly better. A comparison without a size is an opinion; with a size, it is an analysis.

1. Why is "more riskier" wrong?
A Because risky is an irregular adjective.
B Because the word can grow with -er or take more in front, but never both at the same time.
C Because you can only compare two things with as ... as.
2. What is missing in "April was worst month of the year"?
A The word the. A superlative always takes the in front of it.
B The word than, after month.
C Nothing. The sentence is correct.
3. The text says a comparison without a size is an opinion. Which sentence is the analysis?
A "Our rate is better."
B "Our rate is better than the benchmark, by half a point."
C "Our rate is the better one."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Comparatives and Superlatives

GRAMMAR

-er and than for two. The and -est for all of them. Never more and -er together.

JobFormExample
Two things, short wordword + -er + thanhigher than · thinner than · riskier than
Two things, long wordmore + word + thanmore volatile than · more attractive than
One against all, short wordthe + word + -estthe highest rate · the steadiest month
One against all, long wordthe most + wordthe most volatile quarter
Irregulargood · bad · fargood → better → the best · bad → worse → the worst
Same sizeas + word + asThis quarter is as volatile as the last one.
The size of the differenceby + figure · far / slightly / muchlower by half a point · far higher · slightly better

Never: more riskier · more high · worst month (without the) · lower that we are · the most best

Stage 2: Put the Argument in Order

Order

A ranking puts you third and the comparison is unfair. Put your answer in order.

?"I saw the table. Mexico is lower than we are on the rate, and that is true."
?"But their book is half the size and much steadier than ours."
?"We grew faster than every market on that list, nineteen per cent against four."
?"The ranking is not wrong. It is incomplete."
?"With growth and the age of the book, the gap narrows to almost nothing."
?"And I would take the fifth market out, because it is an outlier."
