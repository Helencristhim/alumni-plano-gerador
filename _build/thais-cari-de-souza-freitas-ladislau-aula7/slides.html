Lesson 7

Better, Worse, the Highest

Every conclusion you reach is a comparison. Now say it in English

Warm-up

Which Month Was Better?

First: two real figures read out loud, each one with a quantifier. Then: compare two months of your own portfolio, and say which one was better and why.

The Target

A Comparison in Three Moves

1. A against B, with the size of the difference.

2. The best and the worst of the whole group.

3. What you would do about it, in one sentence.

Diagnostic

Compare Two Things You Know Well

Two products, two months, or two groups of customers.

Say which one is better, by how much, and which one is the best of everything you manage. Two sentences.

Chapter 3: The Code

Two Things, or All of Them

One idea, two endings

Grammar Discovery

Which Two Compare Only Two Things?

"This cohort is riskier than the last one."

"The margin is more attractive than the margin on the old product."

"April was the worst month of the year."

"The first three months are the most volatile of any cohort."

Look at the end of each red word: two of them grew by three letters, two of them took a whole word in front instead. Then look at the blue words: one of them appears only when you compare two things, the other only when you compare one against everything.

JobFormExample
Two things, short wordword + -er + thanhigher than · thinner than · riskier than
Two things, long wordmore + word + thanmore attractive than · more volatile than
One against all, short wordthe + word + -estthe highest limit · the steadiest month
One against all, long wordthe most + wordthe most volatile quarter
Irregulargood / bad / fargood → better → the best · bad → worse → the worst
Same sizeas + word + asThis quarter is as volatile as the last one.
The size of the differenceby + figure · slightly / far / muchlower by half a point · far higher · slightly better

In one line: -er and than for two; the and -est for all — and never more and -er in the same word.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Five Markets, One Table

One conversation, three recordings, one ranking

Dialogue

The Ranking Everybody Saw First

M
Have you seen the table? Brazil is third of five. Mexico looks a lot better than us.
T
I saw it. Mexico is lower than we are, yes, but their book is half the size and much steadier.
M
So you are saying the ranking is wrong?
T
Not wrong. Incomplete. We grew faster than every market on that list, and the first three months of a new cohort are always the most volatile.
M
Fair. What would make the comparison better?
T
Two more columns. Growth, and the age of the book. With those, the gap between us and Mexico narrows to almost nothing.
M
And the fifth market? That number looks strange to me.
T
That one is an outlier. One large customer moved it, and I would take it out of the ranking before Monday.
Comprehension

About Mark

1. What does Mark say about the table when he opens the conversation?
That Brazil is third of five, and that Mexico looks a lot better.
2. How does he react when she disagrees?
He asks whether she is saying the ranking is wrong, and then he asks what would make the comparison better.
3. What does he notice about the fifth market?
That the number looks strange to him.

Five Markets, Ranked

A head of analytics presenting a ranking. Sound first — no text.

1. Which market has the lowest rate, and which has the highest?
Mexico has the lowest rate and Peru has the highest.
2. What does he say about Brazil compared with Mexico?
Brazil is worse on the rate but grew faster than any market on the list, and its book is twice the size.
3. What does he say he will not do with the fifth market this month?
He will not use it in the ranking, because one large customer makes it an outlier.
Listening 2 · A Podcast

Why Rankings Lie

An analyst on the missing column. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say is the first question to ask about any ranking?
What is being left out of it, and who chose the column it is sorted by.
2. What example does she give of two things that look comparable and are not?
A large old business and a small new one, where the new one always looks worse on a rate and better on growth.
3. What does she recommend saying in the meeting, instead of arguing about the position?
Name the two columns that are missing, and offer to add them.
Listening 3 · Business Radio

The Region, Compared

A short report comparing credit markets. Sound first — no text.

1. Which country does the report call the largest market, and which the fastest growing?
Brazil is the largest, and Colombia is the fastest growing.
2. What does the report say about interest rates in the region compared with the United States?
They are far higher, and the gap has not narrowed in twenty years.
3. What warning does the report give about comparing the countries at all?
That the numbers are collected differently in each country, so a small difference between two of them means almost nothing.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I use -er and than for two things, and the plus -est for one against all of them.
I never say more riskier or more high, and I never forget the than.
I know better, the best, worse and the worst without thinking.
I can agree with a figure and disagree with the conclusion, in the same answer.
I know the words: a benchmark, a peer, to outperform, a margin, to narrow, to widen, steady, volatile, a ranking, an outlier.
