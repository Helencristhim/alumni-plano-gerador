Two Suppliers, One Decision -- Comparing Offers

The words of a decision: proposal, upfront cost, running costs, reliable, response time, trade-off, to outweigh, value for money. Structure: comparatives, superlatives and not as... as. Mission: defend one proposal against another, out loud, with numbers.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Proposal -- a written offer that explains what a company will do and for how much
"We received two proposals for the forklift maintenance."
Upfront cost -- the money you pay at the start, before you get any benefit
"The upfront cost is higher, but the contract lasts three years."
Running costs -- the money you keep paying to use something over time
"Cheap machines often have the highest running costs."
Reliable -- able to be trusted to work well every time
"Their technicians are more reliable than anybody we have used before."
Response time -- how long it takes somebody to react after you call them
"A four hour response time is much better than two days."
Trade-off -- a situation where you accept something bad to get something good
"The trade-off is simple: we pay more and we stop less."
To outweigh -- to be more important or bigger than something else
"The savings do not outweigh the risk of a stopped line."
Value for money -- the quality you get compared with the price you pay
"The cheapest proposal is not always the best value for money."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On the first page, NorthLift is cheaper than Atlas: about twenty-five percent. But Atlas is faster, with a four hour response time against two days, and it is more reliable, because the most common parts are already on site. Of the three companies we looked at, Atlas had the best record: two days lost last year, against nine.

So which proposal is better? NorthLift is not as expensive as Atlas in the first year. Over three years it is the most expensive option, because every part is a new invoice. Notice the pattern: short words take -er and the -est, long words take more and the most, and good becomes better and the best.

1. Why is it more reliable and not reliabler?
A Because reliable is an irregular adjective, like good, bad and far.
B Because more is used with every adjective in a formal business proposal.
C Because reliable is a long adjective, so it takes more.
2. What does NorthLift is not as expensive as Atlas mean?
A NorthLift is cheaper than Atlas in the first year.
B NorthLift and Atlas cost exactly the same amount of money every year.
C NorthLift is more expensive than Atlas.
3. Over three years, which option is the most expensive?
A Atlas, because the price on the first page is higher than the other.
B NorthLift, because every part is a new invoice.
C Both cost the same.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Comparatives and Superlatives

GRAMMAR

Two things, or one thing against all the others. The length of the adjective decides the form.

FormUse it forExample
short + -er thanOne syllable, or two ending in -y.NorthLift is cheaper than Atlas.
more + long + thanTwo or more syllables.Atlas is more reliable than NorthLift.
the + -est / the mostOne against all the others.It is the fastest and the most reliable.
Irregulargood, better, the best · bad, worse, the worst · far, further, the furthest.Atlas is the better deal.
not as ... asA softer way to say less.It is not as cheap as it looks.
much / a lotMakes the difference bigger.Four hours is much faster than two days.

Never two comparison words together: not more cheaper, not more better. And after a comparative comes than, never that.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are comparing two offers in a meeting. Choose the best thing to say.

You want to say that NorthLift costs less. You say:
A NorthLift is cheaper than Atlas.
B NorthLift is more cheaper than Atlas on the first page of the proposal.
C NorthLift is cheapest that Atlas.
You compare Atlas with all the other suppliers. You say:
A Atlas is the more reliable supplier of all the companies we spoke to.
B Atlas is reliabler than all.
C Atlas is the most reliable of all.
You want to say, politely, that the price is high. You say:
A Your price is more higher than the other.
B Your price is not as low as the other proposal.
C Your price is the most high.
The service of Atlas is good, and the service of NorthLift is less good. You say:
A Atlas has a more good service than NorthLift, in my personal opinion.
B Atlas has a gooder service.
C Atlas has a better service than NorthLift.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think about a real decision between two suppliers, two machines or two service contracts that you made, or have to make soon. Record about ninety seconds. Present the two options, then compare them on at least three points: price, speed and reliability. Use one comparative, one superlative and one not as... as. Name the trade-off, and finish with your choice and the reason. Use at least four words from this lesson. Do not stop to correct yourself.
