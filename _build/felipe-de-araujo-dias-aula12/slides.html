Chapter 4: Side by Side

Cheaper, the Cheapest, Not as Cheap As

Comparing two things, or many

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"NorthLift is cheaper than Atlas."

"Atlas is more reliable than NorthLift."

"It was the most reliable option of all."

"Their price is not as low as it looks."

FormUse it forExample
short adjective + -er thanTwo things, one syllable (or ending in -y).NorthLift is cheaper than Atlas.
more + long adjective + thanTwo things, two or more syllables.Atlas is more reliable than NorthLift.
the + -est / the mostOne thing against all the others.It is the fastest and the most reliable.
better / the best · worse / the worstGood and bad have their own forms.Atlas is the better deal.
not as + adjective + asA softer way to say less, very useful in a meeting.It is not as cheap as it looks.
much / a lot + comparativeTo make the difference bigger.Four hours is much faster than two days.

Never more cheaper and never more better. One comparison word is enough: either -er or more, never both.

Grammar Practice

Two Things, or All of Them?

Say the full sentence, then click to compare

1. A four hour response time is much ______ (fast) than two days.
"A four hour response time is much faster than two days."
2. Of the three proposals, Atlas is ______ (reliable).
"Of the three proposals, Atlas is the most reliable."
3. NorthLift has a ______ (good) price, but a ______ (bad) service.
"NorthLift has a better price, but a worse service."
4. The cheap proposal is not ______ (cheap) it looks.
"The cheap proposal is not as cheap as it looks."
