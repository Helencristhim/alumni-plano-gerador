Chapter 4: The Code

The Question Moves to the Front

direct · polite · very polite — and the word order that changes

Grammar Discovery

From Direct to Very Polite

"Where is the station?"

"Could you tell me where the station is?"

"Do you happen to know if the kitchen closes at nine?"

"I was wondering if you could help me with my bags."

These four questions go from the most direct to the most polite. Look at the colored words. In the first one, the verb comes BEFORE the subject. What happens to the order in the other three? And where did the word do go?

FormWhat it doesExample
direct questionVerb before the subject. Fine with friends, and in Norway.Where is the station?
Could you tell me + wh-wordPolite. The second half has statement order.Could you tell me where the station is?
Do you know + if / whetherFor yes/no questions. No do in the second half.Do you know if the kitchen closes at nine?
Would you mind + -ingA polite request. The answer no means yes, I will.Would you mind opening the window?
I was wondering if + couldVery polite, typical in the UK. Past forms make it softer.I was wondering if you could help me.
choose the levelThe more you impose, and the more British the listener, the higher you go.a coffee in Oslo ≠ a favor in London

In one line: the polite part goes to the front, and the question itself goes back to normal order.

Dialogue

Is It Okay to Ask?

J
Hi, Ana! Welcome to Vermont. Did you find the cabin okay?
A
Yes, thank you. Sorry to impose on you, but could you tell me where the nearest supermarket is?
J
Sure, no problem! It is about ten minutes down the road. You are not imposing at all.
A
Great. And do you know if it is open on Sundays?
J
It opens at eight on Sundays. Anything else? Just ask. You do not need to beat around the bush with me.
A
Okay, then I will get to the point. Would you mind lending me a heater? The cabin is cold at night.
J
Not at all. I will bring one over in an hour. Americans love a straightforward question.
A
Good to know. In Brazil I would ask about your family first!
Comprehension

About Josh

1. How far is the supermarket, according to Josh?
About ten minutes down the road.
2. What time does the supermarket open on Sundays?
At eight.
3. What does Josh say about straightforward questions?
That Americans love a straightforward question — she does not need to beat around the bush.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I put indirect questions in statement order: could you tell me where the station is.
I do not use do or does in the second half of an indirect question.
I use would you mind + -ing and I was wondering if + could.
I choose how polite to be for the person in front of me: Norway, the UK or the US.
I know the words: blunt, tactful, straightforward, pushy, to hedge, to beat around the bush, to get to the point.
