Real-Speed English

In a real interview, words do not come one by one. "What did you" becomes "wha-dja", and your brain has to fill in the rest. It can only do that with sounds it already knows. These twenty-four lines are the ones interviewers use most, with how they really sound.

Real-Speed English 01

The First Two Minutes

How an interviewer opens a call. These lines come fast, often before you are ready, and they set the tone.
0%
▼

Stage 1: Hear it

Listen

Listen to the expression, then hear it inside a real sentence. Use the speed buttons at the top of the page: first 0.75x, then 1x.

Thanks for making the time -- thank you for finding time for this call
Sounds like: thanks-fer-making-the-time
“Hi Fernando, thanks for making the time on a Friday.”
Can you hear me okay? -- a quick check that the audio of the call works
Sounds like: k-nya-hear-me-okay
“Can you hear me okay? The connection here is not great.”
Walk me through your background -- tell your career story in order
Sounds like: walk-me-thru-yer-background
“So, walk me through your background, starting wherever you like.”
What brings you here? -- why you are interested in this job
Sounds like: wha-brings-ya-here
“What brings you here? You had a great role at your last company.”
Tell me a bit about yourself -- give a short introduction
Sounds like: tell-me-a-bit-a-bout-yer-self
“Tell me a bit about yourself, the short version.”
Before we dive in -- before the main part of the interview starts
Sounds like: b-for-we-dive-in
“Before we dive in, let me explain how today is going to work.”

Real-Speed English 03

The Hard Questions

The questions that come quickly and cut words in half. You usually know the answer. The problem is catching the question.
0%
▼

Stage 1: Hear it

Listen

Listen to the expression, then hear it inside a real sentence. Use the speed buttons at the top of the page: first 0.75x, then 1x.

Why'd you leave? -- why did you leave your last job
Sounds like: why-dja-leave
“Why'd you leave after only two years?”
How'd that go? -- how did that situation end
Sounds like: how-dthat-go
“You presented the plan to the banks. How'd that go?”
What's your take on -- what is your opinion about
Sounds like: wats-yer-take-on
“What's your take on the new tax rules in Brazil?”
Give me a ballpark -- give me an approximate number
Sounds like: gimme-a-ballpark
“I don't need the exact figure, just give me a ballpark.”
What would you have done differently? -- looking back, what would you change
Sounds like: wud-ja-have-done-differently
“The deal failed. What would you have done differently?”
Where do you see the risk? -- which part worries you most
Sounds like: where-dya-see-the-risk
“This plan looks good on paper. Where do you see the risk?”

▼

Stage 1: Hear it

Listen

Listen to the expression, then hear it inside a real sentence. Use the speed buttons at the top of the page: first 0.75x, then 1x.

Sounds like: we-r-running-short-on-time
“We're running short on time, so one last question.”
Any questions for me? -- your turn to ask the interviewer something
Sounds like: any-questions-fer-me
“That covers my side. Any questions for me?”
