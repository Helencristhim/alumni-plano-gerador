Chapter 3 -- The Code

Could You Tell Me...?

A question inside a question

Grammar Discovery

What Do the Purple Words Have in Common?

"Could you tell me whether Lane has any litigation at the moment?"

"Do you know how much the supplier is asking for?"

"I was wondering why it isn't in the last audit."

"Could you let us know when the last audit was completed?"

What comes right after each purple word: question order or sentence order?

Direct questionIndirect question
"Does Lane have any litigation?""Could you tell me whether / if Lane has any litigation?"
"How much is the supplier asking for?""Do you know how much the supplier is asking for?"
"Why isn't it in the audit?""I was wondering why it isn't in the audit."
RuleOpening + question word (or if / whether) + subject + verb. No do / does / did.
NeverNot "Could you tell me how much is it?" / "Do you know where does he work?"
Grammar Rule

Accusation or Question?

Direct (sounds like an accusation)Indirect (same information)
"Is there a problem with your accounts?""Could you walk me through how you prepare your accounts?"
"Why did you hide the case?""I was wondering why the case isn't in the data room."
"How much do you owe?""Do you know how much the company owes in total?"
"Is the pension plan funded?""Could you tell me if the pension plan is fully funded?"
The CFO stops talking.The CFO explains, and you get the details.

Chapter 4 -- Getting There

Before Monday

One conversation, two recordings, one request list

Dialogue

Eduarda and Paul

E
Paul, NorthPack opens the data room on Monday. Before they do, I'd like to ask you the hard questions myself.
P
Go ahead. I'd rather hear them from you than from Mark's lawyers.
E
Could you tell me whether Lane has any litigation at the moment?
P
Just one case. A former supplier says we broke a contract. Our lawyers think it's small.
E
Do you know how much the supplier is asking for?
P
About two million dollars. It's a liability, but I don't think it's material for a deal this size.
E
Maybe not, but we shouldn't gloss over it. I was wondering why it isn't in the last audit.
P
Because the case started after the audit. The supplier went to court in March.
E
Then we need a clear disclosure in the data room. If NorthPack finds it alone, it becomes a red flag.
P
Fair enough. What else do you think they'll dig into?
Comprehension

About Paul

1. What does the former supplier say, according to Paul?
That Lane broke a contract.
2. How much is the supplier asking for?
About two million dollars.
3. Why isn't the case in the last audit, according to Paul?
Because it started after the audit, in March.

Listening 2 -- The Consultant

Better Questions, Better Answers

A consultant explains how to ask due diligence questions. Sound first -- no text.

1. What kind of questions should you start with?
Open questions, with what, how and why.
2. Why can a direct question about a sensitive topic be a problem?
It can sound like an accusation.
3. What should you do if someone says it's not a big deal?
Ask how much it is in dollars.
