Lesson 27 -- Pre-class

Due Diligence -- Asking the Right Questions

Before NorthPack opens the data room, Eduarda interviews Paul Grant, Lane's CFO, and finds a problem first. Key words: data room, liability, disclosure, litigation, audit, material, dig into, gloss over. Structure: indirect questions (Could you tell me whether... / Do you know how much... / I was wondering why...).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Data room -- an online place where a seller shares private documents with buyers
"NorthPack will have two weeks in the data room."
Liability -- a legal or financial responsibility, such as a debt
"The pension plan could be a large liability for the buyer."
Disclosure -- information that a company shares officially, especially about a problem
"A clear disclosure protects the seller later."
Litigation -- the process of taking a dispute to court
"Is there any litigation with former suppliers?"
Audit -- an official check of a company's accounts by an independent firm
"The last audit was completed in January."
Material -- important enough to change a decision
"A two-million-dollar case is not material for a deal this size."
Dig into -- to look at something in detail, to find more information
"Their team will dig into every customer contract."
Gloss over -- to talk about a problem quickly, to avoid the details
"Don't gloss over the supplier case. They will find it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

NorthPack opens the data room on Monday, so on Thursday Eduarda sits down with Paul Grant, Lane's CFO. She wants to ask the hard questions before the buyer does.

She does not ask "Do you have problems with the law?". She asks: Could you tell me whether Lane has any litigation? Paul explains that a former supplier went to court in March. Eduarda asks: Do you know how much the supplier is asking for? About two million dollars. It is a liability, but Paul doesn't think it is material.

Eduarda says: I was wondering why it isn't in the last audit. The case started after the audit. She tells Paul not to gloss over it: NorthPack will dig into everything, so they need a clear disclosure in the data room.

1. When does NorthPack open the data room?
A On Thursday, right after Eduarda's meeting with Paul and the lawyers of the supplier.
B On Monday.
C In March.
2. Which question is correct?
A "Could you tell me whether Lane has any litigation?"
B "Could you tell me does Lane have any litigation with former suppliers at the moment?"
C "Could you tell me has Lane litigation?"
3. Why does Eduarda want a disclosure in the data room?
A Because Paul thinks two million dollars is a material problem that could stop the whole deal next week.
B Because the supplier is asking for a hundred million.
C Because NorthPack will dig into everything and find the case anyway.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Indirect Questions

GRAMMAR

Put a question inside a polite opening. After the question word (or if / whether), use normal sentence order: subject + verb. No do, does or did.

OpeningDirect questionIndirect question
Could you tell me...Does Lane have any debt?Could you tell me whether Lane has any debt?
Do you know...How much is the supplier asking for?Do you know how much the supplier is asking for?
I was wondering...Why isn't it in the audit?I was wondering why it isn't in the audit.
Could you let us know...When was the audit completed?Could you let us know when the audit was completed?
We would like to know...How many customers do you have?We would like to know how many customers you have.
Yes / no questionIs the plan funded?Do you know if / whether the plan is funded?

Never: Could you tell me how much is it? · Do you know where does he work? · Could you tell me Lane has any debt?

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose what a confident advisor would say.

You want to know if the company has any debt. You ask the CFO:
A "Could you tell me whether the company has any debt?"
B "Could you tell me does the company have any debt that the buyer should know about?"
C "Debt? Yes or no?"
You want to know when the biggest contract ends. You ask:
A "When does it end the contract with your biggest customer, and who signed it?"
B "Do you know when the contract with your biggest customer ends?"
C "Do you know when does the contract end?"
The CFO says a problem is "not a big deal". You say:
A "OK, then we don't need to talk about it."
B "I understand that you think it is not important, but you must tell me everything now."
C "Could you tell me how much it is in dollars?"
The CFO wants to leave a small court case out of the data room. You say:
A "We should gloss over it, because the buyer will probably not dig into the details of a small case."
B "We shouldn't gloss over it. A clear disclosure protects you."
C "We shouldn't gloss it."
