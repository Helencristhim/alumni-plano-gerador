Complementary Activities

Optional extras to enjoy outside class. Mark each one as done after you watch or listen to it.

Talk
Simon Sinek -- "How great leaders inspire action" (TED)

Eighteen minutes built almost entirely out of the words you practised today: because, so, therefore, however. He speaks slowly and repeats his own chain three times, which makes it one of the easiest talks in English to follow at this level.

Tip: turn the English subtitles on and count the times he says because. Then say one of his chains back, out loud, about your own work.

Watch on TED ↗
Video
TEDx -- "The danger of mixing up causality and correlation" (Ionica Smeets)

Twelve minutes on the exact trap of this lesson: two things moved together, and somebody said one caused the other. It is the reason your decision note has a line called assumption and another one called evidence.

Tip: after each example, say out loud what the real driver could be, starting with "My assumption is that...".

Watch on YouTube ↗
Podcast
The Indicator from Planet Money (NPR) -- "Your Lifetime Value Score"

Worth a second listen for a different reason this week. The first time you listened for the words about data; this time listen only for the explanations. Every claim in the episode arrives with a because or a so attached, and the hosts speak slowly enough to catch each one.

Tip: write down four sentences from the transcript that contain because, and mark which ones are followed by a full sentence and which by a noun.
