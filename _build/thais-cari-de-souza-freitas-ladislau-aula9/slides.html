Chapter 3: The Code

Cause, and What Follows

Five little words, three different shapes

Grammar Discovery

What Comes After Each Blue Word?

"We tightened the rule because the losses came from one cohort."

"The rate moved because of one cohort."

"The sample was small, so we waited another month."

"Growth was strong. However, most of it came from one cohort. Therefore we tightened the rule."

Look at the red part after each blue word. Three of them are complete sentences with a verb inside. One of them is only a noun. And two of the blue words belong to an email rather than to a conversation.

WordWhat follows itExample
becausea full sentence (subject + verb)We tightened it because the losses came from one cohort.
because ofa noun only, no verbThe rate moved because of one cohort.
sothe result, after a commaThe sample was small, so we waited.
therefore · as a resulta new sentence — formal, for writingThe sample was small. Therefore we waited.
howevera new sentence that turns against the last oneGrowth was strong. However, most of it came from one cohort.
Soundbecause = be-COZ, two syllables, stress on the secondNot BE-cause, and no E at the end.
The trapa sentence that starts with Because needs the result tooNot Because the sample was small. Say Because the sample was small, we waited.

In one line: because takes a sentence, because of takes a noun, so takes the result — and therefore and however belong to the email.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Question You Get Every Week

One conversation, three recordings, one decision note

Dialogue

Why Did the Rate Move?

M
The rate moved half a point this month. Why?
T
The main driver is the cohort that joined in April. It moved because that group reached its third payment this month.
M
Could it be the model?
T
We ruled out the model in the first hour, so the policy was next. That part I know. What I do not have yet is evidence for the second half.
M
Say the second half anyway.
T
My assumption is that the root cause is a rule we loosened in March. However, that is a theory, and I will say so in the note.
M
And what do you want to do?
T
Tighten it again. The trade-off is growth: as a result, we lose two quarters. I would rather explain that than explain a write-off.
Comprehension

About Mark

1. What does Mark ask in his first two questions?
Why the rate moved half a point, and whether it could be the model.
2. She says she has no evidence for the second half. What does he answer?
He tells her to say it anyway. He wants the theory even without the proof.
3. What is his last question, and what does it ask her for?
He asks what she wants to do. He asks for a decision, not for more analysis.
Listening 1 · The Decision

Why We Did It

A head of risk explaining one decision, end to end. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he give as the driver of the change?
The cohort that joined in April, which reached its third payment that month.
2. What did the team rule out, and how long did it take?
The model, in the first hour. After that they went to the policy.
3. What trade-off does he name, and what does he say he would rather explain?
Two quarters of growth. He says he would rather explain that than explain a write-off.
Listening 2 · A Podcast

What You Know and What You Assume

A researcher on saying the difference out loud. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say happens to people who never say I do not know?
They get believed less over time, because nobody can tell which of their sentences are solid.
2. What two words does she recommend putting in front of a theory?
My assumption. She says naming it costs nothing and protects everything after it.
3. What does she say about people who wait for complete evidence?
They arrive at the meeting after the decision has been made.
Listening 3 · Business Radio

The Price of Being Careful

A short report on what tightening credit costs. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the report say happens to lending after banks tighten their rules?
It falls quickly, and it takes far longer to come back than it took to fall.
2. Who does the report say pays for that, and why?
The customers who were only just above the line, because they are the first ones the new rule removes.
3. What does the report say is the hardest part for the person who owns the rule?
That the cost of tightening is visible in a month and the benefit takes a year, so the decision always looks wrong at first.

Chapter 6: Your Turn

One Decision, Three Times

Guided > Semi-free > Free

Role-Play 1 of 3 — Guided

Explain the Half Point

Scenario: Mark asks why the rate moved. Give the driver, say what you ruled out, separate what you know from what you assume, and finish with the decision and the trade-off — without being asked for it.

Keyword chips:

a driver to rule out an assumption a trade-off
Role-Play 2 of 3 — Semi-free

Ask Why Before You Agree

Scenario: An analyst tells you the rate moved because of the new product, and he sounds certain. Ask three questions before you accept it — what the evidence is, what he ruled out, and what else could explain it — and only then say what you think.

Keywords:

What is the evidence? a root cause an assumption however
Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

Your Real Decision, No Notes

Scenario: Explain a real decision of your own, end to end. What moved, what caused it, what you ruled out, what you still only assume, what you decided, and what it cost you. Say the whole chain out loud, without stopping.

No keywords, no notes, two minutes.
