Because, So and That Is Why -- Explaining a Decision

The reason behind a decision, said out loud. Key words: a driver, a trade-off, a root cause, an assumption, evidence, a consequence, to rule out, therefore, however, as a result. Structure: connectors of cause and consequence — and the sound of because.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A driver -- the thing that makes a number move
"The main driver of the rate is the cohort that joined in April."
A trade-off -- what you lose when you choose one thing over another
"Every credit policy is a trade-off between growth and risk."
A root cause -- the first reason behind a problem, not the last symptom
"The root cause was a rule somebody loosened nine months earlier."
An assumption -- something you believe is true without checking it
"The whole model rests on one assumption nobody has tested since 2022."
Evidence -- the facts that support what you say
"I have a theory, but I do not have evidence yet, and I will say both."
A consequence -- what happens because of a decision
"We accepted the consequence: less growth for two quarters."
To rule out -- to decide that something is not the reason
"We ruled out the model in the first hour, so the policy was next."
Therefore -- for that reason (formal, in writing)
"The sample was small. Therefore we waited another month."
However -- used to say the opposite of what came before
"Growth was strong. However, most of it came from one cohort."
As a result -- because of that (used at the start of the result)
"We tightened the rule. As a result, growth fell for two quarters."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

An explanation is a chain, and English gives you five links. Because takes a whole sentence after it: "We tightened the rule because the losses came from one cohort." Because of takes only a noun, with no verb: "The rate moved because of one large customer." Those two are not interchangeable, and mixing them is the most common mistake here: never because of the rate was high.

To put the result second, use so, after a comma: "The sample was small, so we waited another month." In writing, the same job is done by therefore and as a result, and they start a new sentence: "The sample was small. Therefore we waited." To turn against what you just said, use however, also at the start of a new sentence: "Growth was strong. However, most of it came from one cohort." These three belong to an email; because and so belong to a conversation.

Two last things. A sentence that begins with Because is only half a sentence until the result arrives: not Because the sample was small. but "Because the sample was small, we waited." And the sound. Because has two syllables, and the stress is on the second one: be-COZ. It does not end in a vowel, and the first syllable is short and almost swallowed. It is the single most common word in any explanation you will ever give in English, which is why it is worth five minutes of your life.

1. Why is "because of the rate was high" wrong?
A Because the rate cannot be a reason.
B Because because of takes only a noun, and this one has a verb after it.
C Because the sentence needs however instead.
2. Which of these belongs in an email rather than in a conversation?
A so
B therefore
C because
3. Where does the stress fall in the word because?
A On the first syllable: BE-cause.
B On the second syllable: be-COZ.
C The two syllables are equally strong.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Cause and Consequence

GRAMMAR

Because takes a sentence. Because of takes a noun. So takes the result.

WordWhat follows itExample
becausea full sentence (subject + verb)We tightened it because the losses came from one cohort.
because ofa noun only, no verbThe rate moved because of one cohort.
sothe result, after a commaThe sample was small, so we waited.
therefore · as a resulta new sentence — formal, for writingThe sample was small. Therefore we waited.
howevera new sentence that turns against the last oneGrowth was strong. However, most of it came from one cohort.
The soundbecause = be-COZ, stress on the second syllableNot BE-cause, and no vowel at the end.
The trapa sentence starting with Because needs the result tooNot Because the sample was small. Say Because the sample was small, we waited.

Never: because of the rate was high · "Because the sample was small." on its own · the sample was small, however we waited · BE-cause

Stage 2: Put the Explanation in Order

Order

Your boss asks why the rate moved. Put the whole chain in order.

?"The rate moved half a point, and the main driver is the April cohort."
?"It moved because that group reached its third payment this month."
?"We ruled out the model in the first hour, so the policy was next."
?"My assumption is that the root cause is a rule we loosened in March."
?"However, that is a theory, and I do not have evidence yet."
?"I want to tighten it again. As a result, we lose two quarters of growth."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A full sentence comes after the reason. You say:
A "The rate moved because of the cohort reached its third payment."
B "The rate moved because the cohort reached its third payment."
C "The rate moved because of the cohort reaching that it was the third payment."
Only a noun comes after the reason. You say:
A "The rate moved because of one large customer."
B "The rate moved because one large customer."
C "The rate moved therefore one large customer."
You want to say the result of a small sample, in conversation. You say:
A "The sample was small, however we waited another month."
B "The sample was small, so we waited another month."
C "The sample was small, because we waited another month."
You have a theory and no proof. Which answer sounds senior?
A "I am not sure. Maybe. I do not know."
B "The root cause is the rule we loosened in March."
C "My assumption is that the root cause is the March rule. However, I do not have evidence yet."
