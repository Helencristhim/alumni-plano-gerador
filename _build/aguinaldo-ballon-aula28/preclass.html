Lesson 28 -- Pre-class

The Language of Institutional Documents -- From the Page to the Room

How to explain a clause in clear spoken English. Key words: a provision, to stipulate, binding, to waive, a breach, jurisdiction, in force, a remedy, to terminate, a party. Structure: formal document language turned into speech -- shall becomes has to, provided that becomes as long as, in the event of becomes if.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A provision -- a part of a contract or a law that says what must or may happen
"The provision on early termination is only four lines long."
To stipulate -- to say clearly, in a contract or a rule, what must be done
"The contract stipulates a minimum investment every five years."
Binding -- having legal force, so that the parties have to obey it
"The arbitration decision is final and binding."
To waive -- to choose not to use a right or a rule that protects you
"The agency may waive the penalty in exceptional cases."
A breach -- the act of not doing what a contract or a law requires
"A delay of six months is a breach of the contract."
Jurisdiction -- the official power of a court or an agency over a place or a subject
"Disputes fall under the jurisdiction of the federal courts."
In force -- valid and in use, legally effective now
"The new rule has been in force since January."
A remedy -- what the law gives you to correct a wrong, such as money or an order
"The only remedy in the contract is a fine."
To terminate -- to end a contract or an agreement officially, before its normal end
"The state may terminate the concession after a serious breach."
A party -- one of the people or organizations that sign a contract
"Neither party may transfer the contract without consent."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Contracts and regulations are written in a special register. "The Concessionaire shall maintain the port" means the operator has to maintain it. "Shall not" means cannot, and "may" means can. When you explain a document to people who are not lawyers, you keep the rule and change the words.

The conditions change too. "Provided that the operator pays" becomes "as long as the operator pays". Notice the verb: after provided that and as long as, English uses the present, never will. "In the event of a breach" becomes "if there is a breach". "Subject to approval" becomes "only if the agency approves". And "notwithstanding clause 9" becomes "even if clause 9 says something different".

Finally, use real names. On the page, the parties are the Grantor and the Concessionaire. In the room, they are the state and the operator. The rule is exactly as precise as before. Only the register is different.

1. How do you say "shall maintain" in the room?
A "has to maintain"
B "shall to maintain", because the modal needs to before the verb in formal English
C "will maybe maintain"
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "The state pays, provided that the damage will come from a storm and not from the operator."
B "The state pays, provided that the damage comes from a storm."
C "The state pays, provided that the damage came from a storm."
3. What is the spoken version of "notwithstanding clause 9"?
A "because of clause 9"
B "in addition to clause 9, and also everything that clause 9 says about it"
C "even if clause 9 says something different"

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- From the Page to the Room

GRAMMAR

Keep the rule. Change the modal, the condition and the name of the party.

On the pageIn the roomExample
shallhas toThe operator has to maintain the port.
shall not / maycannot / canThe operator cannot sell it. The agency can waive it.
provided thatas long as + presentas long as the operator pays
in the event of + nounif + subject + verbif there is a breach

Never: shall to maintain · provided that it will pay · in the event of the operator fails · subject of approval

Stage 2: Put the Explanation in Order

Order

You have sixty seconds to explain clause 14 to investors. Put your explanation in order.

?"Let me explain clause 14 in plain English."
?"First, the operator has to keep the port in good condition."
?"If it does not, the state can apply a penalty or end the contract."
?"But only after ninety days of notice."
?"And the state cannot end it during an emergency, even if there is a breach."
?"So, in short: who has to do what, by when, and what happens if not."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

An investor asks what clause 14.1 means. You say:
A "The Concessionaire shall maintain the port infrastructure in good working order for the full term."
B "The operator has to keep the port in good condition for the whole contract."
C "The operator shall to keep the port."
An engineer asks: "Can the state cancel the contract tomorrow?" You answer:
A "Only if there is a breach, and only after ninety days of notice."
B "In the event of the operator fails, the Grantor may terminate, subject of notice."
C "Yes, the state can always do what it wants with the contract, at any time, with no notice."
Which sentence is correct?
A "The tariff goes up only if the agency will approve it next month."
B "The tariff goes up only if the agency approves it."
C "The tariff goes up subject of the agency."
You want to explain "notwithstanding clause 9" to a journalist:
A "Even if clause 9 says something different."
B "Because clause 9 says the same thing, and so the two clauses work together."
C "Notwithstanding."
