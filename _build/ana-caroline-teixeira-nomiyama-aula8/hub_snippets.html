English Without Borders -- I Used to / I’m Getting Used to

Accents, confidence and speaking again after many years. Key words: an accent, fluent, a native speaker, to pronounce, to speak up, shy, confident, a honeymoon, to make a mistake, rusty. Structure: used to + verb x be used to / get used to + -ing.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An accent -- the way people from a place or a country pronounce a language
"Her accent is from Texas, but I understand her well."
Fluent -- able to speak a language easily and well
"My husband is fluent in English."
A native speaker -- a person who learned a language as a child, as their first language
"Most people in the meeting were not native speakers."
To pronounce -- to say the sounds of a word
"How do you pronounce this word?"
To speak up -- to start talking and say what you think, especially when it is not easy
"In the next meeting, I want to speak up."
Shy -- nervous and uncomfortable when you talk to other people
"I was too shy to ask the guide a question."
Confident -- sure that you can do something well
"After a few weeks, I felt more confident."
A honeymoon -- a trip that a couple takes right after the wedding
"We went to Japan on our honeymoon."
To make a mistake -- to do or say something that is not correct
"Everybody makes mistakes when they speak a new language."
Rusty -- not as good as before, because you did not practice for a long time
"My English is a bit rusty, but it is coming back."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

When Ana was a teenager, she used to study English every Saturday morning, and she was good at it. Then she went to law school, started working, and stopped. Ten years later, her English is a bit rusty. She understands a lot, but speaking is harder.

On her honeymoon in Japan, she wasn’t used to the accent, and she felt shy. Her husband is fluent, so he used to do all the talking on their trips. At the wineries in Italy, she understood the guides, but she never asked a question.

Now things are changing. In her new job, she is getting used to hearing English every week, from native speakers and from people of many countries. She still makes mistakes, but she is used to speaking up in short meetings, and she feels more confident every month.

1. Why does the text say “she used to study English”?
A Because it was a habit in the past, and it is not true now.
B Because she studies English every Saturday now.
C Because it is becoming normal.
2. What does “she is getting used to hearing English” mean?
A Hearing English was always easy for her.
B She heard English in the past, on her honeymoon in Japan and at the wineries in Italy, but not now.
C Hearing English is becoming normal for her, step by step.
3. Who used to do the talking on their trips?
A Ana, because she studied English on Saturdays and was the best student in her class.
B Her husband, because he is fluent.
C The tour guides at the wineries.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used to x Be Used to x Get Used to

GRAMMAR

Use used to + verb for a habit or a state in the past that is not true now. Use be used to + noun / -ing for something that is normal for you. Use get used to + noun / -ing for something that is becoming normal. For habits now, use usually.

JobFormExample
Used to + verba past habit or state that is not true nowI used to study English on Saturdays.
Negative and questiondidn’t use to / Did you use to...?I didn’t use to speak in meetings.
Be used to + noun / -ingsomething is normal and familiar for youI’m used to working late.
Get used to + noun / -ingthe process: something is becoming normalI’m getting used to the accent.
After be / get used toto is a preposition here: -ing, never the base verbI’m used to speaking, not used to speak.
The trapused to + verb is only for the past; for now, use usuallyNot I use to wake up at six. Say I usually wake up at six.

Never: I use to wake up at six · I’m used to speak · I didn’t used to · I’m getting used to wake up

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A colleague asks if you always took the subway. Before your new job, you drove. You say:
A "No, I was used to drive to work every day when I worked at my old law firm."
B "No, I use to drive to work."
C "No, I used to drive to work."
You want to say that the Texas accent is becoming easier for you. You say:
A "I’m getting used to the Texas accent."
B "I’m getting used to understand the Texas accent of my colleagues in the Miami office."
C "I used to the Texas accent."
You did not understand a client on a call. You say:
A "Sorry, I’m not used to understand you."
B "Sorry, could you say that again a bit more slowly?"
C "Sorry, repeat."
A friend asks how your English is after ten years without practice. You say:
A "It’s a bit rusty, but it’s coming back."
B "It’s a bit fluent, because I didn’t practice for a long time and I forgot many words."
C "It’s a bit shy."
