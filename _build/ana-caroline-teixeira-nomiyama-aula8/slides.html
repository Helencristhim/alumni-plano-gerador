Chapter 4: The Code

I Used to... I’m Getting Used to...

The past habit, the normal thing and the change

Grammar Discovery

Past, Normal or Changing?

"She used to take a class every Saturday morning."

"When people spoke to her, she used to let her husband answer."

"Nobody is used to all the accents."

"I’m getting used to asking the questions."

Look at the red words: a habit in the past that is not true now. Now the blue words: something that is normal and familiar. And the green words: something that is becoming normal, step by step. After the blue and the green forms, look at the next word: a noun or a verb with -ing.

JobFormExample
Used to + verba past habit or state that is not true nowI used to study English on Saturdays.
Negative and questiondidn’t use to / Did you use to...?I didn’t use to speak in meetings.
Be used to + noun / -ingsomething is normal and familiar for youI’m used to working late.
Get used to + noun / -ingthe process: something is becoming normalI’m getting used to the accent.
After be / get used toto is a preposition here: -ing, never the base verbI’m used to speaking, not used to speak.
The trapused to + verb is only for the past; for now, use usuallyNot I use to wake up at six. Say I usually wake up at six.

In one line: used to + verb = before, not now; be used to + -ing = normal; get used to + -ing = becoming normal.

Listening 1 · An Australian Engineer

A New Accent in Houston

An engineer from Sydney remembers his first months working in Texas. Sound first — no text.

1. What surprised him when he arrived in Houston?
People did not always understand him, even though English is his first language.
2. Which word was a problem for him, and why?
Water: he did not pronounce the r, so people did not understand.
3. What does he say about accents today?
He is used to the Texas accent now, and he thinks every accent is just practice.
Listening 2 · A German Lawyer

From Silent to Speaking Up

A lawyer from Berlin explains how she stopped being silent in international meetings. Sound first — no text.

1. What did she use to do in international meetings?
She used to stay silent and only take notes.
2. Why did she stay silent?
She was afraid of making mistakes with her accent and her grammar.
3. What are her two tips?
Prepare one question before the meeting, and speak in the first ten minutes.
Listening 3 · A Tour Guide in Kyoto

Getting Used to Japanese English

A tour guide from India talks about understanding English in Japan. Sound first — no text.

1. Why was English in Japan difficult for her at first?
The rhythm and some sounds were different, so she did not understand everything.
2. What did she use to do when she did not understand?
She used to smile and say yes.
3. What does she do now?
She asks people to repeat, and she listens to the rhythm and the important words.
