Vocabulary

Nothing new here. Listen to each expression, say it before you read the definition, and remember which lesson it came from.

walk you through -- to take someone step by step through something, in order
"Let me walk you through the three entities first."
let me start by -- the phrase that opens the first move of a presentation
"Let me start by saying where the region stands today."
that brings me to -- the phrase that carries the audience from one part to the next
"That brings me to the headcount plan for next year."
as you can see -- the phrase that points the audience to what is in front of them
"As you can see, the three markets did not move at the same speed."
to recap -- to say the main points again, short and in order, before you close
"To recap: three entities, one policy, two open decisions."
any questions so far -- the check you make before you move on to the next part
"Any questions so far, before I go into the numbers?"
in closing -- the phrase that announces the last part of a presentation
"In closing, I would like to come back to the people agenda."

Stage 3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

This is the checklist of the first block, and it is also the checklist of your presentation. Nine lessons produced four structures, and all four appear when you describe your own job.

The first one is the word family, from lessons 1 and 4. After the verb be, the adjective: I am responsible for three entities. After my, the noun: hiring is my responsibility. And the preposition is part of the word: responsible for, report to.

The second one is the present simple, from lesson 3. Your routine is not in the past. Every morning I go through my inbox, I decide what the priority is, I get back to the managers by end of day. "I needed to approve" describes something that finished; "I need to approve" describes your Tuesday.

The third one is purpose, from lesson 5. So that, when the purpose belongs to somebody else: we send a local adaptation so that headquarters understands the agreement. In order to, when it belongs to the same subject: we changed the policy in order to have one rule.

The fourth one is the polite refusal, from lesson 4. I'm afraid we can't, never "I have afraid", and always with a reason and a date behind it. In a presentation, this structure appears when you describe a decision you had to communicate, and it is the moment a listener decides whether you sound like a professional or like a translation.

The presentation itself has five parts and ten minutes: the business, your role, your routine, one real case and what is not in your scope. The last part is not modesty, it is precision: a manager who knows where your work ends stops sending you the wrong requests.

1. Which structure is used in "I am responsible for three entities"?
A The word family: the adjective after the verb be.
B The present simple for routine.
C A purpose clause.
2. Why does the presentation include the part about what is not in your scope?
A Because a manager who knows where your work ends stops sending the wrong requests.
B Because it makes the presentation longer.
C Because HR is not allowed to talk about payroll.
3. Which sentence follows the rule from lesson 5?
A We send a local adaptation so that headquarters understands the agreement.
B We send a local adaptation for headquarters understand the agreement.
C We send a local adaptation to headquarters understand the agreement.

Stage 4: Grammar Tip -- The four structures of block one

GRAMMAR

Everything the block taught, on one page. Use this as your checklist while you prepare the presentation.

FormWhat it doesExample
word families (lessons 1 and 4)the form follows the place in the sentenceI am responsible for / my responsibility / the request was approved
present simple (lesson 3)the routine lives in the presentEvery morning I go through my inbox.
purpose (lesson 5)so that + person + verb; in order to + verbWe send it so that headquarters understands.
polite refusal (lesson 4)be + adjective, with a reason and a dateI'm afraid we can't ... at this stage.
Watch outthe four classic slips together: "responsible of", "I needed to approve", "to her understand" and "I have afraid". Read your presentation once looking only for these.
