Chapter 4: The Code

The Four Structures of Block One

Word families, present routine, purpose, polite no

Grammar Discovery

Where Does Each Sentence Come From?

"I am responsible for three entities in three countries."

"Every morning I go through my inbox before the first meeting."

"We changed the policy so that the three countries have one rule."

"I'm afraid we can't approve a new position at this stage."

Each sentence comes from one lesson of this block. Which lesson, and what was the rule?

FormWhat it doesExample
word families (lessons 1 and 4)the form changes with the place in the sentenceI am responsible for. It is my responsibility. The request was approved.
present simple (lesson 3)the routine lives in the present, not in the pastEvery morning I go through my inbox. Not "I needed to approve".
purpose (lesson 5)so that + person + verb; in order to + verbWe changed it so that the countries have one rule.
polite refusal (lesson 4)be + adjective, and the door left openI'm afraid we can't ... at this stage.
Watch outmixing them under pressure: "I have afraid", "responsible of", "I needed to approve" and "to her understand". These four are the checklist of your presentation.

Sound Check

Say It Clearly

H and R · point 1 of 14

Why this one is hard for a Brazilian speaker

In Portuguese: Silent H and throat R — the same two habits as lesson 1.

In English: Breathed H and tongue-back R. Under the pressure of presenting, these are the first two to slip.

If it slips: The first sentence of a presentation is the one the room remembers, and it is the one that carries "HR".

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Two Ways to Present the Same Job

The list and the explanation, side by side

Listening 1

The Presentation That Lists

A business partner presents the role as a list of tasks. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. How does this speaker organise the presentation?
As a list of tasks: hiring, performance, budget, policies, one after the other.
2. What does the speaker say about the companies?
Only that there are three of them, without saying what each one does.
3. What is missing at the end?
Any example of a real situation, and anything about the people she works with.
Listening 2

The Presentation That Explains

The same role, presented through the business. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. How does the second speaker open the presentation?
With the business: three companies, three clients, and only then her own role inside it.
2. Which real example does she use?
A hiring freeze: how one request travels from the manager to the CFO, and what she does at each step.
3. What does she say about what is not in her scope?
That payroll operations belong to shared services, and she says it on purpose, to show where her work starts and ends.

That brings me to … moves to the next part
Any questions so far? … checks before moving on

In closing … announces the last part
Sample script for this lesson. The real presentation carries your own examples.
1. How long is the section about your own role?
Two minutes, the same as the business and the routine.
2. Why does the script include a section about what is not in your scope?
Because it shows where the work starts and ends, and it prevents wrong requests.
3. What happens at the end of the presentation?
The teacher plays the manager and asks questions, and one part is presented again.
