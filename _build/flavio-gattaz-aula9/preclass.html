Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

Tell Me About a Time When -- The Behavioral Question

The hardest question in the room, and the only honest answer is a story with other people in it. Key words: a pushback, buy-in, to align, a stakeholder, to defuse, to follow through, a turnaround, to own something, to go over somebody's head, accountable, a workaround, morale. Structure: reported speech.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A pushback -- open resistance from people who do not agree, said to your face
"There was pushback from two of the engineers, and they were right."
Buy-in -- the real agreement of the people who have to make a decision work
"I had the authority. What I did not have yet was buy-in, and that is slower."
To align -- to get people who want different things to want the same outcome
"It took three meetings to align the client, the architect and my own team."
A stakeholder -- anybody whose work, money or reputation is affected by the decision
"The neighbours were stakeholders too, and we found that out late."
To defuse -- to take the danger out of a tense situation before it breaks open
"I defused it by asking him to say the whole thing again, slowly, in front of everybody."
To follow through -- to do the part that comes after the decision, when nobody is watching any more
"Announcing it took ten minutes. Following through took nine months."
A turnaround -- the change from a failing situation to a working one, over a period you can name
"The turnaround took fourteen months and nobody called it that until it was over."
To own something -- to take public responsibility for a result, including a bad one
"It was my call and I owned it in front of the whole team, which cost me nothing."
To go over somebody's head -- to take a matter to somebody's boss without telling them first
"He went over my head, and the annoying part is that he was right to."
Accountable -- answerable for a result by name, so that everybody knows who it belongs to
"Three people were involved and one person was accountable. That is the whole difference."
A workaround -- a way of getting round a problem without actually solving it
"We had a workaround for two years, and everybody had stopped calling it that."
Morale -- how a team feels about the work, which decides what it does without being asked
"Morale is not a soft number. You can read it in how late people report a problem."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A behavioral answer is a story with other people in it, and other people means reporting what they said. The site manager walked in and said, "the schedule is not real". Reported, that becomes: he said the schedule was not real. Every verb takes one step back. A present becomes a past, a past becomes a past perfect, and will becomes would: "we will lose one of them" turns into he said we would lose one of them. Portuguese allows the verb to stay where it is, which is exactly why this is the structure Brazilian speakers skip.

Then there is the pair that catches everybody. Say and tell do the same job with different company. Say takes the words: he said that the client had refused. Tell takes a person first: he told me that the client had refused. There is no he said me in English, and there is no he told that either. The rule is short and the mistake is permanent unless you fix it deliberately, because Portuguese puts the person in the same place for both.

Questions behave differently again. A reported question stops looking like a question: no inversion, no question mark. "Why did you approve it?" becomes she asked me why I had approved it. A yes-or-no question needs whether or if: he asked whether we had a contingency. This matters more than it sounds, because a panel does not want a summary of a meeting, it wants what people said in it — the pushback, the question you were asked, the moment you decided to own the result. Those are all somebody else's words, and this is how you carry them.

1. What happens to the verb when you report what somebody said?
A It stays exactly as it was.
B It takes one step back: present to past, past to past perfect, will to would.
C It always becomes an infinitive.
2. What is the difference between "say" and "tell" in this structure?
A "Tell" takes a person first; "say" takes the words. There is no "he said me".
B "Say" is formal and "tell" is informal.
C "Tell" is only used for orders.
3. According to the text, what happens to a question when it is reported?
A It keeps the question order and the question mark.
B It stops looking like a question: no inversion, no question mark, and "whether" for yes-or-no.
C It becomes an order.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

Every verb takes one step back. Tell needs a person, say does not.

JobFormExample
Present becomes pastis / are → was / wereHe said the client was angry.
Past becomes past perfectrefused → had refusedHe told me the client had refused.
Will becomes wouldwill / can / may → would / could / mightThey said they would not sign.
Say and tellsay (that) ... / tell somebody (that) ...He said it. / He told me it.
Reported questionasked + why / which + statement orderShe asked me why I had approved it.
Yes or no questionasked whether / ifHe asked whether we had a contingency.
Pronouns and timeyou → I, today → that dayHe said he would call me that day.

Never: he said me that · he told that · he said the client is angry · she asked me why did I approve it · he asked me if did we have a contingency.

Stage 2: Put the Answer in Order

Order

An interviewer has asked about a time when somebody told you that you were wrong. Put your answer in a logical order.

?"In 2019 we had just taken a fourth project."
?"My site manager came in and told me the schedule was not real."
?"He asked me which project I wanted to lose."
?"I asked him to say it again in the Monday meeting, in front of everybody."
?"We handed the fourth project back and lost the fee, and I owned it with the client."
?"What stayed is that people tell me things earlier now."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to report what your site manager told you. You say:
A "He said me that the schedule was not real."
B "He told me the schedule was not real."
C "He told that the schedule is not real."
You want to report a question he asked you. You say:
A "He asked me which project I wanted to lose."
B "He asked me which project did I want to lose."
C "He asked me which project do I want to lose?"
The interviewer asks what the outcome was. You answer:
A "In the end I was right and the team understood that."
B "We handed the project back and lost the fee. It was my call, and I owned it with the client."
C "There were some difficulties but everything was resolved."
You want to credit the person who disagreed with you. You say:
A "Somebody on the team had a different opinion at the time."
B "My site manager was right, and I have said so since, in front of the team."
C "In a way we were both right about different things."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Tell me about a time when somebody on your team told you that you were wrong. Give the situation with a date, let at least two other people speak in the story, say what it cost, and say what stayed afterwards. Do not be the only person in your own answer.
