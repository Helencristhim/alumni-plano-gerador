Lesson 9 · The Behavioral Question

Tell Me About a Time When

Six words that begin the hardest question in the room, and the only honest answer is a story with other people in it.

Warm-Up

The Case Had Numbers. This One Has People.

Last time you argued about a decision from the outside. The behavioral question puts you inside it, with everybody who was in the room and everything they said.

In two sentences: tell me about a time when somebody on your team told you that you were wrong. What did they say, and what did you say back?

Today’s Goal

A Story With Voices In It

1. The Words

a pushback, buy-in, a stakeholder, morale...

2. One Structure

saying what other people said, without quoting

3. Your Answer

situation, what you did, what happened, what stayed

A behavioral answer is not an opinion about yourself. It is a story with other people in it, and a story with people in it means reporting what they said — which is a structure in its own right.

One Question

What Did He Actually Say?

Think of the last time somebody on site told you something you did not want to hear. Say what they said, in one sentence, without quoting them.

Then the second one: what did you tell them, and what did you ask them?

Chapter 3: The Code

Saying What Other People Said

Reported speech, and the two verbs that behave differently

Grammar Discovery

What Moved in the Second Line?

"The client is angry." → "He said the client was angry."

"The client refused." → "He told me the client had refused."

"We will not sign." → "They said they would not sign."

"Why did you approve it?" → "She asked me why I had approved it."

Everything in blue is what the person actually said. Everything in red is how you report it, and three things have moved: the verb has taken one step back in time, the pronoun has changed owner, and in the last one the question has stopped looking like a question.

JobFormExample
Present becomes pastis / are → was / wereHe said the client was angry.
Past becomes past perfectrefused → had refusedHe told me the client had refused.
Will becomes wouldwill / can / may → would / could / mightThey said they would not sign.
Say and tellsay (that) ... / tell somebody (that) ...He said it. / He told me it.
Reported questionasked + why / whether + statement orderShe asked me why I had approved it.
Yes or no questionasked whether / ifHe asked whether we had a contingency.
The trapsay never takes a person directlyNot he said me. Say he told me.

In one line: every verb takes one step back, tell needs a person and say does not, and a reported question stops sounding like one.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Behavioral Question, at Full Speed

One interview, three recordings, one score sheet

Dialogue

The Third Interview, Twelve Minutes In

G
Tell me about a time when somebody on your team told you that you were wrong.
F
Two thousand and nineteen. We had taken a fourth project and my site manager came into my office and told me the schedule was not real.
G
What did he say, exactly?
F
He said we would lose the fourth project or the third one, and that I had already chosen without knowing it. Then he asked me which one I wanted to lose.
G
That is quite a pushback. What did you do with it?
F
I asked him to say it again in the Monday meeting, in front of the other three engineers. It defused it, and it also meant I could not quietly ignore him afterwards.
G
And what happened?
F
We handed the fourth project back and lost the fee. It was my call, and I owned it with the client. What stayed is that people now tell me things earlier, which I can read in the morale and in nothing else. Does the program use panels for this, or is it always one interviewer?
Comprehension

About Grace

1. What does Grace ask for in her first question?
A time when somebody on his team told him that he was wrong.
2. What does she ask immediately after his first answer, and what is she not asking for?
She asks what the man said, exactly. She is not asking how he felt about it or what he thought of the man.
3. What are Grace's last two questions, and how long are they?
"What did you do with it?" and "And what happened?" Both are very short, which keeps the story on his side of the table.
Listening 1 · A Candidate

A Time When Somebody Told Her No

A candidate answering a behavioral question, recorded in a practice session. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the head of operations tell her, and what did she ask him?
He told her the plan would not survive the first week and that two of his people had already said so. She asked him what he would do instead.
2. What did she do about the disagreement, and why did she do it in public?
She asked him to bring it to the leadership meeting. She says a private objection can be quietly ignored, and one said in front of eight people cannot.
3. What was the outcome, and what does she say stayed afterwards?
They changed the plan and lost six weeks. What stayed is that people bring her bad news earlier, and she says that is worth more than the six weeks.
Listening 2 · An Interviewer

What We Are Actually Measuring

An interviewer on why the behavioral question exists at all. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say the question is really testing?
Whether the candidate can describe other people accurately, because everybody can describe themselves.
2. What is wrong with an answer that is all I, in her words?
If nobody else says anything in the story, either the candidate was working alone or they have edited everybody else out, and neither is good news.
3. What does she say about candidates who name the person who was right?
That they are the ones the panel remembers, and that it is the cheapest thing a candidate can do and almost nobody does it.
Listening 3 · Business Radio

Why Nobody Tells the Boss Anything

A radio report on how long bad news takes to travel upwards. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the study measure, and what was the headline figure?
How long a known problem took to reach the person who could decide about it. The average was eleven weeks.
2. What did the researchers find about the teams where bad news travelled fastest?
It was not that those teams had fewer problems. It was that somebody senior had visibly owned a bad result in front of everybody in the previous year.
3. What does the report say about workarounds?
That a workaround is a problem that somebody decided not to report, and that after two years nobody in the team remembers it was ever a workaround.

Scenario: The interviewer asks a behavioral question you have not prepared. Give the situation with a date, let at least two other people speak in the story, say what it cost, and say what stayed afterwards. Do not be the only person in your own answer.
