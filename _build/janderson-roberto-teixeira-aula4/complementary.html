"The paradox of choice" -- Barry Schwartz, TED

Nineteen minutes from a psychologist on why more options make us less happy and slower to decide. Connection to Lesson 4: it is the same problem you worked on in class, one level up -- comparing is easy, and finally choosing is the hard part. The page carries the full English transcript.

Tip: watch the first ten minutes with English subtitles. Count how many comparatives he uses in one minute -- the answer will surprise you, and it is the fastest proof that this structure is everywhere.

"How advertisers make us spend money" -- 6 Minute English, BBC Learning English

Six minutes on the tricks that make one option look better than another. Connection to Lesson 4: this is the vocabulary of price and value in its natural habitat, and you will hear value for money, a deal and overpriced used by native speakers without any effort. Audio, vocabulary and full transcript are free on the page.

Tip: listen once without the transcript. Then write down two sentences where a speaker compares two things, and say them out loud with your own examples.

"Comparatives and superlatives" -- 6 Minute Grammar, BBC Learning English

Six minutes on today's exact structure: when the adjective takes -er, when it takes more, and how the superlative works with the. Connection to Lesson 4: it drills the syllable rule you met in class, and it covers the three irregular adjectives you cannot avoid.

Tip: pause after each example and compare two real things from your own week out loud. Then repeat the sentence with much or slightly in front.
