Chapter 3: Have You Met?

Have you met...? · Have you been...?

questions about your experience

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Have you met Anna?" -- "No, I haven't."

"Have you been to Miami?" -- "Yes, I have."

"Have you met our host?" -- "Yes, I have."

"Let me introduce you to Anna."

FormUseExample
Have you met + person?ask if you know a personHave you met Mike?
Have you been to + place?ask if you visited a placeHave you been to Brazil?
Yes, I have.short answer -- yesHave you met Anna? -- Yes, I have.
No, I haven't.short answer -- noHave you been to Texas? -- No, I haven't.
Let me introduce you to + personpresent one person to anotherLet me introduce you to my colleague.

met = conhecer (uma pessoa) · been to = ir / estar (num lugar). Answer with have or haven't, not with met. / Responda com have ou haven't, sem repetir o verbo.

Chapter 4: The Event

A Night in Miami

an invitation · a conversation with Mike

Real Artifact

The Invitation

You are invited

Business Aviation Networking Night

Guest: Karina Macedo -- Aircraft sales, Sao Paulo

Host: Anna Brooks -- Brooks Flight School, Texas

Date: Friday, June 12 · 7 p.m. to 10 p.m.

Place: Ocean Hotel, Miami -- Room 3

Program: 7 p.m. welcome · 8 p.m. talk by the host · 9 p.m. dinner

Bring your business cards. Meet three hundred guests from twelve countries.

Dialogue

Mike at the Event

M
Karina! Welcome to Miami. It is great to see you in person.
K
Hi Mike! Nice to see you too. This event is very big!
M
Yes, there are three hundred guests tonight. Have you been to an event like this before?
K
No, I haven't. It is my first time. I want to make new connections.
M
Great. Have you met Anna Brooks? She is the host.
K
No, I haven't. Who is she?
M
She has a flight school in Texas, and she knows many buyers. Let me introduce you to her.
K
Thank you, Mike! What can I say to her?
M
Start with small talk. Ask: Have you been to Brazil? Then talk about your business.
K
Perfect. After the event, I can keep in touch with her by email.
