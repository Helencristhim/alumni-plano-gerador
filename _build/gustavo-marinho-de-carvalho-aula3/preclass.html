A delay -- a period of time when something happens later than planned

To come up -- to appear suddenly, without being planned
"Something has come up on our side, and it changes Friday."
To keep you posted -- to keep telling someone what is happening, as it happens
"I will keep you posted, even if there is nothing new."
A workaround -- a way of getting round a problem without removing its cause
"We have a workaround for this week, but it is not a solution."
To run into -- to meet a difficulty you were not expecting
"We ran into a problem on Monday and it cost us two days."
Realistic -- possible in practice, not only on paper
"Friday is not realistic. Tuesday is."
To commit to -- to promise firmly that you will do something
"I can commit to Tuesday, and I would rather promise less and deliver."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Every promise you make at work has a condition hidden inside it, and English puts that condition in a shape of its own. If you send the brief today, I will have the draft ready by Friday. Two halves: the condition, and what follows from it. The strange part for a Brazilian speaker is that the condition half stays in the present, even though the whole sentence is about next week.

That is where the most common mistake in this structure lives. In Portuguese the future is already inside the verb of the condition, so the hand wants to write if you will send. English does not allow it, in any register, in any variety. The rule has no exceptions worth learning at this level: after if, present. If we run into another problem, I will call you the same day.

Two other words do the same job with a twist. Unless is if not in one word: unless we hear from them before noon, we will keep the old date. Because the not is already inside it, unless you do not send it says the opposite of what you mean. And as long as is the condition you are willing to promise on: as long as nothing else comes up, Tuesday is realistic. Notice what that last sentence really does. It is not a weaker promise. It is a clearer one, because it names the only thing that could break it.

1. Why does the condition half stay in the present?
A Because the sentence is about something that already happened.
B Because that is the shape English uses, even when the sentence is about the future.
C Because the condition is less important than the promise.
2. According to the text, why is “unless you do not send it” wrong?
A Because unless already contains the not, so the sentence says the opposite.
B Because unless can only be used in writing.
C Because unless needs will after it.
3. What does the text say “as long as nothing else comes up” does to a promise?
A It makes the promise weaker, so it should be avoided with a client.
B It only works in writing, never on a call.
C It makes the promise clearer, because it names the only thing that could break it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- First Conditional

GRAMMAR

The half with the condition stays in the present, even when the whole sentence is about next week.

JobFormExample
A real, likely conditionif + present simple, will + base verbIf you send it today, I will have it ready.
The same condition, in the negativeunless + present simple (= if not)Unless you send it today, I will not have it ready.
The condition you are promising onas long as / provided thatAs long as nothing comes up, Tuesday is realistic.
The order is freeeither half can come firstI will call you if anything changes.
The commaonly when the condition comes firstIf it slips, I will call. / I will call if it slips.
The trapnever will after ifNot If you will send it. Say If you send it.
The second trapunless is already negativeNot Unless you do not send it. Say Unless you send it.

Never: If you will send it · Unless you do not send it · If nothing is coming up · If we will run into a problem

Order

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You find out on Tuesday that Friday will not happen. The client does not know. You:
A Call the same day, say what has changed, and give a new date with a condition.
B Wait until Friday, in case the team catches up in time.
C Send nothing and mention it at the next scheduled meeting.
The client asks what happens if the file arrives after six. The correct answer is:
A If it will arrive after six, it goes out the next morning.
B If it arrives after six, it goes out the next morning.
C If it is arriving after six, it will go out the next morning.
You want to say that silence from them means yes. You say:
A Unless you tell me otherwise, I will assume Monday works.
B Unless you do not tell me otherwise, I will assume Monday works.
C If you will not tell me otherwise, I assume Monday works.
Your client in England says your new date “might be a bit tight”. The strongest move is:
A Thank her for the flexibility and move on to the next point.
B Treat it as a no and ask what would need to change for it to work.
C Apologise three times and offer to work through the weekend.
