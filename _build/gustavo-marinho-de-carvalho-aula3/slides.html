Chapter 3: If, Unless, As Long As

Three Words That Carry a Condition

A promise with a condition is not a weaker promise. It is a clearer one.

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Do?

"If you send the brief today, I will have the draft ready by Friday."

"Unless we hear from them before noon, we will keep the old date."

"As long as nothing else comes up, Tuesday is realistic."

"I will keep you posted if anything changes."

The red half is the condition, and it is in the present even though it is about the future. The blue word already contains a not. The orange one is the condition you are promising on. Notice sentence four: the condition can come second, and then there is no comma.

JobFormExample
A real, likely conditionif + present simple, will + base verbIf you send it today, I will have it ready.
The same condition, in the negativeunless + present simple (= if not)Unless you send it today, I will not have it ready.
The condition you are promising onas long as / provided thatAs long as nothing comes up, Tuesday is realistic.
The order is freeboth halves can come firstI will call you if anything changes.
The commaonly when the condition comes firstIf it slips, I will call. / I will call if it slips.
The trapnever will after ifNot If you will send it. Say If you send it.
The second trapunless is already negativeNot Unless you do not send it. Say Unless you send it.

In one line: the half with the condition stays in the present, even when the whole sentence is about next week.

Dialogue

The Call You Make on Tuesday

C
Gustavo, hello. This is a surprise — we were not speaking until Thursday.
G
I know, and that is why I am calling. Something has come up on our side and Friday has moved. I wanted you to hear it from me today, not on Friday.
C
I see. How big is the delay?
G
Two days. We ran into a problem with one of the files on Monday. If we work through it tomorrow, I can commit to Tuesday morning.
C
Tuesday might be a bit tight for us, to be honest. Our own review starts on Tuesday afternoon.
G
Then let me offer a workaround. I can send you the first half on Monday, so your team starts, and the rest on Tuesday morning.
C
That would help, actually. And if anything else changes?
G
I will keep you posted, even if there is nothing new. What else do you need from me before your review?
Comprehension

About Claire

1. Why is Claire surprised at the beginning of the call?
Because they were not going to speak until Thursday, and he called on Tuesday instead.
2. What is the first thing she asks about, and what does she not ask about?
She asks how big the delay is. She does not ask why it happened.
3. She says Tuesday “might be a bit tight”. What does she mean, and how do you know?
She means Tuesday does not work for her. You know because of the reason she gives immediately afterwards: their own review starts on Tuesday afternoon.
Listening 1 · Business Radio

The Damage Is Not the Delay

A short radio piece on what a moved deadline actually costs. Sound first — no text.

1. Where does the damage come from, according to the speaker?
Not from the delay itself, but from how the other side finds out about it.
2. What changes if they find out on the day the work was due?
It stops being about the calendar and starts being about whether they can trust what you tell them, which is more expensive and lasts longer.
3. What three sentences does she say you need?
One saying what has changed, one saying what you are doing about it, and one asking what they need from you.
Listening 2 · A Voice Note

A Bit Tight

A voice note about a sentence that means the opposite of what it looks like. Sound first — no text.

1. What does “that might be a bit tight” actually mean?
It is a no, delivered in the politest possible way. It is not a small doubt and it is not encouragement.
2. What two other sentences does he say do the same job?
“I am not sure that would work” and “we could possibly look at that”.
3. What does he recommend doing when you hear one of them?
Do not thank them and move on. Ask a direct question: what would need to change for this to work?
Real Document

The Email That Follows the Call

Revised timeline — as discussed
Gustavo Marinho → Claire Hensley · Tuesday, 15:40

Hello Claire,

Thank you for taking the call at short notice. In writing, so that we are on the same page:

1. First half — Monday, so that your team can start.
2. Second half — Tuesday morning, as long as nothing else comes up.
3. If we run into anything on Thursday, I will call you the same day.

Unless you tell me otherwise, I will assume Monday works for the first half. And I will keep you posted on Thursday either way, even if there is nothing new to report.

Kind regards,
Gustavo

Sample email written for this lesson, following the call in the dialogue above.
1. Point 2 carries a condition. What is it, and what happens if the condition fails?
The condition is “as long as nothing else comes up”. If something does come up, point 3 applies: he calls the same day.
2. “Unless you tell me otherwise” — what is he asking Claire to do?
Nothing. Unless means if not, so silence is agreement. It is a way of moving forward without asking for another reply.
3. He promises to write on Thursday “even if there is nothing new”. Why does that matter?
Because silence is what makes the other side worry. A message saying nothing has changed is still information, and it is what keeping someone posted means.
