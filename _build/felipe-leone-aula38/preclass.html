Lesson 38 -- Pre-class

Presenting Results -- There Was, There Were and Since

Ten to twenty minutes before class. How to give numbers about a past period and say what has changed since then. Key words: an increase, a drop, a quarter, a target, to grow, to fall, a trend.
0%
▼

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An increase -- a rise in a number or an amount
"There was an increase in online applications."
A drop -- a fall in a number or an amount
"There was a small drop in late payments."
A quarter -- a period of three months in the business year
"The first quarter was slow for the whole market."
A target -- a number that a team wants to reach
"We reached the target two weeks early."
To grow -- to become bigger over time
"Our client base has grown in the south."
To fall -- to go down in number or value
"Complaints have fallen since the new app."
A trend -- the general direction that numbers follow
"The trend this year is positive."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Paula presents her team’s results every quarter. She starts with the past period: “Last quarter, there was a 12% increase in new accounts, and there were 25 new partners.” She uses was for one thing and were for many.

Then she talks about the change until today: “Since April, costs have fallen, and the team has grown to fifteen people.” This is not a finished moment. It started in April and it is still true.

Why does Paula say "there were 25 new partners"?
A Because partners is plural.
B Because it is a question.
C Because it is the future.
"Since April, costs have fallen." What does it mean?
A Costs are going to fall in April.
B Costs started to fall in April, and they are still lower.
C Costs fell only in April.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- There Was, There Were, Have Grown

GRAMMAR

A past period with numbers: there was / there were. A change until today: have + third form.

JobFormExample
One thing, a past periodthere was + singularThere was a 12% increase.
Many things, a past periodthere were + pluralThere were 25 new partners.
Change until todayhave / has + third formCosts have fallen since April.

Never: It had a 12% increase · There was 25 new partners · Costs fell since April

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

Listen first, then put the lines in order. A manager presents results in a meeting. Put the sentences in a natural order.

Step 1: listen to the whole conversation. Step 2: put the lines in the order you heard.
?"Since April, our costs have fallen."
?"Let me start with last quarter."
?"So the trend is positive for next quarter."
?"There were also 25 new partners in the south."
?"There was a 12% increase in new accounts."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The director asks about new clients last quarter. You had 30. You say:
A "There were 30 new clients."
B "It had 30 new clients."
C "There was 30 new clients."
Sales started to go up in January and they are still going up. You say:
A "Sales grow since January."
B "Sales grew since January."
C "Sales have grown since January."
You want to report one problem from last quarter. You say:
A "There were a delay in the reports."
B "There was a delay in the reports."
C "Had a delay in the reports."
