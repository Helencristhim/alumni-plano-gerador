Grammar Discovery

The Words Around the Number

"The total cost comes to roughly sixty-nine thousand dollars."

"The offer is just under the top of the range."

"Benefits add up to thirty percent of the salary."

"Headcount cost is up fifteen percent year over year."

Which words make a number less exact? What never changes after a number?

FormWhat it doesExample
roughly / about + numbera number that is close, not exactroughly seventy thousand
just under / just over + numbera little less / a little more than the numberjust under five thousand eight hundred
number + percentpercent never takes -s after a numberthirty percent, not thirty percents
up / down + number + percent year over yeara change compared with last yearup eight percent year over year
grow / increase / fall + by + numberthe size of the change: by, not inpayroll grew by ten percent
Watch outafter a number, thousand and million have no -s: fifty thousand dollars, never fifty thousands. And no of after roughly: roughly seventy thousand, never roughly of seventy thousand.

Sound Check

Fifteen or Fifty?

-teen and -ty · point 10 of 14

Why this one is hard for a Brazilian speaker

In Portuguese: Quinze and cinquenta are two very different words. Nobody confuses them.

In English: Fifteen and fifty start the same way. The difference is the strong syllable: fif-TEEN, with a clear n at the end, and FIF-ty, with a short, soft t.

If it slips: Fifteen percent becomes fifty percent. In a budget review, that is not a small mistake: it is a different number.
