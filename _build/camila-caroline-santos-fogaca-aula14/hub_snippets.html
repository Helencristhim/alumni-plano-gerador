Stage 2: Grammar Tip -- The small words around a number

GRAMMAR

A number on a sheet is easy. A number in a sentence needs a few small words, and English puts them in fixed places.

FormWhat it doesExample
roughly / about + numbera number that is close, not exact"The total cost comes to roughly fifty-eight thousand."
just under / just over + numbera little less / a little more than the number"The offer is just under the top of the range."
number + percentpercent never takes -s after a number"Benefits are thirty percent of the salary."
up / down + number + percent year over yeara change, compared with last year"Payroll is up thirteen percent year over year."
grow / increase / fall + by + numberthe size of the change: by, not in"Payroll grew by ten percent."
Watch outafter a number, thousand and million have no -s: "fifty thousand dollars", not "fifty thousands". And no of after roughly: "roughly seventy thousand", not "roughly of seventy thousand".

First a ballpark figure with roughly, then the exact number for the committee.

Stage 3: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

The words behind the numbers you have just checked. Listen to each expression and read it in context: they come back in every conversation about the cost of a request.

salary range -- the minimum and the maximum salary for a position
"The salary range for grade six is thirty-six to forty-six thousand."
pay grade -- the level of a position, which decides its salary range
"The pay grade decides the minimum and the maximum."
total cost -- the salary plus the benefits: the full amount the company pays
"The committee wants the total cost, not only the salary."
budget line -- the place in the budget where the money for a position is planned
"A replacement is already on a budget line."
a ballpark figure -- a number that is close, but not exact
"Can you give me a ballpark figure before Friday?"
year over year -- compared with the same period in the year before
"Payroll in the team is up thirteen percent year over year."
add up to -- to make a total of
"Benefits add up to roughly thirty percent of the salary."

Stage 8: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Real sentences from conversations about the cost of a request. Only one option is both correct and natural.

1. Your manager asks how much payroll grew. Which sentence is correct?
A Payroll grew by ten percent this year, compared with the same period last year.
B Payroll grew in ten percent this year.
C Payroll grew ten percents this year.
D Payroll grew of ten percent this year.
2. The salary is $50,000. How do you say it?
A Fifty thousands dollars a year.
B Fifty thousand of dollars a year, before the benefits.
C Fifty thousands of dollars a year.
D Fifty thousand dollars a year.
3. The manager wants a number now, and you need to check the details. What do you say?
A The exact number is seventy thousand, but maybe it is not exact.
B A ballpark figure is seventy thousand. I'll send the exact number tomorrow.
C I can't say any number before I check every detail of the request with the system.
D A ballpark figure is exactly seventy thousand.
4. Salary and benefits make a total of $68,900. Which sentence is correct?
A Salary and benefits add to up sixty-eight thousand nine hundred dollars.
B Salary and benefits add up in sixty-eight thousand nine hundred dollars, more or less.
C Salary and benefits add up to sixty-eight thousand nine hundred dollars.
D Salary and benefits adds up to sixty-eight thousand nine hundred dollars.
5. Headcount cost is 15% higher than last year. How do you say it?
A Headcount cost is up fifty percent year over year.
B Headcount cost is up fifteen percent year over year.
C Headcount cost is up fifteen percents year by year, compared with the plan.
D Headcount cost is over fifteen percent the year.
