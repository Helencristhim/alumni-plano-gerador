Lesson 24 -- The Renewal That Almost Did Not Happen

Film
Jerry Maguire (1996) -- the client calls after the memo

A sports agent writes what he really thinks about his industry, loses his job and ends up with one single client. The whole film is one long save call: he has been building that relationship for years, he has been losing it for weeks, and every scene is somebody deciding whether to stay or go to another agent. Connection to Lesson 24: this is your account review turned into a story. Watch how he stops selling and starts saying, out loud, what has been going wrong.

Tip: watch with English subtitles and write down 5 sentences with has been or have been + -ing. Then take one real account of yours and write the same 5 sentences about it, with a for or a since in each one.

CSM Practice -- "Cutting Churn with QBRs and Renewals: A Small Team's Big Win"

Mary Migiano runs customer success at Compt with a team of four, and in thirty minutes she explains how that team used business reviews and a tighter renewal process to stop losing accounts. She talks about the same three things you read in class today: the usage numbers that warn you first, the engagement that has been falling before anybody notices, and the review that gets you buy-in above your main contact. Connection to Lesson 24: this is the account review of the class, told by somebody who does it every quarter.

Tip: listen once for the story. Listen again and write down 5 sentences where she says what a team has been doing over a period of time. Say each one out loud and check that "has" or "have" is always followed by "been" + -ing.

YouTube
"Present perfect simple or continuous?" -- Ask BBC Learning English

A short answer to exactly the question this lesson is built on: when do you say "I have worked here for four years" and when do you say "I have been working here for four years"? The teacher separates the result (the number that is finished) from the activity (the work that has not stopped), which is the difference between the two columns of your one-pager. Connection to Lesson 24: the rule behind every sentence you said in the save call, in under five minutes.

Tip: copy the two columns into your notebook -- result on the left, activity still going on the right. Then write 6 sentences about one of your accounts, three in each column, and read them out loud with the weak form: we've been, it's been.
