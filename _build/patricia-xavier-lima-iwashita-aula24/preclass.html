Lesson 24 -- Pre-class

The Renewal That Almost Did Not Happen

Reading an account review and telling the story of a near-loss: what has been happening on the account since March, and what has been done about it. Key words: at risk, red flag, usage, engagement, stall, sponsor, buy-in, vendor, turnaround, win back. Structure: present perfect continuous -- has been / have been + -ing, with for and since.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Eight weeks before the renewal, the account is at risk. Nothing has broken since March, and that is exactly the problem: nothing has moved either. Usage has been falling for three months, and two of the five teams have not opened the dashboard since the outage. Engagement is low as well, because the main contact has not answered the last two invitations. Those are the first two red flags. The third one is quieter: the adoption plan has been stalling since April, and the finance team has been asking for a comparison of the market since the same week. Another vendor presented to the director last Thursday. The new executive sponsor arrived in February and has never seen a business review, so there is no buy-in above the main contact. The good news is in the last paragraph of the review: weekly sessions have been running with both inactive teams for six weeks, and their usage has gone up by eleven per cent. A turnaround is possible. It is also work: this renewal has to be won back, not signed.

1. "Usage has been falling for three months." Is usage falling today?
A No. It stopped three months ago.
B Yes. It started three months ago and it is still going on.
C We cannot know; the sentence is about the future.
2. Why does the text say "for six weeks" but "since April"?
A There is no rule; the two words are the same.
B "For" comes with a length of time, and "since" comes with the point when it started.
C "Since" is only used in questions.
3. What is the difference between "sessions have been running" and "usage has gone up by eleven per cent"?
A The first is the work that is still going on; the second is the result it has already produced.
B The first is about the past and the second is about the future.
C The first is formal and the second is informal.
4. Which sentence is correct?
A "They are talking to another vendor since March."
B "They have been talk to another vendor since March."
C "They have been talking to another vendor since March."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect Continuous: has been + -ing

GRAMMAR

The tense of work that started in the past and has not stopped -- and how to keep for and since apart.

StructureUseExample
has / have been + -ingStarted in the past and is still going on now.Usage has been falling for three months.
for + a length of timeHow long it has lasted.for three months, for six weeks
since + a point in timeWhen it started.since April, since the outage
has / have + past participleThe finished result, with the number.Usage has gone up by eleven per cent.
How long has / have + subject + been + -ing?Ask how long something has been going on.How long has this been going on?
AvoidAfter been the verb always ends in -ing: not "has been fall" → "has been falling". Do not use the present continuous here: not "they are talking to another vendor since March" → "they have been talking to another vendor since March". And it is for three months, never "since three months". In speech, have been and has been lose their weight: we've been (uv-BIN), it's been (its-BIN).

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each real work situation.

Your customer asks what has been going on with his account since March. You answer:
A "Usage has been falling since March, and two of your teams have been off the dashboard for eleven weeks."
B "The usage is falling since March and two teams are off the dashboard since eleven weeks."
C "Nothing important happened after the incident in March."
He asks what your team has been doing about it. The strongest answer is:
A "We have been working really hard on this account."
B "We have been running weekly sessions since May, and usage has already gone up by eleven per cent."
C "We ran a couple of sessions and it is more or less better now."
You want to know how long a problem has existed before you answer for it. You ask:
A "How long time this is happening?"
B "How long has this been going on?"
C "Since when it happens?"
A director asks why he should renew. The answer that works is:
A "Because the account has stopped falling: the two inactive teams have been back on the platform for six weeks, and you will have the twelve-month plan on Thursday."
B "Because we can give you a discount on the renewal."
C "Because changing vendor is always a lot of work for your teams."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think of one real account of yours that came close to leaving, or one that is at risk right now. Tell the story in four or five sentences, using has been and have been + -ing: what the first red flag was, how long it has been going on, what you and your team have been doing about it, and what has changed so far. Two rules: every "has been" gets a for or a since, and every result gets a number. Take your time.
