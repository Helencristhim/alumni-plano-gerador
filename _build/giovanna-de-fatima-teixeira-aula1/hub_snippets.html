Ten Years Later -- Giovanna Starts Again

Talk about your own work in English again: the site, the crew, the shift and the commute -- and put in, on and at in the right place. Take your time, and repeat as often as you like.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen as many times as you want.

Site -- the place where a tunnel or a building is under construction
"I work on a metro site."
Crew -- the group of people who work together on the same job
"I work with the tunnel crew."
Shift -- the hours you work in one day, for example from seven to five
"My shift starts at seven in the morning."
Commute -- the trip between your home and your work
"My commute is about one hour."
Degree -- the title a university gives you when you finish your course
"I have a degree in civil engineering."
Background -- your studies and the work you did before now
"My background is civil construction."
Break -- a short stop in the middle of work
"We take a break at nine thirty."
Catch up -- to talk with someone about the news since you last met
"Let's catch up at the break."
Again -- one more time, from the start
"Sorry, could you say that again, please?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the text out loud, then answer the questions below. Read slowly -- nobody is timing you.

My name is Giovanna Teixeira, and I am a civil engineer. I work on a metro site, with the tunnel crew. My shift starts at seven in the morning and finishes at five, so my commute begins very early. I have a degree in civil engineering, and my background is construction. We take the first break at nine thirty. A new engineer arrives on Monday, and I want to catch up with him at the break. He speaks fast, so I say: "Sorry, could you say that again, please?"

1. Why do we say "at seven" and not "in seven"?
A Because a clock time is a point, and a point takes "at".
B Because seven is a small number.
C Because the sentence is about the morning.
2. Which preposition goes with a day of the week?
A "in Monday"
B "on Monday"
C "at Monday"
3. What does Giovanna say when someone speaks too fast?
A "I no understand you."
B "Speak slow now."
C "Sorry, could you say that again, please?"

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- In, On, At

Grammar

Three very small words decide where and when. One idea for each.

WordPlaceTime
ata point: at the gate, at the office, at homea clock time: at seven, at noon, at night
ona surface or a line: on the site, on Line Nine, on the tablea day or a date: on Monday, on July 3
ininside, an area or a field: in the tunnel, in Brazil, in civil engineeringa long period: in the morning, in September, in 2027
Present simpleUse it for things that are true every day: I work on a metro site. My shift starts at seven. Remember the final s with he, she, it and my shift.
