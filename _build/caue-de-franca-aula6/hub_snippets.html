Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

The rat race -- a busy, competitive way of life in which people work hard and have no time to rest
"After ten years in the rat race, he wanted a slower life."
To unwind -- to relax after a period of work or stress
"Reading helps me unwind at night."
To switch off -- to stop thinking about work or problems
"On the train, I try to switch off."
A novel -- a long written story about imaginary people and events
"She has just finished her first novel in English."
A character -- a person in a story, a movie or a play
"The main character is a taxi driver."
A page-turner -- a book so exciting that you want to keep reading
"It’s a real page-turner."
A plot twist -- a sudden change in a story that you did not expect
"The plot twist in chapter twelve surprised everybody."
To get hooked -- to become very interested in something, so that you want more of it
"I got hooked after the first chapter."
Hard to put down -- so interesting that you do not want to stop reading
"The book was hard to put down."
A bookworm -- a person who loves reading and reads a lot
"As a kid, I was a real bookworm."
A commute -- the trip you make every day between home and work
"My commute takes an hour each way."
To drift off -- to fall asleep slowly, without wanting to
"I drifted off with the book on my chest."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

After ten years in the rat race, Rafael never read for pleasure. One rainy Thursday, he was standing on a crowded train when he noticed a book on the seat. Someone had left it there.

The novel was a page-turner. The main character was looking for a brother she had never met. Rafael got hooked so fast that he almost missed his stop. It was hard to put down.

That night he drifted off with the book on his chest. Now his commute is his reading time: it is how he switches off and unwinds. His old friends would say he is a bookworm again.

1. Why does the text say “someone had left it there”?
A Because it happened before Rafael noticed the book.
B Because the person who left the book was still on the train and was standing next to Rafael.
C Because had left is the polite form of left, used in stories and novels.
2. What was the main character of the novel doing?
A She was writing a book on a train, because she had no time at home to write.
B She was looking for a brother she had never met.
C She was working in the rat race and wanted to find a slower life in the country.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “He read on the train when he was noticing the book.”
B “He had stood on the train when he was noticing a book on the seat.”
C “He was standing on the train when he noticed the book.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Narrative Tenses -- Background, Events and Before

GRAMMAR

A story in English uses three past tenses together: the past simple for the events, the past continuous for the background, and the past perfect for what happened before.

JobFormExample
The eventspast simpleRafael left the office. He noticed a book.
The backgroundpast continuous (was / were + -ing)It was raining. He was standing on the train.
An action interruptedpast continuous + when + past simpleHe was reading when his phone rang.
Further backpast perfect (had + past participle)Someone had left it there.
By the time / alreadyoften go with the past perfectBy the third station, he had forgotten the rat race.
The trapthe tense changes the storyWhen he arrived, the train left (he saw it go) x the train had left (it was gone).

Never: It rained when I left (for the background) · I was knowing · When I arrived, the train left (when you mean it was gone) · He drift off

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to describe the background of a story. You say:
A "It was raining, and the train was full."
B "It rained all the time, and the train has been full every day that week."
C "It had rained and the train is full when the story begins."
You want to say the train was gone when you got to the station. You say:
A "When I got to the station, the train left without me."
B "When I got to the station, the train had left."
C "When I got to the station, the train was leaving already before."
A book is so good that you cannot stop reading. You say:
A "It’s a real rat race of a book, from beginning to end."
B "It’s a real commute, I read it every single day."
C "It’s a real page-turner."
You fell asleep while you were reading. You say:
A "I drifted off with the book in my hands."
B "I switched off the book and went to sleep."
C "I unwound the book and closed my eyes for the night."
