Chapter 4: The Code

Three Tenses, One Story

Background, events and what happened before

Grammar Discovery

Three Tenses, One Story

"It was raining when Rafael left the office."

"He had spent ten hours in front of a screen full of numbers."

"He was standing on a crowded train when he noticed a book."

"Someone had left it on the seat next to him."

Three colors, three jobs. The red verbs are the events: what happened, one after the other. The blue verbs are the background: what was already happening. The green verbs go further back: what had happened before the story started.

JobFormExample
The eventspast simpleRafael left the office. He noticed a book.
The backgroundpast continuous (was / were + -ing)It was raining. He was standing on the train.
An action interruptedpast continuous + when + past simpleHe was reading when his phone rang.
Further backpast perfect (had + past participle)Someone had left it there.
By the time / alreadyoften go with the past perfectBy the third station, he had forgotten the rat race.
The trapthe tense changes the storyWhen he arrived, the train left (he saw it go) x the train had left (it was gone).

In one line: past simple moves the story forward, past continuous paints the background, past perfect goes back in time.

Listening 1 · A Librarian

Books for People With No Time

A British librarian gives advice to people who say they have no time to read. Sound first — no text.

1. What do people usually tell her?
That they used to read a lot, but now they have no time.
2. What kind of book does she recommend to them first?
A page-turner with short chapters, not a classic.
3. What is her advice about when to read?
Ten pages a day, on the commute or before bed.
Listening 2 · A Reader on the Train

Two Hours a Day

A man in Mumbai tells how he started reading on his commute. Sound first — no text.

1. How long was his commute?
An hour each way, two hours a day.
2. What was he doing on the train before he started reading?
He was checking emails and the news on his phone.
3. What had his colleague given him?
A detective novel.
Listening 3 · A Novelist

What Makes a Page-Turner

An American novelist explains why some books are hard to put down. Sound first — no text.

1. What does every chapter in her books end with?
A question, so the reader wants to start the next one.
2. What does she say about the characters?
The reader has to care about them, or nothing else works.
3. When does she plan the plot twist?
Before she writes the first page.
