Chapter 4: The Code

Expected, Estimated, Said

How the press reports what nobody can promise yet

Grammar Discovery

Who Expects? Who Says?

"The deal is expected to close in the first quarter."

"It is estimated that the combined company will save forty million dollars a year."

"Mark Ellison is said to be staying as president."

"Other buyers are believed to have made offers."

Nobody in these sentences says who expects, estimates or believes. The blue words are passive: the source is not known or not important, so the sentence sounds neutral and careful. Look at the red verbs: after to you use the base form, and to have + past participle for the past.

JobFormExample
Report an expectationsubject + is expected to + verbThe deal is expected to close in Q1.
Report an estimateIt is estimated that + sentenceIt is estimated that the company will save $40M.
Report what people say or believesubject + is said / believed to + verbHe is said to be staying.
The same, about the pastsubject + is believed to have + past participleOther buyers are believed to have made offers.
Why use itthe source is unknown or not importantNeutral, careful, and still true tomorrow.
The trappassive, not -ingNot The deal is expecting to close. Say The deal is expected to close.

In one line: is expected / said / believed to + verb; It is estimated / expected that + sentence; to have + past participle for the past.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

The Deal Goes Public

One press release, one briefing, one news report

Real Document

The Press Release

PRESS RELEASE -- FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE
Northway Group · Brightline Logistics · Advised by Accenture Strategy (Carlos Vinicius Vale Bassan)
HeadlineNorthway Group to acquire Brightline Logistics
ValueThe deal is valued at 1.2 billion dollars.
TimingThe deal is expected to close in the first quarter, subject to regulatory approval.
SavingsIt is estimated that the combined company will save 40 million dollars a year.
LeadershipMark Ellison is expected to stay as president of Brightline.
Media contactLaura Chen, spokesperson
A press release reports expectations, not promises. The facts are the value and the names; everything in the future is expected or estimated.
1. When is the deal expected to close?
In the first quarter, subject to regulatory approval.
2. Why does the release not say “the company will save 40 million dollars”?
Because it is only an estimate: nothing is certain until the deal closes.
3. Who should journalists contact?
Laura Chen, the spokesperson.
Listening 1 · The Spokesperson

The Press Briefing

The spokesperson opens the press briefing on announcement day. Sound first — no text.

1. How much is the deal valued at?
One point two billion dollars.
2. What is expected to happen in the first quarter?
The deal is expected to close, subject to regulatory approval.
3. What will she not comment on?
Rumors about other buyers.
Listening 2 · The News

The Deal on the Radio

A business journalist reports the deal on the morning news. Sound first — no text.

1. What happened to Northway’s stock?
It rose four percent in early trading.
2. What could take six months?
Regulatory approval in two countries.
3. What are other buyers believed to have done?
They looked at Brightline last year, but nobody made a formal offer.
