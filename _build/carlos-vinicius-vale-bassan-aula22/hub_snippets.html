Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Announcement -- an official public statement about something important
"The announcement came before the market opened."
Press release -- an official written statement that a company sends to journalists
"Legal checked every word of the press release."
Shareholder -- a person or company that owns part of a company
"Shareholders read every word of the release."
Combined company -- the new company that exists after two companies join
"The combined company will serve clients in twelve countries."
Regulatory approval -- official permission from the authorities for a deal to go ahead
"The deal still needs regulatory approval in two countries."
Accretive -- making the earnings per share of the buyer go up
"The deal is expected to be accretive in the first year."
Speculation -- ideas about what might happen, without all the facts
"We do not comment on speculation about job cuts."
Spokesperson -- the person who speaks officially for a company
"Our spokesperson will answer questions at ten."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At seven in the morning, Northway Group sends a press release: it is buying Brightline Logistics. The deal is expected to close in the first quarter. It is estimated that the combined company will save forty million dollars a year.

By nine, the news is on the radio. Mark Ellison is said to be staying as president, and other buyers are believed to have looked at Brightline last year.

Notice the pattern. Nobody says who expects, estimates or believes. The passive makes the sentence neutral and careful: is expected to + verb, It is estimated that + sentence, and to have + past participle for the past.

1. When is the deal expected to close?
A Today.
B In the first quarter.
C Next year, after the town hall.
2. What are other buyers believed to have done?
A Looked at Brightline last year.
B Bought Northway.
C Closed an office.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “The deal is expecting to close in March.”
B “The deal is expected to close in March.”
C “The deal expected to close in March.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Passive Reporting: Expected, Estimated, Said

GRAMMAR

Use the passive when the source is not known or not important. It makes the message neutral and careful -- perfect for announcements.

JobFormExample
Report an expectationsubject + is expected to + verbThe deal is expected to close in Q1.
Report an estimateIt is estimated that + sentenceIt is estimated that we will save $40M.
Report what people say or believesubject + is said / believed to + verbHe is said to be staying.
The same, about the pastsubject + is believed to have + past participleOther buyers are believed to have made offers.

Never: The deal is expecting to close · It estimated that · He is said that he stays · They are believed to made offers

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A journalist asks when the deal will close. You say:
A “It will close on March 3, for sure.”
B “It is expected to close in the first quarter.”
C “It is expecting to close in the first quarter.”
A journalist asks about the savings. You say:
A “It is estimated that we will save forty million dollars a year.”
B “It estimates we save forty million.”
C “We promise forty million.”
A journalist asks about a rumor. You say:
A “We do not comment on speculation.”
B “We do not comment speculation.”
C “No comments about speculating.”
You report what the market believes about the past. You say:
A “Other buyers are believed to made offers.”
B “Other buyers are believed to have made offers.”
C “Other buyers believe to have made offers.”
