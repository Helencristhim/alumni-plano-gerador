The Tense That Connects Two Careers

Telling your professional story without saying when. Key words: achievement, transition, background, currently, to pursue, to specialize, to revalidate, journey. Structure: present perfect with ever, never, just, already, yet, for and since.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These are the words that turn fifteen years of work into four sentences.

Achievement -- something important that you have done and are proud of
"My biggest achievement is my master in trauma surgery."
Transition -- the process of changing from one situation to another
"The transition from surgery to data took two years."
Background -- your studies and your experience before now
"My background is in medicine, not in technology."
Currently -- at this moment in time
"I am currently studying for an American medical exam."
To pursue -- to follow a goal over a long period of time
"I have decided to pursue a career in research."
To specialize -- to become an expert in one specific area
"I specialized in trauma surgery after medical school."
To revalidate -- to have a qualification accepted in another country
"I want to revalidate my medical degree in the United States."
Journey -- a long process of change and development
"It has been quite a journey, and it is not finished."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Ana Duarte has worked as a cardiologist for twelve years. She has never worked outside Brazil, but that has now changed: she has just accepted a position in a research group in Canada. The transition has not been easy. She has already sold her apartment and she has started the paperwork, but she has not told her patients yet. When people ask her why she is doing this, she says the same thing: I have always wanted to do research, and I have waited long enough.
Why does the text say "has worked for twelve years" and not "worked for twelve years"?
A Because she stopped working twelve years ago
B Because she started twelve years ago and is still a cardiologist
C Because nobody knows the exact year
What does "she has not told her patients yet" mean?
A She told them a long time ago
B It has not happened until now, and it is still going to happen
C She has decided never to tell them

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect

GRAMMAR

Use the present perfect when the past is still connected to now: have / has + past participle. The moment is not important — the result today is.

life experienceever / neverHave you ever worked abroad? I have never operated outside Brazil.
recent newsjust / already / yetI have just started the course. I have not taken the exam yet.
how longfor / sinceI have worked here for fifteen years. I have lived here since 2010.
finished pastpast simple, with a momentI finished my degree in 2007.

Careful: the moment kills the present perfect. If you say yesterday, in 2007 or two years ago, English goes to the past simple: "I finished the course yesterday", never "I have finished the course yesterday".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
An interviewer asks: "How long have you worked in healthcare?" Which answer is correct?
A "I work in healthcare since fifteen years."
B "I have worked in healthcare for more than fifteen years."
C "I am working in healthcare from fifteen years."
You started the preparation course last week and you are still doing it. What do you say?
A "I have just started the preparation course."
B "I have just finished the preparation course."
C "I started the course and I have stopped."
Someone asks: "Have you ever been to the United States?" Which answer is natural?
A "Yes, I went. Two times per year."
B "Yes, I have. I go there twice a year with my son."
C "Yes, I have been there yesterday."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Someone at an international event asks about your career. Record ninety seconds: what your background is, how long you have worked in healthcare, what you have already done toward the American exam, and what you have not done yet. No dates — use for, since, already and yet.
