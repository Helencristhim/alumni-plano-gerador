Chapter 3: The Code

Started Before, Still Counts Today

One tense for experience, for recent news, and for how long

Discover the Rule

What Do These Have in Common?

I have worked in healthcare for fifteen years.
She has never operated outside Brazil.
I have just started the preparation course.
Have you ever been to the United States?
Click to reveal the rule
None of them says when. That is the point. The present perfect (have / has + past participle) connects the past to now: life experience (ever, never), recent news that still matters (just, already, yet), and how long something has been true (for, since). The moment is not important — the result today is.

Why Did You Change?

A

S
Of course. Please, go ahead.
A
You have worked as a trauma surgeon, and now you work with data. Why?
S
I have always been interested in the decisions behind the treatment, not only in the treatment itself.
A
That is a good answer. Have you published anything on that?
S
Not yet. I have started a systematic review, but I have not finished it.
A
I have seen many doctors make this transition, and most of them come back to the clinic.
S
I do not know yet if I want to come back. That is exactly why I have applied to your program.
