Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. You already read all eight in the exam course. Today they come out of your mouth.

Residency -- the years of supervised training a doctor does after medical school
"A residency in the United States usually lasts three years."
Application -- the set of documents you send to ask for a place
"My application has to be complete before the deadline."
Sponsor -- an institution or a person who officially supports your visa or your position
"The hospital agreed to be my sponsor."
Fellowship -- a paid position for advanced training or research after residency
"He is doing a fellowship in trauma research."
Deadline -- the last day on which something can be done
"The deadline for the application is the first of March."
Requirement -- something you must have or do to be accepted
"An English certificate is a requirement for the program."
Advisor -- the person who guides your research or your career decisions
"My advisor in Maryland is a trauma researcher."
To apply for -- to formally ask for a position, a place or a visa
"I will apply for the fellowship next year."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Tiago Ramos is an anesthesiologist, and he wants to work in Canada. His plan has three steps, and each one depends on the one before. If he passes the medical exam in June, he will send the application in July. If the hospital accepts him, they will sponsor his visa. And if the visa arrives before December, he will start in January. He knows the plan can break at any point. If he does not pass in June, he will take the exam again in November, and everything will move one year. He says the important thing is to have an answer ready for every if.
Why does the text say "If he passes" and not "If he will pass"?
A Because the if- half is in the present simple, even about the future
B Because he has already passed the exam
C Because "pass" is an irregular verb
What will happen if he does not pass in June?
A He will give up the plan
B He will take the exam again in November and everything will move one year
C He will start in January anyway

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- First Conditional

GRAMMAR

Use the first conditional for a real, possible future: if + present simple, and then will + verb.

conditionif + present simpleIf I pass the first step...
resultwill + base verb...I will apply for the fellowship.
negativeif + do / does not + verbIf I do not pass, I will retake it.
questionwhat will + subject + verb + if...What will you do if the sponsor says no?

Careful: never put will in the if- half. "If I will pass" does not exist in English — the future already lives in the other half of the sentence.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
You are telling a colleague about your plan. Which sentence is correct?
A "If I will pass the exam, I will apply."
B "If I pass the exam, I will apply."
C "If I pass the exam, I apply."
You want to know what happens in the bad scenario. What do you ask?
A "What you will do if the sponsor says no?"
B "What will you do if the sponsor says no?"
C "What will you do if the sponsor will say no?"
The certificate is not ready and the deadline is next week. What do you say?
A "If the certificate does not arrive in time, I will apply in the next cycle."
B "If the certificate will not arrive in time, I apply in the next cycle."
C "If the certificate not arrive in time, I will apply."
