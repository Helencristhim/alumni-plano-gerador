Chapter 3: The Code

Two Halves, One Future

The condition, the result, and the word that can only live in one of them

Discover the Rule

What Do These Have in Common?

If I pass the first step, I will apply for the fellowship.
If the position is available in the fall, I will send the application.
I will retake the exam next year if I do not pass this time.
What will you do if the sponsor says no?
Click to reveal the rule
Every sentence has two halves: a condition and a result. The condition half — the if half — is in the present simple, even though it is about the future. The result half takes will. So: If I pass, I will apply, never if I will pass. You can put the result first, and then the comma disappears: I will apply if I pass.

The First of March

Listening 1

Three Answers from Your Advisor

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What happens if the application is not complete by the first of March?
The committee will not look at it.
2. What will the hospital do if the department accepts her, and for how long?
It will sponsor the visa, but only for six months.
3. When will she hear from them, and what happens if she does not get in?
In April, if she sends everything before March and the committee likes the project. If not, they will keep the file for the following year.
