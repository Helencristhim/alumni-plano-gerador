Series
Manifest -- Season 1, Episode 1

A plane lands five years late, and everybody on board has to be told what happened to the people they left behind. That is the whole first episode: one person after another saying he lives here now, she works at, they do not know you are back. It is the grammar of this lesson, spoken under pressure. And it is suspense with a spiritual question underneath, which is exactly the kind of story you said you like.

Tip: watch with English subtitles and stop at three sentences that describe somebody else. Say each one out loud, then change the name to somebody in your own life.

Video
BBC Learning English -- "Using adverbs of frequency to describe your daily routine"

Under two minutes, and it answers the one question this lesson leaves open: where exactly do always, usually, sometimes and never go in the sentence? Before the verb, but after am, is and are. Hearing somebody say it at normal speed makes the rule stop feeling like a rule.

Tip: it is short, so watch it twice. The second time, pause after each example and build the same sentence about your own week before the speaker moves on.

Talk
Matt Cutts -- "Try something new for 30 days" (TED)

Three minutes, very simple English, and the shortest argument you will ever hear for what your homework asks you to do: one small thing, every day, for a month. He says the days become memorable instead of disappearing. That is the same claim the second recording in today’s class makes about routine.

Tip: turn the English subtitles on. Then say his idea back in three sentences, in the third person: he tries something new, he does not stop after one week, he says the days become memorable.
