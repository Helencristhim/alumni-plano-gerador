Monday and Thursday. She has an English class at one on Monday, and she goes to the theater at eight on Thursday.

The dog. It goes to the vet at nine in the morning.

It says whoever writes it, does it, and that if it is not on the door it does not happen. Nobody moves the Sunday line.
Chapter 4: The Code

One Letter Changes Everything

And it only ever lands on one word

Grammar Discovery

What Do the First Two Have That the Last Two Do Not?

"She wakes up at half past five."

"He takes forty minutes to get ready."

"She does not shout, and she does not help him."

"Does she eat at her desk?"

In the first two sentences the red verb ends in s. In the last two it does not — because the blue word took the s for itself. It only ever lands on one word.

JobFormExample

I / you / we / theybare verb, no sI wake up at five.

Question word in frontWhat / When / How often + does + subject + bare verbHow often does he call you?
Short answerYes, she does. / No, he does not."Does he help?" — "No, he does not."
How oftenalways, usually, often, sometimes, never — before the verbShe never eats at her desk.
...but after am / is / aream / is / are + always, neverShe is never late.
The trapthe s lands on ONE word onlyNot she does not works. Say she does not work.

In one line: with he, she and it the verb takes an s — and the moment does turns up, it takes the s instead.

Listening 1 · A Brother

My Sister Wakes Up at Five

A man comparing his routine with his sister’s. Sound first — no text.

1. What three things does his sister do before seven in the morning?
She wakes up at five, she runs for forty minutes, and she takes a cold shower. By seven she has also answered all her messages.
2. Name two things he says she never does.
She never leaves the house without breakfast, she does not check her phone after nine at night, and she does not watch television.
3. How does he describe his own routine, and what does he say about the two of them?
He wakes up late, works from home, forgets lunch and goes to bed at two in the morning. He says they get on very well, and that they never see each other before noon.
Listening 2 · On the Radio

The Hour Nobody Can Move

A woman arguing that routine is not a smaller life. Sound first — no text.

1. How does the speaker define a routine?
A set of decisions you make once, so that you do not have to make them again every morning while you are still half asleep.
2. What does her neighbor do at the same time every day, including Sunday?
She gets up, she walks her dog on the same street, and she buys bread at the same place. The man there starts making her coffee when he sees her cross the road.
3. What does the speaker say the real problem is?
Not the meetings, not the hours, not the traffic. The problem is having no hour in your week that belongs to you and always happens.
