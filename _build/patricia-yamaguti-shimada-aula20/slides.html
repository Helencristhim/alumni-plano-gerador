Dialogue

The Last Evening, and Nothing to Solve

J
You are the one with the two children on the third floor, aren't you? I am James.
P
Yes, that is me. Nice to meet you. I am Patrícia.
J
Nice to meet you too. Two weeks, someone said. How has it been?
P
Very good. Difficult at the beginning, but easy now.
J
What was the difficult part? For most people it is the money.
P
The numbers, yes! At the till I never understood the total.
J
Nobody does. I have lived here twelve years and I still say "sorry, how much?"
P
Really? That makes me feel better.
J
It should. And what did you like most — the museums, the shops?
P
The shops. I like buying clothes for my children — and I did it alone, the first time.
J
With two children and a language you are learning? That is quite something.
P
Thank you. Maybe we come back in December. And you? Do you live here?
J
Twelve years, and I am still from Manchester. Do not ask me about it — I never stop.
Comprehension

What Did James Say?

1. What does James say about understanding the total?
That nobody does — he has lived there twelve years and still says "sorry, how much?"
2. What does he say most people find difficult?
The money.
3. Where is James from, and what does he say about it?
Manchester — and he says not to ask him about it, because he never stops.
Listening

One Ordinary Tuesday

A woman describes a normal day abroad, on a podcast. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What did she do in the morning, and what did it save her?
She read the sign at the museum and saw it was closed — so they did not cross the city for nothing.
2. What happened at the till?
She did not understand the total, so she asked the man to write it down. He did, and nobody looked at her.
3. What does she say was the day everything changed?
The ordinary day — not one emergency in it.
Listening 2

What Twenty Lessons Actually Buy You

A teacher talks about beginners on the radio. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does the teacher say NOT to count?
The words you know — count the situations you no longer avoid.
2. What are the three situations beginners usually avoid?
The shop, the telephone, and the conversation with no purpose.
3. Who does he say learns faster?
The ones who ask people to repeat, not the ones who pretend to understand.

Scenario: Tell me one whole day, from the morning to the evening, in your own words. What you did, what was easy, what was difficult, and what you did alone.
