Lesson 10 -- Pre-class

People, Places and Regions

One sentence shape for introducing a colleague, explaining a company and describing a wine region. Key words: a division, a specialist, a representative, a sector, a point of contact, a reporting line, a region, a vineyard, soil, a colleague, to be known for, the person to talk to. Structure: relative clauses with who, which, that and where.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A division -- a large separate part of a company, with its own management
"That is the division which was created two years ago, after the merger."
A specialist -- somebody who knows one narrow thing extremely well
"She is the specialist who can approve this without going upstairs."
A representative -- a person who speaks and acts for a company with its customers
"Marco is our representative in the north, and he knows every dealer personally."
A sector -- a whole part of the economy, such as farming, banking or transport
"The south is the part of the sector that grew fastest last year."
A point of contact -- the one person the other side should call first, before anybody else
"For anything about the accounts, I am your point of contact."
A reporting line -- the chain inside a company that says who answers to whom
"The reporting line goes from me to the finance director, and no further."
A region -- a defined part of a country, with its own weather, soil and reputation
"The south is the region where most of our volume sits."
A vineyard -- a field where grapes are grown
"The best vineyard on that hill belongs to a family who have farmed it for four generations."
Soil -- the earth a plant grows in, and what it gives to the fruit
"It is almost never the grape. It is the soil, and what happens at night."
A colleague -- somebody you work with, at roughly the same level as you
"The colleague who handles the south has been doing it for nineteen years."
To be known for -- to be the thing people associate with a place or a person
"That valley is known for cold nights, which is why the wine keeps its acidity."
The person to talk to -- the one person who can actually answer a particular question
"For anything in the north, he is the person to talk to. The chart will not tell you that."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Somebody joins a large company and is handed an organisation chart with two hundred names on it. Within a month they discover it answers a question nobody asks. What they need is the second map: the specialist who can approve a file without going upstairs, the division which was created after the merger, the region where most of the volume sits. Look at what those three sentences have in common. A short clause hangs off a noun and tells you WHICH ONE. Take the clause away and you are left with a job title, which carries no information at all.

The choice of word is simple once you see it. A person takes who. A thing takes which. A place takes where. That works for both people and things in ordinary speech, and it is the safe default a Portuguese speaker reaches for first, which is why where is the one that never comes out. You can also drop the word altogether when the noun is the object of the clause: the file I sent yesterday is complete. What you cannot do is drop it when the noun is doing the action: the analyst handles the south is a different sentence and not the one you meant. One more trap: nothing extra at the end. Not the region where the volume sits there.

There is a second version, with two commas around it, and it does a different job: it adds information rather than identifying anybody. Ana, who has been here nineteen years, will know. There is only one Ana, so the clause is not choosing between Anas; it is telling you something extra. In that version that is never used. And the reason all this is worth an hour is what it unlocks outside work. Ask anybody about a wine region and the answer comes out in exactly this shape: a valley where the days are hot and the nights are cold, a soil which holds water badly, families who have farmed the same vineyard for four generations, and a place that is known for something long before it is famous. Learn the shape once, and introducing a colleague and describing a valley become the same skill.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Relative Clauses

GRAMMAR

The clause is not decoration. It is the part that says which one.

The noun isUseExample
a personwho (or that)the specialist who can approve this
a thingwhich (or that)the division which was created two years ago
a placewherethe region where most of our volume sits
When you can drop itonly when the noun is the objectthe file I sent yesterday
When you cannotwhen the noun is doing the actionNot the analyst handles the south. Say the analyst who handles the south.
Extra informationtwo commas, and never thatAna, who has been here nineteen years, will know.
The trapnothing extra at the end of the clauseNot the region where the volume sits there.

Never: the specialist which can approve this · the region where the volume sits there · Ana, that has been here nineteen years · a valley which the nights are cold.

Stage 2: Put the Introduction in Order

Order

A visitor has asked who she should talk to. Put your answer in a logical order.

?"For anything about the accounts, I am your point of contact."
?"Ana is the specialist who can approve a file without going upstairs."
?"Marco is our representative in the north, who knows every dealer personally."
?"After that, the reporting line goes to the finance director."
?"The south is the region where most of our volume sits."
?"And if you get invited to the harvest dinner, ask them about the soil."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to introduce a colleague by what she can do. You say:
A "She is the specialist which can approve this."
B "She is the specialist who can approve this without going upstairs."
C "She is a specialist. She approves things."
You want to say which part of the country your volume is in. You say:
A "The south is the region where most of our volume sits."
B "The south is the region where most of our volume sits there."
C "The south is the region which most of our volume sits."
There is only one Ana, and you want to add something about her. You say:
A "Ana that has been here nineteen years will know."
B "Ana, who has been here nineteen years, will know."
C "Ana, which has been here nineteen years, will know."
Somebody at dinner asks about a wine region. You start:
A "It is a good region. Very famous, very traditional."
B "It is a valley where the days are hot and the nights are cold, which is what keeps the acidity."
C "It is a valley which the nights are cold and the wine is good."
