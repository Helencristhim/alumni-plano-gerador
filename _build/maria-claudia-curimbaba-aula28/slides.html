Chapter 3 -- The Code

The Words That Show the Way

to begin with... having said that... as a result...

Discovery

Four Sentences, One Update

Cover the rest of each sentence. What does each highlighted phrase tell the listener is coming next?

"To begin with, the May container has been cleared."

"Having said that, the June consignment is still at risk."

"As a result, the second container will arrive two weeks late."

"That brings me to the decision we need to make today."

PartFormExample
start and sequenceto begin with · first of all · turning to · finally"To begin with, the May container..."
contrasthaving said that · even so · on the other hand"Having said that, June is at risk."
resultas a result · which means that"As a result, it will arrive late."
say it more simplyin other words · to put it simply"In other words, we need an answer today."
move to the next partthat brings me to · let me turn to"That brings me to the decision."

Key idea: a discourse marker goes at the start of the sentence, usually followed by a comma, and it tells the listener what kind of sentence is coming before it arrives. Two traps: it is "having said that", never "said that"; and "as a result" + comma, but "as a result of" + a noun ("as a result of the new route").

The Structure

Three Jobs in Five Minutes

The same small phrases, doing three different jobs for the listener.

1 · Open with the headline, not the history

"The June consignment will arrive two weeks late unless we choose a route today."

The board knows in ten seconds why they are listening.

2 · Let the listener find the way back

"That brings me to the second point: the cost."

Someone who drifted for ten seconds is back with you.

3 · Turn bad news into a decision

"Having said that, both options are ready, and we only need to choose one."

The contrast moves the room from the problem to the choice.

Notice what is missing: "so, basically". A filler tells the room nothing; a marker tells it where you are.

PITTSBURGH LINE -- UPDATE TO THE FAMILY BOARD

Speaker: Maria Claudia Curimbaba · Friday · 5 minutes

Hook (30 sec)

"The June consignment will arrive two weeks late unless we choose a route today."

1 · To begin with (1 min)

The May container: cleared on the 3rd, two days of demurrage paid.

2 · Having said that (1 min)

June: the carrier changed the route; lead time is now five weeks.

3 · That brings me to (2 min)

Option A -- reroute via Paranaguá: + USD 2,400, nine days faster. Option B -- expedite: + USD 6,100, fourteen days faster.

Call to action (30 sec)

Approve one option today. Full costs in the handout.

Your first line: "I have five minutes and one message for you."

Listening 1

The Houston Update

Sound first -- no text on screen.

0:000:00
1. What is his one message?
The Houston warehouse will be full by October unless they act this quarter.
2. Why does he call it a good problem?
Because it exists because sales are ahead of plan, not behind it.
3. What are the two options, and what does each one cost?
Extend the lease next door (it costs money, done within six weeks) or move slow stock to Dallas (cheaper, but two extra days on every delivery).
4. What exactly does he ask for at the end?
A decision between the two options -- and, if it is the lease, approval to sign it by the thirtieth.
Listening 2

The One Slide

Sound first -- no text on screen.

0:000:00
1. How many slides had she prepared, and why?
Twenty-two, because she wanted the board to see how much work was behind the plan.
2. What did she discover when she rehearsed out loud?
It took nineteen minutes, and she only had seven.
3. What question did she ask about each slide?
Whether it helped the one sentence she needed them to remember, or only helped her look prepared.
4. What does she say was the most important moment?
The silence after she asked for the decision, which she did not fill.

"As a result the new route, the parts will arrive late."

"In other words, we need an answer today." (always plural: "words")
"That brings me the decision we need today."
