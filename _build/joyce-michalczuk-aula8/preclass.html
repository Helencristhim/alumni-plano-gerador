Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

The 3-Minute Update -- Open, Run, Land

How to present a short update to the global team: open with context and the ask, move through three points and tell the audience when you change point, land with one takeaway, and say long words with the stress in the right place. Key words: the ask, the audience, a slide deck, a backup slide, a takeaway slide, to walk someone through, to rehearse, to run over time, to move on, key figures. Structure: the phrases that tell listeners where you are in a presentation.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

The ask -- the specific thing you want the audience to decide or give you
"My ask today is approval for the new tax system."
The audience -- the people who listen to a presentation
"Know your audience: the CFO wants the number, the lawyers want the risk."
A slide deck -- the full set of slides for a presentation
"I sent the slide deck the night before the meeting."
A backup slide -- an extra slide at the end, used only if somebody asks
"The detailed numbers are on a backup slide."
A takeaway slide -- the last slide, with the one idea people should remember
"My takeaway slide has one sentence and one number."
To walk someone through -- to explain something step by step, from the start to the end
"Let me walk you through the three changes."
To rehearse -- to practice a presentation out loud before you give it
"I rehearsed the update three times with a timer."
To run over time -- to speak for longer than the time you were given
"The last speaker ran over time, so I had only two minutes."
To move on -- to stop talking about one point and start the next one
"If there are no questions, let me move on to the cost."
Key figures -- the most important numbers in a report or a presentation
"Here are the three key figures for Brazil this quarter."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Helena, a finance manager in Lima, had three minutes in the quarterly global call. The year before, she had read her slides one by one and run over time. This time she rehearsed with a timer and built the update like a short walk with signs on the road.

She opened with one sentence of context and the ask: “The new tax rules start in January, and today I would like to ask for approval for one more analyst.” Then she told the audience where she was at every step: “First, the change. … Moving on to the cost. … This brings me to my last point, the risk.”

When the CFO interrupted with a question, she answered in one sentence and came back: “Coming back to the cost, …” She ended with her takeaway slide: “So, to wrap up: one change, one cost, one ask.” She finished in two minutes and fifty seconds, and the analyst was approved in the same call.

What did Helena put in her opening?
A All her key figures.
B One sentence of context and the ask.
C The history of the company.
What is the job of phrases like "Moving on to" and "This brings me to"?
A They tell the audience that she is changing to the next point.
B They ask the audience a question.
C They are polite ways to say thank you.
What did she do after the CFO interrupted?
A She started the presentation again.
B She stopped the presentation.
C She answered in one sentence and came back to her structure.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Signposts: Telling People Where You Are

GRAMMAR

In a presentation, the audience cannot see your plan. The signposts show it to them.

JobFormExample
Open with the askToday I would like to…Today I would like to ask for approval.
Walk them through itLet me walk you through…Let me walk you through the three changes.
Change pointMoving on to… / Next, …Moving on to the cost.
The last pointThis brings me to…This brings me to my last point.
After a questionComing back to…Coming back to the cost, …
The endSo, to wrap up, …So, to wrap up: one change, one ask.

Never: I will present about the reform · now I talk about the cost (with no signpost) · reading every slide · ending with only thank you

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

Put Helena’s three-minute update in the order she gave it.

?"This brings me to my last point, the risk of errors in the first filings."
?"Let me walk you through three points: the change, the cost and the risk."
?"So, to wrap up: one change, one cost, one ask."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You have three minutes. You open with:
A "Hello, I will present about many things."
B "So, let me start with slide one of thirty."
C "Today I would like to ask for approval for one more analyst."
You finish your first point and start the second. You say:
A "And, and, also the cost."
B "Moving on to the cost."
C Nothing. You just continue.
The CFO interrupts with a question in the middle. After you answer, you say:
A "Where was I? I forgot."
B "Coming back to my second point, …"
C "Let me start again from the beginning."
You want to end clearly. You say:
A "Thank you, sorry for the long presentation."
B "That is all, I think."
C "So, to wrap up: one change, one cost, one ask."
