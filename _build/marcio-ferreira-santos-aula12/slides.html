Chapter 3: The Code

The Question Inside a Statement

how you arrived · not how did you arrive

Grammar Discovery

Read and Notice

DIRECT: "How did you arrive at that number?"

INDIRECT: "Could I ask how you arrived at that number?"

INDIRECT: "Do you know what the margin is in that category?"

TAG: "The premise is never tested, is it?"

In the direct question there is a did. In the indirect one it is gone, and the word order is normal. Why?

StructureRuleExample
Opener + wh-wordAfter the opener the clause is a statement: subject, then verb.Could I ask how you arrived at that figure?
No auxiliaryDrop do, does, did. The main verb carries the tense.what the margin is — never what is the margin
Yes / no insideUse if or whether when there is no wh-word.I was wondering whether the data is gross or net.
Tag questionPositive sentence takes a negative tag, and the reverse.That is interesting, isn't it? / You did not test it, did you?
Openers to ownCould I ask... · Do you know... · I was wondering whether... · I would be curious to understand... · Would you mind telling me...

One line to remember: after the opener, nothing is inverted.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"Do you know what is the margin in that category?"
"Do you know what the margin is in that category?"
"Could I ask how did you arrive at that number?"
"Could I ask how you arrived at that number?"
"That is an interesting result, no?"
"That is an interesting result, isn't it?"

After an opener, the clause is a statement. And a tag question always repeats the auxiliary and the pronoun.

Practice

Make It Indirect

Say it first, then click to check

"Is that number gross or net?" → "I was wondering ___whether that number is gross or net."

"How much did the pilot cost?" → "Would you mind telling me ___how much the pilot cost?"

Detail

Add the Tag

Positive sentence → negative tag  ·  negative sentence → positive tag

"You have not tested the second market, ___have you?"
"The exhibit shows the aggregate, ___doesn't it?"

The House Rule of the Case Room

A professor opens the session. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. According to the house rule, who learns more in that room?
The person who asks the sharpest question, not the person who gives the longest answer.
2. What is the difference between a question and a comment, in his words?
A question pushes on the premise. "The author is wrong" is a comment, and comments end conversations.
3. When should you ask for clarification?
In the first ten minutes, never in the last five.

You Answered Too Fast

Buy the Room, Then Answer

Scenario: A consular officer asks one wide question: "Why do you need this course in the United States?" Wait four seconds. Buy the room with one line, ask one indirect question back, and only then answer the narrower question.

Keywords:

that is an important question before I answer directly could I ask what kind of...
Free Practice

Your Own Five Questions

Scenario: Take a real report or earnings release from your own sector. Ask five indirect questions about it: one for clarification, one for elaboration, one that tests a premise, one that draws out an implication, and one tag question that checks the nuance.

No keywords. Five questions are enough. No inversion after the opener.
