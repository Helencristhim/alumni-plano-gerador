Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

The First Minute -- Who You Are and What You Are Doing Now

The ninety seconds that open every conversation in English: who you are, what you do, and what you happen to be doing this week. Key expressions: to be based in, a colleague, to take care of, at the moment, busy, to deal with, a client, to cover for, a break, straight away. Structure: present simple versus present continuous.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. These ten stay here in your material -- they do not disappear after the class.

To be based in -- to have your home or your office in a place
"I am based in Sao Paulo, but the bank has offices in four countries."
A colleague -- a person who works with you
"She is a colleague from the fourth floor."
To take care of -- to be responsible for something or somebody
"I take care of investments for about forty families."
At the moment -- right now, but not always
"At the moment I am studying English twice a week."
Busy -- with a lot of things to do and very little free time
"Tuesday is the day I am most busy, so I never book anything after six."
To deal with -- to work with people or problems as part of your job
"I deal with clients who invest money outside Brazil."
A client -- a person or a company that pays you for a service
"One client of mine lives abroad and we only speak on video."
To cover for -- to do another person's work while they are away
"Somebody has to cover for her while she is away."
A break -- a short time when you stop working
"We take a break at eleven and everybody goes to the coffee machine."
Straight away -- immediately, with no delay
"If a client writes to me, I answer straight away."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Janderson works at a bank in Sao Paulo. He is based in the same building every day, on the eighth floor, and he takes care of investments for about forty families. He deals with clients who put money outside Brazil, and if one of them writes to him, he answers straight away. That is his job, and it does not change from one month to the next.

This week, however, three things are different. He is covering for a colleague who is on holiday, so he is dealing with two desks instead of one. He is also starting an English course, twice a week, at half past eight in the evening. And on Thursday he is taking the whole afternoon off, which almost never happens.

On Tuesday he is the most busy, so he never books anything after six. The team takes a break at eleven and everybody walks to the coffee machine at the end of the corridor. He does not drink coffee in the afternoon. At the moment he is drinking tea instead, and his colleagues find this very funny.

If you meet him by the coffee machine and ask him what he does, he has about ninety seconds to answer. Two sentences about the job that never changes, one sentence about the week that will be over on Friday, and one question back. That is the whole first minute.

1. "He works at a bank" and "he is covering for a colleague." Why are the two verbs in different forms?
A Because the second sentence is about the past.
B Because the job is permanent and covering for the colleague is only this week.
C Because the second sentence is a question.
2. Which verb in the text takes an S, and why?
A Works, takes and deals, because the subject is he.
B All of them, because the text is in the present.
C None of them, because English never adds an S to a verb.
3. What does the text say about Tuesday?
A He takes the whole afternoon off.
B It is the day he is most busy, so he books nothing after six.
C The team takes a longer break.
4. According to the text, what does a good first minute contain?
A A long explanation of the whole company.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple and Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

The question is never "when?" It is always "permanent or temporary?" Answer that first and the verb chooses itself.

FormWhat it saysExample
Present simple — positivePermanent. Your job, your routine, the facts about you."I work at a bank."
"She takes care of the visitors."
Present simple — negative and questionThe sentence borrows do or does, and the main verb loses its S."I don't work on Saturdays."
"Does she deal with clients?"
Present continuous — positiveTemporary. True this week, this month, this project, and it will stop."This week I am covering for a colleague."
Present continuous — negative and questionNothing is borrowed. Am / is / are simply moves."I am not covering for anybody."
"Are you working from home?"
The words that give it awayEvery day, usually, never, on Tuesdays → simple. At the moment, this week, right now, today → continuous."I usually answer straight away."
"At the moment I am reading it."
The trapA small group of verbs about the mind never takes the -ing form: understand, know, want, need, mean, remember. They are not actions.NOT "I am not understanding"
"I don't understand."

In a first conversation you will almost always need both, one after the other: what you do, and what you happen to be doing this week. That second sentence is what makes you sound like a person and not a job title.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are standing by the coffee machine four minutes before a meeting. Choose the best thing to say.

Somebody asks what you do. The best answer is:
A "I am working in a bank since a long time."
B "I work at a bank, in investments. And you?"
C "My professional function is in the area of financial investments."
You want to say that something is only true this week:
A "This week I cover for a colleague."
B "This week I am covering for a colleague."
C "This week I am cover for a colleague."
You want to ask where the other person usually works:
A "Where you are based?"
B "Where are you based?"
C "Where do you are based?"
You did not catch what the other person said. You say:
A "Sorry, I don't understand. Could you say that again?"
B "Sorry, I am not understanding you."
C Nothing, and you nod.
A client sends you an email and you reply immediately. You say:
A "I answer in the same moment."
B "I answer straight away."
C "I am answering already now."
