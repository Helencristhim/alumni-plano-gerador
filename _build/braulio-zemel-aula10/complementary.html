"Life's third act" -- Jane Fonda, TED

Eleven minutes on the thirty years that were added to a human life in the last century, and on what to do with them. Connection to Lesson 10: she is talking about exactly the chapter you are standing at the beginning of, and she says out loud that the middle of it is uncomfortable. Tip: watch once with English subtitles and once without. Then write two sentences about your own thirty-one years, each one with a past behind a past, and say them out loud.

"Riley Moynes: Making the most of retirement" -- NPR

A short American English interview with the man who mapped the four phases of retirement, including the second one, where the holiday feeling ends and nothing has replaced it yet. Connection to Lesson 10: it is the empty middle from today's reading, described by somebody who has interviewed hundreds of people who went through it. The full transcript sits under the player.

Tip: listen once without the text, writing down only the four phases. Then open the transcript and check. Afterwards, say out loud in English which phase you think you are in, and why.

Grammar Video
"The past perfect continuous tense" -- 6 Minute Grammar, BBC Learning English

Six minutes on the second half of today's grammar: the form that carries years of repetition up to a moment in the past. Connection to Lesson 10: it is the difference between I worked there and I had been working there, which is the difference between a fact and a life.

Tip: pause after each example and say the same idea about your own years, once with had worked and once with had been working. Notice which one sounds heavier, and why.
