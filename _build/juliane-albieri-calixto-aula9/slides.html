Chapter 3: The Code

Who, Which, That, Where

One small word to join two ideas

Grammar Discovery

One Word, Two Ideas

"The woman who helped me at baggage claim was very kind."

"The taxi that goes to the hotel is outside."

"This is the hotel where the conference is."

"Currywurst is a dish which you can eat on the street."

Each sentence has two ideas joined by one red word. Look at what comes before it: the woman is a person, so who. The taxi and a dish are things, so that or which. The hotel is a place, so where. And after the red word, there is no extra it, she or there.

JobFormExample
Peoplewho (or that)The driver who took me was from Berlin.
Thingswhich or thatThe bag that I lost was blue.
PlaceswhereThe restaurant where we ate was small.
Short versionthat / who can go when the next word is a personThe dish (that) I ordered was great.
Questions with placesDo you know a place where...?Do you know a place where I can eat late?
The trapno repeated word after the relativeNot the hotel where I stayed there. Say the hotel where I stayed.

In one line: who for people, which or that for things, where for places, and no extra word after them.

Chapter 4: The Taxi

The Taxi

One conversation, two recordings, one travel plan

Dialogue

From the Airport to the Hotel

P
Good evening! Where are you going?
J
Hi! To this hotel, please. It’s the hotel where the design conference is. How much is the fare, more or less?
P
About forty euros, it depends on the traffic. Is this your first time in Berlin?
J
Yes, it is. I had a long layover in Frankfurt, and the bags took forever at baggage claim.
P
That happens a lot. I’ve driven a taxi here for fifteen years, and I was born in Berlin. Are you hungry?
J
Very hungry! Is there a local dish that I have to try?
P
Currywurst! It’s a sausage with curry sauce. There’s a small place near your hotel where they make the best one in the city.
J
Perfect. And in restaurants, is the tip included?
P
Not always. People usually round up the bill, about ten percent. Here we are. That’s thirty-eight euros.
J
Here you go. Could I have a receipt, please? Thank you so much, Petra!
Comprehension

About Petra

1. How long has Petra driven a taxi in Berlin?
For fifteen years.
2. Which local dish does Petra recommend?
Currywurst, a sausage with curry sauce.
3. What does Petra say about tips in restaurants?
They are not always included; people usually round up the bill, about ten percent.

Welcome to Berlin

At belt number four.

To the lost luggage office, which is next to belt six.
3. Where is the taxi stand?

Listening 2 · Giulia’s Berlin

Tips From a Frequent Visitor

Giulia, the designer from Milan, goes to Berlin every year for design events. She shares her tips. Sound first — no text.

1. Which neighborhood does Giulia like most, and why?
Kreuzberg, because it has small restaurants and many designers live there.
2. What does she say about paying?
Some small places only take cash, so always have some euros.
3. Which local dish does she recommend for a cold night?
A hot currywurst, from a street stand.
