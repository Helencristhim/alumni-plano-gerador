Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

Landing in Berlin -- Airport, Taxi, Hotel and Food

You arrive in Berlin and do the simple things of a trip in English. Key words: a layover, baggage claim, a taxi stand, a fare, a receipt, to check in, a reservation, a local dish, a tip, to split the bill. Structure: relative clauses with who, which, that and where.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A layover -- a stop between two flights, when you change planes
"I have a two-hour layover in Frankfurt."
Baggage claim -- the place at the airport where you get your bags after the flight
"Let’s meet at baggage claim."
A taxi stand -- the place where taxis wait for passengers
"The taxi stand is just outside the terminal."
A fare -- the money you pay for a trip in a taxi, a bus or a train
"How much is the fare to the city center?"
A receipt -- a paper or message that shows what you paid
"Could I have a receipt, please?"
To check in -- to arrive at a hotel and give your name to get your room
"I would like to check in, please."
A reservation -- an arrangement to have a room or a table for you
"I have a reservation under the name Calixto."
A local dish -- a typical food from the place where you are
"Currywurst is a local dish in Berlin."
A tip -- extra money you give to a waiter or a driver for good service
"Is the tip included in the bill?"
To split the bill -- to divide the cost of a meal between the people who ate
"Can we split the bill, please?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Juliane lands in Berlin after a long layover in Frankfurt. The woman who works at the information desk tells her the belt where her bags are, at baggage claim.

At the taxi stand, she takes a taxi that goes to the center. The driver, Petra, tells her about the fare and recommends a local dish which people eat on the street: currywurst.

At the hotel where the conference is, Juliane gives her name to check in. Her reservation is ready, and she asks for a receipt for the taxi.

1. What does the woman at the information desk tell Juliane?
A The belt where her bags are.
B The price of the taxi.
C The name of a restaurant.
2. Why does the text say “the hotel where the conference is”?
A Because where is used for people.
B Because where is used for places.
C Because where is used for food.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “Petra is the driver which helped her.”
B “Petra is the driver who she helped her.”
C “Petra is the driver who helped her.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Relative Clauses -- Who, Which, That, Where

GRAMMAR

Join two ideas with one word: who for people, which or that for things, where for places. After the relative word, do not repeat the person, the thing or the place.

JobFormExample
Peoplewho (or that)The driver who took me was from Berlin.
Thingswhich or thatThe bag that I lost was blue.
PlaceswhereThe restaurant where we ate was small.
Short versionthat / who can go when the next word is a personThe dish (that) I ordered was great.
Questions with placesDo you know a place where...?Do you know a place where I can eat late?
The trapno repeated word after the relativeNot the hotel where I stayed there. Say the hotel where I stayed.

Never: The man which helped me · The hotel where I stayed there · The taxi who goes · The restaurant that we ate there
