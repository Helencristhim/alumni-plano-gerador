Video
CareerVidz -- "Tell Me About a Time You Demonstrated Leadership Skills"

A British interview coach taking apart the exact question you practiced today: what a panel is really asking for, and two model answers built around a decision instead of a description. The delivery is slow and very clearly articulated, which makes it easy listening as well as useful preparation.

Tip: stop the video after each model answer and time it. Then say your own version out loud and compare where the decision arrives -- his lands in the first sentence.

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart (Stanford GSB) -- "Directive Speech vs. Dialogue: How Leaders Communicate with Clarity, Balance"

Jonathan Levin, then dean of Stanford Graduate School of Business, on when a leader should be direct and when he should open the room to debate. Free to listen, two academics speaking at natural American speed, and closer to your October conversation than any interview guide.

Tip: listen for the moment he names a decision he had to make alone. Write down the sentence he opens it with, and copy its shape for your own story.

Talk
TED -- Simon Sinek, "Why good leaders make you feel safe" (12 min)

Twelve minutes on the one thing that separates a manager from a leader: who pays when the decision goes wrong. It is the argument behind the phrase you learned today, "to take the heat", and it ends with a story about a soldier that is worth hearing twice.

Tip: watch it once with subtitles off. Then write two sentences about your own crew using "not once" and "only after", and say them out loud.
