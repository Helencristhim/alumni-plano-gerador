Chapter 4: The Code

Who, Which, Whose — and the Comma

How English adds information about people and things

Grammar Discovery

Which Sentences Need the Commas?

"The alumni who give first get the most from the network."

"The program, which I started at forty-four, was eye-opening."

"My learning team, whose members came from six countries, became my sounding board."

"The peers I met in the second residency are now clients."

Take out the words in red: do you still know which alumni and which peers? Now take out the words in blue: do you still know which program and which team? Which kind has commas — and in the last sentence, where is the who?

ToolFormExample
Defining clausewho / that (people), which / that (things); no commas; it tells you which oneThe alumni who give first get the most.
Non-defining clausewho / which between commas; extra information onlyThe program, which I started at forty-four, was eye-opening.
whosepossession, for people and organizationsa team whose members came from six countries
wherea place (= in which)the company where the founder refused to step back
No pronounonly in a defining clause, when the pronoun is the objectThe peers (that) I met are now clients.
Never after a commathat never starts a non-defining clauseThe program, which ... — never The program, that ...

In one line: no commas = it tells you which one; commas = extra information; after a comma, never that.

Grammar Practice

Join the Sentences

Say the joined sentence first, then click to compare

"I met peers in the residency. They are now clients." (no pronoun) ___The peers I met in the residency are now clients.
"The program is in Barcelona. It lasts eighteen months." (which, with commas) ___The program, which lasts eighteen months, is in Barcelona.
"I spoke to an alumna. Her company builds hospitals." (whose) ___I spoke to an alumna whose company builds hospitals.
"We visited a site. The company started there in 1978." (where) ___We visited the site where the company started in 1978.
"Some alumni give first. They get the most from the network." (who) ___The alumni who give first get the most from the network.
Chapter 5: Real Voices

Real Alumni, Two Voices

Real Document

A Reply From an Alumna

Re: Coffee chat about the Executive MBA
From: Marta Ruiz, Class of 2022 · To: Flavio Gattaz

Dear Flavio,

Thank you for your message. I am always happy to talk to people who are thinking about the program, especially engineers, because there were only three of us in my class.

Two things that nobody told me before I started. The residencies, which are the only weeks you spend on campus, are where the real friendships begin, so do not miss any of them. And the learning team that you are given in the first week is the group you will work with for the whole first year.

I could do a call next Thursday, at any time that works for you in São Paulo. Please send me the questions that matter most to you before the call.

Best,
Marta

Sent through the school’s alumni network · Operations Director, Class of 2022
1. Why is Marta especially happy to talk to engineers?
Because there were only three engineers in her class.
2. What does she say about the residencies?
They are the only weeks on campus and they are where the real friendships begin, so he should not miss any of them.
3. What does she ask Flavio to do before the call?
To send her the questions that matter most to him.
Listening 1 · An Interviewer

Did You Really Talk to Them?

An admissions interviewer on the alumni question. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the first thing she checks?
Whether the candidate really had the conversation, or only read the testimonials on the website.
2. Why is a real conversation different from the testimonials?
The testimonials are written to sell. A real conversation contains something the website does not say — something surprising or even worrying.
3. What do the best candidates do?
They quote one person by name and say what changed in their plan because of that conversation.
Listening 2 · An Alumnus

The Part That Paid Off

An alumnus on whether the program was worth the money. Sound first — no text.

1. What is his answer when people ask if it was worth the money?
The part that paid off was not the part he paid for.
2. Who changed his career, and why?
A hospital director from Lisbon who sat next to him in the first residency, whose problems were almost the same as his. They were each other’s sounding board.
3. What is his advice to people who are thinking about applying?
Choose the program where the people in the room are the ones you want to learn from, not the one with the best ranking.
