Chapter 3: The Code

Can You Count It?

many for numbers · much for amounts

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"How many schools are in the program?"

"How much time does a teacher need?"

"We have some data about the big schools."

"Do we have any data about the small schools?"

WordWhenExample
how manythings you COUNT: schools, teachers, questionsHow many schools?
how muchthings you MEASURE: time, data, money, informationHow much time?
someaffirmative sentencesWe have some data.
anyquestions and negativesDo you have any questions? / We do not have any data.
Key ideaOne test decides it: say one, two, three in front of the word. Schools? Yes -> many. Time? No -> much.

And some in a question means you expect a yes: "Would you like some coffee?"

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"How much schools are in the program?"
"How many schools are in the program?"
"We have any data about the big schools."
"We have some data about the big schools."
"How many school do you have?"
"How many schools do you have?"

After many the word is always PLURAL: many schools, many teachers, many questions.

Dialogue

How Many Schools?

R
Hi, Joice. Do you have any questions about the report?
J
Yes, I have some. How many schools are in the program?
R
Four hundred schools, in six states.
J
And how much time does a teacher need to learn the platform?
R
Not much. About thirty minutes, with the short videos.
J
Good. Do we have any data about the small schools?
R
We have some, but not enough. That is a real problem.
J
I think the number of small schools is very big in Brazil.
R
You are right. How many are there, more or less?
J
I do not have the exact number, but I can send it tomorrow.
Comprehension

About Ruth

1. How many schools does Ruth say are in the program?
Four hundred schools, in six states.
2. How much time does a teacher need for the platform?
Not much -- about thirty minutes.
3. What does Ruth say about the small schools?
That they have some data, but not enough.
