Lesson 02 -- Pre-class

Inside the Cockpit

How a flight is planned, checked and cleared -- and why almost every sentence in that world leaves the person out. Key words: a flight plan, a crew, to file, a briefing, clearance, to taxi, a runway, a checklist, to divert, an alternate, a fuel reserve, to be cleared for takeoff. Structure: the passive voice.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A flight plan -- the route, the height and the fuel, written down and filed before departure
“The flight plan is filed with air traffic control hours before anybody boards.”
A crew -- the people who work on board and fly the aircraft
“The crew is briefed an hour before departure, never on the aircraft.”
To file -- to send an official document to the authority that has to approve it
“The route is filed the night before, and it can still be changed in the morning.”
A briefing -- the short meeting where the crew is told what to expect
“Everything unusual about the day is said out loud in the briefing.”
Clearance -- the permission air traffic control gives before an aircraft moves
“Nothing moves on that ground without clearance, not even one metre.”
To taxi -- to move the aircraft on the ground, between the gate and the runway
“The aircraft is taxied to the runway only after the checklist is complete.”
A runway -- the strip an aircraft uses to take off and to land
“The runway has to be clear before any flight is cleared for takeoff.”
A checklist -- the printed list that is read out loud before each phase of a flight
“The checklist is read out loud on the four hundredth flight exactly as on the first.”
To divert -- to send an aircraft to a different airport from the one planned
“The flight was diverted, and from the cabin it looked like a fast decision.”
An alternate -- the second airport, chosen in advance, in case the first one closes
“An alternate had been named in the flight plan hours earlier.”
Turbulence -- rough air that makes the aircraft shake
“Turbulence is uncomfortable and it is almost never dangerous.”
A cruising altitude -- the height at which the aircraft flies for most of the journey
“The cruising altitude is chosen from the winds, not from the view.”
A fuel reserve -- the extra fuel that has to be on board and must not be used
“The fuel reserve had been loaded for exactly this situation.”
To be cleared for takeoff -- to be given permission to leave the ground
“You are cleared for takeoff when the runway is empty and not one second earlier.”

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Open any airline manual and count the sentences that begin with a person. There are almost none. The route is filed. The fuel is calculated. The crew is briefed. The aircraft is cleared for takeoff. Nobody is hiding anything and nobody is being polite. The sentences are built that way because in a procedure the thing that has to happen matters more than whoever happens to be doing it that morning.

The machine is simple once you see it. Take the ordinary sentence, move the object to the front, put the right form of be in the middle, and finish with the past participle. Somebody files the route becomes the route is filed. The tense does not disappear; it moves into be. Present is is filed, past is was filed, and one step further back is had been filed. The participle never changes.

You can still say who did it, and English gives you one word for that: by. The fuel was calculated by a second person. Notice that the sentence is only worth writing that way because the second person is the whole point. If the agent adds nothing, leave it out, and that is why the checklist is read out loud is complete as it stands. Adding by the crew would tell the reader something already obvious.

Two things go wrong for almost everybody. The first is the missing be: the route filed yesterday is not a sentence, it is a label. The second is the participle, because English keeps a pile of irregular ones. Read is spelt like the infinitive and pronounced like red. Flown, taken, given, chosen, written are the ones you will meet first in this world. There is no rule for them and there is no shortcut: you meet them one at a time and you keep them.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive Voice

GRAMMAR

The right form of be plus the past participle. The tense lives in be, never in the participle.

JobFormExample
A routine procedureis / are + participleThe route is filed the night before.
One finished eventwas / were + participleThe flight was diverted to the alternate.
Before another past eventhad been + participleAn alternate had been named hours earlier.
With a modalmust / can / has to be + participleThe runway must be checked before takeoff.
Naming who did itby + person, only if it adds somethingThe fuel was calculated by a second person.
Leaving the person outno by at allThe checklist is read out loud.
The first trapnever drop the beNot the route filed yesterday. Say was filed.
The second trapirregular participlesflown, taken, given, chosen, written, read (said like red).

Never: the route filed yesterday · the flight was divert · the checklist is readed · it was calculated from a second person
