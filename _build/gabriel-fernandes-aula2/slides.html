Chapter 4: The Code

Four Sentences, Nobody in Them

What English does when the person does not matter

Grammar Discovery

What Disappeared, and What Appeared?

"Somebody files the route." → "The route is filed the night before."

"They diverted the flight." → "The flight was diverted to the alternate."

"They had named an alternate." → "An alternate had been named hours earlier."

"A second person calculated the fuel." → "The fuel was calculated by a second person."

In every red pair the object moved to the front, a form of be appeared, and the verb became a participle. The person vanished, and nothing was lost. Only the last one keeps the person, in orange, because there the second person is the entire point of the sentence.

JobFormExample
A routine procedureis / are + participleThe route is filed the night before.
One finished eventwas / were + participleThe flight was diverted.
Before another past eventhad been + participleAn alternate had been named.
With a modalmust / has to be + participleThe runway must be checked.
Naming who did itby, only if it adds somethingcalculated by a second person.
Where the tense livesin be, never in the participleis filed · was filed · had been filed.
The first trapnever drop the beNot the route filed yesterday.
The second trapirregular participlesflown, taken, given, chosen, written, read (RED).

In one line: put the procedure first, be plus participle in the middle, and the person only if it changes something.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

A Pilot, a Dispatcher and Your Own Sheet

Two recordings and one real document

Listening 1 · A Pilot

Checking Work That Was Done by Other People

A commercial pilot on the first half hour. Sound first — no text.

1. What has already been done by the time he sits down?
The route has been filed, the fuel has been calculated twice, and an alternate airport has been chosen for him by somebody he has never met.
2. What does he say his job is in the first half hour?
To check work that was done by other people.
3. Why is the checklist read out loud on the four hundredth flight?
Precisely because it is the four hundredth. Nothing on it is difficult, but everything on it has been forgotten by somebody, once.
Listening 2 · A Dispatcher

A Slow Decision, Executed Quickly

What actually happens when the weather closes an airport. Sound first — no text.

1. What is her honest answer about the moment an airport closes?
That almost nothing is decided in that moment, because almost everything was decided before the aircraft left.
2. What two things had already been prepared, and when?
An alternate had been named in the flight plan hours earlier, and the fuel reserve had been loaded for exactly this.
3. Why does it look like a fast decision from the cabin?
Because the execution is fast. She says it is a slow decision that was made on paper, and then executed quickly.
Real Document

Your Briefing Sheet

CREW BRIEFING — FLIGHT GRU / MIA
Prepared for: G. R. M. Fernandes · Departure 22:40 local · Block time 08h10

Route: filed at 16:20 and accepted by air traffic control. Cruising altitude 37,000 ft, revised to 39,000 after the second hour.

Fuel: calculated from distance, forecast winds and take-off weight. Checked a second time by the dispatcher on duty.

Alternate: Fort Lauderdale. Named at the time of filing. Reserve loaded accordingly and not to be used in normal operation.

Weather: moderate turbulence expected between hours three and four. Cabin service to be suspended for that period.

Crew: briefed at 21:40. Checklist to be read in full before the aircraft is taxied.

Note: nothing on this sheet was decided tonight. Every line was written by somebody who is now asleep.

Count the sentences on this page that name a person. There are two, and both of them are there for a reason.
1. Which two lines name a person, and why is the name worth writing there?
The fuel, checked a second time by the dispatcher on duty, and the note saying every line was written by somebody who is now asleep. In both, the second person is the whole point.
2. What is going to happen during hours three and four, and how does the sheet say it?
Moderate turbulence is expected, and cabin service is to be suspended. Nobody is named in either sentence, because it does not matter who suspends it.
3. What has to happen before the aircraft is taxied?
The checklist has to be read in full. It is written in the passive because the procedure matters and the reader does not.
Go Deeper · Optional

Two Things You Will Meet in a Real Manual

Both of them are on the briefing sheet you just read.

The instruction that has not happened yet. "Cabin service is to be suspended." "The checklist is to be read in full." This is not the future and it is not a suggestion. It is an order, written down, and it is how procedure is given in English without anybody giving it.

The sentence that turns itself around. "Only when the runway is clear is the flight cleared for takeoff." After only when, never and not until, English inverts the verb and the subject. You have read this a hundred times without noticing it.

Your turn. Write one instruction for your own simulator flight using is to be, and one sentence starting with only when.
