Plans and Predictions -- Talking About the Croatia and Slovenia Trip

Talking about the trip you take at the end of August. Key words: itinerary, coastline, ferry, lookout point, recommend, explore, budget, scenic. Structure: going to for plans you already made, will for decisions you make on the spot, and future time expressions.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Itinerary -- the plan of a trip, day by day
"Our itinerary starts in Dubrovnik and ends in Ljubljana."
Coastline -- the line where the land meets the sea
"The Croatian coastline has more than a thousand islands."
Ferry -- a boat that carries people and cars across water
"We are going to take the ferry to the island in the morning."
Lookout point -- a high place where you stop to see the view
"There is a lookout point above the old town with the best view."
To recommend -- to tell someone that something is good and they should try it
"Elena recommended a small place behind the cathedral."
To explore -- to go around a new place to discover what is there
"I want two free days to explore the islands."
Budget -- the amount of money you plan to spend
"Our budget for food is thirty euros a day."
Scenic -- beautiful to look at, especially nature and views
"The scenic road along the coast takes two hours longer."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

In three weeks my sister and I are going to fly to Split. Our itinerary is almost ready. We are going to spend the first four nights on the coast, and then we are going to drive north to Ljubljana.

On the fourth day we are going to take the scenic road along the coastline instead of the fast motorway. There is a lookout point above one of the villages, and I am looking forward to sitting there with a coffee. The rest is open on purpose. We have a small budget, so we are not going to eat in the tourist places. We will decide at lunchtime where to eat. I have no idea what to expect from Ljubljana, but I am sure it will be a good surprise.

1. Why does the text say "we are going to fly" and not "we will fly"?
A Because the flight was decided before this text was written — it is a plan.
B Because "will" cannot be used with the verb "fly".
C Because the flight is not certain yet.
2. The text says "we will decide at lunchtime where to eat". What does that tell you?
A That they have already booked the restaurants.
B That the decision has not been made yet and will happen in the moment.
C That they decided it three weeks before the trip.
3. The text says "I am looking forward to sitting there". Why not "to sit"?
A Because "sit" is an irregular verb.
B Because the action happens more than once.
C Because after "looking forward to" the verb always takes -ing.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Going To and Will

GRAMMAR

Two futures. The difference is not the time — it is when you decided.

FormRuleExample
am / is / are going to + verbA plan you decided before this conversation. Tickets, bookings, intentions.I am going to spend four nights in Dubrovnik.
will + verbA decision you make right now, while you are speaking. Often after a problem.The ferry is full? Then I will take the bus.
will for predictionsWhat you believe about the future. Usually with I think or I am sure.I am sure it will be incredible.
Negativenot after am/is/are, or won't for will.We are not going to eat there.
I won't have time on Friday.
Time expressionsnext week · in August · at the end of the month · soon · in a few weeks. They open or close the sentence: At the end of August we are going to fly to Split.

Watch the trap: after looking forward to the verb always takes -ing. You say "I am looking forward to visiting the islands", never "to visit".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are planning the trip with a friend. Choose the best thing to say.

Your flight is already booked for the thirtieth. Someone asks when you travel. You say:
A "I will fly on the thirtieth."
B "I am going to fly on the thirtieth."
C "I fly on the thirtieth for the first time."
Your friend says the restaurant is closed tonight. You decide something on the spot:
A "No problem, we will find another place near the harbor."
B "No problem, we are going to find another place near the harbor."
C "No problem, we find another place near the harbor."
You want to say you are excited about the islands. The correct sentence is:
A "I am looking forward to visit the islands."
B "I am looking forward visiting the islands."
C "I am looking forward to visiting the islands."
You have three days and want a local opinion. You ask the receptionist:
A "What do you recommend me to do the three days?"

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
