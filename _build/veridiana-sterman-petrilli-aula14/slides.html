Chapter 4: The Code

Whose Is It?

My, mine, and the little 's

Grammar Discovery

Whose Is It?

Lucas brought his grandfather's watch.
Ana showed her grandmother's recipe book.
It is not mine. It is my mother's.
The girls' bags are by the door.

In the blue words, which comes first: the owner or the thing? Why his for Lucas and her for Ana? Where is the apostrophe in the last one?

JobFormExample
Before a thingmy · your · his · her · its · our · theirIt is her book.
Instead of the thingmine · yours · his · hers · ours · theirsIt is hers.
His or her?it follows the OWNER, not the thingLucas and his watch · Ana and her book.
A person owns itowner + 's + thingmy grandfather's watch
Plural owner with -sowners + ' + thingthe girls' bags · my parents' house
The questionWhose + thing?Whose jacket is this?
The trapnever the thing of the person, never her for a man, never her'sNot the watch of my grandfather. Say my grandfather's watch.

In one line: the owner comes first, his and her follow the owner, and hers, ours and theirs never take an apostrophe.

Listening 1 · A Message From School

The Lost and Found Box

A teacher calls the families about things nobody took home. Sound first — no text.

1. What is in the box, and whose is the jacket probably?
A blue jacket, two water bottles, a lunch box and a library book. The jacket is small, so it is probably a girl's or a younger boy's.
2. How can a family know if the pink bottle is their daughter's?
It has the letters S and P on it, so if those are her initials, it is probably hers.
3. Whose is the library book, and what happens on Friday?
It is the school's. On Friday the clothes go to a charity and the book goes back to the library.
Listening 2 · A Father at Home

Whose Camera Is It?

One object, three owners, and a son with a worksheet. Sound first — no text.

1. Whose camera did the son choose, and whose is it now?
His grandfather's old camera. Now it is his grandmother's, because the grandfather gave it to her before he retired.
2. What did the son do on Sunday, and what did he learn?
He called his grandparents. His grandfather bought the camera on his first trip abroad and took every family photo with it for thirty years.
3. When the teacher asked whose idea it was, what did the son say, and what did the father do?
The son said: mine. The father only drove.
