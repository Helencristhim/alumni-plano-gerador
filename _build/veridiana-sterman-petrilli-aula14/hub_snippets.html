My Daughter's Homework -- Whose Is It?

A school newsletter, and how to say whose things are in English. Key words: a worksheet, to hand in, a classmate, a field trip, a permission slip, to belong to, to borrow, to lend, a keepsake, proud of. Structure: possessive adjectives, possessive pronouns and the possessive 's.

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A worksheet -- a page of school exercises that a student fills in
"The worksheet about the object is due next Friday."
To hand in -- to give your finished work to the teacher
"My daughter hands in her homework every Monday."
A classmate -- a person who is in the same class as you
"Ana's classmates wanted to see the recipe book."
A field trip -- a school visit to a place outside the school
"The field trip to the museum is on Thursday."
A permission slip -- a paper that parents sign to say their child can go
"Please sign the permission slip and send it back by Monday."
To belong to -- to be somebody's, to be their property
"This watch belongs to my grandfather."
To borrow -- to take something of somebody else's and give it back later
"Can I borrow your pen for a minute?"
To lend -- to give something of yours to somebody for a short time
"My mother lends me her old camera."
A keepsake -- a small object you keep because it reminds you of a person
"A child's first shoe is a lovely keepsake."
Proud of -- very happy about something good that you or somebody did
"The children are very proud of their museum."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Every note from school answers one small question again and again: whose is it? Whose project, whose jacket, whose signature. English has three tools for this, and none of them works the way it does in Portuguese.

The first tool goes before a thing: my, your, his, her, its, our, their. "It is her worksheet." The important detail is that his and her follow the owner, not the thing. Lucas brought his grandfather's watch, because Lucas is a boy. Ana showed her grandmother's book, because Ana is a girl.

The second tool goes instead of the thing: mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs. "The pink bottle is hers." "It isn't mine." These words never take an apostrophe.

The third tool is the little 's, and here the order is the opposite of Portuguese: the owner comes first. Not the watch of my grandfather, but my grandfather's watch. When the owner is plural and already ends in -s, only the apostrophe is added: the girls' bags, my parents' house. And to ask, English uses one word: "Whose jacket is this?"

1. How do you say that the jacket belongs to your daughter?
A "It's the jacket of my daughter, the blue one with the small pocket on the left."
B "It's my daughter's jacket."
C "It's my daughters jacket."
2. Lucas talks about his grandfather's watch. Which word is correct: "Lucas brought ___ grandfather's watch"?
A Her, because the watch is a thing, and English uses her for most things.
B Its, because a watch is an object and not a person in the family.
C His, because the owner, Lucas, is a boy.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "The pink bottle is hers."
B "The pink bottle is her's, and the green one is my son's, I think."
C "The pink bottle is of her."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Whose Is It?

GRAMMAR

The owner comes first. His and her follow the owner. Hers, ours and theirs never take an apostrophe.

JobFormExample
Before a thingmy · your · his · her · its · our · theirIt is our project.
Instead of the thingmine · yours · his · hers · ours · theirsThe book is theirs.
A person owns itowner + 's + thingmy mother's camera
Plural owner with -sowners + ' + thingthe girls' school
Negativeit isn't + mine / yours / hersIt isn't mine.
Questionwhose + thing + is / areWhose bag is this?

Never: the jacket of my daughter · Lucas and her watch · her's · it is of me

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

At the school gate, the teacher holds up a blue jacket. It is your daughter's. You say:
A "Oh, that is the jacket of my daughter, she lost it last week in the playground."
B "Excuse me, I think this jacket is my daughter's."
C "This jacket is of my daughter."
Another mother asks if a pen on the table is yours. It is your friend's. You say:
A "Sorry, it isn't my, it is of my friend, she is sitting over there by the window."
B "No, is her's."
C "Sorry, it isn't mine. It's my friend's."
Your daughter needs a book that her classmate has. You write to the classmate's mother:
A "Can my daughter borrow your son's book until Friday?"
B "Can your son lend my daughter's book back to her before the test on Friday morning?"
C "Can my daughter lend your son's book?"
The teacher asks whose idea the family museum object was. It was your daughters' idea. You say:
A "It was the idea of the girls, not of me, because they always have the best ideas at home."
B "It was the girls' idea, not mine."
C "It was girl's idea, not my."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.
