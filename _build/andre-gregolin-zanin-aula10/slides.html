Column one is easy and slightly embarrassing. Column two is where everybody lies, including the person who invented the page. Column three is short, boring, and the only honest one. The test at the bottom is the whole method compressed into a single question.
1. What is the rule for column two, and why does the page need one?
Nothing goes in until it has already happened four times, because column two is where everybody lies.
2. How is column three described, and why is that the point?
As short, boring and the only honest one — because it records what survived after the interest ran out.
3. What is the October test, in one question?
Were you still doing it when nobody was watching?
Chapter 4: The Code

The Verb in Front Decides

And with four verbs, it decides the meaning too

Grammar Discovery

What Comes After the First Verb?

"I gave up playing competitively, and I keep telling myself I will go back."

"I intend to take the exam in November, and I have decided to apply to two places."

"I stopped playing basketball." — "I stopped to think about why."

"My teacher told me to review the unit, and my father wants me to rest."

The verb in front chooses. Some verbs always take the -ing form, some always take to, and a small group take both. Read the third line twice: it is the same verb, and the two sentences are not about the same thing at all.

JobFormExample
Always -ingenjoy · avoid · consider · keep · practise · suggest · finish · mind · postpone · give upI gave up playing competitively.
Always towant · need · decide · plan · manage · hope · expect · afford · agree · refuse · intendI intend to take the exam.
Both — and the meaning changesstop · try · remember · forgetI stopped playing = I quit. / I stopped to think = I paused in order to.
Verb + object + totell · want · need · ask · advise · allowMy teacher told me to review it.
After a preposition, always -ingat · on · in · about · and to when it is a prepositionI look forward to seeing it. I am used to waking up early.
Three fixed patterns, all -ingend up + -ing · cannot help + -ing · be worth + -ingYou end up not doing it. It is worth keeping.
The trapafter to as a preposition it is -ing, not the bare verbNot I look forward to see you. Say I look forward to seeing you.

In one line: the verb in front decides — and with stop, try, remember and forget it decides the meaning as well.

Listening 1 · A Voice Note

The Thing I Resumed at Thirty-One

The woman who keeps the notebook, on the entry she waited two years to write. Sound first — no text.

1. What did she stop doing, and how does she describe the way it stopped?
She stopped playing the piano at twenty-three. She did not decide to — she ended up not doing it, one week at a time, until it had been eight years.
2. Why did she hesitate to put it in the third column?
Because the third column is for things you kept, and she was afraid of writing something she would have to remove the following year.
3. What does she say the actual work was?
The two years of not writing it down. She says anybody can start again in January, but deserving the third column takes longer.
Listening 2 · A Podcast

Nobody Remembers Stopping

A researcher on why people cannot name the day they quit. Sound first — no text.

1. What could the four hundred people not do, and what could they do easily?
They could not name the date they stopped. They could name the day they started, in extraordinary detail.
2. Why does he say a beginning is stored and an ending is not?
A beginning is a decision, so it is stored as an event. An ending is usually the eleventh missed Tuesday, and there is nothing to store.
3. What is the slightly rude question he asks, and why does the answer matter?
Did you give it up, or did you end up not doing it? The second is much easier to reverse, because there is no decision standing in the way.
