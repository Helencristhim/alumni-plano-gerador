FormWhat it doesExample
redundant pairstwo words that mean the same thingeach and every → every; past history → history
weak verb + nouna general verb carrying a noun that should be the verbmake a decision → decide; give consideration to → consider

The C2 trapcutting the voice along with the padding. Concision is not a telegram. Keep the detail, the rhythm and the one sentence that sounds like you; cut only what says nothing.

Chapter 5: Voices on Revision

The Cut, Out Loud

A counselor, a magazine editor and a writing instructor

Dialogue

Mr. Brooks and the 812-Word Draft

D
Your draft is 812 words. The limit is 650. How do you feel about losing 162 of them?
S
Honestly? Like someone is asking me to choose which fingers to keep.
D
Then let us not start with fingers. Let us start with fingernails. Look at your second paragraph.
S
It says, it is important to note that at that particular moment in time I made the decision to step back.
D
How would you say that out loud to a friend?
S
At that moment, I stepped back. Oh. That is twenty words gone and nothing lost.
D
Exactly. Do that to every paragraph before you touch a single idea. Now, the sentence about the guard warning you four times stays.
S
Even if it is a bit long?
D
It is the only sentence in the essay that nobody else could have written. When I edit, that is the sentence I protect.
S
So I cut the padding around my voice, not my voice.
Comprehension

About Mr. Brooks

1. How does Mr. Brooks suggest Stella should begin cutting, using his image of fingers and fingernails?
By removing small, unnecessary words first, before cutting any important ideas.
2. What does he ask Stella to do with the padded sentence?
To say it out loud as she would to a friend.
3. Which sentence does Mr. Brooks refuse to cut, and why?
The sentence about the guard warning her four times, because nobody else could have written it.
Listening 1

The Magazine Editor

A magazine editor speaks at a student writing festival. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. What does the editor say every writer she works with believes about their first draft?
That every word in it is necessary.
2. What exercise does she describe for cutting?
Cutting ten percent of any piece before sending it, without removing a single idea.
3. What does she say is the difference between a short piece and a concise one?
A short piece has few words; a concise piece has no wasted ones, so it can be long and still concise.
Listening 2

The Writing Instructor

A college writing instructor talks to first-year students. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. How does the instructor say students usually react to word limits?
They see them as a punishment or an obstacle to saying everything they want to say.
2. According to him, what does a word limit force a writer to do?
To decide what really matters, which is the hardest and most important part of writing.
3. What order of cutting does he recommend?
Words first, then sentences, and whole ideas only at the very end.
