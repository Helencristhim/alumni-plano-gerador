"Rehearse it tonight, then leave the paper in the hotel room."

"Stage fright is normal; reading from paper is what kills a panel."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Izabel walks on stage at eleven fifteen, right after the keynote. Never had she spoken about a failure in front of three hundred people, and the moderator opens with the hardest question in the room. She gives the hook first: one week. Not only did AVRT lose the account, it lost the confidence of an entire warehouse. She tells one anecdote — the Thursday call, and a warehouse that could not trust its own sensors — and the room goes quiet. Only after the weekly report started did the client come back, she says, and that is the whole point. At no point did AVRT ask Meridian to keep the failure quiet. Then David Chen leans into his microphone: rarely does a supplier publish a number that makes it look worse, and that is exactly why he agreed to sit on this panel. Seldom is credibility built by a slide; it is built by the ugly number nobody asked you to show.

1. "Never had she spoken about a failure in front of three hundred people." What comes right after "Never"?
A the auxiliary had, and only then the subject — the same order as a question
B the subject, because the sentence is not a question
C the main verb, because the auxiliary is optional here
2. Why "Not only did AVRT lose the account" and not "Not only AVRT lost the account"?
A Because "lose" is an irregular verb.
B Because a negative adverbial at the front forces auxiliary + subject.
C Because the sentence is in the past.
3. In "Only after the weekly report started did the client come back", which part is inverted?
A The time clause — "only after did the report start".
B Both clauses at the same time.
C Only the main clause — "did the client come back".

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Inversion After Negative Adverbials

GRAMMAR

The switch English uses to put the spotlight on one sentence — the grammar of a stage, a speech and a strong opening paragraph.

TriggerStructureExample
Never / Rarely / Seldomadverb + auxiliary + subject + verbNever had we lost a client that fast.
Not onlyNot only + do / does / did + subject + base verbNot only did we publish the numbers, we published the ugly ones.
Only after / Only when / Not untilthe time clause stays normal; the main clause invertsOnly after the report started did adoption climb.
At no point / Under no circumstancessame switch, strongest registerAt no point did AVRT ask them to keep it quiet.
The one ruleAfter the negative adverbial the sentence takes question word order: auxiliary first, subject second. Never "Never we had lost", never "Not only we published".
Watch outAfter do / does / did the main verb goes back to its base form: "At no point did we ask", not "did we asked". And keep it rare — two or three per talk. This is a spotlight, not a habit.
