Aula 19 -- Telling the Story on Stage -- The Client Panel

Film
The King's Speech (2010)

A man who has to speak in public, hates it, and does it anyway. Stage fright, rehearsal, a coach who refuses to let him read from paper, and a single broadcast that has to land. Every idea from Lesson 19 is in this film, dramatized: the hook, the pause, and the difference between reading a text and owning a room.

Tip: watch the rehearsal scenes with English subtitles and count the pauses before the stressed word. Then record your own panel opening and try to leave the same amount of silence after "Never".

Talk
Chris Anderson -- "TED's secret to great public speaking"

The head of TED explains what every memorable talk has in common: one idea, built in the listener's mind, opened with a hook and closed with a line worth repeating. Short, free and open on TED, with an English transcript and subtitles.

Tip: write down the one idea you would build in the room's mind at the Meridian panel, in a single sentence. If it takes two sentences, it is not ready yet.

YouTube
"Inversion 1: After Negative or Limiting Adverbs" -- English Masterclass, BBC Learning English

A short, precise masterclass on exactly the structure from Lesson 19: what happens to the word order after never, rarely, seldom, not only and at no point. It also shows the register question — when this sounds powerful and when it sounds theatrical.

Tip: pause after each example and rebuild it with a real AVRT fact. Then say three of your own out loud, standing up, stressing the first word.
