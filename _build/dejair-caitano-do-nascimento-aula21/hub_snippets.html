Saying It Like a Pro -- The Panel and the Hallway

The same finding for a formal panel and for a casual conversation in the hallway. Key words: register, formal, casual, hallway, precise, stance, to convey, to demonstrate. Structure: panel verbs and hallway verbs -- discover / find out, conduct / carry out.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. Four words describe the style of your English; four describe what a good speaker does with it.

Register -- the style of language you choose for a situation, formal or casual
"On the panel, I use a formal register; at lunch, a casual one."
Formal -- serious and careful, for official situations like a panel
"In a formal answer, I say "we conducted the study"."
Casual -- relaxed and friendly, for conversations with colleagues
"At the coffee break, a casual "we found out" is perfect."
Hallway -- the long area outside the rooms, where people talk between sessions
"The best conversations happen in the hallway, not in the room."
Precise -- exact and correct in every detail
"On the panel, my numbers have to be precise."
Stance -- your opinion or position on a topic
"My stance is clear: inflammation is part of the story."
To convey -- to communicate an idea or a feeling to somebody
"A good example can convey a complex idea in ten seconds."
To demonstrate -- to show clearly that something is true
"Our results demonstrate a clear pattern in the data."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Ana is a nurse researcher from Porto Alegre. On Monday she spoke on a panel in Toronto. She said: "We conducted the study in two hospitals. Our results demonstrate that pain increased after the second week, and we will investigate the cause." Ten minutes later, in the hallway, a young doctor asked her about the same study. This time she said: "Basically, we carried out the study in two hospitals, and we found out that the pain went up after week two. We're going to look into it next year." Same facts, different register.
In the hallway, Ana says "we found out" instead of "we discovered". Why?
A Because "discovered" is wrong, and native speakers never use it in any situation at a congress
B Because the hallway is casual, and casual English often uses phrasal verbs
C Because the young doctor did not understand her
Which pair has the same meaning?
A "conducted" and "looked into", because both verbs are about the work that a research team does in a hospital
B "demonstrate" and "carry out"
C "increased" and "went up"

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Panel Verbs and Hallway Verbs

GRAMMAR

Many ideas have two verbs: a phrasal verb (verb + small word) for casual English, and one longer verb for formal English. Same meaning, different room.

hallway (casual)panel (formal)example
find outdiscoverWe found out / discovered the cause.
carry outconductWe carried out / conducted the study.
go upincreaseThe pain went up / increased.
look intoinvestigateWe'll look into it / We will investigate it.
set upestablishWe set up / established a network.

Negative and question: We didn't find out the cause. / Did you carry out a second study? The formal verb takes no small word: we investigated the problem (never investigated into).

Both are correct. The room decides: panel = formal verb; hallway = phrasal verb.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
You are on a formal panel. Which sentence is best?
A "We discovered an unexpected pattern in the data."
B "Basically, we found out some cool stuff about pain, and it was really surprising for all of us in the lab."
C "Pain stuff, very interesting."
A friend at the coffee break asks about your next step. Which sentence sounds natural?
A "The research team will investigate the aforementioned phenomenon in the subsequent academic year, as planned."
B "Investigation will be."
C "We're going to look into it next year."
Which sentence is correct?
A "We investigated the side effects."
B "We investigated into the side effects of the drug in our second study, with a bigger group of patients."
C "We looked the side effects into it."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Two minutes. Choose one finding from your research. Say it first for a formal panel (discover, conduct, demonstrate), then for a friend at the coffee break (find out, carry out, basically). Same facts, two registers. No script -- just speak.
