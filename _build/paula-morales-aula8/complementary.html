Lesson 8 -- Who, What, Where, When, How: Asking the Right Questions

Series
Emily in Paris · Season 1, Episode 1 — “Emily in Paris” · Netflix — included in the subscription

This episode is here for one very specific reason, and it is not the fashion: it is your situation, on screen. A marketing executive lands alone in a country whose language she does not speak, with work waiting on Monday morning. She does not understand the doorman, the neighbour or the menu — and she still gets to the office. How? By asking. The whole episode is a grown, competent woman crossing a strange place on where is, what is and how do I. Notice one thing: she gets it wrong all the time, and nothing bad happens.

Tip: watch it with English subtitles — the goal here is not to understand everything. Do ONE thing only: every time Emily asks a question, hit pause and look at the second slot in the sentence — it is always is, are, do or does, never the subject. Then say your own version out loud: “Where is the gate?” You do not have to finish the episode — the first 20 minutes (her arrival) are enough for the task.

This episode is our lesson, in audio, and Shayna opens with the line that sums up the day: “If you want to have a good conversation in English, learn to ask good questions.” She teaches the question words and then gives 100 ready-made examples — which is exactly what you are missing: it is not understanding the rule, it is having the question in your mouth before you need it. She is American, so the accent is the one you will hear at Zurich and in Miami. The page has the lesson PDF for free.

Tip: listen the first time without stopping and without reading anything, just to let your ear catch the rhythm — listening is your strength, you understand more than you think. The second time, download the PDF and pause at each example to say YOUR version out loud: where she says “Where do you live?”, you say “Where is the gate?”. Do not try all 100 — pick five you would really use this week and repeat only those, three times each. Open and free, with no sign-up to listen.
