How to Write Professional Emails in English

A North American teacher builds a professional email on the board, part by part: the subject line, the greeting, the opening sentence, the body and the sign-off. He shows several versions of the same message at different levels of formality, which is exactly the choice you made tonight when you picked between Best regards and Best.

Tip: pause at each sign-off he writes and decide, out loud, which reader it fits.

The 1 Tip to Improve Your Communication Skills at Work

An American product marketer explains the Bottom Line Up Front method with a realistic work situation: the same message written the way most people write it, and then written with the conclusion at the top. Six minutes, fast natural American English, and the whole video is the rule you practiced tonight.

Tip: stop the video before he shows his version and write your own bottom line first.

Plain Language: Writing for Understanding

The official writing standard of the United States federal government, and the source of the rule you learned tonight. The section called Avoid hidden verbs is nominalization seen from the other side: it shows, with real before-and-after sentences, what a noun costs when there is a person who should be in the sentence.

Tip: read only the sections on hidden verbs and the active voice, then run the test on one email you sent this month.
