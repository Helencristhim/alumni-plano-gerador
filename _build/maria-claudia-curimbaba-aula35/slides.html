Chapter 3 -- The Code

Started Then, Still Going

I have been painting... How long have you been...?

Discovery

Four Sentences, One Story

Read the highlighted words. When did each action start? Is it finished?

"I have been painting for about ten years."

"Lately I have been experimenting with vermiculite."

"How long have you been working on this series?"

"Mixing vermiculite with pigment is harder than it looks."

UseFormExample
started in the past, still goinghave / has been + -ing (+ for / since)"I have been painting for ten years."
a recent activity, again and againlately / recently + have been + -ing"Lately I have been experimenting with vermiculite."
asking about durationHow long have you been + -ing?"How long have you been working on it?"
result, not activityhave + past participle (present perfect)"I have finished three canvases."
an activity as the subject-ing + singular verb"Mixing the colors is the hardest part."

Key idea: have been + -ing tells the story of an activity that started before and is still part of your life. Three traps: "I am painting since 2016" (use have been painting); verbs like know and like do not take -ing here ("I have been knowing" -- say "I have known"); and an -ing subject takes a singular verb ("Mixing colors is", never "are").

The Structure

Three Jobs in Your Story

The same tense, doing three jobs when you talk about your work -- in the studio or at the office.

1 · Say how long

"I have been painting for about ten years."

The listener hears a long story, still going.

2 · Say what is new

"Lately I have been experimenting with vermiculite."

The listener hears what you are curious about now.

3 · Ask about the other person

"How long have you been working as a curator?"

The conversation becomes a real exchange, not an interview.

Notice: the same sentences work at the holding -- "We have been working with Houston for three years."

Maria Claudia Curimbaba · Houston · program, page 4

Medium

acrylic, earth pigment and vermiculite on canvas.

Training

self-taught; painting since 2016, in the evenings and on weekends.

Dialogue

The Artist Interview

D

"Thanks for doing this. I have been preparing the program for the gallery evening all week, and the guests want to know the artists. How long have you been painting?"

M

"I have been painting for about ten years. It is not my profession, though. It is what I do when I leave the office."

D

"I understand that. I have been working as a curator for twenty years, and I still paint on Sundays. What have you been experimenting with lately?"

M

"Vermiculite. I mix it with the pigment, so the canvas has a texture you almost want to touch."

D

"That is brave. I tried sand once, and it was a disaster. Is vermiculite difficult to work with?"

M

"Very. Getting the mix right is pure trial and error. I have ruined a lot of canvases this year."

D

"That is the only way to find a technique. And where does your inspiration come from?"

M

"From Minas Gerais, where my family is from. The colors of the earth there are in this whole body of work."
