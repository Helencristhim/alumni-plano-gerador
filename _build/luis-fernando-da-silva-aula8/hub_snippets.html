The Year-End Room

Where things get approved, and where they get decided. Key words: to announce, to approve, to submit, to review, to delegate, to negotiate, to hire, the board, to hand over, to be held accountable, above my pay grade, it is out of my hands. Structure: the passive voice, present and past simple, and when to switch it off.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To announce -- to say something officially, so that everybody hears it at the same time
"The new head of region will be announced before March."
To approve -- to say officially that something may go ahead
"The revised limits were approved without objection."
To submit -- to send a document formally to somebody who will decide on it
"The quarterly figures were submitted to the finance committee on the ninth."
To review -- to look at something again in order to judge it
"The credit policy is reviewed every quarter, whether anything has changed or not."
To delegate -- to give a task, and the authority for it, to somebody who reports to you
"The southern portfolio was delegated to the regional analyst in the meantime."
To negotiate -- to discuss terms until both sides can accept them
"Terms with the two largest distributors are still being negotiated."
To hire -- to take somebody on as an employee
"A new head of region will be hired before March."
The board -- the small group that oversees a company on behalf of its owners
"The board asked to be kept informed weekly, which is unusual."
To hand over -- to pass a task, a case or a responsibility formally to somebody else
"The file was handed over to the legal team on Friday."
To be held accountable -- to be the person who has to answer for a result, whoever did the work
"Somebody is always held accountable for a bad season, and it is rarely the weather."
Above my pay grade -- a modest way of saying the decision is made far above you
"That is above my pay grade, but I can find out who decided it."
It is out of my hands -- the matter has left you and you can no longer influence it
"The file was handed over on Friday. Honestly, it is out of my hands now."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Open any set of minutes and notice what is missing. The figures are submitted every quarter. The policy is reviewed. The revised limits were approved on the fifteenth. A new head of region will be hired before March. Things happen, dates are recorded, and the person who did them has left the sentence. The shape is always the same: a form of the verb be, and then the past participle. Portuguese usually solves the same idea with a reflexive construction and no auxiliary at all, and that is exactly why the verb be is the part Brazilian speakers drop. The report submitted every quarter is not a sentence in English.

Three variations do most of the work. Present, for what happens regularly: the report is submitted. Past, for one event: the limits were approved. And the version for something still in progress: terms are being negotiated with the two largest distributors. If the actor does matter after all, English brings it back with by: all accounts are reviewed by the compliance team. Notice it is by, never from. And a case that has been handed over is a case that is out of your hands, which is the same idea said twice, once formally and once not.

Then comes the part that decides how you are remembered. Three hours after the meeting, the same people are at the year-end dinner, and the grammar has to go into reverse. Nobody at a table wants to hear that the file was reviewed and the limit was raised. They want to hear that you read it on a Sunday, that you called the dealer yourself, and that you were wrong about him. If a decision genuinely was not yours, you have honest ways of saying so — that it is above my pay grade, that somebody else will be held accountable, that it was handed over in March. What you must not do is describe your own week as if it happened to somebody else. On the page the actor disappears and the decision survives. At the table it is the other way round.

1. What is the one part Brazilian speakers most often drop, and why?
A The verb "be". Portuguese solves the same idea with a reflexive construction and no auxiliary, so there is nothing to remind you to say it.
B The past participle, because it is irregular.
C The date, because minutes rarely need one.
2. How does English bring the actor back when it does matter?
A With "from": reviewed from the compliance team.
B With "by": reviewed by the compliance team.
C It cannot. Once the actor is gone, it is gone.
3. According to the text, what must you not do at the dinner table?
A Admit that a decision was not yours.
B Describe your own week as if it had happened to somebody else.
C Mention the minutes at all.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive Voice

GRAMMAR

On the page the actor disappears and the decision survives. At the table it is the other way round.

JobFormExample
Something done regularlyis / are + past participleThe report is submitted every quarter.
Something done once, in the pastwas / were + past participleThe limits were approved on the fifteenth.
Still in progressis / are being + past participleTerms are being negotiated.
In the futurewill be + past participleA new head of region will be hired before March.
Bringing the actor back... by + person or team (never from)All accounts are reviewed by the compliance team.
The other roomat a dinner, put yourself back inI read the file on a Sunday and I called him myself.
The trapthe verb be is not optionalNot the report submitted every quarter. Say is submitted.

Never: the report submitted every quarter · the limits were approve · reviewed from the compliance team · at a dinner, "the dealer was called by me".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

In the minutes, you want to record that the figures go in every quarter. You write:
A "The report submitted every quarter."
B "The report is submitted every quarter."
C "The report is submit every quarter."
You want to name the team that checks the accounts. You say:
A "All accounts are reviewed by the compliance team."
B "All accounts are reviewed from the compliance team."
C "All accounts are review of the compliance team."
At the year-end dinner, somebody asks what you actually do. The best answer is:
A "The files are reviewed and the limits are raised or maintained on a quarterly basis."
B "Last month I read a file on a Sunday, called the dealer myself, and I was completely wrong about him."
C "It was decided that the exposure would be maintained at current levels."
Somebody asks who decided something that was genuinely not your decision. You say:
A "Mistakes were made and it is complicated."
B "That one is above my pay grade. It was handed over to the finance director in March."
C "I prefer not to comment about this subject."
