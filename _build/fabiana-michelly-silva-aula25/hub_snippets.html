Lesson 25 -- Pre-class

Capstone Fluency Sprint -- The Rapid Round

Preparing for the QBR rapid round: ten minutes of questions with no script. Key words: think on your feet, off the cuff, curveball, rambling, concise, articulate, get to the point, lose your train of thought, circle back, pivot, rephrase, hesitation. Structure: the B2 toolkit -- choosing the right structure for the job (distancing, negative questions, past perfect continuous, was supposed to, should + inversion), fast.

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Think on your feet -- to react quickly and well when there is no time to prepare
"The rapid round is meant to test how well you think on your feet."
Off the cuff -- said without any preparation
"She answered off the cuff, and it still sounded clear."
Curveball -- a surprising question or problem that you did not expect
"The CFO threw a curveball about freight costs."
Rambling -- talking for too long without a clear point
"A rambling answer loses the room in thirty seconds."
Concise -- short and clear, with no extra words
"Keep each answer concise: one point, one number."
Articulate -- able to express ideas clearly and well
"Priya wants an articulate answer, not a long one."
Get to the point -- to say the most important thing directly
"Get to the point in your first sentence."
Lose your train of thought -- to suddenly forget what you were going to say
"If you lose your train of thought, go back to the number."
Circle back -- to return to a topic later, when you have more information
"Can I circle back to that after I check the figure?"
Pivot -- to move a conversation smoothly in a new direction
"Pivot from the problem to the plan."
Rephrase -- to say something again in different words
"If the question is unclear, rephrase it before you answer."
Hesitation -- a short pause because you are not sure what to say
"A one-second hesitation is fine; a long silence is not."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Monday, Fabiana presents the supplier update at the quarterly business review. She has five minutes, and then the leadership team asks questions for ten. It is called the rapid round, and it is meant to show how well a presenter can think on their feet. On Friday, Marcus runs a practice round and throws her a curveball: "Why don't we just drop Penang?"

Fabiana does not learn anything new for this. She chooses tools she already has. To tell the backstory, she says that Penang had been running at full capacity when the fire hit. To report news she cannot confirm, she says it is thought that the new line will be ready by November. To persuade, she asks: "Wouldn't one supplier leave us exposed again?" And to sound in control, she says: "Should the CFO ask for a number I don't have, I'll circle back by Friday."

Her rules for Monday are simple. Get to the point in the first sentence. Keep every answer concise, never rambling. If a question is unclear, rephrase it. If it goes the wrong way, pivot. A short hesitation is fine, and if she loses her train of thought, she goes back to the number.

1. Why does Fabiana say that Penang "had been running" at full capacity?
A To give an opinion about Penang.
B To tell the backstory: what was going on before the fire.
C To talk about the future.
2. Why does she say "It is thought that the new line will be ready by November"?
A Because she cannot confirm the news yet.
B Because she is sure about the date.
C Because she does not care about the date.
3. What is the rapid round meant to show?
A How many slides a presenter can prepare.
B How long a presenter can talk.
C How well a presenter can think on their feet.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The B2 Toolkit

GRAMMAR

No new grammar today. Fluency means choosing the right structure for the job, fast.

The jobThe toolExample
Report news you cannot confirmit is thought that / is reported toIt is thought that the line will be ready by November.
Persuade without pushingWouldn't / Isn't / Don't + subject + verb?Wouldn't one supplier leave us exposed?
Tell the backstoryhad been + -ingPenang had been running at full capacity when the fire hit.
Name a plan that did not happenwas / were supposed to + base verbThe shipment was supposed to arrive on Monday.
Sound in control of a possible futureShould + subject + base verb, ...Should the port close again, we will reroute.
Question formIs it thought that...? · Hadn't they been...?Is it thought that the line will be ready?

Never: It is thought the line will ready · Wouldn't one supplier leaves us · They had been run · The shipment supposed to arrive · Should the port closes

Stage 2: Put the Opening in Order

Order

Fabiana plans her opening for the rapid round. Put her plan in order.

?"First, she opens with the decision: the board is asked to approve the dual-supplier contract for 2027."
?"Second, she gives the backstory in one sentence: Penang had been running at full capacity for months when the fire hit."
?"Third, she reports the latest news with care: it is thought that the new line will be ready by November."
?"Then she makes her case with a question: wouldn't one supplier leave us exposed again?"
?"Finally, she closes with a plan: should the port close again, the team will reroute through Singapore within forty-eight hours."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a senior professional would give.

The CFO asks for a number you do not have with you. You say:
A "Hmm, I think it was maybe around two million, more or less, I am not sure."
B "I'd rather not guess. I'll circle back to you with the exact figure by Friday."
C "That is not my area."
Someone asks a long, unclear question in the rapid round. The best first move is:
A "Let me rephrase that to make sure I've got it: you're asking whether the second supplier is worth the cost?"
B Start answering and hope it is the right question.
C "Can you repeat? I didn't understand anything."
The board wants to drop the second supplier to save money. You want them to reconsider. You say:
A "You are wrong about this."
B "Maybe it is a good idea, I don't know."
C "Wouldn't one supplier leave us exposed if Penang goes down again?"
You lose your train of thought in the middle of an answer. The best move is:
A Stop and apologize for your English.
B Take a short pause and go back to the key number: "The key figure is twelve days of lost supply."
C Keep talking until you remember.
