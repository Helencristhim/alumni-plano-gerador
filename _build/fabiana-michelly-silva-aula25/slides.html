Chapter 3: The Toolkit

The Right Tool for the Job

Five structures you already know, five different jobs

Grammar Discovery

Five Jobs, Five Tools

"It is thought that the new line will be ready by November."

"Wouldn't one supplier leave us exposed again?"

"Penang had been running at full capacity for months when the fire hit."

"The shipment was supposed to arrive on Monday."

"Should the port close again, we will reroute through Singapore."

Each purple structure does a different job. Which one reports news without committing? Which one persuades? Which one tells the backstory? Which one names a plan that failed? Which one sounds in control of the future?

The jobThe toolExample
Report news you cannot confirmit is thought that / is reported toIt is thought that the line will be ready by November.
Persuade without pushingnegative question: Wouldn't / Isn't / Don't...?Wouldn't one supplier leave us exposed?
Tell the backstorypast perfect continuous: had been + -ingPenang had been running at full capacity when the fire hit.
Name a plan that did not happenwas / were supposed to + base verbThe shipment was supposed to arrive on Monday.
Sound in control of a possible futureShould + subject + base verb, ...Should the port close again, we will reroute.
The trapunder pressure, everything becomes present simpleNot the line is ready in November, not one supplier leaves us exposed.

In one line: fluency is not more words; it is the right tool, chosen fast.

Chapter 4: The Rehearsal

The Friday Rehearsal

One practice round, two messages, one run sheet

Dialogue

Ninety Seconds per Question

M
Okay, rapid-round practice. Ninety seconds per question, and I'm going to throw you a curveball or two.
F
Go ahead. If I start rambling, stop me.
M
First one. Why did Penang fall behind in July?
F
Short version: they had been running at full capacity for months when the fire hit. It is thought that the new line will be ready by November.
M
Good, concise. Now the curveball: why don't we just drop Penang and stay with one supplier?
F
Let me rephrase that: is a second supplier worth the cost? Wouldn't one supplier leave us exposed again?
M
Nice pivot. And if the CFO asks for a number you don't have?
F
Should that happen, I won't guess. I'll circle back to him by Friday.

Listening 1 · A Tip from Sales

What the CFO Does

Daniel calls with advice about Monday. Sound first — no text.

1. What happened to the logistics manager last quarter?
The CFO asked why freight costs had gone up, and the manager lost his train of thought halfway through the answer.
2. What three things does Daniel tell Fabiana to do in each answer?
Get to the point in the first sentence, give one number, and stop.
3. What should Fabiana do if she can't answer a question, and what does the CFO hate?
Say she will circle back by the end of the week, and really do it. He hates slides with more than three bullet points.
Listening 2 · The Format

The Rapid Round

Priya Nair, VP of Operations, leaves a voice message. Sound first — no text.

1. How does the QBR work for each presenter?
Five minutes of presentation, then ten minutes of questions from the leadership team: the rapid round.
2. What are the rapid-round questions meant to do?
Show how well the presenter thinks on their feet, not catch them out.
3. What does Priya recommend Fabiana open with, and why?
The decision she needs, not the history, because the board already knows about the fire in Penang.
