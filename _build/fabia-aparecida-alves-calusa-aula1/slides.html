The Pattern

Four ways to say what a firm does

We specialise in payroll and tax outsourcing.
We handle the whole back office for our clients.
My team takes care of around two hundred companies.
I look after the human resources side.

What a company always does → present simple

Not "we are handling", not "we handled". This is what you do every day, so the verb stays plain — and it takes an s only with he / she / it / my team / the firm.

Did You Catch It?

Who is who?

1. Which area does David look after?
Operations. Karen is on the finance side.
2. Why are they looking for a partner in São Paulo?
They are opening a branch in January and do not want to build a finance team from scratch.
3. Who actually signs off on this deal?
Neither of them — Ralph, at head office in London.
4. What is David worried about, and why?
Payroll, and the size of the firm — they have been burned by a small firm before.
Listening 1

The message before the meeting

1. How many people will be on the call, and why?
Five — she invited one person from each area.
2. What does she want covered first?
Payroll, because that is where they have had problems.
3. What does she ask you to send before the call?
A short agenda, so nobody talks over anybody.
Listening 2

The two-minute briefing

1. How did this prospect find the firm?
Through the website, and a referral from a current client.
2. What does the colleague warn about?
That more people may join than are on the invite.
3. What does he suggest doing in the first minute?
Asking each person to say their name and their area.
