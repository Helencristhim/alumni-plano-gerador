Learn English Tenses: FUTURE SIMPLE with “will” (engVid, with Adam, free)

A whole class about will only — the same piece you learned today, in another voice and with other examples. He writes on the board and covers exactly the points that slip most: the form (will + bare verb), the contraction 'll, the negative won't and the short answers. Hearing the same rule twice, from different people, is what makes it automatic.

Tip: watch it with English subtitles (the video's own CC). Do one thing only: every time he writes a sentence with will, pause and say ONE sentence of your own in the same mould, about your work (“I will send the report today”, “I will confirm the hotel tomorrow”). By the end of the video you should have said about five. Open channel, no sign-up.

Short video / audio
Future tenses: present continuous, ‘be going to’, ‘will’ (BBC Learning English, free)

This is the answer to the question that probably stayed in your head after the lesson: when is it will and when is it going to? It is a few minutes, with the teacher explaining the difference through everyday examples — a plan already decided on one side, a decision made on the spot on the other. British English, which is good on purpose: you hear American all week on the Miami calls.

Tip: listen twice. The first time, just listen. The second time, pause at each example and decide out loud “plan” or “on-the-spot decision” BEFORE she answers. Then invent one example of your own for each type, with the November trip (“I'm going to travel in November” × “I'll call you when I land”). Open and free, no sign-up.

Video story
Let's Learn English — Level 2, Lesson 30: Dream a Little Dream (VOA Learning English, free)

It is not a grammar class: it is a story acted out, with subtitles on screen and American English spoken slowly on purpose. Anna asks her colleagues what they are going to do with their lives, and everybody answers by talking about the future — dreams, plans and promises, exactly the three things you learned today. It is a chance to hear will loose in an office conversation, and not in a table.

Tip: watch it once only and do one thing: write down every sentence that starts with I'll or I will. Then close the video and answer the same question about yourself, out loud: what are you going to do this year at Zurich? You do not have to understand every word; the goal is to get your mouth producing will without freezing. VOA is an American public service, free and with no sign-up.
