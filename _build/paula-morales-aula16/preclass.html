Lesson 16 -- Pre-class

What Will Happen? -- Will for Promises, Predictions and Decisions on the Spot

In lessons 14 and 15 you told what had already happened. Today English turns the other way: what is still going to happen. In lesson 13 you learned the going to of plans already decided; the other half is missing, and it is the most useful of all: will. It does three jobs — promising (I will send it today), predicting (the meeting will probably be long) and deciding as you speak (don't worry, I'll check now). And it is that third one that solves your biggest fear: when somebody asks what you do not know, you do not have to apologize — you promise. The setting is next year at Zurich and the annual meeting in Miami, in November. Six new words: promise, probably, maybe, confirm, delay, worry. This Pre-class is a first contact — come to class with the six in your ear.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Six words that only exist when we talk about what has not happened yet. Listen to each one and repeat it out loud. The sound traps of the day: PRO-mis · PRO-ba-bli · kon-FERM · di-LEI.

Promise -- to say you will certainly do it
"I promise I will send it today." Say PRO-mis — stress on the first syllable and a dry mis at the end, never “pro-MAIZ”.
Probably -- almost certainly, but not one hundred per cent
"The meeting will probably be long." Say PRO-ba-bli — an American speaking fast says almost “PRO-bli”.
Maybe -- possibly yes, possibly no
"Maybe the flight will be late." Say MEI-bi — and notice: maybe is less certain than probably.
Confirm -- to say again that it is certain
"I will confirm the hotel tomorrow." Say kon-FERM — stress on the second syllable, never on the first.
Delay -- extra time before something happens
"There is a delay at the airport." Say di-LEI — stress on the second syllable, rhyming with day.
Worry -- to think that something bad can happen
"Don't worry — I'll check it now." Say WUR-ri — the o sounds like er, never “wo-ri”.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the story and answer the questions. The sentences in bold are today's future — notice that after will the verb is always in its simple form, with no to and no -s.

Sofia sent the team a note with the whole year in it. It starts like this: in March the new system arrives, and nobody will learn it alone — everyone will learn it together. She will book the training days herself. And she warns nobody to panic: it will probably take two months.

In June the new large-risk clients come in. Paula takes over the international accounts, and from then on there is a call with Miami every week: she will speak with Miami every week. The first ones may be hard — and the note says so plainly, with no decoration: maybe the first calls will be difficult.

And there is November. The annual meeting is in Miami, three days, and Paula is going. It is her first international trip for the company. Sofia closes the paragraph with a promise: “you will not be a voice on a call. You will be in the room.” At the end, the practical part: I will confirm the dates in September, and the tickets are booked in October.

1. In “she will speak with Miami”, why is it not “she will speaks”?
A Because speak never takes an -s.
B Because after will the verb stays bare — the third-person -s goes to sleep.
C Because the sentence is a question.
2. What is going to happen in March?
A The new system arrives, and the whole team will learn it together.
B The new large-risk clients come in.
C The annual meeting in Miami takes place.
3. What promise does Sofia make to Paula about November?
A That her English will be perfect by then.
B That she will not be a voice on a call — she will be in the room.
C That she will not have to say anything at the meeting.
4. In “everyone will learn” and “I will confirm”, what changes with the person (I, she, everyone)?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- one word, three uses

GRAMMAR

will is the easiest verb in English: one form, the same for everybody, and the verb after it stays bare. What changes is only the situation you use it in — and there are three.

UsoIn EnglishWhen you use it
PromessaI will send it today.a promise — you give your word
PrevisãoIt will probably be long.a prediction — you think it will be like that
Decisão na horaDon't worry — I'll check it now.a decision made while you are speaking
NegativeI won't forget.the negative — will plus not, in one word
QuestionWill you come? · When will you arrive?the question — will jumps to the front

The three mistakes that feel natural to make, and the fix for each one:

1) “I will to call you tomorrow.” — you glued on a to that does not exist. After will the verb comes bare: “I will call you tomorrow.”

2) “She will confirms the meeting.” — you added the third-person -s, the one from lesson 4. After will it goes to sleep: “She will confirm the meeting.”

3) “I don't will travel in November.” — you called don't in to help. will makes its own negative: “I won't travel in November.”

And where does the going to from lesson 13 fit? One test only: was this already decided before I opened my mouth? If yes, going to (“I'm going to travel in November” — the ticket already exists). If you are deciding now, promising or guessing, it is will (“I'll check”, “it will probably be long”). In everyday English the two mix a lot — nobody will correct you for it, so do not freeze choosing.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record your answer. Speak for 60 to 90 seconds, with no script. Do not redo it more than twice. This is exactly the homework audio — start building it here.

Sofia asks you two questions, one after the other: “What will happen this year, and what will happen in November?” and “What will you do when somebody asks you something you don't know?” Answer out loud. Use at least three promises with will, one prediction with probably or maybe and one negative sentence with won't. The only rule of this recording is: never apologize — promise. It does not have to be perfect — it has to be yours.
