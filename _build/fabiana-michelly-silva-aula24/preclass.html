Lesson 24 -- Pre-class

Managing Global Relationships -- The Unwritten Rules

Working with New York, Monterrey and Penang across time zones and cultures. Key words: norm, deference, come across as, misread, put someone on the spot, tone-deaf, overlap, asynchronous, handoff, follow the sun, touch base, face time. Structure: be expected to / be supposed to / be meant to -- describing norms and expectations without sounding like a judgment.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Norm -- what most people in a group usually do and expect others to do
"In Penang, a slow reply is the norm, not a sign of trouble."
Deference -- polite respect shown to someone older or more senior
"Greeting the director first is a sign of deference."
Come across as -- to seem to other people in a particular way
"A one-line email can come across as rude in Monterrey."
Misread -- to understand a situation or a signal wrongly
"We misread his silence as agreement."
Put someone on the spot -- to ask someone a hard question in public, without warning
"Don't put Hafiz on the spot in front of his director."
Tone-deaf -- not noticing how your words or timing will make other people feel
"A cost report on their national holiday would be tone-deaf."
Overlap -- the hours when two time zones are both at work
"We only have one hour of overlap with Penang."
Asynchronous -- not happening at the same time; people reply when they can
"Most updates can be asynchronous; save the call for decisions."
Handoff -- the moment one team passes its work to the next team
"The handoff note reaches Penang before their day starts."
Follow the sun -- a way of working where tasks pass between time zones, so the work never stops
"With three sites, we can follow the sun."
Touch base -- to have a short conversation to check how things are
"Let's touch base on Thursday before the forecast goes out."
Face time -- time spent talking with someone directly, on video or in person
"Lucia values face time more than long emails."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The board has approved a second supplier, and now Fabiana runs one team across three sites: New York, Monterrey and Penang. Before her first call with Penang, Marcus explains the unwritten rules. "There, new partners are expected to greet the director first. That kind of deference is the norm. And you aren't supposed to put Hafiz on the spot in front of his boss. Touch base with him afterward."

Three structures do the work. Be expected to is a norm that other people count on. Be supposed to is what the rule or the plan says should happen, and in the past it often means it did not happen: the handoff note was supposed to reach Penang by six, but it arrived at nine. Be meant to is the purpose of something: in Monterrey, the Monday call is meant to build trust, not just report numbers. None of them is a personal opinion, so none of them comes across as a judgment.

The clock matters too. There is only one hour of overlap with Penang, so most updates are asynchronous, and the daily handoff lets the three sites follow the sun. Lucia in Monterrey prefers face time to long emails, and a request on Mexico's Independence Day would be tone-deaf. Fabiana's first lesson: never misread a silence as agreement.

1. Why does Marcus say new partners "are expected to greet" the director first?
A Because it is Marcus's personal opinion.
B Because it is a norm that people in Penang count on.
C Because it happened once, in the past.
2. "The handoff note was supposed to reach Penang by six." What does this tell you?
A It was the plan, but the note did not arrive on time.
B The note arrived early.
C Nobody knows when the note arrives.
3. Why are most updates asynchronous?
A Because Lucia does not like calls.
B Because New York asked for it.
C Because there is only one hour of overlap with Penang.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Norms and Expectations

GRAMMAR

These structures describe the rules of the place, not your opinion, so they never sound like a judgment.

StructureWhat it saysExample
be expected toa norm or a duty that other people count onNew partners are expected to greet the director first.
be supposed towhat the rule or the plan says should happenThe note is supposed to reach Penang by six.
be meant tothe purpose or the intention of somethingThe Monday call is meant to build trust.
Negativenot supposed to = not acceptable · not expected to = not necessaryYou aren't supposed to disagree in front of the director.
Pastwas / were supposed to = the plan, but it did not happenThe note was supposed to arrive at six. It came at nine.
QuestionAm I supposed to...? · What are we expected to...?Who am I expected to address first?

Never: You are suppose to · They are expected greet · The note supposed to reach Penang (no be) · It is meant to building trust

Stage 2: Put the Team Guide in Order

Order

Fabiana writes a short guide for her team about the three sites. Put her guide in order.

?"Our team works across three sites with three very different norms."
?"First, in New York, you are expected to raise problems in the meeting itself."
?"Second, in Penang, you are not supposed to put someone on the spot in front of their boss."
?"Third, in Monterrey, the Monday call is meant to build trust before business."
?"Because we only have one hour of overlap with Penang, most updates should be asynchronous."
?"Finally, the handoff note is supposed to reach Penang by six p.m. Monterrey time, every day."
