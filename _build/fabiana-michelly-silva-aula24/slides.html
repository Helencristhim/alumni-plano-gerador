Chapter 3: The Unwritten Rules

What Are You Supposed to Do?

Three structures for rules nobody writes down

Grammar Discovery

Whose Rule Is It?

"In Penang, new partners are expected to greet the director first."

"The handoff note is supposed to reach Penang by six p.m."

"The Monday call is meant to build trust, not just report numbers."

"You aren't supposed to put someone on the spot in front of their boss."

What do the purple words have in common? Is each sentence about what Fabiana wants, or about what other people expect?

StructureWhat it saysExample
be expected toa norm or a duty that other people count onNew partners are expected to greet the director first.
be supposed towhat the rule or the plan says should happen (it often doesn't)The note is supposed to reach Penang by six.
be meant tothe purpose or the intention of somethingThe Monday call is meant to build trust.
not supposed tonot acceptable, not allowedYou aren't supposed to put him on the spot.
was / were supposed toexpected in the past, but it did not happenThe note was supposed to arrive at six. It came at nine.
The trapalways be + participle + to + base verbNot I suppose to, not expected greet, not supposed to sending.

In one line: these structures describe the rules of the place, not your personal opinion, so they never sound like a judgment.

Chapter 4: The First Calls

Before You Dial In

One briefing, two messages, one working agreement

Dialogue

The Briefing Before Penang

M
Before your first call with Penang, a heads-up: Hafiz's director is going to join.
F
So who am I expected to address first, Hafiz or the director?
M
The director. That kind of deference is the norm there, even if Hafiz does all the talking.
F
Got it. And if the October numbers look bad, can I ask Hafiz about them directly?
M
Not on the call. You're not supposed to put someone on the spot in front of their boss. Touch base with him afterward.
F
That's the opposite of New York. There, you're expected to raise it in the meeting.
M
Exactly. And Monterrey is different again. Lucia wants face time before business.
F
So the Monday call is meant to build trust, and the numbers can go in the asynchronous update.
Comprehension

About Marcus

1. Who does Marcus tell Fabiana to address first on the call?
The director, even if Hafiz does all the talking.
2. What does Marcus say she should do about the October numbers?
Not ask Hafiz on the call, but touch base with him afterward, because she isn't supposed to put him on the spot in front of his boss.
3. What does Marcus say about Lucia in Monterrey?
That Monterrey is different again: Lucia wants face time before business.
Listening 1 · A Message from Penang

What the Silence Meant

Hafiz calls after the first meeting. Sound first — no text.

1. Why was Hafiz's director pleased?
Because Fabiana greeted him first, which is what a new partner is expected to do in Penang.
2. What did Hafiz's silence mean, and why was he silent?
It did not mean that he agreed. In his team, people are not supposed to disagree with a figure in front of the director.
3. What does Hafiz propose, and when?
A one-to-one call on Thursday, in the overlap window from seven to eight in the morning, São Paulo time. Until then, he will send his comments in writing.
Listening 2 · Welcome from Monterrey

How We Work Here

Lucia leaves a welcome message. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the Monday call meant to be, and what happens in the first five minutes?
A real conversation. They talk about how everyone is doing, which is how they build trust.
2. What does Lucia ask Fabiana not to do, and why?
Not to send requests on September sixteenth, Mexico's Independence Day. A cost report that day would feel tone-deaf.
3. What problem does Lucia want to fix with Fabiana?
The daily handoff note is supposed to reach Penang by six p.m., but last week it was late twice.
