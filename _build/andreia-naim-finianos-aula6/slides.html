Chapter 4: How Sure Are You?

The Word That Sets the Temperature

Same claim, four different pressures

Grammar Discovery

One Word Changes

"This will save your team about two weeks a quarter."

"This might reduce your exposure, depending on the volume."

"You may want to look at the liability clause before Thursday."

"You must consider the audit risk before you sign anything."

The verb after the coloured word never changes: save, reduce, look, consider. What changes is how sure you are — and how much room you leave the other person to disagree with you.

TemperatureWordExample
A promise you can be held towillThis will save your team two weeks.
Possible, and you are not suremight / may / couldThis might reduce your exposure.
Advice, with room to refuseyou may want to / you might want toYou may want to look at that clause.
Your recommendation, ownedI would suggest / I would ratherI would suggest we meet halfway on the date.
An offer with a conditionwe could ..., but ...We could move the date, but the scope would change.
Very strong — use with caremustIn English must sounds like an order, not like deve.
The form, alwaysmodal + plain verb, no to, no snot she must to sign, not it mights work

In one line: use the weakest word that still says the thing. The weaker word survives being wrong, and it usually gets the stronger result.

Three Moves

What to Do With “It Is Too Expensive”

1 · ACKNOWLEDGE — in their words, without softening it

"So the number is higher than what you had budgeted."

2 · REFRAME — name the concern underneath, as a question

"It sounds like the issue may not be the number itself, but having to defend it to somebody who was not in this meeting. Is that fair?"

3 · REDIRECT — to something that can be tested

"If that is it, I would suggest we start with one company. You would have a real number to take to your board in six weeks, and we could talk about the rest after that."

Now take the objection you hear most often and build the three moves out loud. The second one is the one everybody skips.

Listening 1 · A Sales Director

I Used to Have Three Answers Ready

Somebody who stopped answering objections and started naming them. Sound first — no text.

1. What did he used to do when somebody said the price was too high?
He treated it like an exam question: he had three answers ready and delivered all three, and the deal died anyway.
2. What are the two things he does now, in order?
He repeats what they said in their words, and then he says what he thinks is actually going on — as a question, not as a statement.
3. What happens the half of the time when he is wrong, and why does he say that is useful?
They correct him, and he says that is just as useful, because now he knows what the real issue is.
Listening 2 · A Country Manager

The Word I Had to Unlearn

Somebody who was being polite in her own head and rude in English. Sound first — no text.

1. Which word did she have to unlearn, and why did she not notice the problem?
Must. In her own language it is soft — you say it to a colleague and it sounds like a suggestion — so in her head she had said something perfectly friendly.
2. What did her counterpart tell her, and how did he put it?
That when she says must, it sounds like her boss talking. He suggested she try might instead.
3. What does she say happens now, and how long did it take her to believe it?
People actually do the thing she suggests. The weaker word gets the stronger result, and it took her about four years to believe that.

The Dial

One Sentence, Four Temperatures

"You must move the go-live date."an order
"You should move the go-live date."strong advice
"You may want to move the go-live date."room to refuse
"I would suggest moving the go-live date, and I can tell you why."yours, and open

Read all four out loud. Then say which one you would use with a sceptical buyer, and which one you would use with a partner you have known for two years.

Disagreeing About Nothing Important

Scenario: Second round of a negotiation. The other side has pushed back on the price, the timeline and the scope, all three at once. Decide out loud which one you will hold, which one you will give ground on, and which one you will work around — and say all three to them in a way that keeps the relationship for the next two years.
