Lesson 6 -- Handling Objections

Talk
William Ury -- "The walk from no to yes" (TED, 18 min)

The man who wrote the book on negotiation, arguing that the way out of a deadlock is almost never a better argument. His move is the same one you practised today: step out of the two positions and name what is actually underneath them. Slow, deliberate American English, with a story running through the whole talk.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Write down the one question he says changes a negotiation, and try it on the next objection you hear at work.

Podcast
NPR Life Kit -- "How to ask for a raise, and what to do when the answer is no"

The table is a salary rather than a licence, but the moves are identical: what to say when somebody says no, how to find out whether the no is about the money or about something else, and how to keep the relationship after it. Listen for how often the guest uses might and would instead of will and must.

Tip: read the transcript on the page and underline every modal verb in the guest’s answers. Count how many are will, and how many are softer than that.

Series
Formula 1: Drive to Survive -- a negotiation episode (Netflix)

You met this one in lesson 3 for the story shape. Watch it again for something else entirely: the contract conversations. Team principals disagreeing with each other on camera, in English, with everything at stake and nobody raising their voice. It is the temperature dial of today, used by people who cannot afford to get it wrong.

Tip: find one scene where somebody disagrees. Write down the exact words they use to say no, and notice how few of them are the word no.
