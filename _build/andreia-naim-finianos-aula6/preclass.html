Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

Handling Objections -- Disagreeing Without Breaking Anything

Why the first objection is almost never the real one, and how one word changes the temperature of a claim. Key words: an objection, pushback, a concession, leverage, a counterpart, scope, liability, a timeline, to back something up, to work around, to meet halfway, to hold your ground. Structure: modal verbs of certainty and diplomatic softening.

Stage 1: Words · 2 min

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An objection -- a reason the other side gives for not agreeing yet
"The first objection is almost never the real one."
Pushback -- resistance from the other side, said out loud rather than hidden
"There was real pushback on the timeline, and I was glad to hear it."
A concession -- something you give up so that the other side can say yes
"I would rather make one concession on the date than touch the price."
Leverage -- something you have that the other side wants, which gives you room to negotiate
"The date is the only leverage we have left, so I am not giving it away first."
A counterpart -- the person on the other side who has the same job as you
"My counterpart there cannot defend the number to his own board."
Scope -- exactly what is included in the work, and what is not
"We could move the date, but the scope would have to change with it."
Liability -- legal responsibility if something goes wrong and somebody loses money
"You may want to look at the liability clause before we talk about price."
A timeline -- the plan of when each part of the work happens, from start to finish
"There was real pushback on the timeline, not on the product."
To back something up -- to support what you said with a number, a document or an example
"Can you back that up with a number? Then I will take it to my side."
To work around -- to find a way past an obstacle instead of removing it
"We cannot change their approval process, but we can work around it."
To meet halfway -- to agree on something between the two positions, so both sides move
"I would suggest we meet halfway on the timeline and hold the price."
To hold your ground -- to keep your position when the other side pushes, without being rude
"The ones who hold their ground on everything win the meeting and lose the account."

Stage 3: Grammar Tip -- Modal Verbs and Temperature · 2 min

GRAMMAR

Read the rule, then answer three quick questions.

TemperatureWordExample
A promise you can be held towillThis will save your team two weeks.
Possible, and you are not suremight / may / couldThis might reduce your exposure.
Advice, with room to refuseyou may want to / you might want toYou may want to look at that clause.
Your recommendation, ownedI would suggest / I would ratherI would suggest we meet halfway on the date.
An offer with a conditionwe could ..., but ...We could move the date, but the scope would change.
Very strong — use with caremustIn English must sounds like an order to a client.
The form, alwaysmodal + plain verb, no to, no snot she must to sign, not it mights work

Never: She must to sign it · It mights work · You must consider our proposal (to a client) · This will solve all your problems (when you have not seen the data)

1. "This will fix it" and "this might fix it". What is the difference?
A The same claim at two temperatures: the first is a promise you can be held to, the second survives being wrong.
B The second one is a grammar mistake.
C The second one is about the past.
2. "You may want to look at the audit risk" and "You must consider the audit risk". What is the difference?
A Must is not correct English.
B Both ask for the same thing, but only one leaves the other person room to say no without losing face.
C The first one is about the past.
3. You meet halfway on price, timeline and scope in the first round. What happens in the second round?
A You win the account and keep the relationship.
B The other side gives you a concession back.
C You have nothing left to negotiate with.

Stage 5: Say It · 2 min

Speaking

Listen and record two sentences, then speak freely for thirty seconds.

I hear you on the price, and I think the real question is the timeline.

The other side has pushed back on the price, the timeline and the scope. In thirty seconds: say which one you will hold, where you would meet halfway, and ask them to back up their number. Use might or may at least once.
