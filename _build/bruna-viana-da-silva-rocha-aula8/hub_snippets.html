Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

Behind the Scenes -- How the Thing Actually Works

Airports, hotels and the systems that move you through them. Key words: a procedure, a policy, automated, a security checkpoint, a photo ID, to be authorized, a luggage tag, housekeeping, a wake-up call, to be turned away. Structure: the passive voice — a form of be plus the third form, for when the action matters more than who did it.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A procedure -- the fixed set of steps a place follows every single time
"There is a procedure for this, and nobody at the desk can change it."
A policy -- a rule a place sets for itself and applies to everybody
"It is not personal. It is the policy, and she is not allowed to break it."
Automated -- done by a machine, with nobody deciding each time
"Check-in is fully automated now, which is faster until something goes wrong."
A security checkpoint -- the point where you and your bag are checked before you can go further
"Everything is scanned at the security checkpoint, including the coffee you just bought."
A photo ID -- any official document with your picture on it
"You will be asked for a photo ID at least four times before you board."
To be authorized -- to have official permission for something
"Only two people in the building are authorized to open that door."
A luggage tag -- the small label with the code that tells the system where your bag goes
"The luggage tag is the only thing standing between your bag and another country."
Housekeeping -- the hotel team that cleans and prepares the rooms
"The room had not been cleaned yet, so housekeeping was sent up while we waited."
A wake-up call -- a phone call the hotel makes to wake you at a time you asked for
"I booked a wake-up call for five and I was awake at four anyway."
To be turned away -- to be refused entry or service, and sent back
"Three people were turned away at the gate because they arrived after it closed."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Somebody explains how an airport works and you will hear a sentence with nobody in it. "Your bag is scanned at the security checkpoint." Who scans it? A machine, a person, a system — and none of that matters to you, which is exactly why English puts the bag first and drops the rest.

The shape is small: a form of be plus the third form of the verb. Present: "Your bag is scanned." "Operations are handled remotely." Past: "My team was told last week." "The rooms were cleaned at noon." Negative: "The room was not cleaned before we arrived."

Use it when the action matters more than who did it, or when the person is obvious or unknown. "The check-in counter is managed by airline staff" — if you do want to name them, by puts them back in. Most of the time nobody bothers, and that is the point.

Your ears will do the real work here. The third form of a regular verb ends in three different sounds and nobody slows down for you: /t/ in scanned... no — in checked and processed, /d/ in managed and authorized, and a whole extra syllable, /id/, in automated and deleted. You do not choose. Your mouth does, and this is the week you teach it.

1. Why does "Your bag is scanned at the security checkpoint" not say who scans it?
A Because the action matters more than who does it, and the doer is obvious or unknown.
B Because it is rude to name airport staff.
C Because the sentence is in the future.
2. What is the shape of the passive?
A have + the third form
B a form of be + the third form
C do + the bare verb
3. How many different sounds does the -ed ending of a regular verb make?
A One, always the same.
B Two: /t/ and /d/.
C Three: /t/, /d/ and /id/ — and the last one adds a syllable.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Sentence With Nobody In It

GRAMMAR

A form of be plus the third form. And three different sounds for the same -ed ending, which your mouth has to learn and your ear has to catch.

JobFormExample
The action matters, not the doeris / are + third formYour bag is scanned at the checkpoint.

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

Eleven at night, the room is still not ready, and you are at the front desk. Put your side of the conversation in a logical order.

?"Hi -- I checked in two hours ago and the room still is not ready."
?"Rocha. R-O-C-H-A. I was told at noon that it would be thirty minutes."
?"Has housekeeping been up there at all, or was the room never on the list?"
?"Okay. And is there a policy about this, or does it depend on the night manager?"
?"No, I do not need a voucher. I need a time, and I need it to be true."
?"Fine. I will be in the lobby, and you have my number if anything changes."
