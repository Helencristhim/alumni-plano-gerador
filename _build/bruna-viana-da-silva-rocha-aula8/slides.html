The Six Steps

Thirty-Five Minutes, Six Steps

Narrate all six, in order, with nobody in any of the sentences.

ONE BAG, ONE CONNECTION
you get 70 minutes · the bag gets 35 · the tag decides everything
1. tag printed and attached at the counter2 min
2. screened, below the floor, by machine4 min
3. sorted by the code on the tag3 min
4. loaded onto a cart and driven across the field14 min
5. scanned again at the aircraft2 min
6. loaded into the hold, by hand10 min
If the tag cannot be read at step three, the bag is pulled out of the line and handled by a person — and a person is slower than the machine, every time. Old tags are read first. Take them off.

The Core · Round 1

Seventy-One, and Flying for the First Time

Ruth is seventy-one, her daughter booked her a flight, and she has never been inside an airport. She is not nervous about the plane. She is nervous about not knowing what she is supposed to do. You are yourself.

  • Block 1: the front door to the counter — what is shown, what is printed, what is taken.

Closing

One Minute, No Notes

Somebody who has never left the country asks you what happens at passport control. You have one minute, and you are not allowed to name a single person. Go.

I can describe a process step by step without putting a person in every sentence.
I can say what was done and what was not done, in the past, without blaming anybody.
I let the -ed land as /t/, /d/ or a whole extra syllable, and I keep going.
