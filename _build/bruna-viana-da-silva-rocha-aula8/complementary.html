Series
Explained — Netflix, any episode, 16 to 20 minutes

Vox takes one thing the world runs on and pulls it apart in under twenty minutes: how it is made, who it is controlled by, what happens when it breaks. American narration at normal speed, and almost every sentence in it is built the way this lesson is built.

Tip: pick any episode and write down eight sentences with no person in them — is made, was built, are handled. Then use three of them about something in your own week.

NPR — "Inside the baggage-handling operation at the world's busiest airport"

What actually happens to your suitcase between the counter in Atlanta and the belt in another country: the tag, the sorting, the tug drivers, and the twenty percent of bags that used to miss the connection. Reported at normal American speed, transcript on the same page.

Tip: listen once with the transcript closed. Then find five sentences where the bag is the subject and nobody is named, and read them out loud without stopping on the -ed.

Rachel's English — "How to Pronounce -ed Verb Endings"

The same three letters, three different sounds, and no rule you get to think about in real time. She sorts them by what comes before, which is the only thing that decides — and it is the difference between checked, managed and automated.

Tip: after the video, say "Your bag is scanned, the room was cleaned and check-in is automated" five times without pausing on any of the three endings.
