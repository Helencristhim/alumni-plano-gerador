Lesson 04 -- Pre-class

The Agenda and the Recap -- What Stays in Writing

Everybody remembers the call differently, and in two weeks nobody remembers it at all. What survives is the email somebody wrote afterwards. Key words: an action item, a recap, a deadline, an owner, to follow up, minutes, a blocker, to circulate, a takeaway, to touch base. Structure: modals of obligation and recommendation.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An action item -- one task that somebody agreed to do, with a name on it
"Three action items came out of the call, and two of them are mine."
A recap -- a short summary of what was said and decided
"I will send a recap this afternoon, so nothing gets lost."
A deadline -- the date by which something has to be finished
"The deadline is Friday, and Friday means Friday morning for them."
An owner -- the person who is responsible for one task
"Every line needs an owner, or nobody does it."
To follow up -- to contact somebody again about something you already discussed
"I will follow up on Tuesday if we have not heard back."
Minutes -- the written record of what a meeting decided
"Who is taking the minutes? Whoever writes them decides what happened."
A blocker -- something that stops the work until it is solved
"The only blocker is the security review. Everything else can start."
To circulate -- to send a document to everybody who needs it
"I will circulate the deck before the next call."
A takeaway -- the one thing you want people to remember afterwards
"If there is one takeaway from this call, it is that the integration is three weeks."
To touch base -- to make brief contact to check how things are going
"Let us touch base in two weeks, even if there is nothing new."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A call ends and, for about an hour, everybody remembers roughly the same meeting. After that, everybody remembers a different one. That is why the person who writes the recap is the person who decides what was agreed. It is not a clerical job. It is the last move of the meeting.

A useful recap has three parts. What was decided. What was not decided, which almost everybody leaves out. And the action items, each with an owner and a deadline. A line without a name on it is not a task; it is a wish. And a line without a date can never be late, so it never happens.

The words that carry all of this are small. "The questionnaire must be returned before we start" is a rule, and it comes from outside the two of you. "We need to agree the date by Friday" is not somebody's rule — it is what the situation requires. "You should talk to their technical team first" is a recommendation: skip it and nothing formally breaks. And "you do not have to sign anything yet" means you are free, not that you are forbidden. The forbidden one is "you must not share the document", and it is a completely different sentence.

One more line belongs in every recap, and it is the cheapest one to write: when you will follow up. If the partner cannot commit to a date, write down that the date is open and say when you will touch base. If something is a blocker, name it. Then circulate the whole thing the same afternoon, while it is still the version everybody agrees with.

1. "You do not have to sign anything yet." What is the writer telling you?
A That signing is forbidden at this stage.
B That signing is not required at this stage. You are free to sign or not.
C That the contract does not exist yet.
2. Why does the text say a line without a date never happens?
A Because without a date nothing can ever be late, so nobody is ever behind on it.
B Because people forget tasks after one week.
C Because emails without dates are automatically deleted.
3. The text calls the recap the last move of the meeting. What does it mean?
A That the recap should be written during the call, not after.
B That the last person to speak in the meeting has to write it.
C That writing it is part of the negotiation: the written version becomes the agreed version.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Modals of Obligation and Recommendation

GRAMMAR

One question sorts them all: if nobody does it, what happens?

JobFormExample
A rule, from outsidemust + bare verbThe form must be returned first.
Necessary, from the situationneed to / have to + verbWe need to agree the deadline.
A recommendationshould + bare verbYou should talk to them first.
Not necessary, but alloweddo not have to / do not need toYou do not have to sign yet.
Forbiddenmust notYou must not share the document.
The trapmust not is NOT the negative of mustdo not have to = free · must not = forbidden
No to after a modalmust, should + bare verbNot must to send, not should to talk
Softer, in writingwould need to / it would be good toWe would need to see the numbers first.

Never: We must to send it · He should sends the recap · We not have to pay · You must not sign yet (when you mean: it is optional) · It needs that we agree

Stage 2: Put the Recap in Order

Order

These six lines are a recap email, out of order. Put them the way you would send them.

?"Thank you for your time today. Here is a short recap of what we agreed."
?"Decided: we will run a small pilot with two of your customers."
?"Still open: the start date, because you need to speak to your team first."
?"Action item: I will send the security questionnaire by Wednesday."
?"Action item: Marta will confirm the technical contact by Friday."
?"If I have not heard back, I will follow up next Tuesday."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

Their legal team will not start until the form arrives. You write:
A "The form must to be returned first."
B "The form must be returned before legal can start."
C "You should return the form, it is not important."
Signing now is optional and you want to reassure them. You write:
A "You must not sign anything at this stage."
B "You do not have to sign anything at this stage."
C "You cannot sign at this stage."
The partner will not commit to a date. In the recap you write:
A "The start date is still open. I will follow up next Tuesday."
B "We will look into it and see what happens."
C "Nothing was decided about the date, so I did not write anything."
You want the recommendation to sound like a recommendation, not an order. You write:
A "You must talk to your technical team before the demo."
B "You should to talk to your technical team before the demo."
C "You should talk to your technical team before the demo."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.
