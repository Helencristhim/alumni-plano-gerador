Chapter 4: The Code

Must, Need To, Should

One word decides whether a line is a rule or a suggestion

Grammar Discovery

Which of These Is an Obligation?

"The security questionnaire must be returned before we start."

"We need to agree the deadline by Friday."

"You should talk to their technical team before the demo."

"You do not have to sign anything at this stage."

Ask yourself the same question about each line: if I do nothing, what happens? Two of them have a consequence. Two of them do not.

JobFormExample
A rule, from outsidemust + verbThe form must be returned first.
Necessary, from the situationneed to / have to + verbWe need to agree the deadline.
A recommendationshould + verbYou should talk to them first.
Not necessary (but allowed)do not have to / do not need toYou do not have to sign yet.
Forbiddenmust notYou must not share the document.
The trapmust not is NOT the negative of mustdo not have to = free · must not = forbidden
No to after a modalmust, should + bare verbNot must to send, not should to talk
Softer, in writingwould need to / it would be good toWe would need to see the numbers first.

In one line: must and need to have a consequence. Should does not. And do not have to means free, never forbidden.

Grammar Practice

Rule, or Suggestion?

Say it first, then click to check

"Legal will not start until the form arrives, so it ___must be returned first." (must / be)
"Nothing bad happens if you skip it, but it helps: you ___should call them before the demo." (should)
"It is not required at this stage: you ___do not have to sign anything." (not / have to)
"It is forbidden by their policy: you ___must not share the document outside the team." (not / must)
"The date is not agreed yet, and it cannot wait: we ___need to agree the deadline by Friday." (need to)

Prepared byMarta — Partner Manager
You do not have to prepare anything for this call, and you should not send a deck beforehand: I would rather hear it than read it. If you want to bring somebody technical, please tell me by tomorrow, because I must add them to the invitation myself. We will keep the last three minutes for action items, and I will circulate a recap the same afternoon.
1. The note says you do not have to prepare anything and you should not send a deck. Are those two lines the same kind of instruction?
No. The first says preparation is not required, but you may do it. The second is a recommendation against sending a deck, and she gives her reason: she would rather hear it than read it.
2. Which line in the note is a real obligation, and who is it an obligation for?
I must add them to the invitation myself. The obligation is hers, not yours, and it is why she needs to know by tomorrow.
3. Three of the five agenda points have a number of minutes. What does the last one tell you about how she runs a meeting?
That the last three minutes are reserved for action items and owners, before the meeting ends. Nothing is left to be worked out afterwards.
Chapter 6: Your Turn

Two Recordings, Then You

Listening 1 · The Hour After

Why She Writes It Immediately

An executive on the email she sends within an hour of every call. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say she writes the recap within an hour?
Because after an hour everybody has a different version of the meeting, and the first written version becomes the true one.
2. What are the three things she says every recap has to contain?
What was decided, what was not decided, and the action items with a name and a date on each one.
3. What does she say about writing down what was NOT decided?
That it is the part everybody leaves out and the part that saves the deal, because an open question in writing is a question somebody will answer.
Listening 2 · Why Tasks Die

A Task With No Name on It

A project manager on the action items that never happen. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say a task with no name on it is?
A wish. He says it is not an action item at all until somebody owns it.
2. What is wrong with writing we will look into it, according to him?
Nobody is we. He says the sentence has no owner and no date, so nothing in it can ever be late, and therefore nothing in it ever happens.
3. What does he say to do when somebody refuses to take a deadline?
Write down the date they refused and say you will follow up on it. He says the refusal itself is information, and it belongs in the recap like everything else.
