Understood, Not Perfect -- Your English at Work

Most people on global calls are not native speakers, and the calls work anyway. Key words: a native speaker, an accent, fluent, confident, to improve, a mistake, progress, to get used to. Structure: present perfect for experience.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A native speaker -- a person who learned a language as a child, at home
"Most people on our calls are not native speakers."
An accent -- the way a person from a place says words
"Wei has a Chinese accent, and I understand him better now."
Fluent -- able to speak a language easily and well
"You do not need to be fluent to lead a good meeting."
Confident -- sure that you can do something well
"I feel more confident on Teams calls now."
To improve -- to become better
"My listening has improved a lot."
A mistake -- something that is not correct
"Everybody makes mistakes on calls, even native speakers."
Progress -- the process of getting better
"I can see my progress from lesson 1 to lesson 10."
To get used to -- to become comfortable with something new
"I am getting used to different accents."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Fernanda has worked with global teams for years, but ten weeks ago she was not very confident on calls. Wei’s accent was difficult for her. She often stayed quiet.

Now things are different. She has learned to ask people to repeat. She has given a presentation in English. Her listening has improved, and she is getting used to different accents.

She still makes a mistake sometimes. She has never been a native speaker, and she is not fluent yet. But she can see her progress, and people understand her.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect for Experience

GRAMMAR

Use have or has + participle to talk about experiences in your life, with no specific time. With a specific time (last month, in 2024), use the past simple.

JobFormExample
I / you / we / theyhave + past participleI have given a presentation.
he / she / ithas + past participleWei has worked with twenty countries.
QuestionHave / Has + subject + ever + participle?Have you ever worked in China?
Negativehave never / haven’t + participleI have never spoken perfect English.
Participlesregular = -ed · irregular = new formwork → worked · give → given · speak → spoken · be → been
The trapa specific time = past simpleNot I have been to Florida last month. Say I went to Florida last month.

Never: I have went · Wei have worked · I have been to Florida last month · She has gave
