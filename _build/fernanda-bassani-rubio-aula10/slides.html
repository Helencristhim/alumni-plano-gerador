Chapter 4: The Code

Have You Ever?

The present perfect for experience

Grammar Discovery

Four Sentences About Experience

"I have given a presentation in English."

"Have you ever worked with a team in China?"

"Wei has worked with people from twenty countries."

"I have never spoken perfect English, and that is OK."

These sentences are about experience in your life, not about a specific time. There is no yesterday or last week. The verb has two parts: have or has in red, and a special form in blue: given, worked, spoken.

JobFormExample
I / you / we / theyhave + past participleI have given a presentation.
he / she / ithas + past participleWei has worked with twenty countries.
QuestionHave / Has + subject + ever + participle?Have you ever worked in China?
Negativehave never / haven’t + participleI have never spoken perfect English.
Participlesregular = -ed · irregular = new formwork → worked · give → given · speak → spoken · be → been
The trapa specific time = past simpleNot I have been to Florida last month. Say I went to Florida last month.

In one line: have or has + participle for experience in your life; with a specific time, use the past simple.
