Lesson 2 -- The Mandate

Grammar Video
"How to use the third conditional" -- BBC Learning English, 6 Minute Grammar

Six minutes on the one structure this lesson is built around: if plus had plus past participle, then would have plus past participle. Connection to Lesson 2: this is the grammar behind "if I had asked in month one, I would have got the backing" — the sentence every panel is waiting for and almost nobody prepares.

Tip: pause after each example and rebuild it with your own career. One sentence about the last group, one about the transition. Say them out loud — never write them first.

Talk with transcript
"On being wrong" -- Kathryn Schulz, TED (18 min)

The panel does not ask what you would have done differently in order to find a weakness. It asks because a leader who has never revised a decision is either inexperienced or not being honest. This talk is eighteen minutes on exactly that idea, in very clear American English, and the full transcript runs beside the video.

Tip: listen once without the transcript and write the single sentence you think carries her argument. Then read the transcript and check. The gap between your sentence and hers is the measurement, not the talk itself.

Executive Interview
"Alphabet CFO Ruth Porat" -- The David Rubenstein Show (24 min)

A CFO being interviewed on stage by someone who does not let vague answers pass. Watch how she states the job she was given before she describes anything she achieved in it — the mandate first, the numbers second. That order is the whole lesson today.

Tip: watch the first ten minutes only, then close the page and describe your own last role in the same order: mandate, what you inherited, two numbers. Record it once and time it. Ninety seconds is the target.
