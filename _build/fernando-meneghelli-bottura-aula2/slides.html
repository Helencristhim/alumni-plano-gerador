Chapter 3: The Road Not Taken

What Would You Have Done Differently?

One structure. One answer. Then stop.

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"If I had asked for the headcount in month one, I would have got it."

"If the direction had not changed, I would have stayed another two years."

"We would have scaled back the project if the backing had arrived later."

"If I had inherited a clean system, the first year would have looked very different."

None of these things happened. What are the two verb shapes that tell you that?

PartFormExample
The conditionif + had + past participleIf I had asked earlier...
The resultwould have + past participle...I would have got the headcount.
OrderEither half can come first. With if first, you need a comma: If I had asked, I would have got it. With the result first, no comma: I would have got it if I had asked.
NegativeIf I had not waited, we would not have lost the year.
The short formIn speech both halves contract: If I'd asked, I'd have got it. Both 'd sound identical, and only the verb after them tells you which is which.
The interview moveThree parts, in this order: what I actually did, then the condition, then what the result would have been. "I waited for the full picture. If I had asked in month one, I would have got the headcount." Then stop. One turning point is an answer; three is a confession.

This is not regret. It is the evidence that you reviewed a decision and learned something specific from it.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"If I would have asked earlier, I would have got the headcount."
"If I had asked earlier, I would have got the headcount."
"If I inherited a clean system, the first year would have been easier."
"If I had inherited a clean system, the first year would have been easier."
"With hindsight, I would scaled back the project."
"With hindsight, I would have scaled back the project."

The condition takes had and never would. The result takes would have and never loses the have.

The Move

Three Parts, Then Stop

1. What you did

"I waited until I had the full picture before I asked for more people."

2. The condition

"If I had asked in month one, with the numbers I already had..."

3. The result

"...I would have got the backing while the board was still listening."

"I waited until I had the full picture. If I had asked in month one, I would have got the backing while the board was still listening. That is the one thing I would change."

Chapter 5: The Conversation

The Debrief

What the panel wrote down, read back to you

Dialogue

Claire Reads the Panel Feedback

C
Fernando, I have the note from the panel. The numbers landed. What they wrote is that they never understood what you were actually hired to do.
F
That is fair. I gave them results and I never gave them the job.
C
So give me the job now, in one sentence, the way you would open the second round.
F
My mandate was to bring nine agencies onto one reporting line. I inherited three legacy systems and a headcount built for a group half that size.
C
Better. That is thirty seconds and I already know what good looked like. Now, was treasury in your remit?
F
Not in year one. It came into the remit when we ramped up the shared services model, in the second half of my tenure.
C
Last one, and this is the one they flagged. What would you have done differently?
F
I waited eight months for the full picture before I asked for the backing. If I had asked in month one, I would have got it. With hindsight, that was the turning point, and I missed it.
Comprehension

About the Debrief

1. What does Claire say landed well, and what did the panel not understand?
The numbers landed well, but the panel never understood what he had actually been hired to do.
2. What does Claire ask him to do in one sentence?
To give her the job itself, the way he would open the second round.
3. Which question does Claire say the panel flagged?
What he would have done differently.
Listening

A Voicemail with the Panel Note

Claire is reading you the debrief. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the panel write down that surprised Claire?
That they never quite understood what he had been hired to do — which surprised her, because he told her in five seconds.
2. What exact order does she want for the second round?
The mandate in one sentence, then the state of the finance function he got, and the numbers last.
3. What was wrong with his answer about what he would have done differently?
It was very careful and sounded like he was protecting somebody. She wants a real one, because nobody trusts a CFO who has never changed his mind.
Listening 2

A CFO Answers the Hard Question

Forty seconds, one turning point, no excuse. Sound first — no text.

1. What was his remit, and what headcount did he have for it?
His remit was to bring the reporting line under control, and his headcount was about a third of what the job needed.
2. Why did he wait before asking for backing, and what did waiting cost him?
He waited until he had the full picture, because that is how he is built. By the time he asked, the year was gone and the board had stopped listening.
3. What is the lesson he says he has applied since?
That you buy credibility more cheaply by being early and wrong than by being late and right.
