Plans for Next Quarter -- Plans and Promises

The director wants to know your plans for the next three months. Key words: a goal, a budget, a trade show, to expand, a new market, to hire, a training session, to look forward to. Structure: going to for plans, will for decisions and promises.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A goal -- something you want to get or do in the future
"Our goal for next quarter is to reach the target."
A budget -- the money you can spend on something
"The budget for the trade show is small this year."
A trade show -- a big event where companies show their products
"We are going to have a stand at the trade show."
To expand -- to grow into new places or areas
"We want to expand in Argentina and Chile."
A new market -- a country or group of customers you do not sell to yet
"Mexico is a new market for our team."
To hire -- to give someone a job
"We are not going to hire anyone this year."
A training session -- a short class to learn a new skill at work
"The team has a training session on the new product."
To look forward to -- to feel happy about something in the future
"I’m looking forward to the trade show."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Fernanda has a call with Mark about next quarter. Her main goal is clear: the team is going to expand in Argentina, a new market for them. They are going to have a small stand at the trade show in November.

The budget is small, so Fernanda is not going to hire anyone. The team is going to do a training session on the new adhesive.

At the end of the call, Mark asks for the plan in writing. Fernanda decides immediately: “Sure, I’ll send it tomorrow.” Mark says he is looking forward to reading it.

1. What is Fernanda’s main goal for next quarter?
A To hire new people.
B To expand in Argentina.
C To go to a trade show in Germany.
2. Why is she not going to hire anyone?
A Because the budget is small.
B Because the team is too big.
C Because Mark said no.
3. Mark asks for the plan and she decides at that moment. What does she say?
A “I’m going to send it tomorrow.”
B “Sure, I’ll send it tomorrow.”
C “I send it tomorrow.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Going To and Will

GRAMMAR

Use going to for plans you decided before. Use will for decisions you make now, offers and promises.

JobFormExample
A plan, decided befoream / is / are + going to + verbWe are going to visit three car makers.
Negative planam not / isn’t / aren’t + going to + verbI’m not going to hire anyone.
A decision made now, an offerwill (’ll) + verbSure, I’ll send it tomorrow.
A promisewill + verbDon’t worry, I will call the customer.
QuestionAre you going to...? / Will you...?Are you going to go to the trade show?
The trapno to after willNot I will to send it. Say I will send it.

Never: We going to expand · I will to send it · Are you going go? · (plan made last month) We will have a stand
