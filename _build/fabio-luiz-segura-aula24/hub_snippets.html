Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A bilateral agreement -- an official agreement between two sides, for example two banks or two countries
"The two banks signed a bilateral agreement on cross-border payments."
A joint initiative -- a project that two or more organizations plan and run together
"It is a joint initiative, so both teams share the work and the results."
A memorandum of understanding -- a written document that describes what two sides plan to do together, before a full contract
"We want to sign a memorandum of understanding before the end of the year."
Co-investment -- money that two or more partners put into the same project together
"The pilot needs co-investment from both banks, about half each."
A governance structure -- the rules that decide who makes decisions in a project, and how
"Before we start, we need a clear governance structure: who decides, and who signs."
A working group -- a small team of people from different areas who meet regularly to solve one problem
"The working group meets every month to track the progress of the project."
A strategic roadmap -- a plan that shows the main steps and dates of a long project
"The strategic roadmap shows three phases over the next eighteen months."
To iron out -- to solve the last small problems or disagreements in a plan
"There are still a few commercial points we need to iron out."
To build consensus -- to help different people slowly reach an agreement they all accept
"It takes time to build consensus inside a big bank."
To protect both sides -- to make sure that an agreement is fair and safe for each partner
"A good MOU should protect both sides if the project changes."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

When two banks of the same size meet to discuss a partnership, nobody signs a contract on the first day. The goal of a first bilateral meeting is smaller and more important: to agree on the problem, to build trust, and to leave with a plan. Often that plan is a memorandum of understanding and a working group.

A good partnership is a list of milestones, and English has a perfect tense for them: the future perfect. By March, the working group will have mapped the main corridors. By the end of the second quarter, we will have signed the MOU. The action is finished before a moment in the future.

The form is will have + past participle. The future moment usually comes with by: by June, by the end of the year. With by the time, the verb after it stays in the present simple: By the time we meet in Frankfurt, our teams will have tested the first messages. For a question, say: Will your legal team have finished the review by June?

Milestones help you build consensus, because each side can see what will be done, by whom, and when. And they help to protect both sides: if a milestone is late, everybody knows it early.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Future Perfect for Milestones

GRAMMAR

An action that will be finished before a moment in the future.

UseFormExample
Finished before a future momentwill have + past participleBy March, we will have signed the MOU.
The future momentby + date · by the time + present simpleBy the time we meet, we will have tested it.
Negativewon't have + past participleWe won't have finished the tests by June.
QuestionWill + subject + have + past participleWill you have finished the review by June?
Comparefuture simple = the action happens thenWe will sign in March. · We will have signed by March.

Never: by the time we will meet · will have sign · will signed · Will your team finished...?

Stage 2: Build the Bilateral

Order

You are chairing a meeting with a partner bank. Put the five phases of the meeting in a logical order, from the introductions to the close.

?"From our side, the priority is clear. What I want to understand is where you see the value on your end."
?"What I'd propose is a joint initiative, with a working group that meets monthly."
?"I understand the timeline concern. Let me suggest a way to frame this."
?"So, to confirm: by the end of the quarter, we will have signed a draft MOU."
?"Thank you for coming to Frankfurt. Let me share where we are as an institution."
?"I think we both want faster and cheaper payments between Brazil and Germany."
