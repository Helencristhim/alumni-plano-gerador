Lesson 7 -- How Often Do You...? My Professional Habits

Series (mockumentary)
Working: What We Do All Day · Season 1, Episode 2 — “The Middle” · Netflix — included in the subscription

The whole series answers exactly the question of our lesson: what do you usually do at work? Barack Obama walks into the homes and offices of real Americans and asks what their day is like — and people answer by talking about routine: what they always do, what they never do, what time they start. Episode 2, “The Middle”, is the office one — sales people, with targets, email and a boss. It is Paula's world, in American English, told by the people who do the work.

Tip: watch it with English subtitles — the goal here is not to understand everything. Do ONE thing only: every time someone says always, usually, often, sometimes or never, hit pause and notice where the word fell — before the verb, or after is? Then say your own version out loud: “I always check my email at eight.” You do not have to finish the episode — 20 minutes is enough for the task.

Speak English Podcast — #056 “Expressing the Frequency in English” (free, with transcript)

This episode is our lesson, in audio. Georgiana does literally what we did on the scale slide: “I'll go from words with 100% frequency to words with 0% frequency” — and she opens by explaining the question of the lesson: “When you want to know how often something occurs, you ask: How often?” She is the same teacher as the lesson 3 episode, and that is on purpose: you already know her voice and her pace, so your ear is free for the content. The page has the full transcript for free — use it as subtitles.

Tip: listen the first time without stopping and without reading anything, just to let your ear catch the rhythm — you understand more than you think, listening is your strength. The second time, open the transcript and pause at each example to say YOUR version out loud: “I always check my email.” “I never reply at night.” One honest warning: Georgiana is Spanish, so her accent is not the American one you will hear at Zurich — listen for the content, and leave the American accent to the video below. YouTube
Adverbs of Frequency in English — How to Use Always, Usually, Often, Sometimes, Never · English with Abby and Torai (~8 min, free)

Eight minutes, a real teacher in front of a whiteboard, and the rule of the lesson written by hand with the percentages next to it: “I always eat breakfast — 100% of the time”, “I usually walk to work — most of the time”. And she gives the whole rule, exactly as we saw it: with most verbs the adverb comes BEFORE the main verb; with the verb to be, it comes AFTER. If one day you forget which side the word falls on, come back to this video — it settles it in eight minutes.

Tip: watch it once all the way through, without taking notes. The second time, pause at each example and rebuild the sentence with YOUR routine before you carry on: where she says “I usually walk to work”, you say “I usually reply in the morning”. Then close the video and say five sentences about your work, one with each adverb — that is exactly the homework audio. The channel is open, with no sign-up to watch.
