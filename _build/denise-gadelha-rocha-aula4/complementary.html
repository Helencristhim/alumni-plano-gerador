Lesson 4 -- Owning the Numbers

Documentary
Inside Bill's Brain: Decoding Bill Gates -- Part 2

Part two is the one about numbers: a problem is broken down, measured against a benchmark, and reported without drama. Watch how often a bad result is described without anyone being blamed for it — that is exactly the register you built today, in the wild.

Tip: watch with English subtitles and write down three sentences in the passive voice. Then rewrite each one in the active and notice what changes about who is on trial.

Podcast
WorkLife with Adam Grant -- episodes on feedback and difficult conversations

The organisational psychologist on how teams talk about failure without turning it into a trial — the human argument behind the grammar you practised today. Useful for the question the second listening left open: where your own line sits between protecting a team and hiding a problem.

Tip: listen for the moment he describes a mistake without naming anyone. Note the sentence, and note whether it is passive or simply carefully built.

YouTube
Hans Rosling -- The Best Stats You've Ever Seen (TED)

Twenty minutes of a man making numbers unforgettable. Every technique you need for a results presentation is in there: the comparison that makes a figure mean something, the moment he strips out what is not comparable, and the discipline of one message per chart.

Tip: watch once for pleasure, then again with one question: what does he do in the first thirty seconds of each chart, before he says any number at all?
