Same Fact, Two Reports

Which One Starts an Argument?

Version A

"The Argentina team missed the forecast by nine percent because they did not update the pipeline."

Version B

"The forecast was missed by nine percent in Argentina. The pipeline had not been updated since May, and that is being corrected this quarter."

Both are true. One puts a team on trial in front of the board. The other puts a problem on the table. You will need both — but almost never the first one.

Chapter 3: The Code

Who Did It, and When It Does Not Matter

The passive, as an executive instrument

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The forecast was missed by nine percent in Argentina."

"The pipeline had not been updated since May."

"That is being corrected this quarter."

"It is expected that the run rate will be recovered by December."

In each sentence, do we know who did it? And does the sentence need us to?

FormShapeExample
Simple past passivewas / were + past participleThe forecast was missed by nine percent.
Past perfect passivehad been + past participleThe pipeline had not been updated since May.
Continuous passiveis / are being + past participleThat is being corrected this quarter.
Impersonal reportingIt is expected / believed / understood that...It is expected that the run rate will be recovered.
Keeping the agent...by + who did it, only when it is the informationThe limits were approved by the board, not by us.
The rule behind the rulePassive for the problem, active for the credit. Take the person out when the person is not the information — and put the name back in when the news is good: the corporate team drove this.
Practice

Say it first, then click to check

"___It is expected that the margin will recover in the fourth quarter." (expect, impersonal)
"The claims in the south ___were offset by the corporate renewals." (offset, keep the agent)
The Structure

Number, Cause, Fix

Every variance you will ever report has the same three moves. Leave the third one out and it stops being a report.

1 · The number, plainly

"The forecast was missed by nine percent in Argentina."

2 · The cause, without a defendant

"The pipeline had not been updated since May, and two renewals were counted twice."

3 · The fix, with a date

"That is being corrected this quarter, and it is expected that the run rate will be recovered by December."

Number → cause → fix. And when the news is good, drop the passive and name the team.

The Question Behind the Number

S
Denise, I read the note. Eleven percent is good. Argentina is not. Walk me through it.
D
The forecast was missed by nine percent there. Two things: the pipeline had not been updated since May, and two renewals were counted twice.
S
Counted twice. Is that a one-off, or is it going to be in the next note as well?
D
A one-off. The reconciliation is being rebuilt this quarter. It is expected that the run rate will be recovered by December.
S
And the margin? The board will not care about volume if the margin moved.
D
The margin held. The claims in the south were offset by the corporate renewals — and I want to be clear that the corporate team drove that, not the market.
S
Good. Give me one number I can say out loud in Milan without a footnote.
D
Six percent, like for like, against a market benchmark of four. Nothing in that number needs a footnote.
S
That is the one I will use. And Argentina stays off the slide until December.

Listening

What a Board Actually Asks

A finance director on the three questions that always come. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is the first question a board always asks?
Is this number comparable — like for like, with the one-offs stripped out?
2. What is the second?
Is it going to happen again, or was it genuinely a one-off?
3. What is the third, and why is it the dangerous one?
What are you doing about it — dangerous because people prepare the number and the cause, and forget the fix.
4. What does he say about the phrase "we are monitoring it"?
It means nothing. Monitoring is not a fix, and a board hears it as no answer at all.
Listening 2

The Line Between Protecting and Hiding

A director on when not to use the passive. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. When does she say the passive is the right choice?
When the person is not the information — naming them would only move attention away from the problem.
2. When does she say it becomes hiding?
When the agent is you. If it was your decision, say "I decided" — "it was decided" makes everyone in the room look for the author.
3. What does she always use the active voice for?
Credit. Good news gets a name attached to it, every time.
4. What does she say the board is really testing?
Not the number — whether you know which parts of it are yours.

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "The forecast missed by nine percent in Argentina."
"The forecast was missed by nine percent." (a forecast cannot miss; it is missed)
2. "The pipeline had not been update since May."
"The pipeline had not been updated since May." (the passive needs the past participle)
3. "I will inform of the variance to the board."
"I will inform the board of the variance." or "I will report the variance to the board."
4. "The datas show that the margin is stable."
"The data show that the margin is stable." (data has no plural -s in English)
