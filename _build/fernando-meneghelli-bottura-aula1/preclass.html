Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

Who Is Fernando Bottura? -- The Career Story in English

Telling twenty years of career in one minute: the screening call with an executive search consultant. Key words: track record, milestone, to spearhead, turnaround, seasoned, hands-on, transition, to steer, to bring to the table, at the helm, stakeholder, groundwork. Structure: past simple for a closed chapter, present perfect for what is still true today.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. Do not translate — the example sentence is the meaning. These are the twelve words that carry a career story in an interview.

Track record -- a documented history of what someone has achieved
"I have a track record of turning finance teams into business partners."
Milestone -- a point that marks important progress in a project or a career
"Becoming Group CFO was the biggest milestone of my career."
To spearhead -- to lead an important change from the front
"I spearheaded the shared services project across nine agencies."
Turnaround -- a change from weak performance to strong performance
"The turnaround took two years and a very unpopular first quarter."
Seasoned -- highly experienced after many years in the same field
"They were looking for a seasoned finance leader, not a first-time CFO."
Hands-on -- directly involved in the practical work, not only supervising
"In a lean team you have to be hands-on, and I never stopped being."
Transition -- a move from one role, company or structure to another
"I am in transition right now, and I am being selective about the next role."
To steer -- to guide a company or a team in a chosen direction
"My job was to steer the group through a very hard consolidation."
To bring to the table -- to offer something valuable that others do not have
"What I bring to the table is twenty years inside agency economics."
At the helm -- in the position of leading a company or a function
"I was at the helm of finance for nine years in the same group."
Stakeholder -- a person or group with a real interest in a decision
"Every stakeholder in that room wanted a different number."
Groundwork -- the preparation that makes a later result possible
"We laid the groundwork in year one, and the savings appeared in year two."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

My name is Fernando Bottura and I have spent most of my career in advertising groups. I have been at the helm of finance for fourteen years, and I have worked in this industry for over twenty. Over that time I have led two consolidations, and I have never worked in a company without an international parent.

The dates tell a different part of the story. I joined a large advertising group in 2017 and I left at the end of last year. Before that, from 2011 to 2016, I reported directly to a CFO in New York, and I used English every single day. In my first year at the group we laid the groundwork for a shared services model, and in 2019 I spearheaded the consolidation of the back office across nine agencies.

Right now I am in transition. So far this year I have had three interview processes, and two weeks ago I did one for a CFO role in a Belgian group. I have not signed anything yet, because I am looking for the right role rather than the fastest one.

1. Why does the text say "I have worked in this industry for over twenty years" but "I joined a large advertising group in 2017"?
A Because the twenty years are still going on today, while joining in 2017 is a single finished event with a date.
B Because "have worked" and "joined" mean exactly the same thing in a career story.
C Because you use the present perfect for long periods and the past simple for short ones.
2. The text says "two weeks ago I did one" and not "I have done one". Why?
A Because interviews are always described in the past simple.
B Because "two weeks ago" is a closed point in time, and a closed time marker takes the past simple.
C Because the sentence already has another verb in the present.
3. Which words in the text signal that "have had three interview processes" is the right choice?
A "two weeks ago" and "in 2019"
B "so far this year", because the year is not over yet
C "right now", because it refers to the present moment only
4. In an interview, what does "I have led two consolidations" claim that "I led two consolidations in 2019" does not?
A That the experience is part of who he is today — a track record, not a dated episode.
B That the consolidations were bigger and more complex.
C Nothing. The two sentences carry exactly the same weight.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- A Closed Chapter or One Still Open

GRAMMAR

One question, two answers. That is the whole decision in this lesson — and it decides half of every answer you give about your career.

FormUseExample
Past simple
I joined / we closed		A closed chapter: a date, a company or a period that is over.I joined the group in 2017.
We closed the project in 2021.
Present perfect
I have worked		A chapter still open, or experience with no date attached to it.I have worked in agency finance for twenty years.
I have led two consolidations.
NegativeThe past simple uses did not. The present perfect uses have not or has not.I did not stay after the change.
I have not signed anything yet.
QuestionDid for the past simple, Have or Has for the present perfect.Did you report to Brussels?
Have you ever led a turnaround?
Time markersin 2017, last year, two weeks ago, when I was at the group point to the past simple. for, since, ever, never, already, yet, so far point to the present perfect. The sentence usually tells you before you have to decide.
for vs sincefor takes a length of time: for nine years, for over a decade. since takes a starting point: since 2017, since I left the group.
Why it matters in an interviewThe present perfect is what turns a list of jobs into a track record. "I have managed the finances of nine agencies" is a claim about you today. "I managed them in 2019" is a story about the past. Same fact, very different answer.

When you are not sure, choose the past simple with a date and keep going. A finished answer with the wrong tense is worth far more than a perfect tense you never say out loud.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are on a screening call with an executive search consultant. Choose the best thing to say.

She says: "Walk me through your career." You start with:
A "I have spent most of my career in advertising groups, and I have been at the helm of finance for fourteen years."
B "I am in advertising finance since more than twenty years."
C "I work like a CFO in publicity companies from 2005."
She asks when you left your last group. You answer:
A "I have left at the end of last year."
B "I left at the end of last year, and I am in transition now."
C "I am leaving the group last year."
You did not understand her question and you need three seconds. The best thing to say is:
A "Sorry, I did not catch that. Could you say it again?"
B Answer a different question and hope it was close enough.
C "Sorry, my English is not very good today."
She asks: "Have you ever led a turnaround?" You want to say yes and give evidence. You say:
A "Yes, I am leading turnarounds since a long time."
B "Yes. I have led two, and the second one is the one I would walk you through."
C "Yes, I have led two turnarounds in 2019 and in 2021."
She asks what makes you different from a local candidate. You answer:
A "I think I am a good professional and I learn fast."
B "What I bring to the table is twenty years inside agency economics, and I have already laid that groundwork once."
C "I have more experience than the others in the process."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. Nobody is correcting this one, and there is no score.

A search consultant opens the call and says: "Tell me about your career trajectory as a CFO." Speak for about two minutes, with no notes and in one take: how long you have worked in the industry, the last group and the dates, one thing you spearheaded, and where you are now. If a sentence breaks in the middle, say "let me make sure I answer that properly" out loud and keep going. Do not restart the recording — finishing the answer is the whole point.
