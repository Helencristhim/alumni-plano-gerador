Complementary Activities

Thirty focused minutes a day, with a purpose declared for each one — not input for its own sake. Mark each item as done after you watch or listen.

Lesson 1 -- Who Is Fernando Bottura?

Grammar Video
"Present perfect and past simple" -- BBC Learning English, 6 Minute Grammar

Six minutes and three rules for the only decision you had to make in this lesson: is the chapter closed, or is it still open? Connection to Lesson 1: this is the grammar behind "I joined the group in 2017" and "I have worked in agency finance for twenty years" — the difference between a dated episode and a track record.

Tip: after each rule, pause and say two sentences about your own career out loud — one closed with a date, one still open with "for" or "since". Two sentences, then move on. Six minutes total.

Podcast
"How to ace a job interview and combat your nerves" -- Life Kit, NPR

Twenty minutes of clear American English on the exact thing that happened to you two weeks ago: the nerves, the blank, and what to do about them. A career coach walks through a mock interview and answers the standard questions out loud, so you hear the shape of a good answer rather than reading about it. The page carries the full transcript next to the audio.

Tip: listen once with no transcript and write down the three questions she prepares for. Then read the transcript and check what you missed — that gap, not the interview advice, is the measurement that matters here.

Executive Interview
"View From The Top: Sarah Friar" -- Stanford Graduate School of Business

An hour on stage with a finance leader who was CFO of Square before running a company of her own, answering exactly the questions you were asked two weeks ago: walk me through your career, why did you move, what do you look for in a team. Connection to Lesson 1: listen for how she dates the closed chapters and leaves the open ones in the present perfect. The transcript is on the same page.

Tip: watch only the first ten minutes, where she tells her own career story. Then close the page and tell yours out loud, in the same order and in the same amount of time. Record it once and compare.
