Talk
Hans Rosling — The best stats you've ever seen (TED)

Twenty minutes of a man defending numbers out loud, in front of a room that came in believing something else. Watch what he does that almost nobody does: he never gives a figure without the sentence that says why it moved.

Tip: pick any two minutes and write down every connector he uses — because, so, therefore, which is why. Then count how many facts he leaves without one. The answer is close to zero.

Talk
Mona Chalabi — 3 ways to spot a bad statistic (TED)

Eleven minutes on the questions a good listener asks when a number arrives: where did it come from, who is missing from it, and does it match what you already know. It is the other side of your job — the side that asks.

Tip: after watching, take one number from your own close and ask her three questions about it out loud, in English. That is the rehearsal for the next review call.

Podcast
The Indicator from Planet Money — Have a bad manager? Science explains why

Nine minutes of fast, unscripted American English about something every finance team recognises. The whole episode is built on cause and result — which is exactly the grammar you worked on today.

Tip: listen once for the argument. The second time, write down the three sentences where they explain a cause, and mark the connector each one uses.
