Chapter 3: Cause and Result

Two Facts, One Explanation

The words that decide which one is the reason

Grammar Discovery

Which Half Is the Reason?

"The cost went up because we signed two new contracts in June."

"We signed two new contracts in June, so the cost went up."

"Since the invoice arrived late, the cost sits in this month instead of last month."

"The variance is a one-off, which is why I am not changing the forecast."

All four sentences say the same thing. Look at what comes right after the orange words, and right after the blue ones. Which colour is followed by the reason, and which one by the result?

WordWhat follows itExample
because · since · asthe reason, as a full sentenceThe cost went up because we signed two contracts.
sothe result, as a full sentenceWe signed two contracts, so the cost went up.
thereforethe result, in a more formal register — e-mail and reportsThe item is immaterial. Therefore, we did not reopen the period.
which is whythe result, connected to the whole sentence before itIt is a one-off, which is why the forecast does not change.
due to · because ofthe reason, as a noun — never a full sentenceDue to the late invoice, the cost sits in this month.
the first trapdue to and because of cannot take a verb after themNot due to the invoice arrived late. Say due to the late invoice, or because the invoice arrived late.
the second trapEnglish uses because or so — never both in the same sentenceNot because it was a one-off, so I kept the forecast. Choose one.

In one line: after because comes the reason; after so comes the result; after due to comes a noun.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Explaining It Out Loud

One call, two voice notes and one e-mail

Dialogue

One Cell on a Shared Screen

M
Can you scroll back up? That line, the one that jumped. What is in there?
K
That is professional services. Let me drill down into it. Here is the breakdown: three contracts, and one of them is new this month.
M
New in the sense of new supplier, or new in the sense of we forgot it last month?
K
New supplier. I traced the whole difference back to one invoice from March, and it only reached us in June, so it sits in this month.
M
All right. And is the rest of the increase real, or is something offsetting it?
K
Partly offset. A credit came in on the same line item, which is why the net movement is smaller than it looks.
M
Good. Do I need to worry about it next quarter?
K
No. It is a one-off, which is why I am not changing the forecast. And you — do you want the rationale in writing before the review?
Comprehension

About Mark

1. What does Mark ask her to do at the very beginning of the call?
He asks her to scroll back up to the line that jumped, and to say what is in it.
2. What two meanings of “new” does he ask her to separate?
New in the sense of a new supplier, and new in the sense of something they forgot last month.
3. What is the last thing he wants to know, and what is her answer?
He asks whether he needs to worry about it next quarter, and she says no, because it is a one-off.
Listening 1 · A Voice Note

The Most Expensive Five Words

A regional controller recording a note after a review call. Sound first — no text.

1. Which five words does she call the most expensive ones in the room, and why?
“I will check and come back” — because the meeting stops there and the decision waits another week.
2. What does she say she is not asking people to do?
She is not asking them to guess, and she is not asking them to know every number by heart.
3. What does she say a good answer sounds like when you are not sure?
Say what you do know, say which part you are not sure about, and give a time when you will confirm it.
Listening 2 · Advice

Three Sentences, In This Order

A finance director explaining how he wants a number explained to him.

1. What does he say most people do when they are asked about a number, and why does it fail?
They start with the detail and work forwards, so the listener does not know where the explanation is going.
2. What are the three sentences he wants, and in what order?
What the number is, what caused it, and whether it happens again.
3. What does he say about the word because?
He says that if the second sentence does not contain because or something like it, the explanation has not started yet.
Real Document

The Variance Comment

Re: June variance — professional services
from: Keitma Gomes · to: regional controller · cc: accounting manager

Hi Mark,

Professional services came in at 1.2 million, against 0.9 million in May — a variance of 33 per cent.

The increase is due to one new supplier contract. I traced the whole difference back to a single invoice dated March, which only reached us in June, so the cost sits in this month rather than in the first quarter. Part of it is offset by a credit on the same line item, which is why the net movement is 0.2 million and not 0.3.

The item is a one-off. Therefore, I am not changing the forecast for the next quarter. I will confirm the closing figure once the last reconciliation is signed off, on Thursday.

Best regards,
Keitma

1. What is the size of the variance, and against which month?
Thirty-three per cent — 1.2 million in June against 0.9 million in May.
2. Why does the cost sit in June and not in the first quarter?
Because the invoice is dated March but only reached them in June.
3. What does she say she will do on Thursday, and what has to happen first?
She will confirm the closing figure, once the last reconciliation has been signed off.
