Chapter 4: The Code

Rose Sharply, a Sharp Rise

One fact, two shapes of sentence

Grammar Discovery

Two Sentences, One Fact

"The rate rose sharply in April."

"There was a sharp rise in April."

"Approvals fell gradually from sixty to fifty-two percent."

"There was a gradual fall of eight points."

Look at the blue words. In two sentences there is a verb and a word that ends in -ly. In the other two there is a noun. What happens to sharply when rose becomes a rise?

ShapeFormExample
Verb + adverbrise / fall + sharply, gradually, slightlyThe rate rose sharply.
Adjective + nouna + sharp, gradual, slight + rise / fallThere was a sharp rise.
The pastrise → rose → risen · fall → fell → fallenIt rose in April and fell in June.
Start and endfrom + number · to + numberIt rose from 3.1 to 4.2.
The differenceby + number (verb) · of + number (noun)It fell by 0.6 points · a fall of 0.6 points.
The levelat + numberIt peaked at 4.2 and leveled off at 3.6.

In one line: a verb takes -ly, a noun takes the adjective; from and to give the ends, by gives the difference.

Listening 1 · The Monthly Meeting

An Analyst Describes the Line

Six months of one number, in under a minute. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the approval rate do in the first three months?
It was flat, at around sixty percent.
2. What happened in May, and by how much?
Approvals fell sharply, by eight points, to fifty-two percent, after the rule was tightened.
3. What does she expect for the next quarter?
She expects approvals to recover gradually and to level off at about fifty-six percent.
Listening 2 · A Podcast

Three Questions for Any Chart

A data journalist on how charts lie, and how to catch them. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the first thing he looks at on a chart?
The axis: where it starts. If it does not start at zero, a small rise can look enormous.
2. Why does he say the start date matters?
Because if you start the chart at the lowest point, every line looks like a recovery.
3. What is his third question?
Compared with what? A number that rose by two points means nothing without a comparison.
