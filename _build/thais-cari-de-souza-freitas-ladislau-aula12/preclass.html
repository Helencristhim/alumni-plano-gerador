What the Data Shows -- Describing a Trend

One line on a chart, in one clear sentence. Key words: a trend, to rise, to fall, to peak, flat, a dip, to recover, to level off, sharply, gradually. Structure: verb + adverb (rose sharply) and adjective + noun (a sharp rise), with from, to and by.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A trend -- the general direction a number moves in over time
"The trend is down, even if one month went up."
To rise -- to go up, to become higher
"The default rate rose from three point one to four point two."
To fall -- to go down, to become lower
"Approvals fell when the rule was tightened."
To peak -- to reach the highest point, before going down again
"The rate peaked in May."
Flat -- not going up or down for a period of time
"Applications were flat for the whole quarter."
A dip -- a small, short fall, followed by a return
"There was a small dip in February."
To recover -- to go back up after a fall
"The approval rate recovered in June."
To level off -- to stop going up or down and stay at one level
"After the peak, the rate leveled off."
Sharply -- very quickly and by a large amount
"The rate rose sharply in April."
Gradually -- slowly, a little at a time
"Approvals fell gradually over six months."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

There are two ways to say the same fact about a chart. With a verb: "The rate rose sharply in April." With a noun: "There was a sharp rise in April." The verb takes a word with -ly: rose sharply, fell gradually. The noun takes the word without -ly: a sharp rise, a gradual fall. Both are correct, and senior people use both, so they do not repeat themselves.

Two of the most important verbs are irregular. Rise becomes rose in the past, and fall becomes fell. Never "rised" or "falled". Then the small words. From and to give the start and the end: "It rose from 3.1 to 4.2." By gives the difference: "It fell by 0.6 points." With a noun, the difference takes of: "a fall of 0.6 points". And at gives a level: "It peaked at 4.2."

The most common mistake for Portuguese speakers is "it fell in 0.6 points". In English the difference is never in. It is by.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Describing a Trend

GRAMMAR

Verb + -ly, or adjective + noun. From, to, by and at.

JobFormExample
Verb + adverbrise / fall + sharply, graduallyThe rate rose sharply.
Adjective + nouna sharp / gradual + rise / fallThere was a gradual fall.
Pastrise → rose · fall → fellIt rose in April and fell in June.
Start and endfrom ... to ...It rose from 3.1 to 4.2.
Differenceby (verb) · of (noun)It fell by 0.6 · a fall of 0.6.
LevelatIt peaked at 4.2.

Never: it rised · it fell sharp · a sharply rise · it fell in 0.6 points
