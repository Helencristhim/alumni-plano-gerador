Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

First, Then, After That -- Giovanna's Real Day

Tell your whole day in English, from the alarm to the sofa: how often you do each thing, and in what order. Take your time, and repeat as often as you like.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the text out loud, then answer the questions below. Read slowly -- nobody is timing you.

First, my alarm goes off at five thirty. I always wake up before the sun, because my shift starts at seven. Then I get ready in twenty minutes and I leave home at six. After that, I sit in the traffic, and the traffic is often heavy at that time. I usually arrive at the site at seven, and I read the schedule with my supervisor. At twelve I stop for my lunch break, but it is short: thirty minutes, usually. I sometimes work overtime on Friday, and I am never late for the morning shift. Finally, I get home at eight and I relax on the sofa. That is my routine, and it never changes.

1. Why do we say "I always wake up" and not "I wake up always"?
A Because always, usually, often, sometimes and never come before the verb.
B Because the sentence is about the morning.
C Because "wake up" is two words.
2. The text says "I am never late" and "the traffic is often heavy". What is different here?
A Nothing is different. The position is always the same.
B With am, is and are, the word goes after the verb.
C With am, is and are, you cannot use never or often.
3. Which word closes the story of the day?
A "Then"
B "After that"
C "Finally"

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- How Often, In What Order

Grammar

Two families of small words. One says how many days; the other says what comes next.

How oftenHow many daysExample
alwaysevery dayI always wake up at five thirty.
usuallyalmost every dayI usually get home at eight.
oftenmany daysThe traffic is often heavy.
sometimesa few daysI sometimes work overtime.
neverzero daysMy routine never changes.
PositionBefore the verb: I always wake up. · But after am / is / are: I am never late. The traffic is often heavy.
In what orderWhere in the storyExample
First,the beginning -- only onceFirst, my alarm goes off.
Thenthe next thing -- use it many timesThen I get ready.
After that,the next thing, a bit slowerAfter that, I leave home.
Finally,the end -- only onceFinally, I relax.
Quick trickThey all come at the start of the sentence. Never say "then after" -- say after that.
