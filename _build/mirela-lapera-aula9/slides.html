Chapter 3: The Code

Should, Have To, Don’t Have To

Advice, rules and freedom

Grammar Discovery

Advice, Rule or Freedom?

"You should have a one-on-one with each person."

"We have to finish the report by Friday."

"You don’t have to answer every email at night."

"You mustn’t share salaries with the team."

Blue is a piece of advice: a good idea. Red is an obligation: there is no choice. Green looks negative, but it means freedom: it is not necessary. Brown is a prohibition: it is not allowed. Green and brown are very different!

JobFormExample
Advice (a good idea)should / shouldn’t + verbYou should give feedback often.
Obligation (no choice)have to / must + verbWe have to finish by Friday.
Not necessary (you can choose)don’t have to + verbYou don’t have to reply at night.
Not allowedmustn’t / can’t + verbYou mustn’t share salaries.
Asking for adviceShould I + verb?Should I talk to him today?
The trapdon’t have to is not mustn’t; no to after should and mustNot you should to delegate. Say you should delegate.

In one line: should = good idea; have to = no choice; don’t have to = not necessary; mustn’t = not allowed.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Leadership at Real Speed

One coffee, three recordings, one checklist

Dialogue

Coffee with a New Manager

D
Mirela, thanks for the coffee. Tomorrow is my first day as a manager, and I’m nervous. What should I do first?
M
First, you should have a one-on-one with each person. Listen more than you talk.
D
OK. And do I have to answer every email immediately? Even at night?
M
No, you don’t have to. But you have to set expectations: tell people when they can reach you.
D
Makes sense. My old boss checked everything I did. I hated it.
M
So don’t micromanage. Learn to delegate, and let people take ownership of their work.
D
And if someone misses a deadline?
M
Then give feedback in private, never in front of the team. You mustn’t forget that.

2. What did Daniel’s old boss do?

3. What is Daniel worried about at the end?
What to do if someone misses a deadline.
Listening 1 · A Leadership Coach

Three Things a Leader Should Do

A British leadership coach gives three pieces of advice to new managers. Sound first — no text.

1. What is his first piece of advice?
Leaders should ask more questions and give fewer orders.
2. What does he say about feedback?
It should be specific, and it should happen often, not once a year.
3. What does a leader not have to do, according to him?
A leader does not have to have all the answers.
Listening 2 · A First-Year Manager

My First Year as a Boss

An American woman remembers her mistakes in her first year as a manager. Sound first — no text.

1. What was her biggest mistake?
She tried to do everything herself and did not delegate.
2. What happened to the team?
Morale was low, and two people left.
3. What does she do differently now?
She has a one-on-one with each person every week and lets them take ownership.
Listening 3 · A CEO

Leading From Far Away

The CEO of a company with offices in five countries talks about leading remote teams. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the hardest part of leading remote teams, for him?
Knowing how people really feel, because you cannot see them every day.
2. What rule does his company have about meetings?
Every meeting must have a clear goal, and nobody has to join meetings without one.
3. What mustn’t managers do, according to him?
Send messages to the team late at night or on weekends.
