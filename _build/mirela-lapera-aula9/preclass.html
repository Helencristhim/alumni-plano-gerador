Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

Leading Without Shouting -- Should, Have To, Don’t Have To

Advice for a new manager and the words of leading people. Key words: feedback, a one-on-one, to delegate, to micromanage, a deadline, to set expectations, approachable, to take ownership, morale, a team player. Structure: modals of advice and obligation.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Feedback -- comments about how well someone did something, to help them improve
"Good feedback is specific and kind."
A one-on-one -- a private meeting between a manager and one person from the team
"I have a one-on-one with each person every two weeks."
To delegate -- to give part of your work or responsibility to another person
"New managers find it hard to delegate."
To micromanage -- to control every small detail of other people’s work
"Nobody likes a boss who micromanages."
A deadline -- the date or time when something must be finished
"The deadline for the report is Friday."
To set expectations -- to explain clearly what you want and how you will measure it
"On day one, set expectations with your team."
Approachable -- easy to talk to; friendly
"She’s a director, but she’s very approachable."
To take ownership -- to accept that a task or a problem is yours to solve
"I want people who take ownership of their projects."
Morale -- how happy and confident the people in a group feel
"After the long project, team morale was low."
A team player -- a person who works well with others for the good of the group
"He’s talented, and he’s a real team player."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Daniel is a new manager, and he asks Mirela for advice. “You should have a one-on-one with each person in your first week,” she says. “And you have to set expectations from day one: goals, deadlines and when people can reach you.”

Daniel is worried about emails. “Do I have to answer them at night?” “No, you don’t have to,” she answers. “But you mustn’t micromanage. Learn to delegate, and let people take ownership of their work.”

Her last piece of advice is about people. “Give feedback often, in private. Be approachable. When morale is low, talk to the team. A good leader is also a team player.”

1. What does “you don’t have to answer emails at night” mean?
A It is not necessary, so Daniel can choose.
B It is not allowed: the company will punish Daniel if he answers any email after six in the evening.
C It is a very good idea.
2. Why does Mirela say “you should have a one-on-one”?
A Because it is a law, and all managers in the country must do it every week.
B Because it is a good idea, a piece of advice.
C Because it is not allowed.
3. What should Daniel do when morale is low?
A Send more emails at night.
B Talk to the team.
C Control every small detail of everybody’s work until the end of the project, without delegating.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Modals -- Should, Have To, Don’t Have To, Mustn’t

GRAMMAR

Use should for advice, have to or must for obligations, don’t have to when something is not necessary, and mustn’t when something is not allowed. There is no to after should and must.

JobFormExample
Advice (a good idea)should / shouldn’t + verbYou should give feedback often.
Obligation (no choice)have to / must + verbWe have to finish by Friday.
Not necessary (you can choose)don’t have to + verbYou don’t have to reply at night.
Not allowedmustn’t / can’t + verbYou mustn’t share salaries.
Asking for adviceShould I + verb?Should I talk to him today?
The trapdon’t have to is not mustn’t; no to after should and mustNot you should to delegate. Say you should delegate.

Never: You should to delegate · We must to finish · Do I should...? · You mustn’t (when you mean: it is not necessary)

Stage 2: Put the Advice in Order

Order

A mentor gives advice to a new manager. Put the conversation in a logical order.

?"Tomorrow is my first day as a manager. What should I do first?"
?"You should have a one-on-one with each person."
?"Do I have to answer emails at night?"
?"No, you don’t have to. But set expectations."
?"And what if someone misses a deadline?"
?"Give feedback in private, never in front of the team."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A colleague can choose to come to the meeting or not. You say:
A "You mustn’t come to the meeting, but you can if you want, it is your decision."
B "You don’t have to come to the meeting."
C "You shouldn’t to come to the meeting."
You want to give advice to a tired colleague. You say:
A "You should take a day off."
B "You should to take a day off, because you look very tired after this long project."
C "You must to take a day off."
Your boss checks every small detail of your work. You say:
A "My boss delegates a lot, so he checks every small detail of everything I do."
B "My boss micromanages."
C "My boss is approachable."
You want to say the team feels happy and confident. You say:
A "Team morale is high."
B "Team deadline is high, because everybody feels happy and confident about the project."
C "Team feedback is high."
