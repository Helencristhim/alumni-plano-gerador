Who Is Stephanie? -- The English You Already Have

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These are the words that explain two careers in one breath.

Field -- the area of work or study you specialize in
"My field is medicine, but today I work with data."
Role -- the position you have and what you do in it
"My role is to connect the medical team and the IT team."
Degree -- the qualification you receive when you finish university
"My degree is in medicine, and I have a master in surgery."
To be responsible for -- to be the person who takes care of something
"I am responsible for the quality of the data."
To bridge -- to connect two groups that do not speak the same language
"I bridge the doctors and the engineers every day."
Workload -- the amount of work a person has to do
"My workload changes a lot from one week to the next."
Insight -- a clear and useful understanding of something
"The numbers give us insight into the real problem."
Healthcare -- the system that keeps people healthy and treats them when they are sick
"I work in healthcare, but I am not in a hospital now."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Karen Reis is a doctor, and her field is emergency medicine. She works at a hospital in Lisbon three days a week. On the other two days she works with data: she is building a system that helps the hospital see which patients need care first. Right now she is studying English every morning, because she wants to present her project at a conference in Boston. Her role is unusual, and she likes it: she bridges two teams that do not speak the same language.
Why does the text use "works" and "bridges" with an -s?
A Because the actions happened in the past
B Because the subject is "she" -- third person singular
C Because the actions are temporary
What does "Right now she is studying English every morning" tell you?
A It is temporary -- something happening around now
B It is a fact that is always true about her
C She studied English a long time ago

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple and Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

Use the present simple for facts and routines — what is always true. Use the present continuous for what is happening now or around now — something temporary.

present simpleverb (+ -s for he / she / it)I work in healthcare. She works with data.
present continuousam / is / are + verb-ingRight now I am studying for the exam.
negativedo / does not + verb · am / is / are not + -ingShe does not operate anymore. I am not working today.
questiondo / does + subject + verb · am / is / are + subject + -ingDo you see patients? Are you working on it now?

Careful: some verbs almost never take the continuous — know, want, need, understand, like. Say "I want to revalidate my degree", not "I am wanting".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

A "I am doctor, but now data."

C "Medicine, data, artificial intelligence."
You need one second to organize your answer. What do you say?
A "Wait. Wait a moment. Wait."

A "This month I work on a new model with the IT team."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Someone you have just met asks: "So, what do you do?" Record sixty seconds: your degree, what you did before, what you are doing right now, and one thing you want to do next. Do not write a script first — just speak.
