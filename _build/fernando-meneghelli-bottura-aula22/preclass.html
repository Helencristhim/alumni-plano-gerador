Lesson 22 -- Pre-class

Day One -- The First Words to Your Team

Your first week as CFO: recognize the team, take stock before you change anything, and make one promise you can keep. Key words: onboarding, to hit the ground running, to find your feet, to take stock, a town hall, morale, a one-to-one, a handover, a bottleneck, the month-end close, a fresh pair of eyes, to earn trust. Structure: inversion after negative and limiting words (not only, never, only when, at no point).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These twelve are for your first week in a new job: arriving, listening and earning the right to change things.

Onboarding -- the process of helping a new person join a company and learn the job
“My onboarding started with the chief executive.”
To hit the ground running -- to start a new job and work well from the very first day
“Everybody expects a new CFO to hit the ground running.”
To find your feet -- to become comfortable and confident in a new place or job
“Give me two weeks to find my feet.”
To take stock -- to stop and think carefully about a situation before you act
“I want to take stock before I change anything.”
A town hall -- a meeting where a leader speaks to many employees and answers their questions
“The town hall is on Thursday, with the whole finance team.”
Morale -- how confident and positive a group of people feel about their work
“Morale is low because the team works late every month.”
A one-to-one -- a private meeting between a manager and one member of the team
“My first one-to-one was with the financial controller.”
A handover -- when the person leaving a job passes the work and the information to the next person
“The interim CFO gave me a clear handover.”
A bottleneck -- one point in a process that slows down everything after it
“The bottleneck is the approvals from the regional offices.”
The month-end close -- the work of finishing and checking the accounts at the end of every month
“The month-end close takes eight days.”
A fresh pair of eyes -- a new person who can see problems that the others no longer notice
“A fresh pair of eyes is useful, but only if it listens first.”
To earn trust -- to make people trust you through what you do over time
“You earn trust in the first month, not in a speech.”

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

On my first morning, the chief executive gave me a week of onboarding. Not only had the team prepared my desk, but they had also booked a one-to-one with every manager.

On Tuesday, the interim CFO gave me the handover. Never have I read notes so honest: they showed the bottleneck in the month-end close, and they said that morale was low.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Auxiliary Before Subject

GRAMMAR

When a sentence starts with a negative or limiting word (not only, never, rarely, only when, at no point), the word order changes: auxiliary + subject, as in a question. It is formal, and it gives emphasis. Use it in a speech or an email, not in every sentence.

Opening wordsExample
Not only ..., but ... alsoNot only is this a strong team, but it is also a team that closes on time.
Never / RarelyNever have I read a handover as clear as this one.
At no pointAt no point will you hear about a decision from somebody else first.
Only when + clauseOnly when I have met each of you will I talk about changes.
The trapsNo auxiliary? Add do / does / did: Not only did I accept, not Not only I accepted. With only when, invert the second clause, not the first.

On day one, two inversions in a two-minute speech sound confident. Ten sound like a script.

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen, then record yourself. Stress the opening words (NOT only, NEVer, at NO point) and keep the auxiliary light and quick: never have I sounds like one word, never-av-I.

Give me a month to take stock, and then hold me to it.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

It is your first week as CFO. Choose the answer that works best.

You open your first town hall. Your first sentence is:
A “I have many plans for this department, and I will present all of them to you today in detail.”
B “Not only is this a strong team, but it is also a team that closes on time.”
C “Hello, I am the new boss.”
Somebody asks: “Are you going to change the month-end close?” You say:
A “Only when I have talked to the people who run it will I decide.”
B “Yes, I have already found the bottleneck, and the new process starts next Monday for everybody.”
C “No comment.”
Which sentence is correct?
A “Never I have seen such a clear handover.”
B “At no point I will change a process without asking the people who run it first.”
C “Only when I have met everyone will I talk about changes.”
In your first one-to-one, the controller tells you morale is low. You:
A Say that morale is not your job, and that you will ask the HR team to take care of it tomorrow.
B Thank her and ask what would help.
C Say nothing.
At the end of the first week, the chief executive asks for your plan. You say:
A “I already have ten changes, and I can start all of them this week without the team.”
B “Not yet.”
C “Give me a month to take stock, and I’ll bring you a plan.”
