Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An itinerary -- a written plan of where you go and when
"I am sending you the itinerary tomorrow."
Shoulder season -- the quieter months just before or after the busiest time
"We are riding in shoulder season, in late May."
To be fully booked -- to have no places or rooms left at all
"The whole coast is fully booked in August."
A deposit -- money you pay early to hold a place
"I paid a deposit on the first two nights."
Non-refundable -- impossible to get your money back if you cancel
"The ticket was cheap, but it is non-refundable."
To lock in -- to fix a price or a date so it cannot change later
"Let us lock in the first week."
To pencil something in -- to arrange something for now, knowing it may still change
"I have pencilled in the second week."
A ballpark figure -- a rough number that is close enough to work with
"Can you give me a ballpark figure?"
To sleep on it -- to wait until the next day before you decide
"Let me sleep on it and I will call you."
To play it by ear -- to decide as you go, without a fixed plan
"We will play the last three days by ear."
A one-way ticket -- a ticket to travel out, with no return journey
"I bought a one-way ticket to Porto."
To fall through -- to fail before it ever happens
"That trip fell through twice."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Annika is planning her half of the trip. "I am arriving in Porto on the eighth," she says, "because the cheap train from Bilbao only runs on Tuesdays." She has already paid a deposit on the guesthouse, and it is non-refundable, so that part is locked in. For the ferry she only has a ballpark figure, and the company will not confirm the May timetable until March. "We are going to ride the whole coast," she says, "but let us pencil in the second week and play the last three days by ear. In shoulder season the small places are never fully booked." Notice the shapes. Something already arranged takes the present continuous: I am arriving. An intention you already had takes going to. A decision you make while you speak takes will. And a timetable takes the present simple: the train runs on Tuesdays.

1. Why does Annika say "I am arriving in Porto on the eighth" and not "I will arrive"?
A The date is already arranged, so English uses the present continuous.
B She is deciding it at that exact moment.
C Both forms mean exactly the same thing.
2. Which sentence shows an intention she already had?
A "The company will not confirm the timetable until March."
B "We are going to ride the whole coast."
C "The cheap train runs on Tuesdays."
3. Why is "the train runs on Tuesdays" in the present simple?
A It is a timetable, true every week.
B It is a decision she is making now.
C It is a prediction about next year.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Future Forms

GRAMMAR

Ask yourself one question: when was the decision made?

FormYou use it forExample
present continuousa fixed arrangement: a date, a booking, other peopleI am flying to Porto on the ninth.
going toan intention you already hadWe are going to ride the whole coast.
going toa prediction from evidence you can seeLook at that queue. We are going to miss it.
willa decision made as you speak, an offer, a promiseI will book the guesthouse tonight.
present simpletimetables and schedulesThe ferry leaves at six.

Key point: if it is already in the diary, use the present continuous, not "will". And after when, if, as soon as and before, English uses the present: "When I get to Porto, I will call you", never "when I will get".
