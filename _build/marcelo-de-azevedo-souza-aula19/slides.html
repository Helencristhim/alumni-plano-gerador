Chapter 3: The Code

Three Ways to Talk About What Happens Next

will · going to · present continuous

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

All four sentences are about the future. What makes the highlighted part different in each one?

A. "I am flying to Porto on the ninth. The ticket is booked."

B. "We are going to ride the whole Atlantic coast."

C. "You know what? I will book the guesthouse tonight."

D. "The ferry leaves at six every morning."

Grammar Rule

Choosing the Right Future

FormYou use it forExample
present continuousa fixed arrangement: a date, a booking, other people involvedI am flying to Porto on the ninth.
going toan intention you already had, or a prediction from evidence you can seeWe are going to ride the whole coast.
willa decision made as you speak, an offer, a promiseI will book the guesthouse tonight.
present simpletimetables and schedules, true every dayThe ferry leaves at six.

Key idea: ask yourself one question. Is it already in the diary? Present continuous. Did I decide before now? Going to. Am I deciding this second? Will. Native speakers move between the three in a single conversation, and so should you.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

✗ WRONG

"I will meet Annika at ten tomorrow. It is all arranged."

✓ RIGHT

"I am meeting Annika at ten tomorrow. It is all arranged."

✗ WRONG

"When I will get to Porto, I will call you."

✓ RIGHT

"When I get to Porto, I will call you."

If it is already arranged, use the present continuous. And after when, if, as soon as and before, English uses the present, never "will".

Language Focus

Heard in the Audio

The form you choose tells the listener when the decision was made.

"I am arriving in Porto on the eighth." — already fixed

"I am going to send you the itinerary tomorrow." — decided earlier

"I will pencil in the last three days." — decided as he speaks

Self-Assessment

What I Learned

I use the present continuous for a fixed arrangement: "I am flying on the ninth".
I use "going to" for an intention I already had, or a prediction I can see coming.
I use "will" for a decision I make while I am speaking, and for offers.
I say "when I get there", never "when I will get there".
I can book a room, ask about deposits, and plan a whole trip in English.
