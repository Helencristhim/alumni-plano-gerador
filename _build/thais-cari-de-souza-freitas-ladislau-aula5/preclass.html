Lesson 05 -- Pre-class

Right Now vs Every Day -- The Status Call

Two tenses, one status call, and the bad news said first. Key words: a pilot, a sprint, a backlog, a stakeholder, a rollout, to monitor, to pause, a workaround, on track, behind schedule. Structure: present continuous contrasted with present simple.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A pilot -- a small test of something new, with only a few customers
"We are running a pilot with two thousand customers this month."
A sprint -- a short period of work with one clear goal
"The team is finishing the second sprint of the pilot this week."
A backlog -- the list of work that is waiting to be done
"The backlog is growing, and nobody is looking at the bottom of it."
A stakeholder -- a person the decision affects, who has to be heard
"Risk is the stakeholder we always talk to last, and we should not."
A rollout -- the moment a change reaches all the customers
"The rollout starts on Monday, and it takes three weeks."
To monitor -- to keep watching a number closely while it changes
"We are monitoring the pilot every morning, not every month."
To pause -- to stop something for a while and start it again later
"We are pausing the rollout until the risk team answers."
A workaround -- a temporary way around a problem, not a fix
"The team is using a workaround, and it is working for now."
On track -- going exactly as planned
"Two of the three projects are on track, and one is not."
Behind schedule -- later than the plan said it would be
"The rollout is two weeks behind schedule, and I am saying it early."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

English has two presents, and a status update needs both. The first one you already use: "I read the portfolio every Monday." "The model approves eight requests out of ten." That is the routine — what your job always is, whether or not you are doing it at this minute. The second one has two pieces: am, is or are, plus the verb with -ing. "We are running a pilot this month." "The team is finishing the second sprint." That is work that started and has not finished.

Some words pull one tense and not the other. Every week, usually, never and always belong to the routine. Now, this week, at the moment and today belong to the second one. To say no or to ask, nothing new appears — you already have the two pieces: "The rollout is not moving." "Are you monitoring it every day?"

Two warnings, and both cost a job interview. First, a small group of verbs never takes -ing: know, want, need, understand, mean, believe. Say "I do not know yet", never I am not knowing. Second, and this is the one almost every Brazilian says: to tell somebody how long you have been in a job, English does not use this tense at all. Not I am working here since one year. Say "I have worked here for a year", or simply "I have been here a year".

1. "I read the portfolio every Monday" and "we are running a pilot this month". What is the difference?
A The first one is about the past and the second about the present.
B The first is a routine that is always true; the second started recently and is not finished.
C The first is formal and the second is informal.
2. Why is "I am not knowing the answer" wrong?
A Because know belongs to a small group of verbs that never take -ing.
B Because the sentence is negative and negatives use the simple.
C Because know is an irregular verb.
3. How do you say, in English, that you have been in your job for one year?
A "I am working here since one year."
B "I work here since one year."
C "I have worked here for a year."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Continuous vs Present Simple

GRAMMAR

The simple is what your job always is. The continuous is what is on your screen and not finished.

JobFormExample
A routine, always truepresent simpleI read the portfolio every Monday.
Started, not finishedam / is / are + verb-ingWe are running a pilot this month.
Negativeam / is / are + not + verb-ingThe rollout is not moving.
QuestionAm / Is / Are + subject + verb-ingAre you monitoring it every day?
Words that pull the simpleevery week, usually, never, alwaysWe meet every Monday.
Words that pull the continuousnow, this week, at the moment, todayWe are meeting twice this week.
Verbs that stay simpleknow, want, need, understand, mean, believeI do not know yet.
Time in a jobhave worked + forI have worked here for a year.

Never: I am working here since one year · this week we test a new rule · I am not knowing · you monitoring it every day? · I am wanting an answer
