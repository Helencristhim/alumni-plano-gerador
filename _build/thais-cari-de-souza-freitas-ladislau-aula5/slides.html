Chapter 3: The Code

Always True, or True This Week?

Two tenses, one team

Grammar Discovery

Which Two Are True Only This Week?

"I read the portfolio every Monday."

"We are running a pilot with two thousand customers."

"The model approves eight requests out of ten."

"The rollout is not moving, because we are waiting for the risk team."

The red verbs stand alone. The blue ones always come in two pieces. Look at what the second piece ends in, and at which sentences you could still be doing at this exact minute.

JobFormExample
A routine, always truepresent simpleI read the portfolio every Monday.
Started, not finishedam / is / are + verb-ingWe are running a pilot this month.
Negativeam / is / are + not + verb-ingThe rollout is not moving.
QuestionAm / Is / Are + subject + verb-ingAre you monitoring it every day?
Time words that pull each oneevery, usually, never → simple · now, this week, at the moment → continuousWe meet every Monday. · We are meeting twice this week.
Verbs that stay simpleknow, want, need, understand, mean, believeI know the answer. Not I am knowing.
The traphow long you have worked somewhere is not the continuousNot I am working here since one year. Say I have worked here for a year.

In one line: the simple is what your job always is; the continuous is what is on your screen and not finished.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Status Call, at Full Speed

One conversation, three recordings, one update

Dialogue

Two Minutes Before the Status Call

M
Before we start, give me the short version. Where are we?
T
The rollout is two weeks behind schedule. I am telling you now, not at the end.
M
Good. What is holding it?
T
We are waiting for one stakeholder. Risk reviews every policy change, and they are reviewing three at the same time this month.
M
And the pilot? Is that stopped too?
T
No. The pilot is running, and we are monitoring it every morning. The numbers look normal so far.
M
So what do you need from me?
T
One line to risk, asking them to look at ours first. I am not pausing the pilot while we wait.
Comprehension

About Mark

1. What does Mark ask for before the call starts?
The short version: where they are.
2. What does he want to know about the pilot?
Whether it is stopped too, like the rollout.
3. What is his last question, and what does it tell you about how he works?
He asks what she needs from him. He does not ask for an explanation or for somebody to blame; he asks what would unblock it.
Listening 1 · A Status Update

Three Projects, Two Minutes

A product manager giving a weekly update. Sound first — no text.

1. Which project is on track, and what is the team doing on it this week?
The pilot is on track. This week the team is monitoring it every morning and adding two thousand more customers.
2. Why is the second project behind schedule?
Because they are waiting for one stakeholder, and that team is reviewing three changes at the same time.
3. What does he say about the third project, and what does he ask for?
It is paused, and he is not restarting it this quarter. He asks for one decision, not for more people.
Listening 2 · A Podcast

Say the Bad News First

A leadership coach on the first sentence of a status update. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say most people do with bad news in a meeting?
They put it at the end, after everything that went well, and by then nobody is listening properly.
2. What does she say the first sentence should contain?
The problem, the size of it, and the fact that you are already handling it.
3. What does she say you should never do in that first sentence?
Apologise. She says an apology moves the conversation to your feelings instead of the problem.
Listening 3 · Business Radio

Testing on a Few

A short business radio report on how banks test a change. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the report say a bank does before it changes a rule for everybody?
It runs the change on a small group first, and it keeps a second group with the old rule to compare.
2. Why does the report say credit is harder to test than a button on a screen?
Because the result of a credit decision takes months to appear, so the team has to wait while the money is already out.
3. What does the report say the teams are doing about that wait?
They are watching early signals, like the first missed payment, instead of waiting for the final number.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I use the present simple for a routine and am, is or are plus -ing for work in progress.
I never say I am working here since one year. I say I have worked here for a year.
I never say I am knowing. Know, want, need and understand stay simple.
I can open a status update with the bad news, without apologising first.
I know the words: a pilot, a sprint, a backlog, a stakeholder, a rollout, to monitor, to pause, a workaround, on track, behind schedule.
