Lesson 05 -- Pre-class

Then and Now -- The Life You Used to Have

How to talk about your old routine, your life now and the changes in between, and how to answer the party question: so, what do you do? Key words: to retire, a routine, to slow down, a fresh start, to take up, spare time, to look back, to miss, to be used to, to get used to. Structure: used to and would for the past, be used to and get used to for what is normal now.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To retire -- to stop working, usually because of your age
"He retired at fifty-eight and bought a small boat."
A routine -- the things you do every day, usually in the same order
"My old routine started at six with coffee and the news."
To slow down -- to do things with less speed and less stress
"After years of long days, I finally slowed down."
A fresh start -- a new beginning, a chance to do things differently
"Moving to the coast was a fresh start for both of us."
To take up -- to start a new hobby or activity
"She took up painting when she stopped working."
Spare time -- the time when you are not working and can do what you like
"In my spare time, I read about old sea routes."
To look back -- to think about something in your past
"When I look back, I worked too many weekends."
To miss -- to feel sad because something is not in your life anymore
"I do not miss the traffic, but I miss my old team."
To be used to -- to know something well, so it is normal for you
"I am used to getting up early, even on Sundays."
To get used to -- to slowly start to feel that something new is normal
"It took me months to get used to having no meetings."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Roberto was a lawyer for thirty years. “I used to work twelve hours a day,” he says. “On Fridays, I would stay at the office until ten.” Used to and would both talk about things he did again and again in the past, and does not do now. But only used to works for states: “I used to live in the city.” You cannot say I would live in the city.

Two years ago, he retired and moved to a small town by the sea. “At first, it was strange. I wasn’t used to the silence. Slowly, I got used to it.” Be used to means that something is normal for you. Get used to is the process: it is becoming normal.

“Now I am used to waking up with the sound of the waves,” he says. After be used to and get used to, the verb takes -ing. “And I am still getting used to having so much spare time.”

"On Fridays, I would stay at the office until ten." What does it mean?
A He stayed late only one Friday.
B He wants to stay late on Fridays now.
C He stayed late on Fridays many times in the past.
What is the difference between "be used to" and "get used to"?
A Be used to means it is normal now; get used to means it is becoming normal.
B There is no difference.
C Be used to is the past, and get used to is the present.
Which sentence is correct?
A "I use to wake up early."
B "I am used to waking up early."
C "I am used to wake up early."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used To, Would, Be Used To, Get Used To

GRAMMAR

The past habit: used to or would. What is normal for you now: be used to plus -ing. The change: get used to plus -ing.

JobFormExample
A past habit or stateused to + verbI used to work downtown.
A past action, again and againwould + verbEvery summer, we would rent a boat.
Normal for you nowbe used to + -ingI am used to sleeping on boats.
Becoming normalget used to + -ingI am getting used to having free mornings.
A habit todayusually / present simpleI usually sail on Sundays.
A closed periodpast simple + forI worked there for twenty years.

Never: I am used to wake up early · I use to sail on Sundays · I used to work there for twenty years · I would live in the city

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

At a party, somebody asks a retired man about his life. Put the conversation in a natural order.

?"Oh, nice! Don't you get bored?"
?"And what do you do with all that time?"
?"So, what do you do?"
?"I've taken up sailing. Ask me again in a year!"
?"Not at all. I used to work weekends, so now I'm getting used to having free time."
?"Well, I used to be an engineer, but I retired last year."

A "I use to waking up early."
