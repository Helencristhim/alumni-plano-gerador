Series
Friends — Season 1, Episode 1

Six people in a phase of life that everybody knows is temporary, talking the way Americans actually talk when they are relaxed. It is the densest free source of the expressions you said you never catch the first time, and used to and would turn up in almost every episode.

Tip: watch one episode with English subtitles and write down every sentence with used to or would. Then say your own version of three of them out loud.

Talk
Andrea Powell — “How to help athletes adapt to retirement” (TEDxSioux Falls)

Twelve minutes on the exact thing this lesson is made of: what happens to a person whose identity was built inside a sport when the sport stops. Watch it as content first and as English second.

Tip: turn subtitles on. Count how many times she says used to, and notice that almost all of them are about what athletes WERE, not what they did.

Podcast
BBC Learning English — “Used To & Would” (6 Minute Grammar)

Six minutes, spoken, on precisely the choice you practised today, including the state limit that catches almost everybody. Free, and short enough for the bus.

Tip: after each example, say the other version out loud and decide whether it still works. Half the time it will not, and that is the point.
