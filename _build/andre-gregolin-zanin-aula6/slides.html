Chapter 4: The Code

What You Did, and What You Were

One of the two can carry a state. The other cannot.

Grammar Discovery

Where Does the Swap Break?

"I used to be the tallest boy in every room I walked into."

"Two of them would train before school as well as after it."

"I used to hate Tuesdays. Now Tuesday is the day I check the fixture."

"Did you use to play competitively?" — "I did not use to think about it that way."

Both colors talk about a past that stopped. Now try the swap. Every green sentence also works in red — but the first and the third break if you put them in green. Ask yourself what those two have that the other does not.

JobFormExample
A past habit that stoppedused to + bare verbI used to train five afternoons a week.
The same habit, in a storywould + bare verbWe would train before school as well as after.
A past STATE that stoppedused to only — never wouldI used to be the tallest boy in the room.
Negativedid not use to — no d after didI did not use to think about it that way.
QuestionDid … use to … ?Did you use to play competitively?
Still true todaypresent simple, no used to at allI still play on Sundays.
A different structure, same wordsbe used to + -ing = it is normal for me nowI am used to waking up at twenty to six.
The trapwould cannot carry a stateNot I would be nervous. Say I used to be nervous.

In one line: would only does actions. Used to does actions and states, which is why used to is always safe and would is the one that sounds like a story.

Grammar Practice

Action or State?

Say it first, then click to check

“I ___used to be the tallest boy in the room.” (be — a state, so only one of the two works)
“We ___would train before school as well as after.” (train — a repeated action, in a story)
“I ___used to hate Tuesdays.” (hate — a feeling, so a state)
“___Did you use to play competitively?” (the question form — three words)
“I ___did not use to think about it that way.” (the negative — four words, and no d)
“I ___am used to waking up at twenty to six.” (it is normal for me now, not a past habit)

Listening 1 · A Voice Note

Nostalgia Is a Trick

The coach of that team, recording an answer she did not give the journalist. Sound first — no text.

1. What was the question the journalist asked, and what does she say the right verb is?
He asked whether that era defined them. She says it shaped them, which is a different verb.
2. What does she say memory does with the Sunday and the Tuesday?
It keeps the Sunday and quietly throws away the Tuesday.
3. What does she say you should notice when somebody says those were the days about a whole life?
That they are usually telling you something about right now.
Listening 2 · TED-Ed style

What Nostalgia Is Actually For

A researcher on why the feeling exists at all. Sound first — no text.

1. How was nostalgia classified for two hundred years?
As an illness — a disorder of soldiers far from home, with treatments proposed for it.
2. What does modern research find happens to people who remember a period they loved?
They come out measurably more optimistic, more generous and more willing to try something new.
3. What is the current explanation, and who does it damage?
That nostalgia reminds you that you have been someone before, which is evidence you can be someone again. It damages people who use it as a place to live rather than a place to visit.
