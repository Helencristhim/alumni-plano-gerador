Lesson 11 -- Pre-class

Past Perfect for Turnaround Stories -- What Had Already Happened Before You Arrived

Telling a project story in two layers: the situation you inherited and what you did about it. Key words: to inherit, backlog, setback, to turn around, root cause, downtime, contingency, momentum. Structure: past perfect -- had plus past participle for the earlier action, past simple for the later one, with by the time, before, after, once, already and never.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To inherit -- to receive a team, a system or a problem from the person who had the role before you
"She inherited a platform that had not been updated since 2019."
Backlog -- a large amount of work that is waiting to be done
"I inherited a backlog that had been growing for two years."
Setback -- an event that stops progress or pushes it backwards
"The migration hit a setback in the third week."
To turn around -- to change a failing situation into a successful one
"We turned the platform around in six months."
Root cause -- the real, underlying reason a problem happened
"The root cause was a change that nobody had documented."
Downtime -- a period when a system is not working and cannot be used
"We cut downtime from six hours a month to twenty minutes."
Contingency -- a plan you prepare in case something goes wrong
"We had a contingency ready before we started the migration."
Momentum -- the force that keeps progress moving once it has started
"By March the team had regained its momentum."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

In 2021 Lucia inherited a payments platform. On her first day, everything difficult had already happened. Three engineers had left the team, the backlog had grown to eight months of work, and the platform had been unstable since the 2019 migration. Downtime was six hours a month, and nobody had ever looked for the root cause. Every action here uses one form: had plus a past participle. It marks the earlier of two past moments -- what was already true before she took over. What she did next stays in the past simple: she found the cause, and the team rewrote one service. There was one setback: in month three they lost a release, because they had not built a contingency for the data migration. After that, nothing shipped without one. By month six the team had regained its momentum and turned the platform around. Notice the difference: she joined the company in 2021 -- one moment, one past. But by the time she joined, the backlog had grown -- two moments, and only the earlier one takes had.

1. Why does the text say "the backlog had grown" and not "the backlog grew"?
A Because the backlog is still growing today.
B Because it happened before the other past moment, when she took over.
C Because "grow" cannot be used in the past simple.
2. Why is "she joined the company in 2021" in the past simple?
A There is only one past moment, so there is nothing earlier to mark.
B Because dates always take the past simple.
C Because the sentence is short.
3. In "they lost a release because they had not built a contingency", which action happened first?
A Losing the release.
B Not building the contingency -- the verb with "had" is the earlier one.
C Both happened at exactly the same time.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Perfect

GRAMMAR

How English marks the moment before the moment.

RuleFormExample
The earlier actionhad + past participle"the team had lost three engineers"
The later actionpast simple"I took over in 2021"
Negative and questionhad not (hadn't) + participle
Had + subject + participle?		"We had not built a contingency."
"Had anyone documented it?"
Signal wordsby the time · before · after · once · already · never · until then. The form never changes with the subject: I, she, they — always had. Use it only when there are TWO past moments and the order matters; a single past event stays in the past simple.
