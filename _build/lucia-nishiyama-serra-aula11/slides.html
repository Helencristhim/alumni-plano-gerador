Chapter 1: Before You Arrived

From One Sentence to Two Moments

Last time, relative clauses packed three facts into one sentence: "the engineer who found the bottleneck". Tonight you do the opposite. You separate two moments and put them in order: "By the time I took over, the team had lost three engineers." One tense, and suddenly the interviewer knows how hard the job really was.

The Document

The Handover Note

Platform Handover
Payments · Q1 2021
Prepared for: L. Serra, incoming Director · Day 1

Team. Three senior engineers had left in the twelve months before this handover. Two roles were still open.

Backlog. It had grown from six weeks of work to eight months.

Stability. The platform had been unstable since the 2019 migration. Average downtime: six hours per month.

Root cause analysis. Never completed. Nobody had documented the 2019 changes.

1. What had happened to the team in the twelve months before the handover?
Three senior engineers had left, and two roles were still open.
2. What does the note say about the root cause analysis?
It had never been completed, and nobody had documented the 2019 changes.
The Big Idea

Two Layers, One Story

The earlier layer

what you inherited: "The backlog had grown to eight months."

The later layer

what you did about it: "We rewrote one service."

Say only the second layer and you sound busy. Say both, in the right order, and you sound like someone who turned a situation around.

Chapter 3: The Earlier Past

"It Had Already Happened"

The grammar of what came first

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"By the time I took over, the team had lost three engineers."

"The platform had been unstable for a year before we found the root cause."

"Nobody had ever documented the change, so the search took four hours."

"Once we had found it, we cut downtime to twenty minutes."

In every sentence, the blue action happened before the orange one. What do all the blue verbs have in common?

RuleFormExample
The earlier actionhad + past participle"the team had lost three engineers"
The later actionpast simple"I took over in 2021"
Negative and questionhad not (hadn't) + participle
Had + subject + participle?		"We had not built a contingency."
"Had anyone documented it?"
Signal wordsby the time · before · after · once · already · never · until then. The same form works for every subject: I, you, she, they — always had.

Use it only when there are TWO past moments and the order matters. A single past event stays in the past simple: "I joined in 2021."

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"By the time I took over, the team already lost three engineers."
"By the time I took over, the team had already lost three engineers."
"I had joined the company in 2021."
"I joined the company in 2021."
"After we had found the root cause, we had rewritten the service."
"After we had found the root cause, we rewrote the service."

Only the earlier action takes had. One past event alone stays in the past simple, and two past perfects in a row make the order impossible to read.

Practice

What Came First

Say it first, then click to check

"By the time I took over, the backlog ___had grown to eight months." (grow)
"The platform ___had been unstable for a year before we found the root cause." (be)
"Nobody ___had documented the change." (document)
"We lost a release because we ___had not built a contingency." (not build)
"By month six the team ___had regained its momentum." (regain)
Detail

Past Perfect or Past Simple?

Two past moments → only the first takes "had"

"Once we had found the root cause, we ___rewrote one service." (rewrite)
"I ___joined the company in 2021." (join — one moment only)
"The queue ___had already grown to four hundred thousand messages by the time he opened the dashboard." (already grow)
"After the migration had failed, we ___added a contingency to every release." (add)
Chapter 4: The Interview Story

Mark Asks About the Rescue

A senior recruiter for a VP of AI Safety role

Dialogue

The Project That Was Already Broken

M
Lucia, tell me about a project you inherited that was already in trouble when you arrived.
L
In 2021 I inherited a payments platform. By the time I took over, the team had lost three engineers, and the backlog had grown to eight months of work.
M
And what was the state of the system itself?
L
Bad. The platform had been unstable since 2019. Downtime was six hours a month, and nobody had ever looked for the root cause.
M
So where did you start?
L
With the cause, not the symptoms. Once we had found it, we rewrote one service and cut downtime to twenty minutes a month.
M
Was there a setback along the way?
L
One big one. In month three we lost a release, because we had not built a contingency for the data migration. After that, nothing shipped without one, and by month six the team had regained its momentum and turned the platform around.
