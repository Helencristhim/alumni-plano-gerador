Complementary Activities

Optional extras to enjoy outside class. Mark each one as done after you watch or listen to it.

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart: “Can We Be Candid? How to Communicate Clearly and Directly”

Matt Abrahams, from Stanford, talks with Susan Rice about saying exactly what you mean, in rooms where a vague answer is read as a weak one. It is the same idea as this lesson: the precise sentence is the one people trust.

Tip: every time you hear a verb followed by to or by -ing (decide to, avoid saying, keep doing), pause and say one sentence of your own about Dataside with the same verb.

Listen on Stanford GSB ↗
Talk
Kim Scott: “How to Lead with Radical Candor” (TEDxPortland)

The talk starts with one question: how can you say what you mean without being mean? A former Google and Apple manager explains why clear, direct words are a kindness, not a risk, and the advice works just as well across a negotiating table.

Tip: fifteen minutes, American accent, clear pace. Watch once with English subtitles, then again without, and note the verbs she uses to say what people should stop doing.

Watch on TED ↗
Video
Steve Jobs: “Focusing is about saying no” (WWDC 1997)

Three minutes of a founder explaining what his company stopped doing, and why stopping was the hardest decision. It is the Stop, Start, Keep conversation from this lesson, told by somebody who had to do it in public.

Tip: listen for the sentences with stop and keep. Then answer the same question about Dataside out loud: what did you stop doing to focus?
