Lesson 11 -- Pre-class

Saying What You Mean -- Decisions, Suggestions and the Next Step

After a session with a buyer, the first question is always what you agreed to. The verb after the verb decides whether it was a promise or a conversation. Key words: to restructure, to proceed, to disclose, to facilitate, to align, to execute, to finalize, to commit to. Structure: verbs followed by to or -ing.

To disclose -- to give information that was private until now
"I refused to disclose the client list before exclusivity."
To facilitate -- to make a process easier for other people to move through
"I will facilitate a call with their counsel on Tuesday."
To align -- to get people to agree on the same goal or the same position
"I want to align the board before Thursday."
To execute -- to carry out a plan that has already been decided
"We decide what to execute after that call, not before."
To finalize -- to complete the last details, so that nothing is open any more
"Nobody finalizes governance in week two."
To commit to -- to promise that you will definitely do something
"I will commit to sending the retention numbers by Monday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

After a session with a buyer, a good adviser asks one question before anything else: what did you agree to? The answer sounds simple, and it usually is not. In English, the difference between a promise and a conversation often sits in one small word — the word that comes after the verb.

Some verbs look forward, to an action that has not happened yet. Decisions, plans, offers and refusals work like this, and they take to: we agreed to proceed, they decided to wait, he refused to disclose the figures, they failed to send the file. Other verbs name the activity itself, and they take -ing: he suggested restructuring the earnout, we avoid discussing price, I recommend waiting, you risk losing the deal. After a preposition it is always -ing, and that includes the to in commit to: I commit to sending the numbers.

A small group of verbs change their meaning. I stopped to check the figures means I paused in order to check them. I stopped checking the figures means I no longer check them. Remember to send it is a task in front of you; I remember sending it is a memory behind you.

The most common mistake for Portuguese speakers is I suggest to restructure, because the Portuguese sentence has a clear place for the infinitive. In English, suggest and recommend never take to + verb. And the permanent target survives here too: he suggests, she refuses — the first verb still takes its -s.

1. According to the text, what kind of verbs take to?
A Verbs that describe something in the past.
B Verbs that look forward, like decisions, plans, offers and refusals.
C Verbs that come after a preposition.
2. What is the difference between "I stopped to check" and "I stopped checking"?
A The first means I paused in order to check; the second means I no longer check.
B There is no difference; both are correct and mean the same.
C The first is formal and the second is informal.
3. Why does the text say "I suggest to restructure" is a common mistake?
A Because restructure is the wrong word in that sentence.
B Because suggest always needs a person after it.
C Because the Portuguese sentence has a place for the infinitive, but suggest never takes to + verb.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Verbs Followed by To or -ing

GRAMMAR

To looks forward to a decision. -ing names the activity itself.

JobVerbsExample
Decisions, plans, promisesdecide, agree, plan, expect, refuse, offer, fail, manage + toWe decided to proceed.
Suggestions, avoidance, risksuggest, recommend, avoid, consider, risk, keep, delay + -ingI recommend restructuring it.
After a prepositionalways -ingI commit to sending it by Monday.
Stopstop to = pause in order to · stop -ing = quitI stopped to check. / I stopped checking.
Rememberremember to = a task · remember -ing = a memoryRemember to send it. / I remember sending it.
Negativenot goes before the second verbWe decided not to proceed. I suggest not disclosing it.
Questionthe second verb keeps its formDid they agree to proceed? Have you considered restructuring it?
The permanent targetthe first verb keeps its -sHe suggests restructuring. Not he suggest.

Never: I suggest to restructure · we decided proceeding · I commit to send · he avoid discussing
