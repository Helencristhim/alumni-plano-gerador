Chapter 3: Decision or Activity

To, or -ing?

The word after the verb says whether anybody promised anything

Grammar Discovery

What Comes After the Verb?

"We agreed to proceed with the full due diligence."

"He only suggested restructuring the earnout."

"I refused to disclose the client list, and we will avoid discussing price."

"I stopped to write down his words."  /  "I stopped calling the board at night."

The red ones point forward, to an action that has not happened yet. The orange ones name the activity itself. And in the last line, the same verb means two different things.

JobVerbsExample
Decisions, plans, promisesdecide, agree, plan, expect, refuse, offer, fail, manage + toWe decided to proceed.
Suggestions, avoidance, risksuggest, recommend, avoid, consider, risk, keep, delay, finish + -ingI recommend restructuring the earnout.
After a prepositionalways -ing — even after to in commit toI commit to sending it by Monday.
Meaning changes: stopstop to = pause in order to · stop -ing = quitI stopped to check / I stopped checking.
Meaning changes: rememberremember to = a task ahead · remember -ing = a memoryRemember to send it. / I remember sending it.
Meaning changes: try, regrettry to = make an effort · try -ing = test it · regret to say = bad newsI regret to say the price does not work.
The trapsuggest and recommend never take to + verbNot I suggest to restructure. Say I suggest restructuring.
The permanent targetthe first verb still takes the -sHe suggests restructuring. She refuses to disclose.

In one line: to looks forward to a decision; -ing names the activity.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

What Did You Agree To?

One debrief call, two recordings and one short email

Dialogue

A Decision, or a Conversation?

M
Before I call their side back, I need one thing from you. What did you agree to in there, exactly?
C
We agreed to proceed with the full due diligence. But I refused to disclose the client list before exclusivity is signed.
M
That was the right call. And the earnout? Did Grant offer to change it, or did he only suggest looking at it?
C
He suggested restructuring it. He did not offer to put a number on it, and he kept coming back to the retention of the engineers.
M
Then it is a conversation, not a proposal. We avoid discussing the earnout until they put a number in writing. What are you planning to do before Thursday?
C
I want to align the board first. I started calling them last night, but I stopped to write down what Grant had said, and then it was too late.
M
Remember to send me those notes tonight. And stop apologizing for the delay. They delayed answering us for three weeks.
C
Fair. Should I risk finalizing the governance terms this week, while they are still interested?
M
No. Nobody finalizes governance in week two. I will facilitate a call with their counsel on Tuesday, and we decide what to execute after that call, not before.
C
Then I will commit to one thing only: sending the retention numbers by Monday.
M
Good. That is a promise you can keep. Everything else, we consider, and we let them wait.
Comprehension

About Meredith

1. What does Meredith want to know before she calls the other side back?
What Caio agreed to in the meeting, exactly.
2. What does she decide to avoid, and until when?
Discussing the earnout, until the buyer puts a number in writing.
3. What does she offer to do on Tuesday, and why not finalize governance now?
She will facilitate a call with their counsel. Nobody finalizes governance in week two — they decide what to execute after that call.
Listening 1 · A Deal Adviser

Listen to the Verbs

A deal adviser on what he listens for in a negotiation. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he listen to more than the numbers, and why?
The verbs, because the verbs tell him what each side has actually promised.
2. What is the difference between the two sentences about the deadline?
Agreed to extend the deadline is a decision. Discussed extending the deadline is only a conversation, and nobody has to honor a conversation.
3. What does he say the word consider really is?
Not a promise. It is a polite way of delaying a decision.
Listening 2 · An Executive Coach

The Phrase She Bans

An executive coach on promises, and on apologies. Sound first — no text.

1. Which phrase does she ban, and why?
I will try to. Because nobody on the other side of the table can do anything with it.
2. What does a buyer hear when you say you will try to send the numbers by Friday?
That he will not get them by Friday — and he plans for that.
3. What do senior people do instead of apologizing for a delay?
They explain what they decided to do, and when — for example, we decided to wait for the auditor.
Real Document

Where We Stand, in Four Lines

Subject: Where we stand after today
From: Meredith Cole · To: Caio Amante

Caio,

Short version, so we are both working from the same list.

1. Keller agreed to proceed with full due diligence from Monday. Nothing else was agreed.

2. Grant suggested restructuring the earnout, but he did not offer to put a number on it. Until he does, we avoid discussing it.

3. You refused to disclose the client list before exclusivity. Keep refusing.

4. Please remember to send me your notes tonight, and commit to nothing else in writing before we speak on Tuesday.

Meredith

Every verb in this email is followed by either a decision or an activity. Only one line contains an agreement.
1. What is the only thing the buyer actually agreed to?
To proceed with full due diligence from Monday. Nothing else was agreed.
2. Why does she want to avoid discussing the earnout?
Because Grant only suggested restructuring it — he did not offer to put a number on it.
3. What does she ask Caio to remember, and what should he not commit to?
To send her his notes tonight. He should commit to nothing else in writing before they speak on Tuesday.
