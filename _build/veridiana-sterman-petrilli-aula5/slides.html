Warm-up

The Last Time You Did Not Ask

First, your short version of last week’s story. Then: think of one time you wanted something and said nothing. What was it, and why did you stay quiet?

The Target

Three Parts of a Request That Works

1. A question, not a statement. A statement leaves the other person nowhere to go.

2. A short reason. One line, so it stops being a favour and becomes a problem to solve.

3. A friendly answer to no. It costs you nothing, and it is what gets you the call back.

Chapter 3: The Code

Four Ways to Ask, and One to Avoid

The distance you put between yourself and the request

Grammar Discovery

Four Ways to Ask for One Thing

"I need a late check-out."

"Could I have a late check-out?"

"Would you mind holding the room until two?"

"I was wondering if there was any chance of a late check-out."

All four ask for the same thing. The first one is red because it is not a question at all. Look at how the blue openings get longer, and at the shape of the verb after each one.

JobFormExample
Ask for a thingCould I have + noun?Could I have a spare key?
Ask somebody to do somethingCould you + bare verb?Could you double-check the name?
Ask for a small inconvenienceWould you mind + verb-ing?Would you mind swapping seats?
The most careful of allI was wondering if + could / there wasI was wondering if you could hold the room.
Softening a yes-or-no questionIs there any chance of + noun?Is there any chance of an upgrade?
Answering would you mindNo = I am happy to. Yes = it is a problem."Would you mind waiting?" — "No, not at all."
The trapnever would you mind to; and the past in I was wondering is distance, not timeNot would you mind to wait. Say would you mind waiting.

In one line: the longer the opening, the softer the request — and after would you mind the verb always ends in -ing.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Four Requests in Two Minutes

Dialogue

The Morning You Check Out

P
Good morning. Checking out today?
V
We are, yes. I was wondering if there was any chance of a late check-out. Our flight is at nine in the evening.
P
I am afraid not today. The hotel is full and the room goes to somebody at three.
V
That is fine, thank you. Then could you keep our bags here until the evening? And do you charge for that?
P
No charge at all. Leave them with me. Anything else?
V
Two small things. Would you mind double-checking the name on the taxi booking? And do you have a spare map of the bus lines? It would be really handy today.
P
Of course. The name is right, and here is the map. Actually, one moment — there is a shower room by the pool. It is for guests who are leaving late. Would you like the code?
V
That is exactly the kind of thing I never think to ask for. Yes, please. And thank you for the favor.
Comprehension

About Paul

1. Why does he say no to the late check-out?
The hotel is full, and the room goes to somebody else at three.
2. What does he say about keeping the bags, and about the price?
He tells her to leave them with him, and there is no charge at all.
3. What does he offer at the end that nobody asked for, and who is it for?
The code for the shower room by the pool. It is for guests who are leaving late.
Listening 1 · A Hotel Trainer

Why One Guest Gets It and the Other Does Not

Fifteen years of training hotel staff, in three rules. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the difference between the two sentences he compares, and why does it matter to him?
"I need a late check-out" is a statement; "is there any chance of a late check-out" is a question, and a question leaves him somewhere to go.
2. Why does he say a short reason changes the request?
Because now he is solving a problem instead of doing a favour.
3. What does he say about the guest who accepts the first no?
That is the guest he calls back twenty minutes later, when a room becomes free.
Listening 2 · An English Teacher

The Question Brazilians Answer Backwards

One question, and the answer that means the opposite. Sound first — no text.

1. If you are happy to wait, what is the correct answer to "Would you mind waiting five minutes?"
No. The question is really asking whether it is a problem for you, and you are saying it is not.
2. What does she say you tell the other person if you answer yes?
That you do mind, and that waiting is a problem.
3. What is the one form she says you have to learn by heart?
After would you mind, the verb always ends in -ing. Never would you mind to open.

Chapter 6: Your Turn

Now You Ask

Guided > Semi-free > Free

Role-Play 1 of 3 — Guided

Four Requests, Four Forms

Scenario: Check-out morning, evening flight. Ask for a late check-out, ask about luggage storage and the price, ask somebody to double-check the taxi booking, and ask for a spare map. Use a different form for each one, and give a short reason every time.

Keyword chips:

Could I have...? Could you...? Would you mind...? I was wondering if...
Role-Play 2 of 3 — Semi-free

Three Times No

Scenario: Everything you ask for is unavailable today. Ask for three different things, accept each no in a friendly way, and keep going. Finish by asking one open question: what can you do for us?

Keywords:

That is fine, thank you. an upgrade in advance What can you do for us?
Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

Sixty Seconds, No Notes

Scenario: You and your mother are checking in for ten nights. Ask for five things you would genuinely want on that trip, each one with a short reason, and each one in a different form. Nothing on this list has to be reasonable.

No keywords, no notes, sixty seconds. Five requests, five reasons.
