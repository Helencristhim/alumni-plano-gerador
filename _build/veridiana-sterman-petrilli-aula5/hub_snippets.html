Asking for What You Need -- The Question That Gets a Yes

Two guests ask for the same thing thirty seconds apart, and only one of them gets it. Key words: a favor, an upgrade, spare, to swap, a request, to charge, handy, to be short of, in advance, to double-check. Structure: polite requests with could, would you mind and I was wondering if.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A favor -- something you ask somebody to do for you, out of kindness
"Could I ask you a favor? It will take one minute."
An upgrade -- a better room or seat than the one you paid for
"They gave us an upgrade because the hotel was quiet that week."
Spare -- extra, and not being used by anybody right now
"Do you have a spare key, by any chance?"
To swap -- to change one thing for another, with somebody else
"Would you mind swapping seats, so we can sit together?"
A request -- something you ask for, in a polite and clear way
"I have one small request, and then I will leave you in peace."
To charge -- to ask somebody for money for a service
"Do you charge for that, or is it included?"
Handy -- useful, and close enough to use straight away
"A map of the bus lines would be really handy."
To be short of -- to not have enough of something
"We are a bit short of time, so could we do this quickly?"
In advance -- before the moment it is needed, and not at the last minute
"I am asking now, in advance, so you have time to organise it."
To double-check -- to check something a second time, to be completely sure
"Could you double-check the name on the reservation?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Two guests ask for the same thing at the same desk, thirty seconds apart, and only one of them gets it. The difference is almost never the vocabulary. It is that the first one said "I need a late check-out" and the second one asked a question. A statement leaves the other person nowhere to go. A question does.

English has four sizes of question, and the longer the opening, the softer the request. For a thing: "Could I have a spare key?" For an action: "Could you double-check the name?" Note that after could the verb is bare — never could you to check. For something that costs the other person a little trouble: "Would you mind swapping seats?" And after would you mind, the verb always ends in -ing. Never would you mind to swap. That is the one form to learn by heart.

The softest of all is the longest: "I was wondering if you could hold the room until two." The past form here is not about time — it is distance, and distance is what makes it polite. The same works with "Is there any chance of an upgrade?"

Two more things, and they are worth more than the grammar. Give one short reason: "our flight is at nine in the evening", "we are short of time". A reason turns a favor into a problem somebody can solve. And when the answer is no, say "that is fine, thank you" and stay friendly. It sounds like it costs you the thing you wanted; in fact it is what gets you the call back twenty minutes later. One last trap: if somebody asks would you mind waiting and you are happy to wait, the answer is no. Not yes.

1. Why does "I need a late check-out" work less well than a question?
A Because need is an impolite verb in English.
B Because a statement leaves the other person nowhere to go, and a question does not.

2. What shape does the verb take after would you mind?
A The -ing form: would you mind swapping.
B The infinitive with to: would you mind to swap.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Polite Requests

GRAMMAR

The longer the opening, the softer the request. And after would you mind, always -ing.

JobFormExample
Ask for a thingCould I have + noun?Could I have a spare key?
Ask somebody to do somethingCould you + bare verb?Could you double-check the name?
Ask for a small inconvenienceWould you mind + verb-ing?Would you mind swapping seats?
The most careful of allI was wondering if + could / there wasI was wondering if you could hold the room.
Softening a yes-or-no questionIs there any chance of + noun?Is there any chance of an upgrade?
Answering would you mindNo = I am happy to. Yes = it is a problem."Would you mind waiting?" — "No, not at all."
Accepting a noThat is fine, thank you.And then ask something else, in the same tone.

Never: would you mind to hold · could you to check · "Would you mind waiting?" — "Yes, no problem" · I need a late check-out

Stage 2: Put the Request in Order

Order

It is check-out morning and your flight is in the evening. Put what you say in a logical order.

?"Good morning. We are checking out today."
?"I was wondering if there was any chance of a late check-out. Our flight is at nine."
?"That is fine, thank you. Then could you keep our bags here until the evening?"
?"And do you charge for that, or is it included?"
?"Would you mind double-checking the name on the taxi booking?"
?"Thank you. That is everything, and you have been very kind."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

Check-out is at eleven and your flight is at nine in the evening. You say:
A "I need a late check-out, my flight is at nine."
B "I was wondering if there was any chance of a late check-out. Our flight is at nine in the evening."
C "Would you mind to give me a late check-out?"
You want the person next to you to change seats with you. You say:
A "Would you mind swapping seats, so we can sit together?"
B "Would you mind to swap seats, so we can sit together?"
C "You swap seats with me, so we sit together."
The receptionist says the hotel is full and there is no late check-out. You say:
A "But I already asked yesterday and nobody told me."
B "That is fine, thank you. Then could you keep our bags here until the evening?"
C "Are you sure? Could you check again, please, and again after that?"
"Would you mind waiting five minutes while I check?" You are happy to wait. You answer:
A "Yes, of course, no problem."
B "No, not at all. Take your time."
C "Yes, I do not mind at all."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

You and your mother are checking in for ten nights. In sixty seconds, ask for five things you would genuinely want on that trip. Each one with a short reason, and each one in a different form. Nothing on the list has to be reasonable.
