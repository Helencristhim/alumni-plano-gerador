Who You Are, and What You Actually Do

Key words: a background, to be responsible for, to handle, to deal with, currently, to be based in, to report to, a shareholder, crop protection, a grower, a dealer, a wide range of. Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A background -- the studies and the jobs a person had before the job they have now
"My background is in numbers, but this job is mostly about people."
To be responsible for -- to be the person who has to make sure something gets done, and who explains it if it does not
"I am responsible for the farms and the dealers in this region."
To handle -- to take care of a task or a difficult situation yourself, from beginning to end
"My colleague handles the north region and I handle the south."
To deal with -- to have regular working contact with a person or a company as part of your job
"I deal with about two hundred customers, from the smallest to the largest."
Currently -- at this moment, in a situation that is true now and may change later
"I am currently the only analyst for this region."
To be based in -- to have your office, your factory or your home in a particular place
"The plant is based in the interior, and the board meets in the capital."
To report to -- to have a particular person as your direct manager, the one you answer to
"I report to the finance director, and she reports to the board."
A shareholder -- a person or a company that owns part of a business and expects it to do well
"The shareholders visit the site once or twice a year."
Crop protection -- the products that keep a farm’s plants safe from insects, weeds and disease
"We make crop protection products, so our customers are farms and the businesses that sell to farms."
A grower -- a farm business that produces crops on a large scale, as a company and not as a hobby
"A grower pays us after the harvest, not every month."
A dealer -- a local company that buys from the factory and sells to the farms nearby
"I deal with a dealer who opened last spring and with growers who have been here for thirty years."
A wide range of -- a group that goes from one extreme to the other, with everything in between
"We cover a wide range of customers: family farms, local dealers and large groups."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A member of the board lands once or twice a year, walks in with a coffee, and asks the same four words: so, what do you do? The answer starts with what is true every single week. He works in credit and collections. He is responsible for the growers and the dealers in one region, and he deals with about two hundred customers. Nothing there is finished, so the verb stays in its base form. Only the third person is different: his colleague handles the north, and a grower pays after the harvest. That small -s is the one thing English asks for in return.

Then the visitor asks the second question, and the verb changes shape. How did you end up in credit? He studied business, he started in customer service, and he moved into credit two years later. Each of those happened once and is over, so each verb carries the past form. The clue is usually next to it: in 2011, two years later, before that.

The rest of the answer follows the same two rules. The company makes crop protection products and is based in the interior. He reports to the finance director and is currently the only analyst in the region, which is true today and may change next year. He covers a wide range of customers, from a family farm to a group with an office in the capital, and his background is in numbers even though the job turned out to be about people. One warning for a Portuguese speaker: the past mark goes on ONE part of the verb only. Not Where did you studied?, but Where did you study?

1. "He works in credit and collections." Why the base form and not "he worked"?
A Because "work" is a verb that has no past form.
B Because it describes what is true every week, not something that finished.
C Because the sentence has no time expression in it.
2. Why is it "his colleague handles the north" and not "his colleague handle the north"?
A Because in the present the third person adds an -s to the verb.
B Because "the north" is singular.
C Because "handle" is an irregular verb.
3. The text warns against "Where did you studied?". What is the problem?
A "Where" cannot start a question about the past.
B The past is marked twice. "Did" already carries it, so the second verb stays in its base form.
C "Study" cannot be used in a question.
