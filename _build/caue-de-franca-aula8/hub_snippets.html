Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A habit -- something you do regularly, often without thinking
"Listening in the car became a habit."
To stick to -- to continue doing something you decided to do
"It’s easy to start a plan and hard to stick to it."
Consistency -- doing something in the same way, regularly, over time
"Consistency matters more than intensity."
A streak -- a number of days in a row when you do something
"My streak on the app is 200 days."
An episode -- one program in a series of programs
"Each episode is about twenty minutes."
A host -- the person who presents a podcast or a show
"The two hosts have very different accents."
To subscribe -- to sign up to receive something regularly
"I subscribed to two podcasts in English."
On the go -- while you are busy or moving from one place to another
"I listen on the go: in the car, at the gym."
To make time for -- to find time for something important, even when you are busy
"You have to make time for practice."
To fall behind -- to not keep up with the pace you planned
"I fell behind last week, but I started again."
Bite-sized -- small and easy to deal with
"Bite-sized episodes are perfect for the commute."
To tune in -- to start listening to or watching a program
"Thousands of people tune in every Monday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Marina used to scroll the news on her commute. Every morning she would read the same headlines. She wanted a new habit, so she subscribed to a podcast with bite-sized episodes.

At first, she wasn’t used to the speed of the hosts. But she stuck to it, ten minutes a day, and kept a streak. Some weeks she fell behind, and she started again.

Today she is used to listening on the go, and she tunes in to three shows. “Consistency is the secret,” she says. “You have to make time for it.”

1. What does “Marina used to scroll the news” mean?
A Scrolling the news is normal for her, and she still does it every day on the train.
B She is getting used to scrolling the news, little by little, every morning.
C She scrolled the news in the past, but she does not do it on the train now.
2. What helped Marina when she fell behind?
A She started again the next day.
B She changed to longer episodes, because short ones were too easy for her level.
C She stopped listening for a month and then subscribed to a new podcast.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “She is used to listen on the go.”
B “She is used to listening on the go.”
C “She used to listening on the go every day now, on the train.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used To / Would x Be Used To / Get Used To

GRAMMAR

Used to + verb talks about the past: a habit or a state that is not true now. Be used to and get used to talk about something that is normal, or becoming normal, and they take a noun or -ing.

JobFormExample
An old habit or stateused to + verb (not now)I used to scroll the news.
A repeated past actionwould + verb (actions, not states)Every morning she would read the headlines.
Negative and questiondidn’t use to / did you use toI didn’t use to listen to podcasts.
It is normal for mebe used to + noun or -ingI’m used to listening on the go.
It is becoming normalget used to + noun or -ingI’m getting used to the speed.
The trapafter be / get used to, -ing, not the verbNot I’m used to listen. Say I’m used to listening.

Never: I didn’t used to · I’m used to listen · I would live near the beach (a state) · I’m used to watch dubbed movies (= I used to)
