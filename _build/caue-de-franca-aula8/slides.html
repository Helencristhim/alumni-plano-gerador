Chapter 4: The Code

Used to or Used to It?

Old habits and new normals

Grammar Discovery

Used to or Used to It?

"Marina used to scroll the news on her commute."

"Every morning, she would read the same headlines three times."

"At first, she wasn’t used to the speed of the hosts."

"After a month, she got used to listening without subtitles."

The red verbs talk about the past: something she did again and again, and does not do now. The blue ones are different: be used to and get used to. Is that about the past, or about something feeling normal? And what comes after them: a verb, a noun, or -ing?

JobFormExample
An old habit or stateused to + verb (not now)I used to scroll the news.
A repeated past actionwould + verb (actions, not states)Every morning she would read the headlines.
Negative and questiondidn’t use to / did you use toI didn’t use to listen to podcasts.
It is normal for mebe used to + noun or -ingI’m used to listening on the go.
It is becoming normalget used to + noun or -ingI’m getting used to the speed.
The trapafter be / get used to, -ing, not the verbNot I’m used to listen. Say I’m used to listening.

In one line: used to + verb = the past, not now; be / get used to + -ing = normal now, or becoming normal.

Listening 1 · A Podcast Host

What My Listeners Tell Me

A British podcast host talks about the listening habits of his audience. Sound first — no text.

1. Where do most of his listeners tune in?
On the go: in the car, on the train, at the gym.
2. What did he use to think about long episodes?
That longer was better.
3. Why did he change to shorter episodes?
Because listeners told him short episodes were easier to stick to.
Listening 2 · A Night-Shift Nurse

The Habit After the Shift

A nurse who works at night talks about the podcasts on her way home. Sound first — no text.

1. What did she use to do on the bus after work?
She used to sleep, or try to.
2. What is she used to now?
Listening to a podcast on the bus home.
3. What was hard to get used to at first?
The speed of the hosts and their accents.
Listening 3 · A Habit Coach

Don’t Break the Chain

An American habit coach talks about streaks, and what to do when you fall behind. Sound first — no text.

1. Why do streaks work, according to her?
Because you do not want to lose the number you have built.
2. What is the problem with streaks?
When people break one, they often give up completely.
3. What is her two-day rule?
You can miss one day, but never two days in a row.
