Chapter 4: The Code

More Sensitive or the Most Accurate?

Comparatives and superlatives

Grammar Discovery

Two Things or a Group?

"Dermoscopy was much more sensitive than the naked eye."

"Biopsy is the most accurate test available."

"Specificity was slightly lower with the naked eye."

"This was the largest study of the three."

Look at the red words, then at the blue ones. Which ones compare two things? Which ones choose one from a group? What do much and slightly add?

JobFormExample
Two things, short adjectiveadjective + -er + thanSpecificity was lower than expected.
Two things, long adjectivemore / less + adjective + thanDermoscopy is more sensitive than the naked eye.
How big the difference ismuch / far / slightly + comparativemuch higher · slightly lower
One from a groupthe + -est / the most + adjectiveBiopsy is the most accurate test.
Equal, or times biggeras + adjective + as · twice as many + noun + asIt missed twice as many melanomas as dermoscopy.
Irregulargood → better → the best · bad → worse → the worstThe new device is better, but not the best.

In one line: than compares two things; the + most / -est chooses one from a group; much, far and slightly tell how big the difference is.

Listening 1 · A Step 3 Tutor

Sensitive or Specific?

A tutor on the two numbers that every biostatistics question uses. Sound first — no text.

1. What does a very sensitive test do with a negative result?
It helps you rule the disease out, because it rarely misses a patient who has it.
2. When is a specific test most useful?
When the result is positive: it helps you rule the disease in.
3. What happens to false positives when the prevalence is low?
There are more false positives for every true case.
Listening 2 · A Resident

The Study I Almost Believed

A resident on a study that looked better than it was. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the new app claim?
That it was more accurate than dermatologists at finding melanoma.
2. Why were the numbers less impressive than they looked?
The study was small, the confidence interval was wide, and almost half of the lesions were melanomas.
3. What does he do now before he believes a result?
He looks at the size of the study and at the prevalence.
