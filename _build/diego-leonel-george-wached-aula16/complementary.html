Hans Rosling -- "The best stats you've ever seen" (TED)

A Swedish doctor and statistician makes world health data come alive, comparing countries and decades on one moving chart. It is full of comparatives and superlatives, said at a clear speed.

Tip: listen once without the video. The second time, write down five comparisons he makes, with the modifier he uses.

Talk
Peter Donnelly -- "How juries are fooled by statistics" (TED)

An Oxford statistician shows how easy it is to misread probabilities, with a real case in which a mistake about numbers had terrible consequences. The same mistakes appear in Step 3 questions.

Tip: pause at his example about the test for a rare disease and explain it out loud in English, with the word prevalence.

Talk
Ben Goldacre -- "Battling bad science" (TED)

A British doctor explains how studies can look better than they are: small samples, missing data and headlines that say more than the numbers. It is the journal club skill of this lesson.

Tip: write down two reasons he gives for not trusting a result, and say them using a comparative.
