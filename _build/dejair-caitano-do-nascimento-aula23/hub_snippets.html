Closing Gaps -- Direct When You Know, Careful When You Don't

The last targeted lesson before Bangkok: no silence under pressure, the right amount of certainty, and a long answer that keeps its thread. Key words: weak spot, to hesitate, filler, thread, confident, straightforward, to overstate, to undersell. Structure: boosters -- clearly, definitely, strongly suggest, there is no doubt that.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These are words to talk about how you speak: your weak spots, your strong points and the thread of your answer.

Weak spot -- a part of something that is not as good or strong as the rest
"Fast questions are still my weak spot."
To hesitate -- to stop for a moment before you speak, because you are not sure
"Try not to hesitate: start with a short phrase."
Filler -- a short word like well or so that gives you time to think
"Use a filler like well, not a long silence."
Thread -- the main idea that connects all the parts of a talk or an answer
"In a long answer, don't lose the thread."
Confident -- sure that you can do something well
"I feel much more confident in English than in July."
Straightforward -- easy to understand, not complicated
"It's a straightforward question, so give a short answer."
To overstate -- to say that something is bigger or more important than it really is
"With sixty patients, don't overstate your results."
To undersell -- to make something seem less good or less important than it really is
"Your data are strong. Don't undersell your finding."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Rafael is a pharmacologist from Manaus. At a congress in Lima, he presented a study with four hundred patients in six hospitals. His data were strong, so he was direct: "The new test clearly detects nerve damage earlier. There is no doubt that it is faster than the old one." Then somebody asked about children. Rafael had only fifteen children in the study, so he was careful: "It might work for children too, but we don't know yet." After the talk, an older colleague said: "Good job. You didn't overstate, and you didn't undersell. Your data strongly suggest the test works, and you said exactly that." Rafael felt confident for the rest of the congress.
Why was Rafael direct about the new test?
A Because his data were strong: four hundred patients in six hospitals
B Because he wanted to impress the older colleague, so he used the strongest words he knew for every question in the session
C Because he did not have time to explain
Why did Rafael say "It might work for children"?
A Because "might" is the strongest word for a result, and he wanted to show that the test is very good for children
B Because the children did not like the test
C Because he had only fifteen children in the study, so he was not sure

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Boosters: Strong Words for Strong Data

GRAMMAR

A booster makes a claim stronger. It is the opposite of a hedge. Use it when you can back up the claim with data.

adverb before the main verbclearly / definitely / strongly + verbThe drug clearly works. Our data strongly suggest that...
adverb after bebe + clearly / definitelyThe effect is definitely real.
at the startThere is no doubt that... / It is clear that...There is no doubt that the damage can continue.
questionIs it clear that...? / Are you sure that...?Is it clear that the dose matters?
the hedge (Lesson 16)might / may / could + verbIt might be lower in older patients.

The rule: boost what you can back up, hedge what you can't. Not: "works clearly" -- but: "clearly works".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
You tested three hundred patients in five centers, and the result is very clear. What do you say?
A "Maybe, perhaps, it is possible that the test might help some patients, but I am not really sure about it."
B "The test clearly helps most patients."
C "The test is perfect for everybody."
You have a small pilot study with twelve patients. What do you say?
A "Our first results suggest that it might help, but it is preliminary."
B "There is no doubt that this drug works for every patient with neuropathic pain in the world."
C "It's done."
Somebody asks you a fast question, and you need a moment to think. What do you say?
A Nothing. You wait in silence for ten or fifteen seconds and think about the answer in Portuguese first.
B "Next."
C "Well, that's a fair question. Let me think for a second."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Two minutes. A researcher in Bangkok asks you: "What did you find about leprosy, nerve damage and pain, and what does it mean for treatment?" Answer without notes. Keep the thread: the context, the problem, what you did, what you found and what it means. Boost what is clear, and hedge what you are not sure about. No script -- just speak.
