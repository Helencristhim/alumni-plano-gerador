Chapter 3: The Code

Strong Words for Strong Data

The other side of hedging

Grammar Discovery

How Sure Is the Speaker?

The drug clearly works for most patients.
The effect is definitely real.
Our data strongly suggest that early treatment helps.
There is no doubt that nerve damage can continue after the cure.
The effect might be lower in older patients.
Click to reveal the rule
The orange words in the first four sentences are boosters: they make a claim stronger. Use them when your data are strong. Position: before the main verb (clearly works, strongly suggest) or after be (is definitely real). At the start of the sentence: There is no doubt that... / It is clear that... The last sentence is a hedge (Lesson 16): use it when you are not sure. The rule of the room: boost what you can back up, hedge what you can't. A booster without data overstates; a hedge on strong data undersells.

Panel, Room 3

Strong, but Honest

A
Dr. Ivanova, your talk was clear, but the room has a lot of questions. Let's start with the most direct one: does the drug work?
O
Clearly, yes, for most patients. In our study, the pain went down in sixty-five percent of the group.
A
Sixty-five percent. That's a strong number. Is it the same for older patients?
O
That I'm not sure about. It might be lower. We only had twenty patients over seventy.
A
So you're saying the result is strong in general, but weaker for older patients?
O
Exactly. I don't want to overstate it. But I don't want to undersell it either: the effect is definitely real.
A
One more question from the audience. Someone is asking about side effects.
O
Good question. There were some, mostly sleepiness, and they probably depend on the dose.
A
Thank you. We're running late, so one last sentence, please.
O
To sum up: the drug clearly works, but we need a bigger study in older patients.
Comprehension

About Olga

1. In what percentage of the group did the pain go down?
In sixty-five percent.
2. What is Olga not sure about, and why?
