Chapter 3: Twenty Minutes Before

The Moderator Tests You

Daniel asks the question the investors will ask

Dialogue

Before the Panel

D
Amanda, thanks for coming early. The panel starts in twenty minutes, and you are the second of three speakers.
A
Thank you, Daniel. How long do I have to outline our position?
D
Four minutes, and then questions. Most of the audience are investors, so they will ask about integrity.
A
Then I will address that directly. The evidence suggests that buyers are becoming more careful.
D
Good. Let me try a hard one. Are Brazilian credits reliable or not?
A
Most of them are likely to be robust, but some projects may have weak baselines. We should be honest about that.
D
They will also ask about the new rules. Is there a loophole?
A
There seems to be one in how land use is counted. If nobody closes it, it could weaken the pledges made last year.
D
And who loses if nobody closes it? I want a real answer, not a slogan.
A
Local stakeholders, in most cases. That is why we need rules that mitigate the risk on the ground, not only on paper.

Chapter 4: The Code

Sure, but Not Too Sure

how a careful claim is built

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The evidence suggests that buyers are becoming more careful."

"Most of them are likely to be robust."

"Some projects may have weak baselines."

"There seems to be a loophole in how land use is counted."

Is the speaker completely sure? Which words make each claim softer, and why use them on a panel?

StructureWhat it saysExample
may / might / could + verba possible fact, not a certain oneSome credits may not be additional.
be likely to / be unlikely to + verba forecast: what you expectPrices are likely to rise next year.
seem to / appear to + verbyour impression from what you seeBuyers seem to be more careful.
there seems to be / there appears to besomething exists, probablyThere seems to be a loophole.
the data / the evidence suggests thatwhat the numbers point toThe data suggests that demand is growing.
tend to + verb / in most caseswhat usually happens, not alwaysProjects tend to take longer than planned.
One hedge per claimYour main point has no hedge. Your evidence has one. Two in the same sentence ("it might possibly be") sound like fear.
Practice

Make It Careful

Say it first, then click to check

"Prices / likely / rise" → Prices ___are likely to rise next year.
"Buyers / seem / be more careful" → Buyers ___seem to be more careful this year.
"The data / suggest / demand is growing" → The data ___suggests that demand is growing.
"Some credits / may / not be real" → Some credits ___may not be real.
"Projects / tend / take longer" → Projects ___tend to take longer than planned.

Sound Check

Say It Clearly

weak hedge, strong information · the sentences of this lesson

Why this one is hard for a Portuguese speaker

The habit: giving every word the same weight, so that the careful words sound as loud as the facts, and the main point gets lost.

In English: the hedge is short and weak, and the stress goes on the information: LIKEly to RISE, may have WEAK BASElines. Before your main point, make a short pause.

The test: say "prices are likely to rise" with only two strong words.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

The Briefing and the Coach

How the professionals say what they are not sure of

Listening 1

The Press Briefing

A negotiator talks to the press during the negotiations. Sound first, no text.

1. Why does she say she will be brief?
Because the negotiations are still going on.
2. According to her, what does the evidence suggest about buyers?
That they will not return to the market until the rules are robust.
3. What does she say about the finance pledges, and what should we expect on Thursday?
They have not been fully delivered. There may be new announcements on Thursday, but she would not expect large numbers.
Listening 2

Sure Enough

A speaking coach on how lawyers should sound at a forum. Sound first, no text.

1. What are the two mistakes lawyers make at an international forum?
Sounding too certain, and hedging everything.
2. In the four-part structure, where does hedging belong?
In the evidence. The point and the recommendation should be clear.
3. What does he say about two hedges in the same sentence?
They sound like fear. One hedge for each claim is enough.
