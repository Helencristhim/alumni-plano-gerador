Careful Claims at COP

Four minutes on a COP panel, in front of investors. Key words: to address, stakeholder, to outline, pledge, to mitigate, loophole, integrity, robust. Structure: hedging -- may, be likely to, seem to, the evidence suggests that.

Stage 1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Four words for the room, four for the argument. Listen to each one and read it in context.

to address -- to speak formally to a group of people, or to deal with a problem
"Amanda will address the panel on Tuesday morning."
stakeholder -- a person or a group with an interest in a project or a decision
"Local communities are key stakeholders in any carbon project."
to outline -- to describe the main points of something, without the details
"I will outline our position in four minutes."
pledge -- a formal public promise, often made by a country or a company
"Many pledges made at COP are never fully delivered."
to mitigate -- to make something bad less serious or less harmful
"Better rules can mitigate the risk for local communities."
loophole -- a weakness in a rule that lets people avoid it legally
"A loophole in the old rules allowed the same credit to be counted twice."
integrity -- the quality of being honest and reliable; in a market, being trustworthy
"Without integrity, the carbon market will lose its buyers."
robust -- strong and reliable; able to resist being questioned
"We need robust data before we make that claim in public."

Stage 3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about what it says.

On a panel at an international forum, a lawyer has a problem. If she sounds completely sure, somebody in the room will ask her to prove it. If she sounds unsure about everything, the room stops listening. The answer is a small group of words that make a claim careful without making it weak.

For a possibility, use may, might or could: some projects may have weak baselines. For a forecast, use be likely to: prices are likely to rise next year. For an impression, use seem to: buyers seem to be more careful. And for numbers, let the data speak: the evidence suggests that demand is growing.

The careful words go in the evidence, not in the main point. Amanda opens with "Our position is clear", with no hedge. Then she gives the evidence, with one hedge for each claim. Two hedges in the same sentence, like "it might possibly be", do not sound careful. They sound afraid.

1. According to the text, which words do you use for a forecast?
A may or might
B the evidence suggests that, followed by a number from the latest report
C be likely to
2. Where do the careful words go?
A In the evidence, with one hedge for each claim.
B In the main point, so that nobody can attack it.
C In every sentence, so that the speaker sounds polite to the whole room.
3. What does the text say about two hedges in the same sentence?
A They are the safest choice for a lawyer who is speaking in public at a forum.
B They sound afraid, not careful.
C They are only correct in written English.

Stage 4: Grammar Tip -- careful claims

GRAMMAR

Hedging makes a claim careful without making it weak. Each structure has its own job.

StructureUse it forExample
may / might / could + verba possibilitySome projects may have weak baselines.
be likely to + verba forecastPrices are likely to rise next year.
seem to / appear to + verban impressionBuyers seem to be more careful.
the evidence suggests that + clausewhat the data points toThe evidence suggests that demand is growing.
Negative"Some credits may not be real." "Prices are unlikely to fall." "They do not seem to agree."
Question"Are prices likely to rise?" "Does the evidence suggest that?"

Quick test before you speak: is this my main point, or my evidence? The main point has no hedge.

Stage 6: Build Your Four Minutes

Order

Put the five moves of a panel answer in the order the audience needs them.

?State your point in one sentence, with no hedge.
?Finish with a clear recommendation.
?Thank the moderator and say who you are.
?Explain the impact: who is affected, and how.
?Give the evidence, with a careful verb such as suggests.

Stage 8: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Real situations from the panel. Only one option is both correct and natural.

1. An investor asks if carbon prices will go up. Which answer is correct and careful?
A They are likely rise next year, but it depends on the new rules for the market.
B They will rise, for sure.
C They might possibly perhaps rise.
D They are likely to rise, but it depends on the new rules.
2. You want to say what the data shows. Which sentence is correct?
A The data suggests us that buyers are becoming more careful about the projects they choose.
B The data is suggest that buyers are careful.
C The data suggests that buyers are becoming more careful.
D The data suggests buyers to be careful.
3. Daniel asks for your position in one sentence. Which is the best answer?
A Our position is clear: without integrity, the market will not recover.
B It might be possible that our position is, perhaps, that integrity could matter to the market.
C Maybe integrity is important.
D Our position seems to be likely clear.
4. You think there is a weakness in the new rules, but you are not completely sure. How do you say it?
A There seem being a loophole in how land use is counted.
B There seems to be a loophole in how land use is counted.
C There is surely a loophole in the rules.
D It seems a loophole to be in how land use is counted in the new text.
