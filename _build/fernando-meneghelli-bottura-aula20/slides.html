Chapter 3: The Code

Say Less, Mean the Same

Ellipsis and substitution for natural conversation

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

“Have you ever led an acquisition?” — “I have, twice.”

“Will the board approve the plan?” — “I think so.”

“Is the role going to change?” — “I hope not.”

“I reported to New York for nine years, and so did my whole team.”

Each answer leaves something out. What is missing, and why do we still understand it?

FormWhat it doesExample
subject + auxiliary onlyAnswers without repeating the questionHave you led one? — I have. / Did you sign it? — I did.
think / hope / expect / suppose / be afraid + so / notReplaces a whole clauseI think so. I hope not. I am afraid not. I don’t think so.
so / neither + auxiliary + subjectThe same is true for somebody elseSo did my team. Neither have I.
to / one / do soKeeps the infinitive, replaces a noun or an actionI would like to. The second one. If the board asks, I will do so.
In an interviewShort answers sound self-assured. Repeating the whole question sounds rehearsed.
Practice

Answer It Short

Say it first, then click to check

"Will the audit committee endorse it? — I think ___so."
"Is the print business going to be sold? — I hope ___not."
"Have you worked with a non-executive board before? — Yes, I ___have."
"I left the group in 2017, and ___so did two of my controllers."
The Move

Short, One Fact, Hand Back

1. Answer short

“Have you ever had to cut a budget mid-year? I have, twice.”

2. Add one fact

“The second time was harder, because the cuts came from clients, not from us.”

3. Hand it back

“I can give you the numbers, if that would help.”

The interviewer decides whether she wants more. Your job is to make it easy to ask.

The Second Conversation

J
Thanks for coming back. Did you find us all right this time?
F
I did, thanks. Much easier than last week.
J
Good. I have read the notes from round one. Have you ever run finance for a business that was being sold?
F
I have, once. It was a long eighteen months, but we kept the clients, and that was the part the buyer paid for.
J
And would you do it again?
F
I would, if the board was clear about the timetable. Is a sale on the table here?
J
Not at the moment. Do you think the team here is strong enough?
F
I think so, from what I have seen. I would want to meet the controllers before I said more.
J
You will, next week. Any concerns so far?
F
One small one: the reporting line. I would rather report to you directly, and so would most CFOs.

Listening

Right, but Long

A headhunter on first-round feedback. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the most common note after a first round?
The answers were right, but they were long.
2. Why do senior people over-explain, according to her?
Because they have prepared so much and want to use all of it.
3. What does she tell them to try in the second round?
Start with I have or I haven’t, give one fact, and stop.
