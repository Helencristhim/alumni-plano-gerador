Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

Back in the Rehearsal Room -- Talking About a Past That Ended

You walk into a rehearsal room you left two years ago, and somebody asks what you used to do there. Key words: a rehearsal, the cast, a script, a cue, to project, stage fright, to go blank, a warm-up, to drop out, backstage, an audition, rusty. Structure: used to and would for things that were true then and are not true now.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A rehearsal -- a meeting where the actors practice a play, long before any audience sees it
"We had a rehearsal every Saturday morning for three months."
The cast -- all the actors who perform in one play, taken together as a group
"Half the cast had never been on a stage before."
A script -- the written text of a play, with every single line the actors have to say
"I lost a whole page of the script in front of six hundred people."
A cue -- the word or the movement that tells you it is your turn to speak or to enter
"I could see the other actor waiting for the cue, and I had nothing."
To project -- to push your voice so the back row hears every word, without ever shouting
"You have to project, or the last three rows lose half the play."
Stage fright -- the fear that hits you in the minutes before you walk on stage
"People assume you grow out of stage fright. You do not."
To go blank -- to lose everything you knew, for a few seconds, while people wait
"I still go blank in the wings about twice a season."
A warm-up -- the exercises you do with your voice and your body before you perform anything
"Come twenty minutes early so you have time for a warm-up."
To drop out -- to leave a course or a group before it finishes
"I dropped out twice, and neither time was about the acting."
Backstage -- the part of the theater the audience never sees, on the other side of the curtain
"I get backstage an hour early now, and I do not negotiate with that."
An audition -- a short performance in front of a director, so that you can be given a part
"Auditions run on Tuesday and Wednesday, from six until half past nine."
Rusty -- out of practice, so that what you used to do easily now costs effort
"If you have not performed in a while and you feel rusty, come anyway."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Isabel used to have a rehearsal every Saturday morning. The group would meet at nine, spend the first hour on a warm-up, and only then open the script. She used to be the youngest person in the cast, and the quietest one as well.

She did not use to get stage fright at that age. That came later, in the year she would arrive five minutes before the door closed, with no time to project a single line before the audience walked in. The first time she went blank, the other actor was already waiting for the cue, and there was nothing in her head at all.

She dropped out twice, and neither time was about the acting. Two years later she is standing backstage again, before an audition, and she is honest about it: she is rusty. What she is not is a beginner.

1. "She used to be the youngest person in the cast." Why not "she would be"?
A Because would is only used in questions.
B Because that is how she was, not something she did. Would only works for actions that happened.
C Because would is informal and used to is formal.
2. "The group would meet at nine." How often did this happen?
A Regularly, over a period in the past. It was the normal pattern then.
B Once, on one specific Saturday.
C It has not happened yet, but it is planned.
3. "She did not use to get stage fright at that age." What does the sentence tell you about now?
A That she has never had stage fright in her life.
B That things changed. It was not true then, and the sentence suggests it is true now.
C Nothing at all. It is only about the past.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Saying What Was True Then

GRAMMAR

Two ways to talk about a past that ended, and one of them has a limit.

UseFormExample
Repeated action, finishedused to + base verbI used to rehearse on Saturdays.
Same idea, storytellingwould + base verbWe would run the script twice.
A state, not an actionused to only — never wouldI used to be shy. (not: I would be shy)
Negativedid not use to + base verbI did not use to get stage fright.
QuestionDid + subject + use to + base verbDid you use to perform in English?
Present habitThere is no present form of thisI usually go to the theater in March.

Never: I use to do theater · I would be shy · I didn't used to sing · I used to going there · I use to study at night.

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

The director opens the door and asks about you. Put your side of the conversation in a logical order.

?"Hello, I am Isabel. I am here for the audition on Tuesday."
?"I should say straight away that I am quite rusty."
?"I used to have a rehearsal every Saturday for about a year."
?"I dropped out because of the school, not because of the acting."
?"Did you use to run the whole script in one rehearsal?"
?"Thank you. I will be backstage twenty minutes early on Tuesday."
