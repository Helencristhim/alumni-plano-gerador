Chapter 3: The Code

Things You Do Not Do Any More

Two ways to say it, and one of them has a limit

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"I used to rehearse every Saturday morning."  would

"We would run the whole script twice before lunch."

"I did not use to get stage fright at that age."

"I used to be the quietest person in the cast."  not: would be

All four describe something that was true then and is not true now. Three of them accept the blue form as well. One of them does not, and the reason is what the sentence is about.

UseFormExample
Repeated action, finishedused to + base verbI used to rehearse on Saturdays.
Same idea, storytellingwould + base verbWe would run the script twice.
A state, not an actionused to ONLY — never wouldI used to be shy. (not: I would be shy)
Negativedid not use to + base verbI did not use to get stage fright.
QuestionDid + subject + use to + base verbDid you use to perform in English?
The trapThere is no present formNot I use to go to the theater. Say I usually go.

In one line: used to for anything that was true then, would only for things that HAPPENED.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Room, at Full Speed

One director, two recordings, one call sheet

Dialogue

The Door of the Rehearsal Room

M
Come in, come in. I am Marcus, I run this group. You are here for the audition on Tuesday, is that right?
I
I am, yes. I should say straight away that I am quite rusty. I have not been on a stage in two years.
M
Two years is nothing. What did you use to do before that?
I
I trained at a drama school for a year. We used to have a rehearsal every Saturday, and we would spend the first hour on a warm-up before anyone touched the script.
M
Good. Half the people who come through that door have never warmed up in their lives. So what happened? Why did you drop out?
I
Honestly, it was the school and not the acting. I did not use to mind the hours, but I never felt part of the cast there. I left twice, which probably says everything.
M
It says you know what you do not want, which is more than most. One warning: we start on time and we do not stop for anybody. If you miss a cue you carry on and we fix it after.
I
That is fine. I used to go blank constantly at sixteen, and the only thing that ever helped was carrying on.
Comprehension

About Marcus

1. What does Marcus say about the two years away?
That two years is nothing. He is not impressed by the gap at all.
2. What does he say about the other people who come through his door?
That half of them have never warmed up in their lives.
3. What is the one warning he gives about how his group works?
They start on time and they do not stop for anybody. If you miss a cue you carry on, and they fix it afterwards.
Listening 1 · An Actor

Nineteen Years, and Still Going Blank

Somebody who has done this for a very long time, on the thing that never goes away. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What did she use to do at twenty, and what did it cost her?
She used to arrive four minutes before the call with no warm-up, because she thought preparation was for people without talent. That is how she lost a whole page of the script in front of six hundred people.
2. What are the three things she does now, before a performance?
She gets backstage an hour early, she warms her voice up for fifteen minutes even when she feels ridiculous, and she reads the first two pages out loud to herself.
3. What does she say about the actors who look the calmest?
That they are almost never the ones who feel the calmest. They are the ones who built a routine and stopped negotiating with it.
Listening 2 · A Recorded Message

Everything You Need About Open Auditions

You called the theater and the office was closed. Write down what you have to do. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. When are the auditions, and what is different about the second day?
Tuesday the third and Wednesday the fourth, from six in the evening until half past nine. Tuesday needs no appointment; Wednesday is by appointment only, so you have to send an email first.
2. What do you have to prepare, and what will they ask you to do with it?
One short piece of about two minutes, learned by heart. They will ask you to perform it twice, the second time with a different instruction.
3. What does he say to people who feel rusty, and what proof does he give?
To come anyway. Half the cast of their last show had not been on a stage in over five years.
