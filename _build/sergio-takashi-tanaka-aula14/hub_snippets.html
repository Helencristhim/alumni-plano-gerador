Lesson 14 -- Pre-class

Making the Ask at the Table

The bank has answered your three asks, and now you have to ask again -- politely enough that they can say yes. Key words: the ask, grant, grace period, arrangement fee, in return, meet halfway, hold firm, package, walk away, goodwill. Structure: polite requests and offers (could you, would you be able to, would it be possible to, I would like you to, we would be happy to).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

The ask -- the exact thing you are requesting in a negotiation
"Our ask is simple: move the ceiling to our benchmark."
To grant -- to officially agree to give what someone requested
"The committee granted an increase to one hundred and eighty million."
Grace period -- extra time before a payment or a rule starts to apply
"We asked for a six-month grace period before the first payment."
Arrangement fee -- the one-off charge a bank makes for setting up a loan
"The arrangement fee is one percent, paid on signature."
In return -- what you give back when you receive something
"We accept the fee, and in return we want the ceiling raised."
To meet halfway -- to each give up part of what you want so both sides agree
"They came up, we came down, and we met halfway."
To hold firm -- to refuse to move from your position
"On the ceiling we hold firm; everything else is open."
Package -- a set of conditions offered together, not one by one
"Look at the whole package, not at the fee alone."
To walk away -- to end the negotiation with no deal
"If the ceiling stays where it is, we walk away."
Goodwill -- the friendly credit you build with the other side over time
"Three years of goodwill is why they took our call today."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Three asks went to Nordbank on Tuesday, and the reply came back on Wednesday night. On the grace period, the answer was yes: they granted six months before the first payment. On the ceiling they moved, but not far enough. On the arrangement fee they hold firm. So Thursday is one call with one job. Could you take the ceiling up to our benchmark? Would you be able to confirm the grace period in writing on signature day? And, if the fee is really closed, would it be possible to review it at the first anniversary instead? I would like you to put the whole package in one revised sheet before the committee meets, not three separate notes. On the fee, we would be happy to accept the one percent, and in return we need the ceiling. That is where we can meet halfway and that is the only place where we cannot. Three years of goodwill say nobody here wants to walk away over twenty basis points, so we ask well and we ask once.

1. Why is it "Could you take" and not "Could you to take"?
A Because the verb after a modal always ends in -ing.
B Because a modal like could is followed by the base verb, with no to.
C Because the sentence is a statement, not a question.
2. "Would it be possible to review it?" is used here instead of "Could you review it?" because the writer thinks that
A the request is easy and the answer is obvious.
B the request is difficult, so the most indirect shape gives the other side more room.
C the request is an order and does not need an answer.
3. What does "I would like you to put the whole package in one revised sheet" mean?
A The writer is asking the other person to do it — politely, but clearly.
B The writer is saying that he will do it himself.
C The writer is asking permission to read the sheet.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Polite Requests & Offers

GRAMMAR

The polite shape is not the weak shape. It is the shape the other side can carry to their committee and defend.

ShapeWhen you use itExample
Could you + base verb?The everyday ask. Could is a modal, so the verb stays bare: no to, no -ing.Could you raise the ceiling?
Would you be able to + base verb?Softer, when you are not sure the answer is yes. Here to belongs to able to — and the verb after it is still base.Would you be able to give us six months?
Would it be possible to + base verb?The most indirect. You use it when you know the request is difficult and you want to leave the door open.Would it be possible to drop the fee?
I would like (you) to + base verbYou state your ask instead of asking a question. Two objects, one shape: I would like you to send it, never "I would like that you send it".I would like you to send the package.
We would be happy to + base verb / If you..., we would...The offer and the trade. We would be happy to accept the fee. If you raise the ceiling, we would sign this week. Contractions in speech: I'd like, we'd be happy. Never "able to giving", never "happy accept".

Stage 2: Put the Ask in Order

Order

?Offer something in return, so they can sell the answer internally.
?Put all your asks on the table together, not one at a time.
?Ask for a clear next step and a date.
?Thank the other side for what they have already granted.
?Say which one matters most and why.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best option for each moment at the negotiation table.

You want the ceiling raised, and you want the banker to be able to defend it with his committee. Which is best?
A "Could you to raise the ceiling?"
B "Would you be able to raise the ceiling to our benchmark?"
C "You must raise the ceiling to our benchmark."
You want the revised package in your inbox before the call. Which is best?
A "I would like that you send the revised package."
B "I would like you to send the revised package before Thursday."
C "I want you send the revised package before Thursday."
You are ready to give them the fee if they move on the ceiling. Which is best?
A "We would be happy accepting the fee if you move on the ceiling."
B "We are happy that you accept the fee on the ceiling."
C "We would be happy to accept the fee if you move on the ceiling."
They granted the grace period but not the ceiling. How do you open the call?
A "Thank you for the grace period. Could we come back to the ceiling for a moment?"
B "The grace period is nothing. The ceiling is the only real problem here."
C "Would you be able to giving us a better ceiling today?"

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think of one real change you would like from a bank, a vendor or another team at work. Say it out loud: thank them for something they have already granted, make the ask with "Could you..." or "Would you be able to...", say what you would give in return, name the one point where you would hold firm, and close with a next step and a date. Take your time.
