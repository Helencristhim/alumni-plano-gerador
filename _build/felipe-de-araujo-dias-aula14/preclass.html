Lesson 14 -- Pre-class

If the Container Is Late -- Risk and Consequence

The words of a delay: container, freight, collection, to be stuck, knock-on effect, contingency plan, buffer stock, to reroute. Structure: the first conditional with if, unless and as soon as. Mission: explain a risk, its knock-on effect, and what you will do.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Container -- a large metal box used to send goods by ship, train or truck
"The container with the summer collection is still in Singapore."
Freight -- goods that are transported, or the service of transporting them
"Air freight is ten times more expensive than sea freight."
Collection -- a group of clothes designed and sold for one season
"If the container is late, the summer collection will arrive after Black Friday."
To be stuck -- to be unable to move or to continue
"The ship has been stuck outside the port for four days."
Knock-on effect -- an indirect result that one problem causes in other areas
"One late container has a knock-on effect on forty stores."
Contingency plan -- a plan you prepare in case something goes wrong
"We need a contingency plan before Friday, not after."
Buffer stock -- extra stock that you keep in case of delays or problems
"If we have enough buffer stock, the stores will not notice."
To reroute -- to send something by a different way than planned
"If we reroute the container through another port, we will lose two more days."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The ship is still in Singapore. If it leaves on Thursday, it will arrive in Santos on the 26th, two days before our deadline. If it doesn't leave, the next ship is a week later, and we will miss the launch in forty stores. Unless we pay for air freight, the new prints won't be there for Black Friday. I will call the director as soon as the port confirms.

Look at the verbs. Nothing has happened yet, but after if, unless and as soon as the verb is in the present: leaves, pay, confirms. The will lives in the other half of the sentence, the result. And unless already means if not, so it never takes a second negative.

1. Why is it If it leaves on Thursday and not If it will leave?
A Because after if, the condition takes the present, even about the future.
B Because Thursday is very close, so the present is more natural there.
C Because leave is irregular.
2. What does Unless we pay for air freight mean?
A Because we are going to pay for air freight next week.
B If we do not pay for air freight.
C After we pay for air freight and the container finally arrives.
3. When will the writer call the director?
A Tomorrow morning, before the port says anything.
B On Thursday at six.
C As soon as the port confirms.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The First Conditional

GRAMMAR

A real possibility in the future, and what will happen because of it. The condition takes the present; the result takes will.

FormUse it forExample
If + present, will + verbCondition first, with a comma.If it leaves on Thursday, it will arrive on time.
will + verb if + presentResult first, no comma.We will miss the launch if it arrives late.
unless + presentIf not. Never with a second negative.Unless we pay for air freight, the stores will not have it.
as soon as / when + presentThe moment something happens.I will call you as soon as the port confirms.
Other resultsmight, can, should or an imperative also work.If it is late, call me.
NegativeIf it doesn't leave, we won't make it.

The most common mistake is will in both halves. Only the result takes will: if it arrives, never if it will arrive.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think about one real risk in your areas this month: a delivery, a machine, a supplier or an event. Record about ninety seconds. Explain what will happen if it goes wrong, and then the knock-on effect: who else will feel it, and when. Then give your contingency plan with at least one unless and one as soon as. Finish with what will happen if everything goes well. Use at least four words from this lesson. Do not stop to correct yourself.
