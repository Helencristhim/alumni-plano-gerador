Chapter 4: The Code

We Are Testing

The present continuous for things in progress

Grammar Discovery

Four Sentences From the Update

"We are testing the new adhesive this week."

"The customer is running a trial at the factory."

"I am working on the new label right now."

"We aren’t waiting for the supplier anymore."

All four sentences are about now or this week. The verb has two parts: a red word (am, is, are) and a blue word that ends in -ing.

JobFormExample
Iam + verb-ingI am working on the label.
he / she / itis + verb-ingThe customer is running a trial.
you / we / theyare + verb-ingWe are testing the adhesive.
Negativeam not / isn’t / aren’t + verb-ingWe aren’t waiting for the supplier.
QuestionAm / Is / Are + subject + verb-ing?Are you working on the launch?
The trapevery day, every week = present simpleNot We are sending the report every Friday. Say We send the report every Friday.

In one line: am / is / are + -ing for things happening now or this week; present simple for things that happen every week.

Listening 1 · Priya’s Update

Priya on the Weekly Call

Priya, in India, gives her status update on Teams. Sound first — no text.

1. What two things is Priya working on this week?
The forecast for the new adhesive and the quotes for the launch.
2. Why is the forecast behind schedule?
Because she is waiting for numbers from two customers.
3. What is her issue this week?
The system is very slow.
Listening 2 · The Customer’s Trial

Julien Talks About the Trial

Julien, the customer in France, explains how the trial is going. Sound first — no text.

1. On how many lines is Julien testing the adhesive?
On two lines.
2. What is the small issue on the second line?
The adhesive dries too slowly when the factory is cold, early in the morning.
3. When are they finishing the tests?
Next Friday.
