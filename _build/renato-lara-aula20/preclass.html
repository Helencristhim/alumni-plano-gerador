Lesson 20 — Pre-class

Back in the Water

Eight words for getting better after an injury (recover, heal, rest, physio, sore, warm up, stretch, ice) and how to say rules that are always true: if + present simple, present simple.

Stage 1.1: Getting Better

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes. "Warm up" is one word: the second part never falls off.

Recover — to become healthy again after you were hurt or sick
"Ollie is recovering at home."
Heal — when a cut or an injury becomes normal again
"The cut is healing well."
Rest — to stop and relax, so your body can get better
"For the first three days, you rest."
Physio — a person whose job is to help your body move well after an injury
"The physio gave Ollie a plan."
Sore — a little painful, often after you use a muscle a lot
"My shoulders are sore after training."
Warm up — to do easy exercise before sport, so your body is ready
"We warm up on the sand for ten minutes."
Stretch — to make your arms, legs or body long, to relax the muscles
"Stretch your legs before you paddle out."
Ice — to put something very cold on an injury
"Ice your ankle three times a day."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read Ollie's message to the club group. Then answer the questions.

Hey everyone, thanks for all the messages. I saw the physio today. Her name is Nina and she is really nice, but she is strict. Her first rule is simple: if it hurts, you stop. For three days I rest with my foot up, and I ice my ankle three times a day. She says that if you rest, your body heals faster. The cut is healing well, and I'm recovering, but I can't surf for two weeks. After that I start to stretch, slowly. If my ankle feels sore the next morning, I do less. And here is the rule for all of you: warm up before training. If your muscles are cold, you get hurt more easily. Trust me.
"If you rest, your body heals." When is this true?
A Always. It is a fact about how the body works.
B Only next Saturday.
Why is there no "will" in "If it hurts, you stop"?
A Because it is a rule that is always true: both verbs are in the present.
B Because "will" is only for questions.
Which sentence is correct?
A If your muscles are cold, you get hurt more easily.
B If your muscles will be cold, you get hurt more easily.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

If + present, present

Use it for things that are always true: facts, rules, how the body works. If means almost the same as every time.

Always true

If it hurts, you stop.
If you rest, your body heals.
If you don't warm up, you get hurt.
You stop if it hurts.

One future situation

If it rains tomorrow, we will stay home.
→ that is lesson 12
If you will rest, you recover.
→ never will after if

Positive: If you ice it, the swelling goes down.  ·  Negative: If you don't rest, it doesn't heal.  ·  Question: What happens if you don't warm up?
Comma: if first → comma. If second → no comma: "You stop if it hurts."
Keep the -s: your body heals, the swelling goes down, it hurts.
