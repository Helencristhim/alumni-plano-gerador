Telling My Story

Stage 1.1: Narrative Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

At first — in the beginning, before things changed
"At first I could not stand up at all."
Eventually — after a long time, in the end
"Eventually I caught my first proper wave."
Turning point — the moment when everything started to change
"The first podium was the turning point."
Look back — to think about something that happened before
"When I look back, the bad years taught me more."
Ups and downs — the good moments and the bad ones together
"Every season has ups and downs."
Proud — when you feel good about something you did
"I am proud of the season, not the result."
Journey — the whole path from where you started to where you are
"It has been a long journey and it is not finished."
Thanks to — because of somebody who helped you
"Thanks to my coach, I did not quit at twelve."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the story. Then answer the questions.

I caught my first wave at eight, because my father pushed me into it. At first it went badly: I lost every heat for two years and I nearly quit at twelve. Eventually it clicked, and thanks to a coach who refused to let me leave, I stayed. Looking back, the turning point was not the podium at fourteen. It was the four bad seasons before it. There have been ups and downs since, including six weeks out with a shoulder injury. I am not proud of the results. I am proud that I have not missed a Saturday since I was nine. In February the journey moves to Australia.
What does he say the turning point was?
A The podium at fourteen.
B The four bad seasons before the podium.
What is he proud of?
A That he has not missed a Saturday since he was nine.
B His results.
What do "at first", "eventually" and "looking back" all do?
A They move the story through time.
B They make the sentences longer.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

The words that hold a story together

No new grammar. The facts and the tenses are the ones you already have. What is new is the joining.

To move through time

At first, ... (the beginning)
Then / After that, ...
Eventually, ... (after a long time)
Now, ... (where you are today)

To show what it meant

but ... (the turn)
because ... (the reason)
Thanks to ... (the person)
Looking back, ... (what you know now)

Through time: At first... Then... After that... Eventually... Now...
The turn: but is the smallest and most powerful one. Use it where the story changes.
The reason and the person: because ... / Thanks to my coach, ...
The shape: where I started → what changed → where I am going. Three parts, every time.

Stage 4: Telling it well

Quiz
You want to describe how it was in the beginning.
A At first I was terrible.
B At first I am terrible.
You want to name the person who kept you going.
A Thanks for my coach I did not quit.
B Thanks to my coach, I did not quit.
You want to say how you feel about the season.
A I am proud for the season.
B I am proud of the season.
You want to introduce what you understand now, looking at the past.
A Looking back, the bad years taught me more.
B Look back, the bad years teach me more.

Stage 5: Your whole journey

Reflection
Record ninety seconds. Your whole story, from the first wave to February 2027: where you started, what went wrong, what changed and thanks to whom, what you are proud of, and where you are going. Do not stop and do not restart. This is the recording we keep, next to the one from lesson 1.
