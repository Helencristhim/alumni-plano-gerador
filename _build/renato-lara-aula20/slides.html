Grammar Discovery

One day, or always?

If it hurts, you stop.
If you rest, your body heals.
If your muscles are cold, they get hurt more easily.
If you ice a swollen ankle, the swelling goes down.

What do the orange words have in common?

If + present simple, present simple

Use it for things that are always true: facts, rules, how the body works. Both verbs are in the present — no will. If here means almost the same as when or every time.

The Code

Always true vs. one time

Always true

If you rest, you recover.
If you don't warm up, you get hurt.
If it hurts, you stop.
You stop if it hurts. (no comma)

Listening 1

A call from the physio

1. What is the good news about the ankle?
It is healing well, and the swelling is going down.
2. What happens if Ollie walks a lot in the first three days?
The ankle gets swollen again.
3. How does Ollie know he did too much with the stretches?
If his ankle feels sore the next morning, he did too much.
Listening 2

A new rule from Kai

1. What is Ollie going to do at training next week?
He can't go in the water, so he is going to help Kai on the sand.
2. What is the new rule, and what do they do?
Everybody warms up before training: ten minutes on the sand, run and stretch their legs and shoulders.
3. What is the plan if somebody gets hurt?
The closest person stays with them, and one person gets an adult.

Read "Stop signs". Explain them to Ollie with if.
If it hurts while you move, you stop. If your ankle gets swollen again, you stop and call Nina.
It is day 12. Ollie wants to paddle out. What do you tell him?
Not yet. On days eleven to fourteen you run on the sand, but no water. You go back in the water on day fifteen, and only in small waves.
Read "Training buddy". What do you and Ollie do, and why?
We warm up together for ten minutes, because if your muscles are cold, you get hurt more easily.
