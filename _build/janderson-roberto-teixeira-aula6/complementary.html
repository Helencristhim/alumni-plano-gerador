"Have you ever seen a whale?" -- 6 Minute English, BBC Learning English

Six minutes built around today's exact question, starting with the title itself. Connection to Lesson 6: the presenters ask each other have you ever three times in the first minute, and you hear how naturally the answer turns into a story. Audio, vocabulary and full transcript are free on the page.

Tip: listen once without the transcript. Then count how many times somebody answers with I have never and follows it with what they would like to do instead.

Talk
"Try something new for 30 days" -- Matt Cutts, TED

Three minutes about doing things you have never done, told almost entirely in the tenses of this lesson and the last one. Connection to Lesson 6: it is the shortest possible model of talking about experience, and it ends with the exact idea behind today's class -- that the list of things you have never tried is shorter than it looks.

Tip: after watching, say three sentences out loud about things you have never done and would like to try. No dates.

Grammar Video
"Present perfect with 'ever' and 'never'" -- 6 Minute Grammar, BBC Learning English

Six minutes on today's exact structure, including the two things that cost most: where ever goes in the question, and why never refuses a second negative. Connection to Lesson 6: it repeats the pattern often enough that the shape starts arriving before you think about it.

Tip: pause after each example and ask the same question about your own life out loud. Then answer it without giving a date.
