Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

Have You Ever... -- Talking About Your Life Without Dates

The four words that open more conversations in English than any others, and the tense behind them. Key expressions: abroad, to try out, an experience, so far, to be into, amazing, to get lost, to put off, a highlight, terrified. Structure: present perfect with ever and never.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. These ten stay here in your material -- they do not disappear after the class.

Abroad -- in or to another country
"Have you ever worked abroad for more than a month?"
To try out -- to do something for the first time to see what it is like
"I have never tried out a language exchange, and I probably should."
An experience -- something that happens to you and that you remember afterwards
"It was the strangest experience I have ever had."
So far -- until now, up to this moment
"So far nobody has answered my email."
To be into -- to like something a lot and spend time on it
"I am really into old maps, which surprises people."
Amazing -- extremely good, much better than you expected
"The food was amazing and it cost almost nothing."
To get lost -- to stop knowing where you are
"I have got lost in three countries and I recommend it."
To put off -- to move something to a later date
"I have put off this course for about twenty years."
A highlight -- the best part of an experience
"The highlight of the trip was a market nobody told me about."
Terrified -- very frightened
"I was terrified the first time I spoke English in a meeting."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Janderson has worked in the same bank for years and he has never worked abroad. He has been to London twice, both times for work, and so far he has seen an office, a hotel and the road between them. That is the whole list, and he says it out loud without embarrassment, because it is true of most people.

He is really into old maps, which surprises everybody who meets him, and he has put off doing anything about it for about twenty years. He has never tried out a map market, he has never joined a group, and he has never mentioned it at work. This year, for no particular reason, he decided that was enough.

The first time he spoke English in a meeting he was terrified. He remembers the room, the table and the exact chair. It was the strangest experience he has ever had at work: he understood almost everything and produced almost nothing. He went home and did not talk about it for a week.

Now he is starting again, and this time somebody is keeping him honest. The highlight of the year, he says, has been sitting down twice a week and finding out that it is amazing how much comes back. He has got lost in a few conversations already. He recommends it.

1. "He has never worked abroad." Why is there no date in this sentence?
A Because he cannot remember the date.
B Because the present perfect talks about a whole life, and a date would change the tense.
C Because the sentence is negative.
2. "He went home and did not talk about it for a week." Why the past simple here?
A Because the sentence refers to one finished occasion in the past.
B Because go is irregular.
C Because the sentence is long.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A I have been to London last year.
B I have been to London twice.
C I have gone to London twice and I am here now.
4. What does so far mean in the first paragraph?
A A long distance away.
B Until now, and it could still change.
C A long time ago.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect with Ever and Never

GRAMMAR

This tense has one job: your life, with no calendar attached. The moment a finished date appears, English switches to the past simple.

FormWhat it saysExample
have / has + past participleThe experience exists somewhere in your life. The date is not given and is not the point."I have worked in three banks."
"She has been to Japan."
ever in questionsEver = at any time in your life. It lives in questions."Have you ever been to New York?"
never in answersNever is already negative. Never add not as well."I have never worked abroad."
NOT "I have not never"
been or goneBeen = went and came back. Gone = still there."I have been to Miami."
"She has gone to Miami."
Short answersThe answer copies the auxiliary, exactly like the question."Have you ever been? Yes, I have. / No, I never have."
The switch to the pastYesterday, last year, in 2019, when I was twenty — any finished time forces the past simple."I went to Miami last March."

A practical use at work: have you ever worked with...? and have you been to...? are the two safest openers with a client you have never met. They ask for a story and they do not ask for a date.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are at a dinner with people you have just met. Choose the best thing to say.

You want to ask about somebody's whole life:
A Did you ever go to Brazil?
B Have you ever been to Brazil?
C Have you ever gone to Brazil in the past?
You want to say you have no experience of working outside the country:
A I have not never worked abroad.
B I have never worked abroad.
C I never have not worked abroad.
You are talking about a trip in March, and it is now July:
A I have been to Miami last March.
B I went to Miami last March.
C I have gone to Miami last March.
A colleague is not at her desk because she is at the airport:
A She has gone to the airport.
B She has been to the airport.
C She is went to the airport.
You want to keep the conversation going after answering:
A That is all about me.
B What about you? Have you ever done anything like that?
C I have finished my explanation.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
