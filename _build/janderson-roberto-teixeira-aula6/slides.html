Have You Ever…

Four words that open more conversations in English than any others. They ask about your whole life without asking for a single date.

Chapter 1: The Question

Something You Have Never Done

One thing you have never done and would like to. One thing you have done that most people have not. No dates, no explanations -- just the two facts.

Where You Start

The Question With No Calendar

The wrong question

"When did you go to New York?" -- and now you need a year

The right question

"Have you ever been to New York?" -- and the year does not matter

Why it works

It invites a story instead of a date

There is a tense in English whose whole job is to talk about your life without putting anything on a calendar. It is what people use at a dinner table, in a queue, in the four minutes before a meeting. It is also the tense that Portuguese speakers avoid the longest, because Portuguese does the same job with the simple past.

Chapter 3: The Code

Have You Ever

The tense that talks about your life and never mentions a date

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Have you ever been to New York?"

"I have never worked abroad."

"I have been to Miami three times."

"I went to Miami last March."

"It is the strangest thing I have ever seen."

Four of these sentences never say when. One of them does, and it changes tense because of it. What is the rule hiding behind that?

FormWhat it saysExample
have / has + past participleThe experience exists somewhere in your life. The date is not the point and is not given."I have worked in three banks."
"She has been to Japan."
ever in questionsEver means at any time in your life. It only appears in questions."Have you ever been to New York?"
never in answersNever already carries the negative, so you never add not."I have never worked abroad."
NOT "I have not never"
been or goneBeen means you went and came back. Gone means you are still there."I have been to Miami." (I am here)
"She has gone to Miami." (she is there)
The moment a date appearsSay when and English switches to the past simple immediately. The two tenses never share a sentence."I went to Miami last March."
NOT "I have gone last March"

One test decides everything: if the sentence names a finished time -- yesterday, last year, in 2019, when I was twenty -- use the past simple. If it does not, use the present perfect.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I have been to Miami last March."
"I went to Miami last March."
"Did you ever go to New York in your life?"
"Have you ever been to New York?"
"I have not never worked abroad."
"I have never worked abroad."

The present perfect and a finished date cannot live in the same sentence: the moment you say last March, the verb becomes went. To ask about a whole life, English uses have you ever, not did you ever. And never is already negative, so it never takes a second not. Read the correct versions aloud twice.

Comprehension

About Emma

1. What does Emma ask him in her first line?
Whether he has ever been to London before.
2. How does she react to his answer?
She says it is a terrible record.
3. What does she say about the map markets?
That she has never even heard of them.
4. What advice does she give him for Saturday?
To go, and to get lost on purpose, because it is the fastest way to learn a city.
Listening

Nobody Asks the Right Question

A short radio segment. Sound first -- no text.

1. What does she say is wrong with the question "what do you do?"
It gets a job title, and a job title is not a person.
2. What question does she recommend instead?
"Have you ever..." -- because it asks about a whole life without asking for a date.
3. What does she say happens after somebody answers it?
They usually ask it back, and the conversation keeps going on its own.
Listening 2

Twenty Years of Putting It Off

A short personal story from a podcast. Sound first -- no text.

1. How long had he been putting off the trip?
About twenty years.
2. What was the highlight, according to him?
Getting lost on the second afternoon and finding a street market.
3. What does he say he has learned?
That he has never regretted going, and that he has regretted waiting every single time.

The Habit

One Question, Out Loud

Before the next class, ask yourself one have you ever question out loud each day and answer it in three sentences. Two things you have done, one thing you never have.

This is the tense that lets you talk about forty-eight years of life without remembering a single date.
