Panel Discussions -- Contributing Actively

On a panel in New York, another panelist says that Brazil is too risky. Eduarda accepts part of his point, adds her view and supports it with a number. Key words: moderator, panelist, weigh in, build on, echo, concede, counterargument, devil's advocate. Structure: concessive clauses for partial agreement (Although the courts are slow, the outcomes are good).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Moderator -- the person who leads a panel and asks the questions
"The moderator gives each panelist two minutes."
Panelist -- one of the experts who speak on a panel
"Eduarda is the only Brazilian panelist on stage."
Weigh in -- to give your opinion in a discussion
"May I weigh in on that point?"
Build on -- to add to an idea that another person said
"I'd like to build on what Mark said."
Echo -- to repeat or support what another person said
"I want to echo Sofia's point about the courts."
Concede -- to admit that the other person is right about something
"I concede that the courts are slow."
Counterargument -- a reason that argues against another idea
"Her counterargument was one number: the recovery rate."
Devil's advocate -- a person who argues the opposite side to test an idea
"Let me play devil's advocate for a moment."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Friday, Eduarda is a panelist at the forum in New York. The moderator, Daniel Price, asks: "Brazil: opportunity or risk?" Mark Ellison, from a hedge fund, plays devil's advocate. He says that Brazil is too risky for foreign investors.

Eduarda doesn't say "you are wrong." First, she wants to build on his point: "Although the risk is real, the returns are real, too." Then she concedes one thing: "Even though the courts are slow, the outcomes are improving." Her counterargument is one number: "Despite the delays, creditors recovered sixty percent."

Sofia Reyes, a lawyer from Mexico, echoes Eduarda's point. While Mark still disagrees, he asks the moderator to weigh in, and the discussion continues.

1. What does Mark say about Brazil?
A That it is the best market in Latin America for funds that invest in mid-market companies.
B That its courts are fast.
C That it is too risky for foreign investors.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "Although the courts are slow, but the outcomes are improving every year in Brazil."
B "Despite the delays, creditors recovered sixty percent."
C "Despite of the delays, creditors recovered."
3. Who echoes Eduarda's point?
A Sofia Reyes.
B Mark Ellison, the panelist from the hedge fund in New York.
C Daniel Price.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Concessive Clauses for Partial Agreement

GRAMMAR

Use although, even though, while or despite to accept part of another person's point and add your view in the same sentence. On a panel, this keeps the discussion open.

FormDirectConcessive
although + subject + verbThe courts are slow. The outcomes are good.Although the courts are slow, the outcomes are good.
even though + subject + verb (stronger)Brazil is risky. Investors made money.Even though Brazil is risky, investors made money.
while + subject + verb (formal)I agree with Mark. I see the risk differently.While I agree with Mark, I see the risk differently.
despite / in spite of + noun or -ingThere were delays. The company survived.Despite the delays, the company survived.

Never: Although..., but... · despite of · despite the courts are slow

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose what a confident panelist would say.

Another panelist says: "Brazil is too risky." You only agree with part of it. You say:
A "No, you are wrong, and I think that the people in this room know that you are wrong."
B "Although the risk is real, the returns are real, too."
C "Yes, I agree."
You want to add to what Sofia said. You say:
A "I'd like to build on what Sofia said."
B "I'd like to build in what Sofia said about the courts in Mexico and in Brazil."
C "Sofia, again."
You want to support your view with a fact. You say:
A "Despite the courts are slow, creditors recovered sixty percent of their money last year."
B "Delays, but sixty."
C "Despite the delays, creditors recovered sixty percent."
You want the moderator to give you a chance to speak. You say:
A "Stop, it's my turn now."
B "May I weigh in on that point?"
C "May I weigh on that point, please, before we go to the questions from the audience?"

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Imagine you are on a panel. Another panelist says: "Mid-market companies in Brazil are too small for foreign investors." Record your answer: accept what is true in the point, add your view with "although", "even though" or "while", and support it with one fact using "despite".
