The Elevator Pitch -- Your Business in Sixty Seconds

Introduce yourself and your work in sixty seconds, so people remember you. Key words: hook, concise, memorable, to stand out, unique selling point, niche, well-established, cutting-edge, reliable, to tailor, call to action, long-term. Structures: word order and adjective order (a well-established French company, I speak English very well), and the four moves of a pitch.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the definition. Four are about the pitch, four describe your business, four make the pitch yours.

Hook -- an opening sentence that makes people want to hear more
"My hook is a result: three percentage points in one year."
Concise -- saying what you need in a few clear words
"Be concise: one sentence for who you are."
Memorable -- easy to remember
"A memorable pitch has one number, not five."
To stand out -- to be easy to notice because you are different
"A real result helps you stand out at a global meeting."
Unique selling point -- the one thing that makes you different from the competition
"Our unique selling point is the relationship with the key doctors."
Niche -- a small, specialized part of a market
"Aesthetic medicine is a niche, but it is growing fast."
Well-established -- known and trusted for a long time
"Ipsen is a well-established French company."
Cutting-edge -- the newest and most advanced
"We invest in cutting-edge research in neuroscience."
Reliable -- you can trust it to work every time
"Clinics choose us because we are reliable."
To tailor -- to change something so it fits one person or situation
"I tailor the pitch to the person in front of me."
Call to action -- the clear next step you ask the listener to take
"My call to action is a thirty-minute call next month."
Long-term -- lasting or planned for many years
"We have a long-term plan for the interior."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the story of Renato's pitch and answer the questions. Notice the order of the words in bold.

Renato is preparing a short pitch for the next global meeting. His first version was only his job title, and a coach told him it did not stand out. Now he starts with a hook: last year, his small regional team grew market share by three percentage points.

Then he says who he is, in one concise sentence. He works for a well-established French pharmaceutical company, in a growing niche. His unique selling point is the relationship: his consultants visit the key doctors in person every month, and they tailor every visit to the clinic.

He used to say "I speak very well English", but now he says "I speak English very well". He finishes with a small call to action: "Could I send you the plan by email tomorrow?" The pitch takes fifty-five seconds, and it is memorable.

1. Why is it "a small regional team" and not "a regional small team"?
A Because "small" is a shorter word, and in English the shorter adjective always has to come first in the sentence.
B Because in English the size comes before the type of the noun.
C Because the team is in the South and Southeast.
2. Which sentence has the correct word order?
A "I speak English very well."
B "I speak very well English, and I want to improve it even more this year."
C "I very well speak English."
3. In "the key doctors in person every month", what comes right after the verb "visit"?
A The time: every month.
B The manner: in person, because it explains how the consultants visit the doctors every month.
C The object: the key doctors.
4. Which description of Ipsen is correct?
A "A French well-established pharmaceutical company, with offices in many countries."
B "A well-established French pharmaceutical company."
C "A company French well-established."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Word Order and Adjective Order

GRAMMAR

Two rules decide the order: adjectives go before the noun, in a fixed order, and nothing goes between the verb and its object.

RuleOrderExample
Adjectivesalways BEFORE the nouna reliable product
Adjective orderopinion → size → age → origin → type + NOUNa well-established French pharmaceutical company
Sentence ordersubject + verb + object + how + where + whenWe visit the doctors in person every month.
Verb + objectnothing goes between themI speak English very well.
Negative and questionThe same order: We do not visit the doctors every week. Do you speak English well?Only the auxiliary moves.
Two trapsNever a company French — the adjective comes first. And never I speak very well English — the object comes right after the verb.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

A director you do not know asks: "So, what do you do?" Your first sentence is:
A "Last year, my team grew market share by three percentage points."
B "I am the commercial manager for the South and Southeast at Ipsen Brazil, in the aesthetics area."
C "It is a long story."
You want to describe your company. You say:
A "We are a company French and well-established, with a long history in neuroscience and in aesthetics."
B "We are a French well-established company."
C "We are a well-established French company."
The director asks about your English. You say:
A "I speak English quite well, and I am improving fast."
B "I speak very well English, but sometimes I am nervous in big meetings."
C "I very well speak English."
You have six results you are proud of. In a sixty-second pitch, you:
A Say all six quickly, because the director should know everything your team did this year.
B Choose one result for the hook.
C Say none of them.
You will meet a medical director, not a finance director. You:
A Give exactly the same pitch, because the structure of a good pitch never changes for anybody.
B Skip the pitch.
C Tailor the hook to the medical director.
Your pitch is almost finished. Your call to action is:
A "Could I send you one page by email tomorrow?"
B "Can we organize a full-day meeting with your whole team and my whole team next month in Boston?"
C "Bye."
