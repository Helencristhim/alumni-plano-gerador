Chapter 3: The Code

The Right Word in the Right Place

Adjective order and word order

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Ipsen is a well-established French company."

"I lead a small regional team of seven consultants."

"We visit the key doctors every month."

"I speak English quite well, and I am improving fast."

Where are the blue adjectives: before or after the noun? Where are the purple words: between the verb and the object, or after the object?

RuleOrderExample
Adjectivesalways BEFORE the nouna reliable product (never a product reliable)
Adjective orderopinion → size → age → origin → type + NOUNa well-established French pharmaceutical company
Sentence ordersubject + verb + object + how + where + whenWe visit the doctors in person every month.
Verb + objectNothing goes between them: I speak English very well (never I speak very well English).
Two trapsNever a company French — the adjective comes first. And never We visit every month the doctors — the object comes right after the verb.

The Structure

Four Moves in Sixty Seconds

Not your job title. A reason to remember you.

1 · The hook · 10 seconds

"Last year, a small regional team grew market share by three percentage points."

2 · Who you are · 15 seconds

"I lead that team: seven consultants in the South and Southeast of Brazil."

3 · What makes you different · 20 seconds

"Our unique selling point is the relationship: we visit the key doctors in person every month."

4 · The ask · 15 seconds

"Could I send you our long-term plan for the interior by email tomorrow?"

The Rehearsal

N
Renato, you have sixty seconds. Imagine I am a director from another country, and we meet at the coffee break. Go.
R
Hi, I'm Renato Annunciato. I am the commercial manager for the South and Southeast at Ipsen Brazil.
N
That is your job title. Everybody in the room has one. Where is your hook?
R
OK. Last year, a small regional team of seven consultants grew market share by three percentage points. I lead that team.
N
Now you stand out. Why should I remember your team, and not the others?
R
Our unique selling point is the relationship. We visit the key doctors in person every month, and we tailor every visit to the clinic.
N
Good. Keep it concise, though. One sentence is enough. And what do you want from me?
R
I would like to share our long-term plan for the interior of Sao Paulo with your team.
N
That is a call to action, but make it small and clear. What exactly is the next step?
R
Could I send you the plan by email tomorrow, and we talk for thirty minutes next week?
N
Perfect. Hook, who you are, what is different, and the ask. Fifty-five seconds, and memorable.

Listening

What the Director Hears

A global director on the pitches she hears at coffee breaks. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. How many people introduce themselves to her at a big meeting?
About twenty or thirty people.
2. What is the first mistake she hears?
People give only their job title, which does not tell her why to remember them.
3. Why does she not like a long list of adjectives?
Because it sounds like a brochure, not like a person.
4. What kind of call to action does she like?
A small, clear one, like sending one page by email.
Listening 2

Three Versions of One Pitch

A sales manager on rewriting his pitch. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. How long was his first version, and what was in it?
Almost three minutes, with his whole career, from his first job.
2. What did he do in the second version?
He cut it to one minute and started with a result.
3. Why was the second version still not good?
It had too many numbers, and nobody remembered any of them.
4. What did he learn about the call to action?
To ask for one small thing, like a short call, not for a big meeting.
Chapter 5: Practice

Your Pitch

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "Ipsen is a company French."
"Ipsen is a French company." (the adjective comes before the noun)
2. "I lead a regional small team."
"I lead a small regional team." (size comes before type)
3. "I speak very well English."
"I speak English very well." (nothing between the verb and the object)
4. "We visit every month the key doctors."
"We visit the key doctors every month." (object first, then time)
5. "I assisted your presentation yesterday." (you mean: I was there)
"I attended your presentation yesterday." (assist means to help)

Chapter 6: Your Turn

Three Rounds, Less Time

Same pitch, shorter and freer each round

Round 1

Ninety Seconds

Scenario: You meet a director from another country at the coffee break. Give your pitch in the four moves: the hook, who you are, what makes you different, and the ask. You have ninety seconds.

Keyword chips:

Last year, my team... I lead a small regional team... Our unique selling point is... Could I send you...?
Round 2

Sixty Seconds, One Interruption

Scenario: Same pitch, but now in sixty seconds, and the director interrupts you once. Answer in one sentence, then finish your pitch on time.

Keywords:

hook unique selling point well-established French call to action
Round 3

Your Real Pitch

Scenario: Choose a real person you will meet: a global director, a medical director, or someone from finance. Tailor your hook for that person and give your pitch from memory, in sixty seconds.

No keywords. This is the pitch you will really give.
