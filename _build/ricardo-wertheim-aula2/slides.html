Chapter 4: The Code

The Tense of Every Story

I went · we walked · I didn't take · did you see?

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"We arrived in Split late at night."

"I walked on cobblestone streets for hours."

"We ate fresh fish near the harbor."

"I went back to that square every evening."

Two orange verbs end in -ed. What happened to the other two?

FormRuleExample
Regular verbsverb + ed, for every personWe arrived late. I walked for hours.
Irregular verbsa NEW word — no -ed at all. These seven carry most travel stories.go → went · see → saw · eat → ate · meet → met · take → took · feel → felt · find → found
Negativedid not / didn't + the plain verb. The past moves to did, so the verb goes back to its simple form.I didn't take a single photo.
QuestionSame rule: Did + person + plain verb.Did you see the harbor at sunset?
Time expressionslast year · in 2019 · a few years ago · when I was in Lisbon · on my last trip. They usually open or close the sentence: Last year we went to Portugal. / We went to Portugal last year.

One tense, three shapes: -ed or a new word in the affirmative, and did carrying the past in the negative and in the question.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I go to Lisbon in 2018."
"I went to Lisbon in 2018."
"I didn't went to the harbor."
"I didn't go to the harbor."
"Where you did eat that night?"
"Where did you eat that night?"

A time expression like in 2018 forces the past on the verb. And once did appears, it carries the past alone — the other verb goes back to its plain form, and did comes before the person in a question.

How This Harbor Began

A walking guide, before the tour starts. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Who built the first houses here, and how long ago?
Fishermen, more than eight hundred years ago.
2. What happened in 1667?
A strong earthquake destroyed almost everything.
3. What did the people do after that?
They stayed and rebuilt the same streets with the same stone, in less than thirty years.
