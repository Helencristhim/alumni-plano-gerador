Lesson 30 -- Pre-class

Capstone and Celebration -- Your Fluency Milestone

The last lesson of the program: the year-end review with the board, one final rapid round, and a look back at how far you have come. Key words: come a long way, second nature, comfort zone, track record, pay off, proficiency, stretch goal, momentum, brush up on, sharpen, look back on, hit the ground running. Structures: the language of progress -- wish + past perfect, used to / be used to, cleft sentences, the future perfect, and the more..., the more...

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Come a long way -- to make a lot of progress since you started
"You have come a long way since the first lesson."
Second nature -- something you do so often that you do it without thinking
"Presenting in English is second nature now."
Comfort zone -- the situations where you feel safe and nothing is difficult
"Leading the board review took her out of her comfort zone."
Track record -- all the things a person has done in the past, used to judge them
"Her track record this year speaks for itself."
Pay off -- to bring a good result after a lot of effort
"All that practice paid off in the final review."
Proficiency -- a high level of skill in something, such as a language
"Her proficiency in English opened the door to the Asia review."
Stretch goal -- a goal that is difficult but possible if you push yourself
"Leading the Asia review is a stretch goal for next year."
Momentum -- the energy to keep moving forward once you have started
"Don't lose the momentum when the program ends."
Brush up on -- to practice something again so you do not forget it
"Brush up on your numbers before a big meeting."
Sharpen -- to make a skill better and more precise
"Every rapid round sharpened her answers."
Look back on -- to think about something that happened in the past
"When she looks back on the year, the typhoon week stands out."
Hit the ground running -- to start something new quickly and successfully, with no delay
"She wants to hit the ground running on the Asia review."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

It is the year-end supply chain review, and Fabiana presents the whole year to the board: the fire in Penang, the second supplier in Monterrey, and the typhoon week. When she looks back on the year, she can see that she has come a long way. A year ago she used to read every word on her slides; now she is used to answering questions off the cuff. It is second nature.

After the review, Marcus says her track record speaks for itself. "What helped most was practicing the hard conversations before they happened," she answers. All that practice paid off: Priya wants her to lead the Asia regional review next spring. It is a stretch goal, and Fabiana says, "I wish I had started all this two years ago."

"Better late than never," says Marcus. "The more you do it, the easier it gets." Fabiana makes a plan to keep the momentum: a podcast every week and a recorded presentation every month. "By next December, I will have led my first regional review," she says.

1. "I wish I had started all this two years ago." Did Fabiana start two years ago?
A Yes, she started two years ago.
B No. She started later, and she regrets it now.
C She will start in two years.
2. "Now she is used to answering questions off the cuff." What does this mean?
A It is normal and easy for her now.
B She answered questions in the past, but she does not do it anymore.
C She never answers questions.
3. What does Priya want Fabiana to do next spring?
A Lead the Asia regional review.
B Take a long vacation after the year-end review with the board.
C Train the team in Monterrey.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Language of Progress

GRAMMAR

No new grammar: five structures from the program that help you talk about where you started, what changed, and where you are going.

JobStructureExample
A regret about the pastwish + past perfectI wish I had started earlier.
Then and nowused to + base verb / be used to + -ingI used to read my slides; I'm used to answering questions.
Focus on one ideaWhat + clause + was / isWhat changed most was my confidence.
Finished in the futurewill have + past participleBy December, I will have led a review.
Growing togetherthe + comparative, the + comparativeThe more I practiced, the easier it got.
Questionasking about the futureWill you have finished the plan by March?

Never: I wish I started earlier · I'm used to answer · What changed most it was · I will have lead · the more I practiced, the easiest it got
