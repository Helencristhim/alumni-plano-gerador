Chapter 3: The Whole Toolkit

Five Tools for Progress

Regret, change, focus, the future, and practice

Grammar Discovery

Five Tools for Progress

"I wish I had started this program a year earlier."

"I used to read my slides; now I'm used to answering off the cuff."

"What changed most was my confidence in meetings."

"By next December, I will have led my first regional review."

"The more I practiced, the easier it got."

Each purple structure does a different job. Which one is a regret about the past? Which one compares then and now? Which one puts the focus on one idea? Which one looks at a finished action in the future? Which one shows that two things grow together?

The jobThe toolExample
A regret about the pastwish + past perfect (Lesson 4)I wish I had started earlier.
Then and nowused to + base verb / be used to + -ing (Lesson 9)I used to read my slides; I'm used to answering questions.
Put the focus on one ideacleft: What + clause + was (Lesson 6)What changed most was my confidence.
A finished action in the futurewill have + past participle (Lesson 10)By December, I will have led a regional review.
Two things that grow togetherthe + comparative, the + comparative (Lesson 26)The more I practiced, the easier it got.
The trapon an emotional day, the forms get simpleNot I wish I started, not I'm used to answer.

In one line: the language of progress is the language you already have.
