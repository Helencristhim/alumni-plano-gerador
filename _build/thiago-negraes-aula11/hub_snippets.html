Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

A good travel story has two layers. The first is the scene, the thing that was already happening: "I was walking along the water", "my host family was cooking dinner". For the scene, English uses was or were plus -ing. The second layer is the event, the short action that changes the scene: "it started to rain", "I ran into a classmate". For the event, English uses the simple past. Two small words help: while usually comes before the long action, and when usually comes before the short one. Tell a whole story in the simple past and it still works, but it sounds like a list. The scene is what makes people listen.

1. In "I was walking along the water when it started to rain", which action was already in progress?
A It started to rain.
B I was walking along the water.
C Both started at the same moment.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "While I looked for a bus stop, I was running into a classmate."
B "While I was looking for a bus stop, I ran into a classmate."
C "While I was look for a bus stop, I ran into a classmate."
3. Which word usually comes before the short action?
A when
B while
C was

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past continuous vs past simple

GRAMMAR

One question decides everything: was it already happening, or did it happen at one moment?

FormUseExample
was / were + -ingthe scene: an action in progressI was walking along the water.
simple pastthe event: a short action in the sceneIt started to rain.
Negativewasn't / weren't + -ingWe weren't looking for a restaurant.
Questionwas / were + subject + -ingWhat were you doing when it started?

Use was with I, he, she and it, and were with you, we and they. Verbs of the mind still do not take -ing: "I knew the city after a week", not I was knowing.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best thing to say in each situation.

A colleague asks what you were doing in Canada for a month.
A "I studied English and lived in a homestay every day."
B "I was studying English and living with a host family."
C "I was study English in a homestay."
You want to say you met a friend by chance.
A "While I was waiting for the bus, I ran into a friend."
B "While I waited for the bus, I was running into a friend."
C "I was run into a friend at the bus."
Somebody interrupts your story. What do they ask?
A "What you were doing when it happened?"
B "What did you doing when it happened?"
C "What were you doing when it happened?"
Your plan was to go home, but you stayed out until midnight.
A "We turned out at a bar until midnight."
B "We ended up at a bar until midnight."
C "We were ending up at a bar until midnight."

Stage 5: Think About It

Reflection

No right or wrong. Think in English, tap the microphone and speak freely.

Tell the story of one day of a trip you will never forget, in Vancouver or anywhere else. Start with the scene (where you were, what you were doing, what the weather was like), then say what happened, what you did and how it turned out. Use at least five of the new words: homestay, host family, sightseeing, get lost, run into, all of a sudden, end up, turn out.
