Chapter 3: Background and Action

The Scene and the Event

Two past tenses in the same sentence

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I was walking along the water when it started to rain."

"While I was looking for a bus stop, I ran into a classmate."

"My host family was cooking dinner when I got home."

"We were talking when the restaurant closed."

TenseJob in the storyExample
Past continuousthe background: an action in progress (was / were + -ing)I was walking along the water...
Past simplethe event: a short action that interrupts or moves the story...when it started to rain.
while + continuousintroduces the long actionWhile I was looking for a bus stop...
when + simpleintroduces the short action...when I got home.

One question decides it: was it already happening, or did it happen at one moment? The scene takes was / were plus -ing. The event that changes the scene takes the simple past.

Chapter 4: The Story

Before the Meeting Starts

A colleague asks about Vancouver

Dialogue

So, How Was Vancouver?

M
I heard you spent a month in Vancouver. What were you doing there?
T
I was studying English at a language school, and I was living in a homestay.
M
I did a homestay in Spain in college. It was the best part of the whole year.
T
My host family was great. But on my first Saturday I went sightseeing and got lost.
M
Oh no. What happened?
T
I was walking along the water when, all of a sudden, it started to rain and my phone died.
M
That happened to me in Seattle last year. I was looking for my hotel and the battery just died.
T
While I was looking for a bus stop, I ran into a classmate from Colombia.
M
What luck! So he took you home?
T
Not exactly. We ended up in a small restaurant for three hours, and it turned out to be the best night of the trip.
M
That is a great story. You should tell it at the team lunch on Friday.
