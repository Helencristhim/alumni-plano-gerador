Business Culture: Brazil and the US -- More Direct, More Directly

Ten to twenty minutes before class. How to compare people and the way they work, with adjectives and adverbs side by side. Key words: direct, polite, formal, a deadline, small talk, to get to the point.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Direct -- saying what you think in a clear way, without many extra words
"My American manager is very direct in meetings."
Polite -- speaking and acting in a kind way that shows respect
"Brazilian emails are usually very polite."
Formal -- serious and correct, the way people act in official situations
"The first email to a new client should be formal."
Small talk -- a short, light conversation about simple things
"We always have five minutes of small talk before the call."
To get to the point -- to talk about the important thing quickly
"In the US, people get to the point in the first minute."
A deadline -- the last day or time to finish something
"In Atlanta, a deadline is a deadline."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Diego works with a team in Miami. “My American colleagues are more direct than my Brazilian team. In a meeting, they say more directly what is wrong. Their emails are shorter, too.”

But Diego also sees the other side. “My Brazilian team is more flexible. When a plan changes, they adapt more quickly, and they talk to clients more warmly.”

"They say more directly what is wrong." Why directly and not direct?
A Because it is about a person, so it is an adjective.
B Because it is about how they say it, so it is an adverb.
C Because the sentence is in the past.
What does Diego say about his Brazilian team?
A They write shorter emails.
B They are more direct in meetings.
C They are more flexible and adapt more quickly.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Adjective or Adverb?

GRAMMAR

What a person or thing is: adjective. How a person does something: adverb.

JobFormExample
What they aremore + adjective / adjective + -erAmericans are more direct. Emails are shorter.
How they do itmore + adverbThey speak more directly.
Comparing two groupsmore ... thanThey react more quickly than we do.

Never: They speak more direct · Americans are more directly · more short

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen, then record yourself. Stress the adjective or the adverb, not the word more.

My manager is more direct than my team.
She writes emails more formally than I do.
