Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. All eight are things you can measure against something else.

Elegant -- simple and beautiful, without trying too hard
"The black one is more elegant, but I never wear black."
Casual -- relaxed, for a normal day and not for an event
"My style is casual, so I wear the same three things every week."
Affordable -- cheap enough for a normal person to buy
"That brand is more affordable than it looks."
Limited edition -- made in a small number, and then never again
"It was a limited edition, so it sold out in one day."
Runway -- the long stage where models show a collection
"What you see on the runway is never what reaches the shop."
Accessory -- a small item you add to an outfit, like a bag or a belt
"The cheapest accessory can change the whole look."
Crowded -- full of people, with very little space
"The shop was more crowded than I expected."
Convenient -- easy to use or to get to, and it saves you trouble
"Buying online is more convenient, but I cannot try anything on."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

I had two coats open in two tabs. The first one was cheaper, about sixty dollars, and the shop was the most convenient one: two days of delivery. The second one was more expensive than my whole wardrobe, but the fabric was better and it lasts about four times longer. In my opinion the black one was more elegant, and it was also the most expensive of the two. My friend says the cheap one is fine. I say that in the long run it is worse, because you buy it three times.
Why is it "cheaper" but "more expensive"?
A Because cheap is a short adjective and expensive is a long one
B Because one is in the past and the other is in the present
C Because expensive is an irregular adjective
Why is it "the most convenient" and not "more convenient"?
A Because it compares one shop with all the others
B Because convenient always takes the most
C Because there were only two shops

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Comparatives and Superlatives

GRAMMAR

Two questions decide the form: how many things you are comparing, and how long the adjective is.

short adjective, two thingsadjective + -er + thancheap → cheaper than
short adjective, all of themthe + adjective + -estbig → the biggest
long adjective, two thingsmore + adjective + thanmore affordable than
long adjective, all of themthe most + adjectivethe most elegant
irregulartwo words that ignore the rulegood → better → the best; bad → worse → the worst

Careful 1: never more cheap, and never more good.

Careful 2: the comparative takes than, not that. Portuguese uses one word for both, English does not.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
A friend asks which of two brands you prefer. Which answer keeps the conversation going?
A "I like the second one."
B "In my opinion the second one is better, because the fabric lasts longer."
C "The second."
Which sentence is correct?
A Saturday is the most crowded day of the week.
B Saturday is the more crowded day of the week.
C Saturday is most crowded day of the week.
You want to say that buying online is easier but has one problem. What do you say?
A "Buying online is more convenient, but you cannot try anything on."
B "Buying online is more convenient that the shop."
C "Buying online is the most convenient than the shop."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Choose two things you know well: two brands, two cities, or your two courses. Record ninety seconds comparing them. Use at least four comparatives, one superlative, and one of the irregular pairs (better/worse). End with which one you would choose and why. Do not write a script first — just speak.
