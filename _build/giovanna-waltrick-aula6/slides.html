Chapter 3: The Code

Two Things, or All of Them?

The ending that changes, and the word that appears instead

Discover the Rule

Why Do Some Take -er and Others Take More?

This brand is cheaper than that one.
That coat is more expensive than the whole collection.
It is the cheapest shop in the neighbourhood.
It is the most elegant piece she has ever made.
Click to reveal the rule
Two questions decide everything. How many things? Two things take the comparative (cheaper, more expensive) and always with than. All of them take the superlative (the cheapest, the most elegant) and always with the. How long is the adjective? Short ones take -er / -est: cheap, cheaper, the cheapest; big, bigger, the biggest; nice, nicer, the nicest. Long ones (three syllables or more) take more / the most: more affordable, the most convenient. And two break the rule completely: good → better → the best, bad → worse → the worst. To give an opinion politely, start with In my opinion...

Chapter 4: Two Ways to Buy

The Number Nobody Puts on the Label

Reading

The Cheapest Coat Is Not the Cheapest

The Cheapest Coat Is Not the Cheapest

There is an old idea in fashion called cost per wear. It is very simple arithmetic: you take the price of a piece and you divide it by the number of times you actually wear it. A coat that costs six hundred and that you wear two hundred times is cheaper, by this measure, than a coat that costs sixty and that you wear four times.

The arithmetic is not the interesting part. The interesting part is that almost nobody does it, because the price is on the label and the number of wears is not. The label is certain and the future is not, so the cheaper coat always looks like the safer decision, even when it is the more expensive one.

This is also why the most affordable brands are the ones that change fastest. A piece that is designed to look new for one season does not have to last longer than one season, and that is not an accident. The pieces on a runway are made to be photographed. The pieces in a fast fashion shop are made to be replaced.

None of this means expensive is better. Some of the most elegant things in a wardrobe are the cheapest, and a good accessory can do more for an outfit than a coat that cost ten times as much. It only means that price is one number out of two, and that the second number is the one nobody puts on the label.

Listening 1

The Buyer Who Compares for a Living

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What are the three things he compares before choosing a supplier?
The price, how long the fabric lasts, and how fast they can deliver.
2. Why does he say the cheapest supplier is rarely the best choice?
Because what he saves on the price he loses on returns from customers.
3. What does he say about the most convenient supplier?
The most convenient one is not always the closest one; it is the one that answers the phone.

Two Cities, One Exchange

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What were the two cities she had to choose between, and what decided it?
A big capital and a smaller city. The smaller one had the better course for what she wanted to study.
