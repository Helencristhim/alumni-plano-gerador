Stage 1.1: Board Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Shortboard — a small fast board, good in bigger waves
"A shortboard is faster, but it is harder."
Longboard — a long board, better in small slow waves
"On a flat day the longboard is the best option."
Fin — the piece under the board that keeps it straight
"I lost a fin on the reef."
Volume — how much space a board has, measured in litres
"More volume means the board floats more."
Secondhand — not new -- somebody used it before you
"A secondhand board is much cheaper."
Worth it — good enough for the money or the effort
"The bus takes an hour, but the wave is worth it."
Reef — hard rock or coral under the water
"The reef is the most dangerous part of that spot."
Beach break — a spot where the waves break over sand, not rock
"A beach break is safer for a bad day."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the buyer's note. Then answer the questions.

I looked at three boards. Board A is new and it is the most expensive of the three. Board B is a secondhand shortboard: it is much cheaper and it has more volume, so it floats better. Board C is a secondhand longboard, and it is bigger and slower than both of them. My local spot is a beach break with small waves, so honestly the longboard is the best choice for me, even if the shortboard is faster. A new board is better, of course, but for six months it is not worth it.
Which board floats better, A or B?
A Board A, because it is new.
B Board B, because it has more volume.
Why is the longboard the best choice for him?
A Because his local spot is a beach break with small waves.
B Because it is the fastest one.
Why is it "the most expensive" and not "the expensivest"?
A Because long words take "more" and "the most", not the ending.
B Because it is comparing only two things.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Comparatives and superlatives

The choice is not about meaning. It is about the LENGTH of the adjective.

Short adjectives

fast → faster than → the fastest
big → bigger (double g)
easy → easier (y → i)

Long adjectives

expensive → more expensive than
→ the most expensive
dangerous → more dangerous

Two things always take than: cheaper than that one.
All of them always take the: the best wave of the day.
Two irregulars to learn: good → better / the best  ·  bad → worse / the worst. Never "more good".

Stage 4: In the surf shop

Quiz
You want to compare two boards. Which sentence is correct?
A This board is more fast than that one.
B This board is faster than that one.
One wave was better than all the others. How do you say it?
A It was the best wave of the day.
B It was more good wave of the day.
The board costs more, but you think it is a good decision. What do you say?
A It's more expensive, but it's worth it.
B It's expensiver, but it's worth it.
You want a recommendation for small waves. What do you ask?
A Which one is the better for small waves?
B Which one is better for small waves?

Stage 5: Defend your choice

Reflection
You can only take ONE board to Australia. Choose it and defend the choice out loud for 30 seconds. Why is it better than the others? What is the best thing about it? What is the worst? Is it worth it? Check one thing before you send it — every comparison has "than", and every superlative has "the".
