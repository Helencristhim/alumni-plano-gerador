Grammar Discovery

Why -er here and more there?

A shortboard is faster than a longboard.
A beach break is safer than a reef.
A reef is more dangerous than sand.
This board is more expensive than that one.

It is the length of the word

Short words (one syllable) add -er. Long words (three or more) take more. And after both, the second thing comes with than.

The Code

Two things, or all of them

Comparing TWO — than

fast → faster than
big → bigger than (double the g)
easy → easier than (y turns into i)
expensive → more expensive than

Comparing ALL — the

the fastest board in the shed
the biggest wave of the day
the most expensive one
the most dangerous spot here

Two you just learn: good → better / the best  ·  bad → worse / the worst

Sound Check

The -er is weaker than you think

faster, bigger, safer

the ending is short and soft, never a strong R

It's faster than mine.

the than almost disappears between the two words

the best wave of the day

the superlative always carries the in front

Listening 1

In the surf shop

1. Which board does he say is the fastest, and what is the problem with it?
The shortboard. It is the hardest one to surf and it has the least volume.
2. Why does he recommend the secondhand one?
It is half the price and it is only two years old, so it is better value.
3. What does he say about the most expensive board?
It is the best board in the shop, but it is not worth it for somebody staying six months.
Listening 2

Two beaches, one bus

1. Which beach is closer, and what is wrong with it?
The town beach, ten minutes away, but it is the most crowded one on the coast.
2. Why does she say the other one is worth it?
The waves are much better and it is emptier, even with the longer bus ride.
3. What is her warning about the second beach?
It is a reef, not a beach break, so on a big day it is more dangerous.
