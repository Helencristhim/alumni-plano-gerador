Chapter 4: The Code

Verbs, Nouns and Small Words

The grammar of a line on a chart

Grammar Discovery

Verb or Noun?

“The number rose steadily until June.”

“There was a sharp rise in mock exam scores in the first three months.”

“It reached a peak of just over sixty hours a week.”

“The proportion of students who felt exhausted has doubled since last year.”

Two lines describe the change with a verb, and two with a noun. Look at the word that comes right after the verb, and the word that comes right before the noun.

JobWordsExample
Change as a VERBrise · fall · increase · decrease · drop · fluctuate · level off · remain stableHours rose steadily until June.
Change as a NOUNa rise · a fall · an increase · a decline · a drop · a peakThere was a sharp rise in scores.
How much: VERB + ADVERBsharply · dramatically · considerably · steadily · gradually · slightlyScores rose sharply.
How much: ADJECTIVE + NOUNsharp · dramatic · considerable · steady · gradual · slighta slight drop in hours
Small wordsrise BY 10% (the difference) · rise TO 60 (the end point) · FROM 40 TO 60 · a rise OF 10% · a rise IN scores · reach a peak OF 60Hours rose from 40 to 60.
Approximationroughly · approximately · around · nearly · just over · just underjust over sixty hours
Timepast simple for a finished period · present perfect + since / over the last for up to nowIt has doubled since last year.
The trapa verb with an adjective, or a noun with an adverbNot rose sharp, not a sharply rise.

Listening 1 · A Voice Note

The Line Behind the Number

A basketball analyst on the rule her coach gave her. Sound first — no text.

1. What rule did the coach give her?
Never show him a number without the line behind it.
2. What happened to the team’s three-point attempts?
They rose steadily for four seasons, reached a peak, and then leveled off.
3. What happened to the shooting percentage at the same time?
It dropped slightly.
Listening 2 · A Podcast

Three Steps for Any Graph

A teacher on how to describe a graph so people remember it. Sound first — no text.

1. What are the three steps he suggests?
The big picture, the biggest change, and one detail that surprised you.
2. Why should you round your numbers?
Because nobody remembers forty-seven point three percent, but everybody remembers just under half.
3. What is the difference between rose by and rose to?
By gives the difference; to gives the point where it ended.
