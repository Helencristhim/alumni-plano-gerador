Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A trend -- a general direction in which something is changing over time
"The data shows a clear trend: more hours, but not more points."
A proportion -- a part of a group or an amount, compared with the whole
"A small proportion of the class studies on Sundays."
The majority -- more than half of a group of people or things
"The majority of students in my class want to study medicine."
A minority -- less than half of a group of people or things
"Only a minority of us sleep eight hours a night."
A peak -- the highest point or level that something reaches
"My study hours reached a peak in June."
Approximate -- close to the real number, but not exact
"Sixty hours is an approximate number, not an exact one."
To fluctuate -- to go up and down again and again
"My scores fluctuated between sixty and seventy all semester."
To level off -- to stop rising or falling and stay at about the same level
"After August, my scores leveled off."
On the rise -- increasing; becoming more common
"Burnout is on the rise among cursinho students."
To account for -- to be a particular part or amount of a total
"Medicine students account for almost half of the class."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

English can describe the same change in two ways. You can use a verb: my scores rose sharply. Or you can use a noun: there was a sharp rise in my scores. Notice the small change. After a verb, you need an adverb: rose sharply, fell slightly, increased steadily. Before a noun, you need an adjective: a sharp rise, a slight fall, a steady increase.

Two small words change the meaning of a number. Rose by ten points tells you the difference. Rose to seventy tells you where the number ended. A trend that continues until now takes the present perfect: my study hours have risen since February. A finished period takes the past simple: they rose sharply in March.

Finally, real speakers round their numbers. Instead of forty-seven point three percent, they say just under half. Instead of sixty-one hours, they say just over sixty or roughly sixty.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Language of Trends

GRAMMAR

Verb + adverb, or adjective + noun. Then watch the small words.

JobWordsExample
Verb + adverbrise · fall · increase · drop + sharply · steadily · slightlyMy scores rose sharply.
Adjective + nouna sharp rise · a steady increase · a slight dropThere was a sharp rise in my scores.
Small wordsby (the difference) · to (the end) · from ... to · a rise inHours rose from forty to sixty.
Approximationroughly · around · nearly · just over · just underjust under half
Timepresent perfect + since (until now) · past simple (finished period)It has doubled since last year.

Never: a sharply rise · increased in twenty percent · my hours grew up
