Talk
Hans Rosling — “The best stats you’ve ever seen” (TED)

A doctor and statistician turns fifty years of world health and income data into moving lines and bubbles. It is the best example you will find of numbers that tell a story, and he describes every trend out loud as it moves.

Tip: pause twice and describe what you see in one sentence, with a verb and an adverb: rose sharply, fell steadily, leveled off.

Talk
Mona Chalabi — “3 ways to spot a bad statistic” (TED)

A data journalist on the three questions she asks before she trusts any number. It connects today’s lesson with Lesson 14: when a number is uncertain, you hedge it.

Tip: write down her three questions. Then use them on the infographic you choose for your homework.

Talk
David McCandless — “The beauty of data visualization” (TED)

A designer shows how the same data looks completely different when you draw it well. Useful before you choose a real infographic for your homework.

Tip: choose one of his images and describe it in three steps: the big picture, the biggest change, and one detail.
