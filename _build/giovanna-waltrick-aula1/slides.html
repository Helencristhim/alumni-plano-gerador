Chapter 3: The Code

What Is Always True

One tense for facts and routines — and one pronoun per sentence

Discover the Rule

What Do These Have in Common?

I study Administration and Fashion.
My course starts at seven in the evening.
She works in a fashion studio in New York.
My parents travel to the United States every year.
Click to reveal the rule
All of them are always true — facts and routines, not only today. That is the present simple. With he, she, it the verb takes an -s: she works, my course starts. With I, you, we, they the verb does not change. And one more rule that is not about the verb: when you start a sentence with she, finish it with she. The pronoun does not change in the middle.
