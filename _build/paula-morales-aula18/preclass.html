Lesson 18 -- Pre-class

What Were You Doing? -- The Past Continuous and the Moment Everything Stopped

In lesson 17 you learned was and were and told how things were. Today you stick a verb in -ing behind them and gain something new: telling what was happening at the exact moment something went wrong. And the setting is yours: a fire in warehouse 4 at a client site in Santos, at 6:55 on a Tuesday. You will read the incident report and find out that who was doing what is what decides a claim — and that there is a huge difference between what happened and what was happening. Six new words: happen, claim, warehouse, fire, damage, suddenly. This Pre-class is a first contact — come to class with the six in your ear.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the story and answer the questions. The sentences in bold are today's structure — notice that they all have two parts: was or were, and a verb ending in -ing.

Tuesday, 6:40 in the morning, warehouse 4 at a client site in Santos. Twelve people on the floor, an ordinary morning: twelve people were working in the warehouse. Two men loading a truck at door 2 — two men were loading a truck — and three women checking boxes near the office. And one sentence that looks trivial and is not: nobody was using the old machine.

At 6:55 the night manager was closing his report when he heard a noise: he was closing his report when he heard a noise. He was walking to door 3 when the fire started — he was walking to door 3 when the fire started. It started near the old machine, and it was fast.

The alarm rang and everybody was out in four minutes. Nobody was hurt. And while the team was leaving, one driver was moving his truck away: while the team was leaving, one driver was moving his truck. That truck is intact today. The fire damaged half of the warehouse, and the claim was opened the same morning.

1. In “twelve people were working”, why is it not “twelve people was working”?
A Because working always asks for were.
B Because the rule for was and were is the same as lesson 17: plural (they, twelve people) takes were.
C Because the sentence is negative.
2. In “he was closing his report when he heard a noise”, was the report already finished when he heard the noise?
A No — he was in the middle of the action, and the noise came in and cut it.
B Yes — he finished the report and then heard the noise.
C The sentence says nothing about it.
3. Why is it “he heard a noise” and not “he was hearing a noise”?
A Because hear never takes -ing in any situation.
B Because the SHORT action, the one that arrives and cuts, goes in the past simple from lesson 14 — only the long one stays in was + -ing.
C Because he heard the noise several times.
4. What does the sentence “nobody was using the old machine” do in a claim report?
A Nothing important — it is just extra information.
B It says how much the client will be paid.
C It shows that nobody was doing anything dangerous at that moment — and that is what the reinsurer reads to decide the claim.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- was/were + verbo em -ing

GRAMMAR

You have had the first piece since lesson 17: was for I, he, she, it and were for you, we, they. Today the second one comes in: a verb ending in -ing, stuck behind it. Together, they stop time in the middle of the action.

UsoIn EnglishWhen you use it
Ação em andamentoThe team was loading a truck.the team was loading a truck
Interrupção (when)I was writing the report when the client called.I was writing when the client called
Duas ao mesmo tempo (while)While the team was leaving, he was moving the truck.while the team was leaving, he was moving the truck
NegativeNobody was using the machine. · They weren't working.nobody was using the machine
QuestionWhat were you doing when it happened?what were you doing when it happened?

The three mistakes that feel natural to make, and the fix for each one:

1) “I was write the report when he called.” — you got the was right and forgot the -ing. After was or were, the verb always ends in -ing: “I was writing the report when he called.”

2) “They was loading the truck.” — this is lesson 17 coming back in disguise. With they (and with you and we) it is were: “They were loading the truck.”

3) “I was working when the alarm was ringing.” — you put both actions in -ing. The one that arrives and cuts goes in the past simple: “I was working when the alarm rang.”

And how do you choose between the two past forms? One test only: is this a point on the clock or a line? “The fire started at 6:55” is a point — past simple (the fire started). “At 6:40 the team was loading the truck” is a line, with no start and no end in the sentence — was/were + -ing (the team was loading). And keep this one, because it is the whole lesson: anybody can write what happened; what was happening is what pays the claim.
