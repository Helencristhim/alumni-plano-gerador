Chapter 3: The Code

A Photo of the Middle

O mesmo was e were de sempre — agora com um verbo em -ing colado atrás.

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

As palavras verdes têm duas partes. Qual é a primeira? E o que a segunda tem no fim?

I was writing the report when the client called.

The team was loading a truck at 6:40.

Three women were checking boxes near the office.

Nobody was using the old machine.

A primeira parte é o was ou o were da aula passada. A segunda termina em -ing. E na frase 1: quando o cliente ligou, o relatório estava pronto ou estava no meio?

UsoExemploQuando
Ação em andamentoThe team was loading a truck at 6:40.uma foto do meio da ação
InterrupçãoI was writing the report when the client called.a longa em was + -ing, a curta em past simple
Duas ao mesmo tempoWhile the team was leaving, a driver was moving his truck.as duas rolando juntas
NegativoNobody was using the machine. · They weren't working.wasn't / weren't + -ing
PerguntaWhat were you doing when it happened?was/were vai pra frente
Duas coisas só: (1) a primeira peça é a MESMA da aula 17 — was para I, he, she, it, were para you, we, they. (2) o verbo que vem atrás termina em -ing, sempre: was writing, were checking, was using. E na interrupção, a ação CURTA (a que chega e corta) vai em past simple da aula 14: called, rang, started.
Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I was write the report when he called."
"I was writing the report when he called."
"They was loading the truck at six."
"They were loading the truck at six."
"I was working when the alarm was ringing."
"I was working when the alarm rang."

O primeiro erro esquece o -ing: depois de was ou were, o verbo sempre termina em -ing. O segundo é a aula 17 voltando disfarçada — com they é were, nunca was. E o terceiro põe as duas ações em -ing: a que chega e corta vai em past simple (rang, called, started). Só a ação longa é que fica no meio.

“Send Me the Story”

Dialogue

The Call About Santos

G
Paula, I read the claim from Santos. Warehouse 4. Tell me what happened, and I don't want only the numbers.
P
Of course. The fire started at 6:55 on Tuesday morning, near the old machine.
G
And before that? What was the team doing at that time?
P
They were working normally. Two men were loading a truck at door 2, and three women were checking boxes near the office. Nobody was using the old machine.
G
Good. That is the line the reinsurer will read first. And the night manager?
P
He was closing his report in the office when he heard a noise. He was walking to door 3 when the fire started.
G
So he saw it start. Was anybody hurt?
P
No. The alarm rang and everybody left in four minutes. And while the team was leaving, one driver was moving his truck away from door 2.
G
That driver saved us a truck. Paula, do you know why I asked you for the story and not for the numbers?
P
Because the numbers say how much, and the story says why?
G
Exactly. Anyone can send me a number. Send me the story and I can defend the claim in Miami. Keep writing them like this.
Comprehension

About Greg

1. Why did Greg call Paula about the Santos claim?
He read the claim and he wanted the story, not only the numbers — what everybody was doing when the fire started.
2. Which line does Greg say the reinsurer will read first?
The line that says the team was working normally: two men were loading a truck, three women were checking boxes, and nobody was using the old machine.
3. What does Greg say about the driver?
That the driver saved them a truck — he was moving it away from door 2 while the team was leaving the building.
4. Greg explains why he asked for the story. What is his reason?
The numbers say how much, and the story says why. Anyone can send him a number — with the story he can defend the claim in Miami.
The Artifact

First Notice of Loss

Paula Morales · Zurich, Large Risks
CLAIM BR-4471 — WAREHOUSE 4, SANTOS
Status
Open
What happenedA fire started at 6:55 a.m. on Tuesday, near the old machine.
What was happeningAt 6:40, twelve people were working. Two men were loading a truck at door 2. Nobody was using the old machine.
PeopleNobody was hurt. Everybody left the building in four minutes.
DamageHalf of the warehouse. Photos and the list of the boxes: today.
1. What happened, and at what time? Full sentence.
A fire started at 6:55 a.m. on Tuesday, near the old machine in warehouse 4.
2. What was happening fifteen minutes before that?
At 6:40, twelve people were working. Two men were loading a truck at door 2, and nobody was using the old machine.
3. What does the form say about the people and about the damage?
Nobody was hurt — everybody left the building in four minutes. The fire damaged half of the warehouse, and the photos and the list of the boxes go today.
4. Why does this form have a line called "What was happening"? It is not in the report by accident.
Because it shows that nobody was doing anything dangerous at that moment — nobody was using the old machine, nobody was smoking. That line is what the reinsurer reads to decide the claim. "What happened" says how much; "what was happening" says why.
Listening

David: The Line He Didn't Write

Sound first — no text. Leia as perguntas antes de ouvir.

0:000:00
1. What was David working on twelve years ago, and what did he send to Miami?
He was working on his first big claim — a fire in a factory, at night. He sent Miami a page with correct numbers, good photos and the right dates.
2. What did Miami do, and what was missing from his page?
Miami said no and did not pay for four months. What was missing: nobody wrote what the team was doing when the fire started. Not one line.
3. What did the reinsurer ask, and why could David not answer?
They asked if somebody was working with fire that night, and if somebody was using the old machine. He could not answer because he did not ask the people at the time — he only asked three weeks later, and by then nobody remembered.
4. What is the one thing David wants Paula to keep from the call?
That "what happened" is easy and anybody can write it — "what was happening" is the hard part, and it is the part that pays the claim.
Listening 2

Sofia: Two Questions That Are Not the Same

Sound first — no text. Leia as perguntas antes de ouvir.

0:000:00
1. What does Sofia say Paula did this week for the first time?
Greg called Paula directly about the Santos fire — not Sofia — and Paula answered him in English, alone, about an international account, at seven in the morning.
2. What was different two years ago?
Two years ago Paula was sending the reports to Sofia, and Sofia was talking to Miami. Now Paula is the one talking to Miami — and it happened this week, with no announcement.
3. Sofia says there are two questions that are not the same question. What are they, and why does the second one matter?
"What happened?" — a fire started at 6:55, and anybody can find that. And "what was happening?" — twelve people were working, two men were loading a truck, three women were checking boxes, nobody was using the old machine. The second answer is the one the reinsurer reads first, and the one that decides if the client is paid. It is a small piece of English that carries an enormous amount of money.

Free Practice

The Whole Claim — No Screen, No Notes

Scenario: Greg asks you two questions, one after the other. "What happened in Santos?" And then: "And what was happening before it started?" No screen, no notes. Sixty to ninety seconds.

One rule, and it is the whole lesson: don't stop at what happened. The fire started at 6:55 — that is the easy half. Tell him the other half: twelve people were working, two men were loading a truck, nobody was using the old machine, and while the team was leaving, a driver was moving his truck.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I know my six words: happen, claim, warehouse, fire, damage, suddenly.
After "was" or "were" the verb always ends in "-ing" ("the team was loading a truck").
I use "was" for I / he / she / it and "were" for you / we / they — the same as last lesson.
When something cuts in, the short action goes in the past simple: "I was writing when he called."
I can ask the question that decides a claim: "What were you doing when it happened?"
