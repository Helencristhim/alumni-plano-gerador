Lesson 18 -- What Were You Doing?: The Past Continuous and the Moment Everything Stopped

YouTube
Learn English Tenses: PAST CONTINUOUS (engVid, with Rebecca, free)

A whole class about the structure you learned today — was / were + verb in -ing — in another voice and with other examples. Rebecca writes on the board and covers exactly the points that slip most: the form, the spelling of the -ing (when the final e drops, when the consonant doubles), the negative with wasn't and weren't, and the questions. Hearing the same rule twice, from different people, is what makes it automatic.

Tip: watch it with English subtitles (the video's own CC). Do one thing only: every time she writes a sentence on the board, pause and say ONE sentence of your own in the same mould, about your work (“I was writing the report”, “the team was loading a truck”). By the end of the video you should have said about five. Open channel, no sign-up.

Short video / audio
Past simple and past continuous — 6 Minute Grammar (BBC Learning English, free)

This is the answer to the question that probably stayed in your head after the lesson: when is it started and when is it was starting? Six minutes, two presenters talking, with a direct comparison between the action that started and finished (the point) and the action that was under way (the line). British English, which is good on purpose: you hear American all week on the Miami calls.

Tip: listen twice. The first time, just listen. The second time, pause at each example and decide out loud “point” or “line” BEFORE they answer. Then invent one example of your own for each type, with the Tuesday in Santos (“the fire started at 6:55” × “the team was loading a truck”). Open and free, no sign-up.

Video story
The Race: Shipwrecked — Episode 7 (BBC Learning English, free)

It is not a grammar class: it is an episode of a story, with subtitles on screen. A crew is on a boat and something goes wrong — and to tell what happened they need exactly today's two forms: what was happening before, and what arrived and cut it. It is a chance to hear was and were + -ing loose in a real narrative, and not in a table — which is exactly what you will do when you report a claim to Miami.

Tip: watch it once only and do one thing: write down every sentence you hear with was or were + a verb in -ing. Then close the video and tell the Santos story out loud, the same way: first what was happening at 6:40, then what happened at 6:55. You do not have to understand every word; the goal is to get your mouth producing the structure without freezing. BBC Learning English is open and free, with no sign-up.
