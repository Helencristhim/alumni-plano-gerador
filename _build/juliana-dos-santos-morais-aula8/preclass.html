Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

What Could Go Wrong -- Rules, Risks and Plan B

A delegation is coming in six weeks, and some things are mandatory while others are only a good idea. Key words: a risk, a requirement, to comply with, a backup plan, to approve, paperwork, urgent, a delay, mandatory, optional. Structure: have to, need to, must, should, don’t have to.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A risk -- the possibility that something bad will happen
"The biggest risk is a delay with the visas."
A requirement -- something that you must have or do, because a rule says so
"A valid passport is a requirement for the trip."
To comply with -- to follow a rule or a law
"All sponsors have to comply with the event rules."
A backup plan -- a second plan, ready in case the first one fails
"If the venue cancels, we have a backup plan."
To approve -- to say officially that something is acceptable
"The director has to approve the budget first."
Paperwork -- the forms and documents you need for something official
"The visa paperwork takes about three weeks."
Urgent -- that needs attention or action immediately
"This is urgent: the form is due tomorrow."
A delay -- a situation in which something happens later than planned
"There was a two-day delay at the consulate."
Mandatory -- required by a rule; you cannot choose
"Registration is mandatory for all visitors."
Optional -- that you can choose to do or not to do
"The city tour is optional."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The Canadian delegation arrives in six weeks. Juliana explains the rules to Daniel. “Two participants have to apply for a visa, and the paperwork takes three weeks. They should start this week.”

“Do they need a printed invitation?” Daniel asks. “No, they do not have to bring one,” Juliana answers. “Registration is mandatory, but the city tour is optional. And if the hotel cancels, we have a backup plan.”

Notice the difference. Have to is a rule. Should is advice. Do not have to means it is not necessary, and must not means it is not allowed.

1. How long does the visa paperwork take?
A Three weeks.
B Six weeks.
C Three days.
2. Does the delegation need a printed invitation?
A Yes, it is mandatory.
B No, they do not have to bring one.
C Only for the city tour.
3. Which sentence means “it is not necessary”?
A “You must not bring an invitation.”
B “You do not have to bring an invitation.”
C “You should bring an invitation.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Have To, Must, Should, Don’t Have To

GRAMMAR

Have to and must say something is necessary. Should gives advice. Do not have to means it is not necessary; must not means it is not allowed.

JobFormExample
A rule, necessaryhave to / has to / need to + verbVisitors have to apply for a visa.
A strong rule, often writtenmust + verbAll sponsors must comply with the rules.
Advice, a good ideashould + verbYou should send the documents early.
Not necessarydo not have to / does not have toYou do not have to bring an invitation.
Not allowedmust not (mustn’t)You must not enter without a helmet.
The trapno to after must and shouldNot You must to register. Say You must register.

Never: You must to register · You mustn’t bring it (when you mean it is not necessary) · The visitors has to apply · You should to send
