Chapter 4: The Code

Have To, Should, Don’t Have To

Rules, advice, and what is not necessary

Grammar Discovery

Rules, Advice, or Not Necessary?

"Some visitors have to apply for a visa."

"You should send the documents early."

"You do not have to bring a printed invitation."

"Visitors must not enter the factory without a helmet."

The blue words say that something is necessary or a good idea. The red words are both negative, but they mean very different things: one says it is not necessary, the other says it is not allowed.

JobFormExample
A rule, necessaryhave to / has to / need to + verbVisitors have to apply for a visa.
A strong rule, often writtenmust + verbAll sponsors must comply with the rules.
Advice, a good ideashould + verbYou should send the documents early.
Not necessarydo not have to / does not have toYou do not have to bring an invitation.
Not allowedmust not (mustn’t)You must not enter without a helmet.
The trapno to after must and shouldNot You must to register. Say You must register.

In one line: have to = necessary; should = a good idea; don’t have to = not necessary; mustn’t = not allowed.
