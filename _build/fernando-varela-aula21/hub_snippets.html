Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Incident -- an unexpected event at work, usually a problem
"We had a small incident with the system last night."
Suddenly -- quickly, at a moment when nobody expects it
"I was talking, and suddenly the screen went black."
Interrupt -- to stop someone or something for a short time
"Sorry to interrupt, but the system is down."
Notice -- to see or hear something for the first time
"Nobody noticed the problem until nine o'clock."
Crash -- when a computer or system stops working completely
"The system crashed in the middle of the meeting."
Handle -- to deal with a problem and keep it under control
"Our team handled the problem very well."
Unexpected -- something you did not think was going to happen
"The call from Boston was a little unexpected."
Meanwhile -- at the same time, while another thing is happening
"Meanwhile, the IT team was working on the server."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last Tuesday evening, Fernando was presenting the budget for next year to Sarah and her director in Boston. Everything was going well. Suddenly, his screen went black: the hospital system crashed.

While the IT team was fixing the server, Fernando called Sarah from his phone. Meanwhile, his team was printing the slides. In Boston, Sarah was taking notes when the call stopped. In the end, they finished the meeting by phone, and Sarah's director noticed one thing: Fernando stayed calm.

1. "Fernando was presenting the budget." What does was presenting describe?
A A finished action that took one second, at the moment the screen went black in the meeting room.
B A plan for the future.
C An action in progress at that moment in the past.
2. "While the IT team was fixing the server, Fernando called Sarah." Which action was the long one?
A Fixing the server.
B Calling Sarah from his phone, because the phone call took the whole evening.
C Both were short.
3. Why does the text say "his screen went black" and not "his screen was going black"?
A Because "going" is not a verb that English uses with screens, computers or any kind of machine.
B Because it was a short event that interrupted the presentation.
C Because it is a polite way to talk about a problem with the director.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Past Continuous

GRAMMAR

The past continuous paints the background. The past simple tells the event.

FormRuleExample
AffirmativeI / he / she / it was + -ing · we / you / they were + -ingI was presenting the budget.
Negativewasn't / weren't + -ingWe weren't recording the call.
QuestionWas / Were + subject + -ing?What were you doing at seven?
whenlong action (past continuous) + when + short event (past simple)I was talking when the system crashed.
whilewhile + the background action (past continuous)While IT was fixing the server, I called Sarah.
The trapknow, want, need, believe: no continuousI knew the numbers (not I was knowing)

A good story at work has both: what was happening (the background) and what happened (the event). "I was presenting the budget when the system crashed."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Something went wrong at work. Choose the best thing to say.

Your director asks what you were doing when the system crashed. You say:
A "I was presenting the budget."
B "I presented the budget, and after that the system crashed in the middle of the meeting."
C "I was present the budget."
Your colleague is talking in a meeting, but you have an urgent call from IT. You say:
A "Sorry to notice, but IT is on the phone and they want to talk to somebody now."
B "Sorry to handle, but IT is calling."
C "Sorry to interrupt, but IT is on the phone."
You want to say that two things happened at the same time. You say:
A "The doctors were using paper forms suddenly, and after that the IT team was working on the server."
B "The IT team was working on the server. Meanwhile, the doctors were using paper forms."
C "The IT team worked. Then the doctors used paper forms."
Sarah asks how the story ended. You say:
A "In the end, we were handling the incident well when the meeting finished by phone."
B "In the end, we was handling it."
C "In the end, we handled the incident well."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think of one day at work when something unexpected happened. Take about ninety seconds. Start with "What happened was..." Say what you were doing at that moment, what other people were doing meanwhile, and how it ended. Use at least three sentences with was or were + -ing. Do not stop to correct yourself.
