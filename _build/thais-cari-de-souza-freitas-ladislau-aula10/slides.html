Chapter 4: The Code

Nothing New, All at Once

The words that hold five minutes together

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Actually Do?

"Let me give you an overview first, and then the numbers."

"There are three things here. The first one is the rate."

"To recap: one driver, one decision, one thing I need from you."

"If you remember one thing, the takeaway is that it is one cohort."

Not one of the blue parts carries a fact. Take them all out and the information is still there — but the listener no longer knows what is coming, how many parts there are, or when you are about to finish.

The traprunning out of air before the askSay the ask FIRST if you are short of time. The detail is optional; the ask is not.

In one line: these six signals cost two seconds each and they are what makes five minutes sound planned instead of translated.

Listening 2 · A Podcast

Presenting in Your Second Language

A coach on the seniority that disappears in translation. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say people lose first when they present in a second language?
Not the vocabulary. The structure: the signals that tell the room what is coming and when it ends.
2. What does he say about speaking more slowly?
That slower is better, and that the people who sound most senior in any language are the ones who leave silence between the parts.
3. What is the one thing he tells people to prepare word for word?
The first sentence and the last one. Everything in the middle can be improvised.
