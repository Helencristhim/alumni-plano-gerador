Chapter 3: The Code

Went, Took, Had

Irregular verbs and questions with did

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Show?

"I went to Florida with my son."

"We had a great time."

"Did you go anywhere?"

"I didn’t work on Friday."

Sentences 1 and 2 are in the past, but there is no -ed: go becomes went, have becomes had. These are irregular verbs. Now look at sentences 3 and 4: after did and didn’t, the verb goes back to go and work.

Chapter 4: Before the Meeting

Two Minutes Before the Agenda

One call, two voices, one itinerary

Dialogue

Before the Agenda

D
Hi, Fernanda! Before we start, how was your long weekend?
F
Hi, Daniel! It was great, thanks. I took a day off on Friday, so I had four days.
D
Nice! Did you go anywhere?
F
Yes, I did. I went on a trip to Florida with my son. We had a great time.
D
Florida! How was the flight?
F
It was long, but my son slept. We got back on Tuesday, and I am still a little tired.
D
I know that feeling. I flew to London last month, and I had jet lag for a week.
F
Oh no! Did you go for work?
D
Yes, I did. I visited a customer there. OK, shall we start the meeting?
Comprehension

About Daniel

1. Where did Daniel fly last month?
To London.
2. What problem did Daniel have after the flight?
He had jet lag for a week.
3. Why did Daniel go there?
For work: he visited a customer.
Listening 1 · Anna’s Weekend

Anna Before the Call

Anna, in Germany, talks about her weekend before a meeting starts. Sound first — no text.

1. Where did Anna go on Saturday?
To the mountains, with her husband.
2. What did they do on Sunday?
They stayed at home, cooked and watched a film.
3. What question does Anna ask at the end?
She asks Fernanda how her weekend was.
Listening 2 · Wei’s Holiday

Wei Tells a Story

Wei, in Shanghai, talks about his holiday week in October. Sound first — no text.

1. Where did Wei go for the holiday?
To his hometown, to see his parents.
2. How did he travel, and how long was the trip?
By train, for about six hours.
3. What did he buy for the team?
Some tea.

An itinerary has no verbs. When somebody asks about your trip, you add them in the past: we flew, we arrived, we stayed, we got back.
1. When did they fly out, and when did they arrive?
They flew out on Friday night, and they arrived on Saturday morning.
2. How many nights did they stay at the hotel?
Three nights.
3. Turn the last line into a full sentence.
We got back home on Tuesday morning.
