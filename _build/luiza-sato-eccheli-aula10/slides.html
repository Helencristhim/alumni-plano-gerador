Chapter 4: The Code

Two Years From Now

Four sentences about the future: one finished before a moment, one in progress at that moment

Grammar Discovery

Finished by Then, or Happening Then?

"By the time I apply, I will have taken two practice tests."

"This time in two years, I will be studying in London."

"By December, she will have finished her third semester."

"Do not call me at ten on Friday; I will be taking the test."

All four sentences are about the future. Which ones describe something that will already be finished before a moment, and which ones describe something that will be in progress at that moment? Look at the red and blue forms.

JobFormExample
Finished before a future momentwill have + past participleBy then I will have taken two tests.
In progress at a future momentwill be + -ingThis time next year I will be studying abroad.
The future momentby + time / by the time + present simpleBy the time I apply · by December
A plan that is already arrangedwill be + -ing (neutral, polite)Will you be using the room this afternoon?
Negativewill not have + participle / will not be + -ingI will not have finished by Friday.
After by the timepresent simple, never willBy the time I apply, not will apply.
The trapnever will have took; never by the time I willNot I will have took. Say I will have taken.

In one line: Will have + participle for what is done before the moment, will be + -ing for what is happening at the moment, and present simple after by the time.

Listening 1 · A Writing Coach

Ten Minutes, Three Parts

A coach on the academic discussion task. Sound first — no text.

1. How does the coach divide the ten minutes?
One minute for the outline, eight minutes for the draft, and the last minute to proofread.
2. What should students do with the other students’ posts?
Not repeat them: respond to one of them, and then add something new.
3. Why does she say the outline is not wasted time?
Because it keeps your answer coherent when the clock starts to feel loud.
Listening 2 · An Advisor

Where You Will Be in Two Years

An exchange advisor on planning backwards from the deadline. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the advisor mean by planning backwards?
Starting with the deadline and asking where you will be at each point before it.
2. About how long before the deadline will the student be taking the official test, and why?
About four months before, so that there is still time to retake it.
3. Which milestone does he say is the most important, and why?
The first practice test, because it tells you how far you really are from the requirement.
