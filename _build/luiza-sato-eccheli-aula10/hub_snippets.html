The First Draft -- Plans, Timelines and the Future

The new TOEFL writing tasks reward planning. Write the first draft of your next two years, with the future forms that describe it. Key words: An outline, A draft, To proofread, A thesis statement, A topic sentence, Concise, Coherent, To elaborate, A word count, A milestone, A timeline, To be on track, To revise. Structure: future perfect and future continuous for plans and timelines.

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An outline -- a short plan that shows the main points of a text before you write it
"Make an outline before you write a single sentence."
A draft -- a first version of a text that you will improve later
"My first draft was too long, so I cut it in half."
To proofread -- to read a text carefully to find and correct small mistakes
"Always proofread your email before you send it."
A thesis statement -- the sentence that gives the main argument of an essay
"Her thesis statement was clear from the first line."
A topic sentence -- the sentence that gives the main idea of one paragraph
"Every paragraph needs a topic sentence."
Concise -- short and clear, with no words that are not needed
"A concise answer is better than a long one that wanders."
Coherent -- logical and easy to follow, with ideas that connect well
"The ideas were good, but the paragraph was not coherent."
To elaborate -- to give more details or explanation about an idea
"Could you elaborate on your second reason?"
A word count -- the number of words in a piece of writing
"There is no minimum word count, but a very short answer loses points."
A milestone -- an important stage or event in a process
"Taking the first practice test is a real milestone."
A timeline -- a plan that shows when each step of a process will happen
"We made a timeline for the next two years."
To be on track -- to be making the right progress to reach a goal on time
"If you study every week, you will be on track for the exam."
To revise -- to change a piece of writing to improve it
"I revised the email three times before I sent it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Planning the next two years is a writing task in itself, and it needs two future forms that Portuguese speakers often mix up. Both use will, but they describe the future from different angles: what will already be finished, and what will be happening.

When something will be complete before a moment in the future, use will have + past participle: “By the time I apply, I will have taken two practice tests.” The key phrase is by: by December, by month 14, by the time I apply. After by the time, English uses the present simple, never will.

When something will be in progress at a moment in the future, use will be + -ing: “This time in two years, I will be studying in London.” The same form also makes polite questions about plans: “Will you be using the study room this afternoon?”

A good timeline uses both. Each milestone is something you will have done by a date, and each stage between them is something you will be doing. Write the first draft, keep it concise, and revise it every few months. If you do, you will be on track long before the deadline arrives.

1. According to the text, what does will have + past participle describe?
A Something happening right now.
B Something complete before a moment in the future.
C A habit in the past.
2. What comes after by the time?
A The present simple.
B will + base verb.
C will have + participle.
3. Which sentence describes something in progress at a future moment?
A By December, I will have finished.
B I have finished my outline.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Future Perfect and Future Continuous

GRAMMAR

Will have + participle for what is done before the moment; will be + -ing for what is happening at the moment.

JobFormExample
Finished before a momentwill have + participleI will have taken two tests.
In progress at a momentwill be + -ingI will be studying abroad.
The momentby + time / by the time + presentBy the time I apply
Polite question about plansWill you be + -ing?Will you be using the room?
Negativewill not have + participleI will not have finished by then.
Time phrasethis time next year / in two yearsThis time next year I will be writing.

Never: by the time I will apply · I will have took the test · this time next year I will study right now · I will be taken the test

Stage 2: Put the Writing Process in Order

Order

You have about ten minutes for the academic discussion task. Put your steps in a logical order.

?"Read the professor’s question and the two student posts."
?"Spend one minute on an outline: your position, two reasons and an example."
?"Start your draft by responding to one of the students."
?"Add something new, and elaborate on one reason."
?"Finish with a clear sentence that returns to your position."
?"Use the last minute to proofread for your usual mistakes."
