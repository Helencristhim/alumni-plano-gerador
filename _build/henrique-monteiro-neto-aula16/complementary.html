Lesson 16 -- I Regret to Say, I Regret Saying

Podcast
"Memory and Forgetting" — Radiolab

A classic episode on why a memory is rebuilt every time it is recalled, and why the memories we are surest of are not the most accurate. Connection to Lesson 16: it is the science behind Clara’s notebook — the page does not change, and the memory does.

Tip: listen for every time somebody says I remember + -ing. Then ask yourself, as Clara did, what the page would say.

Talk
"How Reliable Is Your Memory?" — Elizabeth Loftus, TED

A psychologist who spent her career showing how easily confident memories are changed after the fact, and what that means in courtrooms. Connection to Lesson 16: the historian in today’s second listening, with the evidence attached. English transcript on the page.

Tip: she is precise with remember and forget. Note two examples of each pattern and say whether they look back or forward.

Grammar Article
"Verbs That Change with Gerunds and Infinitives" — VOA Learning English

A short American English lesson, with audio, on the verbs that change meaning after -ing or to: remember, forget, stop, try and more. Connection to Lesson 16: the grammar kept outside the class, as you asked.

Tip: after reading, write one sentence for each verb about your own week — one with -ing and one with to — and read the pairs aloud.
