Lesson 05 -- Pre-class

Planning Ahead -- Talking About Decisions Already Made

A year that has not happened yet, with dates on it. Key words: a goal, a thesis, a scholarship, an entry requirement, an offer, an academic calendar, to arrange, to book, to renew, to turn down. Structure: going to for decided plans, with will for decisions made on the spot.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A goal -- what you want to reach, with a date and a way to get there
"My goal is simple: to follow the seminars without a dictionary."
A thesis -- the long piece of writing you have to finish to get the degree
"I am going to write my thesis about delivery clauses in grain contracts."
A scholarship -- money a university gives you so that you can study there
"I am not going to apply for a scholarship this year."
An entry requirement -- something you must have before they can accept you
"The entry requirements are a law degree and five years of practice."
An offer -- the letter that says a university has accepted you
"If the offer arrives in January, I am going to answer the same week."
An academic calendar -- the official list of dates for a university year
"The academic calendar says the first semester is going to start in September."
To arrange -- to organize something so that it is fixed for a future date
"I am going to arrange a call with the admissions team in October."
To book -- to reserve a seat, a room or a flight before you need it
"A trader who books freight in June is going to pay one price."
To renew -- to make a document valid again after it has expired
"I am going to renew my passport before I book anything."
To turn down -- to say no to something that somebody is offering you
"If they only offer me the January intake, I am going to turn it down."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

English has more than one future, and the difference is not about time. It is about when you decided. If the decision existed before this conversation, you use going to: three words in front of the verb, and the verb stays bare. "I am going to submit the last two documents on Friday." "They are going to arrange the interview in November." "The academic calendar says the first semester is going to start in September."

The negative puts not in the middle: "I am not going to apply for a scholarship this year." The question moves am, is or are to the front, and it never calls do: "Are you going to renew your passport first?", "When are they going to answer?" Never do you going to.

Now the second future. Will is for a decision you make during the conversation, in that second. Somebody says "we need one more document today", and you answer "I'll send it this afternoon" — you did not have that plan a minute ago. That is the whole difference, and it is why "I decided in March that I will write about delivery clauses" sounds strange: the decision is old, so the sentence needs going to.

One more use of going to: something you can already see coming, from the evidence in front of you. Two hundred ships are waiting outside the port, so "freight is going to get expensive". And one warning for Portuguese speakers. The present simple is not a future in English. Next month I travel to Amsterdam is wrong; say "I am going to travel to Amsterdam next month."

1. What decides between going to and will?
A How far away the event is: will for the distant future, going to for next week.
B When you decided: before the conversation, or during it.
C How certain you are that it is going to happen.
2. Why is "I decided in March that I will write about delivery clauses" strange?
A Because the decision is old, and will is for a decision made in the moment.
B Because write never goes after will.
C Because March is in the past, so the sentence cannot have a future at all.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "Do you going to renew your passport this month?"
B "Next month I travel to Amsterdam for the interview."
C "Are you going to renew your passport this month?"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You booked the flight last week. Somebody asks about November. You say:
A "In November I travel to Amsterdam for the interview."
B "I'm going to travel to Amsterdam in November. The flight is already booked."
C "I will travel to Amsterdam in November, I think."
The client says: "We need one more document today." You answer:
A "No problem. I'll send it this afternoon."
B "No problem. I am going to send it this afternoon, I decided now."
C "No problem. I send it this afternoon."
You want to ask a colleague about her plans for the interview. You say:
A "Do you going to prepare a speech?"
B "Are you going to prepare a speech, or just answers?"
C "You are prepare a speech for the interview?"
Two hundred ships are already waiting outside the port. You say:
A "Freight is going to get expensive. You can see it from the queue."
B "Freight will get expensive, I decided that last month."
C "Freight gets expensive next month for sure."
