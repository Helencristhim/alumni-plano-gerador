Chapter 3: The Most and the Least

Comparing Two Cultures

Number one, last place, and the same

Grammar Discovery

Number One or the Same?

For Americans, time is the most important thing.

For them, small talk is the least important part of a meeting.

In Brazil, the best way to start is with small talk.

American meetings are not as long as Brazilian meetings.

Brazilian clients are as polite as American clients.

Which sentences say number one? Which say the same, and which say different?

UseStructureExample
Number one (long adjective)the most + adjectiveTime is the most important thing.
Last placethe least + adjectiveIt is the least formal meeting.
Irregulargood > the best · bad > the worstIt was the best deal of the year.
The sameas + adjective + asThey are as polite as us.
Different (less)not as + adjective + asTheir meetings are not as long as ours.

Long adjectives never take -est: the most important, not the importantest. With as ... as, the adjective does not change.

Chapter 4: Claire Comes to Brazil

A Call Before the Visit

One call, two recordings, one plan for a visit

Dialogue

Claire’s First Trip to Brazil

C
Rafael, I’m so excited about my trip to São Paulo next month. Can I ask you a few questions about Brazilian customs?
R
Of course. What is the most important question for you?
C
Time. In Chicago, we are always punctual. Is a meeting in Brazil as punctual as a meeting in the U.S.?
R
Not always. Our meetings are not as punctual as yours, and they are usually longer. We start with small talk.
C
I see. And what about the hierarchy? Can I talk to everybody at the table?
R
Yes, but the director usually speaks first. And the best way to start is to ask about people’s families.
C
Interesting! Americans are very direct. Is that polite in Brazil?
R
It is OK, but we are a little less direct. For us, the relationship is the most important part of a deal.
C
That’s very useful. Last question: is the dress code casual?
R
Not at our headquarters. A suit is the safest choice for the first meeting. Welcome to Brazil, Claire!
