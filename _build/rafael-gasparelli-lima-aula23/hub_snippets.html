Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Eight words to talk about business culture. Tap Listen to hear each one.

Small talk -- a short, friendly conversation about easy topics
"In Brazil, small talk before a meeting can take twenty minutes."
Punctual -- arriving at the right time, not late
"American clients expect you to be punctual."
Hierarchy -- the levels of power in a company, from the top to the bottom
"In many Brazilian companies, the hierarchy is very clear."
Direct -- saying exactly what you think, in a clear way
"Americans are usually very direct in negotiations."
Polite -- speaking and acting in a kind and respectful way
"It is polite to ask about the client's trip."
Relationship -- the connection between two people or two companies
"We have a good relationship with our partners in Chicago."
Custom -- a traditional way of doing something in a country
"A long business lunch is a Brazilian custom."
Casual -- relaxed and informal, not formal
"In many American offices, the dress code is casual."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read Claire's message to her team in Chicago, then answer the questions.

Hi team, I just talked to Rafael about my trip to São Paulo. Here is what I learned. In Brazil, the relationship is the most important part of a deal. The best way to start a meeting is with small talk about family or travel. Brazilian meetings are not as short as our meetings, and they are not as punctual as ours.

The hierarchy is clear: the director usually speaks first. Brazilians are as polite as Americans, but they are a little less direct. And a long lunch is a local custom! The worst mistake is to talk about the contract in the first five minutes. Oh, and the dress code is not casual: I'm taking my most formal suit.

1. According to Claire, what is the most important part of a deal in Brazil?
A The price.
B The relationship.
C The contract.
2. Why does the text say "the most important" and not "the importantest"?
A Because important is a long adjective, so we use the most.
B Because it is about the past.
C Because important is irregular.
3. What does "Brazilian meetings are not as short as our meetings" mean?
A Brazilian meetings are shorter.
B The meetings are the same.
C Brazilian meetings are longer.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Most, the Best, As ... As

GRAMMAR

Use the most / the least for number one and last place, best / worst for good and bad, and as ... as to say two things are the same.

UseStructureExample
Number one (long adjective)the most + adjectiveTime is the most important thing.
Last placethe least + adjectiveFriday is the least formal day.
Irregulargood > the best · bad > the worstIt was the best deal of the year.
The sameas + adjective + asThey are as polite as us.
Different (less)not as + adjective + asOur meetings are not as short as yours.

Never: the more important · the most good · the importantest · as short than
