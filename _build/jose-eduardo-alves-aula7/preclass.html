Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Comparing two things in English costs one decision, and the decision is about the SIZE of the adjective, not about the meaning. Short adjectives change their own ending: big becomes bigger, low becomes lower, fast becomes faster. Two-syllable adjectives that end in -y do the same, with a small spelling change: easy becomes easier. Longer adjectives do not change at all; they borrow a word instead: more complex, more demanding, more competitive.

Whichever shape you use, the second half of the sentence needs than: "This clause is more complex than the last one." Without than, the sentence stops in the middle and the listener waits.

For the top of the list, the same split, plus one small word that is never optional: the. Short adjectives take -est: "Their admission rate is the lowest in the country." Long adjectives take the most: "That is the most demanding program on the list." Three adjectives ignore all of this and you simply have to know them: good becomes better and the best, bad becomes worse and the worst, far becomes further and the furthest.

Two more tools. To go DOWN instead of up, use less and the least: "less famous than", "the least expensive". And when two things are equal, use as ... as: "The second program is as demanding as the first", or in the negative, "not as competitive as the first". Finally, the trap. Your first language lets you put the word for "more" in front of an adjective that already carries the comparison, so the hand writes more bigger on its own. English never allows both at once: it is either the ending or the word in front, never the two together.

1. What decides whether an adjective takes -er or more?
A Whether the comparison is positive or negative.
B The length of the adjective: short ones change the ending, long ones borrow more.
C Whether you are comparing people or things.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "Their admission rate is lowest in the country."
B "Their admission rate is the lowest in the country."
C "Their admission rate is the more low in the country."
3. Why does "more bigger" happen so often to Portuguese speakers?
A Because Portuguese allows the equivalent, so the hand writes it without thinking.
B Because big is an irregular adjective.
C Because more is needed whenever the difference is large.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A client asks why you prefer the second contract. You say:
A "Because the first one is more complexer and more risky."
B "Because its delivery clause is simpler and it names a jurisdiction we know."
C "Because the second is more good for you than the first."
Somebody asks which program is the strongest. You answer:
A "Rotterdam has the lowest admission rate, but the best one for me is Antwerp."
B "Rotterdam has lowest admission rate, but best for me is Antwerp."
C "Rotterdam is the more competitive that Antwerp."
You want to say the two options are equally difficult. You say:
A "The second program is so demanding as the first."
B "The second program is as demanding as the first."
C "The second program is same demanding than the first."
A colleague says "so which city is better?" The strongest first move is:
A "Better for what? For the courts or for the cargo?"
B "Both are good cities with many opportunities."
C "It depends of the situation of each client."
