Lesson 7

Comparing Deals, Programs and Cities

Putting one option above the other, and saying why

Warm-up

Sao Paulo or Miami?

First: two questions about a shipment. Then: for a client who imports diesel, which office is better, and why?

The Target

Three Moves, Two Options

1. Put one option above the other, in one sentence, with the reason attached.

2. Say which one is the best of all — and be ready for the question "the best for what?"

3. Compare two things that are almost equal, without pretending one is perfect.

Diagnostic

Why This One and Not the Other?

Two universities have accepted you. One is famous, one is close to the port.

Choose one and defend it in three sentences. Each sentence must compare it to the other.

Short Words Change, Long Words Do Not

One adjective, three shapes

Grammar Discovery

What Decides the Shape?

"This clause is more complex than the last one."

"Its admission rate is the lowest in the country."

"That is the most demanding program on the list."

The red adjectives changed their own ending. The blue ones did not change at all — a word appeared in front instead. Count the syllables and you have the rule.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Two Cities, One Opinion

One conversation, two recordings, one comparison table

Dialogue

Better for What?

E
You worked in New York before Miami. Which one is better for our clients?
N
Better for what? New York is bigger and the courts are faster. Miami is closer to the cargo.
E
And for you, personally?
N
Miami, easily. My morning is shorter, the office is smaller, and I speak Portuguese half the day.
E
So is the work less demanding here?
N
No, it is more demanding. The clauses are more complex, because everything crosses a border. In New York I had one jurisdiction. Here I have three.
E
Then which is the hardest part of the job?
N
The most difficult part is not the law. It is explaining to a client why the cheapest option is the slowest one. And your two programs — which one is more competitive?
Comprehension

About Nora’s Two Cities

1. What does she say is better about New York, and what is better about Miami?
New York is bigger and its courts are faster. Miami is closer to the cargo.
2. Is the work in Miami easier? What is her reason?
No, it is more demanding. The clauses are more complex because everything crosses a border, and she has three jurisdictions instead of one.
3. What does she call the most difficult part of the job?
Not the law — explaining to a client why the cheapest option is the slowest one.
Listening 1 · The Best for What?

A Name on a Wall, or People Like You

An academic adviser comparing two programs. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say "which is the best" is the wrong question?
Because the right question is "the best for what?" — the answer changes with what you want.
2. Name two things that are better about the second program.
The classes are smaller, the professors answer their email, and the campus is twenty minutes from the port authority.
3. What does she say is the most important number for somebody who is fifty and still working?
Not the ranking — how flexible the timetable is.
Listening 2 · Two Ports

The Cheapest Deal on the Page

Santos or Paranagua, and why the answer is not the freight. Sound first — no text.

1. What is better about Santos, and what is better about Paranagua?
Santos is bigger, faster on paper, and moves more cargo than any other port in South America. Paranagua is closer to the farms in the south, so the trucks are cheaper and arrive sooner.
2. He says the most expensive difference is not the freight. What is it?
The contract — a shorter route with a more complex clause can cost more than a longer route with a simple one.
3. What is his last sentence, and what does it mean for a lawyer?
The cheapest deal on the page is very often the most expensive deal of the year — because the complex clause is the one somebody argues about later.

Both accept applications until 15 September. Neither offers a scholarship to candidates over forty.
1. Which one is more competitive, and how do you know?
Rotterdam. Its admission rate is six percent, which is much lower than the twenty-two percent in Antwerp.
2. Name three things that are better about Antwerp for somebody who still works full time.
The tuition is cheaper, the classes are smaller, and the timetable is in the evening. The campus is also much closer to the port.
3. Which line on this table is not in any ranking, and why does it matter most to you?
The last one: twenty minutes to the port instead of ninety. No ranking measures it, and it decides whether the year is possible at all.
