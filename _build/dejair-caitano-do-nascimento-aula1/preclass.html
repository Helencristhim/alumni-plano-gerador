Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These are the words that carry your science in a conversation.

Field -- the area of science you work in
"My field is pharmacology, and my focus is nerve pain."
To work on -- to spend your time studying one subject
"I work on neuropathic pain caused by leprosy."
Main focus -- the one thing you give most attention to
"My main focus is nerve damage, not pain in general."
To be based at -- the place where you work or study
"I'm based at a research institute in Brazil."
Background -- your studies and experience before now
"My background is in biochemistry and pharmacy."
To attend -- to go to an event, a class or a meeting
"I'm attending a congress in Bangkok in October."
Colleague -- a person who works with you
"I met colleagues from six different countries there."
Topic -- the subject of a talk, a paper or a session
"The topic of the workshop is chronic pain treatment."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Priya Raman is a neurologist from Chennai. She works at a university hospital, and her field is pain medicine. Her main focus is nerve pain in patients with diabetes. Every two years she attends the same international congress, and she always meets colleagues from countries she has never visited. This year she hopes to hear about new research from South America.
Why does the text use "works" and "attends" with an -s?
A Because the actions happened in the past
B Because the subject is "she" -- third person singular
C Because the actions are happening right now
What does "Every two years she attends the same congress" tell you?
A It is a routine -- something that repeats
B She is at the congress at this moment
C She attended it only once

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple

GRAMMAR

Use the present simple for facts, routines and what you do for a living -- things that are always true, not only today.

I / you / we / theyverb with no changeI work on nerve pain.
he / she / itverb + -sShe works at a hospital.
negativedo / does not + verbHe does not teach on Fridays.
questiondo / does + subject + verbDo you attend this congress?

Careful: after does the verb loses the -s. "Does she works?" is wrong -- it is "Does she work?"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
At the congress desk someone asks: "And where are you based?"
A "I am based in a research institute in Brazil."
B "I'm based at a research institute in Brazil."
C "I have a research institute in Brazil."
A colleague speaks too fast and you lose the question. What do you say?
A "I do not understand English."
B "Sorry, could you say that again, please?"
C "Repeat."
Someone asks what you research. Which answer is clearest?
A "I work on neuropathic pain. My main focus is nerve damage caused by leprosy."
B "Neuropathic pain, leprosy, nerve."
C "My research is very difficult to explain."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
You have just arrived at the congress in Bangkok. Someone sits next to you at the coffee break and asks what you do. Record sixty seconds: your name, where you are based, your field, your main focus, and one sentence about why you came to this congress. Do not write a script first -- just speak.
