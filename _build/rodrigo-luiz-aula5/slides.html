Chapter 3: The Pattern

One "Quanto", Two Questions

how much · how many · a lot of · a few · a little

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

How many orders do you have this month?
How much time is the average delay?
We have a lot of orders this month.
Only a few orders are late.

When do we say "how many", and when do we say "how much"?

TypeQuestionQuantity
You can count it
orders, machines, people		How many orders?a lot of · a few
You cannot count it
time, money, work		How much time?a lot of · a little
Botha lot of serve para os dois: a lot of orders, a lot of time.
TruqueSe der para por um número na frente ("two orders"), é MANY. Se não der ("two times" não significa duração), é MUCH.

Chapter 4: The KPI Forum

Nine Countries, Thirty Seconds Each

Brazil is number four

Dialogue

Brazil, Your Numbers

C
Good morning, Brazil. How many orders do you have this month?
R
Good morning, Claire. We have a lot of orders. Two hundred and ten.
C
Two hundred and ten. And how many are late?
R
Only a few. Six orders, so about three percent.
C
Three percent is good. How much time is the average delay?
R
A little. The average is two days.
C
And the cost? Is it on target?
R
Yes, it is. The cost is on target this month.
C
Excellent. Please send the full report by Friday.
R
No problem. The report is ready today. Thank you, Claire.
Comprehension

About the Numbers

1. How many orders does Brazil have this month?
Two hundred and ten.
2. How many orders are late?
Six — about three percent.
3. When does Claire want the full report?
By Friday.
