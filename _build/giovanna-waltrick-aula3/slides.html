Chapter 3: The Code

Putting a Story In Order

Four small words that tell the listener what came first

Discover the Rule

What Are the Orange Words Doing?

First, we arrived at the hotel after midnight.
Then the clerk asked for my passport and my reservation.
After that, he gave me a key card for room 512.
Finally, we went up and the card did not work.
Click to reveal the rule
The verbs are all in the past simple, exactly like last week. What the orange words do is different: they put the story in order, so the listener never has to guess what came first. Use first, then, after that and finally. Notice the last sentence: in a negative, the past sits on did and the verb goes back to normal — the card did not work, never did not worked. Same in a question: Did you call the front desk?

Chapter 4: The Front Desk

Midnight, and the Room Is Not Ready

The conversation where you stop answering and start asking

Listening 1

What the Front Desk Actually Needs

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What two things does he ask every guest for?
A name for the reservation and a document, usually a passport.
2. What does he say guests are afraid of, and why is it not a problem?
They are afraid of asking for things. It is not a problem because most requests are free and easy, and he cannot offer what nobody asks for.
3. What is the one request he says he almost never says no to?
A different floor, when the hotel is not full.

Comprehension

About Marcus

1. Why is the room not ready?
There was a problem with the air conditioning.
2. What does he offer instead, and how long is the wait?
A room on the second floor right away, or about thirty minutes of waiting.
3. What does he offer while she waits?
The bar, and free coffee for the night.
Chapter 5: Practice

Asking Without Sounding Rude

The same request, three different ways

Listening 2

The Night She Said Something

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What was wrong with the first room?
It was next to the elevator and it was very noisy.
2. What did she do on the first two nights, and what changed on the third?
On the first two nights she said nothing and did not sleep. On the third day she went down and asked to change rooms.
3. How long did the whole conversation take?
About two minutes.
