Checking In, Speaking Up -- The Night the Room Was Not Ready

Checking in, asking for what you want, and telling what went wrong. Key words: reservation, front desk, check-in, check-out, floor, key card, would rather, a piece of cake. Structure: past simple with first, then, after that and finally.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. Six of them are things you point at; two of them are how you ask for what you want.

Reservation -- a room you booked before you arrived
"I have a reservation under the name Waltrick."
Front desk -- the counter in the hotel where you check in
"The front desk is open twenty-four hours."
Check-in -- the moment when you arrive and get your room
"Check-in starts at three in the afternoon."
Check-out -- the moment when you leave the hotel and pay
"What time is check-out on Sunday?"
Floor -- one level of a building
"My room is on the fifth floor."
Key card -- the card that opens the door of your room
"My key card stopped working after one day."
Would rather -- used to say which of two things you prefer
"I would rather have a room on a higher floor."
A piece of cake -- something that is very easy to do
"The third time, checking in was a piece of cake."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

We arrived at the hotel after midnight. First, I gave the clerk my passport and the confirmation of my reservation. Then he told me that the room was not ready. After that, he offered us a room on the second floor, but I did not want to change — I said I would rather wait for the room we booked. Finally, thirty minutes later, he came to find us at the bar with a new key card. The second time we checked in, it was a piece of cake.
What do "first", "then", "after that" and "finally" do in this text?
A They say when the story happened
B They put the events in order for the listener
C They make the sentences more polite
"I did not want to change." Why is it "want" and not "wanted"?
A Because the past is already in "did"
B Because the sentence is about the present
C Because "want" has no past form

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Telling a Story in Order

GRAMMAR

The tense is the past simple, the same as last lesson. What is new is the words that put the events in order.

firstthe beginningFirst, we arrived at the hotel.
thenthe next thingThen he asked for my passport.
after thatthe thing after the next thingAfter that, he gave me a key card.
finallythe endFinally, we went up to the room.

Careful 1: after did and did not, the verb goes back to normal. "Did you called?" is wrong — it is "Did you call?"

Careful 2: would rather takes the verb with no to. "I would rather to wait" is wrong — it is "I would rather wait".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
The clerk offers you a room you do not want. Which answer is polite and clear?
A "No. I want the other room."
B "I would rather have the room we booked, if that is possible."
C "I would rather to have the other room."
You want to know if you have to pay extra in the morning. What do you ask?
A "Is breakfast included?"
B "Breakfast is free?"
C "Do I pay the breakfast?"
Which sentence tells the story in the clearest order?
A We went up and the card did not work and we arrived late and I gave my passport.
B First we arrived, then I gave my passport, after that we went up, and finally the card did not work.
C The card did not work, we arrived, I gave my passport, we went up.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Tell the story of a hotel stay, real or invented, where something did not work. Record ninety seconds: what happened first, what you said at the front desk, what they offered you, and how it ended. Use first, then, after that and finally. Do not write a script first — just speak.
