Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Minutes -- the written record of what was said and decided in a meeting
"The minutes said one thing and four people remembered another."
To paraphrase -- to say what somebody said, in different words
"I will paraphrase, because his exact words were not helpful to anybody."
To misquote -- to report somebody's words wrongly
"Nobody misquoted her on purpose. Three people simply heard it differently."
A caveat -- a warning or a condition attached to what you are saying
"He agreed, with one caveat, and the caveat was the whole conversation."
To water something down -- to make something weaker or less strong than it was
"By the third version the warning had been watered down to nothing."
To attribute something to somebody -- to say that a particular person said or did it
"The decision was attributed to the committee, and the committee had not met."
Verbatim -- using exactly the same words, with nothing changed
"She wanted it verbatim, so I wrote down every word, including the pauses."
To read between the lines -- to understand something that was not said directly
"He never said no. You had to read between the lines."
A takeaway -- the main thing you should remember from a meeting
"There was one takeaway and it was in the last two minutes."
To circulate -- to send a document to everybody who needs to see it
"I will circulate the minutes tonight and corrections close on Thursday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Somebody says we are on track. An hour later you write it down for a person who was not there, and the sentence changes shape: she said they were on track. The verb has taken one step backwards, and so have the words for people and time. That step is the whole of reported speech, and it happens forty times in a single recap without anybody thinking about it.

The steps are short and regular. Present becomes past: are to were. Will becomes would: “I will send it” becomes he said he would send it. Can becomes could. And when the thing is still true today, the step is optional: he said the deadline is the fifteenth is correct if it still is.

Two details cause almost every mistake a Portuguese speaker makes here. The first is say and tell. Tell needs a person straight after it — she told me — and say refuses one: she said that, never she said me. The second is what happens to a question. In English a reported question loses its inversion and loses do: “What do you think?” becomes he asked what I thought, never what did I think. A yes-or-no question takes whether or if: she asked whether it was ready.

Requests are a third pattern and the shortest one: “Can you escalate this?” becomes she asked me to escalate it. Ask, plus a person, plus to, plus the verb. And one warning that belongs to the job rather than the grammar: when you paraphrase a caveat, you usually delete it. He agreed is shorter and true, and it has just watered down a sentence whose second half was the entire point.

1. What happens to "will" when you report it?
A It stays the same.
B It becomes would.
C It becomes will have.
2. Which is correct?
A She said me that the filing was ready.
B She told me that the filing was ready.
C She told that the filing was ready.
3. According to the text, what usually happens when you paraphrase a caveat?
A You delete it — he agreed is shorter and true, and the second half was the point.
B You make it longer than it was.
C Nothing changes, because conditions are always recorded.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

The verb takes one step back, and the question loses its inversion.

willwould"I will send it" → he would send it
cancould"We can escalate" → they could escalate

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the line a careful writer would put in the minutes.

Marcelo said: "I will send the final numbers on Friday." You write:
A Marcelo said he will send the final numbers on Friday.
B Marcelo said he would send the final numbers on Friday.
C Marcelo said me he sends the final numbers on Friday.
Rachel asked: "What do you think?" You write:
A Rachel asked what did I think.
B Rachel asked what I thought.
C Rachel asked me what do I think.
Somebody agreed, but only on one condition. The honest line is:
A He agreed.
B He agreed, with one caveat: only if the April file is closed first.
C Everybody agreed and the matter is closed.
You are not sure you have somebody's exact words. The professional thing to write is:
A He said, and I quote, that it was impossible.
B I am paraphrasing, but the takeaway was that he did not think it was possible.
C Nothing. Leave that part out of the minutes.
