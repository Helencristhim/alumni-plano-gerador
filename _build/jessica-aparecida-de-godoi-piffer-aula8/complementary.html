Video
Stanford GSB — “Think Fast, Talk Smart: Communication Techniques”

Fifty-eight minutes of a seminar on speaking under pressure, and the most useful part for this lesson is the structure section: how to summarise somebody else’s point before you answer it. Unscripted American English, with real questions from the room.

Tip: he summarises audience questions before answering them. Write down four of those summaries and notice that every one of them is reported speech.

Podcast
Criminal — “All the Time in the World” (episode 370)

Thirty minutes in which almost nothing is quoted directly — the whole story reaches you through somebody telling you what other people said. It is reported speech doing the work of a narrative, at native speed.

Tip: pick any two minutes and write down every she said, he told her and they asked you hear. Then check what the original sentence must have been.

Talk
Julian Treasure — “How to speak so that people want to listen” (TED)

Ten minutes on the habits that make a listener stop trusting a speaker — including two that matter directly here: exaggeration, and reporting an opinion as if it were a fact. Precise, slow and very easy to follow.

Tip: he lists seven habits to avoid. Decide which two you have seen in a meeting recap you received, and describe them out loud in English.
