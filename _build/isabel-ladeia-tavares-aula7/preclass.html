Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

Something Went Wrong at the Bench -- Reporting a Mistake Without Defending Yourself

The plates are cloudy, the labels are swapped, and the first sentence you say decides what people remember. Key words: a mix-up, a slip, a setback, a root cause, to contaminate, to overlook, hindsight, to flag something, to own up, to trace something back, to cover something up, to make up for something. Structure: must have, might have, cannot have and should have + past participle.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Four of the twelve are expressions.

A mix-up -- a mistake in which two things are confused with each other, such as two labels or two names
"There has been a mix-up with yesterday’s samples."
A slip -- a small, careless mistake made by somebody who normally knows better
"It was a slip, not a lack of knowledge. I knew the rule."
A setback -- something that delays the work or moves it backwards, without ending it
"That is a setback, but a small one."
A root cause -- the original reason underneath a problem, not the first thing you notice
"The cloudy plates were the symptom. The open lids were the root cause."
To contaminate -- to make something impure by letting something unwanted get into it
"Leaving the lids off for ten minutes was enough to contaminate the plates."
To overlook -- to fail to notice something that was there to be seen
"I overlooked the date on the label. It was in front of me the whole time."
Hindsight -- the understanding you have only after something has happened, when it is too late to change it
"In hindsight, I should have checked the sheet before pooling."
To flag something -- to point out a problem so that the right people notice it in time
"Thank you for flagging it before the measurements, not after."
To own up -- to admit that a mistake was yours, before anybody has to ask
"She owned up the same morning, and that is what everybody remembered."
To trace something back -- to follow a problem, step by step, to the moment it started
"I traced it back to my own handwriting on the second rack."
To cover something up -- to hide a mistake so that nobody finds out it happened
"Covering it up would have cost the whole group a week."
To make up for something -- to do something good that compensates for a loss or a mistake
"I can redo it this afternoon and make up for the lost day."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Wednesday morning Isabel finds that two tubes in the second rack are labeled the wrong way round. The handwriting is hers, so she is almost sure about the cause: she must have swapped them when she was relabeling the rack the day before. She goes to Daniel before anybody else notices.

Daniel offers her a way out. Three people used that rack, so it might have been somebody else. Isabel does not take it. It could have been somebody else in theory, but it cannot have been anybody else in practice, because the labels are in her writing. Instead she says the one thing that is useful to him: she should have checked the labels against the sheet before pooling, and she should not have relabeled the rack in a hurry at the end of the day.

Notice that none of these sentences uses the past simple. Each one is a modal, then have, then a past participle, and the modal does all the work: must for what she is sure about, might and could for what is possible, cannot for what she rules out, and should for what did not happen and should have. That is why the report takes three sentences and not ten.

1. "She must have swapped them." What does must mean here?
A She was obliged to swap them, because the protocol told her to do it that way.
B She is almost certain that she swapped them.
C She is planning to swap them again later today, after the measurements.
2. Why does Isabel say it cannot have been anybody else?
A Because she was the only person who used the rack that week, according to Daniel.
B Because Daniel told her that the other two people were not in the lab.
C Because the labels are in her own handwriting.
3. "She should have checked the labels." Did she check them?
A No. Should have is for the right thing that did not happen.
B Yes, and she is proud that she did it carefully before pooling the samples.
C We cannot tell from the sentence whether she checked them or not.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- How Sure Are You About the Past?

GRAMMAR

Modal + have + past participle. The modal tells you how sure you are.

UseFormExample
Almost certainmust have + past participleI must have left the lids off.
Possiblemight have / could have + past participleIt might have been the incubator.
Impossiblecannot have + past participleIt cannot have been the power.
The right thing that did not happenshould have + past participleI should have checked the labels.
The wrong thing that did happenshould not have + past participleI should not have answered the phone.
Obligation in the pasthad to + base verb (not must have)I had to leave early on Friday.

Never: I should checked · I must had swapped · It cannot be the power yesterday · I should of checked.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

You find your own plates contaminated. Your supervisor walks in. The best first sentence is:
A "Somebody must have opened the incubator, because I am always careful with the lids."
B "The plates are contaminated. I think I must have left the lids off yesterday."
C "Nothing, everything is fine, I am just checking the plates again."
You are not sure what caused the problem, but the incubator is one possibility. You say:
A "It must had been the incubator, I think, but I am not completely sure."
B "It was the incubator."
C "It might have been the incubator."
Your supervisor asks what you would do differently. You say:
A "I should have finished the labeling before answering the phone."
B "I should finished the labeling before I answered the phone call yesterday."
C "I must have finished the labeling before answering the phone, it was obligatory."
A colleague left the freezer door open. You want to flag it without accusing anybody. You say:
A "You left the door open and now half the samples are ruined because of you."
B "The door was open this morning. It might have caught on a box. Could we check the top shelves?"
C "I will not say anything, because it is not my problem and it was not my freezer."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Think of something that went wrong this year because of you, at the lab, at university or anywhere else. In one minute, report it as if to a supervisor: what happened, what must have caused it, one thing it cannot have been, and what you should have done. No defending yourself.
