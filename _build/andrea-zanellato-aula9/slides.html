Chapter 3: The Code

Going to Plan, Will Help

Two ways to talk about the future

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Show?

"On Monday, you are going to meet the team."

"We are going to have a workshop on Wednesday."

"You don’t have a laptop? I’ll call IT now."

"Don’t worry. I will help you."

Look at the blue words: going to is for plans you made before (the schedule). The red words: will (I’ll) is for a decision you make now, an offer or a promise.

JobFormExample
A plan (decided before)be + going to + verbYou are going to meet the team.
With I / he / sheam / is going toShe is going to be your mentor.
A decision nowwill + verb (I’ll)I’ll call IT now.
An offer or a promisewill + verbDon’t worry, I will help you.
Negativenot going to · won’tYou won’t be alone.
The trapno to after willNot I will to help. Say I will help.

In one line: going to for the plan you already have; will for the decision, offer or promise of the moment.

Chapter 4: Day One

Day One

One new hire, two voices, one schedule

Dialogue

Monday, 9 a.m. — The First Day

A
Good morning, Josh, and welcome! I’m Andrea, the Head of HR. How was the trip to the office?
J
Good morning! It was fine, thanks. I’m a little nervous, to be honest.
A
That’s normal. Here is your schedule for the week. Today you’re going to meet the team.
J
Great. And when am I going to start on the system?
A
Tomorrow. And on Wednesday we’re going to have a workshop about our values. Please sign up on the website today.
J
OK. Oh, I don’t have a password for the website.
A
No problem, I’ll call IT right now. And Carla is going to be your mentor.
J
That’s great. And when is my first feedback meeting?
A
On Friday, with your manager. Don’t worry, you won’t be alone this week.
Comprehension

About Josh

1. How does Josh feel on his first day?
A little nervous.
2. What problem does Josh have with the website?
He doesn’t have a password.
3. What does Josh want to know at the end?
When his first feedback meeting is.
