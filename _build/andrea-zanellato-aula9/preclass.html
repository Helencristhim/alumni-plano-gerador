Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

Welcome Week -- Onboarding

A new person starts on Monday, and you plan the first week. Key words: onboarding, a new hire, a workshop, a schedule, to sign up, feedback, a goal, a mentor. Structure: going to for plans, and will for decisions, offers and promises.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Onboarding -- the process of welcoming and preparing a new employee
"Our onboarding program takes one week."
A new hire -- a person who has just started in a company
"We have three new hires this month."
A workshop -- a short class where people learn by doing activities
"On Wednesday there is a workshop about our values."
A schedule -- a plan with the times of the activities
"Here is your schedule for the first week."
To sign up -- to put your name on a list to do something
"Please sign up for the safety workshop."
Feedback -- comments about how well somebody is doing
"Your manager is going to give you feedback on Friday."
A goal -- something you want to do or get in the future
"Your first goal is to learn the payroll system."
A mentor -- an experienced person who helps a new person
"Carla is going to be your mentor for three months."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

George is an HR manager. Next month twelve new hires are going to start in his company. On the first day, they are going to meet their mentors. On Wednesday, there is going to be a workshop about the company values.

On Friday, each new hire is going to get feedback from the manager. And if there is a problem? The mentor will help, and HR will answer on the same day. That is the onboarding promise.

Meera is a new payroll analyst. Her first goal is to learn the system. This morning she saw an email about a workshop and thought: I’ll sign up now. Her schedule is full, but she is happy.

1. What are the new hires going to do on the first day?
A Meet their mentors.
B Have a workshop about the values of the company and the history.
C Learn the systems.
2. What did Meera decide when she saw the email?
A To call her manager and ask for a new schedule for the week.
B To sign up for the workshop.
C To learn Excel at home.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “Don’t worry, I will to help you.”
B “Don’t worry, I will help you.”
C “Don’t worry, I going to help you with everything this week.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Going to and Will

GRAMMAR

Use be going to + verb for plans you made before. Use will + verb (I’ll) for a decision you make now, an offer or a promise.

JobFormExample
A plan (decided before)be + going to + verbYou are going to meet the team.
With I / he / sheam / is going toShe is going to be your mentor.
A decision nowwill + verb (I’ll)I’ll call IT now.
An offer or a promisewill + verbDon’t worry, I will help you.
Negativenot going to · won’tYou won’t be alone.
The trapno to after willNot I will to help. Say I will help.

Never: You going to meet the team · I will to help you · She are going to · going to for a decision of the moment
