Chapter 3

How Big Is the Difference?

A little better, or a lot better?

Grammar Discovery

A Little, or a Lot?

"Offer B is slightly cheaper."

"But the commute is far longer."

"Offer A is by far the most flexible."

"The salary is not nearly as high as I expected."

FormUseExample
much / far / a lot / considerably + comparativeA BIG differenceThe commute is far longer.
slightly / a bit / a little + comparativeA SMALL differenceOffer B is slightly cheaper.
by far the + superlativeThe clear winner of a groupIt's by far the most flexible.
not nearly as / nowhere near as + adjective + asA big difference, said politelyIt's not nearly as high as I expected.
just as / about as + adjective + asNo real differenceBoth are just as good.
⚠ CarefulNever use very or more with a comparative: 'much better', not 'very better' or 'more better'. After by far, use the superlative: 'by far the best'. And as ... as, not as ... than.

Chapter 4

Real Voices

Two job offers and a friend who listens

Dialogue

Two Offers

D
Vanessa, can I ask your advice? I have two job offers, and I can't make up my mind.
V
Lucky you! Let's weigh up the pros and cons. What's the upside of the first one?
D
The salary is considerably higher. But the office is downtown, and the commute is far longer.
V
I see. And what about the second offer?
D
It's slightly less money, but I can work from home three days a week. It's by far the most flexible.
V
So the trade-off is money for time. Is the salary as important as your free time?
D
Not nearly as important. The only real drawback is that it's a much smaller company.
V
Then, on balance, I'd go for the second one. It sounds far more like you.
