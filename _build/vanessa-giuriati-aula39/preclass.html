Lesson 39 -- Pre-class

Which One Wins?

Compare two options and say how big the difference is: much / far / slightly + comparative (far longer, slightly cheaper), by far the + superlative and not nearly as ... as. Key words: pros and cons, upside, drawback, trade-off, weigh up, go for, affordable, on balance.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Pros and cons -- The good points and the bad points of something
"Let's write down the pros and cons of each offer."
Upside -- The good part of a situation that is not perfect
"The upside is that the office is ten minutes from home."
Drawback -- A problem that makes something less good or less useful
"The only drawback is the long commute."
Trade-off -- Something you accept to lose so that you can get something else
"Less money for more free time is a trade-off I can accept."
Weigh up -- To think carefully about the good and bad points before you decide
"I need a day to weigh up my options."
Go for -- To choose something from two or more options
"In the end, I went for the cheaper one."
Affordable -- Not too expensive; cheap enough for you to pay
"The second apartment is far more affordable."
On balance -- After you think about all the facts, good and bad
"On balance, the first offer is the better choice."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Last month, my husband and I had to choose a new gym. We weighed up two options. The first gym was slightly cheaper, and it was far bigger, with a pool. But it was forty minutes from home, and that was a big drawback. The second gym was by far the closest: we could walk there in five minutes. The upside was clear: no traffic, no excuses. It was also much quieter in the morning. The equipment was not nearly as modern as in the big gym, but it was just as clean. We wrote down the pros and cons, and we accepted the trade-off: no pool, but more time. On balance, we went for the small one. It is perfectly affordable, and three months later we still go three times a week.

1. What was the big drawback of the first gym?
A It was forty minutes from home.
B It was much more expensive than the second gym, and it closed early on weekends.
C It didn't have a pool.
2. How was the equipment in the small gym?
A It was much more modern than the equipment in the big gym, and it was brand new.
B It was not nearly as modern, but just as clean.
C It was dirty.
3. What was the trade-off they accepted?
A They lost some time, but they got a bigger and more modern gym with a pool.
B They paid more for a pool.
C No pool, but more time.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- How big is the difference?

GRAMMAR

Put a word before the comparative to show if the difference is big or small. Use by far the + superlative for the clear winner, and not nearly as ... as for a big difference, said politely.

StructureUseExample
much / far / a lot + comparativeA big differenceThe commute is far longer.
slightly / a bit + comparativeA small differenceOffer B is slightly cheaper.
by far the + superlativeThe clear winnerIt's by far the best option.
not nearly as + adjective + asA big difference, said politelyIt isn't nearly as high as I expected.

Don't say 'very better' or 'more better': say 'much better'. Use as ... as, not as ... than.
