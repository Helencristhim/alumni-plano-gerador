Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

The Choice You Have Not Made Yet -- Research or Teaching

Somebody asks what you are going to do with a biology degree, and you have three years before you have to answer. Key words: a career path, a trade-off, a fallback, rewarding, daunting, to specialize, a degree, to keep your options open, to weigh up, to put somebody off, to be cut out for something, to commit to something. Structure: talking about a life you have not chosen.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Five of the twelve are expressions.

A career path -- the sequence of jobs a person moves through over the years, in one direction
"My supervisor told me I was throwing away a research career path."
A trade-off -- something you give up in order to get something else. You cannot have both
"The trade-off is two extra semesters for a qualification I might never use."
A fallback -- the second plan, the one you keep for the case where the first one does not work
"People treat teaching as a fallback, and that is exactly the wrong reason to do it."
Rewarding -- giving you real satisfaction, even when the work itself is hard or slow
"Everybody says teaching is rewarding, and almost nobody says what it costs."
Daunting -- frightening because it is large or difficult, before you have even started it
"Choosing a life at eighteen sounds daunting until somebody explains what you are actually choosing."
To specialize -- to become an expert in one narrow area, and to stop doing the rest
"You do not have to specialize in the first year, and most people who do regret it."
A degree -- the formal qualification you receive when you finish a university course
"Doing both adds between two and three semesters to your degree."
To keep your options open -- to avoid deciding on purpose, so that more than one road stays possible
"I am keeping my options open, and that is a decision, not a lack of one."
To weigh up -- to compare the advantages and the disadvantages before you decide
"I have been weighing up two extra semesters against a qualification for life."
To put somebody off -- to make somebody stop wanting to do something, usually by what you say about it
"Everybody tried to put me off teaching, and none of them had ever taught."
To be cut out for something -- to have the right qualities and temperament for a particular kind of work
"I am not sure I am cut out for six hours of pipetting a day."
To commit to something -- to promise yourself to one course of action and stop considering the others
"Nobody is asking you to commit to one life at eighteen."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At this university nobody chooses a track in the first year. Isabel has three years before she has to declare, and she can declare both. That does not stop people asking, and the question sounds daunting until you notice what is really being asked. If she chose the teaching track tomorrow, she would stay two more years. If she did both, her degree would take three more semesters. That is the trade-off, and it is about time, not about identity.

What she is doing instead is weighing up two things at once. She is not going to commit to anything this year, and she is not going to specialize early either. If a friend asked her for advice, she would say the same: if I were you, I would sit in on one class from each track before signing anything.

The thing that annoys her is the word people use. They call teaching a fallback, as if it were what you do when the career path you wanted closes. Everybody who says that has tried to put somebody off it, and almost none of them has ever taught. She is not sure she is cut out for six hours of pipetting a day, and she is not sure she is cut out for a classroom either, and both of those are honest answers. So she is keeping her options open, which people hear as indecision and which is in fact the most rewarding thing she could be doing with a first year.

1. "If she chose the teaching track tomorrow, she would stay two more years." Has she chosen?
A Yes, she chose it yesterday.
B No. The sentence is imaginary, which is why the first half is in the past.
C The sentence does not say anything about her decision.
2. "If I were you, I would sit in on one class." Why were and not was?
A Because this is the fixed form for giving advice about an imaginary situation.
B Because the speaker is talking about more than one person.
C Because it happened in the past.
3. Why does the text say that keeping her options open is a decision?
A Because she has secretly already decided on the research track.
B Because the university does not allow first-year students to decide.
C Because she is refusing to close a road on purpose, which is a choice and not an absence of one.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Talking About a Life You Have Not Chosen

GRAMMAR

Past form after if, would before the other verb, and never both in the same half.

UseFormExample
Imaginary situationIf + past form, + would + base verbIf I chose teaching, I would stay longer.
Either half can come firstno comma when would comes firstI would stay longer if I chose teaching.
Giving adviceIf I were you, I would…If I were you, I would not specialize yet.
Negativewould not + base verbI would not commit to anything this year.
QuestionWhat would + subject + base verbWhat would you do in my position?
Short answerI would. / I would not.Would you declare both? I would not.

Never: If I would choose teaching · If I do both, my degree would take longer · If I were you, I will not specialize · What would you to do? · If I would be you.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

Somebody asks which track you would take if you had to decide tomorrow. You answer:
A "If I would choose, I would take the research track."
B "If I had to decide tomorrow, I would take the research track."
C "If I decide tomorrow, I will take the research track, maybe."
A friend in your year asks for advice about declaring early. You say:
A "If I was you, I will not specialize yet."
B "If I were you, I would not specialize yet."
C "If I would be you, I would not specialize yet."
Somebody calls the teaching track a fallback. The strongest answer is:
A "That is the worst reason to choose it, and an even worse reason to rule it out."
B "Yes, it is my second option if research does not work."
C "I do not know, I have not decided anything."
A relative asks what you are going to do with a biology degree. The best answer:
A "I have no idea, I am only in the first year."
B "I am weighing up two tracks, and I am keeping my options open until the third year."
C "If I would know, I would tell you."
