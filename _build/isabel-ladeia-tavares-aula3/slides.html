Chapter 3: The Code

Talking About a Life That Does Not Exist

Two halves, and the tense of each one is a surprise

Grammar Discovery

What Shape Do These Four Share?

"If I chose teaching, I would stay two more years."

"If I did both, my degree would take three more semesters."

"I would not specialize yet, if I were you."

"What would you do, if the deadline were tomorrow?"

None of these has happened, and yet the red half is in the past. The past there is not time. It is distance from reality. And look at where the blue word never appears.

UseFormExample
Imaginary situationIf + past form, + would + base verbIf I chose teaching, I would stay longer.
Either half can come firstno comma when would comes firstI would stay longer if I chose teaching.
Giving adviceIf I were you, I would…If I were you, I would not specialize yet.
Negativewould not + base verbI would not commit to anything this year.
QuestionWhat would + subject + base verbWhat would you do in my position?
The trapwould NEVER follows ifNot If I would choose teaching…

In one line: past form after IF, would before the other verb, and never both in the same half.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

People Who Already Chose

One conversation, two recordings, one form

Dialogue

Twenty Minutes in the Corridor

V
You look like somebody who has just come out of the progression talk. Let me guess. They made it sound daunting.
I
Slightly. Everyone in there already knew, and I am in my first year. If I had to declare tomorrow, I honestly could not.
V
Nobody in that room knew. They were performing. What are you actually weighing up?
I
Time, mostly. If I did both, my degree would take three more semesters. That is the whole trade-off.
V
It is. Although if I were you I would not specialize early just to save two semesters. What put you off the teaching side, out of interest?
I
Nothing did, really. It is more that people talk about it like a fallback, and I would rather not choose something for that reason.
V
Good. That is the first sensible thing anybody has said to me this week. I declared research, and half my week is still explaining things to first years, so the two are not opposites.
I
Then I am going to keep my options open until the third year, and stop apologising for it. What would you do, if you were starting again?
Comprehension

About Victor

1. What does Victor say about the other people at the progression talk?
That nobody in the room actually knew. They were performing.
2. What is the one piece of advice he gives, and how does he word it?
Not to specialize early just to save two semesters. He words it as if I were you I would not, rather than telling her what to do.
3. Which track did Victor declare, and what does he say about how his week actually looks?
He declared research, and half his week is still spent explaining things to first years, which is why he says the two are not opposites.
Listening 1 · Somebody Who Chose

Everybody Warned Me Off It

A biologist who took the track people told him not to take, on what he would say to you. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What did his own supervisor tell him, and why does he find that strange?
That he was throwing away a research career. He finds it strange because it was said to a twenty-two year old who had not started one yet.
2. What are you actually choosing at this point, according to him?
What to study for two more years. Nobody is asking you to commit to one life at eighteen.
3. Why does he say the two tracks are not opposites, and what does he say about being torn?
Because the best teachers still read the literature every week, and the best researchers can explain their work to seventeen year olds without making it false. And he says that being honestly torn is not confusion, it is information.
Listening 2 · A Recorded Line

The Form, the Deadline and Two Pieces of Advice

You called the department and got the recorded information line. Write down what you have to do, and by when. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. When does the form open and when exactly does it close?
It opens in the first week of March and closes at six in the evening on the last working day of April.
2. Can you declare both tracks, and what does that cost you?
Yes, if you meet the credit requirement. It adds between two and three semesters to your degree.
3. What two things does the department recommend before you decide, and why those two?
Speak to a student already on each track, and sit in on one class from each. Both are free, neither commits you to anything, and in their experience they change more minds than any document.
