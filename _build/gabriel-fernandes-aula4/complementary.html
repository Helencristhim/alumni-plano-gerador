Defining relative clauses - 6 Minute Grammar

Six minutes from BBC Learning English on the half of today's lesson that takes no commas: the clause that tells you which one. Two native speakers build the form out loud, in the same order the lesson used.

Tip: this one covers only the clauses without commas. The next card covers the other half, so watch them in order and the pair will click.

Grammar: Using 'which' to add information - BBC English Masterclass

The other half, and the harder one: the clause that sits inside two commas and adds a comment. This is the form you used to talk about David and about the forty boxes, and it is the one that goes wrong most often in writing.

Tip: after watching, go back to your own homework sentences and read them out loud. Every place your voice pauses should have a comma, and every comma should have a pause.

Documentary
Revenue vs Profit: What's the Difference?

The distinction your spreadsheet is built on, drawn out in English: the money that comes in against the money you keep. Every word here is a word you now own, so watch it as a listening exercise and not as an economics lesson.

Tip: pause it the moment the word revenue appears and say the British word for the same thing out loud. You learned it today.
