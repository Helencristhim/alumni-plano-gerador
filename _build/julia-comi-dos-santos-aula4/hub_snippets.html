Eight words for the internship process (prepare, update, review, search, consider, compete, require, upload) and the difference between "I search" and "I am searching".

Stage 1.1: Words for the Process

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Prepare — to get something ready before you need it
"I am preparing my CV this week."
Update — to change something so it has the newest information
"I am updating my CV right now."
Review — to look at something again to check it
"She is reviewing my cover letter."
Search — to look for something, usually online
"I am searching for internships every night."
Consider — to think carefully before you decide
"I am considering two companies."
Compete — to try to win something against other people
"Two hundred students are competing for the same place."
Require — to say that something is necessary
"Every company requires English."
Upload — to send a file from your computer to a website
"I am uploading my CV to their website."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. Then answer the questions.

I usually search for internships at night, but this week I am searching in the morning too, because the good ones disappear fast. Right now I am sitting in the library with eleven tabs open. I am preparing three applications at the same time and I am updating my CV for each one, because every company requires something different. My friend is reviewing my cover letter today. Two hundred students are competing for the same place, so I am not expecting much — but I am uploading everything anyway. This month I am considering smaller companies. They answer.
When does she usually search for internships?
A At night — but this week in the morning too.
B Only in the morning.
Why is it "I am searching in the morning too" and not "I search"?
A Because it is only this week, not in general.
B Because the morning is a special time.
Why is she considering smaller companies?
A Because two hundred students are competing for the big ones.
B Because they answer.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Two presents, one question: always, or now?

In general → present simple. Now, today, this month → am / is / are + verb-ing.

In general

I search every night.
She reviews my CV.
They require English.

Now / this month

I am searching right now.
She is reviewing it today.
They are not hiring now.

+ I am preparing my CV this week.
− I am not applying to that one.
? Are you searching at the moment? (never "Do you searching")

Nobody says it long: I’m preparing · she’s reviewing · they’re not hiring.

Stage 4: In the conversation

Quiz
A recruiter asks: "What are you doing at the moment?" Which answer works?
A I study Administration and I search internships.
B I'm studying Administration, and I'm looking for an internship for next semester.
You want to say the company is not hiring in this period. Which is correct?
A They are not hiring right now.
B They do not hiring right now.
Which sentence is correct?
A This month I am apply to six companies.
B This month I am applying to six companies.

Stage 5: Your life this month

Reflection

Ask yourself before each: always, or now? Then turn the paper over and record 30 seconds saying them — without reading.
