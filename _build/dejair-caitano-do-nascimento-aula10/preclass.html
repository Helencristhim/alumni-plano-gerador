Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

You already know all eight. This is not learning -- it is checking that they come out without a pause. Say a true sentence with each one before you press Listen.

Main focus -- the one thing you give most attention to
"My main focus is nerve damage caused by leprosy."
To investigate -- to study something carefully to find an answer
"I investigate why the pain continues after the infection ends."
To present -- to give a talk about your work at an event
"I am going to present a poster on Wednesday."
To look forward to -- to feel happy about something that is going to happen
"I am looking forward to meeting your group in Bangkok."
To follow up -- to contact somebody again after the first meeting
"I will follow up next week and send you the protocol."
Relevant -- connected to what you are doing right now
"For my patients, the smaller study is more relevant."
Evidence -- the facts that show something is true
"There is not enough evidence for that conclusion yet."
Sample -- the group of people you actually studied
"Our sample is small, and I say that first."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

One day, eight structures. At eight in the morning: "I am based at a research institute in Brazil, and my main focus is leprosy." At the coffee table: "What are you working on at the moment?" About the past: "I went to a similar event in 2024, but I did not present." About the week ahead: "I am going to take part in the workshop on Wednesday." In the workshop: "Their method is clearer than ours, and for my patients that study is more relevant." When somebody disagrees: "I would rather say it helps many patients than say it works." When you lose the question: "Could you say that again, please?" And at the door: "I will follow up on Monday."
Why is it "I went to a similar event" but "I did not present"?
A Because went is irregular and present is regular
B Because did already carries the past, so the main verb goes back to its plain form
C Because the two things happened at different times
Why is it "What are you working on at the moment?" and not "What do you work on?"
A Because at the moment always needs the continuous
B Because it asks about the project of this period, not about the field in general
C Because it is a more polite question

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Everything, in One Table

GRAMMAR

Nothing new here. Eight structures from nine lessons, side by side, so you can see which one to reach for.

always truepresent simpleI work on nerve damage caused by leprosy.
asking and denyingdo / does + plain verbDoes your group publish in English? / We do not develop drugs.
finished pastpast simple / did + plain verbI went in 2024. / I did not present.
plan and decisiongoing to / willI am going to take part. / I'll send it tonight.
now and temporaryam / is / are + -ingRight now I am collecting data.
comparing-er than / more ... than / the mostclearer than ours / more relevant / the most useful
preferringwould rather + plain verbI would rather say it helps many patients.
asking carefullycould / might + plain verbCould you say that again? / I might have misunderstood.

The three that came back most often: no -s after does, no past after did, and no to after would rather.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
First morning at the desk. Somebody asks what you do. Which answer covers the most ground?
A "I am a pharmacologist. I work on neuropathic pain, and my main focus is leprosy."
B "Pharmacology. Pain. Leprosy."
C "It is complicated to explain in English."
A reviewer says your sample is too small. What do you say?
A "You are right that the sample is small. What the data supports is that pain decreased in most of the group."
B "Sorry, my English is not good enough to explain."
C "Sixty patients is a lot."
The dinner is ending and you want to keep the contact. What do you say?
A "Goodbye, nice to meet you."
B "I will follow up on Monday. Can we exchange contact details?"
C "I am going to follow you up on Monday."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
The whole simulated day, alone, both sides: the registration desk, a stranger at the coffee table, the workshop facilitator, a reviewer who does not like your sample, and somebody at the dinner who asks what you found. Record ten minutes. Repair when you need to, ask questions back, and leave the dinner with one contact and one reason to write.
