Chapter 5: Practice

What Happens After

The part almost everybody gets wrong

Listening 2

Write on the Plane

Read the questions, then listen.

1. When should you write the follow-up emails, and why?
On the plane home, not next week. A contact that is not written down in seven days is gone.
2. How long should the message be, and what should it contain?
Two lines: where you met, and the one thing you promised.
3. Who should you write to, and why?
The people who were kind to you, not only the famous ones -- they are more likely to answer and more likely to be useful.

Chapter 6: The Whole Day

Five People, One Day

From the registration desk to the dinner table, without stopping

Guided

Morning: Desk and Coffee

Eight in the morning. Register at the desk: your name, your institution, your country. Then, at the coffee table, open a conversation with somebody you have never met, say what you investigate in two sentences, and ask what they are working on.

Afternoon: The Workshop

In the workshop, compare two studies and say which one is more relevant to your patients and why. Your partner disagrees: disagree back, with a reason. Then a reviewer says your sample is too small. Accept the limitation, say what your data does support, and do not apologise.

more relevant thanI do not agree, and this is whyour sample is smallwhat the data supports isI would rather say
Free

Evening: The Whole Day, No Stopping

Five people, one after the other, with no pause: the registration desk, a stranger at the coffee table, the workshop facilitator, a reviewer who does not like your sample, and somebody at the dinner who asks what you found. Handle all five. Repair when you need to, ask questions back, and leave the dinner with one contact and one reason to write.
