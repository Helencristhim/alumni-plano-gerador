Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Eight words from the shareholders agreement. Listen to each one and read the example out loud.

Deadlock -- a block where no side has the votes to decide
"The board has been in deadlock since March."
Tag-along -- the right to sell on the same terms as a bigger shareholder
"Without a tag-along right, the minority founder cannot exit with the fund."
Drag-along -- the power to force a minority to sell with the majority
"The acquirer wants a broad drag-along trigger."
Put option -- the right to force the other side to buy your shares
"He can exercise a put option and require the company to buy his shares."
Dilution -- the fall in your percentage when new shares are issued
"We must account for the dilution risk before the next round."
Quorum -- the minimum number of members needed for a valid decision
"The meeting closed again without reaching a quorum."
Veto right -- the power of one member to stop a decision alone
"He claims his veto right covers the share transfer."
Fiduciary duty -- the duty to act in the company's interest before your own
"A director who blocks every exit may fail to fulfill a fiduciary duty."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the file note. The verbs in bold link a closed past to a problem that exists today.

Two years ago Nordeste Labs raised foreign capital. The round closed quickly and the exit provisions were left for a later amendment that nobody ever drafted. There is no tag-along right and no put option in the agreement.

Today one founder wants to sell. The other holds a veto right and blocks every resolution, so the board never reaches a quorum. If the parties had agreed on an exit route at the time of the round, the company would not be in deadlock now. The team must have assumed two founders would always agree, and the provisions should have been drafted before closing.

In "we would not be in deadlock now", when is the result?
A In the past, at the time of the round.
B In the present — today, in the room.
C In the future, after the amendment.
Why "must have assumed" and not "should have assumed"?
A Because the writer is almost sure that is what happened.
B Because assuming it was the right thing to do.
C Because it is only a remote possibility.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Mixed conditional — a past cause, a present problem

The two halves sit in different times:

  • If + had + past participle  →  would / could / might + base verb
  • "If the founders had agreed on a tag-along right, he would not be locked in today."
  • Lesson 2 kept both halves in the past (would have been). Today the result lands in the present (would not be).

Modal perfect — your reading of a past decision

  • must have + past participle — you are almost sure. "They must have assumed it."
  • should have + past participle — it was the right thing, and it did not happen. "It should have been drafted."
  • might have + past participle — it is possible. "They might have underestimated the risk."

Say it slowly. In natural speech have is weak: should've, must've, might've. Rushed, it comes out as should of — and the sentence stops being a modal perfect.

Stage 2: Build the Board Note

Order

This is how the note to the board is built: the history, the block, the analysis, the route. Put the four moves in order.

?"If a tag-along right had been agreed then, he would not be locked in today."
?"The shareholders agreement was signed two years ago with no exit provisions."
?"We can put forward an amendment that fixes the quorum rule for the future."
?"One founder now wants to sell, and the other blocks the resolution."

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Long sentences. Say each one in two halves — the condition, then the result — and keep the weak form: should've, must've.

If the founders had agreed on a tag-along right, Rafael would not be locked in today.
If the agreement had set a quorum of two, the board could decide this week.
The team must have assumed the founders would always agree.
The exit provisions should have been drafted before the round closed.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
The founder asks why he cannot leave. Which answer connects the past gap to today?
A "The agreement had no tag-along right, so we would have sold the shares."
B "If the parties had agreed on a tag-along right, you would not be locked in today."
C "If the parties agree on a tag-along right, you will not be locked in."
The senior partner asks how the gap happened. Which answer analyses without blaming?
A "The lawyer who drafted it forgot the exit provisions."
B "I am sorry, it was a serious failure on our side."
C "The team must have assumed the founders would always agree."
You are almost sure the board misread the scope. Which form says that?
A "The board should have misread the indemnification scope."
B "The board must have misread the indemnification scope."
C "The board might have misread the indemnification scope."
The other side pushes a broad drag-along trigger. Which line disagrees and keeps the room?
A "That is wrong and you know it."
B "I'd push back slightly on that threshold, and here is the risk."
C "We cannot discuss that today."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Think of one deal in your own practice where something was left out of the agreement and the consequence is still open today. Pause one second before you start, and keep the have.
