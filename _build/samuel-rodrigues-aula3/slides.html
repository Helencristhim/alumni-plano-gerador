Grammar

Past Cause, Present Problem

The clause was missing then. The company is stuck now. One sentence holds both.

Discover

What Do These Have in Common?

"If the founders had agreed on a tag-along right, Rafael would not be locked in today."

"If we had drafted a quorum rule, the board could vote this week."

"The team must have assumed the founders would always agree."

"Counsel should have flagged the missing put option."

Mixed conditionalModal perfect
A past condition, a present result.
If + had + past participle
→ would / could / might + base verb
back then · at the time · today, now		Your reading of a past decision.
must have = you are almost sure
should have = it was the right thing, and it did not happen
might have = it is possible

Lesson 2 kept both halves in the past. Today the second half lands in the present: that is what makes it mixed.

Practice

Past Condition, Present Result

"If they had included a put option, he ___would have an exit route today."
"If the agreement had set a quorum of two, the board ___could decide this week."
"The board ___must have misread the indemnification scope." (you are almost sure)
"The acquirer ___might have underestimated the regulatory risk." (it is possible)
The Habit

Slow Enough to Keep the Have

1. Pause. One second before the sentence. Nobody counts it but you.

2. Say the weak form on purpose. should've · must've · might've — never should of.

3. Then expand. Condition first, result second, one breath each.

The Case

Nordeste Labs

Two founders. Foreign capital. No exit route.

Listening 1

The Founder Who Wants Out

0:000:00
1. What is Rafael blocked by, and who holds it?
A veto right, held by his co-founder at board level.
2. Which two exit routes does he say the agreement does not have?
A tag-along clause and a put option.
3. What does he want to know, besides his options today?
What they should have written into the agreement back then.
The Call Before the Call

Two Sentences for Diane

D
Samuel, I have five minutes before the call. Give me the Nordeste position in two sentences.
S
If the founders had agreed on a tag-along right at the time of the round, Rafael would not be locked in today.
D
Good. That is the sentence I want the room to hear first. And the block itself?
S
His co-founder claims a veto right over the transfer, so the board never reaches a quorum and we sit in deadlock.
D
Whose failure was that, in your reading?
S
Nobody in the room. The team must have assumed two founders would always agree, and the exit provisions should have been drafted before the round closed.
D
Careful there. Say that slowly on the call, or it comes out as should of and the board hears an accusation.
S
Understood. Should've been drafted. Structural, not personal.
D
And what is still on the table today? The fund will ask.
S
A negotiated buy-out, a call option on the remaining shares, and an amendment that fixes the quorum rule for the future.
D
One more. If they push a broad drag-along trigger in the amendment, what do you say?
S
I'd push back slightly on that threshold. If we had accepted it in the previous round, we would still be in litigation today.
D
That is the call. Open with the consequence, name the gap, then offer the route. See you in five.
Comprehension

What Did Diane Say?

1. In what order does she want the call opened?
The consequence first, then the gap, then the route.
2. Why does she tell him to slow down on "should have"?
Said fast it comes out as "should of", and the board hears an accusation instead of an analysis.
3. What does she ask him about the drag-along trigger?
What he would say if the other side pushed for a broad threshold.
Listening 2

Lead With the Consequence

0:000:00
1. What does she tell him NOT to lead with?
The gap. He should lead with the consequence and name the gap after.
2. What two things does that first sentence do at once?
It shows the problem is structural, not personal, and that he can separate the drafting failure from the people in the room.
3. What three routes does she say are still available?
A negotiated buy-out, a call option on the remaining shares, and an amendment fixing the quorum rule.
