1. What was she doing at 23:47, and what interrupted her?
She was reading the delivery clause when the shipbroker called.
2. What were the lawyers doing at a quarter past midnight, and what was the broker doing?
The lawyers were arguing about the cause of the delay, and at the same time the broker was asking the port for the berth log.
3. Why does this note have a middle column at all?
Because a list of events does not explain anything. The middle column says what was already going on when each event cut across it.
Chapter 4: The Code

The Long One and the Short One

One moment, two tenses

Grammar Discovery

Two Verbs, Two Shapes

"She was reading the clause when the phone rang."

"While the lawyers were arguing, the broker was asking the port."

"We were finishing due diligence when the regulator asked for a document."

"The deal closed eleven days late, and nobody backed out."

Every red verb was already going on. Every blue one happened once and finished. The last sentence has no red at all — nothing was interrupted there.

JobFormExample
The long action, already going onwas / were + verb-ingShe was reading the clause.
The short action that cuts across itpast simpleThe phone rang.
Joining them with whenwhen goes with the SHORT oneShe was reading when the phone rang.
Joining them with whilewhile goes with the LONG oneWhile she was reading, the phone rang.
Two long actions at onceboth in was / were + -ingWhile they were arguing, he was asking the port.
Setting the scenestart the story with the long oneIt was almost midnight and she was still working.
The trapboth verbs in past simple = both finished, one after the otherI read the contract when he called means you read it AFTER the call.

In one line: the action that was already running takes was or were plus -ing; the one that cuts it takes the plain past.

Listening 1 · The Worst Hour

A Fact Beats an Argument

A commodity lawyer on the hour before the decision. Sound first — no text.

1. What were the three people doing at eleven at night?
Her associate was checking the numbers, the client was waiting on the other line, and she was reading the delivery clause for the third time.
2. She says the first question is never "who is right". What is it?
What exactly was happening at that moment, and who can prove it.
3. Who ended the argument, and what does she say the lesson is?
Somebody with no law degree, by asking the port for a document. The lesson is that a fact beats an argument, and the fact is usually somewhere nobody is looking.
Listening 2 · How Deals Die

Nobody Argued. Nobody Sent It.

Six weeks of silence, and one document. Sound first — no text.

1. What was everybody doing before the regulator asked for the document?
They were finishing due diligence, the lawyers were writing the last version, and everybody was expecting a signature before Christmas.
2. Why did nobody send the document?
Because each side thought the other one was sending it.
3. He says a deadlock almost never looks like a fight. What does it look like?
Nothing happening — the deal simply stops moving while everybody is waiting for somebody else.
