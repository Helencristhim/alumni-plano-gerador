Chapter 3: The Code

To Finish, or Finishing?

Some verbs want to. Some verbs want -ing.

Grammar Discovery

Look at the Next Verb

"We plan to finish the migration in June."

"The vendor agreed to send a weekly report."

"Let's avoid changing the scope after week two."

"I suggest having a short check-in every Monday."

What do the orange words have in common? Which verbs take to, and which take -ing?

PatternVerbsExample
verb + to + verbplan, agree, need, expect, decide, want, would likeWe plan to finish in June.
verb + -ingavoid, suggest, finish, keep, considerLet's avoid changing the scope.
Pastonly the first verb changesThey agreed to send a report.
Negativenot before to / -ingWe decided not to add training.

In one line: plan, agree, need, expect + to; avoid, suggest, keep, finish + -ing.

Chapter 4: Day One

The First Meeting with the Vendor

One meeting, two stories, one page of notes

Dialogue

Rafael and Priya, Day One

P
Thanks for joining, Rafael. I'd like to start with the scope. Is training part of it?
R
No, training is not in the scope. We plan to train our team ourselves.
P
Okay. And who is the point of contact on your side?
R
That's me for now. But I suggest having one point of contact for each workstream.
P
Good idea. What are your expectations for the first month?
R
We expect to see the network part finished by the end of April. And please avoid changing the timeline without telling me.
P
Fair enough. We need to ramp up our team first, so the first two weeks will be slow.
R
That's fine. Let's agree to have a short check-in every Monday at nine.
Comprehension

About Priya

1. What does Priya want to talk about first?
The scope, and if training is part of it.
2. What does Priya want to know about Rafael's side?
Who the point of contact is.
3. Why will the first two weeks be slow, according to Priya?
Because her team needs to ramp up first.
Listening 1 · The Delivery Manager

Three Rules for a Kickoff

A delivery manager on how she starts every project. Sound first — no text.

1. When does she agree on the scope, and why?
In the first meeting. If you wait, people add things and the timeline moves.
2. How many points of contact does she want for each workstream?
One.
3. How long should the weekly check-in be?
Fifteen minutes. If it takes an hour, people stop coming.
Listening 2 · The IT Manager

The Worst Kickoff

An IT manager remembers a project that started badly. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the two teams never talk about in the first kickoff?
Responsibilities: who does what.
2. What happened three months later?
A server failed, both teams said it was not their job, and they lost two weeks arguing.
3. What question does he ask at the end of every kickoff now?
Is there any task without a name?
