Stage 1.1: Words for Feedback

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say each word out loud. Eight words, three minutes.

Feedback — what people tell you about your work, to help you
"The professor gives feedback after every presentation."
Room for improvement — something that can be better
"There is some room for improvement in the slides."
Concise — short and clear, with no extra words
"Your part was concise and clear."
Pace — how fast or slow you speak
"Your pace was too fast at the end."
Eye contact — when you look at the people you talk to
"Your eye contact was much better today."
Filler words — sounds like um or you know, with no meaning
"Try to use fewer filler words."
Take on board — to listen to advice and use it
"Thank you. I’ll take that on board."
Spot on — completely correct, with no mistakes
"The numbers were spot on."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the text. Then answer the questions.

After our presentation, the professor gave group four her feedback. First the good news: our numbers were spot on, and my part was concise. Then the room for improvement. The presentation was too long — twenty-two minutes, not fifteen. The slides had too many words, and at the end my pace was too fast. We didn’t have enough time for questions. A classmate also said I used too many filler words, but that my eye contact was good enough. I wanted to explain, but I just said, “Thank you. I’ll take that on board.” On Friday, we cut five slides. It was short enough, and we had enough time for every question.
What was spot on?
A The time of the presentation, because it was exactly fifteen minutes.
B The numbers.
Why is it “too long” and not “long enough”?
A Because it was more than OK — a problem.
B Because the presentation was shorter than the limit that the professor gave the group.
What did he say to the feedback?
A He explained to the class why the slides had so many words and numbers.
B “Thank you. I’ll take that on board.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

More than OK — or the right amount?

Too + adjective = a problem. Adjective + enough = OK. Enough + noun = the right amount.

too + adjective

It was too long.
My pace was too fast.

too many / too much

too many slides, words
too much text, information

adjective + enough

It was clear enough.
It wasn’t short enough.

enough + noun

We had enough time.
We didn’t have enough questions.

× “enough clear” — after the adjective: clear enough
× “too much slides” — you can count slides: too many slides

For a compliment, use very: “very clear”, not “too clear”.

Stage 4: After the presentation

Quiz
Which sentence is correct?
A “The slides were clear enough.”
B “The slides were enough clear for the class and for the professor.”
A classmate says your pace was too fast. What is the best answer?
A “No, it was not. In Brazil everybody speaks fast, so it is normal for me.”
B “Thank you. I’ll take that on board.”
You want to give useful feedback. What do you say?
A “The numbers were spot on, but it was a bit too long.”
B “It was good, really good, I liked everything in your presentation very much.”

Stage 5: Feedback for yourself

Reflection
Think about your last presentation or meeting at the bank. Record one minute in English. Give yourself feedback: one thing that was spot on, and one point of room for improvement — with too or enough (“It was a bit too long”, “I didn’t have enough time ...”).
