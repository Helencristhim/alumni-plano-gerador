Stage 1.1: Heat-Day Routine

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes. In "muscles", the c is silent.

Routine — the same things you do, in the same order, every time
"Leah has the same routine before every heat."
Focus — to think about one thing only, and nothing else
"Music helps me focus."
Energy — the power in your body to move and do things
"She eats a banana to get energy."
Breathe — to take air in and out of your body
"Close your eyes and breathe slowly."
Muscles — the parts of your body that help you move and be strong
"She jogs to warm up her muscles."
Prepare — to get ready for something
"She uses the last hour to prepare her mind."
Distraction — something that takes your attention away
"The crowd is a big distraction."
Hydrated — with enough water in your body
"Drink a lot of water to stay hydrated."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read Renato's message to his parents, between his heats. Then answer the questions.

Mom, Dad, I advanced! Second place in heat three. The quarterfinal is at two, so now I'm following Dana's plan. I ate a sandwich and some fruit to get energy, and I'm sitting in the shade in order to stay hydrated. The sun here is really strong. Dana gave me a surf magazine with a story about a pro surfer and her routine. She closes her eyes and breathes slowly to prepare her mind, and she wears headphones so that she can't hear the crowd. I'm going to try it! At one thirty I'm going to jog to warm up my muscles. My phone is a big distraction, so I'm giving it to Jess so that I can focus. That means no messages until three. Wish me luck!
"I ate a sandwich to get energy." What does "to get energy" tell us?
A When Renato ate the sandwich, in the morning after his heat.
B Why he ate it: the reason.
"She wears headphones so that she can't hear the crowd." Why "so that" here?
A The reason is a full sentence, with a subject and can't.
B Because the sentence is about music, and we always use "so that" with songs.
Which sentence is correct?
A I'm sitting in the shade for to stay hydrated in the sun.
B I'm sitting in the shade to stay hydrated.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Purpose: three ways to say why

After the action, give the reason: to + verb (most common), in order to + verb (a little formal), or so that + subject + verb (often with can / can't / don't).

to / in order to + verb

I eat a banana to get energy.
I came here to learn English.
I jog in order to warm up.

so that + sentence

I stretch so that I don't get hurt.
I sit wide so that I can catch a set.
I give Jess my phone so that I can focus.

Question: Why do you stretch?  ·  Short answer: To warm up. / So that I don't get hurt.
Remember: for the reason, use to alone, with no for before it, and the base verb after it: to get, to stay.
Your voice: "to" is weak and short: tuh-GET. "So that" is one block.

Stage 4: Between heats

Quiz
Bev asks why you came to Australia. Answer her.
A I came here to learn English.
B I came to Australia for to learn English and to surf every day.
Tom asks why you stretch before a heat.
A So that don't get hurt in the water with the big waves.
B So that I don't get hurt.
The sun is very strong, and your heat is in an hour.
A I'm going to drink water to stay hydrated.
B I'm going to drink a lot of soda to stay hydrated in the sun.
Your phone keeps buzzing before the heat.
A My phone is a routine that helps me prepare my muscles.
B My phone is a distraction.
Dana asks why you want to watch heat one.
A To see where the sets are breaking.
B For see where the sets are breaking this afternoon, Dana.

Stage 5: Your routine

Reflection
You have a big heat tomorrow. Record 30 seconds: tell me your routine from the moment you wake up to the horn, and give a reason for every step (to..., in order to..., so that I can...). Check one thing before you send it: no "for" before "to".
