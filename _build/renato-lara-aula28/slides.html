Grammar Discovery

Why does she do it?

Leah eats a banana to get energy.
She drinks a lot of water in order to stay hydrated.
She stretches so that she doesn't get hurt.
She wears headphones so that she can't hear the crowd.

What do the orange words have in common? What question do they answer?

purpose (why?): to + verb · in order to + verb · so that + sentence

to is the most common. in order to means the same, a little more formal. so that needs a full sentence, often with can / can't or a negative.

The Code

Three ways to say why

Action + purpose

I came to Perth to learn English.
I jog in order to warm up.
I set two alarms so that I don't wake up late.
I sit wide so that I can catch the big ones.

How it works

to + base verb: to learn, to get, to stay
in order to + base verb: the same, more formal
so that + subject + verb: so that I can, so that she doesn't

For the purpose, English uses to alone — with no for before it.

Sound Check

Weak to

I eat a banana to get energy.

"to get" sounds like tuh-GET

I stretch so that I don't get hurt.

"so that" is one block: so-THAT

I came to Perth to learn English.

the first "to" is a place, the second is a reason — both weak

Practice

Give the reason

I watch the ocean for ten minutes. (see where the sets break) — use "to"
I watch the ocean for ten minutes to see where the sets break.
Leah sits down and closes her eyes. (prepare her mind) — use "in order to"
Leah sits down and closes her eyes in order to prepare her mind.
I put my phone in the van. (I / not / get a distraction) — use "so that"
I put my phone in the van so that I don't get a distraction.
Dana stands near the water. (we / can / see her signals) — use "so that"
Dana stands near the water so that we can see her signals.
