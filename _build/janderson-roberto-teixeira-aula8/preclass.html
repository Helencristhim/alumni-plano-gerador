Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

How Much, How Many -- Menus, Bills and the Nouns English Refuses to Count

The two boxes every noun in English falls into, and the eight that surprise Portuguese speakers. Key expressions: a bill, a tip, to split, a receipt, change, a starter, a budget, cash, to afford, plenty of. Structure: countable and uncountable nouns with quantifiers.

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. These ten stay here in your material -- they do not disappear after the class.

A bill -- the paper that says how much you have to pay
"Could we have the bill, please?"
A tip -- extra money you leave for good service
"Nobody agrees about how much of a tip is normal."
To split -- to divide the cost between several people
"Shall we split it, or shall I pay and you send me your part?"
A receipt -- the paper that proves you paid
"Keep the receipt, because the company needs it."
Change -- the money you get back when you pay more than the price
"I never have any change, only cards."
A starter -- the small dish you eat before the main course
"We only had a starter and a coffee and it was plenty."
A budget -- the amount of money you have available to spend
"The budget for the whole trip is not enormous."
Cash -- money in notes and coins, not on a card
"There was not much cash on the table, so we paid by card."
To afford -- to have enough money to buy something
"We can afford one good dinner, not four."
Plenty of -- more than enough of something
"There is plenty of time before the meeting."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

There were eight of them at the table and only one bill. Nobody had any cash, which is normal now, and nobody had any change either, which made the arithmetic worse. They had eaten well: three starters between eight people, plenty of bread, and a great deal of coffee.

Janderson offered to pay the whole thing. There was a small argument, of the pleasant kind, and in the end they decided to split it. He asked how much the service charge was, because in some countries it is already on the bill and in others it is not, and the difference is roughly a tip either way.

He asked for a receipt because the company needs one, and because a payment with no paper does not exist as far as the finance team is concerned. The budget for the trip was not enormous. They could afford one good dinner, not four, and this had been the good one.

There is one small grammatical detail hiding in that whole story. You can count the people, the tables, the starters and the receipts. You cannot count the money, the cash, the change, the bread or the coffee — not in English, anyway. There is plenty of time to learn which is which, and about eight of them will surprise you.

1. Why does the text say "a great deal of coffee" and not "many coffees"?
A Because coffee is uncountable when it means the substance.
B Because the sentence is formal.
C Because there were more than ten cups.
2. Which of these is countable in English?
A change
B a receipt
C cash
3. "There is plenty of time to learn which is which." What does plenty of do here?
A It only works with uncountable nouns.
B It works with both countable and uncountable nouns, so you never have to choose.
C It means a small amount.
4. According to the text, what does the finance team need?
A Cash and change.
B A receipt, because a payment with no paper does not exist for them.
C A bigger budget.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Countable and Uncountable

GRAMMAR

Every noun in English goes into one of two boxes, and the box decides which small words are allowed next to it. Most nouns are obvious. About eight are not, and those eight are the whole lesson.

FormWhat it saysExample
CountableYou can put a number in front and it has a plural.one bill, two bills
a receipt, three receipts
UncountableA substance or an idea. No a, no plural, and the verb is singular.money, cash, change, time
"The news is good."
how many / how muchMany counts; much measures."How many people?"
"How much money?"
a few / a littleA small amount, in each box."a few tables" · "a little cash"
The words for both boxesSome, any, a lot of and plenty of never ask you to choose."plenty of time" · "plenty of tables"
The eight that surprise youEnglish refuses to count these, and Portuguese counts most of them. To count one, borrow a container.money, cash, change, information, advice, news, luggage, furniture
"a piece of information"

The two that cost most at work are information and advice. Both are uncountable, both are constantly used in a bank, and both sound wrong the moment they take an S.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are finishing dinner with colleagues and the bill has arrived. Choose the best thing to say.

You want to know the total cost:
A How many money is it?
B How much is it?
C How many is the money?
There is not a lot of cash on the table:
A There is only a few cash.
B There is only a little cash.
C There are only a few cashes.
A colleague told you something useful:
A He gave me a good advice.
B He gave me a good piece of advice.
C He gave me some good advices.
You want to offer to divide the cost:
A Shall we split it?
B Shall we divide the bill in the middle?
C We make the division of the account?
The company needs proof of the payment:
A Could I have a receipt, please?
B Could I have a paper of payment, please?
C I need the document that proves the transaction, please.
