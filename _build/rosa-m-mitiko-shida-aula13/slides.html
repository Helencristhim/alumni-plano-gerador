Chapter 3: The Code

Doing or To Do?

enjoy working · decide to go · stop to or stop doing

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I enjoy working with international clients."

"She suggested reviewing the proposal before we submit it."

"We decided to postpone the regional meeting."

"The director agreed to present the expansion plan."

Two of the colored verbs end in -ing and two start with to. What decides which one comes after enjoy, suggest, decide and agree?

FormWhat it saysExample
verb + -ingenjoy · avoid · consider · suggest · recommend · finish · keep · admit · mind · risk · involve"She suggested postponing the meeting."
verb + towant · need · decide · plan · hope · expect · agree · refuse · offer · manage · afford · tend · arrange · fail"We managed to reach a decision."
both — the meaning changesremember, stop, try: with to it is the action you do next; with -ing it is the action itself, or a memory."I remembered to send it." (I did not forget)
"I remember sending it." (I have the memory)
after a prepositionAlways -ing. That includes the to of look forward to, which is a preposition, not part of a verb."I look forward to meeting you."
"Before submitting it, check the figures."

There is no perfect logic: the lists are learned by use. A helpful tendency is that to often looks forward (decide, plan, hope, agree), while -ing often talks about the action itself (enjoy, avoid, admit, finish). And suggest never takes to: she suggested reviewing it.

Same Verb, Different Meaning

What Is the Difference?

Explain the difference out loud, then click to compare

1. "I stopped to check the email." / "I stopped checking the email."
"In the first, I stopped what I was doing in order to check it. In the second, I do not check it any more."
2. "I remembered to send the contract." / "I remember sending the contract."
"In the first, I did not forget to do it. In the second, I have a memory of doing it."
3. "Try to arrive early." / "I tried calling, but nobody answered."
"Try to = make an effort. Try + -ing = do something as an experiment, to see if it works."
4. "She stopped to talk to the client." / "She stopped talking to the client."
"In the first, she paused to talk to him. In the second, the conversation ended."
Chapter 4: The Meeting After the Meeting

A decision, a suggestion and one honest admission

Dialogue

After the Team Meeting

J
The director decided to postpone the regional meeting until the third quarter. Nobody expected that.
R
I am not surprised. Santiago kept asking for more time, and we cannot afford to lose them.
J
He also suggested reviewing the whole proposal before we submit it again. He refused to sign it as it is.
R
Fair enough. Did anybody admit missing the deadline, or did we all just go through the motions?
J
I did. I admitted forgetting to send the Lima figures. I remember sending the draft, but not the numbers.
R
Then let us not put off the call. I will arrange a meeting with Santiago for Thursday, and you can give the new format a shot.
J
Fine. But clients tend to say yes in the room and change their minds by email. Is it worth calling them first?
R
It is always worth calling first. And remember to copy the director, so he knows we managed to move it forward.
Comprehension

About Julio

1. What did the director decide, according to Julio?
To postpone the regional meeting until the third quarter.
2. What did the director suggest, and what did he refuse to do?
He suggested reviewing the whole proposal, and he refused to sign it as it was.
3. What does Julio admit?
That he forgot to send the Lima figures. He remembers sending the draft, but not the numbers.
4. What is Julio worried about with the clients?
That they tend to say yes in the room and change their minds by email.
The Real Thing

The Follow-Up Email

Email · to Andres Vidal, Regional Director, Santiago
Team meeting — decisions and pending actions

Decisions. We decided to postpone the regional meeting until September. The director agreed to present the expansion plan himself, and he suggested reviewing the full proposal before we submit it again.

Pending. I have arranged to call the two Santiago clients on Thursday. Julio will send the Lima figures today — he admitted missing Monday's deadline.

Please remember to copy me on any change to the fares. I look forward to hearing from you after the call.

1. Why does Rosa write look forward to hearing and not look forward to hear?
Because the to in look forward to is a preposition, and after a preposition the verb takes -ing.
2. Why does she mention that Julio admitted missing the deadline?
So that the director knows why the figures are late, and that the person responsible has already taken ownership of it.
Listening

Moved to September

A voicemail from the regional director in Santiago. Sound first — no text.

1. When is the regional meeting now, and why did it move?
In September, because they cannot afford to fly twelve people in before the new fares are approved.
2. What does Andres suggest, and why?
Keeping the Thursday call as planned, because two clients refuse to wait until September for an answer.
3. What does he ask Rosa to remember, and why?
To send the revised proposal before Friday, because last time they agreed on everything in the room and forgot to write it down.
Listening 2

Where Decisions Die

Advice from a senior account manager. Sound first — no text.

1. Where does she say most decisions die, and why?
In the week after the meeting, because everybody agrees to do something and nobody arranges a time to do it.
2. What should you do if you cannot afford the time this week?
Say so. Refusing politely is more professional than accepting and doing nothing.
3. Which colleague is easy to work with, according to her?
One who admits mistakes early, for example: I forgot to send the figures on Monday.
