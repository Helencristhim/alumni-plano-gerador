Lesson 13 -- Pre-class

Getting the Words Right -- Plans, Decisions and Commitments

Verbs followed by -ing or by to, for the decisions, suggestions and promises that come out of every meeting. Key expressions: to arrange, to afford, to refuse, to admit, to tend to, to involve, to put off, to manage to, to give it a shot, to go through the motions, to be worth, to look forward to. Structure: suggest doing, decide to do, and remember to or remember doing.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. These twelve stay here in your material -- they do not disappear after the class.

To arrange -- to organize the details of something so that it can happen
"I will arrange a call with the Santiago team for Thursday."
To afford -- to have enough money or time to do something
"We cannot afford to lose another corporate account this year."
To refuse -- to say clearly that you will not do something
"The director refused to sign the proposal as it was."
To admit -- to agree, often unwillingly, that something is true
"Julio admitted forgetting to send the Lima figures."
To tend to -- to usually do something, or to be likely to do it
"Clients tend to book late when prices are going up."
To involve -- to include something as a necessary part
"The new role involves traveling to Lima every month."
To put off -- to delay something until a later time
"We cannot put off the decision until next quarter."
To manage to -- to succeed in doing something difficult
"We managed to close the deal before the convention."
To give it a shot -- to try something, even if you are not sure it will work
"The new format might not work, but let us give it a shot."
To go through the motions -- to do something without real interest or effort
"Nobody believed in the plan, so we just went through the motions."
To be worth -- useful or important enough to justify the time or effort
"It is worth calling the client before you send the email."
To look forward to -- to feel happy about something that is going to happen
"I look forward to meeting the new team in Buenos Aires."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Monday the commercial team met to plan the next six months in South America. It was a long meeting, and it ended with three decisions that Rosa now has to put into an email for the regional director.

First, the team decided to postpone the regional meeting until September, because they could not afford to fly twelve people to Lima before the new fares were approved. Nobody enjoyed making that decision, but everybody agreed to accept it.

Second, the director suggested reviewing the whole proposal before submitting it again. He refused to sign the current version, and he recommended asking the legal team for help with the contract terms.

Third, Julio admitted forgetting to send the Lima figures. He remembered sending the draft, but not the numbers. Rosa did not make it a problem. She arranged to call the two clients in Santiago on Thursday and asked Julio to stop worrying and send the figures that afternoon.

1. Why did the team decide to postpone the regional meeting?
A Because the director preferred to hold it in Santiago instead of Lima, closer to the clients.
B Because they could not afford to fly twelve people to Lima before the new fares were approved.
C Because Julio forgot the figures.
2. What did the director suggest?
A Signing the current version of the proposal as soon as the legal team approved it.
B Cancelling the proposal.
C Reviewing the whole proposal before submitting it again.
3. What does "Julio remembered sending the draft" mean?
A He has a memory of sending the draft.
B He did not forget that he still had to send the draft to the director before Thursday.
C He sent the draft twice.
4. What did Rosa ask Julio to do?
A To call the two clients in Santiago himself on Thursday, and to apologize for the delay.
B To stop worrying and send the figures that afternoon.
C To write the email to the director.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Verbs Followed by -ing or to

GRAMMAR

Some verbs are followed by -ing, some by to + base verb, and a few take both with a different meaning. There is no perfect rule, so learn the ones you use at work.

FormWhat it saysExample
verb + -ingenjoy, avoid, consider, suggest, recommend, finish, keep, admit, mind, risk, involve"She suggested postponing the meeting."
verb + towant, need, decide, plan, hope, expect, agree, refuse, offer, manage, afford, tend, arrange, fail"We managed to reach a decision."
remember · stop · tryWith to: the action you do next. With -ing: the action itself, or a memory."I remembered to send it." · "I remember sending it."
after a prepositionAlways -ing, including the to of look forward to."I look forward to meeting you."
negativenot goes before the second verb."We decided not to sign." · "I regret not calling earlier."

The verb that most often goes wrong in business emails is suggest: it never takes to. Say I suggest reviewing it or I suggest that we review it.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are writing to clients and colleagues after a planning meeting. Choose the best sentence.

1. You want to propose a review of the contract. You write:
A I suggest reviewing the contract.
B I suggest to review the contract with the legal team before we send it to the client.
C I suggest review the contract.
2. You end an email to a new client. You write:
A I look forward to hear from you and to working with your whole team next quarter.
B I look forward hear from you.
C I look forward to hearing from you.
3. You explain why you said no to a discount. You write:
A We cannot afford to lose more margin.
B We cannot afford losing more margin on this route, so the discount is not possible this year.
C We cannot afford lose more margin.
4. A colleague forgot to send a file. He tells the director:
A I admit to forget the file, but I will send it to you and to the legal team before the end of the day.
B I admit forgetting the file.
C I admit forget the file.
5. You are not sure a new idea will work, but you want to test it. You say:
A Let us go through the motions and present the idea to the director anyway, even if nobody believes in it.
B Let us put it off.
C Let us give it a shot.
