Chapter 4: Which, Who, Where

Saying Exactly Which One

Four small words that turn a vague sentence into a precise one

Grammar Discovery

What Does the Coloured Word Do?

"This is the system that handles our cross-border settlement."

"The vendor who presented yesterday offered a proof of concept."

"There are two places where a person still moves a file by hand."

"The reporting change, which was approved last quarter, decides the whole timeline."

Cover the coloured word with your finger and read the line again. In the blue ones the sentence stops making sense. In the red one the sentence survives, and the commas tell you why.

JobWordExample
Says WHICH thing, out of severalthat · whichThis is the system that handles settlement.
Says WHICH personwhoThe vendor who presented yesterday offered a pilot.
Says WHICH placewhereTwo places where a person still moves a file by hand.
Extra information, not identificationcomma + which / who + commaThe reporting change, which was approved last quarter, decides the timeline.
The testremove the clause: does the sentence still identify the thing?Yes = commas. No = no commas.
The trapno second pronoun after the relativeNot the system that it handles. Say the system that handles.
The other trapuse where for places, not thatNot the place that we lose the file. Say the place where we lose the file.

In one line: one small word turns "the system" into "the system that handles settlement" — and in a room with three systems, that is the difference between being understood and being nodded at.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Three Ways to Survive a Live Question

And the page that answers half of them for you

Listening 1 · No Slides

Fifteen Words, and Then Silence

The best explanation of a payment system, given when the projector failed. Sound first — no text.

1. What was the sentence he used to describe the whole system?
Money leaves the client, sits in three places on the way, and arrives two days later.
2. What did he offer the room after that sentence?
To take them through any of the three places they wanted, in any order.
3. Why does he say a forty-box diagram fails?
Not because it is complicated, but because the shape arrives last.
Listening 2 · Taking Questions

You Do Not Have to Answer Immediately

Three moves that buy time, and none of them makes you look weaker. Sound first — no text.

1. Where does she say the fear comes from?
From the belief that you have to answer immediately.
2. What are the first two moves?
Repeat the question back in your own words, which buys five seconds and checks understanding. And say which part you will answer first, when the question has two halves.
3. What is the third move, and why does she say it sounds senior?
Say you do not have that number, name the person who does, and say when you will come back. She says improvising is what junior people do.
Listening 3 · About the Word Workaround

Describe the Real Picture, Not the Ideal One

A head of architecture on why he names the ugly parts out loud. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does he say a workaround should not be an apology?
Because each one exists because somebody solved a real problem quickly, with what they had.
2. What does he describe when a supplier asks about his architecture?
The real picture, not the ideal one, including the two places where a person still moves a file by hand every morning.
3. What does he say happens on the supplier side when he does that?
The good suppliers relax, because they can plan, and the weak ones change the subject.
