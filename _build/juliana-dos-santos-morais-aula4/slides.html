Where We Are Now

Every partnership meeting has the same question in the middle: where are we now? This lesson is about the short, clear answer, and about the tense that carries it.

Chapter 1: First Words

The Question in Every Meeting

You can now present yourself, plan a meeting and take the floor. In the second or third meeting with a partner, the first question is always about progress: what have you done since the last call?

Think of a project at the chamber. What have you finished this month, and what is still open?

Chapter 4: The Code

Have Done, or Did?

Progress without a date, and events with a date

Grammar Discovery

Four Lines from One Update

"We have already sent the invitations."

"We sent them on Monday."

"The director has not signed off on the budget yet."

"So far, twelve companies have confirmed."

Look at the red words. Only one sentence has a specific date: on Monday. The others talk about progress up to now: already, yet, so far. The verb in blue changes when the date appears.

JobFormExample
Progress, no datehave / has + past participleWe have sent the invitations.
Done before nowalready (middle of the sentence)We have already signed the agreement.
Not done, but expectednot + yet (end)The director has not signed it yet.
Very recentjustI have just sent you the draft.
A specific time in the pastpast simple + on Monday / last week / in MayWe sent them on Monday.
The trapno present perfect with a dateNot I have sent it yesterday. Say I sent it yesterday.

In one line: no date = have + participle; a date = past simple.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

The Status Report, and Two Voices

One document, two recordings

Real Document

The Page for the Monthly Call

STATUS REPORT
Business breakfast · Brazil — Canada partnership · October
InvitationsSent · 15 companies confirmed so far
VenueBooked · contract signed on October 3
BudgetPending · waiting for the director
MenuPending · hotel prices not received yet
SetbackSpeaker from Toronto cancelled on October 10
Overall statusOn track for November 12
Look at the lines with a date: the contract was signed on October 3, the speaker cancelled on October 10. Those use the past simple. The lines without a date use the present perfect: we have sent, fifteen have confirmed.
1. Turn the Venue line into two sentences: one without a date, one with a date.
We have booked the venue. We signed the contract on October 3.
2. What is pending, and why?
The budget, because the director has not signed off on it yet, and the menu, because the hotel has not sent the prices yet.
3. Is the event on track?
Yes, it is on track for November 12.

The Update for the Breakfast

An event coordinator gives a status update in a partnership call. Sound first — no text.

Fifteen.
2. What is still pending?
The final menu, because the hotel has not sent the prices yet.

Listening 2 · The Manager

Why We Fell Behind

A manager explains a delay to a partner. Sound first — no text.

1. What was the setback?
Their main contact at the partner company left the job in August.
2. What has the team done since then?
They have met the new contact twice and sent her all the documents.
3. How far behind is the project?
About three weeks.
