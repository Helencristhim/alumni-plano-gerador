Lesson 04 -- Pre-class

Where We Are Now -- Giving a Status Update

In every partnership meeting somebody asks: where are we now? Key words: progress, a milestone, to be on track, to fall behind, a status update, pending, a draft, to sign off on, a setback, to wrap up. Structure: present perfect (already, yet, just, so far) vs past simple.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Progress -- the movement of a project toward its end
"We have made good progress on the partnership this month."
A milestone -- an important point or date in a project
"Signing the agreement was the first milestone."
To be on track -- to be doing what the plan says, at the right time
"The event is on track for November."
To fall behind -- to be slower than the plan
"We have fallen behind because of the holidays."
A status update -- a short report that says where a project is now
"Could you send me a status update by Friday?"
Pending -- waiting for a decision or an action
"Two contracts are still pending."
A draft -- a first version of a document, not the final one
"I have just sent you the draft of the agreement."
To sign off on -- to give final approval to a plan or a document
"The director has signed off on the budget."
A setback -- a problem that stops or slows progress
"The visa problem was a small setback."
To wrap up -- to finish a task or a meeting
"We wrapped up the first phase last week."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Daniel asks for an update, and Juliana answers in three lines. “We have already sent the invitations, and so far fifteen companies have confirmed. We signed the contract with the hotel last Thursday.”

“The budget is still pending,” she continues. “The director has not signed off on it yet. But the event is on track for November.”

Notice the pattern. When there is no date, Juliana uses have or has plus the participle. When she says last Thursday, she uses the past simple: signed.

1. Why does Juliana say “we signed the contract” and not “we have signed”?
A Because she says the date: last Thursday.
B Because the contract is pending.
C Because it is a plan for the future.
2. What is still pending?
A The invitations.
B The budget.
C The contract with the hotel.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “The director has signed off on it yet.”
B “The director has not signed off on it yet.”
C “The director did not signed off on it yet.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect vs Past Simple

GRAMMAR

Use have / has + participle for progress up to now, with no date. When you say the date, use the past simple.

JobFormExample
Progress, no datehave / has + past participleWe have sent the invitations.
Done before nowalready (middle of the sentence)We have already signed the agreement.
Not done, but expectednot + yet (end)The director has not signed it yet.
Very recentjustI have just sent you the draft.
A specific time in the pastpast simple + on Monday / last week / in MayWe sent them on Monday.
The trapno present perfect with a dateNot I have sent it yesterday. Say I sent it yesterday.

Never: We have sent them last Monday · The director has signed it yet · So far, twelve companies confirmed · I have sent you already the draft
