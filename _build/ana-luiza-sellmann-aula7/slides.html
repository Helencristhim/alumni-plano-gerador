Chapter 3: The Code

True for Years, Not True Anymore

Two words instead of a whole explanation

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I used to hide behind the sofa."

"There used to be a bakery on that corner."

"I didn't use to eat anything green."

"Did you use to walk to school?"

Look at the last two. The word used lost its D. Why?

FormUseExample
used to + base verbA habit or a situation that was true for a long time and is not true now.I used to walk to the bakery every Saturday.
didn't use toNegative. The did already carries the past, so use loses its D.I didn't use to eat anything green.
Did you use to...?Question. Same rule: no D after did.Did you use to live here?
One time onlyFor something that happened once, use the past simple: "I went to New York in January", never "I used to go in January".
SoundSay YOOS-to, one block, with an S sound. The D is written but not heard.

Two words that replace a whole sentence: it was true for years, and it is not true anymore.

Detail

Two Structures That Look the Same

A PAST HABIT

"I used to work in an office."

Before, for years. Not anymore.

SOMETHING THAT FEELS NORMAL NOW

"I'm used to working from home."

It was strange at first. Now it is normal.

One is a habit that ended. The other is a habit your body accepted.

You do not have to master the second one today. Just recognise it when you hear it.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I didn't used to eat anything green."
"I didn't use to eat anything green."
"We used to walked to the bakery every Saturday."
"We used to walk to the bakery every Saturday."
"I used to go to New York in January."
"I went to New York in January."

After did the D disappears, and after used to the verb is always the base form. And a single trip with a date is the past simple, never a habit.

Chapter 4: Back Then

Somebody Who Remembers You Small

The old street, thirty years later

Dialogue

On the Old Street

V
Wait. Ana? You used to be about this tall. You used to run down this street with your brother every afternoon.
A
We did. Honestly, the whole street used to look much bigger to me. Back then it felt enormous.
V
Everything did. There used to be a bakery on that corner, where the bank is now. Your mother used to take you there on Saturdays.
A
I remember the smell better than the bread. Do you still live in the same building?
V
Same flat, thirty years. I didn't use to imagine staying that long. Nowadays everybody moves every two years.
A
That sounds old-fashioned in the best possible way. I've moved four times and I miss out on exactly this: knowing the neighbours.
V
Did you use to say you wanted to draw for a living? Or am I inventing that?
A
You're not inventing it. It wasn't a phase, apparently. I work in advertising now, and I still draw badly on purpose.
Comprehension

About Victor

1. What does he say used to be on the corner?
A bakery, where the bank is now.
2. How long has he lived in the same flat, and did he expect that?
Thirty years, and no: he did not use to imagine staying that long.
3. What does he remember about what Ana wanted as a child?
That she used to say she wanted to draw for a living.
Listening

The Story Your Mother Still Tells

She found the photos. Sound first — no text.

1. What did she use to do every time the doorbell rang?
She used to hide behind the sofa. Every time.
2. What did she not use to eat, and why is that funny now?
Anything green. It is funny because now she eats everything.
3. What did the three of them use to do on Saturday mornings?
Walk to the bakery, and she always used to ask for the same bread.
Listening 2

Before the Shared Folder

How the agency used to work. Sound first — no text.

1. How did work reach the client back then?
On paper, and somebody used to drive across the city with a new version in a bag.
2. What was the role of a freelancer at that time?
Somebody you called in an emergency. In-house teams did everything.
3. What is the one thing he misses?
The part where the work stopped when you left the building.
