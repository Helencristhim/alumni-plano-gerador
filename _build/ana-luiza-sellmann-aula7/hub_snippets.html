Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

The Way It Used To Be -- Old Streets, Old Habits, Old Agencies

Talking about habits and situations that were true for years and are not true now. Key words: back then, to grow out of, old-fashioned, to miss out on, a phase, nowadays, a freelancer, in-house. Structure: used to plus the base verb, with didn't use to and Did you use to, against the past simple for a single event.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. Do not translate — the example sentence is the meaning.

Back then -- at that time in the past
"Back then, nobody used to work from home."
To grow out of -- to stop liking something as you get older
"I grew out of that music, but I still know every word."
Old-fashioned -- from an earlier time, not modern
"It sounds old-fashioned, but I still print things."
To miss out on -- to lose a chance to do something good
"I didn't want to miss out on the trip, so I said yes."
A phase -- a period that does not last
"It was a phase. It lasted about a year."
Nowadays -- in the present time, unlike before
"Nowadays nobody walks anywhere in that street."
A freelancer -- someone who works for several clients, not one company
"A freelancer used to be somebody you called in an emergency."
In-house -- done inside the company, not by an outside team
"In-house teams used to do absolutely everything."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last week I went back to the street where I grew up and a neighbour recognised me. He said I used to run down that street with my brother every afternoon, which is true. Back then the whole street used to look enormous to me. There used to be a bakery on the corner where the bank is now, and my mother used to take us there on Saturday mornings. I didn't use to eat anything green, not one single thing, which is funny because now I eat everything. My brother used to copy me in absolutely everything.

At work the change is just as big. A colleague told me how the agency used to work before I started. They didn't use to have a shared folder. Everything went to the client on paper, and somebody used to drive across the city with a new version in a bag. In-house teams did everything, and a freelancer was somebody you called in an emergency. Nowadays it is the opposite. He says the only thing he misses is that back then the work stopped when you left the building.

1. Why does the text say "I used to run down that street" and not "I ran down that street"?
A Because it was a habit repeated for years, and it stopped.
B Because it happened once, on one specific afternoon.
C Because the neighbour was the one who saw it.
2. Why is it "I didn't use to eat" and not "I didn't used to eat"?
A Because the sentence is negative and negatives never take the D.
B Because "did" already carries the past, so "use" goes back to the base form.
C Because both are correct and it is only a question of style.
3. What does "a freelancer was somebody you called in an emergency" tell you about that time?
A That freelancers were more expensive than in-house teams.
B That almost all the work used to be done inside the company.
C That there were no freelancers at all.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- True for Years, Not True Now

GRAMMAR

Two words that replace a whole explanation.

FormUseExample
used to + base verbA habit or situation repeated over a long period, and no longer true.I used to walk to the bakery.
She used to live here.
there used to beFor places and things that existed and do not exist anymore.There used to be a bakery on that corner.
didn't use toNegative. The D disappears, because did already carries the past.I didn't use to like coffee.
Did you use to...?Question. Same rule: no D after did.Did you use to walk to school?
One single eventIf it happened once, use the past simple: "I went to Texas in January", never "I used to go in January".
be used to + -ingA different structure, only for recognition: "I'm used to working from home" means it feels normal now. You do not have to produce this one yet.

Say it as one block: YOOS-to, with an S sound. The D is written but never heard, which is exactly why the negative and the question lose it.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

People are asking you about before. Choose the best thing to say.

You want to say you walked to the bakery every Saturday for years. You say:
A "We used to walk to the bakery every Saturday."
B "We used to walked to the bakery every Saturday."
C "We use to walk to the bakery every Saturday."
You want to say that as a child you did not eat vegetables. You say:
A "I didn't used to eat anything green."
B "I didn't use to eat anything green."
C "I don't used to eat anything green."
You travelled to Texas once, in January. You say:
A "I used to go to Texas in January."
B "I went to Texas in January."
C "I use to go to Texas in January."
You want to ask a colleague about his routine ten years ago. You ask:
A "Did you use to work with paper back then?"
B "Did you used to work with paper back then?"
C "Are you used to work with paper back then?"

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer, and nobody is correcting this one.

Somebody outside your field asks how your work has changed since you started. Speak for about ninety seconds. Say three things that used to be different, one thing you did not use to do that you do every day now, and one thing you honestly miss. Use "used to" at least four times and one "didn't use to". If a sentence breaks in the middle, say "let me start that again" out loud and keep going.
