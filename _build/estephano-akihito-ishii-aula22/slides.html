Chapter 3 -- The Pattern

Cheaper Than

Compare two things: -er than / more ... than

Grammar Discovery

What Do the Orange Words Have in Common?

"The NovaBook is cheaper than the ByteBook."

"The ByteBook is faster than the NovaBook."

"The NovaBook screen is bigger than the ByteBook screen."

"The ByteBook is more expensive than the NovaBook."

Look at the orange words. When do we use -er? When do we use more?

WordRuleExample
cheap / fast / lightshort word + -er + thancheap→cheaper, fast→faster
bigone vowel + one consonant: double it + -erbig→bigger
expensivelong word: more + word + thanmore expensive than
goodspecial formgood→better
NeverNot more cheaper. Not cheaper that.

In one line: short word + -er than / more + long word + than.

Questions

Which One Is Cheaper?

To choose between two things, ask Which...? or Is it ...-er than...?

"Which laptop is cheaper?" -- "The NovaBook."

"Is the ByteBook lighter than the NovaBook?" -- "Yes, it is."

"Which one is better for college?"

Short answers: Yes, it is. / No, it isn’t.
