Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

Next Steps -- Closing a Meeting Clearly

A meeting is only as good as its last five minutes. Key words: next steps, an action item, a deadline, to follow up, to commit to, to confirm, to assign, a reminder, to touch base, to keep someone posted. Structure: by, until, within, and as soon as / when + present.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Next steps -- the actions that people will take after a meeting
"Before we finish, let us agree on the next steps."
An action item -- one task from a meeting, with a person responsible for it
"We have three action items from today."
A deadline -- the last day or time to finish something
"The deadline for the list is Friday."
To follow up -- to contact someone again to continue or check something
"I will follow up with the hotel tomorrow."
To commit to -- to promise that you will do something
"Can you commit to a date for the contract?"
To confirm -- to say that something is true or will happen as planned
"Please confirm the time of the call."
To assign -- to give a task to a specific person
"Daniel will assign one person to each company."
A reminder -- a message that helps someone remember something
"I will send you a reminder on Monday."
To touch base -- to talk briefly to check how things are going
"Let us touch base again next Thursday."
To keep someone posted -- to give someone regular news about something
"Please keep me posted about the visas."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The meeting has five minutes left, and Juliana stops the discussion. “Let us agree on the next steps.” Daniel will send the list of companies by Friday. Juliana will follow up with the hotel.

“As soon as the hotel confirms the room, I will let you know,” she says. The director is traveling until Wednesday. “When she comes back, she will sign the budget.”

Notice the pattern. By Friday is a deadline. Until Wednesday is a period that continues and then stops. After as soon as and when, the verb is in the present: confirms, comes.

1. By when will Daniel send the list?
A By Friday.
B Until Wednesday.
C As soon as the hotel confirms.
2. When will the director sign the budget?
A Today.
B When she comes back, after Wednesday.
C By Friday.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “As soon as the hotel will confirm, I let you know.”
B “As soon as the hotel confirms, I will let you know.”
C “As soon as the hotel confirm, I will let you know.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- By, Until, Within, As Soon As

GRAMMAR

By is a deadline. Until is a period that continues and then stops. Within is inside a period. After as soon as and when, use the present.

JobFormExample
A deadline: that day or beforeby + day / timeI will send it by Friday.
A situation that continues, then stopsuntil + day / timeThe offer is valid until May 30.
Inside a period of timewithin + periodWe will answer within two days.
Right after another actionas soon as + present, will + verbAs soon as he signs, I will call you.
At the time of another actionwhen + present, will + verbWhen the list is ready, I will send it.
The trapno will after as soon as / whenNot as soon as he will sign. Say as soon as he signs.

Never: I will send it until Friday (for a deadline) · As soon as he will sign · in the maximum of two days · When the list will be ready
