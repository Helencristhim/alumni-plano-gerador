Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Same Two Minutes, Done Properly

One meeting, three recordings, one page

Dialogue

Two Minutes with a European Desk

H
Hanna Reuter, Meridian Clearing. I have twenty minutes and I have never worked with a Brazilian bank. Start wherever you like.
F
Then let me start with the country. To give you some context, Brazil moved to instant domestic payments in 2020, and trade volume with Asia has grown every year since.
H
I know the domestic story. Where does your bank sit in it?
F
We are one of the largest wholesale banks in the country, and my division owns the international products: foreign exchange, trade services and trade finance. Our corporate clients are exporters and multinationals, not individuals.
H
Good. And what is not working?
F
Let me walk you through it. A client sends money to Frankfurt, it goes through the old correspondent chain, and we still run two legacy systems on that leg. What that means in practice is two days, and a client who calls me on day three.
H
That is refreshingly honest. Most people spend the twenty minutes telling me how modern they are.
F
There is no point. Every institution has legacy systems. So that is what I am here for: a partner to address a gap on the cross-border leg. What would you need to see from us before that conversation becomes real?
Comprehension

About Hanna

1. Who is Hanna, and what does she say about her experience with Brazil?
Hanna Reuter, from Meridian Clearing. She says she has twenty minutes and she has never worked with a Brazilian bank.
2. Which part of the country story does she cut short, and what does she ask for instead?
She says she already knows the domestic story, and she asks where his bank sits in it.
3. What does she call refreshing, and what does she say most people do with the twenty minutes?
She calls his honesty about what is not working refreshing, and she says most people spend the time telling her how modern they are.
Listening 1 · Forty Meetings a Year

What She Needs, In Order

Somebody who meets banks she has never heard of, every week. Sound first — no text.

1. What are the three things she needs, and in what order?
First the country, in numbers rather than flags. Second where the bank sits inside it. Third what is broken.
2. What does she say about the country, exactly?
Not the flag, the numbers: whether the market is growing, whether it is regulated, and whether the money is actually moving.
3. What does she say she does not need, and why?
The history of the institution, because she will not remember the founding year, and neither will her investment committee.
Listening 2 · The Shape

Five Sentences, In This Order

Somebody who coaches these two-minute pitches for a living. Sound first — no text.

1. What are the five sentences, in order?
The country, the bank, the division, the problem, and the ask.
2. What does he say about saying that something is broken?
It should be said without embarrassment, because every institution has legacy systems and pretending otherwise makes you look worse, not better.
3. What is the most common mistake he sees?
People use the whole two minutes on sentences one and two, and never arrive at four and five.
Listening 3 · A European Desk

Brazil, Described From Outside

How your market looks from a desk in Europe. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say they watch Brazil closely, and what is it not about?
Because of the payment infrastructure. It is not about the size of the population.
2. What does she say has moved, and what has not moved at the same speed?
Domestic behaviour has changed fast since the instant payment system launched, and trade volume with Asia has grown. The cross-border side has not moved at the same speed.
3. Why does she say they listen when a Brazilian bank mentions a settlement platform?
Because the gap between what the domestic market can already do and what the cross-border market still cannot do is very wide, and somebody is going to close it.
