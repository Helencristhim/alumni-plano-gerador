The Bradesco Story

Key words: corporate client, legacy system, digital asset, regulatory framework, economic indicator, GDP growth, trade volume, to walk somebody through, to address a gap, to scale a solution. Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A corporate client -- a large company that the bank serves, not an individual
"Our corporate clients are exporters, importers and multinationals."
A legacy system -- old software a company still runs because replacing it is hard
"Every institution on earth has legacy systems. Pretending otherwise looks worse."
A digital asset -- something of value that exists only as a record on a network
"Since 2022 we have expanded the portfolio to include digital assets."
A regulatory framework -- the set of rules a central bank or a government puts around an activity
"The regulatory framework here is stricter than people abroad expect."
An economic indicator -- a number that says how a country's economy is doing
"I open with two economic indicators, never with the history of the bank."
GDP growth -- how much bigger a country's economy has become in a year
"GDP growth is the number they ask for first, every single time."
Trade volume -- the total value of the goods a country buys and sells abroad
"Trade volume with Asia has grown every year since 2019."
To walk somebody through -- to explain something step by step, in order, so nobody gets lost
"Let me walk you through how a payment moves today."
To address a gap -- to do something about a hole in what you offer today
"We are here to address a gap, not to describe one."
To scale a solution -- to make something that works for a few work for many, without rebuilding it
"The pilot works. The question is whether we can scale the solution."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A presentation in a second language fails for a reason nobody says out loud: not because the words are wrong, but because the listener does not know where they are. Somebody starts talking about a country, then a division, then a system, then a number, and after ninety seconds the person opposite has stopped following and started waiting. The fix costs three words at a time, and it is not vocabulary. It is a set of short phrases whose only job is to tell the listener what kind of sentence is coming next.

There are five that carry almost everything. To give you some context means background is coming, so do not expect the point yet. Let me walk you through means several steps, in order — and note the form: in English you walk a person through something. You never explain somebody something; you explain something to somebody, or, far more naturally in a meeting, you walk them through it. What that means in practice is announces the plain version of the technical sentence you just said, and it is the phrase that saves you when the other side does not know your jargon. The key challenge we are facing is tells everybody to stop taking notes and look up. And So that is what we are looking for says you have finished, which is how the other person knows it is their turn.

Inside those signposts, the two past tenses keep doing their work. A named year takes the past simple: Bradesco launched this division in 2018. The same story with no finished time takes the present perfect: Since then we have expanded into digital assets. Putting them the wrong way round is the most common slip in a company presentation, and it is worth saying why it matters here. The past simple closes the story: it says the division was launched and that was that. The present perfect keeps it open: it says the portfolio is still growing, which is exactly the impression you want to leave with somebody deciding whether to work with you.

Stage 2: Put the Two Minutes in Order

Order

A partner at a European fund has two minutes and has never heard of your bank. Put your presentation in a logical order.

?"To give you some context, Brazil moved to instant domestic payments in 2020."
?"We are one of the largest wholesale banks in the country."
?"My division owns the international products for corporate clients."
?"Let me walk you through what happens when a client sends money to Frankfurt."
?"The key challenge we are facing is the cross-border leg: two legacy systems, two days."
?"So that is what we are looking for. What would you need to see from us?"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You are about to explain a process in several steps. You say:
A I will explain you the process.
B Let me walk you through the process.
C I go to explain the process for you.
You have just used a technical term and you are not sure the other side knows it. You say:
A Do you understand what I said?
B What that means in practice is two days and three intermediaries.
C This is very technical, sorry.
The division opened in 2018 and the portfolio has grown ever since. You say:
A Bradesco has launched this division in 2018 and since then we expanded a lot.
B Bradesco launched this division in 2018, and since then we have expanded into digital assets.
C Bradesco launches this division in 2018 and expands since then.
You have finished your two minutes and you want the other person to speak. You say:
A That is all from me. Thank you very much.
B So that is what we are looking for. What would you need to see from us?
C I finished my presentation now.
