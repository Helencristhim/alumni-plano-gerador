Lesson 02 -- Pre-class

Your Results in English -- Telling a Career Story

Talking about professional achievements. Key words: achievement, track record, revenue, cost reduction, rollout, impact, target, to deliver. Structure: present perfect and past simple -- what you have done, and when you did it.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Achievement -- something important that you did successfully
"My biggest achievement has been our new pricing model."
Track record -- everything a person has done well over time
"She has a strong track record in food retail."
Revenue -- the total money a company receives from sales
"Revenue grew by six percent in twelve months."
Cost reduction -- a decrease in the money a company spends
"The team delivered a cost reduction of eleven percent."
Rollout -- the act of introducing a new plan across a company
"The rollout started in the south region."
Impact -- the effect an action has on a result
"The impact was visible in the quarterly report."
Target -- a number that a team has to reach
"We reached the target three months early."
To deliver -- to produce the result that you promised
"I have delivered results in every company I have worked for."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Laura Bennett has worked in pricing for eighteen years, and she has been at Northfield Grocers since 2016. In 2018, she led the first rollout of a category pricing model, and revenue grew by six percent in twelve months. Two years later, her team delivered a cost reduction of eleven percent. Since that project, Northfield has reached its target every year. Laura has published two papers, but she did not study economics: she started her career as a store manager.

1. Why do we say "she has worked in pricing for eighteen years"?
A The period is not closed. She still works in pricing today.
B The sentence gives a closed year, so we need the present perfect.
C The action is a daily routine, so we need the present simple.
2. Why is "In 2018, she led the rollout" in the past simple?
A "In 2018" is a closed moment in the past.
B The rollout is still happening today.
C "Lead" is an irregular verb, so it never takes the present perfect.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "She has delivered the cost reduction in 2020."
B "She has delivered three cost reductions since 2018."
C "She delivers the cost reduction since 2018."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect vs Past Simple

GRAMMAR

One question decides the tense: is there a closed moment in the sentence?

FormUseExample
Present perfect
have / has + past participle		Experience and results with no closed moment. It is still true today. Signal words: for, since, already, just, never, ever, yet.I have delivered three projects.
Past simple
verb + -ed or new form		A closed moment in the past. Signal words: in 2022, last year, last quarter, two years ago.In 2022, I led the project.
for / sinceOnly with the present perfect. For takes a period; since takes a starting point.for ten years / since 2019
Negative & questionPresent perfect: Have you ever presented to a board? / I have not presented yet. Past simple: Did you present in 2022? / I did not present.
