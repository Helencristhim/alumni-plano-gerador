Chapter 3: The Code

What I Do, and What I Do Not

One verb, three shapes

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"I write the credit policy for a digital bank."

"We look at risk before we look at growth."

"We do not have branches."

"Do you work on pricing as well?"

Every red verb is exactly the same shape, in all four sentences. Now look at the blue words: they only turn up when the sentence says no, or asks a question.

JobFormExample
Something true every weekI / you / we + bare verbI write the credit policy.
Negativedo not + bare verbWe do not have branches.
QuestionDo + subject + bare verbDo you work on pricing?
Question word in frontWhat / Who / How often + do + subject + bare verbWho do you report to?
Short answerYes, I do. / No, we do not."Do you write the policy yourself?" — "Yes, I do."
How oftenevery day, twice a month, usually, neverWe meet twice a month.
The trapno to be in front of the verbNot I am work in a bank. Say I work in a bank.

In one line: the verb never changes shape, and do carries the no and the question for it.

Comprehension

About Mark

1. How does Mark open the conversation, and what does he say about the two of them?
He gives his name and says he runs analytics, and he says he does not think they have met.
2. Why is he not satisfied with the answer "I am the credit director"?
He says credit covers a lot of ground, so he asks what she does exactly.
3. What does he assume about her week, and what does he want to know at the end?
He assumes she spends her week with the branches, and at the end he wants to know who she reports to.
Listening 1 · The Quarter Call

How This Group Works

A manager opening the first call of the quarter. Sound first — no text.

1. How often does the group meet, and on which days?
Twice a month, on the first Monday and on the third Monday.
2. What three things does he say a presentation needs?
What changed, why it changed, and what you do next. He says that is enough.
3. What does he say the call expects, and what does it not expect?
It does not expect perfect English. It expects you to say what you think, and to stop him when you do not follow.
Listening 2 · A Podcast

Do Not Translate Your Title

A coach on saying what you do in a second language. Sound first — no text.

1. Which two sentences does she compare, and which one does she prefer?
She prefers "I decide who gets credit and at what price" to "I am responsible for the credit area", because the first one tells the work and the second one only gives a box on a chart.
2. What mistake does she say she hears every week?
The person who explains fifteen years of career before anybody asks for it.
3. What is the one thing she says the room is listening for?
What the person decides on a Tuesday morning.
Listening 3 · Business Radio

The Bank That Never Meets You

A short business radio report on digital banking. Sound first — no text.

1. How do most of these customers open the account, and how long does it take?
On a phone, in about ten minutes, and nobody at the bank ever meets them.
2. What does the report say a traditional bank does, and what does a digital bank do instead?
A traditional bank looks the customer in the eye, and a digital bank looks at data.
3. Why does the report say these banks hire economists?
Because somebody has to write the rule the model follows, and answer for it when the rule is wrong.
