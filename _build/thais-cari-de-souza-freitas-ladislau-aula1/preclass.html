Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Credit -- money the bank lends today, and the customer pays it back later
"We give credit to people who never walk into a branch."
A credit policy -- the rules a bank uses to decide who receives credit, and how much
"I write the credit policy, and my team applies it every day."
Pricing -- the work of deciding what a customer pays for a product
"Pricing is the part of my work I like most."
An offer -- the product and the price the bank shows to one customer
"We do not send the same offer to every customer."
A digital bank -- a bank with no branches, where the customer does everything in an app
"I work for a digital bank, so I do not work in a branch."
Risk -- the chance that something goes wrong and the bank loses money
"We look at risk before we look at growth."
Growth -- when a product reaches more customers, or earns more, than before
"I work on the growth of the credit product."
A portfolio -- all the credit a bank has given, looked at together
"The portfolio tells us if the policy works."
A team -- the group of people who work with you every day
"My team is small, and we talk every morning."
To report to -- to have somebody as your boss
"I report to a VP who lives in the United States."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A call of strangers asks what you do, and the title in your head does not travel well. Here is the whole answer, in three shapes. The first one is the easy one: "I write the credit policy for a digital bank. I also work on pricing and on the offer." Look at the verb. It is bare. In this tense, with I, you and we, the verb never changes shape at all.

The second shape is the one people get wrong. To say no, English does not touch the verb. It puts do not in front of it: "We do not have branches. We do not send the same offer to every customer." Never we not have, and never I am work. In Portuguese you say that you are a director; in English the verb does the work by itself.

The third shape is the question, and it is the one that keeps a conversation alive. English puts do in front again: "Do you work on risk as well?" A question word simply goes before it: "Who do you report to?" And the short answer is short: "Yes, I do." One last use of this tense: it says how often something happens. "We meet twice a month, and I read the portfolio every Monday."

1. "I write the credit policy for a digital bank." Why is there nothing in front of the verb?
A Because the sentence is short.
B Because a positive sentence with I, you or we needs only the bare verb.
C Because write is a verb that never takes anything in front of it.
2. Why is it "we do not have branches" and not "we not have branches"?
A Because a negative sentence in this tense needs do not in front of the bare verb.
B Because have is an irregular verb.
C Because a digital bank has an app instead.
3. The text warns against "I am work on pricing". What is the problem?
A Pricing needs the word the in front of it.
B The verb does the work alone. There is no am, is or are in front of it.
C The sentence has to start with a question word.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple with I, You and We

GRAMMAR

The verb never changes shape. Do carries the no and the question for it.

JobFormExample
Something true every weekI / you / we + bare verbI write the credit policy.
Negativedo not + bare verbWe do not have branches.
QuestionDo + subject + bare verbDo you work on pricing?
Question word in frontWhat / Who / How often + do + subject + bare verbWho do you report to?
Short answerYes, I do. / No, we do not."Do you write the policy yourself?" — "Yes, I do."
How oftenevery day, twice a month, usually, neverWe meet twice a month.
In speechdo not becomes don’tWe don’t have branches.

Never: I am work in a bank · I am director of credit · we not have branches · you work on pricing? · who you report to?
