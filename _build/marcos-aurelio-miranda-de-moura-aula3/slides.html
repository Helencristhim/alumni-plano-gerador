Chapter 3: The Code

What You Did, and What You Did Not

The past, in three shapes

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"We launched the campaign in March."

"Revenue grew nine per cent last quarter."

"We did not reach the plan in April."

"Did the competitor cut the price before us?"

Two red verbs took an ending and two changed shape completely. Now look at the blue parts: two of them say when, and two of them take the past away from the verb.

JobFormExample
Regular verbsverb + ed, for every subjectWe launched · she tracked · they reported
Spelling-e takes only -d · consonant + y becomes -ied · short verbs double the last letterclosed · tried · dropped
Irregular verbsa different word, learned one by onegrow → grew · go → went · have → had · make → made · take → took · see → saw · tell → told · come → came · give → gave · cut → cut
Negativedid not + bare verbWe did not reach the plan.
QuestionDid + subject + bare verbDid the competitor cut the price?
Whenlast quarter, last month, in March, two months ago, in Q1Volume dropped two months ago.
The trapthe past appears once, never twiceNot did not reached. Say did not reach.

In one line: the verb carries the past until did shows up, and then did carries it instead.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Quarter, at Full Speed

One conversation, three recordings, one summary

Dialogue

Five Minutes Before the Numbers

D
Before we start. Give me the quarter in four sentences, and do not open the deck.
M
We launched the new line in March. Revenue grew nine per cent, and two regions exceeded the plan. One did not.
D
Which one, and by how much?
M
The south. Volume dropped eleven per cent in April. Our largest competitor cut the price in March and we did not follow.
D
You did not follow. Was that a decision, or did it just happen?
M
It was a decision. The base oil price was our main challenge, and a discount there cost us more than the volume was worth.
D
Good. That is the sentence I want in the room. And the outcome?
M
Volume came back in June and the performance of the line held. And you — did the other markets see the same movement?
Comprehension

About Diane

1. What does Diane ask for at the very start, and what condition does she add?
She asks for the quarter in four sentences, and she adds that he must not open the deck.
2. When she hears that one region missed the plan, what two things does she want to know?
Which region it was, and by how much it missed.
3. Which sentence does she say she wants in the room, and what does that tell you about what a board values?
The one where he explains that not following the price was a decision, and why. It tells you a board wants a bad number with a reason, not a bad number hidden.
Listening 1 · A Director

Closing the Quarter in Four Sentences

A marketing director summarising three months at a board. Sound first — no text.

1. What did they launch, and when?
A new line for heavy vehicles, in the second week of March.
2. What happened to revenue, and what happened to the cost?
Revenue grew nine per cent and the cost grew eleven, which she says is the number that actually matters.
3. What does she say she got wrong, and what did she do about it?
She waited three weeks too long to move the price in the south. She says she moved it in May and volume came back in June.
Listening 2 · A Podcast

How to Report a Bad Quarter

A business podcast on presenting numbers you are not proud of. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say people do wrong when the quarter went badly?
They start with the explanation and arrive at the number twenty minutes later, and by then the room has stopped listening.
2. What order does he recommend instead?
The number first, then what you decided and why, then what you did about it.
3. What does he say a room forgives, and what does it never forgive?
It forgives the number. It never forgives the surprise.
Listening 3 · Business Radio

The Year the Base Oil Moved

A business radio report on what happened to lubricant costs. Sound first — no text.

1. What happened to the price of base oil, and over what period?
It rose sharply over about five months and then fell back almost as fast, ending the year close to where it started.
2. What did the companies that raised their prices early discover?
That they lost volume to the ones that waited, and that some of that volume did not come back.
3. According to the report, what did the workshops do during those months?
They bought less each time and bought more often, so the orders got smaller and the deliveries got more frequent.
