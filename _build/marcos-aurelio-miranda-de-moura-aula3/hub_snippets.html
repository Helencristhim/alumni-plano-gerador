What Happened Last Quarter -- Three Months, Four Sentences

Somebody asks how the quarter went, and the whole answer fits in four sentences. Key words: results, performance, revenue, growth, a quarter, a challenge, an outcome, a competitor, to exceed, to drop. Structure: past simple, regular and irregular.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Results -- the numbers a period produced, good or bad
"The results came in on the fifth, and they were better than we expected."
Performance -- how well a product, a team or a market did over a period of time
"The performance of the new line surprised everybody, including us."
Revenue -- the money that comes in from sales, before any cost is taken out
"Revenue grew nine per cent, and the cost grew eleven. That was the problem."
Growth -- the increase in sales or in size from one period to the next
"All the growth came from two states, and we did not plan it that way."
A quarter -- a period of three months, one of the four in a year
"We closed the quarter three points below the plan."
A challenge -- a difficult part of the work that somebody has to solve
"The main challenge was the price of the base oil, not the demand."
An outcome -- what a decision or an action finally produced
"We changed the discount in May, and the outcome took two months to appear."
A competitor -- another company that sells to the same customers as you
"Our largest competitor cut the price in March, and we did not follow."
To exceed -- to end above the number you had to reach
"Two regions exceeded the plan and one missed it by a long way."
To drop -- to go down from one period to the next
"Volume dropped in April and came back in June."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Here is a whole quarter in four sentences. "We launched the new line in March. Revenue grew nine per cent. Two regions exceeded the plan and the south did not. The main challenge was the base oil price." Look at the verbs. Most of them simply took an ed: launched, exceeded, dropped. That ending never changes, whoever the sentence is about — I launched, she launched, they launched.

Some verbs refuse the ending and change shape instead, and there is no rule for those. You learn them one by one, and these are the ones a results meeting needs most: grow becomes grew, go becomes went, have becomes had, make becomes made, take becomes took, see becomes saw, tell becomes told, come becomes came, give becomes gave, and cut stays cut. Never growed, and never costed.

Then the two shapes that a board hears most, because a board asks. To say no, English brings in did not, and the verb goes straight back to being bare: "We did not follow the competitor on price." To ask, it brings in did: "Did the volume come back in June?" Never did not reached and never did the volume came — the past appears once, and did has already taken it. One last thing: the past almost always says when. In March, last quarter, two months ago, in Q1. A sentence about a result without a date is a sentence a board will interrupt.

1. "We launched the new line in March." What tells you the sentence is finished and dated?
A The word new.
B The ed on the verb and the time expression in March.
C The fact that the sentence is about we.
2. Why is "we did not reached the plan" wrong?
A Because did already carries the past, so the verb after it stays bare.
B Because reach is an irregular verb.
C Because a negative sentence cannot talk about the past.
3. The text says a sentence about a result without a date gets interrupted. Why would a board interrupt?
A Because the sentence is too short.
B Because the past simple is not allowed without a date.
C Because a number without a period does not tell anybody whether it is good news.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Simple

GRAMMAR

The verb carries the past until did shows up, and then did carries it instead.

JobFormExample
Regular verbsverb + ed, for every subjectWe launched · she tracked · they reported
Spelling-e takes only -d · consonant + y becomes -ied · short verbs double the last letterclosed · tried · dropped
Irregular verbsa different word, learned one by onegrow → grew · go → went · have → had · make → made · take → took
More irregularsthe rest of the ten a results meeting needssee → saw · tell → told · come → came · give → gave · cut → cut
Negativedid not + bare verbWe did not follow on price.
QuestionDid + subject + bare verbDid the volume come back in June?
Whenlast quarter, last month, in March, two months ago, in Q1Volume dropped two months ago.

Never: last quarter we launch two campaigns · revenue growed nine per cent · the campaign costed a lot · we did not reached the plan · how much the campaign cost?

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The board asks how the quarter went. You open with:
A "Last quarter we launch the new line and the revenue is growing nine per cent."
B "We launched the new line in March, and revenue grew nine per cent."
C "The quarter was with a growth of the revenue in nine per cent."
You need to say a region missed the plan. You say:
A "The south did not reach the plan. Volume dropped eleven per cent in April."
B "The south did not reached the plan. Volume dropped eleven per cent in April."
C "The south not reach the plan, the volume drop eleven per cent."
You want to explain that holding the price was deliberate. You say:
A "We not followed the competitor because the base oil costed too much."
B "We did not follow the competitor on price, and it was a decision."
C "We did not followed the competitor, was a decision of us."
The other person has finished. You want to keep the conversation going. You say:
A "Interesting. Our quarter was better."
B "And you, did the other markets see the same movement?"
C "The other markets saw the same movement?"
