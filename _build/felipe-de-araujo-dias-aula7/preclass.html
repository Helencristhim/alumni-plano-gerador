Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

Since I Joined the Company -- Telling Your Own Career

The words of a career instead of a job title: to join, career path, to be promoted, to take over, scope, headcount, milestone, track record. Structure: present perfect with for and since for what is still running, past simple for what is closed. Mission: tell ten years to somebody who has never heard of you.

Vocabulary

To join -- to start working at a company

Career path -- the sequence of roles a person moves through over the years
"My career path did not start in operations. It started in a law firm."
To be promoted -- to move to a more senior role in the same company
"I was promoted twice, in 2018 and in 2021."
To take over -- to start being responsible for something that somebody else had
"I took over the fourth area three years ago."
Scope -- the range of things a role or a project covers
"The scope has doubled since the merger."
Headcount -- the number of people who work in a team or a company
"We have run the night shift with half the headcount since January."
Milestone -- an important point that marks real progress in a project or a career
"Opening the second distribution center was the milestone of my career."
Track record -- everything a person or a company has achieved so far, seen as proof
"Nobody asks for your opinion. They ask for your track record."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

I joined this company in March 2016, as facilities manager. Before that I studied law and I worked in a law firm for two years. I did not enjoy it, so I left. All of that is over, and every verb in it is in the past simple.

Now look at what is still true. I have been here since 2016. I have been supply chain director for five years. The scope has doubled since the merger, and the headcount has tripled. None of those periods has an end yet, so none of them can use the past simple. And notice what English never does: it never says I am here since 2016. The present simple cannot carry a period that started in the past.

1. Why does the text say I worked in a law firm for two years in the past simple?
A Because for always takes the past simple.
B Because that period is closed. He left, so it has an end.
C Because a law firm is not the current company.
2. Why is I am here since 2016 wrong in English?
A Because since cannot be used with the verb to be.
B Because the present simple cannot carry a period that started in the past. English needs have been.
C Because 2016 is too far away for the present.
3. What is the difference between for and since here?
A for measures the length of the period, since names the point where it started.
B for is for work and since is for study.
C for is informal and since is formal.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect with For and Since

GRAMMAR

The same period, seen from two ends. What chooses the tense is not the word for or since. It is whether the period has closed.

FormUse it forExample
have / has + participle + forA period still running, measured by its length.I have worked here for ten years.
have / has + participle + sinceA period still running, named by its starting point.I have been a director since 2021.
Past simpleA closed period. It ended, so it can take a date.I worked there for two years, and then I left.
for + lengthsix months, ten years, a while, ages. Never since ten years.
since + starting pointMarch, 2016, the merger, I joined, we opened the second center.
The questionHow long have you been in this role? — never How long do you work here?

Before you answer, ask one question: is this period over? If it is not, you need have or has plus the participle, whatever the Portuguese wants to do.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Somebody wants to hear about your career. Choose the best thing to say.

You started at the company in 2016 and you are still there. You say:
A I am in this company since 2016.
B I have been with this company since 2016.
C I am with this company for ten years.
You worked at a law firm from 2014 to 2016 and then left. You say:
A I have worked in a law firm for two years.
B I worked in a law firm for two years, and then I moved to operations.
C I am working in a law firm for two years.
Somebody asks about the length of time in your current role. The correct question is:
A How long are you in this role?
B How long have you been in this role?
C How long do you have this role?
You want to say the team grew from ninety to three hundred people and is still growing. You say:
A The headcount has tripled since the merger.
B The headcount tripled since the merger, and it still grows.
C The headcount is tripled for the merger.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Somebody who has never heard of your company asks about your career. Record about ninety seconds. Start with what is finished: what you studied, where you worked before, and how long, all in the past simple. Then move to what is still running: how long you have been at your current company, how long you have been in your role, and what has changed since the last big event there. Name one milestone and one thing you have not repeated since. Use at least four words from this lesson. Do not stop to correct yourself.
