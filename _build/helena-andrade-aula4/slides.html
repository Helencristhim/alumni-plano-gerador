Who Did the Work?

"The operator had the containers surveyed the same afternoon."

Written for this lesson -- one sentence, one very common misreading

Say who looked inside the containers. It was not the operator. English has a structure that lets a report name who arranged the work and hide who performed it, and this sentence is it.

Chapter 4: The Code

Arranged, Not Performed

Have something done, get something done

Grammar Discovery

Who Actually Did It?

"The operator had the containers surveyed the same afternoon."

"The agent got the documents reissued before the end of the working day."

"The charterer had the delay costed at just under forty thousand dollars."

"Operators should have the two documents reconciled before arrival."

In all four sentences the subject did not do the work. Say who did, in each case -- and then find the one thing the four sentences have in common, in the same position every time.

FormWhat it doesExample
have + object + past participlethe subject arranges it; somebody else performs itThe operator had the containers surveyed.
get + object + past participlethe same idea, less formal, often with effort or pressure behind itThe agent got the documents reissued.
the object is the keythe noun sits BETWEEN the two verbs. That is what separates the causative from a perfect tensehad the delay costed / had costed
with a modalshould, must, will + have + object + participleOperators should have the documents reconciled.
The reading traphad the delay costed is NOT the past perfect of cost. If there is a noun between had and the participle, the subject did not do the work -- and an option that says the charterer calculated the figure himself is adding a fact the text does not carry.

The causative is how commercial English says who is responsible without saying who is at the keyboard. In the options the swap is always the same: the arranger is turned into the performer, and everything else is left untouched.

Chapter 6: Say It Back

Say It Back

Two recordings, then the case in your own words

Listening 1

The Agent on the Quay

The man who had the documents reissued explains his Saturday. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What was the first thing he arranged, and why?
He had the containers surveyed that same afternoon, because until somebody physically opens one, everything you tell the authority is only an opinion.
2. What did the trace to the loading port reveal?
A clerk had amended the stowage plan by hand after the bill of lading had already been issued. Nobody was hiding anything: somebody was tidying up.
3. What did he do with the documents, and when was clearance restored?
He got the whole set reissued overnight and had it lodged first thing on the Monday. Clearance came back on the Tuesday.
Listening 2

What the Port Authority Looks At

An inspector describes the check that stopped this ship. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What does the inspector say most of the work actually is?
Comparison. They read the bill of lading, the manifest and the stowage plan against each other, and the ship keeps working only if the three tell the same story.
2. What was the specific difference in this case?
The bill said machinery parts in transit, and the stowage plan put those same containers in a hold that had been declared empty on arrival. On paper the cargo had two destinations.
3. Why were the containers sealed rather than opened?
Precisely because the problem was documentary and not physical.
