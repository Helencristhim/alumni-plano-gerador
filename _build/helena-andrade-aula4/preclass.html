Cargo, Ports and Operations -- The Register of Maritime Commerce

The register of maritime commerce and the causative that names who arranged the work. Key words: freight, consignment, bill of lading, port authority, clearance, stowage, hold, gross tonnage, discharge, in transit. Structure: the causative have and get something done.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read it inside a real sentence. These are the words the exam text will use.

Freight -- goods carried commercially, and also the money paid to carry them
"The freight had been prepaid to this port and no further."
Consignment -- one batch of goods sent together to one receiver
"The bill of lading described the consignment as machinery parts."
Bill of lading -- the document that proves a carrier received the goods and states their terms
"A clerk amended the plan after the bill of lading had been issued."
Port authority -- the public body that controls what happens inside a port
"By midday the port authority had stopped the operation."
Clearance -- official permission for a ship or a cargo to proceed
"Clearance was restored on the Tuesday."
Stowage -- the way cargo is arranged and secured inside the ship
"The stowage plan showed the same containers in an empty hold."
Hold -- the enclosed space below deck where cargo is carried
"The containers were in a hold that had been declared empty."
Gross tonnage -- a measure of a ship's total enclosed volume, not of her weight
"A container vessel of 41,000 gross tonnage arrived on the Friday."
Discharge -- the operation of taking cargo off a ship
"Discharge began at 0700 and was stopped by midday."
In transit -- passing through a country without being imported into it
"The goods were declared as machinery parts in transit."

GRAMMAR

The operator had the containers surveyed the same afternoon and had the discrepancy traced to the loading port. The agent then got the documents reissued and had the amended set lodged with the port authority.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Causative -- Have and Get Something Done

GRAMMAR

Commercial English needs a way of saying who is responsible without saying who is at the keyboard. That is the causative, and the giveaway is always the same: a noun sitting between the two verbs.

FormWhat it doesExample
have + object + past participlethe subject arranges it; somebody else performs itThe operator had the containers surveyed.
get + object + past participlethe same idea, less formal, often with effort or pressure behind itThe agent got the documents reissued.
the object is the keythe noun sits BETWEEN the two verbs. That is what separates the causative from a perfect tensehad the delay costed / had costed
with a modalshould, must, will + have + object + participleOperators should have the documents reconciled.
The reading traphad the delay costed is NOT the past perfect of cost. If there is a noun between had and the participle, the subject did not do the work -- and an option that says the charterer calculated the figure himself is adding a fact the text does not carry.

The causative is how commercial English says who is responsible without saying who is at the keyboard. In the options the swap is always the same: the arranger is turned into the performer, and everything else is left untouched.
