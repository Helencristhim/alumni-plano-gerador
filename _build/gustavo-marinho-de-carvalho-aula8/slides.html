Grammar Discovery

What Did Each Person Actually Say?

"He said he needed more time."

"She told me she would confirm on Friday."

"She asked when the report was ready."

"He admitted that the figures had been wrong."

Every verb in red has moved one step back in time, even though nothing has changed: need became needed, will became would. Now look at the blue one and notice what is NOT there — no question mark, and the words are in the order of a normal sentence. And the orange verb is doing something the others are not: it is telling you how the speaker felt about saying it.

JobFormExample
Present becomes pastneed → neededHe said he needed more time.
will becomes would, can becomes couldwill → wouldShe said she would confirm on Friday.
Present perfect becomes past perfecthas been → had beenHe admitted the figures had been wrong.
tell needs a person, say does nottold me / said (to me)She told me. She said she would.
An indirect question loses the inversionno do, no question markShe asked when the report was ready.
Yes or no questionif / whetherShe asked whether we could confirm.
The reporting verb carries the meaningsaid / agreed / promised / admittedHe said he would try is not he promised.
No backshift when it is still truepresent is allowedShe said the deadline is Friday, and it still is.

In one line: the backshift is automatic, and the reporting verb is a choice. The choice is where the meaning is.

Listening 1 · Business Radio

Between the Room and the Page

A short radio piece on what happens to a meeting when it gets written down. Sound first — no text.

1. What is in the room, and what is on the page?
In the room, eight people, four subjects and a lot of half sentences. On the page, five lines, and every one of them finished.
2. Why does she say that is not dishonesty?
Because it is what writing does to speech.
3. What does she say the interesting question is?
Not whether the record is accurate, but who chose which four things out of forty were worth a line, and whether the person who disagreed got a sentence.
Listening 2 · A Voice Note

One Verb, One Lost Week

A voice note about a small argument caused by choosing one word. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the client actually say, and how did he pass it on?
The client said he would try to get the figures over before the end of the week. He passed it on as: he promised the figures by Friday.
2. What did his team do, and what happened?
They planned around Friday. Friday came and went, and when he went back to the client, the client was confused, because he had never promised anything.
3. What does he say the reporting verb is?
Not decoration around the message. It IS the message. Five verbs, one event, five different weeks for everybody who reads it.
Real Document

The Minutes of That Call

Minutes — supplier review, Thursday
Circulated Friday 09:12 · four lines, one owner each

1. GM said the revised timeline had been approved and would be sent the same day. Action: GM, done.

2. CH asked whether the volumes could still be confirmed within five working days. GM said they could. Action: GM, Tuesday.

3. The point about the customs delay was noted.

4. CH promised to introduce GM to the contracts team before the end of the month. Action: CH, 30th.

Sample minutes written for this lesson. The meeting is invented.
1. Line 1 has two backshifts. Find them and say what the original words were.
had been approved and would be sent. His words were: the revised timeline has been approved and it will be sent today.
2. Line 3 is the line to argue about. Why?
It is the only line with no person and no action. Somebody raised the customs delay and the record says the point was noted, which keeps the concern and loses the owner.
3. Line 4 uses promised. What would change for the reader if it said said she would try to?
Everything about the 30th. Promised sets a date the reader can plan around; said she would try sets an intention with no date to hold anybody to.
