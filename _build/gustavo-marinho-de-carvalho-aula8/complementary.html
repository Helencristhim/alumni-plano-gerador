Complementary Activities

Optional extras to enjoy outside class. Mark each one as done after you watch or listen to it.

Series
Making a Murderer — Season 1, Episodes 1 and 2

Ten hours of people relaying what somebody else said, and of records that do not match the room they came from. It is the subject of this lesson taken to the place where it costs the most, and every interview in it is a lesson in which reporting verb the speaker chose.

Tip: English subtitles on. Every time somebody says “he told me” or “he said he would”, stop and ask what the original words probably were.

Podcast
BBC 6 Minute English — “How advertisers make us spend money”

Six minutes, two British speakers, normal speed, on an industry that exists entirely to choose the exact words. It is the commercial version of what you did today: the decision is the same, and the wording decides what people do about it.

Tip: listen once with no text, then relay the episode to somebody in four sentences, using four different reporting verbs.

Talk
Elizabeth Loftus — “How reliable is your memory?” (TED)

Seventeen minutes from the researcher who showed that changing one word in a question changes what people remember afterwards. It is the scientific version of the argument in this lesson: the wording is not a record of the event, it becomes the event.

Tip: watch it once with English subtitles. Then write the one sentence you would put in the minutes of your own last meeting, and read it back asking what it would make somebody do.
