Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

What She Actually Said -- Relaying a Meeting

Half your work with a client is telling somebody else what the client said, and the verb you choose decides what happens on Monday. Key words: minutes, an action point, to touch on, to go over, a takeaway, to be on the record, to paraphrase, wording. Structure: reported speech.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Minutes -- the written record of what was said and decided in a meeting
"Could we put that in the minutes, in those words?"
An action point -- a task that comes out of a meeting, with somebody's name on it
"Three action points, and every one of them has an owner."
To touch on -- to mention a subject briefly, without going into it
"We touched on the budget and then moved straight past it."
To go over -- to review something carefully, from the beginning
"Let us go over the three dates one more time."
A takeaway -- the main thing you keep from a meeting or a talk
"If there is one takeaway from that call, it is the deadline."
To be on the record -- said openly, and able to be quoted afterwards
"Everything in this call is on the record, so let us be precise."
To paraphrase -- to say what somebody said, in your own words
"To paraphrase her, the answer was no, but politely."
Wording -- the exact words chosen to express something
"Nobody disagrees about the decision. They disagree about the wording."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

When you repeat what somebody said, English moves the verb one step back in time, even when nothing has changed. His words were I need more time; you report he said he needed more time. I will confirm on Friday becomes she said she would confirm on Friday. The figures have been wrong becomes he admitted the figures had been wrong. Present goes to past, will goes to would, can goes to could, and the present perfect goes to the past perfect. It is mechanical, and it is the easy half.

Two shapes catch people out. Tell needs a person after it and say does not: she told me she would confirm, or she said she would confirm, never she said me. And a question, once reported, stops behaving like a question: there is no inversion, no do and no question mark. When is the report ready? becomes she asked when the report was ready. For a yes-or-no question, use if or whether: she asked whether we could move the date.

And then the half that is not mechanical at all. He said he would try, he agreed to do it and he promised to do it can describe the same twenty seconds of a call, and they set three different expectations for the week that follows. The reporting verb is not a neutral frame around the message; it is part of the message, and the person reading your report will plan around the verb, not around the facts. Choose it on purpose. One last thing: if what was said is still true, you do not have to backshift at all. She said the deadline is Friday is fine, and it quietly tells the reader that it still is.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

The backshift is automatic. The reporting verb is a choice, and the choice is where the meaning is.

JobFormExample
Present becomes pastneed → neededHe said he needed more time.
will becomes would, can becomes couldwill → wouldShe said she would confirm on Friday.
Present perfect becomes past perfecthas been → had beenHe admitted the figures had been wrong.
tell needs a person, say does nottold me / saidShe told me. Not she said me.
An indirect question loses the inversionno do, no question markShe asked when the report was ready.
Yes or no questionif / whetherShe asked whether we could confirm.
No backshift when it is still truepresent is allowedShe said the deadline is Friday.

Never: She asked when was the report ready · He said me that · She said she will confirm · He asked if did we have it
