Grammar Video
"English Class: The first and second conditional" — BBC Learning English

A class contrasting the real, likely first conditional with the distant, hypothetical second one — including the rule that keeps would out of the if-clause. Connection to Lesson 9: this is the difference between offering something and exploring it.

Tip: after each example, say the same sentence in both conditionals and ask yourself which one you would be safe saying at a negotiating table. That question is the whole lesson.

A short workplace-English episode on the language of getting a better deal: opening, conceding and closing. Connection to Lesson 9: it is the vocabulary of this lesson used by people doing it for real, at conversational speed.

Tip: write down every conditional you hear and mark it 1 or 2. Then count how many of the offers were actually offers and how many were explorations that could still be withdrawn.

An American law firm on termination for cause against termination for convenience, notice periods, and what makes each of them enforceable. Connection to Lesson 9: it is the clause you negotiate in class, described from the other side of the table.
