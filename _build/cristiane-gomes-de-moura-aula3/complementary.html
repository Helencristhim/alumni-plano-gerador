Film
My Octopus Teacher -- the Netflix documentary

A man tells the story of one year of his life, and he tells all of it in the past. I went into the water. I found her. I did not understand what she was doing. That is ninety minutes of exactly the tense you learned today, spoken slowly, by one calm voice, over the quietest images you will see this month. It is also a film about paying attention, which is closer to a spiritual documentary than to a nature one.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Write down five sentences he says in the past — three regular and two irregular — and say each one out loud before you go to sleep.

Video
BBC Learning English -- "The past simple tense" (6 Minute Grammar)

Six minutes on the whole of today’s grammar, from two native speakers who take turns and never rush. It covers the -ed, the irregulars, and the did that takes the past off the verb — the same three pieces, in a different voice, which is what makes a rule stop being a rule.

Tip: listen once without pausing. The second time, stop after every example and say it back before they move on.

Audio lesson
BBC Learning English -- "6 Minute Vocabulary: How to use irregular verbs"

The irregulars are the only part of today’s lesson that no rule can help you with — went, got, grew, met, took, saw, had. This one goes through the most common ones with real examples, and it is audio, so your ear does the work instead of your eyes.

Tip: listen to it while you walk the dog. Repeat each irregular verb out loud as it comes up — nobody in the park is listening to you.
