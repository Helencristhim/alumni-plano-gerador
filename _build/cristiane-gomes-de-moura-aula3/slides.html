Chapter 3: The Code

Two Letters, and the Ones That Refuse Them

And the word that takes the past away from the verb

Grammar Discovery

Two of These End the Same Way. Two Do Not.

"I travelled with my son, and we arrived on a Sunday."

"We went to the coast and we got back on Friday."

"I did not open my laptop once."

"Where did you grow up?"

Two red verbs took -ed. Two changed shape completely, and no rule tells you which — you learn those one by one. Then look at the last two sentences: the moment the blue word turns up, the verb goes back to normal, because did is already carrying the past.

JobFormExample
Finished, regular verbverb + -edI travelled. She moved. We learned.
Finished, irregular verba new shape you learn one by onego → went · get → got · grow → grew · meet → met · take → took · see → saw · have → had
Was and wereI / he / she / it was · you / we / they wereIt was the first time in years. They were tired.
Negativedid not + bare verbI did not open my laptop.
QuestionDid + subject + bare verbDid you enjoy it?
Question word in frontWhere / When / Why + did + subject + bare verbWhere did you grow up?
Short answerYes, I did. / No, we did not."Did you go away?" — "Yes, I did."
Whenyesterday, last week, two years ago, when I was nineI got my first job at nineteen. We moved ten years ago.
The trapthe past lands on ONE word onlyNot I did not went. Say I did not go. Same rule as the s last week.

In one line: the verb carries the past — until did turns up and takes it.

Listening 1 · A First Job

She Only Asked One Question

A woman telling how she got hired at nineteen. Sound first — no text.

1. Why did she go to the interview at all?
Because her mother asked her to go, because it was raining, and because she had nothing else to do that afternoon.
2. What was the only question the interviewer asked her?
What she did when something went wrong.
3. What did she answer, and what happened after that?
She told the story of the night the shop flooded and she stayed until four in the morning moving boxes. The woman said nothing else, only that she started on Monday. She stayed eleven years.
Listening 2 · A Memory

We Moved When I Was Nine

A man remembering the day his family left. Sound first — no text.

1. Why did the family move, and how old was he?
His father got a job three hundred kilometres away, and he was nine. Nobody asked him.
2. What three things does he remember about the day they left?
It rained, the truck was late, and his mother cried in the kitchen when she thought nobody could see her.
3. How did the problem at the new school end?
A boy called Tiago sat down next to him and asked if he wanted half his sandwich. They are still friends thirty years later.
Listening 3 · On Memory

You Do Not Remember the Good Days

A short talk on which days survive, and which ones disappear. Sound first — no text.

1. Which days does the speaker say we actually remember?
Not the good days. The days that were different from the others.
2. What three examples does she give of days people remember well?
The week they moved house, the day the dog arrived, and the first time they travelled somewhere alone.
3. What does she say you need, if a whole year feels empty?
Not a bigger life. About four days in it that were not like the others.

Five short messages, and four of them are about things that already happened. That is what most messages are.
1. What did Daniel do after he got home, and what did he find?
He looked for his old town on the map, and he found that it did not change at all.
2. What did he get wrong in the conversation, and what is the true answer?
He said he moved here last month. He actually moved in March, and he says he always gets that wrong.
3. What does he ask at the end, and what does that tell you about the conversation?
He asks if they will meet at the same time next Sunday. It means the conversation did not end at the park.

Role-Play 2 of 3 — Semi-free

Ask Before You Tell

Scenario: I just came back from somewhere and I only tell you one sentence about it. Ask me at least three questions in the past — where, who with, and one thing I did not do — before you say a single word about your own week.

Keywords:

Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

One Story, No Notes

Scenario: Tell me one thing that already happened to you — the week you just had, the day you got your dog, your first job, or the year you changed something. Say where, say when, say one thing that did not happen, and finish with a question for me.

No keywords, no notes, ninety seconds. Nobody will interrupt you.
