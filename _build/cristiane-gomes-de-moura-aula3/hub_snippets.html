Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

Life Before Now -- Telling a Story That Already Finished

Somebody asks where you went last week, and the answer needs a tense you have not used yet. Key words: to grow up, a childhood, a memory, to move, to get a job, to meet somebody, to miss somebody, a trip, to change, to learn. Structure: past simple — the -ed on regular verbs, the irregulars you learn one by one, and the did that takes the past off the verb in negatives and questions.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To grow up -- to go from being a child to being an adult, usually in one place
"I grew up in Sao Paulo, and I still live in the same city."
A childhood -- the years when you were a child, before anybody expected anything of you
"My childhood happened on one street, and I remember all of it."
A memory -- something from the past that stays in your head, sometimes for years
"My first memory is the smell of my grandmother’s kitchen."
To move -- to leave one home for good and start living in another one
"He moved here when he got his first job, and he never went back."
To get a job -- to be chosen for work and start doing it
"I got my first job at nineteen, and I stayed eleven years."
To meet somebody -- to see and talk to a person for the very first time
"I met him about ten years ago, and we are still friends."
To miss somebody -- to feel sad because a person or a place is not with you any more
"I missed my old street for a whole year after we moved."
A trip -- a journey to a place and back again, usually short
"We took a short trip to the coast and we got back on Friday."
To change -- to become different from what you were, or from what it was
"He complained for two days, and then everything changed."
To learn -- to get a new skill or a new piece of knowledge, and keep it
"I learned everything I know there, in eleven years."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Somebody asks about last week, and suddenly none of the verbs you know are the right shape. English has two answers to this, and the first one is easy: most verbs simply take -ed. "I travelled with my son. We arrived on a Sunday. He complained for two days." Say them out loud: travelled, arrived, complained — the -ed does not add a syllable to any of them. Only after a t or a d does it become one, as in wanted and started.

The second answer is that the most common verbs refuse the rule entirely, and you learn each one on its own: go becomes went, get becomes got, grow up becomes grew up, meet becomes met, take becomes took, see becomes saw, have becomes had. And to be splits in two: I, he, she and it take was; you, we and they take were. "It was a short trip, and we were tired when we got back."

Then comes the part that catches everybody. The moment a sentence says no or asks a question, did turns up and takes the past for itself, and the verb goes back to the shape you find in a dictionary: "I did not open my laptop once." "Did you enjoy it?" "Where did you grow up?" Never I did not went, and never did you liked. It is the same rule you met last week with the small s: the marker lands on one word only. Finish with when it happened — yesterday, last week, ten years ago, when I was nine — and then hand the question back: "And you? Where did you grow up?"

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Past Simple, and the Did That Takes It

GRAMMAR

The verb carries the past — until did turns up and takes it.

JobFormExample
Finished, regular verbverb + -edI travelled. She moved. We learned.
Finished, irregular verba new shape, learned one by onego → went · get → got · grow → grew · meet → met · take → took · see → saw · have → had
Was and wereI / he / she / it was · you / we / they wereIt was a short trip. We were tired.
Negativedid not + bare verbI did not open my laptop.
QuestionDid + subject + bare verbDid you enjoy it?
Question word in frontWhere / When / Why + did + subject + bare verbWhere did you grow up?
Short answerYes, I did. / No, we did not."Did you go away?" — "Yes, I did."
When it happenedyesterday, last week, ten years ago, when I was nineWe moved ten years ago.
In speechdid not becomes didn’tI didn’t open my laptop once.

Never: I did not went · Did she liked the trip? · Where you went last week? · He growed up in a small town · I was go to the coast

A "Where you grew up?"

A "I met him about ten years ago, and we are still friends."
