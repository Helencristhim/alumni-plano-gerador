Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A plot -- the story of a film, a series or a book
"The plot is simple, but the dialogue is very fast."
A cliffhanger -- an exciting end of an episode that makes you want to see the next one
"Every episode ends with a cliffhanger."
Subtitles -- the words of the dialogue, written at the bottom of the screen
"I watch with English subtitles the first time."
To binge-watch -- to watch many episodes of a series, one after the other
"We binge-watched the whole season in one weekend."
A spoiler -- information that tells you what happens before you see it
"Don’t tell me the end. No spoilers!"
A twist -- a sudden, surprising change in the story
"I didn’t see the twist coming."
To mumble -- to speak quietly and not clearly
"The main actor mumbles, so I need the subtitles."
To make out -- to manage to hear or see something, with difficulty
"I couldn’t make out the last line."
To pick up -- to learn something, like a word or an accent, without studying it
"I picked up a lot of slang from that series."
To catch on -- to begin to understand something
"It took me a few episodes to catch on to the jokes."
Slang -- very informal words that a group of people uses
"British slang is hard to understand at first."
To rewind -- to go back to an earlier part of a video or a recording
"I rewind the same scene three times."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Fast Speech -- Would, Had and Gonna

GRAMMAR

At native speed, would and had both become ’d. The next word tells you which one it is. Gonna, wanna and gotta are normal in speech, but you write going to, want to and have got to.

JobFormExample
’d + base verb= wouldI’d watch it again.
’d + past participle= hadI’d seen it before.
’d better + verb= had better (strong advice)You’d better turn on the subtitles.
gonna= going to (only in speech)I’m gonna start season two.
wanna / gotta= want to / have got toDo you wanna watch it? I gotta go.
The trapyou hear gonna, you write going toIn an email, not I’m gonna send: I’m going to send.

Never: I’d saw it · I’d watched it again tomorrow · You’d better to go · gonna in a formal email
