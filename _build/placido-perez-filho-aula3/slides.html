Chapter 3: The Code

Would or Had?

One sound, two meanings

Grammar Discovery

One Sound, Two Meanings

"I’d watch it again tomorrow."

"By episode three, I’d already guessed the twist."

"You’d better turn on the subtitles."

"I’m gonna start season two tonight."

In the first three sentences you hear the same sound: ’d. Look at the blue word after it. A simple verb (watch), a past participle (guessed) or better: each one tells you what ’d means. And gonna? You hear it everywhere, but what is the full form?

JobFormExample
’d + base verb= wouldI’d watch it again.
’d + past participle= hadI’d seen it before.
’d better + verb= had better (strong advice)You’d better turn on the subtitles.
gonna= going to (only in speech)I’m gonna start season two.
wanna / gotta= want to / have got toDo you wanna watch it? I gotta go.
The trapyou hear gonna, you write going toIn an email, not I’m gonna send: I’m going to send.

In one line: the word after ’d decides: base verb = would, past participle = had, better = had better.

Listening 1 · A Film Podcast

Two Minutes of Fast Talk

An American film podcast host talks about a new series, at full speed. Sound first — no text.

1. What did she do last weekend?
She binge-watched a new series, all eight episodes.
2. What does she say about the first episode?
It is slow; you’d better give it two episodes.
3. What would she do differently if she watched it again?
She would turn the subtitles on, because one actor mumbles.
Listening 2 · Slang, Explained

What Did He Just Say?

An Australian English teacher explains why series are full of words you never learned. Sound first — no text.

1. Why do series use so much slang, according to him?
Because writers want the characters to sound like real people.
2. What is his advice when you hear a new slang word?
Do not stop the episode; pick it up from the context and check it later.
3. How long does he say it takes to catch on to a new accent?
About three or four episodes.
Listening 3 · A Listening Coach

A Method for Series

A British listening coach explains a method for watching series to learn. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she recommend for the first time you watch a scene?
English subtitles.
2. How many times should you rewind, at most?
Three times.
3. Why does she say the third step is the most important?
Because it trains your ear for the real speed, without help.
