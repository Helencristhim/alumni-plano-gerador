Chapter 3: The Code

The Order Nobody Taught You

Adjective order · and the difference between tiring and tired

Grammar Discovery

Why Can You Not Swap Them?

"A gruff old Italian cheese seller."

"A lovely old wooden gate."

"Two big green metal pots."

"A chatty young Brazilian stallholder."

In all four, the word that says what you think comes first, and the word closest to the noun says what the thing is made of or where it is from.

SlotWhat goes thereExample
1. OpinionWhat YOU think of it.lovely, gruff, chatty
2. SizeHow big or small.small, big, tiny
3. AgeHow old.old, young, new
4. ColorThe color.green, white
5. OriginWhere it is from.Italian, Brazilian
6. MaterialWhat it is made of.wooden, metal
In real speechTwo adjectives, three at the most. Use a comma only between two of the SAME type.a small, tight-knit town

In one line: opinion first, then the facts, and the noun keeps its closest friend.

Grammar Discovery

Tiring, or Tired?

"The Saturday market is fascinating."

"I am fascinated by the people there."

"Everybody knowing your business is exhausting."

"After two hours of chatting I was exhausted."

The orange word describes the thing that causes the feeling. The blue one describes the person who feels it.

EndingIts jobExample
-ingThe thing that causes the feeling.The market is fascinating.
-edThe person who feels it.I am fascinated.
The trapSaying the -ing one about yourself.I am boring = I make others bored.
The prepositionby or with after the -ed one.tired of it · fascinated by them
Where it livesBoth work before a noun too.a tiring week · a tired woman

In one line: -ing is what it does to you; -ed is what you feel.

Chapter 4: Many Englishes

Small Towns, Different Accents

A conversation, and two people who cannot be anonymous

Dialogue

Who Are All Those People?

G
I keep seeing photos of that Saturday market of yours. Who are all those people?
A
Regulars, mostly. It is a small, tight-knit town, and I bump into the same faces every week.
G
I grew up in a town like that in Ohio. Two thousand people. Everybody welcoming, everybody nosy.
A
That is exactly it. People are kind and people are interested. Word gets around before you have finished telling it.
G
So who is the character? Every town has one.
A
A gruff old Italian cheese seller with a weathered face. He barely says hello, and he keeps the best piece for me.
G
Ours was the barber. Forty years, same chair. I moved to Chicago and I missed him more than the house.
A
I tried to keep to myself the first year. It lasted a month. It was exhausting at first, and now it is the reason I stay.
Comprehension

About Grant

1. Where did Grant grow up, and how big was the town?
In Ohio, in a town of two thousand people.
2. How does he describe the people there, in two words?
Everybody welcoming, everybody nosy.
3. Who was the character in his town, and what happened when he left?
The barber — forty years in the same chair. Grant moved to Chicago and missed him more than the house.
Listening

The Square at Seven

A woman who left Milan for four thousand people. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What was the first thing she noticed about the town?
That nobody there is anonymous.
2. She planned to keep to herself. How long did that last, and what ended it?
Two weeks. Somebody knocked on her door with a plate of food, and after that she was a regular in three shops.
3. What does she compare it with at the end?
Eleven years in the same building in Milan, where she never learned one single name.
Listening 2

The Same Six Men at the Same Table

A man who left, and goes back twice a year. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Does he miss the town or the people, and why?
The people, not the town. It is not the buildings — it is that somebody notices when you are not there.
2. What did he find exhausting when he was seventeen?
That everybody knew what he did on Friday before he got home on Saturday.
3. What happened in the city of three million?
For two years nobody said his name in the street.
