Lesson 05 -- Pre-class

The People of a Small Town -- The Neighbors and the Market

The faces you meet every Saturday and how English describes them. Key words: tight-knit, a stallholder, down-to-earth, chatty, nosy, weathered, welcoming, gruff, to bump into somebody, a regular, to keep to yourself, word gets around. Structure: adjective order, and -ed against -ing adjectives.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Tight-knit -- describing a group where everybody knows and looks after everybody else
"It is a small, tight-knit town of forty thousand."
A stallholder -- the person who runs a stall at an open-air market
"The stallholders arrive before six on Saturday."
Down-to-earth -- practical and straightforward, with no pretension at all
"People here are down-to-earth. They say what they think."
Chatty -- somebody who enjoys talking and talks a lot
"My chatty neighbor stops me at the gate every morning."
Nosy -- too interested in what other people are doing
"Small towns have nosy people. That is the price."
Weathered -- marked and changed by many years of sun, rain and wind
"A weathered old man sells tomatoes on the corner."
Welcoming -- making a new person feel comfortable and wanted
"They were welcoming from the very first week."
Gruff -- sounding rough and unfriendly, without actually being unkind
"The butcher is gruff, and he keeps the best cut for me."
To bump into somebody -- to meet somebody by chance, with no plan at all
"I bump into the same five people every Saturday."
A regular -- a person who goes to a place so often that everybody knows them
"After three years I am a regular at that bakery."
To keep to yourself -- to live quietly and not mix much with other people
"I tried to keep to myself. It lasted about a month."
Word gets around -- news travels fast from person to person
"Word gets around fast in a town this size."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Saturday the square fills up before seven. The stallholders are there first. At stall four there is a gruff old Italian cheese seller with a weathered face, and at stall one a chatty young Brazilian woman selling flowers who knows everything about everybody.

Ana walks in through a lovely old wooden gate and bumps into the same five people every week. It is a small, tight-knit town: people are welcoming, and people are nosy. Word gets around before you have finished telling it.

She tried to keep to herself in the first year. It lasted a month. The market is fascinating and she is still fascinated by it; some weeks it is exhausting, and some weeks she comes home exhausted and happy. Now she is a regular in three different shops.

1. Why is it "a gruff old Italian cheese seller" and never "an Italian old gruff cheese seller"?
A Because the longest adjective always comes first.
B Because English fixes the order: opinion, then age, then origin, then the noun.
C Because "Italian" is a proper noun and cannot start a phrase.
2. "The market is fascinating and she is fascinated by it." Why the two different endings?
A Because -ing describes the thing that causes the feeling and -ed describes the person who feels it.
B Because one is present and the other is past.
C Because the second one is a passive sentence.
3. "A small, tight-knit town" has a comma. Why?
A Because every list of adjectives takes a comma.
B Because the sentence would be too long without it.
C Because those two adjectives are of a similar type, so neither one clearly comes first.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Order, and the Two Endings

GRAMMAR

Where each adjective goes, and which ending belongs to whom.

SlotWhat goes thereExample
1. OpinionWhat you think of ita lovely gate · a gruff man
2. SizeHow big or smalla small town · big pots
3. AgeHow oldan old stall · a young woman
4. Color / 5. Origin / 6. MaterialThe facts, closest to the noungreen metal pots · an Italian seller
-ing adjectiveThe thing that causes the feelingThe market is fascinating.
-ed adjectiveThe person who feels itI am fascinated by it.

Never: a wooden old gate · a woman Italian chatty · I am boring in this town · the market was fascinated.
