Chapter 3: The Story Code

Was Happening, Happened

past continuous · past simple · when · while

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The board was meeting when the news broke."

"I was driving to the stadium when the spokesperson called me."

"While the journalists were waiting, the president left by the back door."

"The club published a statement, and then the coverage changed."

What do the orange words have in common? Which action is long, in progress? Which action is short?

FormUseExample
was / were + verb-ingpast continuous: a long action in progress, the backgroundThe board was meeting...
verb-ed / irregular pastpast simple: a short, complete action, or the main events...when the news broke.
when + past simplethe short action that interruptswhen the spokesperson called
while + past continuousthe long action in the backgroundwhile the journalists were waiting

Use the past continuous for the background and the past simple for the events. A list of events in order is all past simple: "The club published a statement, and then the coverage changed." Verbs like know, believe, want stay in the past simple: "I knew", not "I was knowing".

Did You See the News?

Dialogue

The Coffee Break

S
Good morning, Nilo. Did you see the headline about the club president this morning?
N
Yes, I was having breakfast when the breaking news came in. What happened exactly?
S
The board was meeting when a journalist published an allegation about the transfer payments.
N
An allegation is not proof. Did the club publish a statement?
S
Yes. While the reporters were waiting outside, the spokesperson read a short statement.
N
And who was the source of the story? An insider?
S
Nobody knows. Some people say it is only a rumor, but the coverage is huge.
N
In compliance, we wait for the facts. A controversy is not a scandal until somebody proves it.
