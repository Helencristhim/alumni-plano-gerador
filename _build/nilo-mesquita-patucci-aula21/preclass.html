Lesson 21 -- Pre-class

News and Current Affairs -- What Was Happening When the Story Broke

Discuss sports governance news with colleagues: tell what happened with the past simple and what was happening with the past continuous (when / while). Key words: headline, breaking news, allegation, scandal, spokesperson, statement, source, coverage, controversy, rumor.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Headline -- the title of a news story, in big letters
"The headline was about the club president."
Breaking news -- very new and important news, reported as it happens
"The breaking news came in while we were having lunch."
Allegation -- a statement that someone did something wrong, without proof yet
"The allegation is serious, but nobody has proved it."
Scandal -- a situation that shocks people because it is wrong or illegal
"The scandal damaged the reputation of the league."
Spokesperson -- a person who speaks officially for an organization
"The club spokesperson answered the journalists."
Statement -- an official message that an organization gives to the public
"The federation published a short statement this morning."
Source -- the person or document that gives information to a journalist
"The journalist did not name her source."
Coverage -- how much and how the media reports a story
"The coverage of the case was fair and balanced."
Controversy -- a strong public disagreement about a decision or an event
"The new rule created a big controversy among the clubs."
Rumor -- a story that people repeat but that may not be true
"It was only a rumor; the coach did not leave."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Continuous vs Past Simple

GRAMMAR

How to tell a story: the background and the events.

FormUseExample
was / were + verb-inga long action in progress (the background)The board was meeting...
past simplea short, complete action (the event)...when the president left.
when + past simplethe short action that interruptsI was driving when he called.
while + past continuousthe long action in the backgroundWhile the reporters were waiting, ...
negative / questionwasn't / weren't + -ing; was / were + subject + -ing?What were they discussing?
RuleUse the past continuous for the scene and the past simple for what happened. A list of events in order is all past simple. State verbs (know, believe, want, understand) do not take -ing: "I knew", not "I was knowing".
