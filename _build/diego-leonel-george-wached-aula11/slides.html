Chapter 3: The Code

The First Verb Decides

-ing or to, and why it is not a matter of taste

Grammar Discovery

Which Ones Take -ing?

"I enjoy following a patient for years."

"I plan to apply for a dermatology residency."

"The patient denied using any steroid cream."

"I finally managed to pass Step 2 on the first attempt."

Look at the red verbs, then at the blue ones. The meaning does not tell you the form. What does?

JobFormExample
Verb + -ingenjoy, avoid, consider, keep, recommend, suggest, admit, deny, finish, practice, involve, riskI recommend applying sunscreen every morning.
Verb + toplan, want, need, decide, expect, hope, manage, agree, refuse, tend, offer, failI hope to match into dermatology.
After a prepositionalways -ingI am interested in doing research. Thank you for coming.
Expressions with -ingit is worth, can’t help, look forward toIt is worth reviewing biostatistics. I look forward to hearing from you.
Recommend / suggestnever + toNot I suggest to refer. I suggest referring the patient.

In one line: the first verb chooses the form of the second — learn the verb with its partner, recommend doing, plan to do, never one without the other.

Grammar Practice

Same Verb, Different Meaning

stop, remember, try, forget — say it first, then click

"She stopped ___using the medication when she noticed a rash." (use — she no longer uses it)
"On the way to the clinic, he stopped ___to buy coffee." (buy — the reason he stopped)
"Please remember ___to apply sunscreen before you go out." (apply — a task for later)
"I remember ___seeing this rash in a textbook." (see — a memory of the past)
"If the cream does not help, try ___taking a shorter shower." (take — an experiment)
Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Interview, at Full Speed

One interview, two recordings, one schedule

Dialogue

The Program Director

H
So, Diego. What do you enjoy most about dermatology?
D
I enjoy following a patient over several years. With chronic conditions you see the whole story, not one visit.
H
And what do you find hard? Be honest.
D
I admit finding case presentations difficult in English. I tend to avoid presenting, because I get nervous.
H
What are you doing about it?
D
I have decided to present one case every week this year. It is worth feeling uncomfortable now.
H
Good. Last one: what would you do if you did not know an answer on the ward?
D
Could I clarify one thing first? On a round, or with a patient?
H
With a patient.
D
I would admit not knowing, look it up, and go back to the patient before the end of the day.
Comprehension

About Dr. Hughes

1. What does Dr. Hughes ask about first?
What he enjoys most about dermatology.
2. What does she ask him to be when she asks what he finds hard?
Honest. She wants a real weakness, not a polished answer.
3. Her last question was not completely clear. What did she mean?
What he would do if he did not know an answer with a patient, not on a round.
Listening 1 · A Program Director

What I Listen For

A program director on the first five minutes of an interview. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say the weak answer to the question about enjoying dermatology sounds like?
A list of nouns: research, patients, procedures. The strong answer is a sentence with a verb in it.
2. What makes her believe a candidate who talks about a weakness?
That he admits a real problem and then says exactly what he has decided to do about it.
3. What does she say the candidates she remembers answer when they do not know something?
That they would admit not knowing, look it up, and go back to the patient before the end of the day.
Listening 2 · A Resident

The Habit I Was Avoiding

A resident on the year he studied the wrong way. Sound first — no text.

1. What did he keep doing during his first year of preparation?
He kept reading the same review book, cover to cover, three times.
2. What did he stop doing, and what did he start doing instead?
He stopped reading the book and started doing forty timed questions a day before work.
3. What small change did he try, and what happened?
He tried writing his first answer on paper before he clicked, because he tended to change correct answers. His score went up twelve points in a month.
