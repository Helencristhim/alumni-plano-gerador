Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To involve -- to include something as a necessary part
"The procedure involves taking a small sample of skin."
To tend to -- to usually do something, often without planning it
"Patients tend to underestimate how much sun they get."
To risk -- to do something that could lead to a bad result
"If we wait, we risk missing the diagnosis."
To conduct -- to organize and carry out something formal
"Diego conducted a full skin examination."
To clarify -- to make something clearer by explaining it again
"Could you clarify what you mean by occasionally?"
To proceed -- to continue with something, often after a pause
"If the results are normal, we can proceed with the treatment."
To acknowledge -- to say openly that something is true or exists
"It is important to acknowledge what the patient is feeling."
To deny -- to say that something is not true or did not happen
"The patient denied using any steroid cream."
To admit -- to say, often unwillingly, that something is true
"I admit finding case presentations difficult in English."
To be likely to -- to be probably going to happen
"This rash is likely to come back in the summer."
To go over -- to review something carefully, step by step
"Let us go over the criteria before the exam."
To be worth -- to deserve the time or effort; followed by -ing
"It is worth checking the margins twice."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

In English, when one verb follows another, the first verb decides the form of the second. Some verbs take -ing: you enjoy following a patient, you avoid presenting a case, a patient denies using a cream, a candidate admits finding something difficult. Others take to: you plan to apply, you decide to present, you hope to match, you tend to avoid things that make you nervous.

The meaning does not tell you the form, and Portuguese does not help: it uses the infinitive almost everywhere. So the verbs have to be learned with their partners. The two that matter most in a clinic are recommend and suggest, and both take -ing: I recommend applying sunscreen, I suggest referring the patient. Never suggest to.

After a preposition, the verb is always -ing: interested in doing research, thank you for coming, I look forward to hearing from you. The expression it is worth also takes -ing: it is worth checking the margins twice.

Four verbs accept both forms and change meaning. She stopped using the cream means she no longer uses it; he stopped to buy coffee gives the reason he stopped. Remember to apply sunscreen is a task for later; I remember seeing this rash is a memory. Try and forget work the same way.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Gerunds and Infinitives

GRAMMAR

The first verb chooses the form of the second. Learn each verb with its partner: recommend doing, plan to do.

JobFormExample
Verb + -ingenjoy, avoid, keep, recommend, suggest, admit, deny, involve, riskI recommend applying sunscreen.
Verb + toplan, want, decide, hope, manage, agree, refuse, tend, expectI plan to apply for a residency.
