Recovery

Four Sentences, Four Times

"I work on a metro line."
"Right now I am working on the second platform."
"Last month I went to the north for two weeks."
"Did you work last weekend?"

Same verb, four different times. Which one is now, which one is every day, which one is finished, and which one is a question?

When you use itThe formYour sentence
Every day, alwaysI work / she worksI work on a metro line.
Right now, todayI am workingRight now I am working on the second platform.
Finished, regular verbI workedLast Monday I worked twelve hours.
Finished, irregular verbI went / took / madeLast month I went to the north for two weeks.
Finished, negativeI did not goI did not go underground on Wednesday.
Asking about the pastDid you...? / What did you...?Did you work last weekend?
Ability and permissionI can / I cannotI can read a drawing, but I cannot drive the big machine.
How oftenalways / usually / sometimes / neverI usually get home at seven.
Where things arethere is / there are + prepositionThere is one gate for the trucks, next to the fence.

Nine rows. One row per lesson. This is what ten minutes is made of.

Recovery

In, On, At — One More Time

"My shift starts at seven."
"We have the meeting on Monday."
"The station opens in March."
"I work at the site, in Brazil."

Three little words, and they are never random. Why at seven, on Monday, and in March?

TimePlace
inthe big box: in March, in 2019, in the morninginside something: in Brazil, in the tunnel
onthe day: on Monday, on Friday morningon a surface: on the wall, on the second platform
atthe exact point: at seven, at middaythe exact point: at the gate, at the site
The funnelBig to small: in the year, on the day, at the hour. The same order works for places.
No prepositionNever before next, last, this or every: last Monday, next week, every day. Not "in last Monday".
