Chapter 1: First Words

From Your Doubt to Their Voice

Last time you learned to say exactly how sure you are. In academic writing there is a second question: who says so? You, or an author you read? A good paragraph never leaves the reader guessing.

Which author has influenced your research on regulation the most? What is his or her main idea?

Chapter 3: The Code

As Ostrom Argues, Admittedly

Attribution and stance

Grammar Discovery

Who Is Speaking?

"As Ostrom argues, local rules can work."

"According to the classic view, independence comes from the law."

"Several authors contend that regulation slows growth."

"Admittedly, the sample is small. However, the finding is robust."

The blue words give the idea to someone else: an author or a group of authors. The red words are the writer's own voice. Now look closely: after as Ostrom argues, is there a that? And after contend?

JobFormExample
Report with the author's positionAs + author + argues / notes / suggests, + sentenceAs Ostrom argues, local rules can work.
Give the sourceAccording to + author or source, + sentenceAccording to the classic view, ...
Reporting verb + thatauthor + argues / contends / suggests / claims + thatSeveral authors contend that ...
Your own stanceClearly, / Arguably, / Admittedly, / Surprisingly, + sentenceAdmittedly, the sample is small.
Your own opinionIn my view, / I would argue thatIn my view, the law is not enough.
The trapnever according to me; no that after as X arguesNot According to me · not As Ostrom argues that.

In one line: as X argues and according to give the voice to someone else; in my view and admittedly are your own voice.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Draft, at Real Speed

One conversation, two recordings, one abstract

Dialogue

The Editor Reads the Draft

M
Aguinaldo, I read your draft. The research is strong, but I have one problem: I can't always hear your voice.
A
I see. Can you give me an example?
M
In section two, you say that agencies need fixed mandates. Is that your finding, or are you citing someone?
A
That's the classic view. I should write: according to the classic view, independence comes from the law.
M
Exactly. And then tell me your stance. Do you agree?
A
Only partly. I would argue that the first directors matter more. Admittedly, my sample is small.
M
That's fine, as long as you say it. Reviewers respect a writer who shows the limits. Is the result robust?
A
It appears in all six countries. As Ostrom argues, the people who apply a rule matter as much as the rule.
Comprehension

About Moira

1. What is Moira's problem with the draft?
She cannot always hear his voice in it.
2. What does Moira want to know about section two?
If the idea is his finding or if he is citing someone.
3. According to Moira, what do reviewers respect?
A writer who shows the limits of the research.

Listening 2 · The Conference Paper

Independence in Practice

A researcher presents his paper on regulatory agencies in Latin America. Sound first — no text.

1. What is most of the body of research about, according to him?
Europe and the United States.
2. According to the classic view, where does independence come from?
From the law: fixed mandates and separate budgets.
3. What mattered more in his findings?
The track record of the first directors.
