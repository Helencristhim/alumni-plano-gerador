Lesson 23 -- Pre-class

The Academic Voice -- Whose Idea Is This?

A journal editor reads a draft article, and the writer learns to separate his voice from the authors he cites. Key words: to cite, a stance, a finding, peer-reviewed, to contend, a body of research, to attribute, robust, a footnote, admittedly. Structure: as X argues, according to, argue / contend that, admittedly, in my view.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To cite -- to mention a book, an article or an author as the source of an idea
"Always cite the author when you use her idea."
A stance -- your position or opinion on a question
"The reader needs to know your stance on the new rule."
A finding -- a result that a study or a research project discovers
"The main finding is that independent agencies attract more investment."
Peer-reviewed -- checked by other experts before it is published
"Only peer-reviewed articles count for the doctorate."
To contend -- to say firmly that something is true, in an argument
"Some authors contend that regulation slows growth."
A body of research -- all the studies that exist on one subject
"There is a large body of research on independent agencies."
To attribute -- to say that an idea or a result comes from a person or a cause
"The author attributes the success to the first directors."
Robust -- strong, and still true when you test it in different ways
"The finding is robust: it appears in six countries."
A footnote -- a short note at the bottom of a page, often with a source
"The reference to the court decision is in a footnote."
Admittedly -- I accept that this is true, although it weakens my point a little
"Admittedly, the sample is small."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Aguinaldo is revising his article for an international journal. The editor says one thing is missing: the reader cannot hear his stance.

So he rewrites section two. According to the classic view, independence comes from fixed mandates. As Ostrom argues, local rules can work. Several authors contend that regulation slows growth.

Then he adds his own voice. In my view, the first directors matter more than the law. Admittedly, the sample is small. However, the finding is robust. Notice: there is no that after as Ostrom argues, and he never writes according to me.

1. What is missing from the draft, according to the editor?
A His stance.
B A list of all the court decisions and laws he used in section two of the article.
C More authors from Europe and the United States, because the editor wants a bigger literature review.
2. Why does the text say "As Ostrom argues, local rules can work" with no that?
A Because Ostrom is a woman, and in English there is no that after the name of a woman.
B Because as X argues is followed directly by a comma and a sentence.
C Because the sentence is in the present tense, and that is only used for the past.
3. Which sentence is in Aguinaldo's own voice?
A According to the classic view, independence comes from fixed mandates.
B Several authors contend that regulation slows growth.
C In my view, the first directors matter more.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- As X Argues, According To, Admittedly

GRAMMAR

Attribution gives an idea to its author; stance shows your own position. As X argues and according to take a comma; argue and contend take that.

JobFormExample
Report with the author's positionAs + author + argues / notes / suggests, + sentenceAs Ostrom argues, local rules can work.
Give the sourceAccording to + author or source, + sentenceAccording to the classic view, ...
Reporting verb + thatauthor + argues / contends / suggests + thatSeveral authors contend that ...
Your own stanceClearly, / Arguably, / Admittedly, + sentenceAdmittedly, the sample is small.
Your own opinionIn my view, / I would argue thatI would argue that the directors matter more.
QuestionWhat does + author + argue? · According to whom?What does Ostrom argue?

Never: According to me · As Ostrom argues that... · Several authors contend regulation slow growth · Admittedly..., but however...

Stage 2: Put the Paragraph in Order

Order

You write one paragraph of your article. Put the sentences in a logical order.

?"Admittedly, four agencies is a small sample."
?"In the four agencies I studied, the first board mattered more than the law."
?"According to the classic view, independence comes from fixed mandates."
?"My findings suggest a different picture."
?"However, I would argue that people matter as much as the rules."
?"There is a large body of research on independent agencies."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A professor asks where an idea comes from. You give the source. You say:
A "According to me and to many other authors, independence comes from the law and the budget."
B "According to the classic view, independence comes from the law."
C "As the classic view argues that independence comes from the law and from a fixed budget."
You give your own opinion in a thesis defense. You say:
A "I would argue that the first directors matter more."
B "According to me, the first directors of the agencies matter more than the law in all cases."
C "As I argue that the first directors of the agencies matter more than the law in all cases."
A reviewer says your sample is small. You accept it and defend your point. You say:
A "Admittedly, the sample is small, but however the finding is very robust in all the cases I studied."
B "Admittedly the sample is small, however finding robust, because I studied four agencies in Brazil."
C "Admittedly, the sample is small. However, the finding is robust."
The source of a quote is at the bottom of the page. It is in:
A a stance.
B a footnote.
C a finding.
