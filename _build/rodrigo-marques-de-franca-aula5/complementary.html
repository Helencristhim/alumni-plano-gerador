Lesson 5 -- Two Weeks in Italy

Podcast
NPR -- "The original travel expert, Rick Steves, on how to avoid contributing to overtourism"

The interview version of the argument you built in class: the famous place is packed, the town forty minutes away is not, and the second one is often the better day. He compares constantly and never exaggerates, which is exactly the register the lesson trained.

Tip: write down three comparisons he makes and mark the modifier in each one. Then use those three modifiers about two cities you know, out loud.

Talk
Pico Iyer -- "The art of stillness" (TED, 15 min)

A travel writer arguing for doing less on a trip, in some of the most carefully built English on the TED site. Slow, precise, and full of the kind of sentence you could steal whole. It is also the strongest possible answer to the question the first listening asked.

Tip: pick the three sentences you would most like to be able to say. Copy them out, read them aloud, and change one detail in each so that they become yours.

Series
Somebody Feed Phil -- the Venice episode (Netflix)

An hour of somebody describing places and meals out loud, in easy American English, and comparing everything to everything. The show is unashamedly enthusiastic, which makes it a good test: count how often he says the best and ask yourself whether you believe him.

Tip: every time he calls something the best, pause and rephrase it with a modifier that is more honest. Far better than, about as good as, nowhere near as good as. That rewriting is the whole exercise.
