Lesson 05 -- Pre-class

Two Weeks in Italy -- Comparing Without Sounding Like a Brochure

Somebody asks which city you liked best, and beautiful is not an answer. Key words: a stopover, to wander, a stretch, bustling, a local haunt, sprawling, to take something in, a day trip, packed, a vantage point, off the beaten track, to be worth the detour. Structure: comparatives and superlatives, with the modifiers that say how big the difference is.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A stopover -- a short stay in a city between two flights, on the way somewhere else
"We had a nine-hour stopover in Lisbon and went into the city."
To wander -- to walk without a fixed destination, for the pleasure of walking
"We did one thing in the morning and wandered for the rest of the day."
A stretch -- a continuous section of something long, such as a coast or a road
"That stretch of coast is far quieter than the one further north."
Bustling -- full of noisy, energetic activity, in a way people usually enjoy
"The market was bustling at eight in the morning and empty by two."
A local haunt -- a place local people go to regularly, and which visitors usually miss
"It looked like nothing from the street. It was a local haunt."
Sprawling -- spreading out over a large area, without a clear plan behind it
"It is a sprawling city, so you need a plan or you lose the day."
To take something in -- to look at something slowly enough to absorb it properly
"We sat there for half an hour, just taking it in."
A day trip -- a journey out of the city and back on the same day
"The day trip was worth far more to us than another day in the centre."
Packed -- so full of people that moving through is uncomfortable
"In August the centre is packed, and you spend the day in queues."
A vantage point -- a place from which you get a wide, clear view of something
"There is a vantage point above the old town, and almost nobody walks up."
Off the beaten track -- away from the places tourists normally go
"Anywhere twenty minutes off the beaten track is quieter and cheaper."
To be worth the detour -- good enough to justify going out of your way for it
"It is forty minutes in the wrong direction, and it is worth the detour."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Three days in Florence in August, which is two days more than anybody needs. The centre was packed, the queues were far longer than the guidebook admits, and the city itself is nowhere near as walkable as it looks on a map. On the second morning they gave up and took a train to a smaller town fifty minutes away, which almost nobody had mentioned.

It was slightly less beautiful and a great deal quieter. They walked the whole wall in an hour, found a vantage point above the roofs where nobody else had bothered to climb, and sat there for half an hour just taking it in. Then they wandered until they were tired, which is about as good a plan as any. Lunch was in a place with six tables, a proper local haunt, and it was by far the best meal of the two weeks.

The honest conclusion is not that the small town beat the city, because it did not, and they would go back to Florence tomorrow. The point is that a day trip twenty minutes off the beaten track was worth far more to them than a third day of standing in a queue. On the way home there was a nine-hour stopover in Lisbon, which turned out to be the second-best surprise of the trip: a bustling market at eight in the morning, an empty stretch of river at noon, and a sprawling city that needed three days and got nine hours. Even so, it was worth the detour.

1. "It was slightly less beautiful and a great deal quieter." What are the two modifiers telling you?
A That both differences are the same size.
B That the difference in beauty is small, and the difference in noise is large. The modifier is what carries the proportion.
C That the writer is not sure which town was better.
2. "The city is nowhere near as walkable as it looks on a map." What does nowhere near as mean here?
A Much less walkable. Nowhere near as marks a very large gap, not a small one.
B Slightly less walkable than expected.
C Exactly as walkable as the map suggests.
3. The last paragraph refuses to say the small town was better. Why does that make the account more convincing?
A Because a traveller should never express an opinion.
B Because it separates two different judgements: which place is more impressive, and which day was worth more. Somebody who can hold both sounds like they were there.
C Because Florence is objectively the better city and the writer knows it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Comparatives and Superlatives

GRAMMAR

Anybody can say better. The modifier is what tells your listener you were actually there.

JobFormExample
Short adjective-er than · the -estquieter · the quietest
Long adjectivemore / less · the mostmore crowded · the most crowded
A big differencefar · much · a lot · a great dealfar quieter · a great deal cheaper
A small differenceslightly · a little · marginallyslightly less crowded
Roughly equalabout as ... as · just as ... asabout as good as the other one
Not remotely equalnowhere near as ... asnowhere near as expensive
Irregulargood → better → the bestbad → worse → the worst

Never: more cheaper · more good · as good than · best meal of the trip (without the) · the most cheapest

Stage 2: Put the Recommendation in Order

Order

Somebody asks whether the day trip is worth it. Put your recommendation in a logical order.

?"It is fifty minutes on the train, so you lose half a day."
?"The town itself is nowhere near as impressive as the city."
?"But it is far quieter, and in August that matters more than you think."
?"Walk the wall first, then find the vantage point above the roofs."
?"Lunch was by far the best meal we had in two weeks."
?"So yes, it is worth the detour. How many days do you have?"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to say the small town is much less crowded. You say:
A "It is more quieter than the city."
B "It is far quieter than the city."
C "It is the most quiet of the city."
Somebody asks if the food outside the centre is worse. It is not. You say:
A "The food is as good than in the centre."
B "The food is about as good as anything in the centre, and a lot cheaper."
C "The food is more good outside the centre."
A famous place disappointed you and you want to say so politely. You say:
A "It was nowhere near as impressive as I had expected, and it was packed."
B "It is terrible and nobody should go."
C "It was more bad than the other one."
You are recommending a detour and being honest about the trade-off. You say:
A "You must go there, it is the best place in the world."
B "It is slightly less impressive and a great deal quieter, so yes, it is worth the detour."
C "It is off the beaten track, so it is packed."
