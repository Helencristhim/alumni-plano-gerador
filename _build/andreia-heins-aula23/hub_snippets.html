Q&A Under Pressure -- Handling Questions

Answer tough questions after a talk calmly and honestly. Key words: hedge, evidence, likely, tend to, skeptical, bluff, get back to, off the top of my head, fair question, beyond the scope. Structure: hedging -- I would say that, it seems that, tend to / be likely to + base verb.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Hedge -- to speak carefully, so you do not say something is 100% certain
"When the data is not clear, I hedge."
Evidence -- facts or data that show something is true
"Is there any evidence for that?"
Likely -- probably true, or probably going to happen
"The new console is likely to arrive in May."
Tend to -- to usually do something, or usually be a certain way
"Younger patients tend to recover faster."
Skeptical -- not easy to convince; having doubts
"Some surgeons are skeptical about new technology."
Bluff -- to pretend that you know something when you do not
"If you bluff, people stop trusting you."
Get back to -- to answer or contact someone later, with more information
"I will get back to you this afternoon."
Off the top of my head -- from memory, quickly, without checking the exact information
"Off the top of my head, we have twelve nurses on the team."
Fair question -- a good, reasonable question that you accept politely
"That is a fair question, and I am glad you asked."
Beyond the scope -- outside the topic or the limits of what you are talking about
"Insurance is beyond the scope of today's meeting."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

After her pitch, Andreia gets questions from the audience. A skeptical director asks: "Is robotic surgery always better?" Andreia does not say yes or no. She says: "That is a fair question. I would say that it tends to be better for complex cases."

Then he asks for the exact cost. Andreia does not bluff. "Off the top of my head, it is about five thousand dollars per nurse, but let me check and get back to you." She adds: "It seems that trained teams finish faster, so the program is likely to pay for itself."

This is hedging: careful language that shows you are honest about the evidence. After tend to, seem to and be likely to, use the base verb. After I would say that and it seems that, use a full sentence.

1. Why does Andreia say "I would say that it tends to be better"?
A To be careful and honest: it is not better for every patient.
B Because she does not know anything about robotic surgery and wants to finish the talk early.
C To end the conversation fast.
2. Which form comes after "is likely to"?
A -ing: "is likely to paying."
B The base verb: "is likely to pay."
C Past: "is likely to paid."
3. What does Andreia do when she does not know the exact cost?
A She invents an exact number, so the director does not think she is unprepared.
B She says nothing.
C She gives an estimate and promises to get back to him.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Hedging

GRAMMAR

Hedging shows you are careful and honest, not weak.

HedgeThenExample
I would say that ...full sentenceI would say that it is worth it.
It seems that ...full sentenceIt seems that the cost goes down.
tend to / seem tobase verbPatients tend to recover faster.
be likely to / be unlikely tobase verbIt is likely to save money.
QuestionIs it likely to ...?Is the price likely to go up?

Never: tend to recovering · is likely saving · It seems to the team is faster · I will come back to you

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each moment of the Q&A.

Someone asks: "Is robotic surgery always better?" You say:
A "Yes, it is always better for every patient in every hospital. There is no doubt about it."
B "I would say that it tends to be better for complex cases."
C "It tends to being better."
You do not know the exact number. You say:
A "Off the top of my head, about forty, but let me check and get back to you."
B "It is exactly forty-three." (You are not sure.)
C "I will come back to you."
The question is about a topic outside your talk. You say:
A "That is a stupid question."
B "I do not want to answer."
C "That is a fair question, but it is beyond the scope of my talk."
