Lesson 23 · Q&A Under Pressure

Any Questions?

The pitch is over, and a hand goes up. Today you learn to answer tough questions calmly and honestly — with careful language, and without bluffing.

Warm-up

From the Case to the Questions

First: tell Mr. Ribeiro's case in three sentences. Then: what is the most difficult question someone asked you at work? What did you say?

The Target

Three Moves in the Hot Seat

1. Welcome the question and take a breath.

2. Answer carefully: say what you know, and how sure you are.

3. If you do not know, do not invent: promise to find out.

Diagnostic

A Hand Goes Up

A hospital director asks you: "Is robotic surgery really better for every patient? It is very expensive." Answer now.

Do not worry about the grammar yet. Just answer.

Chapter 3: The Code

Sure or Careful?

Hedging language

Grammar Discovery

Words That Soften

"I would say that it is better for complex cases."

"It seems that trained teams have fewer complications."

"Robotic patients tend to go home earlier."

"The program is likely to pay for itself in two years."

Is the speaker 100% sure? What comes after the orange words? And after the blue ones?

HedgeThenExample
I would say that ...full sentenceI would say that it is worth it.
It seems that ...full sentenceIt seems that the cost goes down.
tend to / seem tobase verbPatients tend to recover faster.
be likely to / be unlikely tobase verbIt is likely to save money.
The evidence suggests ...noun or that + sentenceThe evidence suggests that training helps.

Hedging shows you are careful and honest, not weak. After tend to, seem to and be likely to: base verb.

Grammar Practice

Make It Careful

Say it first, then click to check

"Trained teams tend to ___finish surgeries faster." (finish)
"The program is likely to ___save money in the long run." (save)
"It ___seems that the results are better in complex cases." (seem)
"I would ___say that the training is worth the cost." (say)
"Older patients seem to ___need more time to recover." (need)
"A second robot is ___unlikely to arrive this year." (not probable)
Build

From Sure to Careful

Make each sentence careful, then click to compare

1. "Robotic surgery is always better."
"I would say that robotic surgery tends to be better for complex cases."
2. "The program will pay for itself."
"The program is likely to pay for itself in about two years."
3. "Training stops all complications."
"It seems that training reduces complications. The evidence suggests it."
4. "It was exactly forty-two nurses."
"Off the top of my head, it was about forty, but let me check and get back to you."
Chapter 4: The Questions

After the Panel

One skeptical director, one voice message, one answer

Dialogue

A Skeptical Director

P
Thank you, Ms. Heins. I'm a bit skeptical. Is robotic surgery really better for every patient?
A
That's a fair question. Not for every patient. I would say that it tends to be better for complex cases.
P
And the training program? It sounds very expensive. What's the real cost per nurse?
A
Off the top of my head, about five thousand dollars, but I don't want to bluff. Let me check and get back to you.
P
I appreciate that. Do you have any evidence that it saves money?
A
It seems that trained teams have shorter surgeries, so the program is likely to pay for itself in about two years.
P
Interesting. One last thing: how does this affect our insurance contracts?
A
I'm afraid that's beyond the scope of my talk, but our finance director can answer it. I'll connect you by email.
