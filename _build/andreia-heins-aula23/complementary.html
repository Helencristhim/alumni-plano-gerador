Lesson 23 -- Q&A Under Pressure

Talk
Celeste Headlee -- "10 ways to have a better conversation" (TED)

A radio host who interviews people for a living shares ten rules for real conversations. One of them is exactly today's lesson: if you do not know, say that you do not know.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Choose the two rules that help you most in a Q&A and say them in your own words.

Talk
Julia Galef -- "Why you think you're right -- even if you're wrong" (TED)

A short talk about looking at the evidence honestly instead of defending your side. Perfect for answering a skeptical audience without getting defensive.

Tip: write down two careful sentences she uses, then make your own about robotic surgery with "it seems that" or "I would say that".

Watch on TED →
Talk
Kathryn Schulz -- "On being wrong" (TED)

A writer explains why we feel so sure, and why being certain can be a trap. It helps you see hedging as honesty, not weakness.

Tip: after watching, record one minute answering this question with at least three hedges: "Is robotic surgery the future of every hospital?"
