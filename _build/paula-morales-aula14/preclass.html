Lesson 14 -- Pre-class

What Did You Do? -- The Past Simple and the Story of Your Career

In lesson 13 you talked about the future. Now you do the opposite: you talk about the past — and the past is your career, from Gallagher to Zurich. For twelve lessons your story happened to you. Today you tell it — and in the English past you are at the start of every sentence: I began, I got the job, I became a specialist, I left, I went, I made the move. You take six irregular verbs (began, got, became, went, made, left) and the affirmative past simple: regular verbs with -ed, irregular verbs that change shape. This Pre-class is a first contact — come to class with the six verbs in your ear, ready to tell your story.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Six verbs in the past — and all of them are your career. Listen to each one and repeat it out loud. The sound traps of the day: bi-GAN · bi-KEIM · went (rounded lips on the W, never a V) · meid. All six are irregular: they change shape, they do not take -ed.

Began -- the past form of begin
"I began my career in insurance." Say bi-GAN, stress on the second syllable — the open A of cat.
Got -- the past form of get
"I got a job at Gallagher." Say gaht, one short dry syllable — an open O.
Became -- the past form of become
"I became a specialist in large risks." Say bi-KEIM — the ending rhymes with name.
Went -- the past form of go
"I went to Zurich last year." Say went — it starts with rounded lips on the W, never a V.
Made -- the past form of make
"I made a big decision." Say meid — the a becomes an ay sound, and the final e is silent.
Left -- the past form of leave
"I left Gallagher and went to Marsh." Say left — one dry syllable, with the ft stuck together at the end.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the story and answer the questions. The sentences in bold are today's past — notice that in each one I comes first, and the verb is already in its past form.

This is Paula's story, written by her. “Many years ago, I began my career at a small office in São Paulo. At first I was an assistant and I did not speak a word of English.”

“Then I got a job at Gallagher. I stayed there for years, I worked hard, and I became a specialist in large risks. One day, a director saw my work. She went to Marsh and took me with her — so I left Gallagher and I went to Marsh. I did not choose that; it happened.”

“For a long time, other people made the big decisions, and I only said yes. But last year Zurich called, and I made the move. That time, the decision was mine — and, for the first time, I am the one telling the story.”

B Because become is irregular — it changes shape (became), it does not take -ed.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- the past has two kinds of verb

GRAMMAR

The affirmative past in English is simple: the form is the SAME for I, you, she, we — it does not change with the person. The only thing to decide is: is the verb regular (it takes -ed) or irregular (it changes shape)?

TipoPresent → PastWhen you use it
Regular (+ed)work → workedtrabalhei / trabalhou
Regular (+ed)start → startedan irregular verb — it changes shape
Irregularbegin → began · get → gotcomecei · consegui
Irregularbecome → became · go → wentme tornei · fui
Irregularmake → made · leave → lefttwo more irregulars from today

The two mistakes that feel natural to make, and the fix for each one:

1) “I go to Gallagher in 2015.” — you used the bare verb for the past. When the sentence talks about something that is already finished, the verb needs its past form: “I went to Gallagher.”

2) “I becomed a specialist.” — you put -ed on an irregular verb. become does not take -ed; it changes shape: “I became a specialist.” The -ed is only for regular verbs (work → worked).

Why this matters: in lesson 13 you put yourself at the start of your future sentences. Today you do the same in the past — I began, I got, I became, I made — and that is where your career, which always happened to you, starts being told by you.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record your answer. Speak for 60 to 90 seconds, with no script. Do not redo it more than twice. This is the homework audio and your rehearsal for the Zurich interview — start building it here.

The Zurich panel will ask you one question: “Tell us about your career. What did you do?” Answer out loud, telling your story in the past — from the first office to Zurich. Use at least four of the six verbs (began, got, became, went, made, left) and at least two time markers (at first / then / after that / last year). The only rule of this recording is: start every sentence with I. It does not have to be perfect — it has to be yours.
