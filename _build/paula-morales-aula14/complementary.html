Lesson 14 -- What Did You Do?: The Past Simple and the Story of Your Career

YouTube
The Past Simple Tense — 6 Minute Grammar (BBC Learning English, ~6 min, free)

Six minutes from the BBC, with two presenters who speak slowly and on purpose — made for learners. It is the “official” explanation of the past your lesson gave you from the inside: they show the regular verbs (which take -ed) and the irregular ones (which change shape) — exactly the two kinds from your lesson. You need nothing beyond what you have already seen today; hearing the same rule in a calm British voice helps it settle.

Tip: watch it with English subtitles (the video's own CC). Do one thing only: every time they give an example in the past, pause and say a sentence of YOUR own in the same mould, about your career (“I began at a small office”, “I became a specialist”). By the end of the video you should have said about five sentences of your story out loud. Open channel, no sign-up.

Podcast / audio
How to Use Irregular Verbs — 6 Minute Vocabulary (BBC Learning English, ~6 min, free)

The BBC episode about the most annoying part of the past: the irregular verbs, the ones that change shape and have to be learned one by one. It is exactly the problem of the six verbs in your lesson (began, got, became, went, made, left). The BBC gives you the groups and the tricks to remember them — and since it is a podcast, you can listen with your eyes closed, in the coffee queue, and just train your ear.

Tip: listen twice. The first time, just listen. The second time, pause at each irregular verb and say the present → past pair out loud (go → went, make → made, become → became). Write the six verbs from your lesson on a piece of paper and check how many of them turn up in the episode. Open and free, no sign-up.

Video story
Learn Past Simple Through Story — My School Life (YouTube, for beginners, free)

It is not a grammar class: it is a short, simple story, told entirely in the past, with subtitles on screen. It is the same thing you did today — hearing a life told in I began, I went, I made — only now with the text on screen, so you READ and HEAR the same sentence at once. It is the fastest way for the past to stick in your ear, and it prepares your homework audio directly.

Tip: watch it once only and do one thing: every time a past verb turns up, repeat the whole sentence out loud along with it. Then close the video and tell YOUR story in the same mould — from the first office to Zurich — out loud. You do not have to understand every word; the goal is to get your mouth through the past without freezing. Free, no sign-up.
