Plans and Priorities -- Future HR Projects with the Italian Leaders

Presenting the priorities of the next quarter: what is already decided, what you decide on the call, and what you need approved. Key words: priority, budget, forecast, target, timeline, resource, scope, trade-off, quarter, approval. Structures: be going to for plans already made, will for decisions taken at the moment and for predictions, and the negative of both.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the definition. These are the words of any planning meeting.

Priority -- the thing that has to come first, before the others
"My first priority next quarter is the leadership programme."
Budget -- the money available for a plan or a team
"I am going to ask for a small budget in January."
Forecast -- a guess about what will happen, based on numbers
"My forecast is twelve new hires this quarter."
Target -- the result you want to reach, usually a number
"Our target is seventy-five per cent in the next survey."
Timeline -- the plan of when each step will happen
"Here is the timeline for the next six months."
Resource -- the people, money or time you need to do something
"I do not have the resources to do all three projects."
Scope -- everything a project will include, and everything it will not
"The scope of the pilot is four stores only."
Trade-off -- what you give up in order to get something else
"The trade-off is simple: speed or quality."
Quarter -- a period of three months in the business year
"We are going to close the titles this quarter."
Approval -- a yes from the person who decides
"I will start as soon as I get your approval."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Viviane has three priorities for the next quarter. The first one is retention in the stores, and the plan is ready: they are going to start with the store managers in April. The target is store turnover below twenty per cent by December. The second one is the leadership programme, and they are going to run two groups of twelve people. The third one is the new performance process, but the scope is small: only the head office. She is not going to touch the stores this quarter, because she does not have the resources. On the call with Milan, Andrea asks for the numbers before Friday. Viviane thinks for a second and says: "Fine, I will send them on Monday morning." Then she adds: "I think the rollout will take about six months, but I will need your approval before April."

1. Why does the text say "they are going to start with the store managers"?
A Because it is a plan she already made, before this conversation.
B Because she is deciding it right now, while she speaks.
C Because it is a prediction about the market.
2. Why does Viviane say "I will send them on Monday" and not "I am going to send"?
A Because Monday is far away.
B Because she is deciding it at that moment, during the call.
C Because will is more polite than going to.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "I will to send you the numbers."
B "I go to send you the numbers."
C "I will send you the numbers."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Going To and Will -- Two Futures, Two Jobs

GRAMMAR

One is the plan you arrived with. The other is the decision you take on the call.

FormUseExample
be going to + base formA plan you already made, before this conversation.We are going to run the survey in June.
will + base formA decision you take at this moment, in the conversation.Fine, I will send it today.
will for predictionsWith I think, probably, maybe.I think it will take six months.
negativeam/is/are not going to · will not (won't)We are not going to hire in January.
questionsAre you going to...? · Will you...?Are you going to present it? Will you send it today?
two trapsNever to after will. Never go to for going to.I will send it. We are going to start.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

Andrea asks about a plan you decided last week. You say:
A "We are going to start with the store managers in April."
B "We will start with the store managers in April."
C "We go to start with the store managers in April."
He asks for the numbers earlier. You decide right there. You say:
A "I am going to send them on Thursday."
B "I will send them on Thursday."
C "I will to send them on Thursday."
You want to say that the project is not happening this quarter:
A "We are not going to touch the stores this quarter."
B "We do not go to touch the stores this quarter."
C "We will not to touch the stores this quarter."
An interviewer asks where you see yourself in five years. The recruiter said the best answer is:
A "I will be a director and I will change the company."
B "I am going to keep working on culture, and I want to do it in an international company."
C "I do not know, it depends on the market."
You are giving an opinion about how long something takes. You say:
A "I think it will take about six months."
B "I think it is going to take, I am sure."
C "I think it takes six months in the future."

Series
The Office (U.S.) -- Season 2, Episode 1 (The Dundies)

A whole episode built on a plan that goes wrong. Everybody announces what they are going to do, and almost nothing happens the way they said. The English is slower than a real meeting, which makes it a good place to hear going to and will.

Tip: watch with English subtitles and write down three sentences with going to and three with will.

Podcast
TED Talks Daily -- any recent episode

One short talk a day, always about an idea and what will happen next. Ten to fifteen minutes is the right size for the habit you are building, and the speakers are chosen for being clear.

Tip: listen at 0.75x the first time. Pick one episode a week and listen to the same one twice.
