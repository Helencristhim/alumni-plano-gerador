Why This Matters

The Voice of an Official Invitation

"We will hold the meeting in Boston."

who is "we"? a person is speaking

"The meeting will be held in Boston."

the company is speaking, not a person

"Attendance must be confirmed by the tenth."

the rule matters, not who wrote it

Agendas, invitations and company notes are written this way everywhere. Reading them quickly is a skill you can learn in one hour.

Chapter 3: The Code

Who Did It? Nobody Says

The structure that runs every agenda in the world

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The meeting will be held in Boston in March."

"A full itinerary was sent with this invitation."

"Badges are collected on site, at the registration desk."

"The keynote is given by the Global Head of Commercial."

In three sentences you cannot see who does the action. In one you can. What two parts appear in every single one?

PartShapeExample
The formbe + past participleThe badge is collected.
Presentis / are + participleDelegates are expected from twenty-two markets.
Pastwas / were + participleThe itinerary was sent last week.
With a modalwill be / must be / should be + participleAttendance must be confirmed by the tenth.
The agentby + person, only when it mattersThe meeting is hosted by the Global Commercial team.
Why it existsThe action and the rule matter more than the person. It is the normal voice of invitations, agendas, minutes and company notes.
Three trapsIrregular participles: send → sent, hold → held, give → given (never sended). The verb be agrees with the subject: All delegates are expected. And by is who does it, for is who it is for.

The Structure

Three Sentences, One Reply

Every international invitation is answered the same way. Build it once and reuse it for the rest of your career.

1 · Confirm

"Thank you for the invitation. I am writing to confirm my attendance on both days."

2 · The one question that matters

"Could you confirm which breakout I am expected to take part in?"

3 · Offer something

"I would be happy to present the results from the South and Southeast of Brazil."

Confirm → ask → offer. Never a reply that only says yes.

Comprehension

About Priya

1. Why is she calling him today?
She is closing the delegate list for Boston tomorrow.
2. Why does she explain the itinerary out loud?
Because it was sent with the invitation but people never open it.
3. Which two practical things does she insist on?
Badges are collected on site from seven thirty, and a shuttle is provided from the hotel every thirty minutes.
4. What does she say about how most managers reply?
They reply with one word, and then they are surprised when nobody knows who they are.
Listening

The Agenda Is Not the Meeting

Twelve years of these events, in three minutes. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Where does he say the conversations that change something happen?
In the twenty minutes between the sessions, not inside them.
2. What does he tell people to do before they fly?
Read the delegate list, choose three people, and find out one real thing about their market.
3. What is his advice about lunch?
Do not have lunch with your own country team every day — you already know them. Sit somewhere else.
4. What does he say about English at these meetings?
Nobody speaks perfect English and nobody cares. What people remember is whether you had something to say about your own market.
Listening 2

The Three Things Delegates Always Forget

Four meetings a year, the same three mistakes. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Why does the confirmation date matter so much?
Rooms are booked from that number, and the airport transfers are arranged from it too.
2. What happens if you confirm late?
You will still come, but you may be put in a hotel twenty minutes away from everybody else.
3. What goes wrong with the badge?
People arrive at nine, walk straight to the first session, and are stopped at the door — the badge is collected on site, on the first morning.
4. What do delegates get wrong about the breakouts?
They read the keynote and the plenary sessions carefully and ignore the breakouts, which is exactly where they are expected to speak.

Your Reply

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "The agenda was sended last week."
"The agenda was sent last week." (send is irregular: send, sent, sent)
2. "All delegates is expected to arrive on Sunday."
"All delegates are expected to arrive on Sunday." (the verb be agrees with the subject)
3. "The keynote will be present by the global head."
"The keynote will be presented by the global head." (the passive needs the past participle, not the infinitive)
4. "The hotel was booked for the company, so we did not pay."
"The hotel was booked by the company, so we did not pay." (by = who does it, for = who it is for)
