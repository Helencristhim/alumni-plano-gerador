Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the definition. Every one of these appears in an international invitation, every time.

Itinerary -- the full plan of a trip, with times, places and what happens when
"A full itinerary was sent with the invitation."
Venue -- the building or place where an event is held
"The venue is the conference centre on the harbour."
Delegate -- a person who is sent to a meeting to represent a company or a country
"Around ninety delegates are expected from twenty-two markets."
Session -- one part of an event, with its own subject and its own time
"Day one is organised into plenary sessions."
Keynote -- the main talk that opens an event and sets the direction
"The first morning is opened by a keynote from the Global Head."
Breakout -- a small group meeting inside a larger event
"Day two is given to breakouts, where each region presents."
To attend -- to go to a meeting or an event and be present at it
"All country commercial managers are invited to attend."
Host -- the person or team that organises an event and receives the guests
"The meeting is hosted by the Global Commercial team."
Badge -- the card with your name on it that you wear to get into the rooms
"Badges are collected at the registration desk."
On site -- at the place where the event happens, not before and not remotely
"Badges are collected on site, on the first morning."
To confirm attendance -- to tell the organiser officially that you will be there
"Attendance must be confirmed by the tenth of February."
Shuttle -- a bus or car that takes people between two fixed points, again and again
"A shuttle is provided between the hotel and the venue."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the invitation and answer the questions. Notice how rarely the document says who does the action.

The Global Commercial Meeting will be held in Boston in March. All country managers are invited to attend, and around ninety delegates are expected from twenty-two markets. The meeting is hosted by the Global Commercial team, and the venue is the conference centre on the harbour.

A full itinerary was sent with the invitation last week. Attendance must be confirmed by the tenth of February, because rooms and airport transfers are booked from the confirmed list. A shuttle is provided between the hotel and the venue every thirty minutes. Badges are collected on site, at the registration desk, and they are needed for every room in the building.

The first morning is opened by a keynote from the Global Head of Commercial. Day one is organised into plenary sessions, and day two is given to breakouts. Managers who are invited to a breakout are informed by the end of January. One slide should be sent in advance; nothing is presented from a laptop.

1. "The meeting will be held in Boston." Why does the text not say who holds it?
A Because the action and the arrangement matter, not the person — and that is the normal voice of an official invitation.
B Because the writer forgot to finish the sentence.
C Because nobody knows yet where the meeting will be.
2. What two parts does every passive sentence in this text share?
A A modal verb and a date.
B A form of the verb "be" and a past participle.
C The word "by" and the name of a person.
3. "The meeting is hosted by the Global Commercial team." Why does this sentence name the agent?
A Because here it matters who does it — and "by" is how you add that information when it is useful.
B Because the passive always needs "by" to be correct.
C Because the sentence is in the past.
4. "Attendance must be confirmed by the tenth of February." What is "by the tenth" here?
A The person who confirms.
B A deadline — the same word "by" also means "not later than", and only the context tells you which one it is.
C The place where you confirm.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive Voice

GRAMMAR

One question decides it: does it matter who does the action? If it does not, the passive is the natural choice.

PartShapeExample
The formbe + past participleThe badge is collected.
Presentis / are + participleDelegates are expected from twenty-two markets.
Pastwas / were + participleThe itinerary was sent last week.
With a modalwill be / must be / should be + participleAttendance must be confirmed by the tenth.
The agentby + person, only when it mattersThe keynote is given by the Global Head.
Question and negativeIs it ...? · is not / was not + participleIs a shuttle provided? — No, lunch is not included.
Three trapsIrregular participles: send → sent, hold → held, give → given (never sended). Be agrees with the subject: All delegates are expected. And by is who does it, for is who it is for.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

The invitation arrives and you are going. Your first sentence is:
A "Ok."
B "Thank you for the invitation. I am writing to confirm my attendance on both days."
C "I will try to go, I need to see my agenda first, maybe next week I answer."
You want to say that the company organises the meeting. You say:
A "The meeting is hosted by the global team."
B "The meeting is hosted for the global team."
C "The meeting is host by the global team."
The agenda went out last week and you want to say so. You say:
A "The agenda was sended last week."
B "The agenda was sent last week."
C "The agenda is send last week."
Ninety people are coming from twenty-two countries. You say:
A "Around ninety delegates is expected."
B "Around ninety delegates are expected."
C "Around ninety delegates are expect."
You arrive on the first morning and go straight to the auditorium. What is the problem?
A Badges are collected on site, at the registration desk, and they are needed for every room.
B The auditorium is only used on the second day.
C There is no problem at all.
Your name is printed next to a breakout on day two. This means:
A You are in the audience and you only have to listen.
B You are expected to speak in a small group, so you prepare for it.
C The session was cancelled and your name stayed there.

Documentary
Downfall: The Case Against Boeing

An investigation into a global company where the meetings, the reports and the official statements decide everything. It is the register of this lesson in its natural habitat: nobody ever says who did it. Listen for how often a spokesperson says "it was decided" and "mistakes were made" instead of naming a person.

Tip: watch with English subtitles and keep a note open. Every time you hear a passive sentence in an official statement, write it down. You will finish with fifteen, and you will never miss one in an email again.

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart -- What Is Normal? How Culture Affects Communication Styles

A free episode from the Stanford business school on what changes when the room is not from your country: how directly people say no, how much silence is normal, who speaks first. This is the preparation nobody does before a meeting abroad, and it is the difference between attending and taking part.

Tip: listen once without the transcript, then again with it. Afterwards, say out loud one thing you would do differently in a room of twenty-two nationalities.

YouTube
Pellegrino Riccardi -- Cross Cultural Communication (TEDxBergen)

Eighteen very funny minutes on what happens when people from different countries sit in the same room and each one is certain that their own way is the normal one. He is Italian, he lives in Norway, and he speaks slowly and clearly on purpose, which makes this one of the easiest talks on this list to follow.

Tip: watch it once with subtitles and once without. Then think of the two nationalities you would most likely sit next to in Boston, and say out loud one thing you would expect to be different.
