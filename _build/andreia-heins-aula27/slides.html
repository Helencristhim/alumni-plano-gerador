The Target

Three Things That Change

Time

start on time, or ten minutes later?

Rapport

two minutes of small talk, or ten?

Tone

direct and soft, or indirect and warm?

Neither style is better. A good professional can move between both.

Diagnostic

Tom Gets to the Point

You are on a video call. Tom, in Boston, starts: "We need the dates by Friday. March doesn't work." Lucia, in Mexico City, has not spoken yet, and the call is very early for her. What do you say?

Chapter 3: The Code

Past Words, Present Meaning

How to sound softer

Grammar Discovery

Past Form, Polite Meaning

Can we keep this call short? → I was hoping we could keep this call to forty minutes.

Can everyone hear me? → I wanted to check that everyone can hear me.

We will start in March. → We were thinking of starting the training in March.

Will you move the call? → Were you planning to move the call?

Click to reveal the rule
We use a past form to talk about now, because it creates distance and sounds softer and more polite. It is very common in American calls and emails. I was hoping (that) we could + verb = a polite request. I wanted to + verb (check / ask / mention) = a polite start. We were thinking of + verb-ing = an open proposal, not a final decision. Were you planning to + verb? = a soft question. After thinking of, use -ing; after hoping we could, wanted to and planning to, use the base verb.

The Call Invite

VIDEO CALL · ROBOTIC NURSING TRAINING

Organizer: Andreia Heins, Einstein Hospital

Boston9:00 a.m.Tom Becker, Program Lead
Mexico City8:00 a.m.Lucia Ramos, Nurse Educator
São Paulo11:00 a.m.Andreia Heins, Organizer

Agenda: 1. Training calendar for new nurses · 2. Simulator schedule

40 minutes · Please stay on mute when you are not speaking.

1. Who has the earliest time? Ask that person politely if the time is OK.
Lucia, in Mexico City, at 8:00. Lucia, I wanted to check if eight is OK for you.
2. How long is the call? Say it politely at the start.
Forty minutes. I was hoping we could finish in forty minutes.
3. Propose a new time for the next call as an open idea.
We were thinking of moving the next call to ten a.m. in Boston.

Dialogue

Tom Gets to the Point

T
Okay, let's get to the point. We need the training dates by Friday, and March doesn't work for us.
A
Thanks, Tom. I was hoping we could hear from Lucia first. It's her team, too.
T
Sure, sorry. I didn't mean to talk over anyone. Lucia, go ahead.
A
Lucia says April is better for Mexico City. So we were thinking of splitting the group: half in March, half in April.
T
That works for me. And to be honest, I don't want to come across as pushy.
A
You don't, don't worry. I wanted to mention one more thing: nine in Boston is very early for Lucia.
T
Good point. Were you planning to move the call?
A
Yes. I was hoping we could meet at ten your time from now on.
T
Perfect. Let's circle back to the simulator schedule next week.
A
Great. I'll send a summary today, and we can touch base on Monday.
Comprehension

About Tom

1. What does Tom need by Friday?
The training dates.
2. Why does Tom say sorry?
Because he did not let Lucia speak first: he talked over her.
3. What does Tom want to discuss next week?
The simulator schedule.
