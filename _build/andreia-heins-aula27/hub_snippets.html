Cross-Cultural Communication -- LATAM & the US

Run a video call with colleagues in Boston and Mexico City, and adapt your tone to two styles. Key words: tone, straightforward, blunt, rapport, come across as, time zone, unmute, talk over, circle back, touch base. Structure: past forms for polite distance -- I was hoping we could, I wanted to check, we were thinking of + -ing.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Tone -- the feeling in your voice or your words, like friendly, serious or angry
"Her email had a very friendly tone."
Straightforward -- honest and clear, saying what you mean without a long introduction
"My manager in Boston is very straightforward."
Blunt -- so direct that it can sound rude
"'No, that's wrong' can sound blunt in a meeting."
Rapport -- a friendly relationship where people understand each other well
"Good rapport makes difficult calls much easier."
Come across as -- to seem a certain way to other people
"On video, short answers can come across as cold."
Time zone -- one of the areas of the world that has its own time
"Our team works in three time zones."
Unmute -- to turn your microphone on again in a video call
"Don't forget to unmute before you speak."
Talk over -- to speak at the same time as another person, so they cannot finish
"On calls with a delay, people often talk over each other."
Circle back -- to come back to a topic later
"Can we circle back to the dates at the end?"
Touch base -- to contact someone briefly to share news or check progress
"I'll touch base with Lucia after lunch."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Andreia organizes a video call with Tom, in Boston, and Lucia, in Mexico City: three time zones. Tom is very straightforward. He wants to decide everything in five minutes. Lucia prefers to build rapport first, with a little small talk.

Andreia opens the call: "I was hoping we could keep this call to forty minutes. I wanted to check that everyone can hear me." When Tom starts to talk over Lucia, Andreia stays calm: "We were thinking of hearing from Lucia first." Notice: the verbs are in the past, but the ideas are about now. The past form makes the tone softer.

Tom does not want to come across as blunt, so he says sorry. At the end, they agree to circle back to the simulator schedule and to touch base on Monday.

1. What does Lucia prefer before talking about work?
A To decide everything quickly, in the first five minutes of the call.
B To build rapport with some small talk.
C To send all her ideas by email after the call.
2. "I wanted to check that everyone can hear me." When is Andreia checking?
A Yesterday, before she sent the invitation to the team.
B Next week, when they touch base again on Monday.
C Now, at the start of the call.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "We were thinking of hearing from Lucia first."
B "We were thinking to hear from Lucia first."
C "We were think of hearing from Lucia first."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Forms for Polite Distance

GRAMMAR

Past form, present meaning: the past makes a request or a proposal softer.

DirectSofterStructure
Can we finish at ten?I was hoping we could finish at ten.was hoping (that) we could + verb
Is the room ready?I wanted to check if the room is ready.wanted to + verb
We will start in March.We were thinking of starting in March.were thinking of + verb-ing
Will you join on Monday?Were you planning to join on Monday?were you planning to + verb?

Remember: the idea is about now or the future. The past form only makes your tone softer.
