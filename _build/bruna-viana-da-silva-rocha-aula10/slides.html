The Wall

Six Frames, No Captions

Say the caption out loud. One sentence each, and every one needs a clause hanging off it.

ROOM 2 — PLACES PEOPLE KEPT
six photographs · captions written by the visitor · please speak, do not write
1.a laundromat, 1962, the same sign it opened with
2.a fire escape with two chairs and a plant on it
3.a square at midnight, full, nobody photographing anything
4.a woman outside a shop, forty years on the same corner
5.a road with a mountain at the end and no cars on it
6.a kitchen window, at night, with the light on
Every visitor writes six captions and nobody writes the same six. The curator says the fourth one is the one people argue about, because it is the only frame in the room that is not a place.

The Core · Round 1

Nine Minutes, On Foot

I have never been where you are taking me and I have nine minutes. You choose the place, the hour and the route. I will keep asking what I am looking at.

  • Block 1: the arrival — where we start, what I see first, why this hour.
  • Block 2: the one thing every guidebook misses, and why it matters.

Closing · End of the Block

One Minute, No Notes

Somebody who has never left their own country asks you which place they should go to first. One minute, one place, and tell them why that one.

I can describe a place precisely enough that somebody who has never been can see it.
I hang the extra information off the noun instead of starting a second sentence — and the pronoun never comes back twice.
