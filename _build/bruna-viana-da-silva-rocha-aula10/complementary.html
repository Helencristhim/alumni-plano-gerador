Series
Street Food: USA — Netflix, any episode

Each episode is one American city told through the people who cook in it: New York, Portland, Miami, Oahu. Nobody in it describes a landmark. They describe the block, the corner, the woman who has been there thirty years — which is exactly the sentence shape this lesson is built on.

Tip: pick one episode and write down six sentences that describe a place through a person or a detail instead of a monument. Then write three about a place of yours, in the same shape.

Podcast
NPR Life Kit — "Making the most of travel and your time off"

How to look at a place, including the one you live in, the way you look at somewhere you flew eleven hours to see. Walking with no destination, noticing outside the categories you normally use, and what changes when you stop collecting landmarks. Normal speed, transcript on the same page.

Tip: after listening, walk nine blocks in your own neighborhood and describe five things out loud in English, each one with a relative clause. Nobody has to hear you.

Video
Rachel's English — "Function Words: Intonation and Word Stress"

Which words in a long sentence carry the weight and which ones get flattened almost to nothing. This is what keeps a sentence with two clauses in it from collapsing into a wall of syllables — and it is the difference between being understood and being asked to repeat.

Tip: after the video, say "the city that I have always wanted to live in is Austin" and mark out loud which three words you are actually landing on.
