Listening 1 · A Coach

The Learners I Worry About Least

A language coach on why advanced learners stop improving. Sound first — no text.

1. Which learners does she worry about least, and why?
The ones who say they have stopped improving, because they are paying attention. The plateau is visible to them.
2. What does she say an advanced learner does when he is under pressure?
He falls back on the three or four structures he trusts, and the other twenty never leave the page.
3. What does she ask her learners to do, and what is the cost?
To use one structure they are not sure of in every conversation. The cost is a few mistakes, and that is the price of leaving the comfort zone.
Listening 2 · A Partner

Five Minutes, Once a Month

A lawyer who is not a native speaker, on how he measures his own English. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he record, and how often?
Five minutes of himself speaking about his week, once a month, always on the same topic.
2. Why does he say you should never listen to the new recording on the same day?
Because on the same day you hear what you meant to say. A week later you hear what you actually said.
3. What improved first, and what followed?
Fluency came first: fewer pauses and fewer restarts. Accuracy followed about six months later.
