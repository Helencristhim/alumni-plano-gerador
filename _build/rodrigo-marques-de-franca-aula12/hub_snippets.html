Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To take stock -- to stop and think carefully about a situation before deciding what to do next
"Before the second block starts, let us take stock of the first."
A plateau -- a stage where progress stops for a while, after a period of improvement
"Most advanced learners hit a plateau, and most of them mistake it for a limit."
Fluency -- the ability to speak smoothly, without stopping to build each sentence
"His fluency in meetings is ahead of his fluency at dinner."
Accuracy -- the ability to use the language without mistakes
"In a contract, accuracy matters more than speed."
A benchmark -- a fixed point you measure later progress against
"The two minutes you recorded in lesson one are your benchmark."
Rusty -- weaker than before, because you have not used it for a while
"After a year without classes, my speaking was rusty, but my reading was not."
To fall back on -- to use something familiar and safe, when the new option feels risky
"When I am tired, I fall back on the same three structures."
Second nature -- something you do so often that you no longer think about it
"Reading a contract in English is second nature. Talking about it is not, yet."
To fine-tune -- to make small changes to something that already works, to make it better
"The structure is there. Now we fine-tune the rhythm."
A comfort zone -- the situations where you feel safe and do not have to try hard
"Listening is my comfort zone. Speaking at speed is not."
To stretch yourself -- to do something harder than usual, so that you grow
"Use a structure you are not sure of. That is how you stretch yourself."
A milestone -- an important point in a process that shows how far you have come
"The end of the first block is a milestone, not a finish line."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Six weeks ago Rodrigo's speaking was rusty. He had not had a class for a year, and although his reading had never stopped, his English came out in short, safe sentences. Today he takes stock. His fluency has improved more than his accuracy, which is exactly what the research predicts.

He knows what he falls back on. When a call gets difficult, the past simple does everything, and the conditional, which he studied in lesson 7, stays on the page. His teacher told him that this was normal, and asked him whether he would try one new structure in every conversation. It is a small risk, isn't it? But it is the only way to stretch yourself off a plateau.

The five-minute recording he makes today is his benchmark. If he listened to it the same day, he would hear what he meant to say; a week later, he will hear what he actually said. Reading contracts is second nature. Speaking at a dinner table is still outside his comfort zone. The end of the first block is a milestone, and the next one is about fine-tuning.

1. "He had not had a class for a year." Why the past perfect here?
A Because the year without classes happened before the moment six weeks ago that the text describes.
B Because the sentence is negative.
C Because it is reported speech.
2. "His teacher told him that this was normal." Which tool from the block is this?
A A question tag.
B Reported speech: told him that, with the tense stepping back.
C A second conditional.
3. "If he listened to it the same day, he would hear what he meant to say." Why listened and would hear?
A Because the recording happened in the past.
B Because it is a first conditional about a real plan.
C Because it is a second conditional: an imagined situation he is advised not to create.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Block 1 in One Table

GRAMMAR

Eleven lessons, eleven tools. For each one: the job it does, and one sentence you can use tomorrow.

JobFormExample
1. Present perfect vs past simpleopen period · closed dateI have worked here since 2005. I started in May.
2. Relative clauseswho / which / that · commas add, no commas identifyThe firm, which ran the trial, published it.
3. Indirect questionsnormal word order after the openerCould you tell me when they sent it?
4. Modals of obligationmust · may · may not · need notNeither party may assign it.
5. Comparatives and superlatives-er than · more ... than · the mostFlorence is quieter than Rome.
6. Passive and impersonal reportbe + participle · it is said thatThe play was panned by the critics.
7. First and second conditionalsif + present, will · if + past, wouldIf you moved on price, we would move too.
8. Concession and contrastalthough · despite · whereasAlthough it is elegant, it is wrong.
9. Reported speechthe tense steps back · tell somebodyShe told me they could not accept it.
10. Narrative tenseshad + participle · was + -ingWhen I arrived, they had already signed.
11. Question tagscopy the auxiliary, flip the signYou have met Paulo, haven't you?

Never: I have signed it last week · could you tell me when did they send it · she said me · isn't it? after every sentence · if they would sign, we would close

Quiz

A "My English is good. I understand everything."

C "I have improved my English last month."

A "Could you tell me when is the hearing?"
