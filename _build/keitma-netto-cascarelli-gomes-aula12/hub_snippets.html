Lesson 12 -- Pre-class

The Update That Says What You Decided

A closing update is a list of decisions, and they live in the verbs. Key words: to avoid, to manage to, to consider, to recommend, to risk, to refuse, thorough, to put off. Structure: verbs followed by to or by -ing.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To avoid -- to make sure you do not do something, or that it does not happen
"I avoided posting anything until the numbers were confirmed."
To manage to -- to succeed in doing something difficult
"I managed to close on time, even with two entries late."
To consider -- to think seriously about doing something before you decide
"We considered posting it manually, but the audit trail was weak."
To recommend -- to say that something is the best thing to do
"I recommend reviewing the entries once more before the call."
To risk -- to do something even though it may cause a bad result
"If we post it today, we risk reopening the period in March."
To refuse -- to say clearly that you will not do something
"I refused to post the adjustment without support."
Thorough -- complete and careful, with nothing left unchecked
"The auditors asked for a thorough review of the accruals."
To put off -- to move something to a later time, often because you do not want to do it
"I kept putting off the conversation with the BPO."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Two updates about the same late entries. The first: The BPO sent two entries late. The tax file was late too. It was a difficult week. All true, and the director still has to ask what you did. The second: I decided to post one entry manually, I refused to post the other without the invoice, and I avoided reopening the period. Same week, but now every sentence is a decision.

Look at the verb that comes after the decision verb. It does not choose its own form: the first verb chooses it. Some verbs take to + base verb: decide to, agree to, plan to, refuse to, manage to, expect to. Others take -ing: avoid, consider, recommend, risk, keep, finish. So it is I recommend reviewing the entries, never recommend to review, and I managed to close on time, never managed closing.

A few verbs take both, but the meaning changes. I stopped to check one figure means you paused in order to check it. I stopped checking the figures means you do not check them any more — a very different message for a director. Phrasal verbs behave like the -ing family: I kept putting off the conversation.

1. What is the difference between the two updates in the text?
A The second one describes a different week, with fewer problems and no late entries from the BPO.
B In the second one, every sentence is a decision.
C The first one is more polite.
2. According to the text, what chooses the form of the second verb?
A The first verb.
B The tense of the sentence, because past sentences always take the -ing form after the first verb.
C The speaker's preference.
3. What does "I stopped checking the figures" mean?
A I paused for a moment in order to check one figure, and then I continued with my work.
B I checked the figures twice.
C I do not check the figures any more.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- To or -ing?

GRAMMAR

The first verb chooses. Learn each verb with its partner.

FamilyVerbsExample
verb + to + base verbdecide, agree, plan, expect, offer, refuse, manage, need, hope, prepareI decided to escalate it.
verb + -ingavoid, consider, finish, keep, suggest, recommend, risk, mind, enjoy, practiceI recommend reviewing the entries.
phrasal verb + -ingput off, keep on, give upI kept putting off the call.
stop: two meaningsstop to = pause in order to · stop -ing = no longer do itI stopped to check one figure.
remember: two meaningsremember -ing = a memory · remember to = do not forget the taskRemember to send the entries.
the one that travels with itdiscuss takes no prepositionWe discussed the delay, not discussed about it.

Never: I decided escalating it · I recommend to review · I managed addressing it · we discussed about the delay

Stage 2: Put the Update in Order

Order

You are sending a closing update to your director after a delay from the BPO. Put the update in order.

?"I decided to post one of them manually, with full support attached."
?"I plan to discuss it with them on Monday."
?"I refused to post the second one without the invoice, so it moves to next month."
?"I recommend agreeing a cut-off with the BPO for day two."
