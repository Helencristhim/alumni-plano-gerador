Lesson 05 -- Pre-class

The Trade Mission -- Telling a Work Story

Many meetings start with a story: the trip, the fair, the person you met. Key words: a trade mission, a delegation, a trade fair, a booth, to pitch, a lead, a keynote, a venue, jet lag, to network. Structure: past simple vs past continuous.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A trade mission -- a trip that a group of companies makes to another country to find business
"The chamber organized a trade mission to Toronto last year."
A delegation -- a group of people who travel or act for an organization
"A delegation of ten Canadian companies visited Sao Paulo."
A trade fair -- a large event where companies show their products and meet buyers
"We met three new partners at the trade fair."
A booth -- a small area at a fair where a company shows its products
"Our booth was near the main entrance."
To pitch -- to present an idea or a product to convince someone
"She pitched the program to five companies in one afternoon."
A lead -- a person or company that may become a client or a partner
"We came back from the fair with twenty good leads."
A keynote -- the main talk at a conference or an event
"The minister gave the keynote on the first day."
A venue -- the place where an event or a meeting happens
"The venue was a hotel in the center of the city."
Jet lag -- the tired feeling after a long flight across time zones
"I had terrible jet lag on the first day."
To network -- to meet people who can help you in your work
"The dinner was a great chance to network."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last year Daniel went to a trade fair in Sao Paulo. On the first morning, it was raining, and hundreds of people were waiting at the entrance. Daniel was setting up the screen at his booth when a buyer from a big supermarket chain stopped to talk.

He pitched his program in ten minutes, and she asked for a meeting the next day. That buyer is now his best lead.

Notice the pattern. The scene of the story uses was or were plus -ing: it was raining, people were waiting. The actions that move the story use the past simple: stopped, pitched, asked.

1. What was Daniel doing when the buyer stopped?
A He was setting up the screen.
B He was pitching to a minister.
C He was waiting at the entrance.
2. Why does the text say “people were waiting” and not “people waited”?
A Because it describes the scene, an action in progress.
B Because it is a plan for the future.
C Because it happened every day.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “I talked to a buyer when the director was arriving.”
B “I was talking to a buyer when the director arrived.”
C “I was talk to a buyer when the director arrived.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Simple vs Past Continuous

GRAMMAR

Use was / were + verb-ing for the scene, the background of a story. Use the past simple for the main actions.

JobFormExample
The background, the scenewas / were + verb-ingIt was raining. People were waiting.
The main actions, in orderpast simpleWe set up the booth and opened the doors.
An action interrupts the scenewas + -ing ... when + past simpleI was talking to a buyer when the director arrived.
Two things at the same timewhile + was / were + -ingWhile she was pitching, the sound stopped.
QuestionsWhat were you doing? / What happened?What were you doing when he called?
The trapstate verbs stay simpleNot I was knowing him. Say I knew him.

Never: I talked when the director was arriving · While she pitched, the sound was stopping · I was knowing the buyer · What you were doing?

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A partner asks how the trade fair was. You describe the scene:
A "When we arrived, a lot of people were waiting at the entrance."
B "When we arrived, a lot of people waited at the entrance since hours."
C "When we were arriving, a lot of people waited."
You want to say that a call interrupted your presentation. You say:
A "I presented when the phone was ringing."
B "I was presenting when the phone rang."
C "I was present when the phone rang."
You want to ask about the moment of a problem. You say:
A "What were you doing when the projector stopped?"
B "What you were doing when the projector stopped?"
C "What did you doing when the projector stopped?"
You want to start a conversation at a conference. You say:
A "What brought you to this event?"
B "Why you are here?"
C "What is your job title?"
