Lesson 5 · A Work Story in Two Tenses

The Trade Mission

Before the business starts, people tell stories: the trip, the fair, the person they met by chance. This lesson gives you the words and the two tenses of a good work story.

Chapter 1: First Words

Before the Business, a Story

You can present yourself, plan a meeting, take the floor and give an update. But many international meetings start with a story: how was your trip? How was the fair? That small talk builds the relationship.

Think of a work event you went to: a fair, a conference, a visit. Where was it, and what happened there?

Today’s Goal

Three Missions

1

Ten words for business trips and events.

2

Separate the background of a story from its main actions.

3

Tell a two-minute work story that people want to hear.

The target of the day: I was talking to a buyer from Toronto is the background, the scene; when the director arrived is the action that interrupts it. English uses two past tenses to build the scene and move the story.

One Question

The Scene, or the Action?

Two sentences, out loud, right now.

Describe what people were doing at a work event when you arrived. Then say one thing that happened. Do not think about grammar yet.

Chapter 3: The Code

The Scene and the Action

Two past tenses for one good story

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Show?

"I was talking to a buyer when the director arrived."

"It was raining, and hundreds of people were waiting at the entrance."

"We set up the booth, opened the doors and started the first meeting."

"While she was pitching the program, the sound stopped."

The verbs in red are the scene: actions in progress, the background. The verbs in blue are the story: short, complete actions, one after the other. Look where when and while go.

JobFormExample
The background, the scenewas / were + verb-ingIt was raining. People were waiting.
The main actions, in orderpast simpleWe set up the booth and opened the doors.
An action interrupts the scenewas + -ing ... when + past simpleI was talking to a buyer when the director arrived.
Two things at the same timewhile + was / were + -ingWhile she was pitching, the sound stopped.
QuestionsWhat were you doing? / What happened?What were you doing when he called?
The trapstate verbs stay simpleNot I was knowing him. Say I knew him.

In one line: was / were + -ing paints the scene; the past simple moves the story.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Story, at Real Speed

One conversation, three recordings, one program

Dialogue

How Was the Fair?

J
Daniel, welcome back! How was the trade fair in Sao Paulo?
D
Busy, but very good. We had a booth near the entrance, so hundreds of people walked past us every day.
J
That sounds great. Did you meet anyone interesting?
D
Yes. On the second day, I was setting up the screen when a buyer from a big supermarket chain stopped at the booth.
J
Really? What did you do?
D
I pitched our food program right there, in ten minutes. She loved it. Now she is one of our best leads.
J
Fantastic. And how was the trip? Did you have jet lag?
D
Not really, the time difference is small. But on the last night I was so tired that I fell asleep during the dinner!
Comprehension

About Daniel

1. Where was Daniel’s booth, and why was that good?
Near the entrance, so hundreds of people walked past every day.
2. What was Daniel doing when the buyer stopped at the booth?
He was setting up the screen.
3. What happened on the last night?
He was so tired that he fell asleep during the dinner.
Listening 1 · The Organizer

The Trade Mission to Toronto

An event organizer tells the story of a trade mission. Sound first — no text.

1. How many companies were in the delegation?
Twelve Brazilian companies.
2. What was happening when the group arrived at the airport?
It was snowing, and the flights were late.
3. What was the result of the mission?
The companies came back with more than forty leads.
Listening 2 · The Exporter

The Pitch That Almost Failed

A Brazilian exporter remembers a difficult presentation. Sound first — no text.

1. What was he doing when the problem started?
He was pitching his products to a group of buyers.
2. What was the problem?
The projector stopped working.
3. What did he do?
He continued without slides and told the story of his company.
Listening 3 · The Keynote

Small Talk at a Conference

A short part of a conference recording about networking. Sound first — no text.

1. According to the speaker, when do most partnerships start?
During the coffee breaks and dinners, not during the talks.
2. What question does she recommend to start a conversation?
What brought you to this event?
3. Why does she recommend telling a short story?
Because people remember stories, not job titles.

The Program of the Trip

Day 1 — eveningNetworking dinner with Canadian partners

ResultMore than 40 leads
A program is written in short notes. When you tell the story of the trip, you turn each line into a sentence: the scene with was / were + -ing, and the actions with the past simple.
1. Turn the first line into a story sentence with was / were + -ing.
When we arrived, it was snowing, and the flights were late.
2. What happened on the second day?
The minister gave the keynote, and the companies pitched their products.
3. Tell the evening of day 1 in two sentences.
In the evening we had a networking dinner. People were talking and exchanging business cards all night.
