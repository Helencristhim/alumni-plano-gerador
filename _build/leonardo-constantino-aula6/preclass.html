Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

Passive or Active? -- Describing Regulatory Processes and Tax Reform Mechanisms at ALMA

Describing how a rule is born, applied and replaced, in the voice every regulatory bulletin uses. Key words: enact, enforce, impose, scrutinize, assess, withhold, disclose, overturn, supersede. Expressions: be subject to review, be held liable, be exempt from taxation, in the pipeline, set a precedent, phase out. Structures: present simple passive, past simple passive and present perfect passive, plus the decision to keep or drop the agent after "by".

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Enact -- to make a bill officially become law
"The new reporting framework was enacted in December."
Enforce -- to make sure a rule is obeyed
"The rule is enforced by the federal tax authority."
Impose -- to officially place a tax, a duty or a fine on someone
"Two obligations are imposed on financial institutions."
Scrutinize -- to examine something very carefully and officially
"Every cross-border payment is scrutinized before it is cleared."
Assess -- to officially calculate how much tax must be paid
"The amount owed is assessed on the date of filing."
Withhold -- to keep back part of a payment and send it to the government
"Fifteen percent is withheld at source on these payments."
Disclose -- to make information officially known
"Beneficial ownership must be disclosed for every large account."
Overturn -- to officially cancel an earlier decision
"A decision of the lower court was overturned on appeal."
Supersede -- to replace an older rule with a newer official one
"That requirement has been superseded by the new framework."
Be subject to review -- to be open to being officially examined again
"Every similar ruling is now subject to review."
Be held liable -- to be made legally responsible for paying something
"The group can be held liable for the full amount."
Be exempt from taxation -- to be legally free from paying tax on something
"Only pension funds are exempt from taxation on these flows."
In the pipeline -- being prepared now and coming soon
"Two further decrees are in the pipeline."
Set a precedent -- to create a decision that later cases will follow
"That judgment set a precedent for the whole sector."
Phase out -- to remove something gradually, in steps, over time
"The old credit will be phased out by 2027."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Leonardo opens the client bulletin with the framework itself. It was enacted in December, and the first stage is already in force. From January, quarterly returns are filed through a single federal portal, and the amount owed is assessed on the date of filing. The old state-by-state requirement has been superseded, and no transition period was granted. Two obligations are imposed on financial institutions for the first time: beneficial ownership must be disclosed for large accounts, and every cross-border payment is scrutinized before it is cleared. Fifteen percent is withheld at source, and the rule is enforced by the federal tax authority. Only pension funds are exempt from taxation on these flows. A group that files late can be held liable for the full amount. Two points are still open: the old credit has not been phased out yet, and last month a lower court decision was overturned on appeal. That judgment set a precedent, so every similar ruling is now subject to review. Two further decrees are in the pipeline, but nothing has been decided and no date has been announced.

B It can be held liable for the full amount.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive Voice

GRAMMAR

Every passive sentence carries two pieces: a form of be and a past participle. The tense of be is the only thing that changes.

FormUseExample
Present simple passive
am / is / are + past participle		A routine, a standing rule, the way things work now.Tax returns are filed quarterly.
Past simple passive
was / were + past participle		One finished event at a known moment in the past.The regulation was approved by Congress.
Present perfect passive
has / have been + past participle		A finished change with a result today, when the date does not matter.The framework has been revised three times.
NegativeThe negative sits on the form of be, never on the participle.The credit is not assessed at that rate. / The decree has not been published.
QuestionThe form of be or the auxiliary moves to the front.Is the payment scrutinized? / Has the credit been phased out?
The agentAdd by + agent only when the agent is news: "was approved by Congress". Drop it when it is obvious, unknown or simply not the point: "the deadline was moved".
RegisterDescribe the regulation in the passive, and the action your client must take in the active: "Your team files the return by the twentieth."
