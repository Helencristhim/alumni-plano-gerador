The Price Conversation -- If, Unless, Provided That

A price negotiation with a customer. Key words: a quote, a discount, a margin, volume, a price increase, to absorb a cost, a surcharge, payment terms, a long-term contract, to meet halfway, a deal-breaker, a commitment. Structure: first and second conditionals, unless, provided that, as long as.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A quote -- a written price that a company offers for goods or a service
"We sent them a quote for next year on Monday."
A discount -- a reduction in the usual price
"They asked for a five percent discount."
A margin -- the difference between the price and the cost, the money a company keeps
"With these steel prices, our margin is too small."
Volume -- the quantity of products that a customer buys
"If the volume goes up, the price can come down."
A price increase -- a change that makes the price higher
"Nobody likes a price increase, but costs went up."
To absorb a cost -- to pay for a higher cost yourself, without passing it to the customer
"We can absorb part of the cost, but not all of it."
A surcharge -- an extra amount added to the price for a specific reason
"We added a small steel surcharge for three months."
Payment terms -- the rules about when and how a customer must pay
"Our payment terms are sixty days."
A long-term contract -- an agreement that continues for several years
"They want a long-term contract with a fixed price."
To meet halfway -- to accept part of what the other side wants, so both sides agree
"Let’s meet halfway: three percent instead of six."
A deal-breaker -- a condition that would stop you from accepting an agreement
"A price freeze for five years is a deal-breaker for us."
A commitment -- a promise to do something, for example to buy a certain quantity
"We need a commitment of at least ten thousand parts."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Julius sent a quote to a German customer with a six percent price increase. The customer said, “If you keep this price, we will look for another supplier.” Julius explained that steel was more expensive and that his company could not absorb the cost alone.

Then the customer asked, “What if we doubled the volume?” Julius answered, “If you made a commitment like that, we would reduce the increase.” They decided to meet halfway at three percent, provided that the customer signed a long-term contract.

“Unless steel prices fall, we will keep a small surcharge,” Julius said. The customer agreed, as long as the payment terms were ninety days.

1. Why could Julius’s company not keep the old price?
A Because the German customer had asked for a lower volume and fewer parts for the next three years.
B Because steel was more expensive, and they could not absorb the cost alone.
C Because of the payment terms.
2. Why does the text say “If you made a commitment, we would reduce” and not “If you make, we will”?
A Because the commitment happened in the past, last year, before the meeting with the customer.
B Because it is a question.
C Because Julius is talking about a hypothetical situation that the customer suggested.
3. What condition did the customer set at the end?
A Ninety-day payment terms.
B A five percent discount on every part, and the end of the surcharge in the first month of the contract.
C A shorter contract.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Conditionals for Negotiating

GRAMMAR

Use if + present, will for a real offer. Use if + past, would for an imagined or unlikely situation. Unless means if... not. Provided that and as long as mean only if: they make the condition stronger.

UseFormExample
First conditional: a real offerif + present, will / can + verbIf you sign, we will keep the price.
Second conditional: imagined or unlikelyif + past, would / could + verbIf prices fell, we would cancel the surcharge.
Unlessif... notUnless costs come down, we will need an increase.
Provided that / as long asonly if: a strong conditionWe can do it, provided that you pay in thirty days.
Polite pressuresecond conditional for a softer requestWould you accept it if we offered sixty days?
The trapno will after if, unless or provided thatNot “If you will sign...” Say If you sign...

Never: If you will sign... · If prices fell, we will... · Unless you don’t sign... · Provided that you will pay...

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The customer asks for a discount. You agree, but only if they sign for three years. You say:
A We can offer a discount, provided that you will sign a contract with us for three years or more.
B We can offer a discount, provided that you sign for three years.
C We can offer a discount, unless you sign for three years.
You want to explain what will happen if costs do not come down. You say:
A Unless costs come down, we will need an increase.
B Unless costs don’t come down, we will need a price increase from the first of January.
C If costs will not come down, we need an increase.
You want to talk about a situation that you do not expect. You say:
A If steel prices will fall next year, we cancel the surcharge for all our customers.
B If steel prices fall, we would cancel.
C If steel prices fell, we would cancel the surcharge.
The customer wants six percent off. You can give three. You say:
A Let’s meet in the middle of the way, at three percent, and close the deal.
B Let’s meet halfway at three percent.
C Let’s absorb halfway.
