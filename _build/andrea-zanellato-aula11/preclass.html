Lesson 11 -- Pre-class

Love It or Hate It -- Likes and Dislikes

What you enjoy, what you don’t mind and what you can’t stand, at work and outside work. Key words: paperwork, small talk, overtime, a day off, to unwind, a pet peeve, to deal with, a people person. Structure: verb + -ing after enjoy, love, like, hate, don’t mind, can’t stand and avoid.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Paperwork -- the forms, reports and documents you have to fill in at work
"Nobody enjoys doing paperwork on Friday."
Small talk -- a short, polite conversation about easy topics, like the weather
"I don’t mind small talk before a meeting."
Overtime -- extra hours of work after the normal work day
"She worked three hours of overtime yesterday."
A day off -- a day when you do not go to work
"On my day off, I love reading in the garden."
To unwind -- to relax after a busy or difficult time
"I listen to music in the car to unwind."
A pet peeve -- a small thing that always annoys you
"My pet peeve is phones in meetings."
To deal with -- to take action about a problem, a task or a person
"I deal with difficult conversations every week."
A people person -- someone who enjoys being with other people
"A good recruiter is a people person."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Emma is an HR manager in Edinburgh. Every year she asks her people what they enjoy doing at work. Most people love working with the team. But nobody likes doing paperwork, and a lot of people hate filling in forms.

Oliver is a recruiter in London. He is a people person: he doesn’t mind small talk, and he enjoys meeting candidates. His pet peeve? Candidates who arrive late. He can’t stand waiting without a message.

On his day off, Oliver avoids checking his emails. He loves walking in the countryside. That is how he unwinds.

1. What does nobody like doing in Emma’s company?
A Working with the team on a new project in the office.
B Doing paperwork.
C Meeting new people from other offices.
2. What can’t Oliver stand?
A Waiting for candidates without a message.
B Small talk with candidates before the interview starts.
C Walking in the countryside.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “He avoids to check his emails.”
B “He avoids checking his emails.”
C “He avoid check his emails on his day off from work.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Verb + -ing for Likes and Dislikes

GRAMMAR

After enjoy, love, like, hate, don’t mind, can’t stand and avoid, use the next verb with -ing: I enjoy reading. Negative: I don’t enjoy driving. Question: Do you enjoy cooking?

JobFormExample
Likeslove / like / enjoy + verb-ingI enjoy reading physical books.
Dislikeshate / can’t stand + verb-ingI can’t stand waiting in traffic.
It is OK for medon’t mind + verb-ingI don’t mind working late.
To stay away fromavoid + verb-ingWe avoid having meetings on Friday.
Spellinghave → having · sit → sittingDrop the final -e, or double the last letter.
The trapno to after enjoy, mind and avoidNot I enjoy to read. Say I enjoy reading.

Never: I enjoy to read · I don’t mind to work late · He avoid checking · I can’t stand wait

Stage 2: Put the Small Talk in Order

Order

Put the short conversation before a meeting in a logical order.

?"Hi! Do you have plans for your day off tomorrow?"
?"Yes, I'm going to read in the garden. I love reading."
?"Nice! I enjoy reading too, but I prefer novels."
?"Me too. And how do you unwind after work?"
?"I listen to music in the car. I can't stand silence in traffic."
?"OK, it's time for the meeting. Let's talk later!"
