Chapter 3: The Code

Turn, Go, Don't

Instructions and places

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Have in Common?

"Go out of the hotel and turn right."

"Don't take a taxi."

"The museum is next to a park."

"It's across from a cafe."

Look at the orange words. How do the first two sentences start? What do the last two words tell you?

JobFormExample
Instructionverb first (no "you")Turn left. Go straight.
Negative instructionDon't + verbDon't take a taxi.
Very nearnext toIt's next to the park.
Other side of the streetacross fromIt's across from a cafe.
Where two streets meeton the corner (of)It's on the corner.
Polite startExcuse me + Where is...?Excuse me, where is the museum?

In one line: instructions start with the verb (turn, go, don't); places use next to, across from, on the corner.

Chapter 4: On the Street

On the Street

One conversation, two recordings, one note

Dialogue

Excuse Me, Where Is...?

C
Excuse me. Where is the car museum, please?
S
The car museum? It's not far. Go straight on this street.
C
Go straight. Okay. And then?
S
At the corner, there is a bike shop. Turn left there.
C
Turn left at the bike shop. Is the museum on that street?
S
Yes. It's next to a small park, across from a cafe.
C
Great. Can I walk, or is it far?
S
You can walk. It's ten minutes. But don't walk on the red lanes. They're for bikes!
C
Thank you very much. Have a nice day!
Comprehension

What Does Sanne Say?

1. Where does Sanne say to turn left?
At the corner, at the bike shop.
2. What is next to the museum, according to Sanne?
A small park.
3. What does Sanne say about the red lanes?
Don't walk on them. They are for bikes.
