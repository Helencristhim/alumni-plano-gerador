Chapter 4: The Code

In the Middle, or at the End

Where the pronoun goes in a phrasal verb

Grammar Discovery

Where Does It Go?

"I did not know the term, so I looked it up."

"The attending saw the mistake and pointed it out."

"I came across it in a textbook last year."

"There is a problem with my badge. Could you look into it?"

Look at the blue pronoun. In two sentences it splits the verb, in two it waits until the end. What is the difference between the verbs?

JobFormExample
Separablelook up, point out, put off, bring up, take onLook up the term / look the term up.
Separable + pronounthe pronoun MUST go in the middleLook it up. Not look up it.
Inseparablecome across, look into, deal withCome across it. Not come it across.
Three wordsfall behind on, catch up onI fell behind on my notes. I caught up on them.
No object at allshow up, get by, fall behind, catch upShe showed up at seven. I can get by.

In one line: with a separable phrasal verb the pronoun goes in the middle — look it up, point it out; with an inseparable one it goes at the end — look into it, deal with it.

Listening 1 · A Chief Resident

Three Things for the First Week

A chief resident welcoming the new interns. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say new residents should do when they come across something they have never seen?
Look it up, and if they cannot find it, bring it up at the afternoon huddle. The worst thing is to nod and say nothing.
2. Why does she say catching up at the weekend does not work?
Because every year the person who says that is the one who falls behind. She tells them to catch up every evening, even for twenty minutes.
3. What does she say about problems with a badge, a login or paperwork?
Not to put them off, and to come to the office, because they will look into it.
Listening 2 · A Resident Who Arrived

The Week I Stopped Guessing

An international resident on his first week in the United States. Sound first — no text.

1. What did he think he could do without in his first week, and why?
Asking questions. He thought his English was good enough and he did not want to look slow, so he guessed.
2. What did the attending point out on the Wednesday?
That he had guessed an abbreviation wrong in three notes.
3. What did he change the next morning?
He wrote down every term he did not know and looked them up on his break, and he started bringing things up at the huddle.
