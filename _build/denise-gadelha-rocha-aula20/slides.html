Chapter 3: The Code

The Switches That Move the Register

One position, three levels, and the reason to choose

Grammar Discovery

Same Position, Four Registers

I disagree. January does not work, and here is why.

I see it differently on the timing. Can I take two minutes on it?

I wonder whether we might look at the timing again.

The timing was raised by two of the regions.

The position is the same in all four: the date does not work. What changes is the directness and who is left holding it. Which words do the moving?

MarkerThe same point, movedWhat the listener hears
Modal (can → could → might)It might be worth revisiting the date.You may decline, and it will cost you nothing.
Hedge (I wonder whether)I wonder whether we might look at that again.This is open. Nobody is being overruled here.
Question, not statementShould we look at the timing again?The decision is shared, and rank is left intact.
Passive voiceThe timing was raised by two regions.Nobody is contradicted, because nobody is named.
NominalisationThere is some concern about the timing.A position exists without an author — useful in writing that will be forwarded.
Bare assertionI disagree, and here is why.Confidence in Amsterdam, pressure in Bogota. Same sentence.
The one that costs nothingConcede with and, never with but: That is fair, and the timing is still what worries me. The concession survives. With but, everything said before it is deleted.
Practice

Move It Down a Level

Say it first, then click to check

"I ___wonder whether we might look at that again." (soften a flat "I disagree" with one verb)
"It ___might be worth revisiting the January date." (lower the certainty, keep the point)
"___Should we look at the timing again before Thursday?" (turn the statement into a shared decision)
"The timing ___was raised by two of the regions, not by me." (take the person out of it)
"That is a fair point, ___and the timing is still what worries me." (keep the concession alive)
The Structure

Three Levels, and Who Each One Is For

The content below is identical in all three. Only the level moves.

Level 1 · Direct — low-context

"I disagree. January does not work for the renewal team, and here is why."

For Amsterdam, Frankfurt, New York. Reads as confidence. In Bogota or Tokyo the same words read as pressure.

Level 2 · The middle — the one that travels

"I see it differently on the timing. Can I take two minutes on it?"

For a message going to Milan, Bogota and Singapore at once. The point is made, the person is protected, and nothing is left to be inferred.

Level 3 · Indirect — high-context

"I wonder whether we might look at the timing again."

For contradicting your head in front of other people. Costs him nothing, and says exactly the same thing.

Level 3 is not weaker than Level 1. It carries the same position and pays a different price for it.

D
Kenji, can I check one thing before I write it down? When you say it would be a little difficult, are you telling me that January does not work?
K
I would not put it so strongly. Our renewal cycle closes on the twentieth, and the team would be doing both at the same time.
D
Then let me put it in the note as a group issue, not as your objection. Would the first week of February give your team the room it needs?
K
February would be comfortable. And thank you for asking twice. The second question was the one I could answer.
Comprehension

About Kenji

1. What does he say the first time he is asked about January?
That it is possible, and only a little difficult for his renewal team — he never refuses it.
2. What is the concrete reason behind his hesitation?
His renewal cycle closes on the twentieth, so his team would be doing both things at the same time.
3. What does he thank her for at the end, and why?
For asking twice — the second question was the one he was able to answer.

0:000:00

Politeness — and she had been building plans on it.
2. Which sentence taught her the lesson, and what did it actually mean?

Chapter 5: Practice

Your Sentence, Three Ways

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "It might to be worth revisiting the January date."
"It might be worth revisiting the January date." (after a modal the verb is bare: no "to", no -s)
2. "I wonder whether should we look at the timing again."
"I wonder whether we should look at the timing again." (inside an indirect question the order goes back to subject then verb — the inversion belongs to the direct question)
3. "In my point of view, January is not realistic for that team."
"From my point of view..." or "In my view..." (Portuguese merges the two into one expression that does not exist in English)
4. "The renewal date is a sensible topic in that region."
"...a sensitive topic in that region." ("sensible" in English means having good judgement — the false friend changes the whole sentence)

The Register Calibration Sheet

Register sheet — one message, three levels
Position: the January date does not work
same content
every line
Level 1 · Direct"I disagree. January does not work for the renewal team, and here is why."
For: Amsterdam, Frankfurt, New York — and any room where speed is the courtesy.

In writing"The timing was raised across the regions, and February is being considered."
For: a note that will be forwarded to people you cannot see.
Your lineWhich level do you default to, and who chose it — you, or the last person who made you uncomfortable?
1. Why does the sheet keep the position identical on every line?
Because calibrating is not conceding. If the content changes between levels, you did not shift register — you gave something away.
2. Why does the written line use the passive when the spoken ones do not?
Because a note travels to people you cannot see and cannot read. Naming who objected exposes them in a room you are not in.
