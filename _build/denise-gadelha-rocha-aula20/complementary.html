Lesson 20 -- Owning the Room in Any Language

Series
The Crown -- Season 1, Episode 4 ("Act of God")

An entire institution that refuses in the politest possible English. Almost nobody in it ever says no: the refusal arrives as a question, as a silence, or as a sentence about the weather. Watch it as a register laboratory rather than as history, and it will train the ear you needed for the dialogue in this lesson.

Tip: pause every time someone disagrees with a superior. Write the sentence down, then write the Level 1 version of the same message. The distance between the two is what you are learning to control.

Podcast
WorkLife with Adam Grant -- the episodes on disagreement and on how cultures argue

Look for the episodes on productive conflict and on how teams from different places handle disagreement. His point is the one behind today's grammar: the argument you win is rarely the one you made most forcefully, and directness is a habit of a place rather than a virtue.

Tip: while you listen, decide which of your four working cultures would find his own style normal, and which would find it aggressive. That answer is your calibration sheet in one line.

YouTube
Celeste Headlee -- 10 Ways to Have a Better Conversation (TEDxCreativeCoast)

She spends eleven minutes on the skill this lesson turns into a habit: asking a question whose answer you cannot predict, and then actually listening to it. Her rules travel across cultures better than most advice on communication, because none of them depends on being a native speaker of anything.

Tip: take one rule from her list into the next regional call and use it once. Then write down what came back that you would not have heard otherwise.
