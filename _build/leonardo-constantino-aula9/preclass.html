Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

What I Meant Was -- Reported Speech for Summarizing Client Calls, Legal Advice and Meeting Outcomes at ALMA

Telling a partner what a client said, without repeating the whole call. Key words: outcome, undertaking, representation, position, acknowledgment, disclosure, notification, amendment, correspondence. Expressions: reach a conclusion, give an undertaking, confirm in writing, on the record, off the record, follow through on. Structures: reported speech -- statements, questions and requests, tense backshift, and reporting verbs beyond said.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Outcome -- the final result of a meeting, a case or a decision
"The outcome of the call was better than the client expected."
Undertaking -- a formal promise to do something, given on the record
"The bank gave an undertaking to file before December."
Representation -- a statement of fact that one party makes to another and is expected to be true
"The seller made a representation about the tax position of the group."
Position -- the view a party takes and defends on a legal or tax question
"Our position is that the treaty rate applies to the remittance."
Acknowledgment -- a written confirmation that something was received or accepted
"We still have no acknowledgment from the authority."
Disclosure -- information a party is required to give to a regulator or to the other side
"The disclosure was made two days before the deadline."
Notification -- an official message telling somebody that something has happened
"The notification arrived after the filing deadline."
Amendment -- a formal change made to a document, a return or a law
"The amendment to the return was filed in June."
Correspondence -- the letters and emails exchanged between the parties on a file
"All correspondence with the authority goes through our office."
Reach a conclusion -- to arrive at a final decision after discussing something
"The board reached a conclusion on the second call."
Give an undertaking -- to promise formally, and on the record, that you will do something
"They gave an undertaking to review the file this week."
Confirm in writing -- to put a decision or a promise into an email or a letter
"She asked us to confirm the filing date in writing."
On the record -- said officially, so it can be quoted later
"On the record, we recommended a written confirmation."
Off the record -- said privately, so it cannot be quoted
"Off the record, nobody expects the ruling this year."
Follow through on -- to actually do what you promised to do
"The client rarely follows through on a verbal promise."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Renata Lisboa could not join the Meridian call, and she has four minutes before her next one. She does not want the conversation; she wants the outcome. So Leonardo reports it. The general counsel confirmed that the board had approved the amendment, but that nothing had been filed yet. She warned that the group had not received any notification from the authority, and that there was no acknowledgment anywhere in the correspondence. Their tax director asked whether the disclosure covered the Mexican entity as well; Leonardo told him it did not, and advised them to amend it before the deadline. That did not land well at first. Then the general counsel clarified that she had understood the risk, and she gave an undertaking to review the file this week and to confirm in writing. Renata asks one more thing, off the record: does he believe they will follow through on it? Off the record, he doubts it. On the record, he recommended that the two firms agree on a date and put their position in writing. Renata is satisfied. "Send me five lines tonight," she says. "I want one outcome, not a transcript."

1. "The general counsel confirmed that the board had approved the amendment." Why "had approved" and not "has approved"?
A Because the amendment was cancelled later.
B Because in reported speech the present perfect moves one step back, to the past perfect.
C Because the sentence is in the passive voice.
2. "Their tax director asked whether the disclosure covered the Mexican entity." Why is there no "did" in this sentence?
A Because a reported question is no longer a question: it takes normal subject-verb order, with no do or did.
B Because the question was never actually answered.
C Because "whether" can only be used with the present tense.
3. Leonardo "told him it did not" and "advised them to amend it". Why "told" and not "said"?
A Because "said" is only used in writing.
B Because "told" is more polite than "said" in a professional context.
C Because the person receiving the message comes straight after the verb, and "say" cannot take a person there.
4. What does Renata want at the end?
A A full transcript of the call, sent tonight.
B Five lines with one outcome she can act on.
C A new call with the client on Thursday morning.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

Reported speech is how you give a meeting back to somebody who was not in it. The rule is one movement: the verb steps back in time. "We file every quarter" becomes "She said they filed every quarter". The past form here is grammar, not meaning -- it does not say the fact has changed, only that somebody said it before now.

DirectReportedExample
present simplepast simple"We file every quarter." → She said they filed every quarter.
willwould"We will appeal." → He confirmed they would appeal.
cancould"We can absorb the cost." → She indicated they could absorb the cost.
present perfectpast perfect"We have received it." → He said they had received it.
Yes/no questionUse asked whether (or if) with normal word order, and no do or did: "He asked whether we had reviewed the exposure."
Wh- questionKeep the question word, then subject + verb: "She asked what the outcome was", never "what was the outcome".
Request or orderasked / told + object + to + verb: "The client asked us to prepare a memo." For a negative: not to.
Reporting verbsThe verb carries the responsibility: confirmed (it is certain), warned (there is a risk), advised and recommended (this is what you should do), indicated (a signal, no commitment), clarified (a point that was misunderstood), argued (a view defended with reasons).
Two frequent mistakesNever keep question order: "she asked what was the outcome" is wrong. And say never takes a person straight after it: "she told me", not "she said me".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each real conversation.

A partner asks what the client said about the filing date. You answer:
A "She confirmed that they would file before the end of the quarter."
B "She said: we will file before the end of the quarter, and then she said another thing."
C "She said me that they will file before the end of the quarter."
You need to report a question the tax director asked on the call. The correct sentence is:
A "He asked did the disclosure cover the Mexican entity."
B "He asked whether the disclosure covered the Mexican entity."
C "He asked what did the disclosure cover."
The client told you, on the call, that there was a real risk of a penalty. In the meeting note you write:
A "The client said that a penalty was possible."
B "The client warned that a penalty might be applied."
C "The client confirmed that a penalty would be applied."
A colleague asks whether the client made a real commitment. The precise answer is:
A "They said a lot of things about the deadline."
B "They gave an undertaking to confirm the filing date in writing this week."
C "They indicated that maybe somebody would look at it."
