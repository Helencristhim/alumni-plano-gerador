Documentary
FYRE: The Greatest Party That Never Happened

Former employees, investors and contractors spend ninety minutes reporting what one man told them, promised them and never confirmed in writing. Connection to Lesson 9: almost every sentence on screen is reported speech, and the reporting verb changes with every witness -- he said, he told us, he promised, he assured us.

Tip: watch with English subtitles and write down five sentences that report what somebody said. Mark the reporting verb in each one, then rewrite two of them about a real call of yours.

Podcast
Planet Money -- "The leaked tapes that show how the rich avoid taxes"

NPR reporters work through recordings of tax advisers selling a Maltese retirement structure, and the whole episode is built on reporting what the people on those tapes said, claimed and warned about. Connection to Lesson 9: your own subject matter, told entirely in reported speech, at native speed.

Tip: listen twice. The second time, note every reporting verb the host uses instead of said -- claimed, argued, warned, confirmed -- and decide what each one adds.

Video
BBC Learning English -- "How to use reported speech" (6 Minute Grammar)

Six minutes on exactly the structure you discovered in class: how the verb steps back in time, what happens to reported questions, and when the backshift is optional. Connection to Lesson 9: a second explanation in a different voice, right before you use it on a real client call.

Tip: pause before each answer and say your own version first. Then write two sentences about a live file: one reporting a statement, and one reporting a question.
