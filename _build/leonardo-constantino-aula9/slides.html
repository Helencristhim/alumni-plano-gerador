From a Transcript to a Report

Same Call, Two Versions

Version A — a transcript

"She said: we cannot file before the deadline. Then she said: the board approved it. Then I said: we need a date."

Version B — a report

"She confirmed that the board had approved it, but warned that they could not file before the deadline. I asked for a date."

A transcript repeats. A report tells the partner who committed to what — and it fits in one paragraph.

Chapter 3: The Code

One Step Back in Time

Say → said · will → would · can → could · have → had

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"We file every quarter." → She said that they filed every quarter.

"The reform will affect all fintechs." → She said that the reform would affect all fintechs.

"We have already reviewed the exposure." → He asked whether we had already reviewed the exposure.

"Prepare a full impact memo." → The client asked us to prepare a full impact memo.

The verb on the right always moves in the same direction. Which direction, and what happens to questions and to orders?

DirectReportedExample
present simplepast simple"We file every quarter." → She said they filed every quarter.
willwould"We will appeal." → He confirmed they would appeal.
cancould"We can absorb the cost." → She indicated they could absorb the cost.
present perfectpast perfect"We have received it." → He said they had received it.
Yes/no questionUse asked whether (or if) and normal word order — no do, no did: "He asked whether we had reviewed the exposure."
Wh- questionKeep the question word, then subject + verb: "She asked what the outcome was", not "what was the outcome".
Request or orderasked / told + object + to + verb: "The client asked us to prepare a memo." Negative: not to.
Time and placetoday → that day · yesterday → the day before · next week → the following week · here → there.

The past form is grammar, not meaning. "She said the rate was fifteen percent" does not mean the rate has changed — it only means somebody said it before now.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"She asked what was the outcome of the call."
"She asked what the outcome of the call was."
"The general counsel said me that the position was weak."
"The general counsel told me that the position was weak."
"The client asked to us prepare the disclosure."
"The client asked us to prepare the disclosure."

A reported question is no longer a question: subject before verb. And say never takes a person straight after it — that is what tell is for.

Beyond "Said"

Choose the Verb

Say the verb first, then click to check

She told us there was a real risk of a penalty. She ___warned us about it.

He told us what he thought we should do. He ___advised (or recommended) it.
She gave a signal, without committing to it. She ___indicated it.
He defended his view against ours, with reasons. He ___argued it.
She explained a point we had misunderstood. She ___clarified it.

Chapter 4: The Handover

Meet Renata

Senior partner at ALMA · she missed the call and has four minutes

Dialogue

Four Minutes to Report a Call

R
I could not join the call. What did Meridian actually say?
L
Their general counsel said that the board had already approved the amendment, but that nothing had been filed yet.
R
Did she give you a date?
L
She confirmed that they would file before the end of the quarter, and she asked us to confirm in writing.
R
And the notification from the authority?
L
She said they had not received it. There is no acknowledgment in the correspondence either.
R
Did anyone raise the disclosure?
L
Their tax director asked whether it covered the Mexican entity. I told him it did not, and I advised them to amend it.
R
How did that land?
L
Badly, at first. Then the general counsel clarified that she had understood the risk, and she gave an undertaking to review the file this week.
R
Off the record, will they follow through on it?
L
Off the record, I doubt it. On the record, I recommended that we agree on a date and put our position in writing.
R
Good. Send me five lines tonight. I want one outcome, not a transcript.

Listening 2

The Verb Carries the Risk

A senior lawyer on what a meeting note really records. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What did the junior lawyer write, and what should he have written?
He wrote that the client had said the position was safe; the client had actually warned that it was not.
2. Why does he say a meeting note is not a transcript?
Because a note records who committed to what, not every word in the order it was spoken.
3. What is his advice about the reporting verb?
Choose it before you write the sentence, because the verb is what somebody will rely on later.

The Real Thing

Read This Note

Attendance Note
Meridian Bank · call of 14 October
Prepared by Leonardo Constantino
ALMA · Financial Institutions
ConfirmedThe general counsel confirmed that the board had approved the amendment on 2 October.
WarnedShe warned that the group had received no notification, and that no acknowledgment appeared in the correspondence.
AskedThe tax director asked whether the disclosure covered the Mexican entity.
AdvisedWe advised that the disclosure should be amended before the filing deadline.
UndertakingThe client gave an undertaking to review the file this week and to confirm the filing date in writing.
Internal note. Positions recorded here are those stated on the call and remain subject to written confirmation.
1. Which line contains the only real commitment, and who gave it?
The last one: the client gave an undertaking to review the file and to confirm the filing date in writing.
2. Why is line two "warned" and not "said"?
Because she was flagging a risk, not stating a neutral fact — and in a note the verb is what somebody will rely on later.
