Note: most of this work has arrived by word of mouth. There is one gap, in 2020, and it is on the page on purpose.
1. Two lines on this page measure time in opposite ways. Which are they, and what is the difference?
"Since: 2016" names the moment it started. "Delivered: backing tracks for more than a hundred live shows" counts the result, with no date at all.
2. The note says the gap is on the page on purpose. Why would anyone choose to show it?
Because a timeline with no gap looks edited, and a reader who notices the edit stops believing the rest. Naming it in six words costs nothing and buys back the whole page.
3. According to this page, how does the work usually arrive, and how often is the showreel updated?
Most of it arrives by word of mouth, and the showreel is updated every six months.
Chapter 4: The Code

A Point, or a Length

Since 2016, or for ten years — never both

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"I have worked in television since 2016."

"I have been color grading for seven years."

"Everybody here has been passionate about music since they were twelve."

"I have delivered more than a hundred shows."

The red part has the same shape in all four. Look at the blue part: two of them mark the moment it started, one marks how long it has lasted, and one is missing on purpose.

JobFormExample
Started in the past, still true nowhave / has + past participleI have worked in television. She has run that show for years.
The moment it startedsince + a point in timesince 2016 · since January · since I left school · since they were twelve
How long it has lastedfor + a length of timefor ten years · for six months · for a long time
The activity is still runninghave / has been + verb-ingI have been building the tracks since January.
A result, with no datehave / has + past participle, aloneI have delivered more than a hundred shows. I have been nominated twice.
QuestionHow long have ... ?How long have you been doing this?
The trapnever the present simple or the present continuous with since / forNot I work here since 2016, not I am working here for ten years. Say I have worked here since 2016.

In one line: since points at the beginning, for measures the distance, and the verb keeps the door open.

Listening 1 · The Person Who Reads Them

Three Things That Make Me Stop

Somebody who has read four thousand applications, talking about what works. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the first thing that makes her stop, and what does she say happens the moment she sees it?
A number that is specific: not many years of experience, but a date. The moment there is a date, she believes the rest of the paragraph.
2. She remembers one line from an application last year. What did it say?
That the hardest part of the writer’s job was not the music, it was telling a singer that the key was wrong on the day of the show.
3. What is the third thing, and what does she say about the applications that do the opposite?
Honesty about the gap. Everybody has a year that went quietly; the applications that hide it always look edited, and the ones that name it in six words never do.
Listening 2 · How the Work Arrives

Nobody Has Ever Hired Me Because of My Website

Fifteen years of freelance work, and one pattern with no exceptions. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say his website is actually for?
So that people who have already decided to hire him have somewhere to check that he is real. He has had it since 2014 and updates it about once a year.
2. How has every job he has had arrived, and what does he say you are really building?
By word of mouth: somebody he worked with told somebody else. He says what you are building is not a showreel, it is a memory in about forty people.
3. What two things does he say people remember about working with you?
Whether you were calm at eleven at night when something broke, and whether you said the difficult thing early, while it could still be fixed. Neither of them is your best mix.
