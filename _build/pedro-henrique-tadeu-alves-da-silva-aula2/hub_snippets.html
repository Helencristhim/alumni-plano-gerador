Ten Years In

The four lines that follow you everywhere: how long you have been doing this, and what has come out of it. Key words: a credit, a showreel, self-taught, to run something, a milestone, to specialize in, a gap, to turn into, steady, word of mouth. Structure: present perfect with since for the starting point and for for the length of time.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A credit -- your name in the list of the people who made a song, a film or a show
"I have three album credits, and two of them are records nobody bought."
A showreel -- a short collection of your best work that you send to people who might hire you
"My showreel is two minutes long, and the first ten seconds do all the work."
Self-taught -- having learned something on your own, without a school or a teacher
"I am self-taught, which means I learned the software before I learned the theory."
To run something -- to be the person in charge of it while it happens
"I have run the music side of that show for four seasons."
A milestone -- a moment that marks real progress, and that you can point at later
"The hundredth live show was a milestone, and it took me eight years."
To specialize in -- to make one narrow area the thing you are known for
"I have specialized in live music shows, and that is why people call me."
A gap -- a period when nothing happened, between two things that did
"There is a gap in 2020, and everybody in this industry has one."
To turn into -- to change slowly into something different
"The projects I do for fun have turned into half of my income."
Steady -- continuing at the same rate, without stopping and without surprises
"The work has been steady since 2018, which in this industry is a luxury."
Word of mouth -- the way work arrives when one person tells another that you are good
"Every job I have had has arrived by word of mouth, never from my website."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

There is a paragraph that follows you everywhere: under your photo, at the top of an email to a stranger, next to the link to your showreel. Every sentence in it does the same job, and English gives that job one shape: have or has plus the past participle. He has worked in television. He has specialized in live music shows. He has run the music side of a show for four seasons. All of it started in the past, and all of it is still true today, which is exactly why the past simple does not fit here.

Then the sentence usually needs a measurement, and there are only two. Since points at the beginning: since 2016, since January, since he left college. For measures the distance: for eight years, for six months. The work has been steady since 2018. The projects he does on the side have turned into half of his income. He has been building backing tracks for eight years — and that longer form, have been plus -ing, says the activity is still running right now.

Some sentences carry no date at all, and they are the strongest ones. He has delivered more than a hundred live shows. He has been nominated twice. He is self-taught, and almost every job he has had has arrived by word of mouth. Each of those is a milestone or a fact, not a story, so it needs one line and no year. And the gap? There has been one, in 2020, and he names it in six words. Two warnings for a Portuguese speaker: never the present simple with since (not I work here since 2016), and never the continuous with for (not I am working here for ten years).

1. "He has worked in television since 2016." Why not "He worked in television since 2016"?
A Because "work" has no past simple form.
B Because it started in the past and is still true today, and the past simple would close it.
C Because "since" can only be used with "have".
2. What is the difference between "since 2016" and "for eight years"?
A "Since" points at the moment it began; "for" measures how long it has lasted.
B "Since" is formal and "for" is informal.
C "Since" is for work and "for" is for study.
3. The text says "He has delivered more than a hundred live shows" is one of the strongest sentences. Why?
A Because it uses a longer verb form than the others.
B Because it explains how he got the job.
C Because it is a result that needs no date at all, so one line is enough.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect with For and Since

GRAMMAR

One decision: are you naming the starting point, or measuring the distance?

JobFormExample
Started in the past, still true nowhave / has + past participleI have worked in television. She has run that show.
The moment it startedsince + a point in timesince 2016 · since January · since I left college
How long it has lastedfor + a length of timefor eight years · for six months · for a long time
The activity is still runninghave / has been + verb-ingI have been building the tracks since January.
A result, with no datehave / has + past participle, aloneI have delivered a hundred shows. I have been nominated twice.
Negative and questionhave not / has not · How long have ... ?I have not worked in radio since 2015. How long have you been doing this?
The trapnever the present simple or the present continuous hereNot I work here since 2016, not I am working here for ten years. Say I have worked here since 2016.

Never: I work here since 2016 · I am working here for ten years · I have specialized since eight years · I have deliver a hundred shows · How long you are doing this?

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

Somebody asks how long you have been doing this. You answer:
A "I work in this area since 2016, more or less ten years."
B "I have worked in music and audiovisual production since 2016."
C "I am working in this since ten years ago."
You want to say that one narrow area is what you are known for. You say:
A "I have specialized in live music shows for eight years."
B "I am specialist of live music shows since eight years."
C "My specialization is being live music shows for eight years."
You want to name a result, with no date. You say:
A "I am delivering more than a hundred live shows."
B "I have deliver more than a hundred live shows."
C "I have delivered more than a hundred live shows."
There is a year in your timeline when nothing came out. You say:
A "There has been one gap, in 2020, and I do not hide it."
B "Sorry, I am not working in 2020, it was a bad year for me."
C "In 2020 I have nothing, please do not ask about this."
