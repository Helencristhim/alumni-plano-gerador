Chapter 3: The Code

Like, Want, Would Like

Three patterns for opinion and desire

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I like basketball."

"I like watching series at night."

"I want to learn English."

"I would like a water, please."

After like, what comes? And after want?

PatternWhat comes afterExample
I like + thinga noun"I like basketball."
"I like comedies."
I like + verb-inga verb with -ing"I like watching series."
"I like playing games."
I want to + verbto + the normal verb"I want to learn English."
"I want to travel alone."
I would likethe polite version of want"I would like a water, please."
"I would like to see the menu."
Key ideaIn a shop, a restaurant or an airport, always use would like. I want a coffee sounds like an order; I would like a coffee, please sounds like a person.

Opinion and desire. That is where a conversation stops being a form and becomes a talk.

Chapter 4: The Free Afternoon

The Free Afternoon

Three options, and you have to choose one

Dialogue

What Do You Want to Do?

Z
Hey Bruno. Practice is over. What do you want to do this afternoon?
B
I don't know. What are the options?
Z
The pool, the game room, or a movie about a basketball team.
B
I like games, but I want to see the movie. Basketball is my favorite sport.
Z
Me too. Do you like watching movies in English, without subtitles?
B
Not yet. Sometimes it is difficult and it is boring for me. But I want to learn.
Z
Then sit with me. I explain the difficult parts. What is your favorite series?
B
A mystery series. I like watching one episode every night. And you?
Comprehension

About Zoe

1. What three options does she give?
The pool, the game room, or a movie about a basketball team.
2. Does she want to see the movie too?
Yes, she does. She says "Me too".
3. What does she offer to do in the movie?
She offers to explain the difficult parts.
4. What does she ask at the end?
She asks what his favorite series is.

Quick Fire

Answer Fast

0 / 5

Somebody asks: "What is your favorite sport, and why?" Answer.

"Basketball is my favorite sport. I like playing with my friends."

Somebody offers you a horror movie. You don't like it. Say no politely, and say why.

"No, thank you. I don't like horror movies. They are boring for me."

Somebody asks: "What do you do at night?" Use "I like" plus a verb.

"I like watching series and playing video games."

You are in a coffee shop in Los Angeles. Order a water, politely.

"I would like a water, please."

Somebody asks why you study English. Say what you want to do with it.

"I want to travel alone and I want to talk to my uncle."
