The First Morning

Eight words for somebody else's house (kettle, cupboard, leftovers, charger, password, towel, settle in, bother) and the two questions that get you all of them: Do you mind if I + verb, and Would you mind + verb-ing.

Stage 1.1: Kitchen Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes. The "p" in cupboard is silent.

Kettle — the machine that boils water for tea or coffee
"Do you mind if I use the kettle?"
Cupboard — furniture with doors where plates, cups and food are kept
"The mugs are in the cupboard above the sink."
Leftovers — food from an earlier meal that nobody ate
"There are leftovers from last night in the fridge."
Charger — the cable you use to put electricity back in your phone
"Would you mind lending me a charger?"
Password — the secret word you type to get on the wifi
"Sorry to bother you, what is the wifi password?"
Towel — the cloth you use to dry yourself after a shower
"There is a clean towel on your bed."
Settle in — to start feeling comfortable in a new place
"It takes about a week to settle in."
Bother — to disturb somebody who is busy with something else
"Sorry to bother you, but where are the glasses?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. Then answer the questions.

First morning in the house and I was awake at four. At six I was standing in their kitchen looking at six closed cupboards. My host mum came in and I said: "Sorry to bother you. Do you mind if I use the kettle?" She said "No, not at all, go ahead" — and for one second I thought that was a no. Then I asked: "Would you mind giving me the wifi password?" It was on a note on the fridge the whole time. She told me I never have to ask about food, so I had the leftovers from Sunday. The last one was the shower: "Would you mind showing me how it works?" Three buttons, and none of them was the right one. She said I will settle in faster if I stop apologising and just ask.
Why is it "Would you mind giving me" and not "Would you mind to give me"?
A After "would you mind" the verb always ends in -ing. There is never a "to".
B Because "give" is short, so it takes -ing instead of "to".
He asked "Do you mind if I use the kettle?" and she answered "No, not at all". What does that mean?
A She said no, so he cannot use the kettle.
B It does not bother her, so he can use it. "No" here means yes, go ahead.
In "Do you mind if I use the kettle", which word can never disappear?
A The verb "use". "Do you mind if I the kettle" is not a sentence.
B The word "the". Everything else is optional in a question.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Two polite questions for somebody else's house

Mind means bother. So you are really asking: does this bother you?

The form

Do you mind if I use it?
Do you mind if I have some?
Would you mind showing me?
Would you mind lending me one?

Never

Would you mind to show me
Do you mind if I using it
Do you mind if I the kettle
→ Do you mind if I use the kettle

Which one? "Do you mind if I" is for something you want to do. "Would you mind" is for something you want the other person to do for you.
The answer is upside down: "No, not at all" and "Course not, go ahead" both mean yes, you can. To say no: "Actually, I'm using it right now".
Opening line: "Sorry to bother you" — once, then the question. Not before every sentence.

Stage 4: In their house

Quiz
You want to make a coffee in your host family's kitchen. What do you say?
A Do you mind if I use the kettle?
B Do you mind if I using the kettle?
You cannot work the shower and you want your host mum to show you.
A Would you mind to show me how the shower works?
B Would you mind showing me how the shower works?
Somebody asks you: "Do you mind if I sit here?" You are happy for them to sit down.
A No, not at all. Go ahead.
B Yes, I mind. Go ahead.
Your phone is dead and you need to message home. Which one is a complete question?
A Do you mind if I a charger?
B Do you mind if I borrow a charger?
Your host dad asks if he can take your board out of the car. You would rather do it yourself.
A Actually, I'd rather do it myself — the fins come off first.
B No, not at all — the fins come off first.

Stage 5: Your first morning

Reflection
You wake up at six in a house you slept in for the first time. Record 30 seconds: ask six things you genuinely do not know yet — three with "Do you mind if I", three with "Would you mind". Check one thing before you send it: after "if I" there has to be a verb. "Do you mind if I the kettle" is not a question.
