Stage 1.1: Daily-Life Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Assignment — a piece of work that the teacher gives you to do at home
"I have three assignments this week."
Classmate — somebody who is in the same class as you
"My classmates helped me with the timetable."
Bus pass — the card that lets you take the bus every day
"You need a bus pass to get to the beach."
Weekend job — the small job that you do on Saturday or Sunday
"A lot of students here have a weekend job."
Catch up — to do the work that you did not do before
"I have to catch up on two assignments."
Hang out — to spend time with people, with no plan
"We hang out at the beach after school."
Get used to — to stop finding something strange
"I am getting used to the accent."
Settle in — to start feeling at home in a new place
"It takes about a month to settle in."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the week. Then answer the questions.

Monday to Friday I get up at half past five, because the waves are better before school. Right now I am doing an assignment on Australian history and I have not started it properly yet. Last Saturday I worked at the surf shop for the first time. I have been here for four weeks and I am finally getting used to the accent. This weekend I am going to work again, and then I am going to sleep for eleven hours.
Why does he get up at half past five?
A Because the waves are better before school.
B Because school starts at five.
What has he not done yet?
A He has not worked at the surf shop.
B He has not started the assignment properly.
Why is "last Saturday I worked" in the past simple?
A Because there is a finished date.
B Because the action is still going.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

One week, five tenses

No new structure. Listen to the auxiliary at the front of the question and give it back.

The question starts with

Do you...? / What time do you...?
Are you ...ing?
Did you...?
Have you...? / How long have you...?
Are you going to...?

So the answer is

I get up at six. (routine)
I am doing an assignment. (now)
I went to the beach. (finished)
I have been here for a month. (still true)
I am going to work. (decided)

Routine → present simple: I get up at six.
Right now → present continuous: I am doing an assignment.
Finished date → past simple: Last Saturday I worked.
Still true, no date → present perfect: I have been here for four weeks.
Decided plan → going to: This weekend I am going to work.

Stage 4: Four weeks in

Quiz
Somebody asks: "What are you doing?" You are working on an essay.
A I do an assignment.
B I am doing an assignment.
Somebody asks: "How long have you been here?"
A I am here since four weeks.
B I have been here for four weeks.
Somebody asks: "What did you do last weekend?"
A Last weekend I worked at the surf shop.
B Last weekend I have worked at the surf shop.
You did not understand the question and you need three seconds.
A Sorry, say that again?
B Como? Repete?

Stage 5: Your normal week there

Reflection
Describe a normal week of your life in Australia, four weeks after you arrive. Record 45 seconds. Use all five: what you do every day, what you are doing at the moment, what you did last weekend, how long you have been there, and what you are going to do next. Count them before you send it — five different tenses is the target.
