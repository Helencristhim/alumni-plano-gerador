Grammar Discovery

Two ways to ask

Do you mind if I use the kettle?
Would you mind showing me how the shower works?
Do you mind if I take a towel from here?
Would you mind giving me the wifi password? (not "to give")

Do you mind if I + normal verb  ·  Would you mind + verb-ing

The first one asks for permission — something you want to do. The second one asks the other person to do something for you. After would you mind the verb always ends in -ing, never to. And in both of them the verb has to be there: "do you mind if I use the kettle", never "do you mind if I the kettle".

The Code

Two forms, three traps

The form

Do you mind if I use it?
Do you mind if I have some?
Would you mind showing me?
Would you mind lending me one?

Never

Would you mind to show me.
after "would you mind" the verb ends in -ing
Do you mind if I using it.
after "if I" the verb is normal: use, take, have
Do you mind if I     the kettle.
the verb cannot be missing

Opening line for any of them: Sorry to bother you — then the question.

The Code

Saying no means yes

Mind means bother. So the question is really: does this bother you?

Yes, go ahead

"No, not at all."
"Not at all, go ahead."
"Course not. Go for it."
"Yeah, no worries."

No, please don't

"Actually, I'd rather you didn't."
"Sorry, I'm using it right now."
"Actually, yes, a bit."
Do you mind if I use the kettle? No, not at all.

"No" here is a yes. It is the answer you will hear every day.

Sound Check

Three blocks, not nine words

Do you mind if I use your charger?

"do you mind if I" is one block: d'you-mind-if-eye

Would you mind showing me?

"would you" sounds like one word: wudja

No, not at all. Go ahead.

"not at all" runs together: nod-a-tall

Did You Catch It?

About Bev

1. What does she say about the kettle and the mugs?
Use the black kettle, not the silver one. The mugs are in the cupboard above the sink.
2. Where is the wifi password, and what is it?
On the note on the fridge. A capital B, then the dog and the year.
3. What does she tell him he never has to ask about?
Food. It is his kitchen for six months, so he never has to ask her about food.
Listening 1

The voice message from Dave

1. Where is the bread, and what is in the fridge?
The bread is in the cupboard above the kettle, and there are leftovers from last night in the fridge.
2. Which kettle must he not use, and why?
The small silver one on the shelf. It is broken and it trips the whole house.
3. What does Dave tell him to do if he cannot find something?
Not to stand there being polite about it for twenty minutes. Just ask Bev.
Listening 2

The voice note from Bev

1. What time is dinner tonight, and why is it different?
Six thirty, not seven, because Millie has training.
2. What does she want him to do with his laundry?
Put his things in the basket by the door. She runs it with theirs on Wednesday.
3. What does she say he should have said in the kitchen this morning?
Would you mind showing me where the mugs are. A whole sentence, and it costs him nothing.
