Chapter 4: The Code

Can and Can’t

One small word for ability and for help

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Have in Common?

"You can ask for help."

"You can’t understand every word."

"Can you repeat that, please?"

"She can speak three languages."

Look at the blue words. After can, is there "to"? With she, is there an s? Where is can in the question?

JobFormExample
Abilitycan + verbI can understand American English.
Negativecan’t (cannot) + verbI can’t hear you.
QuestionCan + you + verb?Can you hear me?
Request (asking for help)Can you + verb, please?Can you repeat that, please?
He, she, itno sShe can speak Hindi. (Not cans.)
The trapno "to" after canNot can to speak. Say can speak.

In one line: can + verb, for everybody: I can, she can. No to, no s. Question: Can you...?

Listening 2 · Sarah’s Rule

One Language, Ten Countries

Sarah, the host of the global call, talks about how she speaks on the call. Sound first — no text.

1. What languages can Sarah speak?
Only English.
2. How does Sarah speak on global calls?
Slowly, with short sentences.
3. What can people do when they don't understand?
They can write in the chat or ask her to repeat.
