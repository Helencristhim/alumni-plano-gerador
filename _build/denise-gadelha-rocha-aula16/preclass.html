Lesson 16 -- Pre-class

The Executive Conditional Mind -- Subjunctive and Formal Proposals in Board-Level Communication

The register that turns a request into a proposal. Key words: proposal, submit, rationale, purview, stipulate, safeguard, prerequisite, subject to approval, undertaking, ratify, binding, at the board's discretion. Structure: the subjunctive in formal register -- the bare verb after recommend, require and propose, the negative, the passive, and hypothetical were.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the definition. These words live in documents, not in conversation.

Proposal -- a written recommendation put to a body that can decide on it
"The proposal goes to the November board, not to a working group."
To submit -- to send something formally to the body that will decide on it
"The paper was submitted on the fifteenth, with the treaty note attached."
Rationale -- the reasoning a decision is taken on, written down before it is taken
"The rationale is two paragraphs. If it needs five, the case is not there yet."
Purview -- the area of business that belongs to a person or a committee
"Industrial risks above thirty million fall within the board's purview, not mine."
To stipulate -- to state a condition explicitly, in writing, as part of the agreement
"The paper stipulates a single regional limit, not one limit per market."
Safeguard -- a measure written in to prevent one specific thing from going wrong
"Three safeguards, and the third one is the reason the board will say yes."
Prerequisite -- something that has to be in place before the rest can proceed at all
"The treaty renewal is a prerequisite, not a refinement."
Subject to approval -- valid only if something else is approved first
"The increase is subject to approval of the reinsurance treaty."
Undertaking -- a formal promise, given on the record, that you will do something
"I gave an undertaking that every case would be reported within five days."
To ratify -- to approve something formally, as a body, so that it becomes official
"The authority lapses in December unless the board ratifies it again."
Binding -- creating an obligation that cannot simply be set aside later
"The decision is binding from January, on every market without exception."
At the board's discretion -- left for the board to decide as it sees fit
"Two matters remain at the board's discretion, and they are named on page one."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the account and answer the questions. Watch every verb that comes straight after the word "that".

The first draft asked. It said that the region would like the underwriting limit to be raised, and that the board might wish to consider it at some point before the renewal. The secretary read it once and sent it back with a single comment: a paper that asks gets a discussion, and a proposal gets a vote.

The second draft opened with one sentence. It is proposed that regional authority be raised to thirty-five million, subject to approval of the reinsurance treaty. Nothing about the case had changed. The rationale underneath was the same eleven working days and the same four risks placed elsewhere. What changed was the verb: not "is raised", not "would be raised", but be raised, with no tense and no agreement at all.

The three safeguards moved up into the recommendation, where they were binding on the reader rather than buried in section three. The Directorate asked that every case be reported within five working days, and gave an undertaking that the authority lapse in December unless the board ratifies it again. One line was added at the end, naming what remained at the board's discretion. It is essential, the paper said, that this not be deferred to February, since the treaty will not wait.

1. "It is proposed that regional authority be raised to thirty-five million." Why "be raised" and not "is raised"?
A Because after a verb of proposing, the verb in the that-clause takes the bare form with no tense and no agreement — here the passive "be raised".
B Because the increase has not happened yet, so English uses the future.
C Because "raise" is an irregular verb in the passive.
2. "The Directorate asked that every case be reported within five working days." What would "is reported" change?
A Nothing at all; the two are interchangeable in formal writing.
B It would read as a description of what already happens, rather than as the requirement being asked for.
C It would make the requirement stronger and more formal.
3. "It is essential that this not be deferred to February." Why is there no "does" in the negative?
A Because the subject is impersonal, so the auxiliary is dropped.
B Because "defer" already contains a negative idea.
C Because this verb form has no tense to carry an auxiliary: "not" simply goes in front of it.
4. Why did the second draft get a decision when the first one did not?
A Because the second draft had a stronger business case behind it.
B Because the case was identical and only the register changed — a sentence that can be read aloud and voted on is a proposal, while a sentence that asks is an invitation to discuss.
C Because the second draft was shorter and easier to read.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Subjunctive in Formal Register

GRAMMAR

Portuguese marks this form by changing the ending. English marks it by taking the -s away, which is why correct English here looks like a typing error.

TriggerWhat followsExample
Verbs of recommendation and demandrecommend · propose · request · require · insist · demand · ask · urge — then that + subject + bare verbThe board requires that each region submit its schedule.
Adjectives of necessityit is essential · vital · imperative · critical · advisable · necessaryIt is imperative that the treaty be signed first.
The bare verbNo -s, no does, no tense: that he go, that she report, that it lapse. The verb stays in its dictionary form whatever the subject is.
The negativeOnly not, straight in front: that this not be delayed. Never that this does not be delayed and never that this is not delayed.
The passive — the one you will write mostBoard papers are written in the passive, so the form is be + past participle: that the limit be raised, that the deferral be recorded, that each case be reported.
Hypothetical wereWere replaces was for anything hypothetical: If I were to propose one change · If it were up to me · and inverted, with no if: Were the board to defer, the treaty would not wait.
The spoken alternativeIn speech you can avoid the form entirely: I recommend revising the limits. In a paper you cannot, and the difference is what the reader hears — a suggestion, or a proposal to be voted on.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

You want the board to decide, not discuss. Your first line reads:
A "It is proposed that the regional limit be raised to thirty-five million."
B "We would like the board to consider raising the regional limit."
C "Perhaps the board could look at the regional limit before the renewal."
Which sentence is correct?
A "The board requires that each region submits the schedule by Friday."
B "The board requires that each region submit the schedule by Friday."
C "The board requires that each region will submit the schedule by Friday."
You need the negative in a formal recommendation. You write:
A "It is essential that this does not be delayed."
B "It is essential that this is not delayed."
C "It is essential that this not be delayed."
The whole proposal depends on a treaty that has not been signed yet. In the recommendation you write:
A "...subject to approval of the reinsurance treaty."
B "...if the reinsurance treaty is signed, which we hope it will be."
C Nothing. You mention the treaty in a footnote so the paper reads more strongly.
You promise the board that every case will be reported within five days, and you want that promise on the record. That is:
A A prerequisite.
B An undertaking.
C A purview.
There are two details you can genuinely live with either way. In a board paper you:
A Name them as matters left at the board's discretion, so the rest of the paper is not reopened.
B Decide them yourself and say nothing, to keep the paper short.
C Leave the whole paper open, so the board feels it is deciding everything.
