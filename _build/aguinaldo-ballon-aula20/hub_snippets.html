Midpoint Milestone -- The Panel You Did Not Take

An integration of lessons 11 to 19 through a reading about why experts turn down panels. Key words: a panelist, to turn down, stage fright, a talking point, opening remarks, to rehearse, to get your point across, off the cuff, composure, a milestone. Structures: a review of the second toolbox, no new grammar.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A panelist -- a person who speaks as one of the experts on a panel
"Each panelist has five minutes for opening remarks."
To turn down -- phrasal verb: to say no to an offer or an invitation
"I turned down the invitation because my English was not ready."
Stage fright -- the fear you feel before you speak or perform in front of people
"Even experienced speakers feel stage fright before a big event."
A talking point -- an important idea that you plan to mention in a discussion
"Prepare three talking points, not a full speech."
Opening remarks -- the short speech at the start of an event or a panel
"Keep your opening remarks under two minutes."
To rehearse -- to practice a speech or a performance before the real event
"Rehearse your answer out loud, not only in your head."
To get your point across -- expression: to make people understand your idea clearly
"Simple words help you get your point across."
Off the cuff -- expression: without preparation, in the moment
"The best answers on a panel are often off the cuff."
Composure -- the state of being calm and in control, especially under pressure
"She kept her composure when the audience disagreed."
A milestone -- an important moment or stage in a process
"Lesson twenty is a milestone in the program."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Two years ago, Aguinaldo turned down a panel in New York. Although he knew the topic very well, he was afraid of the questions. Now a new invitation seems to be the right moment: it is a milestone, twenty lessons after the first one.

Roughly two thirds of the audience will be investors. Due to the new rules in Brazil, many of them are interested in his sector. This interest is a good reason to say yes.

He has a plan. He will rehearse his opening remarks every morning until he can say them without notes. As soon as the moderator sends the questions, he will prepare three talking points. Notice how many structures from lessons 11 to 19 are in this short text.

1. Why did Aguinaldo turn down the first panel?
A Because he was afraid of the questions.
B Because he did not know the topic well enough and needed more time to study it first.
C Because the audience was too small and there were not enough investors in the room.
2. Who will most of the audience be?
A Lawyers and officials from the transport agencies and the national regulators.
B Students from the doctorate program and a few journalists from New York.
C Investors.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "As soon as the moderator will send the questions, he will prepare his talking points."
B "As soon as the moderator sends the questions, he will prepare."
C "As soon as the moderator sent the questions, he will prepare his talking points tomorrow."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Twenty Lessons, Two Toolboxes

GRAMMAR

No new grammar today. On the stage, choose the structure by the job you need to do.

JobFormExample
Impressions and certainty (lessons 11, 12)seem to + verb · must / may / be likely toThe moderator seems to know the sector.
Linking and dense ideas (lessons 13, 14)Moreover, · this + noun · noun phrasesThis decision changed the market.
Building rapport (lesson 15)question tagsYou are on the second panel, aren’t you?
Numbers and evidence (lesson 17)roughly · the majority of · a fraction ofRoughly two thirds of the audience are investors.
Cause and contrast (lesson 18)due to + noun · although + sentence · despite + nounAlthough I was nervous, I accepted.
Time and sequence (lesson 19)as soon as / once / until + present · by the time + hadAs soon as the panel ends, I will call you.

Never: As soon as the panel will end · Despite I was nervous · He seems knowing · You are on the panel, isn’t it?

Stage 2: Put the Opening Remarks in Order

Order

You are on the panel. Put your opening remarks in a logical order.

?"Let me make three quick points."
?"First, roughly half of the projects in the region need private capital."
?"Thank you for having me on the panel."
?"Finally, as soon as the rules are stable, more funds will come."
?"I have worked in infrastructure and regulation for more than twenty years."
?"Second, although the risks are real, the rules are getting clearer."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You explain why you said no to the first panel. You say:
A "Although I knew the topic, I was afraid of the questions."
B "Despite I was nervous about the questions, I almost accepted the first invitation from the forum."
C "Although of the questions from the audience, I turned down the first invitation from the forum."
You describe the audience with an approximate number. You say:
A "Roughly of the two thirds audience are investors, and the rest of the room are lawyers."
B "The two thirds roughly audience is investors, and the other people in the room are lawyers."
C "Roughly two thirds of the audience are investors."
The moderator asks what you will do after the panel. You say:
A "As soon as the panel will end, I will talk to the fund partners about the new projects."
B "As soon as the panel ends, I will talk to the partners."
C "By the time the panel ends, I talk to the fund partners and then I go to the networking lunch."
A question surprises you, but you answer calmly. The moderator says you showed:
A "Stage fright, because you answered the question without stopping and without any preparation."
B "A milestone, because it was the first time you answered a question from the audience on a panel."
C "Composure."
