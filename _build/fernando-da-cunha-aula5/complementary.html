Talk
TED -- Chris Anderson, "TED’s secret to great public speaking" (8 min)

The head of TED on the single thing every talk that works has in common: it builds one idea in the listener’s head, in order, instead of listing what the speaker knows. It is the lesson of the first recording, said from the other side of the stage.

Tip: eight minutes and very clear. Watch it once, then say out loud what the one idea in your own proposal is, in a single sentence.

Podcast
How I Built This -- Warby Parker: Dave Gilboa and Neil Blumenthal

Two founders describing how they argued a business into existence against people who thought the whole idea was wrong. The pitch language is exactly the register of this lesson: the problem first, the number second, the objection named out loud.

Tip: listen to the first fifteen minutes and write down the sentence they use for the problem. It comes before any mention of the product.

Video
Aswath Damodaran -- "The Value of Stories in Business" (Talks at Google)

A finance professor on why numbers alone never convince anybody, and why a story without numbers convinces the wrong people. Dense, unhurried American English, and the most useful hour you can spend on the difference between a spreadsheet and a case.

Tip: it is long. Watch twenty minutes at a time. Note one place where he moves the important part of a sentence to the front.
