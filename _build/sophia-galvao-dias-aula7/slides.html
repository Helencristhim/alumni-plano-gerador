The Code

The top of everything

"It's a good museum."  →  "It's the best museum I've ever been to."

"That market is bad."  →  "That market is by far the worst one in the city."

"The rooftop is nice."  →  "It's one of the nicest rooftops I've seen."

"I found a bookshop."  →  "It's the best bookshop I've ever found, and nobody knows it."

FormWhat it does
the best / worst / nicest + noun
+ I've ever + past participle		Puts it at the top of everything you have done up to now, not just this city.
the best museum I've ever been to
by far + superlativeSays the gap to second place is enormous.
by far the worst one
one of the + superlative + plural nounPuts it in the top group, not in first place.
one of the nicest rooftops

Three things to watch. One superlative is enough: not the most best, just the best. After one of the the noun is always plural, because you are choosing one out of several. And the word that opens your whole life is ever, never never: the worst coffee I've ever had.

Listening 1

Ruby ranks her city

1. Which place does Ruby call the most overrated, and why?
The big wheel by the river — you pay a lot, you go up for twenty minutes, and all you see is roofs.
2. What is the best thing she has ever eaten in her city, and where?
A cheese sandwich from a van behind the bus station.
3. What rule does she give at the end for choosing where to go?
Ask somebody who lives there instead of the internet — the places with the most photos are almost never the best ones.

A visitor has one afternoon and wants the place everybody photographs. Change their mind: say why that one is overrated, and put something else at the top instead — with a reason they can argue with.

Your Move

Best and worst

Ninety seconds: the best place you have ever been to in your life, and the one that let you down the most. Not a list — each one in two or three sentences, with the reason, and say which of the two you would go back to tomorrow.

No chips this time. Your words, your pace.
