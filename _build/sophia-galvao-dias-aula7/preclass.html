The Best Thing About It

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Eight words for saying whether a place is any good. Listen to each one and read the example out loud.

Overrated -- that people say is amazing when it really is not
"The tower is the most overrated thing in the whole city."
Underrated -- better than almost anybody says it is
"The little museum behind the park is completely underrated."
Worth it -- good enough to pay for the time, the money or the walk
"You wait forty minutes, but it is worth it."
Hidden gem -- a very good place that almost nobody knows about
"That bookshop on the corner is a hidden gem."
Rooftop -- the flat top of a building that people can go up to
"It is the best rooftop I have ever been to."
Touristy -- full of visitors, and built for them and not for you
"The whole street is too touristy for me."
Turn out -- to end up being something you did not expect
"It turned out to be the best afternoon of the week."
Let down -- to disappoint somebody who was expecting more
"The famous market let me down completely."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. The words in bold are the ones that put something at the top of a list.

My cousin sent me eleven places to visit and asked me which ones were any good. So here is the honest version. The shopping street everybody photographs is by far the most overrated thing here. I went in January, it was touristy from one end to the other, and it let me down completely.

What goes at the top instead is the rooftop on the old bank. It is the best view I have ever seen in this city, and I have been up a lot of buildings. Yes, you wait forty minutes. Yes, it is worth it. Go at six, when the light changes.

Then there is the little museum behind it, which is one of the most underrated museums in the whole country and is not on anybody's list. My cousin had never heard of it. That afternoon turned out to be the best one of her trip, and I am not surprised: every place I have ever loved was a hidden gem that nobody put on a list.

"It is the best view I have ever seen in this city." How many views is the writer comparing?
A Two: this one and the one before it.
B All of them — I have ever seen opens the list to her whole life up to now.
C None — she is only saying that she liked it.
"One of the most underrated museums." Why is museums plural here?
A Because one of takes one item out of a group, so the group has to be plural.
B Because there is more than one museum in that building.
C Because underrated always comes before a plural noun.
"It is by far the most overrated thing here." What does by far add?
A That the writer is not completely sure about it.
B That the place is a long way from the city centre.
C That the distance between first place and second place is enormous.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Superlatives, and the tense that opens your whole life

A comparative puts one thing above another. A superlative puts it above everything — but everything in which list? That is the job of the present perfect: I have ever been to makes the list your entire life up to today.

The top of everything

  • the best / worst / nicest + noun + I have ever + past participle
  • "It is the best museum I have ever been to." · "It is the worst coffee I have ever had."

The size of the gap

  • by far / easily + the + superlative
  • "It is by far the most overrated street in the city." — second place is nowhere near.

The top group, not first place

  • one of the + superlative + plural noun
  • "It is one of the nicest rooftops here." — the noun is plural because you are taking one out of several.

Three traps. One superlative is enough: not "the most best", just the best. The word that opens your life is ever, not never: "the worst film I have ever seen". And once ever is in the sentence you are ranking everything, so it takes the superlative, not the comparative: not "the better rooftop I have ever been to", but the best.

Stage 2: From the famous one to the honest one

Order

This is how you answer "what should I see?" — you knock the famous one down first, then put yours at the top and say what it costs. Put the four lines in that order.

?"Go to the rooftop on the old bank instead — best view I have ever seen."
?"The shopping street is by far the most overrated place here."
?"You wait forty minutes, but it is worth it."
?"I went in January and it let me down completely."

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Say each one twice: once slowly, once at speaking pace. In the first two, ever and far carry the stress — the BEST museum I've EVer been to.

It's the best museum I've ever been to.
It's by far the most overrated place here.
It's one of the most underrated museums in the city.
It turned out to be a hidden gem.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
You have been to a lot of museums in your life, and this one beats all of them. Which sentence says that?
A "It's the most best museum I've seen."
B "It's the best museum I've ever been to."
C "It's the better museum I've never been to."
There are four or five great rooftops in your city and this is one of them — not the winner. Which sentence is correct?
A "It's one of the nicest rooftop in the city."
B "It's one of the nicest rooftops in the city."
C "It's one of the nicest rooftops of the city ever."
The famous market was bad, and nothing else you saw came close to being that bad. How do you say it?
A "It's by far the worst market in the city."
B "It's very the worst market in the city."
C "It's the most worst market by far in the city."
You need the English word for a place where people go up to look at the whole city, and it will not come. What do you do?
A Say the word in Portuguese and hope they understand.
B Say "it's the place where you go up and see the whole city at once".
C Stop the sentence and talk about something else.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Somebody your age is coming to your city for six days and sends you a list of eleven places. In about ninety seconds, tell them what to cross off and what to put at the top instead — and you are not allowed to say nice or very good once. Name one place that is completely overrated, one that is underrated, and one that turned out to be better than you expected. Use the best… I have ever… at least twice, by far once, and one of the with a plural noun after it.
