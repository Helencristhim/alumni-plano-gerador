Chapter 4: The Toolbox

Three Lessons, One Ear

The tools of lessons 21 to 23, at real speed

Grammar Discovery

Which Tool Does the Speaker Use?

"What the agency wants is not higher tolls."

"It is likely that the consultation will be extended."

"As he pointed out, independence on paper is not enough."

"To recap, the goal is maintenance."

You learned the first three in lessons 21, 22 and 23. The red one is a signpost. For each one, ask: what does it tell me about the words that come next?

JobFormExample
Focus (lesson 21)What... is / It is... thatThe next words are the key idea.
Hedge (lesson 22)likely to / tend to / to some extentThe next claim is not certain: note how sure.
Attribution (lesson 23)as X pointed out / according toThe next idea is someone else's, not the speaker's.
Order signpostfirst / second / finallyA new part of the talk starts.
Digression signposta quick digression / back to the main pointYou can relax, then focus again.
Summary signpostto recap / the bottom line isListen hard: this is the summary.

In one line: every tool is a warning about what comes next; listen for the tool, and you know where to put your attention.

Listening 1 · The Agency Director

Three Parts and a Digression

The director of the transport agency opens the public hearing. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the agency want, according to the director?
Not higher tolls: more investment in maintenance.
2. What topic does she call a digression?
The old contracts from the 1990s, which are not part of the proposal.
3. When does the public consultation close?
On November 30.
Listening 2 · The Moderator

Three Panelists, One Bottom Line

A moderator summarizes a panel on regulation and investment. Sound first — no text.

1. What did Professor Lima argue?
That independent regulators attract more private investment.
2. Why was Mr. Carter skeptical?
Because independence on paper is not the same as independence in practice.
3. What is the bottom line of the panel?
Rules matter, but people and history matter just as much.
Listening 3 · The Listening Coach

The Problem Is Not Speed

A coach explains why senior professionals lose track in meetings in English. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the real problem, according to the coach?
Not speed, but strategy: they try to understand every word.
2. Which signposts does she mention?
First, however, the key point is, and to recap.
3. What do expert listeners do during a digression?
They relax, because a digression is rarely the important part.
