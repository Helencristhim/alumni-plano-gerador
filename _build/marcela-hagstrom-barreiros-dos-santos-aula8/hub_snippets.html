Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Feedback -- comments about how well someone did something
"Good feedback is clear and specific."
To praise -- to say that someone did something well
"You should praise people in public."
To challenge -- to tell someone, directly, that something can be better
"A good boss challenges you, but with respect."
Candid -- honest and direct, with no hidden message
"She is always candid with her team."
A one-on-one -- a private meeting between a manager and one person
"We have a one-on-one every Friday."
To push back -- to say no, or to disagree, with arguments
"It is OK to push back when a plan is not good."
Trust -- the feeling that you can believe in someone
"Feedback works better when there is trust."
A team lead -- the person who leads a small team
"The team lead gives feedback every week."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Chris manages a team in Chicago. When he started, he never gave feedback. Now he has a one-on-one with each person every two weeks. His advice: “You should give feedback soon. You shouldn’t give negative feedback by email.”

Priya’s manager was candid with her. He praised her data, and then he challenged her: “The presentation was too long. You should start with the conclusion.”

He said it in private and with care, so Priya didn’t lose trust. Kim Scott’s advice is the same: praise in public, criticize in private.

1. How often does Chris have a one-on-one with each person?
A Every week.
B Every two weeks.
C Every month.
2. What did Priya’s manager praise?
A Her presentation.
B Her data.
C Her conclusion.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “You should to start with the conclusion.”
B “You should start with the conclusion.”
C “You shoulds start with the conclusion.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Should and Shouldn’t -- Advice and Instructions

GRAMMAR

Use should and shouldn’t + the simple verb to give advice. It is the same for everybody. For a direct instruction, use the verb alone: be specific, don’t wait.

JobFormExample
Adviceshould + verbYou should praise people in public.
Negative adviceshouldn’t + verbYou shouldn’t criticize in public.
Asking for adviceShould + I / we + verb?Should I give feedback by email?
Same form for everybodyno -s after he or sheShe should listen more. (not shoulds)
Instruction (imperative)verb · don’t + verbBe specific. Don’t wait too long.
The trapno to after shouldNot you should to praise. Say you should praise.

Never: You should to praise · She shoulds give · You don’t should criticize · You be specific! (as an instruction)

Stage 2: Put the Feedback in Order

Order

Put a good feedback conversation in a logical order.

?"Hi, do you have ten minutes? Can I give you some feedback?"
?"First, your analysis was excellent. The data was very clear."
?"But the report was late, and there were two errors in the numbers."
?"Next time, you should check the numbers with a colleague."
?"What do you think? Is there anything I can do to help?"
?"Thanks for listening. Let's talk again in our next one-on-one."
