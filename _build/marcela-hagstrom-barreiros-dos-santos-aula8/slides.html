Chapter 4: The Code

You Should, You Shouldn’t

Advice and instructions

Grammar Discovery

Four Pieces of Advice

"You should praise people in public."

"You shouldn’t criticize people in front of the team."

"Should I give feedback by email?"

"Be specific, and don’t wait too long."

Look at the red words. After should and shouldn’t, the blue verb is simple, with no to: should praise. In the question, should comes first. Sentence 4 is different: no should, no you. It is a direct instruction.

JobFormExample
Adviceshould + verbYou should praise people in public.
Negative adviceshouldn’t + verbYou shouldn’t criticize in public.
Asking for adviceShould + I / we + verb?Should I give feedback by email?
Same form for everybodyno -s after he or sheShe should listen more. (not shoulds)
Instruction (imperative)verb · don’t + verbBe specific. Don’t wait too long.
The trapno to after shouldNot you should to praise. Say you should praise.

In one line: should and shouldn’t + simple verb for advice; the verb alone for a direct instruction.
