Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A board member -- one of the people who sit on the board and take the decisions
"If the only thing a board member repeats afterwards is your headline, you did the job."
A stakeholder -- anybody the decision affects, inside or outside the company
"The distributor is a stakeholder even though nobody invites him to the meeting."
A fiscal year -- the twelve months a company uses for its accounts, which may not start in January
"We are going to hold the price through the fiscal year."
A projection -- a number for a period that has not happened yet
"The projection for January is a range, not a single number."
A takeaway -- the one thing you want the room to remember afterwards
"The takeaway is that we are three points behind, and we are behind on purpose."
A headline -- the one sentence at the top that says the whole thing
"The presentations we remember all have a headline that survives the meeting."
A range -- two numbers instead of one, when the period has not finished yet
"Between twenty-two and twenty-five. A single number there would be decoration."
To flag -- to point at a problem early, so that nobody is surprised later
"A board can work with a problem it heard about in October."
A dry run -- a full practice of the presentation, out loud, before the real one
"One dry run, out loud, with somebody interrupting. Reading it in your head does not count."
Quarter on quarter -- comparing one three-month period with the one immediately before it
"The first quarter on quarter movement was slower than the projection."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Nothing here is new, and that is the point. A board meeting has four parts and each one has its own tense. The numbers come first, and they are comparisons: coverage is lower than the target, and the gap is the widest in the south. Then the recommendation, which is should when the room may argue and must not when it may not: we should hold the price, and we must not buy the gap with a discount.

Then what happened, which needs two past tenses at once. The background takes was or were plus -ing, and the thing that cut into it takes the past simple: we were visiting the second wave of workshops when the supplier stopped. After that, where things stand today, which is am, is or are plus -ing: the rollout is running a week behind. And then what comes next, which is going to because it is already decided: we are going to hold the price through the fiscal year.

Two more things carry the whole thing. The first is the verb that follows a verb, and the first one decides: I recommend giving a range rather than a single number, and I plan to finish two minutes early. The second is what you say when somebody interrupts, which is not an answer at all but four seconds bought out loud. Give the headline before anybody asks for it, flag the bad news yourself, tell the board member what the takeaway is, and do one dry run before the real thing.

1. Which tense does the text give to "where things stand today"?
A The past simple.
B Am, is or are plus -ing.
C Going to.
2. Why does "we were visiting the second wave when the supplier stopped" use two different past forms?
A Because the visiting was the background and the supplier stopping cut into it.
B Because there are two different subjects in the sentence.
C Because one is positive and the other is negative.
3. What does the text say decides whether the second verb takes -ing or to?
A Whether the sentence is about the past or the future.
B The length of the second verb.
C The first verb: recommend takes -ing, plan takes to.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Map of a Board Meeting

GRAMMAR

Each part of a board meeting has its own tense, and you have had all of them for a while.

Where in the meetingStructureExample
The numberscomparatives and superlativesCoverage is lower than the target.
The recommendationshould · must not · have toWe should hold the price. We must not discount.
What happenedpast simple, with past continuous for the backgroundWe were visiting when the supplier stopped.
Where we are nowam / is / are + -ingThe rollout is running a week behind.
What comes nextgoing toWe are going to hold the price.
Running the roomverb + -ing or verb + to (the first verb decides)I recommend giving a range. I plan to finish early.
The interruptionthe time-buying sentences"Let me make sure I have that right."

Never: more lower than the target · we must to hold the price · I recommend to give a range · we visited when the supplier stopped · a single number for a month that has not happened.
