Complementary Activities

Optional extras to enjoy outside class. Mark each one as done after you watch or listen to it.

Podcast
Life Kit (NPR) — "To give better advice, try less fixing and more listening"

A whole episode built on the grammar of this lesson: when to say should, when to open a door instead, and why the person who receives advice almost never does what they are told. Fifteen minutes, and the examples are ordinary life, not law.

Tip: the transcript is on the same page. Find three sentences where the speaker softens a recommendation, and copy the exact words.

Listen on NPR ↗
Talk
Ruth Chang — "How to make hard choices" (TED)

A philosopher on the question this lesson is named after. Her argument: a hard choice is not hard because you lack information — it is hard because neither option is better, and that changes what advice can even do for you.

Tip: fifteen minutes, clear speech, subtitles available. Afterwards, say out loud which of your two programs is the hard choice, and why.

Watch on TED ↗
Documentary
WGCU Public Media (PBS) — "Dream School: A Journey to Higher Ed"

Admissions staff and counsellors telling applicants what they should do and what they could do — letters, essays, deadlines and the weeks of silence afterwards. The vocabulary of this lesson, spoken by the people who actually read the applications.

Tip: every time somebody gives advice, pause and say the same advice back in your own words, with should or could.
