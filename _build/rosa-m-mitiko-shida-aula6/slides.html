Chapter 3: The Code

The Polite Form of an Opinion

If I were · I would

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"If I were the regional manager, I would restructure the renewal process."

"If we lost that account, our market share would drop in Chile."

"If I had one more analyst, I would take the Chile accounts tomorrow."

"If I were you, I would call them before the renewal, not after."

The colored verbs look like the past. None of these sentences is about the past. So what are they about?

FormWhat it saysExample
If + past simple,
would + base verb		An imagined situation: not true now, or unlikely. The past form is a signal of distance, not of time."If I had one more analyst, I would take Chile."
If I wereWith I, he, she and it, this structure uses were, not was. It is fixed, and it is what sounds professional."If I were you…"
"If she were in my position…"
First vs secondFirst conditional = real and likely. Second = imagined or unlikely. Same situation, different distance."If the board approves it, we will open."
"If the board approved it, we would open."
could · mightSwap would for could (ability) or might (possibility) to soften the result even more."If we streamlined it, renewals could take a week."
Why it matters at workThis is how you disagree without confrontation and propose without demanding. It puts the idea on the table and leaves the decision with the other person."If I were you, I would call them first."
NOT "You have to call them first."

One shape, two halves: past in the condition, would in the result. And with this structure, I were — never I was.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"If I would be the regional manager, I would restructure the process."
"If I were the regional manager, I would restructure the process."
"If I was you, I would call them before the renewal."
"If I were you, I would call them before the renewal."
"If we lose that account, our market share would drop."
"If we lost that account, our market share would drop."

Would never appears in the if half, and the two halves have to agree: past with would, present with will. Mixing them turns an idea into a promise, which is the expensive version of this mistake. And in this structure, English says if I were. Past in the condition, would in the result.

Two Pages, Two Ideas

D
Rosa, did you get the same request from the director's office? One page, informal, what would you change if the decision were yours.
R
I did. And the informal part is the difficult part. If he asked for a formal report, I would know exactly what to write.
D
That is the whole point, I think. He does not want the consultant version.
R
So what is yours? What would you change?
D
One thing. If I were in charge, I would give every account manager a real budget for retention. Right now, if a competitor calls my client, I have nothing to offer except a phone call.
R
That is a good one. In your position I would probably start somewhere else, though. If we streamlined the renewal approval, half of those calls would never happen.
D
Say that exactly like that on your page. Not the version with an executive summary. That version.
R
Then send me yours before the end of the month and I will send you mine. If we read each other's first, neither of us would write something safe.
Comprehension

About Diego

1. What request did he receive from the director's office?
One informal page about what he would change if the decision were his.
2. What would he change if he were in charge?
He would give every account manager a real budget for retention.
3. What does he say he has to offer today when a competitor calls his client?
Nothing except a phone call.
4. What advice does he give her about how to write her page?
To write it exactly as she said it, not the version with an executive summary.
Listening

One Page, Informal

A voice message from the regional director's office. Sound first — no text.

1. What exactly has the director asked four account managers for?
One informal page answering what they would change if the decision were theirs.
2. Why does he not want a formal document?
Because if he wanted one he would ask the consultants, and he already knows what they would say.
3. What is the alternative if she would rather not write it?
To call the office and book twenty minutes to talk it through with him.
Listening 2

Fifteen Seconds and Three Weeks

A colleague on why strategy documents say nothing. Sound first — no text.

1. How long does he say it takes to get a clear answer in conversation?
About fifteen seconds.
2. What happens when the same person has to write it down?
It takes three weeks and comes back as nothing.
3. What would he change about how the company plans?
He would ask people the question in the corridor and write down what they actually say.
