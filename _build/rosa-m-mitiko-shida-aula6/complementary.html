Lesson 6 -- What Would You Do?

Podcast & Reading
"How to use classic negotiation tactics in everyday life" -- Life Kit, NPR

Twenty minutes on asking for what you want without turning the conversation into a fight. Connection to Lesson 6: the second conditional is the grammar of exactly that — you put the idea on the table and leave the decision with the other person. Full transcript on the page.

Tip: read the transcript and find the three sentences you would actually reuse at work. Rewrite each one as a second conditional and say it out loud. Send the page to your Kindle for the flight.

Talk
"How to make choosing easier" -- Sheena Iyengar, TED

Sixteen minutes from a professor who studies choice about why more options make decisions worse, and what to cut. Connection to Lesson 6: this is the argument you would use if somebody asked you to simplify a process instead of adding one.

Tip: watch it once with English subtitles. Then take one of her four rules and say, out loud, what would happen at your company if you applied it. One sentence, second conditional.

Grammar Video
"The second conditional" -- BBC Learning English, 6 Minute Grammar

Six minutes on the structure of this lesson, including the were that everybody gets wrong. Connection to Lesson 6: the past form in the condition is not about time, it is about distance from reality.

Tip: pause after each example and say the same idea twice about your own work — once as a first conditional (real and likely) and once as a second (imagined). Hearing the two next to each other is what fixes the difference.
