Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

What Would You Do? -- The Memo Nobody Asked For

The second conditional, for the ideas you have never said out loud at work. Key expressions: to restructure, strategy, competitor, advantage, market share, to outsource, stakeholder, to prioritize, revenue, to streamline, workaround, to push back. Structure: if plus past simple, would plus base verb -- the polite form of an opinion, and if I were, never if I was.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. These twelve stay here in your material -- they do not disappear after the class.

To restructure -- to change how a company or a team is organized
"If I could restructure one area, it would be the renewal process."
Strategy -- the long-term plan a company follows to reach its goals
"The strategy session is in December, and he wants one page from each of us."
Competitor -- another company that sells the same thing to the same clients
"I know exactly which competitor is calling that client."
Advantage -- something that puts you in a better position than others
"Our real advantage is that we answer the phone on a Friday evening."
Market share -- the part of all the sales in a market that belongs to one company
"If we lost that account, our market share in Chile would drop."
To outsource -- to pay another company to do work your own team used to do
"If we outsourced the reporting, we would lose the detail."
Stakeholder -- a person or a group with an interest in a decision
"If I were leading this, I would talk to the stakeholders first."
To prioritize -- to decide what to do first when you cannot do everything
"If I had to prioritize, I would start with the renewals."
Revenue -- the money a company receives from its sales
"Twelve clients bring in half the revenue of my portfolio."
To streamline -- to make a process simpler and faster by removing steps
"If we streamlined the approval, renewals would take a week."
Workaround -- a temporary way of solving a problem when the proper fix is not available
"Every team has a workaround, and nobody has written it down."
To push back -- to say politely that you do not agree, or to ask for more time
"If I pushed back on that date, would anybody actually mind?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The regional director has asked four account managers for one page each, and the request is unusual: informal, no strategy language, one question only. What would you change if the decision were yours? He said that if he wanted a formal document he would ask the consultants, and that he already knows what the consultants would say.

Rosa knows her answer in about fifteen seconds, which is the strange part. If she were in charge of the commercial area, she would streamline the renewal approval. Today a renewal takes a month, and a month is long enough for a competitor to call her client twice. If the approval took a week instead, half of those calls would never happen.

She would not restructure the whole area, and she would not outsource anything. If the company outsourced the reporting, it would lose the detail that makes her useful to a client. If she had to prioritize one number, it would be the twelve accounts that bring in half the revenue of her portfolio, and if she lost two of those, the market share would move before anybody upstairs noticed.

The difficult part is not the idea. It is writing it as she would say it. Every team has a workaround for the slow approval and nobody has written it down, which is her real advantage in this exercise. If she talked to two other stakeholders first, she would have three examples instead of one. And if the date is too tight, she can always push back and ask for a week more.

1. Why does the text say "if she were in charge" and not "if she was"?
A Because it is a plural subject.
B Because this structure uses were for I, he, she and it.
C Because it happened in the past.
2. "If the approval took a week instead, half of those calls would never happen." Is this likely or imagined?
A Imagined -- it is not the situation today.
B Likely -- it is going to happen next week.
C It already happened last year.
3. What is the difference between "if the board approves it, we will open" and "if the board approved it, we would open"?
A The first is about the past and the second about the future.
B The first is real and likely; the second is imagined or unlikely.
C There is no difference; both are correct in any situation.
4. The text says every team has a workaround. What is a workaround?
A The official process, approved by the director.
B A temporary way around a problem, when the proper fix is not available.
C A meeting to discuss a problem.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Second Conditional

GRAMMAR

How English says what you would do, without saying that you are going to do it. At work this is the polite form of an opinion -- it puts your idea on the table and leaves the decision with the other person.

FormWhat it saysExample
If + past simple,
would + base verb		An imagined situation: not true now, or unlikely. The past form signals distance, not time."If I had one more analyst, I would take Chile."
If I wereWith I, he, she and it, this structure uses were, not was. It is fixed, and it is what sounds professional."If I were you…"
NOT "If I was you…"
First vs secondFirst = real and likely. Second = imagined or unlikely. Same situation, different distance."If the board approves it, we will open."
"If the board approved it, we would open."
could · mightSwap would for could (ability) or might (possibility) to soften the result even more."If we streamlined it, renewals could take a week."
Why it matters at workIt is how you disagree without confrontation and propose without demanding."If I were you, I would call them first."
NOT "You have to call them first."

The two halves have to agree: past with would, present with will. Mixing them turns an idea into a promise, which is the expensive version of this mistake.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

The regional director wants your opinion and a colleague wants to change something you disagree with. Choose the best thing to say.

1. He asks what you would change if the decision were yours. You say:
A If I would be in charge, I would streamline the renewals.
B If I were in charge, I would streamline the renewal approval.
C If I am in charge, I would streamline the renewal approval.
2. You want to warn him about the risk of losing an account. You say:
A If we lose that account, our market share would drop.
B If we lost that account, our market share dropped.
C If we lost that account, our market share would drop.
3. A colleague asks for your advice about a client. You say:
A If I was you, I would call them before the renewal.
B If I were you, I would call them before the renewal.
C If I am you, I will call them before the renewal.
4. You disagree with outsourcing the reporting. The most professional way to say it is:
A That is a bad idea and it will not work.
B If we outsourced the reporting, we would lose the detail the clients pay us for.
C I do not agree, but you decide.
5. The deadline is too tight and you need a week more. You say:
A I would rather push back on the date than promise something we cannot do.
B I push back the date because it is impossible.
C If I would push back, the date will change.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer, and nobody is scoring it.

Think about one thing you would genuinely change at your company if the decision were yours, and record about ninety seconds saying it. Give four sentences: what you would change, what would happen as a result, what would happen if nothing changed, and the one resource you would need. Use if I were at least once, and keep every sentence short enough to finish.
