Chapter 3: The Code

Do You Want, or Would You Like?

The small words that make an offer sound warm

Grammar Discovery

Which One Is Not an Offer?

"Would you like some more rice?"

"Shall I take your coat?"

"Would you like me to bring something?"

"Do you like spicy food?"

Three of them offer something: a plate, a hand, a favor. The blue one only asks about taste — nobody gets anything.

UseFormExample
Offer a thingWould you like + noun?Would you like some wine?
InviteWould you like + to + verb?Would you like to come over on Saturday?
Offer to do it yourselfShall I + base verb?Shall I take your coat?
Ask if they want you toWould you like + me + to + verb?Would you like me to bring something?
Suggest togetherShall we + base verb?Shall we sit down?
Accept / refuseYes, please · That would be lovely · No, thanks, I’m fine · I’d love to, but...No, thanks — I’m fine for now.

In one line: would you like is an offer; do you like is a question about taste.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Saturday, Seven O’Clock

One dinner and three voices around it

Dialogue

Thomas at the Door

T
Hi, Gisele. Thanks so much for having me over. Claire is really sorry — our son has a fever, so tonight it’s just me.
G
Oh no, I hope he feels better soon. Come in, please. Shall I take your coat? And make yourself at home.
T
Thank you. I brought a bottle of wine. I know you said it wasn’t a potluck, but I couldn’t come with empty hands.
G
That’s very kind. Would you like a glass now, or would you prefer something without alcohol?
T
A glass of wine would be lovely. Oh, and I don’t eat meat. I should have said it when I RSVPed.
G
You did, last week. There’s a vegetable lasagna, and a dietary restriction is never a problem in this house. Shall we sit down?
T
This is amazing, Gisele. Would you mind if I had seconds?
G
Please, help yourself. And would you like me to pack some leftovers for Claire? There’s far too much.
Comprehension

About Thomas

1. Why is Thomas alone tonight?
His wife, Claire, stayed home because their son has a fever.
2. What does Thomas bring, and why, if it is not a potluck?
A bottle of wine. He knows it is not a potluck, but he could not come with empty hands.
3. What does Thomas not eat, and what does he think he forgot to do?
He does not eat meat. He thinks he forgot to say it when he RSVPed, but he told Gisele last week.
Listening 1 · The Rules

What Nobody Tells Newcomers

A neighbor explains how a dinner invitation works here. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Why does she say a maybe is worse than a no?
Because the host plans the food around your answer. With a maybe, the host cannot plan anything.
2. What should you do if the host says just bring yourself?
Bring something small anyway: a bottle of wine, flowers or a dessert. Never arrive with empty hands.
3. What does help yourself really mean here, and what is the signal that dinner is over?
Nobody is going to fill your plate for you. If you want seconds, you get up and take them. Around ten, people say well, this was lovely, and that is the signal to go home.

Listening 3 · An Opinion

Stop Apologizing for the Rice

Twenty years of having people over, in two minutes. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she say happens every time a host apologizes?
The guests have to stop and say no, no, it is perfect. The host is giving them a job.
2. If the host’s job is not perfection, what is it?
Attention: notice the empty glass and offer a refill, and notice the quiet person and ask them a question.
3. What do people remember, and what was the best dinner she ever went to?
How they felt at the table, not the food. The best one was soup and bread in a kitchen with four chairs and six people.
