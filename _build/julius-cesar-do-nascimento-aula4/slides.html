Chapter 4: The Code

I Had Been... I Was...

Four tenses, one story, one clear order

Grammar Discovery

What Happened First?

"I had been traveling for almost thirty hours."

"While they were explaining the numbers, I was trying not to fall asleep."

"I had never eaten anything so fresh."

"The next morning, we signed the agreement."

A story has a main line of events (signed). Around it, there is the background: what was in progress (were explaining, was trying). And there is an earlier past: what happened before the story (had never eaten) or what had been going on for some time up to that moment (had been traveling).

JobFormExample
Past simplethe main events, in orderWe signed the agreement.
Past continuouswas / were + verb-ing: the background, an action in progressWhile they were explaining the numbers, I...
Past perfecthad + past participle: an action before another past momentI had never eaten anything so fresh.
Past perfect continuoushad been + verb-ing: how long, up to a past momentI had been traveling for thirty hours.
Linkerswhen, while, by the time, before, afterBy the time we left, we had stopped talking about numbers.
The trapuse the past perfect only for the earlier action, not for every past actionNot “I had arrived and I had gone to the hotel.” Say I arrived and went to the hotel.

In one line: main event = signed; background = was trying; before = had eaten; how long before = had been traveling.

Listening 1 · An American Food Writer

My First Sushi Counter

An American food writer remembers all the mistakes of her first dinner at a sushi counter in Tokyo. Sound first — no text.

1. What had she done on the plane?
She had read a guidebook.
2. What was she doing while the chef was preparing the first piece?
She was taking photos with her phone.
3. Why did the chef decide she was a good student?
Because she had asked so many questions.
Listening 2 · An Italian Chef

From Bologna to Osaka

An Italian chef remembers the year she worked in a small restaurant in Japan. Sound first — no text.

1. How old was she when she went to Osaka?
Twenty-six.
2. What did she do in the first three months?
She washed rice, cleaned the counter and watched; she did not touch any fish.
3. According to her, what do Italian and Japanese food have in common?
Few ingredients, very fresh, and a lot of respect.
Listening 3 · A Dutch Manager

The Night the Room Went Quiet

A Dutch manager remembers his first business dinner in Japan, fifteen years ago. Sound first — no text.

1. How many people were at the table?
Twelve.
2. What did he do wrong?
He filled his own glass with beer; in Japan you do not pour your own drink.
3. What did his colleague do?
He picked up the bottle and filled everybody’s glass, and the conversation started again.
