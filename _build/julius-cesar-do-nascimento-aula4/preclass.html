Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A counter -- a long, narrow table where food is prepared and served in front of the guests
"We sat at the counter and watched the chef work."
A chef -- the main cook in a restaurant
"The chef had been working there for forty years."
A reservation -- an arrangement to have a table at a restaurant at a certain time
"Our host had made a reservation for eight o’clock."
Fresh -- recently caught, picked or made; not old and not frozen
"The fish was so fresh that it tasted of the sea."
Raw -- not cooked
"Most of the fish in sushi is raw."
A dish -- food that is prepared in a particular way, as part of a meal
"Every dish was smaller than the one before."
To recommend -- to say that something is good and that someone should try it
"What would you recommend tonight?"
A tasting menu -- a series of small dishes chosen by the chef
"There was no menu, only a tasting menu."
Jet lag -- the tired feeling after a long flight to a different time zone
"I had terrible jet lag on the first day."
A toast -- words that someone says before people drink together, to honor someone
"Our host raised his glass and made a toast to the partnership."
A host -- a person who invites guests and takes care of them
"Our host had chosen the restaurant himself."
Seasonal -- available only at a certain time of the year
"The menu is seasonal, so it changes every few weeks."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last spring, Julius flew to Tokyo for a meeting at headquarters. He had been traveling for more than a day, and he had terrible jet lag. That evening, his host took him to a small restaurant. The host had made a reservation weeks before.

They sat at the counter. While the chef was preparing the first dish, the two men were talking about their families. There was only a tasting menu, and everything was seasonal. Julius had eaten sushi many times in Brazil, but he had never tried raw fish so fresh.

At the end, the host made a toast to the new project. The next day, Julius recommended the restaurant to all his colleagues.

1. Why does the text say “The host had made a reservation” and not “The host made a reservation”?
A Because the reservation happened before the dinner, an earlier past.
B Because the past perfect is the only form that we use for restaurants, hotels and other reservations in English.
C Because the reservation is happening now.
2. What were the two men doing while the chef was preparing the first dish?
A They were reading the long menu of the restaurant and choosing the dishes for the whole evening.
B They were sleeping.
C They were talking about their families.
3. Had Julius eaten sushi before that night?
A No, never, because before that trip he had never been to a Japanese restaurant, in Brazil or abroad.
B Yes, many times in Brazil, but never fish so fresh.
C Only at home.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Narrative Tenses

GRAMMAR

Past simple for the main events. Past continuous (was / were + -ing) for the background. Past perfect (had + past participle) for something that happened before. Past perfect continuous (had been + -ing) for how long something had been going on.

JobFormExample
Past simplethe main events, in orderWe signed the agreement.
Past continuouswas / were + verb-ing: the background, an action in progressWhile they were explaining the numbers, I...
Past perfecthad + past participle: an action before another past momentI had never eaten anything so fresh.
Past perfect continuoushad been + verb-ing: how long, up to a past momentI had been traveling for thirty hours.
Linkerswhen, while, by the time, before, afterBy the time we left, we had stopped talking about numbers.
The trapuse the past perfect only for the earlier action, not for every past actionNot “I had arrived and I had gone to the hotel.” Say I arrived and went to the hotel.

Never: When I arrived, the meeting already started · I never had eaten... · I was traveling for thirty hours when I arrived · While we were eat...

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

A colleague asks why you were so tired at the dinner. You say:
A "I had been traveling for thirty hours."
B "I was traveling during thirty hours before the dinner, and in the plane I didn’t sleep."
C "I have been traveling for thirty hours."
You want to explain that you saw raw fish like this for the first time. You say:
