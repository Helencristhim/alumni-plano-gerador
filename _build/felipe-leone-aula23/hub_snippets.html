Five Adjectives to Describe People -- What They Are, How They Work

Ten to twenty minutes before class. The difference between what a person is (adjective) and how the person does things (adverb), and adjectives made with -ed. Key words: confident, patient, reliable, demanding, open-minded, big-hearted.

Vocabulary

Patient -- able to wait or to explain things again without getting angry
"He is patient with new analysts."
Reliable -- a person that you can trust to do the work
"Our data team is very reliable."
Demanding -- a person who expects a lot of work and quality from others
"My new director is demanding but fair."

"The client was open-minded about the new model."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Carla manages a team of six people at a credit company in Miami. Her director asks her to describe the team. She says: “Tom is careful. He checks every report carefully. Ana is calm, and she talks to angry clients calmly.”

Then she talks about the new analyst: “Leo is very open-minded and hard-working. He learns quickly.” Her director is happy: the description is short and clear.

Why does Carla say "Tom checks every report carefully"?
A Because the director asked a question.
B Because Tom is a careful person in general.
C Because she is talking about how he does the work.
Which word describes a person who listens to new ideas?
A hard-working
B open-minded
C careful

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Adjective or Adverb?

GRAMMAR

What a person is: adjective. How a person does something: adverb (often -ly).

JobFormExample
What the person isbe + adjectiveShe is confident.
How the person does itverb + adverbShe speaks confidently.
Two words, one adjectiveadjective + noun + -edbig heart → big-hearted
A good adverb with goodwell (not goodly)He works well under pressure.

Never: She speaks confident · He works very good · She is very open-mind
