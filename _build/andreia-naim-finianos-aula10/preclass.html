Lesson 10 -- Pre-class

Connecting the Dots -- Describing the Client You Actually Want

A company that cannot describe its best client in one sentence cannot find the next one. Key words: a segment, a use case, a reference, to tailor, a pain threshold, a champion, churn, a footprint, a case study, to narrow down, to move the needle, right in the sweet spot. Structure: non-defining relative clauses and the relative adverbs where and whose.

Stage 1: Words · 2 min

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A segment -- a group of clients similar enough to be sold to in the same way
"Our best segment is state groups whose filing is monthly."
A use case -- one specific situation in which a client actually uses the product
"The use case we lead with is always the audit."
A reference -- a client who agrees to talk to another client about working with you
"That account, which took nine months to close, is now our strongest reference."
To tailor -- to change something so that it fits one client instead of all of them
"We tailor the deck to one person, and we say who that person is."
A pain threshold -- the point at which a problem stops being tolerable and somebody acts
"Their pain threshold is the audit, not the price."
A champion -- the person inside the client who argues for you when you are not in the room
"The champion is almost never the person who signs."
Churn -- the share of clients who leave over a period
"They send the same deck to everybody, and then explain the churn as a market problem."
A footprint -- how much of a market, or how many countries, a company is present in
"Sao Paulo, where most of our footprint is, is not the whole market."
A case study -- a short written story of one client, used to sell to the next one
"One good case study is worth more than any number of features."
To narrow down -- to reduce a long list to the few that are actually worth the time
"Each clause narrows the list down until what is left is findable."
To move the needle -- to make a difference big enough to show up in the numbers
"Another feature will not move the needle. A named champion will."
Right in the sweet spot -- exactly the client the product was made for, with nothing to adapt
"A group of that size, with an internal auditor, is right in the sweet spot."

Stage 3: Grammar Tip -- Non-Defining Relative Clauses, where and whose · 2 min

GRAMMAR

Read the rule, then answer three quick questions.

Never: Our account, that renewed in March · The company who signed last week · Chen whose team runs the audit is our champion (Chen is already identified — he needs the commas) · the director where we lost the deal

1. Why does "the groups whose filing is monthly" have no commas?
A Because whose never takes commas.
B Because the clause narrows the list — take it away and the sentence stops pointing at anybody in particular.
C Because the sentence is too short for commas.
2. "Our biggest account, which renewed in March, is right in the sweet spot." Why the commas here?
A Because which always takes commas.
B Because the sentence has more than ten words.
C Because you already know which account is meant, so the clause only adds a detail.
3. A team cannot describe its clients. What usually happens?
A It sends the same deck to everybody and calls its churn a market problem.
B It loses its best people.
C It builds too many features and runs out of money.

Stage 4: Use It · 3 min

Practice

Tap the words that complete each sentence, then choose what a real speaker would say.

"Our biggest account, renewed in March, is right in the sweet spot."
"That is the meeting we found the pattern."
A new colleague asks who he should be calling. You say:
A "Big companies that have money."
B "Our best segment is groups whose filing is monthly and who already have an internal auditor."
C "Anyone who answers the phone, honestly."
You want to add a detail about an account both of you already know. You say:
A "Our biggest account that renewed in March is a state group."
B "Our biggest account, that renewed in March, is a state group."
C "Our biggest account, which renewed in March, is a state group."
An investor says your description of the market fits half the companies in Brazil. The right move is:
A Narrow it down with one more clause, until what is left is findable.
B Repeat it more slowly.
C Change the subject to the product roadmap.

Stage 5: Say It · 2 min

Speaking

Listen and record two sentences, then speak freely for thirty seconds.

Our best segment is groups whose filing is monthly.
One good case study would move the needle more than another feature.
In thirty seconds, describe the client you actually want. Narrow it down: which segment is right in the sweet spot, who is usually your champion, what your churn tells you, and which case study would move the needle. Use which, whose or where at least twice.
