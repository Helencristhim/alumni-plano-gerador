Chapter 4: The Comma That Decides

Same Words. One Comma.

And a completely different meaning

Grammar Discovery

Which One Does She Actually Mean?

"The client that renewed last month is in the sweet spot."

"The client, which renewed last month, is in the sweet spot."

"Our best segment is the state groups, whose filing is monthly."

"That is the meeting where we found the pattern."

The first two sentences have exactly the same words. In one of them you have several clients and you are saying which one. In the other you have one, and you are just adding something about it. The commas are the only difference.

The trapthat cannot take commasnot the client, that renewed,

In one line: no commas narrows the list; commas just add a detail. If you can delete the clause and the sentence still points at the same thing, it takes commas.

Two Columns

Narrowing, or Adding?

NO COMMAS — it narrows the list

"The clients that renew twice are the segment."

"The groups whose filing is monthly convert fastest."

"The use case that opens doors is the audit."

"The person who signs is not the champion."

TWO COMMAS — it just adds a detail

"Our biggest account, which renewed in March, is a state group."

"Chen, whose team runs the audit, is our champion there."

"They narrowed the list to eleven, which nobody expected."

"Sao Paulo, where most of our footprint is, is not the whole market."

The test: take the clause out. If the sentence still points at the same client, it takes commas. If you no longer know who you meant, it does not.

Listening 1 · A Head of Sales

Twenty Accounts on a Wall

The afternoon a team found out what their best clients had in common. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the team believe their segment was, and what was it actually?
They believed it was large companies. It was actually companies with an internal auditor — some of them were small.
2. Why does he say the size was the wrong answer?
Because size was the easy thing to see, not the thing that made them buy. The auditor was what created the pain threshold.
3. What changed in the way they worked after that afternoon?
They stopped sending the same deck to everybody and started asking one question early — whether there is an internal auditor — which told them whether to invest the time at all.
Listening 2 · An Account Director

The Person Who Signs Is Not the Person You Are Selling To

Somebody who lost a deal she had already won. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say she lost the deal even though the client liked the product?
Because she had spent nine months with the person who signs and nobody one level below, so when the budget was cut there was nobody inside the company arguing for her.
2. What does she say a champion actually does?
They defend the project in the meetings you are not invited to — which are the meetings where the decision is really made.
3. What is the question she now asks in every first meeting?
Who else is going to be affected by this — because the answer gives her the name of the person she has to win over.
