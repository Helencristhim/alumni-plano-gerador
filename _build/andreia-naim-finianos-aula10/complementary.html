Lesson 10 -- Connecting the Dots

Podcast
NPR Life Kit -- "The connecting power of magical questions"

The account director in class said the whole difference was one question she now asks in every first meeting. This episode is about exactly that: the questions that make somebody tell you what actually matters to them, instead of the answer they give everybody. American English, conversational speed, around twenty minutes.

Tip: listen once without the transcript, then read it and pick the two questions you could ask a client next week. Say them out loud in your own words.

YouTube
Y Combinator -- "How to Talk to Users" (Startup School, 28 min)

Twenty-eight minutes on how to find out who your client really is and what actually hurts them — the spoken version of today’s lesson, from people who watch hundreds of companies get this wrong. The whole talk is built on the vocabulary you learned: segment, use case, pain, the person who signs and the person who feels the problem.

Tip: watch with English subtitles on. Stop at the section on bad questions and write two of your own sales questions again, in a way that cannot be answered with yes.

Film
The Social Dilemma (Netflix, 94 min)

The uncomfortable other side of segmentation: what happens when a company describes its users so precisely that it can predict them. The people talking are the ones who built the systems, and the language they use for a person — a segment, a cohort, a use case — is the same language you practised today. American English, several speakers, natural speed.

Tip: watch the first half with English subtitles. Note three sentences where somebody describes a group of people, and rewrite each one as a relative clause of your own.
