Chapter 4: The Code

Who Did It? It Does Not Matter

The passive, the way news writers use it

Grammar Discovery

Who Did It? Does It Matter?

"Most of the world’s aluminum is produced in China."

"Several smelters have been closed because of energy costs."

"A new tariff is being discussed in Washington."

"Prices are expected to rise again next year."

Who produces the aluminum? Who closed the smelters? Who is discussing the tariff? The text does not say, because it does not matter. Look at the blue words: a form of be and a past participle. Only be changes with the time. And the red words: who expects?

JobFormExample
Facts, in generalis / are + past participleAluminum is produced in China.
Up to nowhas / have been + past participleSmelters have been closed.
Right nowis / are being + past participleA tariff is being discussed.
Finished, in the pastwas / were + past participleThe report was published yesterday.
What people thinkis expected / said / believed to + verbPrices are expected to rise.
The trapthe passive always needs beNot the price set every day. Say the price is set every day.

In one line: be (in the right tense) + past participle; use it when the action matters more than who did it.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Three Voices From the Market

A price alert and three recordings

Real Document

The Market on One Screen

Reason 1A smelter has been closed in Europe (energy costs)

ActionKeep an eye on the price daily; do not stockpile yet
Market alerts are written almost only in the passive: has been closed, are expected, are removed. The person who did it is not important; the price is.
1. Which line describes something happening right now?
Being discussed: a new import tariff in two countries.
2. What could push prices down?
A surplus in China, if energy limits are removed.
3. If you received this alert, would you stockpile now? Why?
Open answer. For example: if I were you, I would wait, because the market is volatile.
Listening 1 · The Weekly Report

The Market This Week

An Indian commodity analyst gives his weekly report on base metals. Sound first — no text.

1. What happened to the aluminum price this week?
It soared by about six percent.
2. What two reasons does he give?
A smelter was closed in Europe and stocks in London have been falling.
3. What is expected for next month?
Prices are expected to stay high, but the market is volatile.
Listening 2 · The Buyer

A Buyer in Lyon

The purchasing manager of a French transformer factory explains how his company handles price changes. Sound first — no text.

1. How often does he check the price of aluminum?
Every morning, before anything else.
2. What did his company do before the tariff?
They stockpiled metal for three months.
3. How much of the increase did they pass on to clients?
About half.
Listening 3 · The Smelter

The View From the Smelter

A sales manager from an aluminum smelter in China talks to foreign buyers at a fair. Sound first — no text.

1. How much aluminum is produced at his company every year?
About one million tons.
2. Why has production been limited?
Because a limit on energy use has been set by the government in his region.
3. What does he expect if the limit is removed?
A surplus, and lower prices.
