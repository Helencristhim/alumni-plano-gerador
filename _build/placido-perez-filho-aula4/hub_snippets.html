Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A commodity -- a basic product, like metal, oil or coffee, that is bought and sold in large quantities
"Aluminum is a commodity: its price is set by the market."
Demand -- how much of a product people want to buy
"Demand for aluminum is growing because of electric cars."
A shortage -- a situation when there is not enough of something
"The energy crisis caused a shortage of aluminum in Europe."
A surplus -- more of something than people need
"A surplus in China pushed prices down."
To soar -- to go up very quickly to a high level
"Prices soared after the tariffs were announced."
To plunge -- to go down very quickly
"Prices plunged when demand fell."
Volatile -- changing quickly and in a way that is hard to predict
"The market has been very volatile this year."
A tariff -- a tax that a government puts on imported products
"A new tariff was put on imported aluminum."
A forecast -- what experts say will probably happen in the future
"The forecast for next year is not very good."
To stockpile -- to buy and keep a large amount of something for the future
"Some buyers stockpiled metal before the tariff."
To pass on -- to make someone else pay a cost that you had to pay
"We had to pass on part of the increase to our clients."
To keep an eye on -- to watch something carefully, so you can act if it changes
"I keep an eye on the London price every morning."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Aluminum is a commodity, and its price is set every day in London. This year the market has been very volatile: prices soared in March and plunged in June.

Why? Several smelters have been closed in Europe, and a shortage was felt in many markets. A new tariff is being discussed, so some buyers stockpiled metal.

Demand is expected to grow. The forecast is uncertain, so companies keep an eye on the price and pass on only part of each increase.

1. In “several smelters have been closed”, who closed them?
A The text does not say, because it is not important here.
B The buyers.
C The government of London.
2. Why did some buyers stockpile metal?
A Because there is a surplus.
B Because a new tariff is being discussed.
C Because demand plunged.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “A new tariff is discussing.”
B “A new tariff being discussed.”
C “A new tariff is being discussed.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive in News -- Be + Past Participle

GRAMMAR

News uses the passive when the action matters more than who did it. Only be changes with the time; the past participle stays the same.

JobFormExample
Facts, in generalis / are + past participleAluminum is produced in China.
Up to nowhas / have been + past participleSmelters have been closed.
Right nowis / are being + past participleA tariff is being discussed.
Finished, in the pastwas / were + past participleThe report was published yesterday.
What people thinkis expected / said / believed to + verbPrices are expected to rise.
The trapthe passive always needs beNot the price set every day. Say the price is set every day.

Never: the price set every day · have been close · is discussing (for a tariff) · are expected rise
