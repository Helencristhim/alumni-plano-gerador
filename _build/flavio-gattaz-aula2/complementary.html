Podcast
How I Built This -- Tate's Bake Shop: Kathleen King

She founded a company at eleven, brought in two partners who then removed her from her own business, and started again. It is the clearest hour you will ever spend on why ownership and authority are not the same thing, told in plain American English by somebody who learned it the expensive way.

Tip: listen for the moment the partners come in. Note the two tenses she uses to tell it, and write one sentence about your company in each.

Film
The Founder (2016) -- John Lee Hancock

Two brothers build something remarkable and lose control of it to a partner who understood the paperwork better than they did. Every word in this lesson is in this film: the founders, the stake, the governance nobody wrote down, and the legacy that ends up with somebody else's name on it.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Stop after the contract scene and say, out loud, what the brothers should have written down and when.

Talk
TED -- Knut Haanaes, "Two reasons companies fail, and how to avoid them" (10 min)

A short talk on the trap that closes on companies which are good at what they already do: they keep doing it, and stop looking for the next thing. It is the argument behind the word you learned today for adding new kinds of work, and it takes ten minutes.

Tip: after the talk, write two sentences about your own company, one with a closed year and one with since, about something you started doing that you did not do before.
