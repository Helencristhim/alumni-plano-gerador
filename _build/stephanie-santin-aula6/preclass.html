Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

If I Had a Year, I Would Go

Imagining the research year abroad and the decisions around it. Key words: hypothesis, to imagine, to consider, scenario, challenge, to adjust, balance, trade-off. Structure: second conditional (if + past simple, would + verb).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These are the words of the conversation you have with yourself, not the one you have with a committee.

Hypothesis -- an idea you test to see if it is true
"My hypothesis is that the delay happens before the patient arrives."
To imagine -- to form a picture of something in your mind
"Imagine you had six months in a foreign research center."
To consider -- to think carefully about something before deciding
"I am considering two very different cities."
Scenario -- a situation that could happen
"In the best scenario I would leave in September."
Challenge -- a difficult situation that tests you
"The biggest challenge would be the language, not the medicine."
To adjust -- to change something slightly so that it works better
"I would have to adjust my whole routine."
Balance -- the right amount of two things that pull in different directions
"The hard part is the balance between work and family."
Trade-off -- something you give up in order to get something else
"Every scenario has a trade-off."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Marina Costa is a radiologist in Recife, and every January she has the same conversation with her husband. If they moved to Portugal, she would earn less and they would live in a smaller apartment. If they stayed, they would keep the house and the salary, but she would not have the research work she wants. She says the honest answer changes with the day. If she were ten years younger, she would go without thinking. If her mother were not ill, the decision would be different too. Every scenario has a trade-off, and she has stopped looking for the one that does not.
Why does the text say "If they moved" when it is about the future?
A Because they moved a long time ago
B Because the past form after if signals an imagined situation, not a plan
C Because moving is always in the past
What does "she has stopped looking for the one that does not" mean?
A She has stopped looking for a scenario with no trade-off
B She has stopped considering Portugal
C She has stopped talking to her husband about it

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Second Conditional

GRAMMAR

Use the second conditional for an imagined situation: if + past simple, then would + verb. The past form is not about the past — it is the signal that the situation is not real.

imagined conditionif + past simpleIf I had six months free...
imagined resultwould + base verb...I would spend them in a research center.
with the verb to bewere for every personIf I were you, I would apply this year.
real against imaginedfirst against second conditionalIf I pass, I will apply. / If I had time, I would apply.

Careful: would never goes in the if- half either. "If I would have time" is the same mistake as "if I will pass" — the modal always lives in the result half.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
A colleague asks about an imagined situation. Which sentence is correct?
A "If I would have more time, I would study every morning."
B "If I had more time, I would study every morning."
C "If I have more time, I would study every morning."
You want advice from someone with more experience. What do you ask?
A "What you would do if you were in my situation?"
B "What would you do if you were in my situation?"
C "What would you do if you would be in my situation?"
You are describing a real, possible plan for this year. Which sentence fits?
A "If I pass the exam, I will apply for the fellowship."
B "If I passed the exam, I would apply for the fellowship."
C "If I would pass the exam, I applied for the fellowship."
