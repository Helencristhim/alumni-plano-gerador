Chapter 3: The Code

A Past Form for a Future That Is Not Real

The signal English uses when nothing is scheduled

Discover the Rule

What Do These Have in Common?

If I had more time, I would study every morning.
If I lived in Maryland, I would visit the trauma center every week.
If I were not working with data, I would probably be back in surgery.
What would you do if you got the position tomorrow?
Click to reveal the rule
The verb after if is in the past — and none of these sentences is about the past. That is the whole point: the past form is the signal that the situation is imagined. Form: if + past simple, would + verb. Compare with last class: If I pass, I will apply is real and possible; If I had a free year, I would go is a picture in your head. With the verb to be, careful English uses were for every person: If I were you...

Chapter 4: The Imagined Year

Six Months, Twelve Months, or None

Everyone in this field has the same conversation with themselves

Listening 1

Would You Go Back?

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What would she do if someone offered her a research year abroad tomorrow?
She thinks she would say yes, but the answer is not simple.
2. Why does her daughter's age matter?
If the daughter were three, she would take her and the girl would learn the language in a month. At eleven it is different.
3. Under what condition would she go tomorrow?
If the offer came with six months instead of twelve.

Hypothetical question over lunch

Any City, Tomorrow

C
Stephanie, hypothetical question. If you could choose any city in the United States tomorrow, where would you go?
S
If it were only about the weather, I would choose Florida. But it is not.
C
So what would decide it?
S
The person. If my old professor were in Alaska, I would go to Alaska.
C
And your son? Would he go with you?
S
If the position lasted a year, yes. If it were three months, I would come back to him.
C
If I were you, I would ask for the short one first.
S
That is what everybody says. If the short one worked, the long one would become easier.
Comprehension

About Chris

1. What hypothetical question does Chris ask?
Which city in the United States she would choose if she could choose any city tomorrow.
2. What does he want to know about her son?
Whether he would go with her.
3. What advice does he give at the end?
That if he were her, he would ask for the short position first.
Chapter 5: Practice

Six Questions Nobody Schedules

The ones that start with if and never appear on a form

Listening 2

If I Were Starting Today

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What would he do first if he were starting the process today?
He would take the English exam, because it is the slowest part and everybody leaves it for the end.
2. Why would he write to the researcher instead of the department?
Because departments do not answer and people do.
3. What does he say about asking for three months instead of a year?
A short visit is much easier to approve, and if it goes well, the second one is almost automatic.
