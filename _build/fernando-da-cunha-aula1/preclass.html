Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

What You Actually Run -- Saying What the Company Does

Somebody at a trade fair asks what your company does, and every door after that one depends on the next two minutes. Key words: a group of companies, a managing director, a manufacturing plant, point-of-sale material, a retail chain, to supply somebody, to source something, a lead time, to scale up, in-house, a track record, to wear several hats. Structure: present perfect simple and present perfect continuous.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A group of companies -- several separate companies that are owned and directed together, as one business
"We are a group of companies: four in retail and one in transport."
A managing director -- the person who runs a company day to day and answers for the whole operation
"I am the managing director. If a plant stops, it is my problem."
A manufacturing plant -- a site with machines and a production line, where the products are actually made
"We have two manufacturing plants, about fifty miles apart."
Point-of-sale material -- the price tags, shelf labels and signs a shop uses to show a price or a promotion
"Everything you read on the shelf is point-of-sale material. That is what we make."
A retail chain -- a group of shops that belong to the same company and share a name
"We work with the largest supermarket chains and drugstore chains in the country."
To supply somebody -- to be the company that regularly provides another company with what it sells or uses
"We supply forty-seven of the fifty largest supermarket chains in Brazil."
To source something -- to find and buy a material or a product, often from another country
"We source part of our raw material in Asia and part in South America."
A lead time -- the time between placing an order and having it delivered
"Our lead time is fifteen days, and that is the reason the big chains stay with us."
To scale up -- to make an operation bigger so that it can produce much more
"We opened the second plant because we could not scale up in the first one."
In-house -- done by your own people inside the company, instead of bought from outside
"The design is in-house. We do not send it to an agency."
A track record -- everything a company has actually delivered so far, which is what a new client judges
"Twenty years and almost every large chain in the country: that is our track record."
To wear several hats -- to do several completely different jobs at the same time, in one role
"In a family group you wear several hats. Today I was in sales and in maintenance."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A man walks up to a stand at a retail fair and somebody asks what his company does. He says that they are a group of companies, and that four of them make point-of-sale material: the price tags and the shelf labels shoppers read without ever thinking about them. Then comes the number, because a buyer always wants the number first. They have supplied forty-seven of the fifty largest retail chains in the country. That sentence counts a result, so it takes the simple form: have plus the past participle.

The next sentence does a different job. They have been supplying most of those chains since 2016, and they are still supplying them this morning. Nothing there is finished, so the verb carries been and an -ing. The two sentences look similar and measure opposite things: one says how many, the other says how long.

The rest of the answer follows the same rule. He has been running the group for ten years, and in that time the group has opened a second manufacturing plant and has added a transport company. The design is in-house; only the raw material is sourced abroad, which is why the lead time is fifteen days and not thirty. One warning for a Portuguese speaker: never use the present tense with since or for. Not I am here since ten years, but I have been here for ten years.

1. "They have supplied forty-seven chains." Why the simple form and not "have been supplying"?
A Because supply is a verb that never takes -ing.
B Because the sentence counts a finished result, not a period still running.
C Because the sentence is about the past and is over.
2. Why is it "they have been supplying them since 2016" and not "they have supplied them since 2016"?
A Because the sentence measures a period that is still open today.
B Because there is more than one chain.
C Because "since" can only be used with the -ing form.
3. The text warns against "I am here since ten years". What is the problem?
A "Here" cannot follow the verb to be.
B Ten years is too long a period for one sentence.
C English does not use the present tense with since or for. It uses the present perfect.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect Simple and Continuous

GRAMMAR

One perfect, two jobs: counting what is finished, and measuring what is still running.

JobFormExample
How many, how muchhave / has + past participleWe have supplied forty-seven chains.
How long, still goinghave / has + been + verb-ingWe have been supplying them since 2016.
A result inside an unfinished periodsimple form, with this year / this monthThis year we have opened a second plant.
Since and forsince + a point in time · for + a length of timesince 2016 / for ten years
State verbsbe, have, know, own: simple form even for durationI have been a director for ten years.
QuestionHow many have you...? vs How long have you been...?How long have you been working with them?
Negativehave not + participle / have not been + -ingWe have not raised the price since March.

Never: I am the director since ten years · we are having two plants since 2019 · this year we have been opening a second plant · how long time you work with them? · we work with them since 2016.

Stage 2: Put the Introduction in Order

Order

A buyer at a trade fair has just asked what your company does. Put your side of the answer in a logical order.

?"We are a group of companies in Brazil."
?"Four of them make point-of-sale material for retail chains."
?"We have supplied forty-seven of the fifty largest chains in the country."
?"We have been working with most of them for over a decade."
?"We make it ourselves, and our lead time is fifteen days."
?"And you, how long have you been buying for this chain?"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A buyer at a fair asks what your company does. You answer:
A "We are in the market of the materials for the supermarkets since twenty years."
B "We are a group of companies in Brazil, and four of them make point-of-sale material."
C "My company is the industry. We do the papers of the price."
You want to say how many chains you already work with. You say:
A "We have been supplying forty-seven chains."
B "We have supplied forty-seven of the fifty largest chains in the country."
C "We supply forty-seven chains since a long time."
You want to say you have been in charge for ten years. You say:
A "I have been running the group for ten years."
B "I am running the group since ten years."
C "I run the group there is ten years."
The buyer has finished answering. You want the conversation to continue. You say:
A "Very interesting. Thank you."
B "Yes. Our lead time is better."
C "And you, how long have you been buying for this chain?"
