Lesson 11 -- What Are You Saying?

Podcast & Reading
"5 ways to be a better listener" -- Life Kit, NPR

Eighteen minutes on hearing what was actually said instead of what you expected to hear. Connection to Lesson 11: you cannot report a sentence accurately if you were half listening, and every technique here is aimed at that. Full transcript on the page.

Tip: read the transcript and pick the two habits you would take into your next regional call. Then, after that call, write down one sentence somebody said, word for word, and check it against the minutes. Send the page to your Kindle for the flight.

Talk
"5 ways to listen better" -- Julian Treasure, TED

Eight minutes on why we lose most of what is said to us, and four exercises that fix it. Connection to Lesson 11: reported speech is only as good as what you actually heard, and this is the training for the first half of that.

Tip: it is short, so watch it twice — once with English subtitles, once without. Then use his RASA method in one real call this week and notice how much more of it you can repeat afterwards.

Grammar Video
"How to use reported speech" -- BBC Learning English, 6 Minute Grammar

Six minutes on the step back in time, with the say and tell pair explained clearly. Connection to Lesson 11: the change is grammatical, not factual, and the video makes that point better than any table can.

Tip: pause after each example and report a real sentence somebody said to you this week. Say it once with said and once with told me, and notice which one needs the person.
