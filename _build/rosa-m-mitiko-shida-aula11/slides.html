Chapter 3: The Code

One Step Back in Time

He said · she told me

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I manage thirty accounts." → "She said she managed thirty accounts."

"The board has approved it." → "He said the board had approved it."

"The office will open in June." → "She said the office would open in June."

"I can attend." → "He told me he could attend."

In every pair the colored verb changes in the same direction. Which direction — and what is different about the last one?

OriginalReportedExample
present simplepast simple"I manage…" → "She said she managed…"
present perfect · past simplepast perfect"It has been approved." → "He said it had been approved."
will · can · maywould · could · might"It will open." → "She said it would open."
say vs telltell needs a person straight after it. say does not take one."He told me that…"
"He said that…" NOT "He said me…"
time and placenow → then · today → that day · tomorrow → the next day · here → there"I will send it tomorrow." → "She said she would send it the next day."

The step back is grammatical, not factual. She said she managed thirty accounts does not mean she stopped — it only means the sentence is being repeated from somewhere else. And that is optional: leaving it out sounds more natural in speech.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"He said me that the second phase had been approved."
"He told me that the second phase had been approved."
"She said the office will open in June."
"She said the office would open in June."
"He told that he could attend the convention."
"He said he could attend the convention."

Tell needs a person immediately after it and say never takes one. The Portuguese me disse pushes you straight into said me, which is the single most common mistake at this level. And the verb takes one step back: will becomes would, has becomes had, can becomes could.

Chapter 4: The Debrief

You Missed the Call

Three announcements, and one of them changes your plan

Dialogue

What Exactly Did He Say?

J
Rosa, you missed the regional call. Three things, and one of them affects your plan directly.
R
Go ahead, and give me the sentences, not the summary.
J
All right. He said the second phase had been approved on Tuesday. He also said the Lima office would open in June, not in March.
R
June. Then every date in my scenario sheet is wrong, and I have already sent it to two people.
J
And the third thing. He told us he wanted one page from every account manager about their biggest client, before the convention.
R
Did he say what should be on the page, or is that your reading of it?
J
Good question. He did not say. I asked him afterwards and he told me that if he specified the format we would all write the same thing.
R
That is the most useful sentence of the three. Send me the minutes when they come out and I will correct my dates today.
Comprehension

About Julio

1. What did he say about the second phase?
That it had been approved on Tuesday.
2. What did he say about the Lima office?
That it would open in June, not in March.
3. What did the director ask every account manager for?
One page about their biggest client, before the convention.
4. Why did the director refuse to specify the format?
Because he told Julio that if he specified it, everybody would write the same thing.
The Real Thing

The Debrief Email

Email · to the Brazil commercial team
Regional call — what was said

What was said. The director said the second phase had been approved on Tuesday. He said the Lima office would open in June, not in March. He told the group he wanted one page from each account manager before the convention.

What he did not say. He did not say what should be on the page, and he did not give a deadline beyond the convention.

My reading, not his words. A June opening probably means our Q1 targets move too. I have not heard that from anybody, so please treat it as my opinion until it is confirmed.

1. Why is the third paragraph separated from the first?
Because it is her interpretation, not something anybody said — and she labels it as hers.
2. Why does she include a paragraph about what he did not say?
Because the gaps are where colleagues invent their own version — naming them stops that.
Listening

Three Things You Need to Know

A voice message from a colleague who was on the call. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the director say about the second phase?
That it had been approved on Tuesday, earlier than anybody expected.
2. What did he say about the Lima office, and why does that matter to Rosa?
That it would open in June and not in March — which makes the dates in her plan wrong.
3. What reason did he give for not specifying the format of the page?
That if he specified it, everybody would write the same thing.
Listening 2

The Twenty Minutes Afterwards

A colleague on how a decision travels. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say is the most useful skill in a large company?
Not speaking well in the meeting, but what you do in the twenty minutes afterwards.
2. What is her first rule?
Separate what was actually said from what you concluded, and label the conclusion as yours.
3. What is her second rule, and why?
If you did not hear it yourself, say so — because passing on half a sentence damages a reputation fast.
