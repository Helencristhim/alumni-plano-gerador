Lesson 27 · Two Seconds to Fix It

Precision in Real Time

A live interview. You say 2019, and it was 2016. You say all concessions, and you meant new ones. Everybody makes these mistakes. Professionals fix them in two seconds, without drama. Today you learn how.

Chapter 1: First Words

From the Page to the Live Mic

Last time you wrote your story with time to choose every word. Live, there is no time. You will say something imprecise. What matters is what you do in the next two seconds.

Have you ever said a wrong number or a wrong name in an important meeting? What did you do?

Today’s Goal

Three Missions

1

Ten words for precision and mistakes.

2

Hear a live interview where a number goes wrong.

3

Repair and rephrase on the spot with I mean, or rather, let me rephrase that and what I meant was.

The structure of the day is self-repair: it was 2019, sorry, I mean 2016; about thirty, or rather, twenty-eight; let me rephrase that; what I meant was new concessions. Short, clear, and then you move on.

One Question

Live, Right Now

"The concession was signed in 2019 and covers all ports in the state."

The real facts: 2016, and only the two southern ports. Read the sentence aloud as if you were live, then fix it.

Chapter 3: The Code

I Mean, Or Rather

Self-repair and reformulation

Grammar Discovery

Fix the Word, or Fix the Idea?

"The concession was signed in 2019, sorry, I mean 2016."

"There were thirty bids, or rather, twenty-eight."

"The rule is bad. Let me rephrase that: the rule has a weak point."

"What I meant was new concessions, not the existing ones."

The blue words fix one word or one number, fast. The red words fix a whole idea: the speaker said something too strong, or not clear. Where is the correct information: before or after the colored words?

JobFormExample
Fix one word or number..., sorry, I mean + correct word2019, sorry, I mean 2016.
Make a number more exact..., or rather, + exact wordthirty, or rather, twenty-eight.
Say a whole idea betterLet me rephrase that: + new sentenceLet me rephrase that: the rule has a weak point.
Explain your intentionWhat I meant was + correct ideaWhat I meant was new concessions.
Add a limitLet me qualify that: + limitLet me qualify that: only in the south.
The trapone short sorry, then the fix; never What I wanted to say it isNot sorry, sorry, my English... · not What I meant it was.

In one line: small fix: sorry, I mean / or rather; big fix: let me rephrase that / what I meant was; then move on.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Live, at Real Speed

One interview, two recordings, one correction

Dialogue

Live on the Podcast

J
Aguinaldo, welcome to the show. When was the first private port concession in your state?
A
It was signed in 2019, sorry, I mean 2016. Time flies.
J
And how many groups competed for it? Just a ballpark figure is fine.
A
Off the top of my head, thirty, or rather, twenty-eight. I can double-check after the show.
J
Some listeners say the new rule is a disaster for investors. Do you agree?
A
The rule is bad. Let me rephrase that: the rule has one weak point, the transition period.
J
So you would change the whole rule?
A
No, what I meant was only the transition. Let me qualify that: the rest of the rule is precise and fair.
Comprehension

About Joanna

1. What does Joanna ask first?
When the first private port concession in his state was signed.
2. What kind of number does Joanna say is fine?
A ballpark figure.
3. What do some listeners say about the new rule, according to Joanna?
That it is a disaster for investors.
Listening 1 · The Interpreter

It Is Not the Mistake, It Is the Repair

A conference interpreter explains what she sees when experts make mistakes on stage. Sound first — no text.

1. What kind of mistakes do experts make, according to her?
The wrong year, the wrong number, sometimes the wrong country.
2. What is the difference between a good speaker and a nervous one?
Not the mistake, but the repair.
3. What does a nervous speaker do?
He stops, apologizes for a long time, and loses the audience.
Listening 2 · The Lawyer

The Most Important Sentence of the Day

A lawyer remembers a correction during a public hearing. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the director say first about the new rule?
That it would apply to all concessions.
2. What did she mean, after the correction?
All new concessions, not the existing ones; the existing ones can choose to join.
3. What did the lawyer ask for afterwards, and why?
The verbatim transcript, to double-check.
