Lesson 12 -- Wish You Were Prepared

Series
The Crown -- Season 1, Episode 4 ("Act of God")

A whole episode about a problem everybody could see and nobody would name, and about the cost of raising a concern to someone more senior than you. Listen for how the objections are worded: almost nothing is said directly, and every sentence is engineered so that no individual is accused. That is the register you practised today, in its most extreme form.

Tip: pick three moments where somebody wanted to say "you should have acted sooner" and said something else instead. Write down what they actually said, then rewrite each one using "I wish" or "if only".

Podcast
WorkLife with Adam Grant -- any episode on speaking up and regret

Grant keeps returning to the same finding: what people regret at work is almost never the thing they said, it is the thing they left unspoken. Listen for the language his guests use when they describe those moments — misgivings, letting something slide, giving someone the benefit of the doubt. It is exactly this week's vocabulary, spoken at natural speed by people who are not teaching it.

Tip: keep a list of every "I wish" you hear and mark which tense follows it. Then write one sentence of your own that you could say in a real meeting this month.

YouTube
Celeste Headlee -- 10 Ways to Have a Better Conversation (TED)

Her rule about not equating your experience with someone else's is the same instinct behind today's grammar: the moment a difficult conversation becomes about who is at fault, it stops being about the problem. Useful here for one thing in particular — how to stay specific and short when the other person becomes defensive, which is the hardest part of the retrospective you rehearsed.

Tip: after watching, record your quarter retrospective again in ninety seconds. If it takes longer, you are defending the point instead of making it.
