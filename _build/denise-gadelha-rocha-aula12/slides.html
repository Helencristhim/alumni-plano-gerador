Wish You Were Prepared

In executive English, "I wish" is not a lament. It is how you put a fact on the record without putting a person on trial.

The Same Fact, Twice

One of These Has a Defendant

"I wish we had known about the reserving gap in June."

"You told us too late."

Same date. Same failure. The first one has no subject who failed and no verb of accusation — and everybody in the room still understands exactly what happened. That is not politeness. It is precision without a defendant.

Chapter 3: The Code

Wish and If Only

One verb, three tenses, three different meanings

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I wish we had known about the reserving gap in June."

"I wish the reserving page arrived before the call, not during it."

"I wish they would tell us earlier when a decision changes."

"I wish to raise a concern before we sign this off."

Three of these describe a reality you do not have. One is a plain request, and nothing about it is unreal. Which is which?

PatternWhat it meansExample
wish + past simpleA present reality you do not have"I wish the page arrived before the call."
wish + past perfectRegret about the past — the safest one at work"I wish we had known in June."
wish + wouldIrritation with somebody ELSE's behaviour. Never about yourself"I wish they would tell us earlier."
wish + couldAn ability you do not have"I wish I could be in both calls."
if onlySame three patterns, more emphatic, and it can stand alone: "If only we had brought the review forward."
wish + to + infinitiveThe odd one out: FORMAL and REAL, a synonym of "I would like to". "I wish to raise a concern" is a request, not a regret. Common in minutes and in writing.
The two trapsNever "I wish I would have known" — after wish, the past is had known. Never "I wish I know" — the verb always steps back one tense: knew.
The formal survivorI wish I were in that meeting — "were" for every person is still standard in careful, written English, and it is the register a board note is in.
Practice

Which Wish

Say it first, then click to check

"I wish we ___had known about the gap in June." (know — a regret about the past)
"I wish the reserving page ___arrived before the call." (arrive — a present reality you do not have)
"I wish they ___would tell us earlier when a decision changes." (tell — their behaviour, and it annoys you)
"If only we ___had brought the review forward by one month." (bring)
"I wish ___to raise a concern before we sign this off." (raise — a formal request, nothing unreal)
Register

Which Wish Survives Being Forwarded

A fact, on the record

"I wish we had known in June."

Safe upwards, sideways and in writing. No person is named, and the date does the work.

A judgement on behaviour

"I wish they would tell us earlier."

Fine with a peer you trust. Upwards, it arrives without your tone attached, and it is repeated in those exact words.

A request for a mechanism

"What I am asking for is a standing item."

Nobody can take offence at a line on an agenda, and the agenda outlives the annoyance.

The move: name the regret once, say it is not a grievance, then ask for the mechanism. A regret with no request is a complaint in a good suit.

The Wish That Is Really a Complaint

A regional director on one sentence she stopped using. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Why does she say "I wish they would tell us earlier" is not a regret?
Because it is a judgement on how people behave now, and everyone hears it that way, including the people upstairs.
2. What is the particular risk when the head office is in another country?
The sentence travels further than she intended and arrives without her tone attached.
3. What rule did she make for herself?
For a fact on the record, use "I wish we had known". For a change in behaviour, do not use the wish at all — ask for a mechanism.
4. What does she say about a line on an agenda?
Nobody can take offence at it, and it outlives whoever is annoyed with whom this month.

1. Why does the form need a line for what the note is not?
Because in a hierarchy a retrospective is read as an accusation unless it says otherwise. The denial has to be written down, not assumed.

Because a promise depends on somebody remembering it, and a line on an agenda does not.
