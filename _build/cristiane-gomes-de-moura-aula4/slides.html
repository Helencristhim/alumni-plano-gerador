Chapter 4: The Code

Every Wednesday, and This Wednesday

Two ways to say a present, and they are not the same present

Grammar Discovery

One Is About Every Week. Three Are About Right Now.

"Every Wednesday about thirty people sit down in that room."

"This month they are watching thrillers."

"The two women in the second row are not agreeing."

"Are you enjoying it?"

The first sentence is about every Wednesday, and the verb stands alone. The other three are about this month and this evening, and each one grew a second part: a blue form of to be, and a red verb ending in -ing. Neither half works on its own.

JobFormExample
A habit, every weekpresent simple — the verb aloneThey sit down every Wednesday. She watches films on the weekend.
Happening now, or these weeksam / is / are + verb-ingThis month they are watching thrillers.
Negativeam not / is not / are not + verb-ingThey are not agreeing.
QuestionAm / Is / Are + subject + verb-ingAre you enjoying it?
Question word in frontWhat / Why + am/is/are + subject + verb-ingWhat are you watching at the moment?
Short answerYes, I am. / No, she is not."Is he watching it alone?" — "Yes, he is."
The words that give it awayevery week, usually, never → simple · right now, at the moment, this month, tonight → continuousI usually watch at home, but tonight I am watching one at the cinema.
The trapnever leave out the am / is / are, and never use -ing for likingNot I watching. Say I am watching. Not I am liking it. Say I like it.

In one line: the verb alone is your life in general — am, is or are plus -ing is your life this week.

Listening 1 · Not a Film Person

He Is On Episode Six

A man explaining how a series took over his week. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he normally watch, and what does he usually do before the end?
Football and nothing else. He usually falls asleep before the end of anything.
2. What is he watching at the moment, and which episode is he on?
A series about a small town where somebody disappears. He is on episode six, and he is watching it alone at night, which he says was a mistake.
3. Who recommended it, and what is she doing now?
His nineteen-year-old daughter. She is not answering his messages, and he says she is enjoying that.
Listening 2 · On Not Recommending

Why She Stopped Recommending Films

A woman on opinions, first impressions and subtitles. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say happens in the first twenty minutes when somebody recommends a film?
The person spends them checking whether they agree with the recommendation, instead of watching.
2. What does she do instead of recommending?
She asks what they watched last and what they thought, and she says nothing about whether it is good.
3. What is her advice about subtitles, and why does she say it is not cheating?
Put them on, in the same language as the film. She says you are reading and listening at the same time, which is what your brain wants to do anyway.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I can say what I usually do and what I am doing this week, in the same answer.
I never leave out the am, is or are. I say I am watching, never I watching.
I can make it negative and I can ask it: I am not enjoying it. Are you enjoying it?
I know which words go with which tense: usually and never on one side, right now and at the moment on the other.
I know the words: a series, an episode, a thriller, a plot, subtitles, to recommend, an audience, to enjoy, a weekend, to join.
