Series
Behind Her Eyes -- six episodes

A British thriller with something under it that is not quite of this world, which is exactly the corner you said you like. Six episodes, so it ends before it can lose you, and the plot is the whole point — people talk about the last ten minutes for years. The English is clear and the sentences are short, because most of the tension is in what nobody says.

Tip: English subtitles on. After each episode, say two sentences out loud: one about what happens in general in the series, and one that starts with "At the moment I am...". That is today’s grammar, practised on something you actually want to watch.

Video
BBC Learning English -- "Present Simple and Present Continuous" (6 Minute Grammar)

Six minutes on the exact distinction this lesson is built on, from two speakers who take turns and never rush. They spend most of it on the part that matters: which words in the sentence tell you which tense to use before you even choose the verb.

Tip: listen once without pausing. The second time, stop after every example and build the same sentence about your own week before they move on.

Talk
Andrew Stanton -- "The clues to a great story" (TED)

The man who wrote Toy Story and Finding Nemo on why some plots hold you and others do not. It is the vocabulary of this lesson used by somebody who builds stories for a living, and after it you will describe what you are watching in a completely different way.

Tip: this one is longer and faster than the others, so do not try to finish it. Watch the first five minutes only, with English subtitles, and stop. Then say his opening idea back in your own words.
