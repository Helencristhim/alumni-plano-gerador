Chapter 4: Who Did It, and Who Says So

The Same Fact, With and Without a Name

One extra verb, and the person disappears

Grammar Discovery

Two Pairs. Same Fact, Different Sentence.

Marcelo sent the file to the wrong address.

The file was sent to the wrong address.

Somebody flagged the issue on 3 October.

The correction was approved by the finance director.

In the red sentences an extra verb appeared and the person went away. In the last one the person came back — at the end, after by, and only because it was worth naming.

In one line: bad news in the passive, commitments in the active. A document where nothing is anybody’s fault and nothing is anybody’s job is useless in both directions.

Two Halves

Which Half Does This Sentence Belong To?

PASSIVE — no name

"The deadline was missed."

"The file was sent to the wrong address."

"The issue was flagged on 3 October."

"A workaround has been put in place."

ACTIVE — with a name and a date

"Maria will send the revised plan by Friday."

"I am accountable for the new timeline."

"The correction was approved by the finance director."

"I will circulate the minutes on Friday."

Now take a real meeting from this week and sort five of its sentences into the two columns, out loud. If everything lands on the left, nothing will happen after that meeting.

Listening 1 · A Programme Director

One Sentence, Four Seconds

Somebody who watched a project die because of a document. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say the minutes actually are?
Not a record of the meeting, but the meeting six months from now, when nobody remembers what was said and somebody is looking for a reason.
2. What did the sentence say, and why was it a disaster?
It said the delay was caused by the client, with the client’s name in it. It was true, and it was a disaster because the document went to the client.
3. What does he say the sentence could have said instead, and what is his point about the two versions?
That the delay was caused by a change in the file format — also true, and the same fact. His point is that it is the same information: one version has a person in it and one does not.
Listening 2 · A Chief of Staff

One Rule I Stole From a Lawyer

Somebody who writes a lot of these, on when the name has to be there. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the rule, in her words?
Anything that went wrong goes in the passive; anything anybody has to do goes in the active, with a name and a date.
2. Why does she say the reason is not politeness?
Because a document where nothing is anybody’s job is completely useless, and she has read hundreds of them: everything was reviewed, discussed and agreed, and then nothing happens for three weeks.
3. If she could keep only one of the two halves of the rule, which would it be and why?
The second one. The first only avoids a fight; the second is the only reason anything moves.

Role-Play 1 of 3 — Guided

The Status Call Where the News Is Bad

Scenario: A deadline was missed and the partner is on the call. Give the facts without putting anybody’s name next to the problem, say what has already been done about it, name the root cause, and finish with one commitment that has your name and a date in it.

Keyword chips:

the deadline was missed it was flagged on... a workaround has been put in place I am accountable for...
Role-Play 2 of 3 — Semi-free

Off the Record, at Dinner

Scenario: Dinner after the call. The same partner leans over and asks what actually happened. Be honest with him without breaking anybody on your own team, say what you learned, and tell him what you will do differently. Nothing you say here should contradict the minutes — it should just be warmer.

Keywords:

off the record... what I would do differently the root cause I am accountable
Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

Three Minutes, No Notes

Scenario: Dictate the minutes of a difficult meeting out loud, as if somebody were typing them. Something went wrong, somebody outside your company caused it, a temporary fix is running, the root cause is not solved, and two people have to do something by Friday. Nobody should be embarrassed by this document and nobody should be able to say it was not their job.

No keywords, no notes, three minutes.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I can give bad news in writing without putting a name next to it.
I put a name and a date on every commitment, including my own.
I use the participle after was, were, is, are and has been.
I bring the name back with by when the news is good.
I know the words: the minutes, an action item, to escalate, to flag, accountable, a root cause.
