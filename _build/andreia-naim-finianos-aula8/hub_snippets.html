Reading the Room on a Call -- Bad News Without Pointing a Finger

Why the minutes are written the way they are, and when the name has to come back. Key words: the minutes, an action item, a deadline, to escalate, a workaround, to flag, accountable, a root cause, a briefing note, to circulate, on the record, to cover yourself. Structure: passive voice, present and past.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

The minutes -- the written record of what was said and decided in a meeting
"I will circulate the minutes on Friday, with one action item per name."
An action item -- one task, with one name and one date, written down after a meeting
"An action item without a name is not an action item."
A deadline -- the date by which something has to be finished
"The original deadline of the twentieth of October was missed."
To escalate -- to take a problem to somebody more senior, because it has stopped moving
"The matter was escalated to the steering committee."
A workaround -- a temporary way of getting past a problem you have not solved yet
"A temporary workaround has been put in place while we fix the root cause."
To flag -- to point at something early, so that nobody is surprised later
"The issue was flagged on the third of October, before anybody asked about it."
Accountable -- having to answer for the result, whoever actually did the work
"The deadline was missed. I am accountable for the revised plan."
A root cause -- the thing that made it happen, underneath everything else that went wrong
"The root cause of the delay was identified as a change in the file format."
A briefing note -- a short written update sent to people who were not in the room
"Two lines of a briefing note can undo an hour of a good meeting."
To circulate -- to send the same document to everybody who needs to have it
"I will circulate the revised plan on Friday."
On the record -- said in a way that can be quoted back to you later
"If it is going on the record, I would write that sentence differently."
To cover yourself -- to write it so that it cannot be used against you later
"Half of what people call covering yourself is just writing the fact without the name."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

In the room, somebody said that Marcelo sent the file to the wrong address. In the minutes, the file was sent to the wrong address. The person disappears. This is sometimes called corporate cowardice, and sometimes it is, but the habit exists for a reason: a record is read by people who were not there, and it is read later, when nobody remembers the room.

If a name is written next to a mistake, the name is what gets remembered. So the bad news goes without one. The issue was flagged on the third of October. The deadline was missed. The root cause was identified as a change in the file format. A temporary workaround has been put in place. The matter was escalated to the steering committee. Every one of those sentences is a fact, and not one of them is an accusation.

Then the document turns. The correction was approved by the finance director — here the name comes back, because the news is good and the authority matters. And the last section has no passive at all: Maria will send the revised numbers by Friday. I am accountable for the new plan and I will circulate it the same day. Those are action items, and an action item without a name is not an action item. The document that gets this wrong in one direction says nothing was anybody’s fault; the one that gets it wrong in the other direction is a document nobody wants to be quoted in, because everything in it is on the record.

1. "The file was sent to the wrong address." Why is Marcelo not in this sentence?
A Because the writer did not know who sent it.
B Because a record is read later by people who were not there, and a name next to a mistake is what gets remembered.
C Because English does not allow names in the minutes.
2. "The correction was approved by the finance director." Why does the name come back here?
A Because the news is good and the authority behind the decision is worth recording.
B Because the writer forgot the rule.
C Because approve is a verb that cannot be used without by.
3. The last section of the document has no passive at all. Why not?
A Because the writer ran out of time.
B Because commitments are always written in the past.
C Because those are action items, and an action item without a name is not an action item.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive Voice

GRAMMAR

Bad news in the passive, commitments in the active. The fact stays; the person is a decision.

JobFormExample
The fact, no personwas / were + past participleThe file was sent to the wrong address.
Right now, or a ruleis / are + past participleEvery change is recorded with a timestamp.
Already done, still truehas / have been + participleA workaround has been put in place.
When you WANT the name... by + personapproved by the finance director
Bad newspassive, and no byThe deadline was missed.
A commitmentactive, first person, with a dateI will circulate the plan on Friday.
The trapthe participle, never the plain pastnot was send, not has been putted

Never: The file was send · A workaround has been putted · It was flag on 3 October · Marcelo missed the deadline (in minutes that go to the client) · The plan will be circulated by me (when it is your own commitment)

Quiz

A "Marcelo missed the deadline."
B "The deadline was missed, and the root cause has been identified."
C "Somebody on our side made a serious mistake."
You are committing to send the revised plan yourself. You write:
A "The revised plan will be circulated in due course."
B "I will circulate the revised plan on Friday."
C "The revised plan is going to be sent by somebody."

A "The correction was approved by the finance director on Tuesday."

Somebody sends you minutes in which nothing is anybody’s job. The problem is:
A Nothing. It is polite and nobody is embarrassed.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Dictate the minutes of a difficult meeting out loud, as if somebody were typing them. Something went wrong, somebody outside your company caused it, a temporary fix is running, the root cause is not solved, and two people have to do something by Friday. Nobody should be embarrassed by this document, and nobody should be able to say it was not their job. Use the passive at least five times and put a name and a date on at least two sentences.
