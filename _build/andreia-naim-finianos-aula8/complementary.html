Lesson 8 -- Reading the Room on a Call

Podcast
NPR Life Kit -- "How to give good feedback"

The spoken version of what you practised in writing today: saying something uncomfortable in a way the other person can actually use. Listen for how often the guests separate the behaviour from the person — it is the same move as taking the name out of the sentence, done out loud.

Tip: read the transcript on the page and find three sentences that describe a problem without naming anybody. Write your own version of each about a real situation at work.

Talk
Margaret Heffernan -- "Dare to disagree" (TED, 13 min)

Thirteen minutes on what happens in organisations where nobody says the uncomfortable thing out loud — which is exactly the document the second recording in class was complaining about. Clear British English, one long example, and a conclusion you can quote in a meeting.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Note the moment she says what people are actually afraid of, and ask yourself whether the last difficult report you wrote said it.

Watch on TED ↗
Film
Downfall: The Case Against Boeing (Netflix, 89 min)

A documentary built almost entirely out of internal documents, and the most serious version of today’s lesson: what a company wrote down, what it did not write down, and what happened when both were read out loud years later. Uncomfortable and unforgettable. American English at natural speed.

Tip: watch the first forty minutes with English subtitles. Note one internal sentence shown on screen and ask what the passive version of it would have hidden — and whether it should have.
