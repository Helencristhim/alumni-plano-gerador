Stage 1.1: Verbs That Take Another Verb

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Avoid — to stay away from something on purpose
"I avoid speaking English on the phone. That has to stop."
Keep — to continue doing something, again and again
"I keep saying I will start tomorrow."
Decide — to choose after thinking about it
"I decided to apply for both."
Tend to — to usually do something, without planning it
"I tend to leave everything until the night before."
Prefer — to like one thing more than another
"I prefer studying in the morning."
Improve — to get better at something, little by little
"My English is improving, slowly."
Mind — to be bothered by something
"I do not mind working on Saturdays."
Promise — to say you will definitely do something
"I promised to send it before Friday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. Then answer the questions.

If somebody asks what I enjoy doing, the honest answer is: working with people. I prefer studying alone, but I learn faster when I have to explain something to somebody else. I avoid speaking English on the phone, which is a problem, so last month I decided to make one call a week in English. I keep saying I will start the assignments early, and I never do — I tend to leave everything until the night before. I do not mind working on Saturdays if the work is interesting. And I promised myself one thing for this year: to improve my speaking enough to stop translating in my head first.
What did she decide to do about the phone?
A To make one call a week in English.
B To avoid phone calls completely.
Why is it "I decided to make" but "I keep saying"?
A Because "decide" takes "to" and "keep" takes -ing — two different lists.
B Because one is past and one is present.
What did she promise herself for this year?
A To work on Saturdays.
B To improve her speaking enough to stop translating in her head.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Two verbs together: -ing or to

There is no rule to work out. It is two lists — and that is honest, not lazy.

+ verb-ing

enjoy · avoid · keep
mind · finish · practise
"I enjoy studying in the morning."

+ to + verb

want · decide · promise
tend · hope · need
"I decided to apply for both."

− I do not mind working on Saturdays. (mind always takes -ing)
? Do you want to work abroad? (want always takes to)
= I like studying = I like to study. (like, love and prefer take both)

What helps: the -ing group looks at something that already happens; the to group looks forward, at something not done yet.

Stage 4: In the conversation

Quiz
An interviewer asks what you enjoy doing. Which answer is correct?
A I enjoy to work with people.
B I enjoy working with people.
Which sentence is correct?
A I decided to apply for both.
B I decided applying for both.
You are asked to do the tasks nobody wants. What is the best thing to say?
A I do not mind doing that — it is how you learn the process.
B I do not mind to do that.
