Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

Thirty-Five Years, One Table -- I’ve Worked / I’ve Been Working

Introducing yourself and your company at an international meeting. Key words: headquarters, a subsidiary, a plant, to oversee, to be in charge of, a track record, a supplier, revenue, market share, to expand, the board, to step into a role. Structure: present perfect simple x present perfect continuous.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Headquarters -- the main office of a company, where the top managers work
"The group’s headquarters is in Tokyo, but my office is in Sao Paulo."
A subsidiary -- a company that is owned and controlled by a larger company
"We are the Brazilian subsidiary of a Japanese group."
A plant -- a large factory, especially one that makes industrial products
"We have two plants in Brazil, and I visit one of them twice a week."
To oversee -- to watch and direct work to make sure it is done well
"I oversee the operations of both plants."
To be in charge of -- to be the person who is responsible for something or someone
"Who is in charge of the new project in Mexico?"
A track record -- all the past results of a person or a company
"Our plant has a strong track record on safety."
A supplier -- a company that provides parts or materials to another company
"We are a supplier to some of the biggest car makers in Brazil."
Revenue -- the money that a company receives from its sales
"Our revenue has grown every year since 2022."
Market share -- the part of all the sales in a market that one company has
"We have been gaining market share in heavy industry."
To expand -- to become bigger, or to start working in new places
"The group has been expanding into new countries."
The board -- the group of people who make the most important decisions in a company
"I present our results to the board twice a year."
To step into a role -- to start doing an important job or position
"I stepped into the role of president five years ago."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Julius has worked for the same Japanese group for thirty-five years. He stepped into the role of president five years ago, and today he is in charge of the Brazilian subsidiary. The group’s headquarters is in Tokyo, but he oversees two plants in Brazil.

The company is a supplier of parts for heavy industry and for car makers. Its track record is strong: revenue has grown every year since 2022, and the subsidiary has won new customers in three countries.

Lately, the group has been expanding into new markets, and the meetings have been getting more international. Julius has been traveling more, and he has been preparing a presentation for the board about how to gain market share.

1. Why does the text say “Julius has worked for the group for thirty-five years” and not “has been working”?
A Because it is a total, a result that you can count.
B Because the present perfect continuous is never used with the word for, only with since and with dates.
C Because the sentence is about the future.
2. What has the group been doing lately?
A It has been closing plants.
B It has been moving its headquarters from Tokyo to a new office in Sao Paulo.
C It has been expanding into new markets.
3. Which sentence describes something that is still going on?
A He stepped into the role of president five years ago, after many years in the same group.
B He has been preparing a presentation for the board.
C The subsidiary has won new customers.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect Simple x Continuous

GRAMMAR

Use have / has + past participle for a result or a total that you can count. Use have / has been + -ing for an activity that started in the past and is still going on. State verbs (know, be, own, belong) stay in the simple form.

JobFormExample
Result, total, how manyhave / has + past participle“We have opened two plants.”
Activity still in progresshave / has been + verb-ing“I’ve been traveling a lot lately.”
Questions about a totalHow many / How much + have you + past participle?“How many countries have you visited?”
Questions about durationHow long + have you been + verb-ing?“How long have you been working there?”
Signal wordsalready, ever, never, so far x lately, recently, all week, for / since“I’ve never lived abroad, but I’ve been studying English for years.”
The trapstate verbs (know, be, own, belong) do not take -ingNot “I’ve been knowing him for years.” Say I’ve known him for years.

Never: I work here since 1990 · I’ve been knowing him... · How long are you the president? · This year we’ve been opening two plants.

Quiz

Someone at the table asks how long you have worked for the group. You say:
A "I’ve worked for the group for thirty-five years."

C "I am working for the group thirty-five years."
