Chapter 3: The Code

I’ve Worked... I’ve Been Working...

A total, or something still going on?

Grammar Discovery

A Total, or Still Going On?

"I have worked for the same group for thirty-five years."

"I’ve been traveling to Atibaia twice a week."

"We have opened two plants in Brazil."

"Lately, the group has been expanding into new markets."

Look at the blue verbs: they give a result or a total that you can count (thirty-five years, two plants). Now look at the red verbs: they describe an activity that started in the past and is still going on, often again and again. Blue = have / has + past participle. Red = have / has been + -ing.

JobFormExample
Result, total, how manyhave / has + past participle“We have opened two plants.”
Activity still in progresshave / has been + verb-ing“I’ve been traveling a lot lately.”
Questions about a totalHow many / How much + have you + past participle?“How many countries have you visited?”
Questions about durationHow long + have you been + verb-ing?“How long have you been working there?”
Signal wordsalready, ever, never, so far x lately, recently, all week, for / since“I’ve never lived abroad, but I’ve been studying English for years.”
The trapstate verbs (know, be, own, belong) do not take -ingNot “I’ve been knowing him for years.” Say I’ve known him for years.

In one line: how many = I’ve visited thirty countries; still going on = I’ve been traveling a lot.

A Coffee Break in Tokyo

K
Hello, I don’t think we’ve met. I’m Katrin Weber. I run the European operations, from our office in Frankfurt.
J
Nice to meet you, Katrin. I’m Julius Nascimento, the president of the Brazilian subsidiary.
K
Brazil! How long have you been with the group?
J
I’ve worked for the group for thirty-five years, and I stepped into the role of president five years ago.
K
That’s an impressive track record. And what have you been working on lately?
J
We’ve been getting ready to expand. Our two plants have been very busy, and our revenue has grown every year.
K
Lucky you. In Europe we’ve been losing market share to Asian suppliers. I’d love to hear how you do it in Brazil.
J
With pleasure. I oversee both plants, so I can show you the numbers. Shall we talk after the board meeting?
Comprehension

About Katrin

1. Where does Katrin work, and what does she run?
In the Frankfurt office: she runs the European operations.
2. What has been happening in Europe, according to Katrin?
They have been losing market share to Asian suppliers.
3. What does Katrin want to hear from Julius?
How the Brazilian subsidiary does it: how it keeps growing.
Listening 1 · An American Buyer

Twenty Years of Buying Parts

The purchasing director of a U.S. truck maker talks about her career and what has changed recently. Sound first — no text.

1. How long has she worked in purchasing?
For twenty years.
2. What has been different in the last three years?
They have been buying more castings from Asia and Brazil, because prices in Mexico have gone up.
3. What does she look for in a new supplier?
A good track record on quality, and people who answer the phone when there is a problem.
Listening 2 · A Plant Manager in India

A Plant That Keeps Growing

The manager of an auto parts plant near Pune talks about his last five years. Sound first — no text.

1. How long has he been the manager of the plant?
For five years.
2. What has changed since he arrived?
They have opened two new lines, hired almost six hundred people, and their revenue has doubled.
3. What have they been testing recently?
A new kind of part for electric cars.
Listening 3 · A British Board Member

What the Board Wants to Hear

A board member in London explains what a good introduction sounds like in an international meeting. Sound first — no text.

1. How many boards has he sat on?
Six.
2. What three things should you say in an introduction?
Who you are, what you are responsible for, and what you have been working on lately.
3. What should you do if you do not understand a question?
Ask: nobody thinks less of you, because everybody has an accent.
