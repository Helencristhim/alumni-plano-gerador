Tell Me About Yourself -- The Opening of the Interview

Six words, no question mark, and the answer decides the next twenty-nine minutes. Key words: a trajectory, a turning point, to take on, a mandate, to step up, a pitch, to land something, understated, a through-line, to sell yourself, credentials, to ramble. Structure: cleft sentences for emphasis (what ... is, it was ... that, all ... is).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A trajectory -- the shape a career has taken, seen as a line rather than a list of jobs
"My trajectory is one company and three different jobs inside it."
A turning point -- the moment after which the direction changed and did not change back
"The turning point was the year my father got ill. Everything after that is a different job."
To take on -- to accept a responsibility that was not yours before
"I took on the commercial side in 2016 because nobody else was going to."
A mandate -- the authority and the scope somebody is given to act inside an organization
"My mandate is everything outside the site: money, clients and new work."
To step up -- to take more responsibility, usually because somebody had to and nobody else did
"I stepped up before anybody offered me the title."
A pitch -- the short, prepared version of what you do and why it matters
"Every candidate has a pitch. The good ones do not sound like one."
To land something -- to succeed in getting something you were competing for
"We landed the hospital contract against two much larger firms."
Understated -- said with less force than the facts deserve, which in English sounds stronger
"His answer was understated, and everybody in the room believed every word."
A through-line -- the single idea that connects everything you have done
"The through-line is that I have always run things that other people build."
To sell yourself -- to present your own value without waiting to be asked
"Nobody in that room is going to sell you. You have thirty minutes to sell yourself."
Credentials -- the qualifications and the proof somebody carries into a room
"My credentials are on the form. What is not on the form is what I do with them."
To ramble -- to talk on and on without arriving at a clear point
"I rambled for four minutes and then apologized for rambling, which took another one."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

"I run a construction company" is a true sentence and a flat one. Every word in it has the same weight, and the listener has to decide for themselves which part matters. English has a way of deciding for them: break the sentence in two and put the important half after the verb to be. What I run is a construction company. Nothing has been added and nothing is untrue — the sentence simply arrives with a shape.

There are four of these worth having. What foregrounds a thing: What I need is two more engineers, with a singular is even though two engineers follow it. The part that foregrounds a piece of something larger: The part that interests me is the decision, not the drawing. It was ... that foregrounds a time or a person: It was 2011 that changed everything, or it was my uncle who taught me the business. And all sets a limit, which is why it sounds modest and lands hard: All I want is to close a gap.

There is a fifth for actions, and it is the one that rescues a trajectory from becoming a list: What I did was take on the commercial side. The verb after was has no to. Two mistakes to avoid, both common. After what, the word order is a statement, not a question: not what do I run is. And all does not take what after it: not all what I want. Used well, none of this sounds theatrical. It is how an understated speaker makes a point without raising their voice.

1. What does "What I run is a construction company" do that the plain sentence does not?
A It adds information the plain sentence did not have.
B It decides for the listener which half of the sentence matters.
C It makes the sentence a question.
2. Why is it "What I need is two more engineers" and not "are"?
A Because the verb agrees with "what", which is singular, even though a plural follows.
B Because "engineers" is treated as one group.
C Because the sentence is in the present.
3. According to the text, what does "all" add to "All I want is to close a gap"?
A It makes the request more urgent.
B It shows the speaker wants many things.
C It sets a limit, which is why it sounds modest and lands hard.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Putting the Weight at the Front

GRAMMAR

Break the sentence in two and put the half you care about after the verb to be.

JobFormExample
Foreground a thingWhat + subject + verb + is ...What I run is a construction company.
Foreground a part of itThe part / the thing that ... is ...The part that interests me is the decision.
Foreground a timeIt was ... that ...It was 2011 that changed everything.
Foreground a personIt was ... who ...It was my uncle who taught me the business.
Set a limitAll + subject + verb + is ...All I want is to close a gap.
Foreground an actionWhat ... did was + base formWhat I did was take on the commercial side.
Agreementwhat takes a singular verbWhat I need is two more engineers.

Never: what do I run is a company · it was 2011 changed everything · what I need are two engineers · all what I want is · what I did was to take on the commercial side.

Stage 2: Put the Opening in Order

Order

An interviewer has said "tell me about yourself". Put your two minutes in a logical order.

?"What I run is a construction company in Sao Paulo, four projects at a time."
?"My mandate is everything outside the site."
?"It was 2011 that changed everything."
?"What I did was take on the commercial side, because nobody else was going to."
?"The through-line is that I have always run things that other people build."
?"Can I ask what the cohort looks like this year?"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The interviewer says "tell me about yourself". You open with:
A "I graduated in civil engineering in 2002, and after that my first job was..."
B "What I run is a construction company in Sao Paulo, four projects at a time."
C "What do I do is manage a company of construction."
You want to make the year 2011 the important part of the sentence. You say:
A "It was 2011 that changed everything."
B "It was 2011 changed everything."
C "The 2011 was what changed all."
You want to say that one thing, and only that thing, is what you want from the program. You say:
A "All what I want is to decide better."
B "All I want is to make those decisions with something better than instinct."
C "I only want one thing that is decide better."
You lose a word in the middle of your answer and pause for two seconds. You:
A Say "sorry, my English is not very good" and start the sentence again.
B Switch to a shorter, simpler subject to be safe.
C Let the pause be a pause, find the word, and carry on from where you were.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Tell me about yourself. Start with what you run today, name one turning point, say what the through-line is, and stop before two minutes. Do not apologize for anything.

Podcast
How I Built This -- Discovery Channel and CuriosityStream: John Hendricks

An hour of one man telling his own trajectory in order, out loud, to somebody who keeps asking what changed. It is the long version of what you have to do in two minutes, and you can hear exactly where the turning points are because he stops on them.

Tip: listen for the first three minutes only, twice. Write down where he starts the story, and ask yourself whether you would have started there.
