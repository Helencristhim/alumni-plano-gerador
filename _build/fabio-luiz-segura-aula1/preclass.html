Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

The First Ninety Seconds at Sibos

A stranger at a payments conference asks which part of the bank you are from, and the answer has to fit ninety seconds. Key words: wholesale banking, cross-border payments, trade finance, foreign exchange, remittance, to oversee, business unit, innovation pipeline, counterpart, to be based in. Structure: present simple and present continuous.

Vocabulary

Wholesale banking -- the side of a bank that serves large companies, not individuals
"I sit on the wholesale banking side, so my clients are corporations."
Cross-border payments -- money that moves from one country to another
"Cross-border payments are the reason my team exists."
Trade finance -- the money a bank lends so an exporter or an importer can finish a sale
"Trade finance is one of the three products my area is responsible for."
Foreign exchange -- the business of changing one currency into another
"Foreign exchange, trade services and trade finance sit in the same division."
A remittance -- one single transfer of money sent abroad
"A remittance from Brazil to Germany still takes two days."
To oversee -- to be in charge of a team or an area and answer for its results
"I oversee the innovation team inside Global Trade and Finance."
A business unit -- one part of a company with its own products and its own numbers
"Each business unit reports its own results to the board."
An innovation pipeline -- the line of new ideas a team is testing, one after another
"Stablecoins are at the top of our innovation pipeline this year."
A counterpart -- the person who has the same job as you, at another company
"I want to meet my counterparts at three European banks."
To be based in -- to have your office, or your home, in a particular city or country
"I am based in Sao Paulo, and half of my meetings are with Europe."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Two people stop at the same stand, one minute apart. The first one says: I work in wholesale banking. My division handles foreign exchange, trade services and trade finance. Every word is correct and the sentence is well built. The person behind the counter nods, smiles, and forgets the conversation before the end of the day. The second one says the same thing, and then adds one line: and this year we are replacing a settlement platform. That second person leaves with a meeting on Thursday.

English has two ways of talking about the present, and they do different jobs. The present simple is for what is always true: the role, the product, the structure of the company. I oversee innovation. A remittance to Europe takes two days. The bank serves large corporations. The present continuous — am, is or are plus the verb with -ing — is for what is happening around now, this quarter, this year. We are replacing a platform. My team is testing three providers. Right now I am looking for a counterpart in Germany.

The trap for a Portuguese speaker is that Portuguese uses one present for both jobs. Translated word by word, a sentence about a project that started in March comes out as this year we replace the platform — which in English sounds like something the bank does every single year, as a routine. The project disappears. On an exhibition floor that is expensive, because the only sentence that separates you from four hundred other visitors is the one about what is on your desk right now. Two more things to remember. In the negative and in questions the continuous keeps am, is or are — we are not testing that yet, are you working with any bank in Latin America? And a small group of verbs never take -ing, even about this exact moment: know, want, need, mean, cost. Not I am wanting a faster settlement. Say I want a faster settlement.
