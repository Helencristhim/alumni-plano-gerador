Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Do?

"The bank serves large corporations, and my division handles international products."

"This year we are replacing two platforms, and we are running a pilot on stablecoins."

"I oversee innovation, and I report to the head of the division."

"Right now I am looking for a provider, and my team is testing three of them."

The red verbs have two parts. Look at the words that open those two lines, and ask yourself when each sentence is true.

Comprehension

About Claire

1. Who is Claire, and how does she open the conversation?
She is Claire Devlin, from Northgate Payments, and she opens by asking whether he is with one of the banks.
2. Why does she ask what is on his desk this year?
Because half the exhibition floor sells the same thing, so the general description does not tell her anything.
3. What does she say the moment he mentions the two projects, and what does she ask next?
She says "now we are talking", and then she asks how long a remittance takes on his side today.
Listening 1 · A Voice Note

What Makes an Introduction Land

A delegate recording a note to her team after the first day on the exhibition floor. Sound first — no text.

1. How many banks did she speak to, and what does she say about their English?
People from about thirty banks, and she says the ones she remembers are not the ones with the best English.
2. What are the three things she wants in the first ninety seconds?
What part of the bank the person sits in, what they are working on right now, and what they came to the conference to find.
3. What does she say happens when somebody only gives a job title?
The conversation dies in front of the stand, and both people pretend it was the noise.
Listening 2 · The Other Side of the Counter

When the Salesperson Starts Taking Notes

Somebody who works a payments stand, on what he listens for. Sound first — no text.

1. What difference does he say he listens for?
The difference between a description and a problem.
2. Why does he say a general description is not useful to him?
Because it tells him nothing he could not read on the bank's website.
3. What does he say makes him start taking notes?
A sentence in the present continuous, because it means the person is inside a real project.
Listening 3 · Business Radio

Why Four Hundred Companies Fly to One Room

A short radio piece on cross-border payments and the conference that exists because of them. Sound first — no text.

1. How has that money travelled for most of the last fifty years, according to the piece?
Through a chain of correspondent banks, and each one adds a day and a fee.
2. What does the piece say has changed recently?
Who is in the room: ten years ago it was banks and card networks, and now it is also stablecoin issuers and infrastructure companies that did not exist before.
3. Why does the piece say a director of innovation walks the exhibition floor?
To find the three companies, out of four hundred, that are already solving the exact problem her team is working on that quarter.

Everybody on this floor can read the first four lines from a metre away. Only the last one has to be said out loud, and it is the one that stops people.
1. Which four lines can somebody read from the badge without asking?
The name, the organization, the role and the city.
2. Which line does the note at the bottom say has to be spoken, and why?
The last one, what he is here to find, because it is the only line the other person cannot read, and it is what stops people.
3. Turn the last line into a full sentence in the present continuous.
"I am looking for a faster settlement layer for our remittances."
