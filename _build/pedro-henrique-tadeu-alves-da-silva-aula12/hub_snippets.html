How a Show Gets Made

From a format bought in another country to an episode on Saturday night, and most of the work is never seen. Key words: a format, pre-production, a call sheet, to schedule, to clear something, a run of show, footage, to air, post-production, to cut something. Structure: the passive voice -- is / are + participle for a routine, was / were + participle for one finished moment.

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A format -- the basic idea and rules of a TV show, which can be sold to other countries
"The format was created in the Netherlands and sold all over the world."
Pre-production -- all the work that is done before recording starts
"Most of the show is decided in pre-production, months before the cameras arrive."
A call sheet -- the document that tells the crew where to be and at what time on a shooting day
"The call sheet is sent to everybody the night before."
To schedule -- to decide the day and time when something will happen
"The extra rehearsal was scheduled for Friday morning."
To clear something -- to get official permission to use something, especially a song
"If a song is not cleared in time, the singer has to choose another one."
A run of show -- the minute-by-minute plan of a live program
"Every second of the live show is controlled by the run of show."
Footage -- video material that has been recorded
"Ten hours of footage were recorded for one ninety-minute episode."
To air -- to be shown on television or played on the radio
"The episode airs on Saturday at half past nine."
Post-production -- all the work that is done after recording: editing, mixing and color
"The episode is really made in post-production, not on the stage."
To cut something -- to remove a part of a recording when you edit it
"The duet was perfect, but it was cut because it was too long."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

When somebody asks Pedro what he does, the easy answer is “I work on The Voice.” The real answer is a process, and most of it happens before anybody sings. The format was created in another country and was bought by the channel. In pre-production, singers are found in auditions, songs are chosen, and every song is cleared before the first rehearsal. Look at those verbs: be + participle. Nobody in these sentences does anything. The work is the subject.

That is the passive voice, and it has two everyday forms. For a routine, use is or are: the call sheet is sent to the crew the night before, and the rehearsals are scheduled around the band. For one finished moment, use was or were: last season, one song was not cleared in time, so it was cut, and a new one was chosen in twenty-four hours. Questions work the same way: When was it recorded? Is the footage sent tonight?

You only add by when the person really matters: the footage is recorded by fifteen cameras. Most of the time it does not, and that is the point. And be careful with two traps. Irregular verbs need the right participle — chosen, not choose; sent, not sended. And a verb with no object has no passive: the show happened, never was happened.

The rest is the vocabulary of the journey. On the day, every minute is controlled by the run of show, and hours of footage are recorded. In post-production that footage is edited, and whole performances are cut. Then the episode airs on Saturday — and notice that air is active here: the episode airs, it is not “aired by” anybody in normal speech.
