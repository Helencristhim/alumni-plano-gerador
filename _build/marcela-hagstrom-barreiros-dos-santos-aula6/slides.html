Chapter 4: The Code

Faster, More, the Best

Comparatives and superlatives

Grammar Discovery

Four Sentences That Compare

"Skimming is faster than reading every word."

"The methods section is more difficult than the abstract."

"The abstract is the shortest part of the article."

"For me, the conclusion is the most important part."

Look at the red words. Sentences 1 and 2 compare two things, with than. Sentences 3 and 4 compare all the parts, with the. Short words take -er and -est: faster, the shortest. Long words take more and the most.

JobFormExample
Short adjectives-er than · the -estfast → faster than → the fastest
Ending in -y-ier · the -iesteasy → easier → the easiest
Long adjectivesmore ... than · the most ...more difficult than → the most difficult
Irregulargood · badbetter / the best · worse / the worst
Two things / all thingsthan / theSkimming is faster than reading. The abstract is the shortest part.
The trapnever more + -erNot more faster or the most easy. Say faster, the easiest.

In one line: short words take -er and -est; long words take more and the most; good becomes better and the best.

Listening 1 · The Reading Tips

Professor Hill’s Advice

A university professor in London gives new master’s students advice about reading. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the slowest way to read, according to Professor Hill?
Reading every article from the first page to the last.
2. Which part is usually the most difficult?
The methods section.
3. Why does he prefer peer-reviewed articles?
Because they are more reliable than blogs or news websites.
Listening 2 · The Translator

Anna’s Method

A PhD student from Germany compares reading with a translator and reading in English. Sound first — no text.

1. What was the problem with the translator in Anna’s first year?
Her English was not better; she didn’t learn the words.
2. What does she write down now?
The five most important words.
3. How does she read now, compared with before?
Much faster than before, with a bigger vocabulary.
