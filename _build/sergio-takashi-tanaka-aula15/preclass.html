Lesson 15 -- Pre-class

Reporting What They Said

The call with the bank is over, and now the committee wants your version of it. Key words: readout, stance, claim, deny, admit, insist, reassure, paraphrase, verbatim, reiterate. Structure: reported speech (he said that, he told me that, he asked when, one step back in time).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Readout -- a short summary of what was said in a meeting or a call
"Give me the readout before I write the committee pack."
Stance -- the position a person takes and defends on an issue
"On the fee, their stance has not moved since March."
To claim -- to say something is true without giving proof
"He claimed that no client of our size had paid less."
To deny -- to say that something is not true
"He denied that the number had changed since March."
To admit -- to accept that something is true, often unwillingly
"He admitted that two hundred had been discussed."
To insist -- to say something firmly again, and not change it
"He insisted that the one percent was closed."
To reassure -- to say something that makes another person stop worrying
"He reassured me that the six months were in writing."
To paraphrase -- to say the same idea in your own words
"Paraphrase most of the call, but not that line."
Verbatim -- using exactly the same words, word for word
"I want that line verbatim, in quotation marks."
To reiterate -- to say the same point again to make it clear
"Do not reiterate the fee: we have already lost that one."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

The call with Nordbank ended at half past nine, and by ten the committee wanted the readout. On the ceiling, the banker said that they could go to one hundred and ninety, but not to two hundred. Later in the call he admitted that two hundred had been discussed on his side, which is useful, because it tells us the door is not closed. On the fee, his stance did not move once. He insisted that the one percent was closed, he claimed that nobody of our size had ever paid less, and he denied that the number had changed since March. He reassured me that the six months of grace were already in writing. Then he asked when we could give him an answer, and I told him that we would come back on Monday. Two lines go into the pack verbatim, in quotation marks; the rest I paraphrase. And I will not reiterate the fee on Monday: we have lost that one, and losing it twice costs us the tone of the whole call.

1. The banker's words were "The fee is closed." Why does the report say "was closed"?
A Because the fee changed after the call.
B Because reported speech moves the verb one step back in time.
C Because "is" cannot be used after the verb "insist".
2. Why is it "I told him that we would come back" and not "I said him"?
A Because tell takes a person right after it, and say never does.
B Because say can only be used in questions.
C Because tell is more formal than say in a report.
3. His question was "When can you give me an answer?" Which report is correct?
A He asked when could we give him an answer.
B He asked when did we can give him an answer.
C He asked when we could give him an answer.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

Reporting is not repeating. The tense steps back one place, and the reporting verb tells the room how it was said.

ShapeWhen you use itExample
say (that) + clauseNo person after it. In English you never say someone anything.He said that the fee was closed.
tell + person + (that) + clauseTell always needs the person right after it: told me, told the committee.He told me that they would move.
one step back in timeis → was · are → were · will → would · can → could · met → had met · has met → had met."It is closed" → He said it was closed.
ask + question word + subject + verbA reported question has no inversion and no do/did. A yes/no question takes if or whether.He asked when we could sign. He asked whether we had signed.
the reporting verb carries the toneclaim, deny, admit, insist, reassure, reiterate + that + clause. A request becomes ask + person + to + base verb: he asked me to send the sheet. Time and place move too: today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, here → there. Never "he said me", never "he asked when could we sign".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best option for each moment in the committee room.

The banker said: "We can go to one hundred and ninety." How do you report it?
A "He said me that they can go to one hundred and ninety."
B "He said that they could go to one hundred and ninety."
C "He told that they can go to one hundred and ninety."
He repeated the same answer on the fee three times and never moved. Which verb reports that best?
A "He reassured us that the fee was closed."
B "He insisted that the fee was closed."
C "He paraphrased that the fee was closed."
He asked: "Have you signed with the other bank?" How do you report the question?
A "He asked had we signed with the other bank."
B "He asked did we sign with the other bank."
C "He asked whether we had signed with the other bank."

A "He admitted that they were nervous about losing us."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think of a real call or meeting you had this month, with a bank, a vendor or another team. Give the readout out loud: what they said they could do, where they held their stance, anything they denied or admitted, what they asked you for, and the next step you agreed. Use he said that, he told me that, he insisted that and he asked when. Take your time.
