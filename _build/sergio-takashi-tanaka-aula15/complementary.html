"The language of lying" -- Noah Zandan (TED-Ed)

A free five-minute animated lesson on the words people choose when they are not telling the whole truth. Connection to Lesson 15: it is a tour of the difference between claim, deny and admit -- the three reporting verbs that decide how a committee reads your readout.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Every time a quoted line appears on screen, report it out loud: "He claimed that...", "He denied that...", "He admitted that...".

"Elizabeth Loftus: How Can Our Memories Be Manipulated?" -- TED Radio Hour, NPR

A free thirteen-minute segment in clear American English, with the full transcript on the same page. Connection to Lesson 15: the memory researcher explains how a remembered conversation quietly changes shape -- which is exactly why a readout is written the same day and why the deciding lines are kept verbatim.

Tip: listen once without the transcript, then read it and pick five lines. Report each one out loud with a different verb: she said that, she told him that, she claimed that, she admitted that, she asked whether.

"How to use reported speech" -- 6 Minute Grammar, BBC Learning English

A free six-minute grammar episode that drills the exact code you learned today: say and tell, the tense that steps back one place, and reported questions with no inversion. Connection to Lesson 15: the presenters report short conversations line by line, which is the same move you make when you carry a call into the committee room.

Tip: pause after every direct line they read out and report it yourself before they do. Finish by reporting three sentences a colleague said to you this week.
