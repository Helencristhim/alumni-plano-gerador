Chapter 3: The Code

Reported Speech

he said that · he told me that · he asked when

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"He said that the fee was closed."

"He told me that they would move on the ceiling."

"She asked when we could sign."

"He admitted that the committee had already met."

Every one of those men spoke in the present. What happened to the verb when the sentence stopped being his and became yours?

ShapeWhen you use itExample
say (that) + clauseNo person after it. You never say a person.He said that the fee was closed.
tell + person + (that) + clauseTell always needs a person right after it.He told me that they would move.
one step back in timeis → was · will → would · can → could · met → had met."It is closed" → he said it was closed.
asked + question word + subject + verbA reported question has no inversion and no do/did. Yes/no questions take if or whether.He asked when we could sign.
the reporting verb carries the toneclaim, deny, admit, insist, reassure, reiterate + that + clause. Time and place move too: today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, here → there. And ask + person + to + base verb reports a request: he asked me to send the sheet.

Choose the reporting verb first. The tense will follow it one step back.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"He said me that the fee was closed."
"He told me that the fee was closed."
"He said that he will confirm on Monday."
"He said that he would confirm on Monday."
"She asked me when could we sign."
"She asked me when we could sign."

Say never takes a person, tell always does. After said, will becomes would. And a reported question goes back to subject + verb, with no inversion.

The Artifact

Call Notes — Nordbank, Thursday 09:00 (Ref. 4471)
Taken by: Sergio Tanaka, Risk Management
Other side: Anders Holm, Nordbank — male

Ceiling: "We can go to one hundred and ninety, but not to two hundred."

Ceiling, later: "Two hundred was discussed here, yes."

Fee: "The one percent is closed. I cannot move it."

Grace period: "The six months are already in writing."

His question: "When can you give me an answer?"

1. How do you report the two ceiling lines to the committee?
He said that they could go to one hundred and ninety, but not to two hundred, and he admitted that two hundred had been discussed.
2. How do you report the fee line, with the right reporting verb?
He insisted that the one percent was closed and that he could not move it.
3. How do you report his question?
He asked when we could give him an answer.

Tell him Monday. And do not reiterate the fee on that call — we have already lost that one, so let us not pay for it twice.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I know the words of the readout: readout, stance, claim, deny, admit, insist, reassure, paraphrase, verbatim, reiterate.
I use say with no person and tell with a person, and I never say "he said me".
I move the tense one step back when I report: is to was, will to would, can to could, met to had met.
I report a question with no inversion, and I use if or whether for a yes/no question.
I choose the reporting verb that carries the tone: he insisted, he admitted, he denied, he reassured.
