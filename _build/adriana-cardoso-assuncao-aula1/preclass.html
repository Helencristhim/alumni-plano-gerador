Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

Tell Me About Yourself

The two-minute answer that opens every interview. Key words: background, track record, to lead, headcount, to report to, scope, to roll out, turnaround. Structure: present perfect with for, since, ever, never, just, already and yet.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These are the words that describe a career instead of a job.

Background -- your studies and your work experience before now
"My background is in organizational pedagogy, not in law."
Track record -- proof of what you can deliver, based on what you have already done
"I have a track record of turning difficult teams around."
To lead -- to be in charge of a team, an area or a project
"I have led human resources for the Americas region."
Headcount -- the number of people a company employs
"The headcount has grown from eight hundred to one thousand."
To report to -- to have someone as your direct manager
"I reported directly to the vice president in Chicago."
Scope -- everything that a role or a project includes
"The scope of the role is much wider than my last one."
To roll out -- to introduce a new policy or program across the whole company
"We rolled out the mental health program in eleven countries."
Turnaround -- the moment when a bad situation becomes a good one
"The turnaround took two years, and the numbers prove it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Renata Alves has worked in human resources for eighteen years. Her background is in psychology, and she has led the area in two logistics companies. She has never worked outside Brazil, but that is about to change: she has just accepted a regional role, and from January she will report to a director in Atlanta. The scope is much wider than her last one, and the headcount is almost double. She has already rolled out the new performance policy in three sites, but she has not presented the results to the global team yet. When people ask her whether she is ready, she says the same thing: I have done this before. Only the language is new.
Why does the text say "has worked for eighteen years" and not "worked for eighteen years"?
A Because she stopped working eighteen years ago
B Because she started eighteen years ago and she still works in HR today
C Because nobody remembers the exact year
What does "she has not presented the results to the global team yet" mean?
A She presented them a long time ago
B It has not happened until now, and it is still going to happen
C She has decided never to present them
"She has already rolled out the new performance policy in three sites." What does already add?
A That it happened sooner than people expected
B That it is going to happen next year
C That she did it twice

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect

GRAMMAR

Use the present perfect when the past is still connected to now: have / has + past participle. The moment is not the message — the result today is.

career experienceever / neverHave you ever led a global team? I have never worked for an American company.
recent newsjust / already / yetWe have just rolled out the policy. I have not met the regional team yet.
how longfor / sinceI have worked in HR for twenty years. I have led the area since 2018.
finished pastpast simple, with a momentI left the company last month.

Careful: the moment kills the present perfect. If you say yesterday, last month or in 2019, English goes to the past simple: "I left the company last month", never "I have left the company last month".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
An interviewer asks: "How long have you worked in human resources?" Which answer is correct?
A "I work in human resources since twenty years."
B "I have worked in human resources for more than twenty years."
C "I am working in human resources from twenty years."
You left your last company last month. What do you say?
A "I have left the company last month."
B "I left the company last month."
C "I am leaving the company last month."
The vice president asks something you did not prepare. What is the professional way to buy two seconds?
A "Sorry, my English is not good."
B "That is a fair question. Let me think for a second."
C "I do not understand your question."
A colleague asks what you introduced across the whole company. Which sentence is natural?
A "I have rolled out a health and wellbeing program in eleven countries."
B "I have rolled a health and wellbeing program eleven countries."
C "I roll out a health and wellbeing program since eleven countries."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
A recruiter says: "Tell me about yourself." Record ninety seconds: your background, how long you have worked in human resources, what you have led, and one thing you have rolled out. No dates — use for, since, already and yet.
