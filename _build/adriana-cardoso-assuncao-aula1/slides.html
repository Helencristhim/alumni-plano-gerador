Chapter 3: The Code

Started Before, Still True Today

One tense for experience, for recent news, and for how long

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Have in Common?

I have worked in human resources for twenty years.
She has never led a team outside Brazil.
We have just rolled out the new policy.
Have you ever worked for an American company?
Click to reveal the rule
None of them says when. That is the point. The present perfect (have / has + past participle) connects the past to now: experience over a career (ever, never), recent news that still matters (just, already, yet), and how long something has been true (for, since). The moment is not the message — the result today is.

So, Tell Me About Yourself

M
Adriana, thank you for making the time. I have read your profile, and Karen has told me a lot about you.
A
Thank you for the invitation. I have been looking forward to this conversation.
M
Let us start with the classic one. Tell me about yourself.
A
My background is in organizational pedagogy, and I have worked in human resources for more than twenty years. I have led the area for a global logistics company, and I have rolled out programs that the head office in Germany later adopted.
M
That is a strong track record. What is the scope you have managed so far?
A
Climate, litigation, benefits, succession and the cost of the workforce. I reported to a director in the United States, so most of that work has already happened in English.
M
Good. Now something that is not on your profile. What is the worst decision you have ever made about people?
A
That is a fair question. I kept a manager in a role for a whole year after the team had told me the problem. I have never made that mistake again.

About Michael

The scope she has managed so far.

He asks for the worst decision she has ever made about people. It is difficult because it is not on the profile, so there is no prepared answer.
Chapter 5: Practice

The Six Questions About Your Career

Every interview, every new team, every first meeting

Listening 2

The Opening of a Leadership Meeting

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What is Paul's background, and how did he arrive in HR?
He studied engineering and spent nine years in operations. He moved to HR almost by accident, because somebody had to fix a plant where nobody wanted to work.
2. How many people does his team support, and in how many countries?
Fourteen thousand people in eleven countries.
3. What does he ask the team to do when they present their numbers?
Not to hide the numbers that did not work.
