Chapter 4: The Code

Higher Than, Twice, Just Under

The three moves that make a number land, and the three that mark you out

Grammar Discovery

How Much Higher?

"Attrition is three points higher than it was last year."

"Cost per hire is almost twice the market benchmark."

"The variance is slightly smaller than we forecast in March."

"We are at just under two hundred, and flat since April."

All four give a number. What is each of the coloured parts adding that the bare figure does not?

FormWhat it doesExample
comparative + thancompares two figures directly, and is the first thing a director asksAttrition is three points higher than last year.
much / slightly / marginallysays HOW different, which is the second thing they askIt is slightly higher than the benchmark.
twice / half / a third ofproportion instead of difference, for numbers far apartCost per hire is almost twice the benchmark.
roughly / just under / in the region ofapproximation that is precise about being approximate -- safer than a wrong exact figureWe are at just under two hundred.
the trapmore big, more cheaper and the double of are the three that mark a non-native speaker instantly. Say bigger, cheaper, twice.

A number without a comparison invites the question compared to what?, and you lose the room while you look for the answer. Say the comparison in the same breath as the figure.

Chapter 5: The Ear

What Finance Actually Wants to Hear

A CFO and an HR director, on the same twenty minutes

Listening 1

The CFO, After the Meeting

A finance director explains what he listens for. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What does he say he is not listening for, and why?
He is not listening for the number itself, because he already has it -- it was in the file. He is listening for whether you know why it moved.
2. What kind of explanation does he say he can act on?
That the attrition is concentrated in one site, that it started when the shift pattern changed, and that two thirds of the leavers had less than a year of service.
3. What are the other two things he listens for?
Whether you tell him the bad number before he finds it, and whether you can say you do not know and give a date.
Listening 2

The Mistake Everybody Makes With HR Data

An HR director talks about her own worst presentation. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What was wrong with her worst presentation, in her own words?
It had eleven slides and every one was true, but eleven true things is not an argument, it is a filing cabinet.
2. What does she do now before she chooses the numbers?
She decides the sentence first -- one sentence -- and then every number either supports it or stays at home.
3. What surprised her about the result?
The questions got harder rather than easier, because people finally understood what she was claiming and could argue with it. She says a room that argues with you is a room that heard you.

One Sentence, Then the Numbers

Task: Decide the one sentence your quarter is actually about, say it, and then defend it with three numbers and nothing else. Three minutes. Your teacher will argue with the sentence, not with the figures.

No support. Target: one claim, three supporting numbers, and no number that does not support the claim.
