Lesson 12 -- Pre-class

Could This Work? -- Two Options and a Soft Proposal

Must closes the door, and the room looks for the problem. Could keeps it open, and the room helps you make it work. Key words: an option, an alternative, a downside, an upside, a risk, feasible, to consider, to suggest, a compromise, tight. Structure: could and might for possibility and soft proposals.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An option -- one of the things you can choose to do
"You asked for options, not a decision, so here are two."
An alternative -- another option you can choose instead of the first one
"Hiring is the more expensive alternative."
A downside -- the bad part of an option, the thing you lose
"The downside of option one is the north."
An upside -- the good part of an option, the thing you win
"The upside is bigger in the long run."
A risk -- something bad that can happen, but has not happened yet
"The risk is time: hiring might take two months."
Feasible -- possible to do in real life, with the time and money you have
"It is a compromise, but I think it is feasible."
To consider -- to think about something carefully before you decide
"We could consider option two in the budget for next year."
To suggest -- to offer an idea for other people to think about
"I would suggest option one as a pilot for one quarter."
A compromise -- an agreement where each side accepts a little less than it wanted
"It is not perfect for anybody. It is a compromise."
Tight -- with very little extra money or time, so it is hard to add anything
"The budget is tight this year, so hiring is the hard option."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

When you say "we must do this", the room starts looking for the problem. When you say "we could do this", the room starts looking for a way to make it work. That is the whole idea of a soft proposal. Could gives the room an option: we could move a team, we could hire, we could wait. Might tells the room something is uncertain: coverage might go up, the north might fall behind, hiring might not be fast.

The form is easy, and it is the same for everybody: could or might, then the verb with nothing added. No to, no -s, no past. We could hire, not we could to hire. It might go up, not it might goes up. For a polite question, put could first: Could we talk about it on Thursday?

A good proposal has a shape. Bring two options, not one: the first one and an alternative. For each one, say the upside and the downside, and name the risk. If the budget is tight, say so. Then suggest one, and say why it is feasible, even if it is a compromise. You can say the other one is something to consider later. And finish with a question, because a room that decides with you supports you.

1. What is the difference between "we could hire" and "hiring might take two months"?
A Could gives an option; might says something is uncertain.
B Could is for the past and might is for the future, so they talk about different times.

2. Which sentence is correct?

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Could and Might

GRAMMAR

Could opens a door. Might warns you about what is behind it. Both take the verb with nothing added.

JobFormExample
An optioncould + base verbWe could move a team.
Uncertain, maybemight + base verbCoverage might go up.
Maybe notmight not + base verbThe campaign might not be ready.
Polite questionCould + subject + base verb?Could we talk on Thursday?
Same for everybodyno -s, no to, no pastI could, she could, it might, they might
Stronger (lesson 7)should, must, have toWe should move a team. (a recommendation)

Never: we could to hire · it might goes up · we could moved · do we could talk?

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The board asks what you can do about the south. You have two ideas. You say:
A "We must move a team to the south immediately, and there is no other way to do it."
B "We could move a team, or we could hire a new one."
C "We could to move a team."
You are not sure the north will stay ahead of plan. You say:
A "The north will fall behind, I am completely sure of it."
B "The north might falls behind if we take the team away for three months."
C "The north might fall behind."
The budget has very little extra money. You want to say that hiring is difficult. You say:
A "We could hire, but the budget is tight."
B "The budget is feasible, so hiring is no problem for us this year."
C "No money."
You have finished your proposal and you want the room to decide with you. You say:
A "So that is decided, and we start on Monday with option one."
B "Could we talk about it on Thursday?"
C "Do we could talk on Thursday?"
