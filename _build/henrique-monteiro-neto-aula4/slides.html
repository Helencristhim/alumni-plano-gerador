Chapter 3: The Code

Same Facts, Different Weight

English can move the spotlight without changing a single fact

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"What strikes me is that nobody read the second report."

"It was the maintenance log, not the weather, that decided the case."

"The reason why the inquiry took two years is simpler than people think."

"All I am saying is that the timeline does not square with the statement."

Every one of these could be said in a plainer way. What does the longer version buy the speaker?

FormWhat it doesExample
What … is / was
(wh-cleft)		Puts the spotlight on what comes after is. Also buys you half a second of thinking time, out loud, without hesitating."What strikes me is the silence."
"What he did was ignore the alarm."
It was X that / who
(it-cleft)		Isolates ONE element as the decisive one — and usually implies the alternative it is not. Use that, never when or where."It was the log, not the weather, that decided it."
The reason why … is thatAnnounces that an explanation is coming, and warns the room it is shorter than expected. Note: is that, never is because."The reason why it took two years is that nobody asked."
All / The only thing
/ The thing is		Narrows the claim before anybody can widen it — the most defensive of the group, and very useful in an argument."All I am saying is that it does not square."
What happened was
· What we need is		Opens a narrative or a proposal at the top. In speech it is how fluent speakers buy a beat before a long sentence."What happened was that the backup never started."

Nothing here adds information. Every one of these moves the WEIGHT, and weight is what a listener remembers an hour later. It is also the structure that lets you start a difficult sentence before you have finished thinking it.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"What me strikes is the silence in the first hour."
"What strikes me is the silence in the first hour."
"It was in 2019 when the second report appeared."
"It was in 2019 that the second report appeared."
"The reason why it took two years is because nobody asked."
"The reason why it took two years is that nobody asked."

Three traps, all of them common in careful speakers. The wh-cleft keeps normal word order inside the clause (what strikes me, not what me strikes). The it-cleft takes that even with a time or a place. And the reason why … is because says the same thing twice — English wants is that.

The Investigator and the Nine Minutes

P
People always ask me what the hardest part of an inquiry is. It is not finding the evidence. It is deciding which question you are actually answering.
H
In this case the question looked obvious from the outside. Why did the city go dark?
P
And that is exactly the wrong question. What we should have asked in month one was why nine minutes passed between the alarm and the disconnection.
H
So the discrepancy was there from the start, and it was in the first report.
P
One line. We downplayed it, and I signed that page. It is the only thing about those two years I would undo.
H
What made you go back to it?
P
An engineer who would not let it go. She kept saying the statement did not square with the recording, and she was right, and we had all stopped listening to her.
H
All the same, the report says who was wrong, including you. That is more than most inquiries manage.
Comprehension

About Priya

1. What does Priya say is the hardest part of an inquiry?
Deciding which question you are actually answering, not finding the evidence.
2. Which question does she say they should have asked in month one?
Why nine minutes passed between the alarm and the disconnection.
3. What does she say she would undo?
Signing the page that downplayed the discrepancy in a single line.
4. Who made them go back to it?
An engineer who kept saying the statement did not square with the recording.
Listening 1

The First Statement

A press briefing, one week after. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the spokesperson give as the cause?
A storm of a severity the equipment had not been designed for.
2. What does he say about the nine minutes?
Almost nothing — a single line saying it is being reviewed.
3. What does he promise at the end?
A full report within ninety days, and that nothing will be withheld.
Listening 2

What the Log Actually Said

An engineer, two years later. Sound first — no text.

1. How many times had the inspection been postponed?
Twice, and then it was removed from the calendar altogether.
2. What does she say she was told when she raised it?
That it was a scheduling matter and not her department.
3. What does she say she is still not able to answer?
Who removed the inspection from the calendar, and why.
