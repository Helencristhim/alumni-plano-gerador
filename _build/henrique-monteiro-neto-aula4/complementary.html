Lesson 4 -- What Really Happened

Podcast
"I Was Wrong" — Planet Money, NPR

Half an hour in which journalists and economists go back to predictions they made publicly and explain, on the record, why they were wrong. Connection to Lesson 4: it is an hour of people separating what was beyond dispute from what they had assumed — and it is full of clefts, because that is how English admits an error without collapsing. Transcript beside the audio.

Tip: fifteen minutes is enough. Count how many times somebody starts a sentence with what happened was or the reason why — it is not a coincidence.

Talk
"How Data-Driven Journalism Illuminates Patterns of Injustice" — Alison Killing, TED

An architect turned investigator explaining how a story was pieced together from satellite images and public records, without ever visiting the place. Connection to Lesson 4: it is the vocabulary of today in action — corroborate, piece together, loose end, beyond dispute. English transcript on the page.

Tip: watch once with subtitles. Then say out loud, in three cleft sentences, what her investigation established, what it could not, and what the whole thing hinged on.

"Cleft Sentences" — Advanced Complex Sentences in English, with a Test

A focused treatment of it-clefts and wh-clefts with a short test at the end. Connection to Lesson 4: this is the grammar of today, kept outside the class exactly as you asked, and it runs comfortably inside twenty minutes.
