Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

Where the Market Is Going -- Trends, Share and the Bet You Made

Two people looking at the same category and disagreeing about the next three years. Key words: a trend, market share, demand, a competitor, a segment, to shift, consumption, an opportunity. Structure: will and going to -- what you expect against what you decided.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A trend -- a change in what people buy that continues over a long period
"The trend started in the cities and it is moving inland."
Market share -- the part of a whole market that one company sells, as a percentage
"We hold nineteen percent market share in the south."
Demand -- how much of a product people want to buy in a period
"Demand is going to grow faster than anybody can produce."
A competitor -- another company that sells to the same customers as you
"Our biggest competitor moved first, and it worked."
A segment -- a part of the market with the same kind of customer inside it
"The premium segment is small and it pays for itself."
To shift -- to move from one thing to another, usually slowly
"Consumers are shifting from the large pack to the small one."
Consumption -- how much of a product people use or buy in a period
"Consumption per person fell for the third year."
An opportunity -- a situation where a company can win something new, if it moves
"That is an opportunity, and it will not wait for us."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Two people are looking at the same page. Claire runs the category for an American distributor. She says consumption per person is flat, and asks Roberto what he expects. He answers with an opinion, and he marks it as one: "I think" the category "will grow" slowly, and the premium segment "will grow" fast.

Then she asks the other question, the one that separates a conversation from a presentation: what have you already decided? Now the form changes. "We are going to enter" the premium segment next year, he says, and "we are not going to touch" the low end. Nothing there is an opinion. The board approved it in March.

Claire pushes back with a fact: his biggest competitor entered two years ago and took four points of market share. He agrees. "We are late, and I will not pretend we are not." But habits are shifting, that is a trend and not one good year, and demand "is going to grow" faster than one company can serve. The opportunity is still there.

1. "I think the category will grow slowly." Why will and not going to?
A Because it is his opinion. Nothing has been decided and there is no evidence on the table.
B Because will is always used with the word category.
C Because going to is only used for the past.
2. "We are going to enter the premium segment next year." What does that form tell the listener?
A That he hopes it happens.
B That the decision is already taken. The board approved it in March.
C That it is less certain than will.
3. "Demand is going to grow faster than one company can serve." Why not will here?
A Because demand is a plural word.
B Because will cannot be used with demand.
C Because he is pointing at evidence he can see right now, not giving an opinion.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Will and Going To

GRAMMAR

Two ways to talk about what has not happened yet. In a meeting, the difference is the difference between a plan and an opinion.

UseFormExample
A decision already takenam / is / are going to + verbWe are going to enter the premium segment.
Evidence you can see nowis going to + verbDemand is going to grow — the data is on the table.
Your opinionwill + naked verbI think prices will fall after the harvest.
Negativewill not + verb · is not going to + verbIt will not be easy. We are not going to touch the low end.
QuestionWill + subject + verb? · Are you going to...?Will demand grow? Are you going to move first?
Short answerYes, we are. / No, it will not.Are you going to enter it? Yes, we are.

Never: Demand will to grow · We go to open two regions · Prices will falls · The board approved it, so next year we enter (for a decision, use going to).

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

Nothing is decided yet and you are giving your view of the next three years. You say:
A "I think the category will to grow slowly."
B "I think the category will grow slowly."
C "I think the category is grow slowly."
The board approved the plan in March. Somebody asks about next year. You answer:
A "We are going to enter the premium segment next year."
B "We go to enter the premium segment next year."
C "We enter the premium segment next year, maybe."
You want to know whether the other company has already decided to move. You ask:
A "You will move before us?"
B "Are you going to move before your competitor?"
C "Do you go to move first?"
Your position is weak and everybody in the room knows it. The strongest thing you can say is:
A "We are not late. The market is late."
B "We are late, and I will not pretend we are not. But demand is going to grow faster than one company can serve."
C "I prefer not to answer that question."
