Chapter 3: The Code

A Plan and an Opinion

Everybody in the room hears the difference

Discovery

Which Two Are Already Decided?

"Demand is going to grow in the premium segment."

"We are going to open two regions next year."

"I think prices will fall after the harvest."

"It will not be easy, but the opportunity is there."

The red form carries a decision already taken, or evidence you can point at right now. The blue form carries what you think. And after both of them, the verb is naked.

UseFormExample
A decision already takenam / is / are going to + verbWe are going to open two regions.
Evidence you can see nowis going to + verbDemand is going to grow — the data is on the table.
Your opinionwill + naked verbI think prices will fall.
Negativewill not + verb · is not going to + verbIt will not be easy. It is not going to wait.
QuestionWill + subject + verb? · Are you going to...?Will demand grow? Are you going to move first?
The trapnever to after willNot will to grow. Not we go to open.

In one line: going to = decided or visible. will = what I think.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

One Category, Two Bets

The call, the research briefing, and the page on the table

Dialogue

Two People, One Category

C
Roberto, before we talk about volumes, tell me what you expect from this category in three years.
R
I think the whole category will grow slowly, and the premium segment will grow fast. That is my opinion, not a plan.
C
Why premium? Our numbers say consumption per person is flat.
R
Flat in volume, yes. But people are buying less and paying more for it. Habits are shifting, and that is a trend, not one good year.
C
All right. And what have you already decided?
R
We are going to enter the premium segment next year. That one is decided. We are not going to touch the low end.
C
Your biggest competitor entered it two years ago and took four points of market share. You are late.
R
We are late, and I will not pretend we are not. But demand is going to grow faster than one company can serve, so there is still an opportunity.
C
That is the first honest answer I have had all week. Send me the plan and I will read it before the category review.
Comprehension

About Claire

1. What do Claire’s numbers say about consumption, and what does she conclude from that?
Her numbers say consumption per person is flat, and she uses that to question the premium argument.
2. What does she say about the competitor?
That the competitor entered the premium segment two years ago and took four points of market share, so Roberto is late.
3. What changes her tone at the end, and what does she agree to do?
He admits he is late instead of arguing. She calls it the first honest answer of the week, and agrees to read the plan before the category review.
Listening 1 · The Research Briefing

What the Data Actually Says

A market researcher summarizing four years of numbers. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What happened to volume, and what happened to the money, in the same four years?
Volume per person was flat, almost zero growth. The money people spend on the category grew nineteen percent.
2. What is her explanation for those two numbers together?
People are buying less and paying more for what they buy: they moved to smaller packs and better products.
3. What does she warn the room about at the end?
That a trend of four years is not a promise. If income falls, the same people go back to the large cheap pack in one quarter.
Listening 2 · How He Decides

Betting on a Trend

A category director on when he moves, and when he waits. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is the difference, for him, between a trend and a fashion?
A trend survives a bad year. A fashion does not: it disappears the moment money gets tight.
2. What mistake does he say he made once, and what did it cost?
He waited for certainty. By the time he was sure, two competitors were already there and he paid three times more to enter.
3. What is his rule now, in one sentence?
Enter small and early rather than big and late: a small bet on a real trend beats a large bet on a certain one.

The Page on the Table

CATEGORY OUTLOOK — NEXT THREE YEARS
Packaged foods · prepared for the category review

Premium segmentprojection: +7% a year

Our market share, premium0% — we are not in it
Main competitor, premiumentered 2 years ago · 4 points
Board decision, Marchapproved: enter premium next year
Everything above the last line is a projection. The last line is a decision.
1. Which line on this page would you say with going to, and which ones with will? Why?
The last line, because it is a decision already approved: we are going to enter premium next year. The projections take will, because they are what we expect: the premium segment will grow seven percent a year.
2. Volume is flat but the money grew nineteen percent. What does that tell you about the customer?
The customer is buying the same quantity, or less, and paying more for it: the shift is to better and smaller, not to more.
3. Where is the opportunity on this page, and where is the risk?
The opportunity is the premium segment, growing seven percent a year with our share at zero. The risk is that the competitor has been there for two years and already holds four points.
