Lesson 3 -- Reading the Room

Series
The Crown -- Season 1, Episode 2

An entire series about people saying difficult things politely, and about what happens in the minutes before the official conversation starts. Almost every scene is someone reading a room and adjusting. The accent is British, and that is on purpose: your real English is spoken with an Italian head and three Latin American teams, not with Americans.

Tip: watch one episode with English subtitles and count how many times a character changes register between two scenes. Note the exact phrase they use to close a conversation without ending it.

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart -- episodes on connecting and building rapport

Back to the Stanford business school podcast, this time for the episodes on rapport, listening and speaking without preparation. This is the one to use as your two-minute reset: put it on before an English call and let your ear switch languages before the client does it for you.

Tip: use it exactly as the second listening in class described. Two minutes of English out loud or in your ear, right before the call. Then notice whether the first ten minutes feel different.

Listen on Stanford GSB ↗
YouTube
Celeste Headlee -- 10 Ways to Have a Better Conversation (TED)

Eleven minutes from a professional interviewer on how to talk to anyone. Her central point is the one from today's first listening: you do not need to be interesting, you need to be interested. Rule number one on her list happens to be the hardest for busy executives, and you will know which one it is before she says it.

Tip: pick the two rules you break most often at work, in Portuguese as well as in English. Try one of them in your next call and see what changes.
