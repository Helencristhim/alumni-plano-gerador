Chapter 3: The Code

A Past That Is Still Here

Where the third conditional meets today

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"If I had kept up my English, I would not be nervous before these calls."

"If we had not lost that broker last year, we would be ahead of target now."

"If I had not moved into the commercial side, I would not be running this region today."

"If they had told us in March, we would not be redoing the plan this week."

The IF part is exactly what you learned last week. Something disappeared from the other side. What, and what does that change?

SentenceResult sideWhen it happened
Third conditional
(last week)		would have + past participleThe result belongs to the past. I would have learned...
Mixed conditional
(today)		would + base verb — no haveThe result is now. I would not be nervous...
The conditionIdentical in both: If + had + past participle. Only the result side moves.
Time markers that give it awaynow · today · this week · still · at the moment on the result side pull would be, never would have been.
Why it is worth havingIt is how you explain a present situation without blaming anyone: the cause is in the past, the consequence is on the table today.
The trap, againStill never If I would have kept up. "Would" never follows "if" — in any conditional.
Practice

Past Cause, Present Result

Say it first, then click to check

"If we had hired a second underwriter in January, I ___would not be reviewing every file myself today." (not review)
"If the group had approved the limits in March, we ___would be in a much better position now." (be)
"If I had not spent three years in the technical area, I ___would not understand these files at all." (not understand)
"If she had taken the head office job, she ___would be living in Milan today." (live)
"If they had flagged the exposure last year, nobody ___would be redoing the plan this week." (redo)
The Structure

Open, Bridge, Exit

Twelve minutes with no agenda is not empty time. It has a shape, and you can run it.

1 · Open — give something first

"How was the flight? I flew that route in March and the jet lag took me three days."

2 · Bridge — you decide when it starts

"Before the others join — is there anything you want to land today that is not on the agenda?"

3 · Exit — close it without cutting them off

"Let us circle back to that at lunch. I want to hear the rest properly, not in four minutes."

Give something → ask the one useful question → close the door gently, and leave it unlocked.

Chapter 4: Twelve Minutes in Milan

Meet Diego

Your counterpart in Mexico · and today, in English

Dialogue

Before the Others Join

D
Denise! Finally in the same room. How was the flight?
D
Long. I landed at six and the jet lag is winning. I flew this route in March and it took me three days to recover.
D
Three days. That is optimistic. And here we are, doing small talk in English, of all things.
D
I know. If we had met in Bogota, this would be a completely different conversation. But the room is half Italian, so English it is.
D
Honestly, my English gets better when I stop translating. Anyway — we never catch up properly. Two years of calls and always fifteen minutes.
D
That is my fault as much as yours. I am always swamped on Mondays. Before the others join — is there anything you want to land today that is not on the agenda?
D
One thing. The broker network. If Mexico had built what you built in the south, we would not be chasing the same three clients now. I want to steal your model.
D
Steal it, please. Let us circle back to that at lunch — I want to hear the whole thing properly, not in four minutes.
D
Lunch it is. And there is Milan, walking in nine minutes late, as always.
Comprehension

About Diego

1. What does he find strange about the conversation?
That the two of them are making small talk in English, when it would be far easier in another language.
2. What does he say makes his English better?
Stopping translating in his head.
3. What does he want that is not on the agenda?
Her broker network model — he says Mexico would not be chasing the same three clients if they had built one.
Listening

You Do Not Have to Be Charming

Thirty years of international meetings. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does he say people get wrong about small talk in a second language?
They think they have to be charming or funny. Nobody expects that — they expect you to be interested.
2. Which three questions does he say cover almost everything?
How was the journey? How long are you here for? And what are you working on that you actually enjoy?
3. Why does he prefer the third question?
Because it gets a real answer instead of a job title, and people remember who asked it.
4. What does he say about silence?
Two seconds of silence feels like twenty when the language is not yours, but it sounds normal to everyone else.
Listening 2

Switching Languages at Four in the Afternoon

Someone who runs a week in three languages. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What happens to her English when she has spent the day in another language?
The first ten minutes come out with the wrong shape — the words are English but the sentences are not.
2. What is the two-minute reset she describes?
Reading something out loud in English, or listening to two minutes of it, before the call starts.
3. What does she say she would be doing if she had not built that habit?
She would still be spending the first ten minutes of every call apologising and warming up.
4. What is her point about the mixing itself?
It is not a sign that you are losing a language — it is the cost of having three, and it goes away with a warm-up.
Chapter 5: Practice

Your Twelve Minutes

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "I am here since Monday, so the jet lag is better now."
"I have been here since Monday..." (since always takes the present perfect)
2. "Your new regional lead is very sympathetic."
"...is very likeable." or "...is very easy to get on with." (sympathetic = feeling sorry for someone)
3. "How do you call this in English?"
"What do you call this in English?" or "What is this called in English?"
4. "If I would have known you were coming, I would have booked lunch."
"If I had known you were coming, I would have booked lunch."
