Chapter 4 -- It Tastes Sweet

Language Discovery

It tastes sweet. / It looks delicious.

Discovery

Listen and Notice

Look at the orange words. What comes after them -- a noun or an adjective?

"Ayran tastes salty."

"The baklava looks delicious."

"The coffee smells wonderful."

"What does it taste like? -- It tastes a little sour."

The Rule

Describe with Your Senses

SensePatternExample
Mouthit tastes + adjectiveIt tastes sweet.
Eyesit looks + adjectiveIt looks delicious.
Noseit smells + adjectiveIt smells good.
AskWhat does it taste like?What does it taste like?
A little / verytastes a little / very + adjectiveIt tastes a little sour.

Key idea: After tastes, looks and smells, use an adjective: It tastes sweet, It looks good -- never "It tastes well". With it, add -s. To ask, say What does it taste like?

Chapter 5 -- Real Voices

Lunch with Emre

A conversation, a menu board and two messages

Dialogue

At the Lokanta

E

"Here we are, Marlene. There is no menu. You look at the food and you point."

M

"Everything looks delicious! What are those green things?"

E

"They are peppers stuffed with rice. They taste a little sour with lemon."

M

"I would like to try them. Can I have a half portion?"

E

"Sure. And I recommend the grilled chicken. It smells wonderful, right?"

M

"Yes, it does. And what is that white drink? What does it taste like?"

E

"That is ayran, a yogurt drink. It tastes salty, not sweet. Try it!"

M

"Mmm, it tastes strange but good! And Turkish coffee after lunch?"

E

"Yes, but it is a little bitter. Have a piece of baklava with it."

Comprehension

About Emre

1. What does Emre say about the menu at the lokanta?
There is no menu -- you look at the food and point.
2. How does Emre describe the stuffed peppers?
They are stuffed with rice and taste a little sour with lemon.
3. What does Emre recommend?
The grilled chicken.
4. What does Emre say about ayran and Turkish coffee?
Ayran tastes salty; the coffee is a little bitter, so have baklava with it.
