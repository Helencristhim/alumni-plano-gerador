Chapter 3: The Code

Signposting & Turn-Taking

start by · move on to · jump in · hand over

Grammar Discovery

Read and Notice

"I would like to start by giving you a short overview."

"Let me move on to the exposure."

"Sorry to jump in, but that figure changed on Friday."

"To wrap up, the bottom line is that we need a decision."

What comes after the orange words?

MomentPatternExample
Openstart by + verb-ingI would like to start by giving you an overview.
Move onlet me + base verb; move on to / turning to + nounLet me move on to the exposure.
Highlightthe point I want to highlight is + noun / that + clauseThe point I want to highlight is the funding cost.
Take a turnSorry to jump in, but... / Let me finish this point and I will come back to you.Sorry to jump in, but that figure changed.
CloseTo wrap up, ... / The bottom line is that ... / I will hand over to + person. Never "let me to move" and never "start by explain".

Signposts do one job: they tell the room where you are and where you are going next.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I would like to start by explain the numbers."
"I would like to start by explaining the numbers."
"Let me to move on to the next point."
"Let me move on to the next point."
"I want to make a question about the handout."
"I would like to ask a question about the handout."

After by, use the -ing form. After let me, use the base verb with no to. And in English you ask a question — you never "make" one.

Detail

Keep or Give the Floor?

Hold it politely · hand it over cleanly

Someone interrupts and you are not finished: "___Let me finish this point and I will come straight back to you."
You want to add something to what a colleague is saying: "___Sorry to jump in, but that number changed on Friday."
You give the turn to your colleague: "___I will now hand over to Marina, who will cover the credit numbers."
You are running out of time: "___In the interest of time, let me move on to the recommendation."

The Brief Before Thursday

A voicemail from Marina. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. How long does Sergio have with the board, and what should he watch?
Twelve minutes, not twenty, so he has to watch his pace.
2. What does Marina want him to do if Claudia jumps in?
Finish the point first and not lose the floor, then take her question.
3. How should he end the presentation?
Wrap up with one sentence, the bottom line, and nothing more.
Listening 2

Paulo's Warning

A colleague who got it wrong last year. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does Paulo say the board does NOT want?
To be walked through every slide; they want the three numbers that changed and the recommendation.
2. What went wrong for Paulo last year?
He let a director jump in twice, never got the floor back, and his last two points died on the page.
3. Which phrase does Paulo recommend?
Let me finish this point and I will come straight back to you.
