Lesson 12 -- Pre-class

Presenting to Stakeholders

Standing in front of the board and staying in control of the room. Key words: overview, handout, walk through, highlight, the floor, jump in, hand over, wrap up, bottom line, pace. Structure: signposting and turn-taking language -- how to open, move on, highlight, keep the floor and close.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Overview -- a short description of the main points, without the details
"I will open with a one-page overview, not with the model."
Handout -- a printed page you give people so they can follow the talk
"The handout goes out before I speak, not during."
To walk through -- to explain something step by step, from start to finish
"Let me walk you through the numbers, page by page."
To highlight -- to give special attention to one important point
"The point I want to highlight is the funding cost."
The floor -- the right to speak in a meeting
"If I lose the floor early, the second point never happens."
To jump in -- to interrupt politely in order to add something
"Sorry to jump in, but that figure changed on Friday."
To hand over -- to give the turn to speak to another person
"At the end I hand over to Marina for the credit numbers."
To wrap up -- to finish a talk with a short, clear ending
"To wrap up, the bottom line is that we need a decision."
Bottom line -- the single most important conclusion
"The bottom line is that funding costs are eating the margin."
Pace -- the speed at which you speak or move through your points
"You have twelve minutes with the board, so watch your pace."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Thursday was my first presentation to the board, and I did not open with the model. I started by giving them a one-page overview, with the handout already on the table, closed. Then I said: let me walk you through what changed since June. After the first topic I said let me move on to the exposure. The point I wanted to highlight was the funding cost, so I slowed my pace there and repeated the number twice. A director tried to jump in after ninety seconds, but I did not lose the floor: I said let me finish this point and I will come straight back to you, and she waited. At the end I handed over to Marina for the credit numbers, and she gave the floor back to me. To wrap up, I said the bottom line in one sentence and stopped.

1. Why did the writer open with an overview instead of the model?
A Opening with the model loses the room in the first minute.
B The board asked him not to use a model at all.
C The model was not ready on Thursday morning.
2. What does "let me move on to the exposure" signal to the room?
A That he is asking the board for permission to speak.
B That he is finished with one topic and is changing to the next one.
C That he is giving the turn to another speaker.
3. Why did the writer say "let me finish this point and I will come straight back to you"?
A To keep the floor politely without ignoring the director.
B To tell the director that her question was not important.
C To hand the presentation over to the director.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Signposting & Turn-Taking

GRAMMAR

Signposts tell the room where you are and where you are going next. Each one has a fixed shape -- learn the shape, not only the words.

MomentPatternExample
Openstart by + verb-ing (never "by explain")I would like to start by giving you an overview.
Move onlet me + base verb (never "let me to move"); move on to / turning to + nounLet me move on to the exposure.
Highlightthe point I want to highlight is + noun, or is that + clauseThe point I want to highlight is the funding cost.
Take a turnSorry to jump in, but ... (to interrupt) · Let me finish this point and I will come straight back to you (to keep the floor)Sorry to jump in, but that figure changed.
Hand over & closeGive the turn with I will hand over to + person; close with To wrap up, ... and the bottom line is that + clause. One more thing: in English you ask a question -- you never "make" one.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best option for each moment in front of the board.

You have the floor and the handout is on the table. How do you open?
A "I would like to start by explain the model."
B "I would like to start by giving you a one-page overview."
C "So, the numbers, page four, second column."
You have finished the first topic and want to change to the exposure. Which is best?
A "Let me to move on to the exposure."
B "Now the exposure."
C "Let me move on to the exposure."
A director interrupts you in the middle of a point. Which is best?
A "Let me finish this point and I will come straight back to you."
B "Please, I am talking now."
C "Sorry, go ahead, I will stop here."
You have one minute left and want the board to remember one thing. Which is best?
A "That is all, thank you very much for the attention."
B "To wrap up, the bottom line is that we need a decision on hedging before December."
C "I want to make a last question about the funding cost."
