"How to speak so that people want to listen" -- Julian Treasure (TED)

A sound expert explains why some speakers hold a room and others lose it, and what the voice actually does: volume, silence, and above all pace. Connection to Lesson 12: this is the delivery side of the language you practiced -- the same overview, said too fast, is a different presentation.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Then read your own opening sentence out loud twice, once fast and once slow, and notice which one sounds like the board would listen.

Podcast
"Quick Thinks: Tips for Speaking up in Virtual Meetings" -- Think Fast, Talk Smart (Stanford GSB)

A short, free episode with the full transcript on the same page. Matt Abrahams gives three ways to insert your idea into a meeting that is already running, plus a simple structure for saying it clearly. Connection to Lesson 12: this is the turn-taking half of today's lesson -- how to jump in without sounding rude, and how to keep your point short once you have the floor.

Tip: listen once without the transcript, then read it and mark every phrase he uses to enter the conversation. Say two of them out loud before your next call.
