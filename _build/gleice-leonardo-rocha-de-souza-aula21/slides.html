Chapter 3 -- The Promise Code

One Small Word: Will

Promises, predictions and plans

Grammar Discovery

What Do the Orange Words Have in Common?

"I will visit every pharmacy chain myself."

"Your revenue will grow in the first year."

"I will send you the plan on Friday."

"I am going to open my company in March."

Three use will. One uses going to. What is the difference?

UseFormExample
PromiseI / we + will + verbI will visit every chain.
Predictionit / they + will + verbYour revenue will grow.
Offer, decided nowI will + verbI will send you the plan.
Plan, decided befoream / is / are going to + verbI am going to open my company in March.
Negativewill not (won't) + verbYou will not pay a fixed fee.
QuestionWill + subject + verb?Will the market grow?
Neverwill + to / will + -ingNot I will to send. Not I will sending.

In one line: will + verb for a promise or a prediction; going to for a plan you already have.

Chapter 4 -- The Pitch

Tuesday, the Call with Mark

One call, two recordings, one business plan

Dialogue

Gleice and Mark

M
Hi Gleice, thanks for the call. So, tell me about your plan for Brazil.
G
Thank you, Mark. I am going to open my company in March, and I will represent your vitamins in Brazilian pharmacies.
M
Brazil is a big market. What will you do in the first year?
G
I will visit fifty pharmacy chains, and I will deliver a sales report every month.
M
And what about the investment? We are a small brand.
G
The investment is small. You will not pay a fixed fee. You will only pay when I sell.
M
I like that. Do you think the revenue will grow fast?
G
Yes. I think the revenue will grow in six months. I promise I will send you the full plan on Friday.
Comprehension

About Mark

1. What does Mark want to know first?
Gleice's plan for Brazil.
2. What does Mark say about his company?
It is a small brand.
3. What does Mark ask about the revenue?
If the revenue will grow fast.

Before the Call

A voice message from Mark Collins, Brightleaf Vitamins. Sound first -- no text.

Twelve products. The best one is vitamin D for adults.
2. What kind of partner does Mark want?
A partner who really knows pharmacies, not only a person who sells.

What she will do in the first six months, and how she will find new pharmacies.
Listening 2 -- The Coach

Three Rules for a Pitch

A business coach talks about pitching to a foreign brand. Sound first -- no text.

1. What is rule one?
Start with the opportunity: say why your market will grow.
2. Why should you say "I will" and not "maybe I can"?
Because a brand wants a partner who is sure.
3. How many numbers are enough in a pitch?
Two or three.
