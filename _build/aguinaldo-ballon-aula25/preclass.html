Integrated Production Checkpoint -- The Panel, This Time With a Yes

A call with the moderator of the New York panel, and every tool from lessons 21 to 24 together. Key words: to field, a curveball, to steer, a soundbite, to pivot, to segue, poised, off-topic, to build on, airtime. Tools: focus, hedging, attribution and signposts.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To field -- to deal with questions, especially difficult ones
"She fielded twenty questions from the audience in ten minutes."
A curveball -- a question or a problem that you do not expect
"The question about the elections was a curveball."
To steer -- to guide a conversation in a direction you choose
"He steered the discussion back to infrastructure."
A soundbite -- a short, memorable sentence that people repeat
"Prepare one soundbite for the end of the panel."
To pivot -- to change the direction of an answer smoothly
"When they asked about politics, she pivoted to regulation."
To segue -- to move smoothly from one topic to the next
"Let me segue from the tariff to the timeline."
Poised -- calm and confident, even under pressure
"Even with the hard question, he looked poised."
Off-topic -- not related to the subject you are discussing
"The question was off-topic, so she answered in one sentence."
To build on -- to use an idea as a base and add to it
"I would like to build on what Professor Lima said."
Airtime -- the time that each person has to speak
"The moderator gives every panelist the same airtime."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Aguinaldo is preparing the New York panel. He starts his opening with one idea: "What foreign investors need is stable rules." He does not overstate: the new rule is likely to attract more funds.

In the discussion, he decides to build on another panelist's point: "As Professor Lima argues, independence brings investment." If someone asks a curveball about politics, he will stay poised, answer briefly and pivot: "That is a fair question. What I can say is that the rules are stable."

For the closing round, he prepares one soundbite: "So the bottom line is that people matter as much as rules." Notice: no new grammar. Four tools, each one in its moment.

1. How does Aguinaldo open the panel?
A With a long list of all the projects he worked on as a lawyer and as a CEO in Brazil.
B With one idea.
C With a joke about the weather in New York and a question to the audience about Brazil.
2. Why does he say "is likely to attract" and not "will attract"?
A Because "will" is never used in formal English, and a panel in New York is a very formal event.
B Because "likely to" is the past form of "will", and the rule was approved last year.
C Because he does not want to overstate.
3. What will he do with a curveball about politics?
A Answer briefly and pivot.
B Leave the stage.
C Ignore it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Four Tools, Four Moments

GRAMMAR

No new structure today. Each tool from lessons 21 to 24 has a natural moment in a panel.

JobFormExample
Open with one idea (21)What... is / It is... thatWhat investors need is stable rules.
Answer without overstating (22)likely to / tend to / to some extentThe rule is likely to attract funds.
Credit a panelist (23)As X argues, / I would like to build on what X saidAs Professor Lima argues, ...
Field a curveballThat is a fair question. What I can say is...What I can say is that the rules are stable.
Close the panel (24)So the bottom line is / To recapSo the bottom line is that people matter.
QuestionWhat do investors need? · Is it likely to work?Is the rule likely to work?

Never: Is the rules that matter · likely to attracting · According to me · To resume, ...

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A question from the floor is about the elections, not about regulation. You say:
A "That is a fair question. What I can say is that the rules are stable."
B "This question is off-topic, so I will not answer it, and please ask about regulation only."
C "I do not know anything about politics in Brazil, I am only a lawyer, sorry, next question."
You agree with another panelist and want to add an idea. You say:
A "According to me, Professor Lima is right, and I want to say the same thing again."
B "I would like to build on what Professor Lima said."
C "As Professor Lima argues that, independence brings investment to all the countries."
The moderator asks for your final sentence. You say:
A "To resume, people matter as much as rules, and rules matter as much as people."
B "Is the people that matter as much as the rules in all the countries of Latin America."
C "So the bottom line is that people matter as much as rules."
Each panelist has two minutes to answer. This is the panelists':
A airtime.
B soundbite.
