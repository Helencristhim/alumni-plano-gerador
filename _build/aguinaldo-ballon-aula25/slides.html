Grammar Discovery

Which Tool, Which Moment?

"What foreign investors need is stable rules."

"The new rule is likely to attract more funds."

"As Professor Lima argues, independence brings investment."

"So the bottom line is that people matter as much as rules."

Each tool has a natural moment in a panel. Think of the opening, your answers, the curveball from the floor, and your final sentence. Where does each one go?

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Panel, at Real Speed

One call, two recordings, one run sheet

Dialogue

The Call With the Moderator

E
Aguinaldo, I'm so glad you said yes this time. Let me walk you through the panel.
A
Thank you, Erin. Honestly, what worries me most is the questions from the floor.
E
That's normal. You'll field two or three, and one of them is likely to be a curveball about politics.
A
Then I'll give a short answer and pivot back to regulation. It's the rules that I can really talk about.
E
Perfect. And if another panelist says something you like, feel free to build on it. The audience loves that.
A
Good. As Professor Lima argues, independence brings investment. I can start from her point.
E
Great. Just remember, everyone gets the same airtime: about two minutes per answer.
A
Understood. So, to recap: three minutes to open, two per answer, and one soundbite to close.
Comprehension

About Erin

1. What kind of curveball does Erin expect from the floor?
A question about politics.
2. What does Erin say the audience loves?
When a panelist builds on what another panelist said.
3. How long should each answer be, according to Erin?
About two minutes.
Listening 1 · The Briefing

Three Parts, One Sentence

The moderator briefs all the panelists on the format of the panel. Sound first — no text.

1. How long is each opening, and can panelists use slides?
Three minutes, with no slides.
2. What does the moderator want in the closing round?
One final sentence, a soundbite the audience can take home.
3. What should panelists do with an off-topic question?
Answer briefly and pivot back to the theme of the panel.
Listening 2 · The Former Panelist

What Surprised Me Most

A lawyer who was on the same panel two years ago shares his advice. Sound first — no text.

1. What surprised him most on the panel?
The questions from the floor, not the questions from the moderator.
2. What does he say the audience remembers?
Your attitude, not your grammar.
3. Why should you prepare your final sentence in advance?
Because it is the sentence people will quote on social media the next day.
