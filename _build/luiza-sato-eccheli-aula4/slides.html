Chapter 4: The Code

The Clause That Tells You Which One

Four sentences about courts, and the commas that change the meaning

Grammar Discovery

Why a Comma Here?

"The court which hears disputes between states is in The Hague."

"The Inter-American Court, which is based in San José, hears human rights cases."

"A citizen whose rights were violated can file a complaint."

"Students who confuse the courts make the same mistake."

Look at the red and the blue parts. If you delete the red part, do we still know WHICH court or WHICH citizen? And if you delete the blue part? Why does only the blue one have commas?

JobFormExample
Identify which one (essential)noun + who / which / that + clause, no commasThe court which hears disputes between states is in The Hague.
Add extra information (not essential)noun, + who / which + clause, (commas)The Court, which is based in San José, hears human rights cases.
Possessionwhose + nounA citizen whose rights were violated.
A placewhere + clauseThe Hague is the city where both courts sit.
People or things in essential clausesthat can replace who or whichThe judges that heard the case.
Extra informationnever that after a commaBrazil, which accepted the court in 1998, …
The trapnever the court who; never whose itsNot a citizen whose his rights. Say a citizen whose rights.

In one line: No commas when the clause tells you which one; commas when it only adds information, and never that after a comma.

Quick Fire

One Question at a Time

Listening 1 · A Lecture

Jurisdiction First

A professor of international law on the first question of every case. Sound first — no text.

1. Where does the professor say her students start, and why is it the wrong place?
They start with who should win, but the first question is whether the court has jurisdiction.
2. How is an international court different from a national court?
A state is judged only if it has agreed to be judged; national courts have jurisdiction almost automatically.
3. When a court says it has no jurisdiction, what is it NOT saying?
It is not saying that the state was innocent; the question was never allowed into the room.
Listening 2 · A Radio Explainer

A Ruling Nobody Follows

A short radio explainer on what happens after a judgment. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the explainer say does NOT happen when a country ignores a ruling?
No international police arrives at the border.
2. What are two quieter ways enforcement works?
Any two: damage to the government’s reputation, pressure from a political body, and lawyers and judges using the ruling to change national law.
3. What do researchers say about compliance?
Full compliance is rare, but partial compliance is common, and it often arrives years after the ruling.
