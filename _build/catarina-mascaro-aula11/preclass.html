Lesson 11 — Pre-class

Have You Ever?

Eight words for talking about experiences (ever, never, twice, so far, bucket list, brave, scary, unforgettable) and the present perfect: "Have you ever...?", "I’ve never...", and when to switch to the past simple.

Stage 1.1: Experience Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Ever — at any time in your life, used in questions
"Have you ever seen snow?"
Never — not one time, not at any moment
"I have never been to Japan."
Twice — two times in total
"I have been on that ride twice."
So far — from the start until now
"So far, I have read three chapters."
Bucket list — the things you want to do one day
"Seeing snow is on my bucket list."
Brave — not afraid to do something difficult or scary
"She was brave and jumped first."
Scary — it makes you feel afraid
"That film was really scary."
Unforgettable — so special you will remember it forever
"My first concert was unforgettable."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read Bia’s post. Notice when she gives a date and when she does not. Then answer the questions.

My bucket list, part one. So far, I have done three things on it. I have been to a big concert — I went last March, and it was unforgettable. I have tried surfing twice. The first time, I fell off the board about twenty times. And I have sung in front of the whole school, which was the scariest thing I have ever done. But there is a lot I have never done. I have never seen snow. I have never been on a really big roller coaster, because I am not that brave. Yet. So, my question for you: have you ever done something that scared you? Tell me in the comments.
How many things on her list has Bia done so far?
A Three.
B Two — the concert and the surfing, but not the singing.
Why does she say "I went" and not "I have been" for the concert?
A Because the concert was unforgettable and she wants to make it sound more important.
B Because she gives a date: last March.
What has Bia never done?
A She has never tried surfing, because the board scared her.
B She has never seen snow.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

have / has + been, seen, tried...

Use it for experiences in your life so far, when the date is not important. When you say WHEN, switch to the past simple.

Yes

I’ve been to Rio.
She’s tried it twice.

No

I’ve never seen snow.
He hasn’t been there.

Question

Have you ever been...?
Yes, I have. / No, I haven’t.

be → been  ·  see → seen  ·  eat → eaten  ·  try → tried  ·  sing → sung
✗ I have been there last year  ·  ✓ I went there last year (a date = past simple)
✗ Have you ever saw it?  ·  ✓ Have you ever seen it?
The conversation: Have you ever been to a concert? → Yes, I have. → When did you go?

Say "have you ever" like one word, and use the short forms: I’ve, she’s, haven’t.

Stage 3: Say it out loud

Speaking

Tap Listen, then Record. Glue "have you ever" together, and use "I’ve", not "I have".

Stage 4: In the conversation

Quiz
A friend says: "I’ve been to Japan!" What is the best way to keep the conversation going?
A "Okay. I have been to the beach a lot of times, and I like it."
B "Really? When did you go?"
Your teacher asks: "Have you ever eaten sushi?" You haven’t. Which answer is correct?
A "No, I’ve never eaten it. Have you?"
B "No, I never have eat it in my life, but my friends say it is good."
Which sentence is correct?
A "I have been to that park with my cousins last Saturday."
B "I went to that park last Saturday."

Stage 5: Your unforgettable moment

Reflection
Record one minute in English. Start with your life: say two things you have done and one thing you have never done. Then pick the most unforgettable one and tell the story: when it happened, who you were with, and how you felt. Use at least three words from Stage 1.1. Finish with a question for your teacher that starts with "Have you ever...?"
