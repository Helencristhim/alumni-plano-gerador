Lesson 31 -- Pre-class

Conference Introduction and Elevator Pitch

At a forum in New York, Eduarda meets an investor at the coffee break and has thirty seconds to introduce herself. Key words: keynote, breakout session, hook, expertise, niche, specialize in, stand out, memorable. Structure: so / such ... that for impact (It was such a complex deal that...).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Keynote -- the main talk at a conference, usually by an important speaker
"The keynote starts at nine in the main hall."
Breakout session -- a smaller meeting at a conference about one specific topic
"Eduarda is speaking at a breakout session on Brazil."
Hook -- the first sentence that catches people's attention
"Start your pitch with a hook, not with your job title."
Expertise -- special knowledge or skill in one area
"Her expertise is cross-border restructuring."
Niche -- a small, specialized part of a market
"Mid-market restructuring in Brazil is a niche."
Specialize in -- to focus your work on one specific area
"I specialize in restructurings in Latin America."
Stand out -- to be easy to notice because you are different or better
"A clear pitch helps you stand out in a room full of bankers."
Memorable -- easy to remember
"A short story makes your pitch memorable."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Eduarda is at a forum in New York. After the keynote, she meets Nathan Cole, an investor, at the coffee break. He asks what she does. She doesn't start with her job title. She starts with a hook: "I help Brazilian companies survive their worst year."

Then she explains her expertise. She specializes in restructurings, and the market is a niche. To give proof, she says: "Last year we had so many cases that we hired three new analysts," and "My last deal was such a complex case that we negotiated with forty creditors."

Her pitch is so short that it takes thirty seconds, but it is memorable. It helps her stand out, and that afternoon Nathan comes to her breakout session.

1. How does Eduarda start her pitch?
A With her name, her company, her job title and the number of years that she has worked at Houlihan Lokey.
B With a hook about the problem that she solves.
C With a question about Bluewater.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "It was such a complex case that we negotiated with forty creditors."
B "It was so a complex case that we negotiated with forty creditors in only six months."
C "It was such complex case that we negotiated."
3. Where does Nathan go in the afternoon?
A To the airport.
B To another coffee break with two other investors from his fund in New York.
C To Eduarda's breakout session.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- So / Such ... That for Impact

GRAMMAR

Use so or such to make a fact stronger, and add that + the result. In a pitch, one sentence like this turns a fact into a story that people remember.

FormNeutralWith impact
so + adjective + thatThe market is small.The market is so small that everyone knows everyone.
so many + countable nounWe had a lot of cases.We had so many cases that we hired three analysts.
so much + uncountable nounThere was a lot of interest.There was so much interest that the room was full.
such a / an + adjective + nounThe deal was complex.It was such a complex deal that we needed six months.
such + adjective + plural / uncountableThe news was good.It was such good news that the client called us at midnight.

Never: so a complex deal · such complex deal · so much creditors

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose what a confident advisor would say.

An investor at the coffee break asks: "What do you do?" You start with:
A "I am an associate. I work at Houlihan Lokey in Sao Paulo, in the restructuring team, since a few years."
B "I help Brazilian companies survive their worst year."
C "Restructuring."
You want to give proof with impact. You say:
A "My last deal was such a complex case that we negotiated with forty creditors."
B "My last deal was so a complex case that we negotiated with forty creditors and their lawyers."
C "Deal complex, forty creditors."
The investor says Brazil is a small market. You answer:
A "Yes, sorry."
B "It's such small market that everyone knows everyone, and it's growing a lot every year."
C "It's a niche, but it's growing fast."
You want to end your pitch. You say:
A "OK, that's all about me."
B "What is Bluewater looking at in Brazil?"
C "So, this is my pitch, and now I think it is your turn to talk, if you want."

Reflection
