Lesson 24 -- Pre-class

Small Talk to Big Talk -- Transitioning Topics

The Lane family meeting: Eduarda moves from friendly chat about sailing and Chicago to the sale of the company. Key words: rapport, segue, pivot, steer, get down to business, touch on, circle back, bring up. Structure: transition phrases + noun or -ing (Speaking of... / Before getting into... / Moving on to... / Going back to...).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Rapport -- a friendly relationship in which people understand and trust each other
"Ten minutes of real conversation built more rapport than any slide."
Segue -- a smooth move from one topic to the next
"Her question about his trip was a perfect segue into the deal."
Pivot -- to change direction quickly, from one topic or plan to another
"When he mentioned his retirement, she pivoted to the sale."
Steer -- to guide a conversation in the direction you want
"A good advisor steers the meeting without controlling it."
Get down to business -- to stop chatting and start talking about the serious topic
"After coffee, we got down to business."
Touch on -- to mention a topic briefly, without a long discussion
"I'd like to touch on the timeline before we finish."
Circle back -- to return to a topic later in the conversation
"Good question. Can we circle back to the price at the end?"
Bring up -- to start talking about a subject
"His daughter brought up the price in the first minute."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Tuesday, Eduarda visits Victor Lane and his family at home. She does not get down to business right away. First she builds rapport: she asks Claire about her year in Chicago, and Victor shows her a photo of his sailboat.

After ten minutes, Victor says sailing is the only time he doesn't think about the factory. Eduarda uses that as her segue: Speaking of time away from the factory, have you thought about retirement? Then she says: Before getting into the numbers, I'd like to touch on your goals.

Claire brings up the price too early. Eduarda doesn't avoid the question. She says they will circle back to it at the end, and she steers the meeting to the most important topic: Going back to your employees, that's why the buyer matters.

1. What does Eduarda use as her segue into the business topic?
A A slide with the valuation of Lane Packaging that she prepared with her team the night before.
B Victor's comment about time away from the factory.
C A question about the weather.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "Before to get into the numbers, I'd like to touch on your goals for the company."
B "Before get into the numbers, I touch your goals."
C "Before getting into the numbers, I'd like to touch on your goals."
3. What does Eduarda do when Claire brings up the price too early?
A She says they will circle back to it at the end.
B She ignores the question and continues talking about sailing with Victor for another ten minutes.
C She gives the final price right away.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Transition Phrases + Noun or -ing

GRAMMAR

Transition phrases end in a preposition (of, into, to). After a preposition, use a noun or an -ing form, never to + verb.

JobFormExample
Build a bridgeSpeaking of + nounSpeaking of Chicago, ...
Bridge with no nounSpeaking of which, ...Speaking of which, how is the factory?
Delay a topicBefore + -ingBefore getting into the numbers, ...
Move to the next topicMoving on to + noun / -ingMoving on to the timeline, ...
Return to a topicGoing back to + noun / what...Going back to what you said, ...
After a past actionAfter + -ingAfter talking about sailing, she pivoted.

Never: Before to start · Moving on to talk about the price · Speaking about which

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose what a confident advisor would say.

The client just told you about his trip to Chile. You want to talk about his expansion plans. You say:
A "OK, enough about your holidays in Chile. Now we need to talk about the expansion plan."
B "Speaking about Chile, you expand?"
C "Speaking of Chile, are you still planning to expand there?"
The client asks about your fee in the first two minutes. You say:
A "I prefer not to answer that question today, because it is not on the agenda for this meeting."
B "Good question. Can we circle back to the fee at the end?"
C "Can we circle to the fee later?"
You finished the topic of buyers and want to start the timeline. You say:
A "Moving on to the timeline, when would you like to start?"
B "Moving on to talk about the timeline, when would you like to start the process?"
C "Next. Timeline."
Ten minutes of small talk are enough. You want to start the serious part. You say:

B "Shall we get down to the business now?"
