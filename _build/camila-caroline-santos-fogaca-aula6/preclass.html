Grammar

Opening, entering, returning and closing. Four jobs, four fixed sentences, and the content comes immediately after the signal.

Stage 2: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

A meeting in English is easier than it looks, because it is built on signals that repeat. The agenda says what the hour is for. The opening gives the first signal: let's get started, and then the points, with the time each one has.

In the middle, three things happen to everybody. Somebody says something you disagree with. Somebody forgets a detail that matters. And the discussion leaves the subject. English has a short sentence for each case, and they all start the same way: a signal, then the content. If I may add, the effective date is a holiday in Chile. Going back to the point, we still need a decision.

Notice what these signals do. They ask for the floor and they take it in the same breath. A speaker who says "sorry, sorry, maybe this is not important, I don't know" has already given the floor back before using it. One signal is enough, and after the signal the sentence has to arrive.

The closing is where the meeting becomes work. To sum up introduces the summary. Then come the decisions, and then the action items: each one with an owner and a date. An action item without an owner is not a task, it is a wish. The minutes arrive the same day, and they repeat exactly what was said in the wrap-up, with nothing new added.

One more word about stress. In these phrases English puts the weight on one word: the NEXT step is, to SUM up, if I may ADD. The strong word carries the meaning, and the small words around it stay light. Say the whole phrase flat and it disappears in a video call; put the weight in the right place and the room stops for you.

Stage 3: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

The words you have just met, one by one. Listen to each expression and read it in context: you already do all of this at work. Here it gets its English name.

agenda -- the list of points a meeting will cover, in order
"I send the agenda the day before the meeting."
owner -- the person responsible for an action item
"Every action item needs an owner and a date."
minutes -- the written record of what a meeting decided
"I write the minutes and send them the same day."
let's get started -- the phrase that opens a meeting
"Everybody is here, so let's get started."
if I may add -- a polite way to enter a conversation
"If I may add, the effective date is a holiday in Chile."
move on -- to go to the next point
"If there are no questions, let us move on to the next point."
to sum up -- used to introduce a short summary at the end
"To sum up: two decisions, three action items, one open point."
