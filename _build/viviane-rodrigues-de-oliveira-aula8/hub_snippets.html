How Much Engagement? How Many Programs? -- HR Data and Resources

Talking about people, money and time in HR data: how many answered, how much time it took, and what the number means. Key words: attendance, ratio, average, data, sample, response rate, overtime, cost per hire, allocation, estimate. Structures: quantifiers -- many and much, a lot of, a few and a little, too many, too much and enough, with countable and uncountable nouns.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the definition. These ten live in every HR report.

Attendance -- how many people come, and how often they come
"Attendance in the training was ninety per cent."
Ratio -- the relation between two numbers
"The ratio is one HR person for every hundred employees."
Average -- the middle number of a whole group
"The average is four years in the company."
Data -- the facts and numbers you collect
"We have a lot of data and not much time to read it."
Sample -- the small group you study to understand the big one
"The sample was sixty people from four stores."
Response rate -- the percentage of people who answered
"Our response rate was seventy-eight per cent."
Overtime -- the extra hours worked beyond the normal day
"There is too much overtime in the north region."
Cost per hire -- the money spent to bring in one new person
"Our cost per hire went down this year."
Allocation -- how money or people are divided between areas
"The allocation gives half the budget to training."
Estimate -- an approximate number, not an exact one
"My estimate is about twelve new hires."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Viviane runs the annual survey. This year many people answered: four hundred and seventy out of six hundred and twelve, which is a response rate of seventy-eight per cent. It took about three weeks and a lot of reminders. There is a lot of good news in the data, and there is one problem. In the north region there is too much overtime: on average, six extra hours a week per person. Too many store managers are covering two jobs, and the company does not have enough people to fix it with training alone. Viviane got about nine hundred open comments. Only a few of them are about salary, and she did not have much time to read all of them, so she took a sample of sixty. Most of them are about time.

1. Why does the text say "many people" but "much time"?
A Because you can count people and you cannot count time.
B Because "time" is a longer word.
C Because the two sentences are in different tenses.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "There is too much overtime in the north."
B "There are too much overtime in the north."
C "There is too many overtime in the north."
3. The text says "only a few of them are about salary". Why "a few" and not "a little"?
A Because comments are countable.
B Because "a little" is only used in questions.
C Because "a few" means the same as "a lot".

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Quantifiers -- Countable and Uncountable

GRAMMAR

One question before the noun: can you count it, one by one?

FormUseExample
many + countable pluralThings you can count: people, stores, answers, comments.How many people answered?
much + uncountableThings you measure: time, money, work, feedback, data.How much time did it take?
a lot of + bothThe natural choice in affirmative sentences.a lot of people · a lot of data
a few / a littlea few for countable, a little for uncountable.a few comments · a little time
too many / too much / enoughMore than you want, and just what you need.too many meetings · too much overtime · not enough people
the trapsThese four nouns are uncountable in English.feedback, information, work, advice — never with an -s

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

Paolo asks about the survey. Which question is correct?
A "How much people answered?"
B "How many people answered?"
C "How many time did it take?"
You want to say the north region works more hours than it should:
A "There is too much overtime in the north."
B "There are too many overtime in the north."
C "There is too many overtimes in the north."
You received comments from the stores. Which sentence is correct?
A "We got a lot of feedback from the stores."
B "We got many feedbacks from the stores."
C "We got much feedbacks from the stores."
The recruiter says a CV needs:
A as many numbers as possible
B two or three numbers, because numbers are easy English
C no numbers, only responsibilities
You have budget for two regions, not four. You say:
A "We do not have enough budget for four regions."
B "We do not have enough of budget for four regions."
C "We have not many budget for four regions."
