Chapter 3: The Code

Can You Count It?

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"How many people answered the survey?"

"How much time did it take?"

"We have a lot of data and not much time to read it."

"There are too many programmes and too much overtime."

People and programmes take one word. Time, data and overtime take another. What is different about the nouns?

FormUseExample
many + countable pluralThings you can count one by one.How many people? many stores, many answers
much + uncountableThings you measure, not count.How much time? much money, much work
a lot of for bothThe safe one, and the natural one in affirmatives.a lot of people · a lot of data
a few / a littlea few for countable, a little for uncountable.a few stores · a little time
too many / too much / enoughMore than you want, and just what you need.too many meetings · too much overtime · not enough people

The Structure

A Number in Three Sentences

Nobody remembers a table. Everybody remembers a number that means something.

1 · The number

"Four hundred and seventy people answered the survey, out of six hundred."

2 · The comparison

"That is a response rate of seventy-eight per cent, twelve points more than last year."

3 · What it means

"So people are talking to us again. The problem is not silence any more -- it is what they said."

The number → the comparison → the meaning. Without the third sentence you gave a report, not an analysis.

The Data Review

P
Viviane, I read your memo. How many people answered in total?
V
Four hundred and seventy, out of six hundred and twelve. A response rate of seventy-eight per cent.
P
That is high. And how much time did the whole survey take?
V
About three weeks, and a lot of reminders.
P
Here it says six extra hours a week in the north. Is that the average or the worst store?
V
Sorry, could you say that again, please?
P
The six hours. Is it the average, or the worst store?
V
It is the average. The worst store is nine, and I did not put that number in the memo because I wanted to check it first.
P
Put it in. A number you checked is worth more than a number that sounds nice.
Comprehension

About Paolo

1. What is the first thing Paolo wants to know?
How many people answered the survey in total.
2. What does he want to clarify about the six hours?
Whether it is the average or the worst store.
3. What does he tell her to do at the end?
To put the number in — a number you checked is worth more than one that sounds nice.
