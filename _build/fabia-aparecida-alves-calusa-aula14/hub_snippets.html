Review -- Twelve Point Five

No new words this week. The same four from lesson 13 — revenue, margin, forecast, overhead — and the comparisons one more time, up and down: higher than, lower than, the lowest. Around ten minutes.

Stage 1: The same four words

Review

Nothing new here. Read each one and say it out loud. If it comes back on its own, that word is yours.

Revenue — all the money a business takes in, before costs
"Revenue was lower than the forecast this quarter."
Margin — what is left after you take the costs away
"The margin is three points higher than last quarter."
Forecast — what you expect the numbers to be, before they happen
"We are a little behind forecast, but not much."
Overhead — the cost of running the business, whatever you sell
"Overhead is the lowest it has been this year."

Stage 3: Two things, or all of them -- one more time

Grammar

No new rule this week. The same shape, going up or going down. Two things: higher than, lower than, better than. All of them: the highest, the lowest, the best. Never both: "more lower" does not exist.

"Revenue was eight percent ______ the forecast."
A more lower than
B lower than
"Overhead is ______ this year."
A the lowest
B lowest than the other months
The client asks: "Is 18% a good margin?" Which answer is analysis?
A It is eighteen percent, and that is the number in the report on my desk.
B It is three points higher than last quarter.
"Our margin is ______ than it was in the first quarter."
A better
B more better

Stage 4: Complete the line

Practice

A different client, a different report. In class you will say these lines with nothing on the screen.

"Revenue was than the forecast."
"The margin was three points higher last quarter."
"Overhead is the this year."
"We are a little behind ."

Stage 5: Say it out loud

Speaking

Three lines. Say the comparison slowly; it is the part the client is waiting for. Listen first, then record yourself.

Revenue was lower than the forecast.
The margin was three points higher than last quarter.
Overhead is the lowest this year.

Stage 6: Before class

Speaking
Choose one real client of your firm (no name needed). Record three sentences about their last month or quarter: one number compared with the forecast, one compared with last month or last year, and one thing that is the highest or the lowest since a date. One sentence each — no more. In class you will use these numbers.
