Chapter 3: The Code

Talking to One, Answering All

Four short structures that steer a room

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

“To your point about the cash cycle, the number was sixty days.”

“Coming back to what you asked earlier, the reporting line went to Brussels.”

“That part is probably one for your operations director, although I can give you the finance side.”

“That is the short answer. I am happy to go deeper if it is useful.”

Which of these are still true today, and what comes after each expression?

FormMeaningExample
To your point about..., to answer a specific personTo your point about the cash cycle, the number was sixty days.I used to report to New York.
Coming back to what you asked earlier..., to close an open loopComing back to your question on headcount, we ended the year at thirty-two.I am used to reporting in English.
That is probably one for..., to pass a question without dropping itThat is probably one for your operations director, although I can give the finance side.I got used to being challenged.
That is the short answer. I am happy to..., to hand backThat is the short answer. I am happy to go deeper if it is useful.

The -ing after be used to is the single most common advanced mistake in this family, and it survives for years because it never blocks understanding.

The Move

Acknowledge, Answer, Hand Back

1. Acknowledge who asked

“To your point about the cash cycle...”

2. Answer the room, not the person

“The gap was sixty days across nine entities, and here is what we did.”

3. Hand back in one sentence

“That is the short answer. I am happy to go deeper if it is useful.”

Never blame the market, the parent company or the previous CFO. Every one of those may be true, and every one of them costs you the room.

Comprehension

About the Panel

1. What three things does he name for the first ninety days?
The close, the cash cycle and the people.
2. What does he ask before the operations director comes in?
To finish the cash part, and he says it is one sentence.
3. How does he answer the part he had not covered?
He comes back to it out loud, and gives the people answer: a review of who owns which number in the first six weeks.
Listening

The One Who Says Nothing

A board member on what she writes down. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she write down?
Who the candidate is talking to, not the answer itself.
2. What do the candidates who do it well do?
Give the first line to the person who asked, and the rest to the room.
3. What is a bigger problem than a weak answer?
A different answer when two panel members ask about the same thing.
Listening 2

When They Ask You Two Things at Once

A chief executive on the overlap between panel members. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does a third panel member ask a question again?
Because he was writing when it was answered.
2. What small thing do the candidates who survive do?
They say out loud that it came up before, without irritation, and repeat the same answer in the same words.
3. What do most people do instead?
They add something new the second time, and the panel finds two versions.
