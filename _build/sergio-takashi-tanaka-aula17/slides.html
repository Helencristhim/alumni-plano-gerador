One Map, Three Versions

The Same Fact, Three Ways

A list

"Daily check. Camila Reis. Every morning."

Two flat sentences

"We have a daily check. Camila runs it."

One sentence a committee can read

"The control that catches a breach is the daily check, which Camila runs."

A control map is not a list of names. It is a set of sentences where the person and the task are locked together — and English locks them with who, which and that.

Chapter 3: The Code

Relative Clauses

the person who · the control which · the step that

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The person who signs off the limit is the head of the desk."

"The daily check, which Camila runs, closes at ten."

"The control that catches a breach is the exposure report."

"The report we send every Monday has nine lines."

In every line a whole sentence is sitting inside a noun. What do those little words do — and why does the fourth one have none?

ShapeWhen you use itExample
who + verbPeople only. Never which for a person.The analyst who monitors the limit.
which + verbThings only: a control, a report, a system.The check which runs at ten.
that + verbPeople or things — but only when the clause defines which one you mean, with no commas.The control that catches a breach.
no second subjectThe pronoun IS the subject. Never repeat it with he, she or it.The control that catches it — never "that it catches".
drop it when it is the objectIf another subject follows, the pronoun is optional.The report (that) we send every Monday.
whose · wherewhose = possession: the desk whose limit changed. where = place or system: the file where the breach appears. And commas change the meaning: with commas the clause is extra information, without commas it says WHICH one.

Person or thing first. The pronoun follows, and it never brings a second subject with it.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"The person which signs off the limit is on holiday."
"The person who signs off the limit is on holiday."
"The control that it catches the breach is manual."
"The control that catches the breach is manual."
"The report who we send every Monday is late."
"The report that we send every Monday is late."

Who for people, which for things, that for both. The pronoun is already the subject, so never add it after it — and when the clause has its own subject, you can drop the pronoun altogether.

Then name Ana as my deputy and send it tonight. And add one line about the second line, because that is the first thing Internal Audit will look for.
