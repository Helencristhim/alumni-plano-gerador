Lesson 17 -- Pre-class

Who Does What -- Controls and Owners

The committee approved the deal, with one condition: a one-page map that names every control and the person behind it. Key words: control, accountable, delegate, deputy, monitor, checklist, manual, enforce, bottleneck, second line. Structure: relative clauses (who, which, that, whose, where).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Control -- a step or a check that stops a risk or catches it early
"The control that catches a breach is the daily exposure check."
Accountable -- having to answer for a result, even when somebody else does the work
"I am accountable for the limit, even when my deputy runs the check."
To delegate -- to give a task to another person, and keep the responsibility yourself
"I delegate the check, but I do not delegate the answer to the committee."
Deputy -- the person who covers the job when the owner is away
"Every control on this map has a named deputy."
To monitor -- to watch something regularly, so that you see a change early
"The team that monitors the exposure sits two desks away."
Checklist -- a written list of steps that you tick as you complete them
"The checklist that we sign every morning has nine lines."
Manual -- done by a person, not automatically by a system
"Two of these controls are manual, and that is the weak point."
To enforce -- to make sure that a rule is actually followed
"A limit that nobody enforces is not a limit."
Bottleneck -- the one slow step that holds up everything after it
"The bottleneck is the approval that only one person can give."
Second line -- the independent team that checks the work of the business
"The second line reviews the file, which is why it is not our sign-off."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

The committee approved the facility on Wednesday, with one condition: a single page that names every control and the person behind it. The page has four lines. The first is the daily exposure check, which Camila Reis runs at ten every morning. It is a manual control, so we also name a deputy, and the deputy who covers her is me. The second line is the cap alert, that fires automatically the moment the exposure passes the number written into the contract; Risk Systems owns it, and no person has to remember it. The third is the sign-off of any new limit. Marina Duarte is the manager who gives it, and the desk whose limit changes cannot approve its own request. The fourth is the quarterly review by the second line, the team that checks our file and does not report to us. Two things are worth saying out loud in the room. First, I delegate the daily check, but I am the person who is accountable for it, and the checklist that we sign every morning has my name at the bottom. Second, the sign-off is the bottleneck: it is the one step that only one person can give, which is exactly why a rule nobody enforces and a limit nobody monitors are the same thing — nothing.

1. "The deputy who covers her is me." Why is it "who" and not "which"?
A Because "which" can only be used in questions.
B Because a deputy is a person, and "which" is only for things.
C Because the sentence is in the present, and "which" is only for the past.
2. The text says "the cap alert, that fires automatically". Which sentence says the same thing correctly?
A "The cap alert that it fires automatically."
B "The cap alert, which fires automatically."
C "The cap alert who fires automatically."
3. "The checklist that we sign every morning has my name at the bottom." What can you do with "that" here?
A Drop it, because the clause already has its own subject: "the checklist we sign every morning".
B Replace it with "who", because a checklist is signed by people.
C Add "it" after it, because every clause needs a subject of its own.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Relative Clauses

GRAMMAR

A control map is a set of nouns with a whole sentence inside each one. The relative pronoun is what puts it there, and it is already the subject of that inner sentence.

PronounWhat it stands forExample
whoA person. Never which for a person.The analyst who monitors the exposure.
whichA thing: a control, a report, a system. It is also the pronoun you use after a comma.The daily check, which Camila runs, closes at ten.
thatA person or a thing, but only when the clause says WHICH one you mean — and then there are no commas.The control that catches a breach on the same day.
no second subjectThe pronoun IS the subject of the inner sentence. Never repeat it with he, she or it.The control that catches the breach — never "that it catches".
drop it when it is the objectIf a new subject comes right after the pronoun, the pronoun is optional.The report (that) we send every Monday.
whose · wherewhose = possession: the desk whose limit changed. where = a place or a system: the file where the breach appears. Three errors to avoid: "the person which", "the control that it catches", "the desk that the limit changed".

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Choose one control that exists on your desk this week: a report, a check, a sign-off or an alert. Describe it out loud to somebody who has never seen your team. Say what it catches, who runs it, who is accountable for it, who covers that person when they are away, and where the bottleneck is. Use who for people, which or that for things, and never add a second subject after the pronoun. Take your time.
