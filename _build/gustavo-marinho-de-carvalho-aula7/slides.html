Chapter 3: Be Plus the Third Form

Count the People in These Sentences

Somebody did each of these things. The sentence does not say who.

Grammar Discovery

Count the People in These Sentences

"The contract was signed last week."

"The report is reviewed every Friday."

"The issue has been escalated, and you will hear from us today."

"Mistakes were made."

All four leave the person out, and all four are built the same way: a form of be plus the third form of the verb. In the first three that is exactly right — the procedure has to work on a day when the usual person is away. Now read the orange one again and ask who owes somebody an apology.

JobFormExample
The action matters more than the personbe + past participleThe contract was signed last week.
A step in a procedure, in generalis / are + past participleThe report is reviewed every Friday.
Something finished, at a known timewas / were + past participleThe date was moved on Tuesday.
Something done, with the result still herehas / have been + past participleThe issue has been resolved.
When who did it DOES matterby + personIt was signed off by the finance team.
With a modalmust / should + be + past participleIt must be confirmed in writing.
The trapnever leave out beNot The report reviewed every Friday.
The other trapthe passive is focus, not politenessIn an apology it removes the person who owes you one.

In one line: the passive moves the action to the front. In a procedure that removes noise. In an apology it removes the person.

Dialogue

The Word Who

C
Thanks for the report. It is clear, and I have one question about it.
G
Go ahead.
C
Line three says the supplier was contacted and the risks were flagged. Who?
G
Sorry — who contacted them, you mean?
C
Who contacted them, and who is accountable for the answer coming back. The line does not say, and half the report is like that.
G
That is fair. Rafael contacted them on Monday and he owns that line. The second one is mine: I flagged the risk and I have not chased it since.
C
Good. That is all I needed. It is not a criticism of the report — it reads well. It is just that a passive line hides exactly the thing I have to know.
G
Understood. That was an oversight on my part, and the next one will have a name in front of every line.
Comprehension

About Claire

1. What exactly does Claire ask about line three?
Who contacted the supplier, and who is accountable for the answer coming back.
2. Does she say the report is badly written? What does she say instead?
No. She says it is clear and that it reads well. Her point is that a passive line hides exactly the thing she has to know.
3. What does she say she needed, and how much of it did she get?
She needed the names. She says “that is all I needed” as soon as he gives two of them.
Listening 1 · Business Radio

Nobody Is in the Manual

A short radio piece on why procedures are written without people in them. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say the document does not care which person did each step?
Because the procedure has to work on a Tuesday when three of them are on holiday.
2. What does she say the structure does?
It moves the action to the front and lets the person disappear.
3. What changes when the same structure leaves the manual?
Nothing in the grammar. What it does changes completely: in a procedure it removes noise, and in an apology it removes the person who owes you one.
Listening 2 · A Voice Note

One Question, Forty Seconds

A voice note about a status report and the one word that broke it. Sound first — no text.

1. What was true about the report, and what was the problem with it?
Everything in it was true and every line was in the passive.
2. What question did his manager ask, and why could he not answer?
Who. He had written the report from a list of things that had happened rather than from a list of people doing them.
3. What rule does he say he still uses?
Write it in the passive if you want, then read it back and put a name in front of every line, silently. Any line where you cannot is a line where nobody is doing the work.
Real Document

The Report That Started the Call

Weekly status — week 12
Sent Friday 17:20 · four lines, no names

1. The revised timeline was approved and has been sent to the client.

2. The supplier was contacted and the delivery risks were flagged.

3. The cost breakdown is being prepared and will be circulated on Monday.

4. One item was held up at customs. It has been escalated.

Everything above is on track. Nothing requires a decision this week.

Sample status report written for this lesson. The project is invented.
1. Which line would be impossible to act on if something went wrong, and why?
Line 4. It has been escalated, but the report never says to whom or who is following it, so nobody knows who to ask on Monday.
2. Line 3 uses a different structure from the others. What is it, and what does it add?
is being prepared — the passive of the present continuous. It says the work is happening right now, not that it happens every week.
3. Rewrite line 2 out loud with a name in front. What does the sentence gain, and what does it lose?
For example: “Rafael contacted the supplier on Monday and flagged the delivery risks.” It gains somebody to ask. It loses the neutral tone, which is the whole reason people write the other version.
