Who Did It Does Not Matter -- Reports, Procedures and the Passive

The structure that fills every manual, and the one sentence it must never write. Key words: to sign off on, a breakdown, to flag, an oversight, to be held up, accountable, to escalate, a rollout. Structure: passive voice.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To sign off on -- to approve something officially, so it can go ahead
"Nothing leaves this office until somebody signs off on it."
A breakdown -- a detailed list showing the separate parts of something
"Send me a breakdown of the costs, not just the total."
To flag -- to mark something so that other people notice it
"I flagged it on Monday and nobody has come back to me."
An oversight -- a mistake caused by not noticing something
"That was an oversight on our part, and it has been corrected."
To be held up -- to be delayed by something or somebody
"It was held up on our side, and I want to be clear about that."
Accountable -- responsible for something, and expected to explain it
"Somebody has to be accountable for each line, or the list is decoration."
To escalate -- to pass a problem to somebody more senior
"It has been escalated, and you will hear from us today."
A rollout -- the process of introducing something in stages
"The rollout starts in one region and reaches the rest in March."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Open any procedure written in English and count the people in it. The order is received. The invoice is checked. The shipment is released. Somebody does each of those things, and the document does not say who, on purpose. It has to work on a Tuesday when the usual person is away. The structure is always the same: a form of the verb be, plus the third form of the main verb. Is checked. Was signed. Has been escalated. Must be confirmed.

The tense lives in the be, and the second word never changes. The report is reviewed every Friday, in general. The contract was signed last week, at a known moment. The issue has been resolved, and the result is still here. It must be confirmed in writing, with a modal in front. If you can build those four, you can read almost any document your client sends you.

And then there is the part that is not about grammar at all. The same structure that removes noise from a manual removes the person from an apology, and mistakes were made is famous for exactly that reason. A status report written entirely in the passive is not wrong; it is simply unanswerable, because nobody can ask a sentence a question. The practical rule is short: write it in the passive if you want, then read it back and put a name in front of every line, silently, for yourself. Any line where you cannot is a line where nobody is doing the work.

1. Why does the text say a procedure leaves the person out on purpose?
A Because it is more polite.
B Because the procedure has to work on a day when the usual person is away.
C Because English does not have a word for the person.
2. Where does the tense live in a passive sentence?
A In the verb be.
B In the third form of the main verb.
C In the word by.
3. What does the text say is wrong with a status report written entirely in the passive?
A It is grammatically incorrect.
B It is too formal for a client.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Passive Voice

GRAMMAR

The passive moves the action to the front. In a procedure that removes noise. In an apology it removes the person.

JobFormExample
The action matters more than the personbe + past participleThe contract was signed last week.
A step in a procedure, in generalis / are + past participleThe report is reviewed every Friday.
Something finished, at a known timewas / were + past participleThe date was moved on Tuesday.
Something done, with the result still herehas / have been + past participleThe issue has been resolved.
When who did it DOES matterby + personIt was signed off by the finance team.
With a modalmust / should + be + past participleIt must be confirmed in writing.
The trapnever leave out be, and use the third formNot The report reviewed. Not was sign.

Never: The report reviewed every Friday · The contract was sign last week · The risks was flagged · It has been hold up

Stage 2: Put the Status Update in Order

Order

You are giving a client the week in five lines. Put them in a logical order.

?"Here is where we are, and there is one thing you need to know."
?"The revised timeline was approved on Tuesday and has been sent to your team."
?"The cost breakdown is being prepared and will be circulated on Monday."
?"One item was held up at customs, and that one is mine."
?"It has been escalated, and you will hear from us today."
?"Nothing on that list is waiting on a decision from you."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You are describing a step that happens every week, with no particular person. You say:
A The report reviewed every Friday.
B The report is reviewed every Friday.
C The report was review every Friday.
Something is finished and its result is still relevant now. You say:
A The issue has been escalated.
B The issue was escalate.
C The issue is escalating.
The client asks who is responsible for a line in your report. The best answer is:
A It was contacted on Monday and the risks were flagged.
B Rafael contacted them on Monday, and I own the answer coming back.
C Mistakes were made, but everything is on track now.
You made a mistake and your client has noticed. The strongest thing to say is:
A Some errors were identified in the process.
B That was an oversight on our part, and it has been corrected.
C It appears that the information was not received correctly.

Reflection
