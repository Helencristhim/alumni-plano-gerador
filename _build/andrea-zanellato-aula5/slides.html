Chapter 3: The Code

What Do You Do? What Did You Do?

The order of a question

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Questions Show?

"What do you do in your current job?"

"Where does your team work?"

"Why did you leave your last company?"

"How many people did you manage?"

Look at the order. First the red question word, then the blue helper: do or does for the present, did for the past. Then the person, then the simple verb.

JobFormExample
The orderquestion word + do / does / did + person + verbWhat do you do?
Present, he / shequestion word + does + person + verbWhere does she work?
Past, everybodyquestion word + did + person + verbWhy did you leave?
With bequestion word + be + personWhat was your biggest challenge?
Question wordswhat · where · when · why · how · how many · how longHow long did you work there?
The trapthe helper comes before the personNot Why you left? Say Why did you leave?

In one line: question word + do, does or did + person + simple verb.

Chapter 4: The Interview

The Interview

One candidate, two interviewers, one scorecard

Dialogue

Video Call, 10 a.m. — The HR Analyst Job

A
Good morning, Ryan. Thank you for coming to this job interview. Can you hear me well?
R
Good morning! Yes, perfectly. Thank you for the invitation.
A
So, what do you do in your current job?
R
I’m an HR assistant at a retail company. I help with payroll and I organize the training calendar.
A
How much experience do you have in HR, and why did you choose this area?
R
Four years. I studied psychology, and I always liked to work with people.
A
What is your biggest strength? And one weakness?
R
My strength is organization. My weakness... I sometimes say yes to too many projects.
A
Thank you, Ryan. You are on our shortlist. We will follow up by Friday.
Comprehension

About Ryan

1. What does Ryan do in his current job?
He helps with payroll and organizes the training calendar.
2. Why did Ryan choose HR?
He studied psychology, and he always liked to work with people.
3. What is Ryan’s weakness?
He sometimes says yes to too many projects.
