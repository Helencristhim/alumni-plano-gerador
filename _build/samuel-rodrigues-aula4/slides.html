Who Was Supposed to Check?

A closing that slipped because two firms each thought the other one had it.

Read

The Transaction Note

File note: Aurora Retail S.A. — warehouse acquisition, closing delayed

Aurora Retail agreed to buy a warehouse in Guarulhos. Two firms acted on the buyer side: our office handled the corporate work, and a second firm was instructed on the real estate side. The parties set a closing date eleven weeks out and the funds were placed in escrow.

Nine weeks in, the buyer had the property appraised and the appraisal came back clean. What nobody had done was search the title. Our note to the client said the title search was being handled by the real estate firm. Their note said the same thing about us. A lien registered by a construction supplier in 2021 was still on the property, and the covenant in the loan agreement did not allow the buyer to take an encumbered asset.

Closing was pushed by five weeks while the lien was discharged. Nothing had been drafted badly. Both firms had done careful work. The transaction slipped because two sentences in two emails each assumed the other side had it done.

Grammar

Same Facts, Different Register

Who did it matters less than who is answerable for it.

Discover

Three Ways to Say One Thing

informal
"We checked the title."

formal, active
"Our team reviewed the agreement."

professional, causative
"We had the agreement reviewed by outside counsel."

"They are having the shareholder register updated."

FormWhat it signals
have + object + past participle
"we had the title searched"
get = same idea, less formal		You arranged it. Someone else did it.
You are answerable for the result.
coordination, oversight, authority

You do not need to name who did it: by outside counsel is optional. What the structure removes is the false promise that you did it yourself.
