Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

Who Is Marcio? -- The First Conversation

Saying who you are and what you do in English: your city, your job, your company and your working week. Key words: retail, wholesale, pricing, margin, supplier, efficiency, results, to negotiate. Structure: the present simple for facts and routines, and the -s of he, she and it.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Retail -- the business of selling products to customers in stores
"I have worked in retail for more than twenty years."
Wholesale -- selling large quantities at a lower price for each unit
"Restaurants and small shops buy from us because we are wholesale."
Pricing -- the work of deciding how much a product costs
"My team is responsible for pricing in hundreds of stores."
Margin -- the money a company keeps after it pays for the product
"A small change in price can protect the margin of a whole category."
Supplier -- a company that sells products to another company
"We talk to every supplier before we set the final price."
Efficiency -- doing the same work with less time, less money and less waste
"Commercial efficiency is the name of my job."
Results -- the numbers a company publishes about its performance
"Our results show the gain from better pricing."
To negotiate -- to talk with someone until both sides agree
"I negotiate with our suppliers almost every week."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

My name is Marcio Santos and I live in Sao Paulo. I work in pricing for a large wholesale company, so I decide how much hundreds of products cost. I start my day at five in the morning, and I train for an hour and a half before work.

My team analyzes the market every morning. It compares our prices with the competition, and it protects the margin of every category. We negotiate with every supplier before we change a price. I don't decide alone, and my director doesn't approve a change without data. Four times a year the company publishes its results, and our work on commercial efficiency appears in the report.

1. Why does the text say "my team analyzes" and not "my team analyze"?
A Because "my team" is an it, and he / she / it takes the -s.
B Because the action is happening right now.
C Because "team" is a plural subject.
2. The text says "I don't decide alone" but "my director doesn't approve a change without data". Why the difference?
A Because one is a routine and the other is happening now.
B Because with he / she / it the -s moves to "does", giving "doesn't".
C Because "approve" is an irregular verb.
3. "I start my day at five in the morning." What does this sentence tell us?
A That he is starting his day at this exact moment.
B That he started early once, in the past.
C That this is his routine, something generally true about him.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Present Simple

GRAMMAR

The tense you use to say who you are and what you do.

FormUseExample
Affirmative
I / you / we / they + verb		Facts about you and things you do regularly.I work in pricing.
Third person
he / she / it + verb + s		Same idea, one other person or thing. Watch out: this -s is the most forgotten letter in English, and "my team" and "the company" are both an it.My team analyzes the market.
The company publishes its results.
Negative
don't / doesn't + verb		The -s moves to does, so the main verb goes back to its plain form.I don't decide alone.
She doesn't approve it without data.
Question
Do / Does + person + verb		Same rule again: does carries the -s.Do you negotiate every week?
Does your team meet on Monday?
Frequency wordsalways, usually, often, sometimes, never go before a normal verb (I usually start at five), but after the verb to be (I am usually early).

One verb is enough for a routine: say I work, never I am work.
