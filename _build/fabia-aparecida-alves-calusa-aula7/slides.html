The Pattern

Where did the person go?

I sent the proposal on Friday.
The proposal was sent on Friday.
You have not approved the budget.
The budget has not been approved yet.
We will issue the invoice on the fifth.
The invoice will be issued on the fifth.

be + past participle — the fact stays, the person steps back

"You have not approved the budget" points a finger. "The budget has not been approved yet" states the same fact and keeps the relationship intact. That is why business English is full of it.

The Pattern

Three tenses, one shape

When
Passive
Past
The proposal was sent on Friday.
Up to now
The budget has not been approved yet.
Future
The invoice will be issued on the fifth.
Naming who, when useful
Every process is audited by an external firm.

Passive is not always better. When the credit is yours, the active voice sells: "We delivered it two days early" — not "it was delivered". Passive for the problem, active for the achievement.

Practice

Passive, or not?

Did You Catch It?

Three commitments, two sides

1. What will be sent by Wednesday, and by whom?
The revised proposal, by Karen — with the four-week timeline in it.
2. What does David want added to it?
A clarification on what happens if the data arrives late.
3. What is still pending, and until when?
The budget — London meets on the twentieth.
4. Why does David say he likes working with her?
Because she puts it in writing, so nobody has to remember it.
Listening 1

Dictating the follow-up

1. What are the three sections she dictates?
What was agreed, what she will do, and what she needs from them.
2. Why does she avoid writing "you have not approved it"?
Because it sounds like blame — she writes "the budget has not been approved yet".
3. What is the only sentence she keeps in the active voice, and why?
"We completed the last onboarding two days early" — because the credit is theirs.
Listening 2

The email nobody could act on

1. What was wrong with the email he received?
It was long and polite, but he could not tell what he was supposed to do.
2. What did he have to do because of it?
Call them to ask — which is exactly what the email was supposed to prevent.
3. What does he say a good follow-up needs?
Names, dates, and one line saying what he has to do.
Your Card

Six lines that always work

To: David Osei · Cc: Karen Whitfield

Subject: Follow-up — BPO proposal, ref. AUR-2291

Dear David,

Thank you for your time this morning. As per our call, here is what was agreed.

On our side. The revised proposal ______ by Wednesday, including the four-week timeline and one ______ on late data.

On your side. The employee file ______ to us by Friday.

Still open. The budget ______ yet. I understand London meets on the twentieth.

One more thing. The last onboarding of this size — we ______ it two days early.

Please quote reference number AUR-2291 in any reply.

Kind regards,
Fábia

Fill the four gaps out loud.
will be sent · clarification · needs to be sent (or: should be sent) · has not been approved — and then: "we completed it two days early".
Why is the last line the only active one?
Because it is the one thing the firm did well. Passive for the problem, active for the achievement.
