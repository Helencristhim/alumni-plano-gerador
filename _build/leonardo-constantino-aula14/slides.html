Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"We have advised the board on this last March."
"We advised the board on this last March, and we have advised them since."
"The authority said that it will issue the guidance in December."
"The authority said that it would issue the guidance in December."
"We recommend to restructure the subsidiary before December."
"We recommend restructuring the subsidiary before December."

None of these is a gap in knowledge. All three come back under speed.

Practice

Same Meaning, Other Form

Say the full sentence first, then click to check

"The board approved the structure in 2019." (WAS) ___"The structure was approved by the board in 2019."
"We will issue the guidance this year," the authority said. (SAID) ___"The authority said that it would issue the guidance that year."
"We advise the group to restructure now." (RECOMMEND) ___"We recommend restructuring now."
"The phase will probably be postponed." (MIGHT) ___"The phase might be postponed."
"The committee drafted the phase. It has suggested postponing it." (WHICH) ___"The committee which drafted the phase has suggested postponing it."
"The group started advising in 2019 and still advises today." (SINCE) ___"We have advised the group since 2019."

Listening

Twelve Minutes, Six People

A voicemail from the client's general counsel. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Why does she tell him to open with the recommendation?
Four of the six board members have only read the first page, and the chair gives him twelve minutes. Walking them through the history spends time he does not have.
2. What does she imply about the finance director?
He is the one who signs, and he stops following if the law and the practice are blended, so that distinction decides whether the decision is taken at all.
3. She says: "If you need two sentences, the position is not clear yet." What does that mean?
That a closing which cannot be summed up in one sentence shows the recommendation itself has not been decided, not that the summary is badly written.
Listening 2

What an Examiner Listens For

Three things, and none of them is vocabulary. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does he say happens around the tenth session?
Candidates hit their stride: they stop translating and stop checking whether the sentence is correct while they are still saying it.
2. Why does stopping to apologize cost more than the mistake itself?
Because he is listening for repair, not for perfection. Three words and keep going protects the turn; an apology breaks it and draws attention to the error.
3. What does he mean when he says connected speech is not an accent?
Nobody expects an American accent. Words said separately make the listener work, and a tired examiner then hears effort instead of content.
