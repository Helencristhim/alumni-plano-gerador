Documentary
Downfall: The Case Against Boeing

Journalists, engineers and lawyers assemble one position out of thousands of documents and then defend it in front of a congressional committee. Connection to Lesson 14: every structure of your program is in this film doing real work — the passive for what was certified, reported speech for what executives said, conditionals for what would have happened, and a single recommendation that has to survive interruption.

Tip: watch with English subtitles and stop after each hearing scene. Write one sentence in reported speech saying what the witness claimed, and one sentence saying what you would have asked next.

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart — Episode 107, "Think Faster, Talk Smarter: How to Speak Successfully When You're Put on the Spot"

Matt Abrahams, of Stanford, on the four techniques that buy you time when a question arrives before you are ready for it. Connection to Lesson 14: this is exactly the part of Thursday you cannot rehearse — the interruption, the question you did not anticipate, and the twelve seconds you have to answer it well.

Tip: write down the four techniques as you listen. Then take the five questions you prepared for the board and answer each one out loud twice: once cold, once using one of the four techniques to open.

Video
MIT OpenCourseWare — Patrick Winston, "How to Speak"

An MIT professor spends an hour on how a talk is built: how to open, how to hold an audience that is deciding in the first sixty seconds whether to keep listening, and how to close so that the room remembers one thing. Connection to Lesson 14: your twelve minutes on Thursday are a talk, and this is the structure underneath it.

Tip: watch the first twenty minutes only, and then rebuild your own opening using what he says about promises and empowerment. Say it out loud twice before you write anything down.
