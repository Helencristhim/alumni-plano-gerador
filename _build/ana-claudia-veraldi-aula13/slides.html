Chapter 3: The Code

A Past Tense With Nothing to Do With Time

if + past · would · and the were that works for every person

Grammar Discovery

None of These Is True

"If I lived in a village, I would miss the noise."

"If I were you, I would visit the village first."

"If the house had a good roof, I would buy it tomorrow."

"If I won the lottery, I might restore a whole street."

Every one of these sentences describes something that is not the case. Look at the verb after if: it is in the past. Now the only question that matters — is any of this about the past?

FormWhat it doesExample
if + past simple, would + verbAn unreal or imaginary present or future.If I lived there, I would miss the noise.
the past is not pastIt marks distance from REALITY, not distance in time.If I had a year off... (I do not have one)
were, for every personThe standard form here, even with I, he and she.If I were you · if she were here
would / could / mightThe result can be softened.I might restore a whole street.
never would after ifThe same rule as the first conditional.never: if I would have more time
first vs secondFirst: it can really happen. Second: it is not the case.If it rains... vs If I won the lottery...

In one line: the past tense here means NOT REAL — it never means yesterday.

Chapter 4: Many Englishes

Somebody Who Did It, Somebody Who Did Not

Italian and British English — and two very different ways to give advice

Dialogue

Marco Bought a One-Euro House

M
So you saw the one-euro houses in Abruzzo? If I were you, I would apply this week.
A
It is very tempting. But if I bought one, I would have to restore it in three years.
M
Three years is feasible. I did mine in two, and I knew nothing about roofs.
A
I know a little about restoring. But the village is so remote. If I lived there, I would feel cut off.
M
In January, yes. The road closes when it snows. But in May you would never want to leave.
A
Honestly? Moving there is a long shot. I am only weighing it up.
M
That is how everybody starts. If people only moved when they were sure, the village would be empty.
A
Okay. If I visited in winter and still liked it, I would send the application.
Comprehension

About Marco

1. What advice does Marco give Ana at the start?
He says: if I were you, I would apply this week.
2. How long did Marco take to restore his house, and what did he know about roofs?
Two years, and he knew nothing about roofs.
3. What happens to Marco’s village in January, and how does he describe May?
The road closes when it snows. In May, he says, you would never want to leave.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I use the past simple after if when the situation is not true.
I use would, could or might in the result, and never would after if.
I say if I were you when I give advice.
I keep the first conditional for things that can really happen, and I never mix the halves.
I know the words: far-fetched, feasible, remote, off the grid, a long shot, wishful thinking.
