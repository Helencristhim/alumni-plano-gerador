Lesson 13 -- Pre-class

If I Lived in the Middle of Nowhere -- Dreams, Hypotheses and What Is Not True

The field next door, the horses she does not have, and the tense English keeps for none of it being true. Key words: far-fetched, to daydream, a long shot, feasible, hypothetically, to picture something, wishful thinking, to weigh something up, tempting, plausible, to be inclined to, to settle for something. Structure: second conditional for unreal and hypothetical situations.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Far-fetched -- so unlikely that it is difficult to take seriously
"Keeping horses out here is not far-fetched at all, it is just expensive."
To daydream -- to imagine something pleasant while you should be doing something else
"I daydream about that field every time I drive past it."
A long shot -- something with a very small chance of working, but worth trying
"Buying the land is a long shot, but I have asked the price twice."
Feasible -- possible to do in practice, with the money and time you actually have
"Two horses would be feasible. Six would not."
Hypothetically -- used to introduce a situation that is imagined rather than real
"Hypothetically, if the field were mine, the first thing would be a fence."
To picture something -- to form a clear image of something in your mind
"I can picture the whole thing: the fence, the gate, the two horses."
Wishful thinking -- believing something because you want it, not because it is likely
"Saying the price will drop is wishful thinking and I know it."
To weigh something up -- to consider the good and bad sides carefully before deciding
"I have been weighing it up for two years and I am no closer."
Tempting -- attractive enough to make you want it, even against your better judgement
"The price is tempting, which is exactly what worries me."
Plausible -- believable, whether or not it turns out to be true
"His explanation was plausible, but I would still check it."
To be inclined to -- to have a mild tendency towards a choice, without having decided
"I am inclined to leave it and see whether anybody else buys it."
To settle for something -- to accept something smaller than what you originally wanted
"I would rather wait than settle for half the field."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The field next to Ana’s house has been for sale for twenty six months and she has been weighing it up for two years. If she bought it, she would fence the open side first, and if the money went further than she thinks, she would keep two horses within a year. She can picture the whole thing: the fence, the gate, the nine old trees.

None of it is far-fetched. Two horses on two hectares would be entirely feasible, and the well already works. The problem is the roof, which needs the same money in the same year. If it were not for the roof, she says, she would have made an offer already. Buying half the field would be settling for something she would resent, so she is inclined to wait.

Her neighbour Marco is blunter about it. If he were her, he says, he would offer something below the asking price this week and let the answer decide it. Waiting for the price to drop after twenty six months is wishful thinking, and everybody involved knows it. It is a long shot, certainly. It is also the only move on the board.

1. "If she bought it, she would fence the open side." Why bought and not buys?
A Because she has not bought it: the past tense marks that the situation is not real.
B Because the purchase happened in the past.
C Because bought is more formal than buys.
2. "If it were not for the roof, she would have made an offer already." What does if it were not for do here?
A It names the single obstacle and dismisses all the others.
B It states that the roof has already been repaired.
C It softens a request so it sounds more polite.
3. "If he were her, he would offer something below the asking price." Why were and not was?
A Because in this structure were is used with every person, and it is the safe form.
B Because he is talking about more than one person.
C Because the sentence is in the plural.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Second Conditional

GRAMMAR

A past tense that has nothing to do with the past. It marks distance from reality.

FormWhat it doesExample
if + past simple, would + verbAn unreal present or futureIf I had more land, I would keep horses.
the past is not pastIt marks distance from REALITY, not timeIf I lived there... (I do not)
were, in every personStandard here, including I and sheIf I were you...
would / could / mightThe result half can softenI might sell the far end.
never would after ifThe same rule as the first conditionalnever: if I would have
first vs secondFirst may happen; second is not the caseIf it rains... vs If I won...
if I were to...A more remote, slightly formal hypothesisIf I were to sell, where would I go?

Never: If I would have more land · if I was you (in this structure) · if I have more time I would restore it · if I lived closer I will visit. The first puts would in the condition, the second uses the form that exams do not accept here, and the last two mix a real half with an unreal one.

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

Marco thinks Ana should make an offer on the field. Put the exchange in a logical order.

?"So the field is still for sale. If I were you, I would go and offer them something this week."
?"I have been weighing it up for two years, Marco."
?"Two years. And in two years, has the price ever gone down?"
?"No. Saying it will is wishful thinking and I know it."
?"Then buy half. Or is that settling for something you would resent?"
?"Honestly, yes. If I had the whole field, I would picture the fence and the two horses."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

A friend asks what you would do with the land if you owned it. You answer:
A "If I owned it, I would fence the open side first."
B "If I would own it, I would fence the open side first."
C "If I own it, I would fence the open side first."
You want to give somebody advice about a decision. The most natural version is:
A "If I was you, I would make an offer."
B "If I were you, I would make an offer."
C "If I would be you, I would make an offer."
You want to name the one thing stopping you. You say:
A "I would move tomorrow if it was not because of the dogs."
B "I would move tomorrow unless the dogs."
C "I would move tomorrow if it were not for the dogs."
Somebody asks what you are doing on Saturday if the weather is good. You answer:
A "If it were dry, I would paint the veranda."
B "If it is dry, I will paint the veranda."
C "If it was dry, I will paint the veranda."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.
