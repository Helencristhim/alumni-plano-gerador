Lesson 10 -- Pre-class

Linking the Long Answer

The answer that gets to the end: position, reason, evidence, caveat and conclusion. Key words: rationale, trade-off, with regard to, in practice, nonetheless, accordingly, to put it simply, bottom line. Structure: linking words of consequence, addition, contrast and concession.

Stage 1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Four words for the argument, four for the way you say it. Listen to each one and read it in context.

rationale -- the reasons behind a decision or a position
"The rationale is simple: the buyer needs certainty."
trade-off -- what you accept to lose in order to gain something else
"The trade-off is acceptable at this stage of the project."
with regard to -- about; on the subject of (formal)
"With regard to the price, our position is that we should accept it."
in practice -- in what really happens, not in theory
"In practice, the price risk is smaller than it looks."
nonetheless -- in spite of what was just said; even so
"The risk is real. Nonetheless, we recommend proceeding."
accordingly -- as a result of this, and in a way that fits it
"The board has approved the plan, and we will proceed accordingly."
to put it simply -- said in fewer and easier words
"To put it simply: accept the fixed price and keep the review clause."
bottom line -- the most important point, the one that decides
"The bottom line is that the first contract matters more than a perfect one."

Stage 3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about what it says.

The most common problem in a professional answer is not grammar. It is length. People give a reason and stop, and the listener is left with half an answer.

A complete answer has five parts, and small words connect them. With regard to the question, you give your position. The rationale comes next, and therefore or as a result takes the listener from the reason to what follows from it. In addition brings one more point in the same direction.

Then comes the part most people skip: the caveat. Although lets you admit a problem in the same sentence, without losing your position: "Although the risk is real, it is smaller than it looks." Whereas puts two sides next to each other: "They need certainty, whereas we need a first contract." On the other hand tells the listener you are about to be fair to the other side, and nonetheless says you are continuing in spite of it.

The end is short. To put it simply, give the bottom line: what you recommend, and what happens accordingly. Every trade-off you named along the way is what makes that last sentence believable.

Stage 4: Grammar Tip -- linking words

GRAMMAR

Each link has one job. Choose the job first, then the word.

JobLinksExample
Consequencetherefore, as a result, accordinglyTherefore, it makes the contract easier to sign.
Additionin addition (to)In addition, the price can be reviewed.
Contrastwhereas, on the other handThey need certainty, whereas we need a first contract.
Concessionalthough, nonethelessAlthough the risk is real, it is small.
Punctuationtherefore, as a result, in addition, on the other hand and nonetheless start a sentence and take a comma. although and whereas join two parts of one sentence.
The classic mistakenever although ... but in the same sentence: "Although the risk is real, but it is small" is wrong. One link is enough.

Quick test before you speak: am I giving a result, adding, comparing, or admitting a problem?

Stage 8: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Real situations from your week. Only one option is both correct and natural.

1. You want to give the result of your reason. Which sentence is correct?
A The buyer needs certainty. Although, a fixed price helps.
B The buyer needs certainty. Therefore, a fixed price makes the contract easier to sign.
C The buyer needs certainty. Whereas, a fixed price helps.
D The buyer needs certainty, in addition a fixed price.
2. You want to admit a risk and keep your position. Which sentence is correct?
A Although the risk is real, but it is small.
B Although the risk is real, it is smaller than it looks.
C The risk is real, although but it is small.
D Nonetheless the risk is real, it is small.
3. You want to compare the two sides in one sentence. Which is correct?
A They need certainty. Whereas we need a first contract.
B They need certainty, whereas we need a first contract.
C They need certainty, therefore we need a first contract.
D Whereas they need certainty. We need a first contract.
4. A director asks for the short version. What do you say?
A In addition, the price can be reviewed.
B On the other hands, the market may fall.
C To put it simply: accept the fixed price and keep the review clause.
D With regard to, the price is fixed.
