Chapter 4: The Code

The Links in the Chain

consequence · addition · contrast · concession

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"A fixed price gives the buyer certainty. Therefore, it makes the first contract easier to sign."

"The buyer needs certainty, whereas we need a buyer who will still be there."

"Although a fixed price protects the buyer more, the trade-off is acceptable."

"In addition, the price can be reviewed after the first period."

Which one gives a result? Which one adds? Which one compares two sides? Which one admits a problem and continues?

JobLinksExample
Consequencetherefore, as a result, accordinglyTherefore, it makes the contract easier to sign.
Additionin additionIn addition, the price can be reviewed.
Contrastwhereas, on the other handThey need certainty, whereas we need a first contract.
Concessionalthough, nonethelessAlthough the risk is real, it is smaller than it looks.
Where they gotherefore, as a result, in addition, on the other hand, nonetheless start a new sentence and take a comma. although and whereas join two parts of the same sentence.
Why it matters herea long answer is a chain. Without the links, it is a list of short answers, and the listener has to connect them alone.

Sound Check

Say It Clearly

the link as a signal · the sentences of this lesson

Why this one is hard for a Portuguese speaker

The habit: running the connector into the rest of the sentence, fast and flat, so the listener does not hear the turn.

In English: the link is stressed and followed by a short pause: THERE-fore, pause, it makes... The pause is where the listener changes direction with you.

The stress to keep: al-THOUGH, where-AS, in ad-DI-tion, none-the-LESS. Say each one twice before the sentences.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Not a One-Line Answer

A request from finance, and the shape of an answer that gets to the end

Listening 1

Please, Not One Line

The finance director calls after reading the opinion. Sound first, no text.

1. What does Paulo ask Amanda not to do, and why?
Not to give him a one-line answer, because the last time he said the risk was small and was asked why, he had nothing else to say.
2. What question does he expect from the board?
What happens if the price falls by half before the credits are issued.
3. What order does he ask for, and by when?
The position, the reason, the numbers and then the risk, said honestly. By Wednesday at noon.
Listening 2

Twenty Seconds, Then Ninety

A senior partner on answering a hard question in a meeting. Sound first, no text.

1. What are the two answers she recommends having ready?
A twenty-second answer with the position and the bottom line, and a ninety-second answer with five parts.
2. Where do most people stop, and why is that a problem?
After the reason. They never get to the caveat, and the caveat is what makes the answer credible.
3. What does she say although does for the speaker?
It lets you admit a problem without losing your position.
