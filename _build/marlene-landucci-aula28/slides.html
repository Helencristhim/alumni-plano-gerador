Chapter 4 -- Which Platform?

Language Discovery

Which platform? / How long does it take?

Discovery

Listen and Notice

Two questions choose between options. Two talk about time. What do the orange words have in common?

"Which platform does the train leave from?"

"Which line goes to the Colosseum?"

"How long does it take to Rome?"

"It takes one hour and thirty minutes."

The Rule

Which and How Long

UsePatternExample
Choose from optionsWhich + noun + question?Which platform does it leave from?
Which + subjectWhich + noun + verb?Which line goes to the Colosseum?
Ask how much timeHow long does it take (to + place)?How long does it take to Rome?
Answer with timeIt takes + timeIt takes five minutes.
Timetable (review)The train leaves at + timeThe train leaves at ten thirty-five.

Key idea: Use Which + a noun when you choose from a few options: Which platform? Which line? Which exit? To ask about time, say How long does it take? and answer It takes + minutes or hours -- never "It is five minutes" for a trip.

P

M

"Oh no. How long is the delay? And which platform does it leave from?"

P

"Twenty minutes. And it leaves from platform eight now, not five."

M

"OK. How long does it take to Rome?"

P

"It takes one hour and thirty minutes. Do we need to validate our tickets?"

M

"No, these are high-speed tickets. In Rome, which line goes to the Colosseum?"

P

"Line B. We take the subway from Termini. There is no transfer."

M

"Perfect. Then we follow the exit signs, and we see the Colosseum!"
