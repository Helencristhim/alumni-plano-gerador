Stage 1.1: Session Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Offshore — when the wind blows from the land to the sea, which makes the waves clean
"It was offshore all morning."
Glassy — when the water is completely smooth, like glass
"I have never seen it that glassy."
Session — one time in the water, from paddling out to coming in
"That session lasted three hours."
Peak — the highest point of the wave, where it starts to break
"I paddled straight for the peak."
Set — a group of bigger waves that arrive together
"A set came through and everybody paddled."
Epic — extremely good, better than normal
"It was the most epic morning of the year."
Exhausted — so tired that you cannot continue
"I came in exhausted and happy."
Freezing — so cold that it hurts
"The water was freezing and nobody cared."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the story. Then answer the questions.

I have surfed a lot of good mornings, but last Saturday was different. I woke up at five and the wind was offshore. The water was completely glassy and there were four people out. A set came through at six and I paddled straight for the peak. The water was freezing and I did not notice once. Three hours later I came in exhausted. I have never seen it like that, and it has been the best session of my life so far.
Why does the story open with "I have surfed a lot of good mornings"?
A Because there is no date yet.
B Because it happened on Saturday.
What were the conditions at five in the morning?
A Offshore wind and glassy water, with four people out.
B Choppy and crowded.
Why does the last sentence go back to the present perfect?
A Because the story comes back out and there is no date again.
B Because it is a question.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Open, zoom in, come back out

No new rule this lesson. What is new is doing it at speed, inside a story.

Opening and closing -- no date

I have surfed a lot of good days.
Have you ever had a session like that?
It has been the best one so far.
I have never seen it that glassy.

Inside the story -- with a date

Last Saturday I woke up at five.
It was offshore and glassy.
A set came through and I paddled.
I came in three hours later, exhausted.

Open with no date: "I have surfed a lot of good days."
Zoom in with a date, and stay there: "Last Saturday I woke up at five. It was offshore. A set came through."
Come back out with no date: "It has been the best session of my life."
The trap: "Last Saturday I have surfed" is broken. A date and a "have" never share a sentence.

Stage 4: Telling the story

Quiz
You want to open a story about good sessions in general.
A I have surfed a lot of good days.
B I surfed a lot of good days.
You are inside the story now, on a specific Saturday.
A Then a set has come through and I have paddled.
B Then a set came through and I paddled.
You close the story, back out of the date.
A It has been the best session of my life.
B It was the best session of my life so far, yesterday.
Somebody asks about yesterday, which is finished.
A Have you gone to the coast yesterday?
B Did you go to the coast yesterday?

Stage 5: Your best session

Reflection
Tell the story of the best session you have ever had, out loud, for 30 seconds. Open with no date, zoom in with a date and stay there, then come back out at the end. Check one thing before you send it — no sentence has a date AND a "have" together.
