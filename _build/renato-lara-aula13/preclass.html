Lesson 13 — Pre-class

What Should I Do?

Eight words for fitting in (advice, fit in, wait your turn, show up, apologize, take it easy, polite, awkward) and the modal that carries all of them: should and shouldn't for advice, plus the question What should I do?

Stage 1.1: Advice Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Advice — words that tell somebody the best thing to do
"Can I ask you for some advice?"
Fit in — to feel part of a group because you are like the people in it
"It takes two weeks to fit in at a new school."
Wait your turn — to let the other people go first and go after them
"You should wait your turn in the line-up."
Show up — to arrive somewhere, especially when people are expecting you
"You should show up ten minutes early on the first day."
Apologize — to say sorry when you do something wrong
"If you drop in on somebody, apologize straight away."
Take it easy — to stay calm and not push too hard
"Take it easy on your first session."
Polite — acting in a way that shows respect for other people
"You should be polite to the older surfers."
Awkward — uncomfortable, when you do not know what to say or do
"The first day at a new school always feels awkward."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. Then answer the questions.

A girl from Perth is on the beach and I asked her for some advice about February. She said I should show up early on the first day and talk to one person, not to twenty. In the water I should wait my turn, and I shouldn't paddle for the first wave I see. If I drop in on somebody, I should apologize straight away. I told her my English gets awkward when I am nervous, and she said I should take it easy: ask questions, listen, and be polite to the older members. That is how you fit in. Then I asked her the only question I really wanted to ask: what should I do if nobody talks to me?
Why is it "I should show up" and not "I should to show up"?
A After should the verb is plain — there is never a "to" in front of it.
B Because "show up" is two words, so the "to" disappears.
The text says "be polite", not "polite". Why is the verb there?
A Because "polite" is a long word and it needs help.
B Because the sentence is about how you ARE, and that verb is "be". It cannot be missing.
Why is the last question "what should I do" and not "what I should do"?
A In a question "should" jumps in front of the person: should I, should he, should we.
B Both are correct, it only depends on how fast you speak.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

should — your opinion about the best thing to do

One word, and the plain verb right after it. Nothing changes for he or she.

The form

I / you / he / she should go
you shouldn't go
Should I go?
What should I do?

Never

You should to wait
He shoulds apologize
You should polite
→ You should be polite

Rule or opinion: "You have to wear a leash" is the club rule. "You should wear a leash" is what I think. You can say no to the second one.
Softer: "Maybe you should ask the coach" · "You should probably sit wide today".
Advice is uncountable: "some advice" or "a piece of advice" — never "an advice", never "advices".

Stage 5: Five pieces of advice

Reflection

Check one thing before you send it — when the sentence is about how he IS, the verb "be" has to be there: "you should be polite", not "you should polite".
