Grammar Discovery

Same story, two tenses

I have surfed a lot of good days.
Last Saturday I surfed the best one.
It has been the best session of my life.
The session was three hours long.

The story opens, then it closes

This is how people really tell stories: they OPEN with the present perfect, because there is no date yet, and then they ZOOM IN with the past simple, because now there is a day, a time and a place. Once you are inside the story, everything stays in the past simple until you come back out.

The Code

Open the story, zoom in, come back out

Opening and closing -- no date

I have surfed a lot of good days.
Have you ever had a session like that?
It has been the best one so far.
I have never seen it that glassy.

Inside the story -- with a date

Last Saturday I woke up at five.
It was offshore and glassy.
A set came through and I paddled.
I came in three hours later, exhausted.

The rule underneath is the same one from lesson 10: a finished date closes the sentence. What is new is doing it at speed, in the middle of a story.

Did You Catch It?

What happened

Why does Renato open with "I have never had a morning like that"?
Because he is opening the story, with no date yet. The date comes in the next sentence.
What were the conditions when he woke up?
The wind was offshore and the water was completely glassy, with only four people out.
What has Zoe done at that spot, and what has she never seen?
She has surfed there twice, and she has never seen it like that.
Listening 1

The morning of the year

1. How long has he been surfing that coast, and how often has he seen it like that?
Twenty years, and maybe five times.
2. What were the three conditions that came together?
The wind went offshore overnight, the swell was the right size, and the water was glass.
3. What happened when he came in?
He was so exhausted that he sat in the car for twenty minutes before he could drive.
Listening 2

The one she missed

1. What did she do with the alarm, and had she ever done it before?
She turned it off in her sleep. She has never done that before.
2. How did she know immediately when she woke up?
The group chat had forty messages.
3. What was it like by the time she arrived?
The wind had swung, it was choppy and full of people.
