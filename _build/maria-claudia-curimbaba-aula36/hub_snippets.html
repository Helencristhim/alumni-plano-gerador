Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Hindsight -- understanding a situation only after it has happened
"In hindsight, we should have checked the cut-off time."
Knock-on effect -- an indirect result of something that then causes other problems
"The late container had a knock-on effect on the whole production plan."
Shortfall -- the amount by which something is less than you need
"Without the valves, Houston has a shortfall of two hundred units."
Surcharge -- an extra amount of money added to the usual price
"The supplier wants a twelve percent surcharge for air freight."
Rush order -- an order that has to be produced or delivered faster than usual
"We placed a rush order for the missing valves."
Backorder -- an order that cannot be delivered now because the product is not in stock
"Two items from the shipment are on backorder until July."
Penalty -- money you must pay when you do not do what a contract says
"The contract has a penalty of one and a half percent for every week of delay."
Bargaining chip -- something you can use to get an advantage in a negotiation
"The penalty clause is our best bargaining chip."
Deal-breaker -- a problem so serious that it stops an agreement
"Another missed deadline would be a deal-breaker for us."
Goodwill -- friendly feelings and trust between people or companies
"They offered free air freight to protect the goodwill between us."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

On Monday morning Maria Claudia receives a call from Greg, the account manager in Pittsburgh. The container for the Houston joint venture missed Friday's cut-off. Now Houston has a shortfall of two hundred valves, and the knock-on effect reaches their customers. Maria Claudia stays calm. She tells Greg: "If the container had left on Friday, our customers would have their parts today." Greg offers a rush order by air, but with a twelve percent surcharge, and two items stay on backorder. Maria Claudia knows that the penalty clause is a bargaining chip. She says: "If we weren't long-term partners, we would have cancelled the order already." In the end, Greg removes the surcharge to protect the goodwill. In hindsight, they both agree on one rule: a second delay without a phone call would be a deal-breaker.

1. What would be different today if the container had left on Friday?
A The customers would have their parts.
B The supplier would offer a much larger discount on the next three orders from Pittsburgh.
C Greg would call every week.
2. Why does Maria Claudia mention that they are long-term partners?
A Because she wants to cancel the order and find a new supplier in Houston before Friday.
B To ask for a longer contract.
C To show she is unhappy but wants to keep the relationship.
3. What do they agree would be a deal-breaker?
A Any surcharge on a future rush order, even a small one of two or three percent.
B A second delay without a phone call.
C A late payment from Houston.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Mixed Conditionals

GRAMMAR

In a mixed conditional, the two halves of the sentence are about different times. Choose the form of each half by its own time: a result NOW takes would + verb; a result in the PAST takes would have + past participle.

MixFormExample
past cause, present resultif + past perfect, would (not) + base verbIf the container had left on Friday, we would have the parts now.
present fact, past resultif + past simple, would (not) have + past participleIf we weren't partners, we would have cancelled the order.
negativeif + hadn't + participle, wouldn't + verbIf we hadn't missed the cut-off, we wouldn't be paying a penalty now.
questionwhat would + subject + verb + if + past perfect?What would you do now if they hadn't called?

Key point: never put "would" in the if-half: "If we would have shipped". And with "now" or "today", do not use would have: "we would have had the parts now".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best sentence for each situation.

The supplier says the delay is "only a few days." What do you say?
A A few days is fine, so we do not need to talk about it again before the next order.
B You are always late.
C If the container had left on Friday, our customers would have their parts now.
The supplier offers a rush order with a big surcharge. What do you say?
A If we weren't long-term partners, we would have cancelled. We expect no surcharge.
B We will pay the surcharge and also the full price for the two items on backorder this month.
C Surcharge no, please.
Your colleague asks what the team should learn from the delay. What do you say?
A If we would have kept stock, we would not have a shortfall.
B In hindsight, if we had kept some stock, we would not have this shortfall now.
C Nothing, because it was the fault of the supplier and the carrier, not ours.
The supplier asks if one late container will end the contract. What do you say?
A One delay is not a deal-breaker, but a second one would be.
B Yes, the contract ends today and we will not discuss it any more with your company.
C Maybe, we do not know.
