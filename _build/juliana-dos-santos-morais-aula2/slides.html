Chapter 4: The Code

Will, Going To and -ing

Three futures, three different messages

Grammar Discovery

Four Sentences About One Meeting

"We are going to discuss the new program. We decided that last week."

"Oh, you do not have the agenda? I will send it right now."

"We are meeting the partners on Thursday at ten."

"Look at this agenda. The meeting is going to be very long."

Look at the red words. One is a plan made before. One is a decision made in this second. One is a meeting already in the calendar. One is a prediction from what you can see. The verb in blue changes with each one.

JobFormExample
A plan, already decidedgoing to + verbWe are going to discuss the new program.
A decision or promise made nowwill + verbI will send it right now.
An arrangement in the calendaram / is / are + verb-ing + timeWe are meeting on Thursday at ten.
A prediction from what you seegoing to + verbThe meeting is going to be long.
An offerShall I...? / I will...I will take the minutes, if you want.
The trapafter if, no willIf Daniel agrees, we will reschedule.

In one line: going to = the plan already exists; will = you decide now; -ing = it is in the calendar.

Real Document

The Page Everyone Reads Before the Call

MEETING AGENDA
Partnership call · Brazil — Canada · Thursday, 10:00 Toronto time
ObjectiveChoose the date and the format of the business breakfast
Item 1 — 15 minThe new partnership program · Daniel
Item 2 — 20 minDate and format of the breakfast · Juliana
Item 3 — 15 minList of member companies to invite · Emma
MinutesEmma
Attendees8 · Toronto, Vancouver, São Paulo
Next meetingThursday, two weeks later, same time
Every line of this page is about the future. Some lines are plans (we are going to discuss the program), and some are arrangements with a day and a time (we are meeting on Thursday at ten).
1. What is the objective of the call?
To choose the date and the format of the business breakfast.
2. Turn Item 2 into a full sentence with going to.
Juliana is going to present the date and the format of the breakfast, for twenty minutes.
3. Which line is an arrangement, and how would you say it?
The next meeting: we are meeting again on Thursday, two weeks later, at the same time.
Listening 1 · The Organizer

How I Write an Agenda

An event organizer explains how she prepares the page before every call. Sound first — no text.

1. When does she circulate the agenda?
Three days before the meeting.
2. How many items does she put on the agenda?
Three, sometimes four.
3. What does she ask one person to do at the start?
To take the minutes.
Listening 2 · The Manager

The Meeting Without a Plan

A manager remembers a meeting that went wrong. Sound first — no text.

1. How long did the group talk about the first subject?
About forty minutes.
2. What happened to the important subject?
There was no time for it, so they had to reschedule.
3. What is he going to do differently now?
He is going to send an agenda with time slots before every meeting.
