Lesson 02 -- Pre-class

The Agenda -- Planning a Meeting

A good meeting starts days before the call, with one short page. Key words: an agenda, an agenda item, to run a meeting, an objective, a time slot, an attendee, to circulate, the minutes, to reschedule, to prioritize. Structure: will vs going to vs present continuous.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An agenda -- a list of the subjects that people will discuss in a meeting
"I will send the agenda to everybody on Monday."
An agenda item -- one subject on the list of a meeting
"The first agenda item is the new partnership program."
To run a meeting -- to lead a meeting and control the time and the subjects
"Daniel is going to run the meeting on Thursday."
An objective -- the result you want to get from a meeting or a project
"The objective of the call is to choose a date for the event."
A time slot -- a fixed period of time for one activity
"Each agenda item has a time slot of ten minutes."
An attendee -- a person who is present at a meeting or an event
"We are expecting twelve attendees from four countries."
To circulate -- to send a document to all the people in a group
"Please circulate the presentation before the call."
The minutes -- the written record of what people said and decided in a meeting
"Who is going to take the minutes today?"
To reschedule -- to change the date or time of a meeting or an event
"We need to reschedule the call because of the holiday."
To prioritize -- to decide which things are the most important and do them first
"We have little time, so let us prioritize the partnership items."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Juliana and Daniel are planning a call with their partners. “We are going to discuss the new program,” Daniel says. “We decided that last week.” The objective of the call is to choose a date for the business breakfast.

Then Daniel sees a problem: Emma does not have the agenda. “No problem,” Juliana says. “I will circulate it now.” She makes that decision while she is speaking.

At the end, they check the calendar. “So we are meeting on Thursday at ten,” Daniel says. The meeting is already in everybody’s calendar, with a day and a time.

1. Why does Daniel say “we are going to discuss the new program”?
A Because he is deciding it at that moment.
B Because the plan was made last week.
C Because it is a promise to Emma.
2. Why does Juliana say “I will circulate it now”?
A Because she decides it while she is speaking.
B Because it is written in the calendar.
C Because she circulates agendas every day.
3. Which sentence describes an arrangement in the calendar?
A “We will meet on Thursday, maybe.”
B “We are meeting on Thursday at ten.”
C “We meet on Thursday, I think.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Will, Going To and Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

Three ways to talk about the future. The difference is when you made the decision, and whether there is a day and a time.

JobFormExample
A plan, already decidedgoing to + verbWe are going to discuss the new program.
A decision or promise made nowwill + verbI will send it right now.
An arrangement in the calendaram / is / are + verb-ing + timeWe are meeting on Thursday at ten.
A prediction from what you seegoing to + verbThe meeting is going to be long.
An offerShall I...? / I will...I will take the minutes, if you want.
The trapafter if, no willIf Daniel agrees, we will reschedule.

Never: If Daniel will agree, we reschedule · We will discuss the program (decided last week) · I am going to send it now (decided this second) · We meet the partners Thursday at ten
