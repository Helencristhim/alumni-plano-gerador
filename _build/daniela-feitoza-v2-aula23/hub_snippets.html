In Lesson 22 you wrote emails with a clear subject line, an attachment and a polite request. Emails ask for things. Reports say what happened. Last weekend there was a problem in the checkout system of the US stores, and Daniela has to write the technical report for the US directors. Today you will learn the words of an IT incident and the passive voice — the voice of technical reports. (In a report, the action is more important than the person.)

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Bug -- an error in a program that makes it work in the wrong way
"A bug was found in the payment page on Friday."
Patch -- a small update to a program that fixes a problem
"The patch was tested by the QA team on Sunday."
Detect -- to discover or notice that something is there
"Our monitoring tool can detect a problem in seconds."
Server -- a powerful computer that stores data and runs systems
"All the servers were restored by six p.m."
Approve -- to officially say that something is good or can happen
"The security team has to approve every patch."
Restore -- to bring a system back to normal, so it works like before
"It took two hours to restore the server."
Release -- a new version of software that is now available to users
"The next release of the checkout system is in December."
Assign -- to give a task or a job to a person or a team
"Greg will assign the ticket to the Brazil team."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

On Friday night, customers in the US stores could not pay by card. At noon it was approved by the security team, and by six p.m. all the servers were restored. No customer data was lost. On Monday, Daniela wrote the incident report for the US directors. Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip — The Passive Voice

GRAMMAR

Use the passive when the action or the result is more important than the person who did it.

UseFormExample
Routine, factsis / are + past participleThe data is backed up every night.
Finished past actionwas / were + past participleThe servers were restored at six.
Who did it+ by + person or teamThe patch was approved by the security team.
Negative / questionwas not + participle / Was ... + participle?No data was lost. Was the patch tested?
RememberNever forget be: "The bug was detected", not "The bug detected". Irregular verbs use the third form: write → written, find → found, send → sent. The agent comes with by, not "for".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

A "The bug was detected on Friday night."
B "The bug detected by the monitoring tool on Friday night at nine."
C "The bug was detect on Friday night."
A director asks who said yes to the patch. What do you say?
A "It was approved for the security team, at noon on Sunday, after the tests."
B "It approved the security team."
C "It was approved by the security team."
You want to thank Carlos personally for writing the fix. Which sentence is better?
A "The fix was written on Saturday by a member of the team."
B "Carlos, thank you for writing the fix so fast."

The customers want to know if their information is safe. What do you write?
A "No customer data were lose during the incident on Friday night."
B "Nobody lost data, I think, but I need to check it again."
