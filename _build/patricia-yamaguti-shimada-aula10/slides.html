The Last Morning, in the Lobby

P
Good morning. Yes, of course.
A
Thank you. I am Alex. Are you leaving today too?
P
Yes, I am. My name is Patrícia. We leave this afternoon.
A
Nice to meet you. Where are you from?
P
I am from Brazil. I live in São Paulo, and I am here with my family.
A
Brazil! Lovely. And how have you found it here, all things considered?
P
Sorry, I don't understand. Can you say that again, more slowly?
A
Of course. Did you like the city?
P
Yes, very much. Ten days, and we did everything together.
A
With children, that is not easy. Was it difficult?
P
A little. One day I was lost in the street, and I asked a man. And you? Are you here on vacation?
A
For work, I am afraid. But your English is very good. Have a safe trip home.
Comprehension

What Did Alex Say?

1. Why is Alex in the city?
For work — not on vacation.
2. What does he say about travelling with children?
That it is not easy.
3. How does he say goodbye?
He says her English is very good, and wishes her a safe trip home.
Listening

The Last Morning

The hotel message on the morning you leave. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What can you do with your bags after check-out?
Leave them in the small room next to the desk, and take them in the afternoon.
2. Why should you leave early on Sunday?
Because the traffic is slow.
3. What do people forget every week, and where?
A passport or a phone, in the safe in the room.
Listening 2

Ten Days, Seen From Outside

A short radio piece about travelling with children. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What was the biggest problem for the families?
Not the money and not the flight — the language.
2. What did one mother not do in ten days?
She did not go into a single shop, because she was afraid of being asked a question.
3. What are the three sentences the expert talks about?
Ask to repeat, ask to speak more slowly, and ask for help.
