Lesson 27 -- Pre-class

Risk and Credit in Plain Language -- Word Families

Ten to twenty minutes before class. The families of risk, analyze and predict, and how to explain credit to someone outside finance. Key words: an analysis, an analyst, analytical, a prediction, predictable, risky, a credit score.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An analysis -- a careful study of the details of something
"The analysis shows that the client is stable."
An analyst -- a person who studies data to understand it
"Our analysts check every new client."
Analytical -- able to think about a problem in a logical way
"She has a very analytical mind."
A prediction -- a statement about what will happen in the future
"Our prediction for next year is positive."
Predictable -- that you can know before it happens
"Small loans are more predictable."
Risky -- with a chance that something bad will happen
"Lending to this company is risky."
A credit score -- a number that shows if a person usually pays on time
"A good credit score makes a loan cheaper."

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Her friend asks if it is risky for the banks. “Less risky with our data,” Sara says. “We can’t predict everything, but people are more predictable than you think.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- One Idea, Four Forms

GRAMMAR

Choose the form by its job in the sentence.

JobFormExample
A thing (noun)analysis, prediction, riskThe analysis is ready.
An action (verb)analyze, predict, riskWe predict late payments.
How a thing is (adjective)analytical, predictable, riskyThis client is risky.
How we do it (adverb)analytically, predictablyShe thinks analytically.

Never: We made an analyze · The client is very risk · Our predict is good
