Lesson 2 -- Who Am I Professionally: The Zurich Interview, Round 1

Series
The Office (US) — season 1, episode 1 (“Pilot”) · HBO Max

In lesson 1 you watched people introducing themselves in a living room. Now the camera changes address: the whole episode happens inside an office — a company, with a manager, sales people, a receptionist and clients. It is your world, in English. Like Modern Family, the characters look into the camera and talk alone — and what they say is exactly the sentence of this lesson: “I'm the regional manager”, “I'm a sales rep”, “I'm the receptionist”. Notice that nobody says the job title on its own: it always comes with a or the in front — the article we practiced in the Grammar Tip (“I am a broker”, never “I am broker”). One honest warning: it is a comedy of embarrassment, and the boss is a disaster on purpose. You do not have to find it funny — listen for the vocabulary.

Tip: watch it with English subtitles. Do ONE thing only: every time a character talks to the camera, hit pause and write down the sentence starting with “I'm...”. In 20 minutes you will have a list of real people saying what they are — the way you will say it at Zurich. Write down the words from the lesson too: company, manager, client.

Podcast
Speak English Now — #155 “Job Interview Stories” (with teacher Georgiana, ~10 min, free)

This is the episode for your lesson because its subject is the subject of your lesson: the job interview. But the real reason it is here is the method. Georgiana does not explain grammar — she asks a simple question and stops, waiting for you to answer out loud. Then she gives the answer, and asks again, and again, changing one detail each time. It is unblocking practice: your mouth answers before your head translates. For someone who freezes when it is time to speak, this is worth more than ten grammar tables. She speaks slowly, with a clear accent, and the episode has a full free transcript on the page.

Tip: an honest warning — this episode is one step above your level, and that is on purpose. Do not try to understand everything (you will catch about 30%, and that is fine). There is one task only: when she asks a question, answer out loud, even if it is wrong, even alone in the car. If you want to check afterwards, open the transcript on the page. Listen twice: the second time always gives more than the first.

YouTube
Talking about your profession in English — Rebecca, from engVid (~10 min)

If you watch only one of the three, make it this one: it is your lesson 2 explained by another teacher, in 10 minutes. Rebecca answers the question Mark will ask you at Zurich — “What do you do?” — and she separates exactly the two things we separated in the Grammar Tip: “I am a...” (what you ARE: a broker, a manager) and “I work in / I work for...” (where you work). On her whiteboard you can read “a doctor”, “a lawyer” — always with the a in front, the article that is obligatory in English. She is a business English trainer, speaks slowly and writes everything on the board as she explains.

Tip: watch it once all the way through. The second time, pause after each structure and say YOUR version out loud — swap her example for yours: “I am a commercial professional. I work in commercial insurance. I work for Zurich.” At the end she shows the advanced forms: ignore them for now, they are for a few months from here. Today the first three are enough.
