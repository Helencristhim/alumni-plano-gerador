Lesson 02 -- Pre-class

Who Am I Professionally -- The Zurich Interview, Round 1

In lesson 1 you learned to say where you work. In this lesson you learn to say what you are: seven words from your sector (insurance, broker, company, manager, commercial, client, professional) and the verb to be in its three forms — I am / I am not / Are you? With them you build the most important sentence of your career change: “I am not a broker — I am a commercial professional.” This Pre-class is only a first contact: come to class with the words in your ear, not on the tip of your tongue.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions. The words in bold are the verb to be — the verb of this lesson.

Tuesday, nine o'clock. The Zurich cafeteria. Paula meets David again.

David asks: "Are you the new commercial manager?" Paula answers: "Almost! I am not a manager yet." Then Paula asks a question — her first question in English: "Are you a manager?" David laughs: "No, I'm not. I am an analyst."

David asks again: "Are you a broker?" This is the important one. Paula was a broker for twenty years — but not anymore. She answers: "No, I'm not. Not anymore. I am on the company side now." And then, the sentence of the day: "I am a commercial professional. I work in commercial insurance."

David smiles: "Big risks! So our clients are your clients. You are in the right place, Paula." (Notice: to say what you are, what you are not, and to ask a question — it is always the same verb. One verb, in three different coats.)

1. David asks "Are you a broker?". Paula was a broker for 20 years, but she is not one today. What does she answer?
A "Yes, I am." — after all, she worked 20 years as a broker.
B "No, I'm not. Not anymore. I am on the company side now."
C "I no am broker."
2. What is David's job?
A He is the commercial manager.
B He is a broker.
C He is an analyst — he says: "No, I'm not. I am an analyst."
3. Where does the word not go in a negative sentence?
A After am: "I am not a broker."
B Before am: "I not am a broker."
C At the end of the sentence: "I am a broker not."
4. To ASK a question, what happens to the verb?
A Nothing — you only raise your voice at the end: "You are a manager?"
B It jumps to the front: "Are you a manager?"
C It disappears: "You a manager?"

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The verb to be (I am / I am not / Are you?)

GRAMMAR

You have already been using the first line of this table since lesson 1 (“I am from Osasco”). Today you get two more pieces only: the negative and the question.

FormInglêsWhen you use it
Affirmative (I)I am a commercial professional. (= I'm)To say what you are.
Affirmative (you)You are a manager. (= You're)To say what the other person is.
NegativeI am not a broker. (= I'm not)To say what you are not.
QuestionAre you a broker?To ask what someone is.
Short answer (yes)Yes, I am.Sim, sou.
Short answer (no)No, I'm not.The short way to say no.

Careful (the two mistakes almost everyone makes here): (1) "I am broker" — in English the a before a job is obligatory: "I am a broker"; (2) "You are a manager?" — a question is not made by raising your voice: the verb jumps to the front: "Are you a manager?".

Do not mix this up with lesson 1: to be says what you are ("I am a professional"). To say what you do, the verb goes alone, with no am: "I work in insurance" — never "I am work in insurance".

Why this matters: this is the structure that tells your career change in two lines — "I am not a broker. I am a commercial professional." In the Zurich interview, this is the sentence that explains who you are today.

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen to each sentence, then record your voice. Nobody is grading you — it is only you and your microphone. Careful: insurance is in‑SHUR‑ance (the stress in the middle, with a “sh” sound), commercial is co‑MER‑shal (never “commerciAL”) and client has two syllables, starting with “ai”: KLAI‑ent.

I am not a broker. I am on the company side now.
