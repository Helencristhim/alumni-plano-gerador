Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

How Long Have You Been Doing That? -- The House You Are Restoring

The house, the tools and the projects that never end. Key words: to restore, to tinker, painstaking, worn out, to come along, a knack, to salvage, rewarding, from scratch, to get around to, to be into something, a labor of love. Structure: present perfect continuous (how long) against present perfect simple (how much is done).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To restore -- to bring something old back to its original condition
"I have been restoring this house since 2021."
To tinker -- to make small adjustments to something for pleasure, with no hurry
"I tinker with something in that house every weekend."
Painstaking -- done very slowly and carefully, with attention to every detail
"Stripping that floor was painstaking work."
Worn out -- damaged and no longer useful after a long time of use
"The roof was completely worn out when we bought it."
To come along -- to make progress little by little
"How is the staircase coming along?"
A knack -- a natural skill for doing something well, without having studied it
"She has a knack for fixing anything with a motor."
To salvage -- to save something usable from what would be thrown away
"We salvaged the original doors from the old kitchen."
Rewarding -- giving you a good feeling that makes all the effort worth it
"Slow work, but the most rewarding thing I do."
From scratch -- from the very beginning, with nothing ready made
"I rebuilt that window frame from scratch."
To get around to -- to finally do something you have been delaying for a long time
"I have never gotten around to painting the hallway."
To be into something -- to be genuinely interested in something, as a passion
"I have always been into old wooden furniture."
A labor of love -- work you do for love, not for money or reward
"Nine years on one house. It is a labor of love."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Ana has been restoring her house since 2021. Room by room, with no hurry. She has finished the kitchen and both bedrooms, and the wooden floor she salvaged from under an old carpet took four painstaking months.

At the moment she has been working on the staircase since March, and it is coming along slowly. The garden wall has been waiting since last winter. And the hallway? She has never gotten around to painting it.

Nobody taught her any of this. She has always been into old things and discovered she had a knack for them. It is slow and it is rewarding — in her words, a labor of love.

1. Why does the text say "she has been restoring" and not "she is restoring since 2021"?
A Because the restoration is finished.
B Because duration in English needs a perfect form, never the present.
C Because "restore" is an irregular verb.
2. Why "has finished the kitchen" and not "has been finishing the kitchen"?
A Because the kitchen is a finished result, not work still going.
B Because the kitchen is small.
C Because "finish" cannot take -ing in any sentence.
3. "She has always been into old things" — why is there no -ing here?
A Because it started recently.
B Because it is a question.
C Because "be" is a state verb, and state verbs do not take the continuous.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Two Perfects, Two Jobs

GRAMMAR

How long the work has been going, or how much of it is done.

FormIts jobExample
have been + -ingThe activity and how long it has lastedI have been restoring it since 2021.
have + past participleThe result: what is finished, how manyI have restored two rooms.
for / sincefor + a length · since + a starting pointfor three months · since March
How long ...?Almost always the continuousHow long have you been doing that?
State verbsknow, be, have, love take NO -ingI have known him for years.
NegativeThe auxiliary carries the notI have not been working on it lately.

Never: I am restoring it since 2021 · How long are you doing that? · I have been knowing him · I have been finishing the kitchen.

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

Somebody asks Ana about the house. Put the exchange in a logical order.

?"Somebody told me you are fixing up an old house."
?"I have been restoring it since 2021, room by room."
?"Four years. What have you actually finished?"
?"Two bedrooms and the kitchen. The floor was painstaking."
?"You clearly have a knack for this. Where did you learn it?"
?"Nowhere. I have always been into old things."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

A colleague asks how long you have been working on the house. You answer:
A "I am restoring it since 2021."
B "I have been restoring it since 2021."
C "I restore it since 2021."
You want to say the kitchen is completely done. The correct form is:
A "I have finished the kitchen."
B "I have been finishing the kitchen."
C "I am finishing the kitchen since March."
You want to ask a colleague about a project of theirs, politely:
A "Why do you spend so much time on that?"
B "How long have you been working on it? How is it coming along?"
C "How long are you doing that?"
You want to say you have known somebody for a long time:
A "I have known my neighbor for six years."
B "I have been knowing my neighbor for six years."
C "I know my neighbor since six years."
