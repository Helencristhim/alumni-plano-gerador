Chapter 3: The Code

The Work, or the Result?

have been doing · have done — and when only one of them fits

Grammar Discovery

Which Ones Answer How Long?

"I have been restoring this house since 2021."

"I have restored two rooms and the kitchen."

"She has been working on the staircase for three months."

"He has fixed about forty old radios."

The orange ones tell you how long the work has been going. The blue ones tell you how much is finished.

FormIts jobExample
have been + -ingThe activity and how long it has lasted.I have been restoring it since 2021.
have + past participleThe result: what is finished, how many.I have restored two rooms.
for / sincefor + a length, since + a starting point.for three months · since March
How long ...?Almost always the continuous.How long have you been doing that?
State verbsknow, be, have, love take NO -ing.I have known him for years.
Finished resultIf it is done, the continuous is wrong.I have finished the kitchen.
The trapPresent simple/continuous with since.I am restoring it since 2021.

In one line: how long = the -ing one; how much is done = the other.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I am restoring this house since 2021."
"I have been restoring this house since 2021."
"How long are you doing that?"
"How long have you been doing that?"
"I have been knowing my neighbor for six years."
"I have known my neighbor for six years."
"I have been finishing the kitchen."
"I have finished the kitchen."

The first two are the same error: in Portuguese the present tense carries duration, in English it does not — duration needs a perfect. The last two are the opposite mistake: the -ing form is for work still going, never for a state and never for something already done.

Chapter 4: Many Englishes

Same Obsession, Different Accents

A conversation, and two people who never finish anything

Dialogue

So What Do You Do on Weekends?

D
Somebody told me you are fixing up an old house. How long have you been at it?
A
Since 2021. I have been restoring it room by room, and I am nowhere near done.
D
Four years. Wow. What have you actually finished?
A
Two bedrooms and the kitchen. The floor took months — it was painstaking. But we salvaged the original wood, so it was worth it.
D
You clearly have a knack for this. Did you learn it somewhere?
A
Nowhere. I have always been into old things. I just started, and I have been learning by ruining things ever since.
D
And what is left? What have you been putting off?
A
The hallway. I have never gotten around to painting it. Four years of walking past it. It is a labor of love, Dave, not a schedule.
Comprehension

About Dave

1. How did Dave find out about the house?
Somebody told him she was fixing up an old house.
2. What does Dave assume about her skill, and is he right?
That she has a knack for it and learned it somewhere. She says she learned nowhere — she has been learning by ruining things.
3. Dave asks three questions. What are they about?
How long she has been at it, what she has finished, and what she has been putting off. All about the project — never about money or about why she does it.

Nine years. She has been working on the staircase since March and it is still not finished.

The kitchen and both bedrooms.
3. What does she say the house did to her?
You never restore a house — the house restores you. She is far more patient than she was nine years ago.

Four Hours in the Garage

0:000:00

How long have you been out there? She has been asking it every Saturday for about three years.
2. What is he into, and what does he do with the radios?
Old radios — repairing them, not collecting. He has fixed about forty and has given away almost all of them.

Not at all. It is painstaking, and half the parts do not exist anymore, so he salvages what he can.
