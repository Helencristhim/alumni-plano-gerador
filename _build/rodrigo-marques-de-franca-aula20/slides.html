Grammar Discovery

Who Has to Act?

"The Rent shall be paid monthly in advance."

"All other repairs are to be carried out by the Lessee."

"The premises may be used only as offices."

"The lease may be terminated by the other party."

Who has to act in each sentence? Where does the sentence tell you? What do the red words have in common?

JobFormExample
A dutyshall / must + be + past participleThe Rent shall be paid monthly.
An arrangement already fixedis / are to be + past participleThe deposit is to be paid on signature.
A right or a possibilitymay + be + past participleThe lease may be terminated.
A prohibitionshall not / may not + be + past participleConsent shall not be unreasonably withheld.
Who owes itby + the party, at the end...carried out by the Lessee.
A right given to a partyparty + shall be given / entitled to + nounIt shall be given thirty days to remedy.
The trapsnever drop be · participle, not base form · by, not fromNOT shall paid · NOT is to be pay · NOT carried out from the Lessee

In one line: the duty comes first; by tells you who owes it — and if there is no by, ask.

Real Document

Notice of Breach

Copy sent to Rodrigo Marques de França, counsel for the Lessee

Notice of Breach · Office Lease, 14th Floor

Dear Sirs, it has come to our attention that two rooms on the 14th floor are being used by an accounting firm. No request for consent was received, and no consent was given. This is a breach of Clause 9. In accordance with Clause 12, you are hereby given thirty days to remedy the breach. If it is not remedied within that period, the lease may be terminated without further notice.

Harbor Point Real Estate Fund · Lessor

1. What is the breach, in your own words?
"The Lessee has sublet two rooms to an accounting firm without the Lessor's prior written consent."
2. Can the Lessor terminate the lease today?
"No. The Lessee must first be given thirty days to remedy the breach."
3. What are the Lessee's two options?
"End the sublease, or ask for consent now. Consent shall not be unreasonably withheld."
4. Find every passive in the letter. Who is the agent in each one?
"Are being used by an accounting firm; was received and was given, by nobody named; are given and is remedied, by the Lessee; may be terminated, by the Lessor."

Listening 1 · The Landlord

Every Cent Back?

A woman who rents out apartments, on the conversation she has most often. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she disagree about most often with tenants?
The deposit: tenants expect every cent back, and she sees a list of repairs.
2. What is the difference between wear and tear and damage, for her?
Wear and tear is normal use, like faded paint or a loose door handle, and it is her problem. Damage, like a hole in the wall, is paid for by the tenant from the deposit.
3. What does she advise tenants to do on the first day?
Take pictures of every room and email them to the landlord, so the date is recorded.
Listening 2 · The Drafter

An Obligation Without an Owner

A contract lawyer, on the one word he spends the most time on. Sound first — no text.

1. What does "shall" mean in a well-drafted contract, according to him?
One thing only: this party has a duty.
2. What is wrong with "The premises shall be kept in good condition"?
It does not say who has to keep them in good condition: the landlord or the tenant.
3. When is the passive useful, in his view?
When the action matters more than the person.
