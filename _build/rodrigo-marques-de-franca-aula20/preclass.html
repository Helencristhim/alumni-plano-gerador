Lesson 20 -- Pre-class

Rights and Obligations -- Who Owes What

How to read a commercial lease and find, in every clause, who has to act. Key words: a lessee, to be liable for, to bear the cost of, wear and tear, to withhold, to be in arrears, to remedy, quiet enjoyment, a security deposit, to sublet, prior written consent, to reimburse. Structure: the modal passive of contracts -- shall be paid, is to be carried out, may be terminated -- with by + the party who owes the duty.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A lessee -- the person or company that rents a property from its owner
"The lessee pays the rent; the lessor owns the building."
To be liable for -- to be legally responsible for something, and for paying for it
"The owner is liable for repairs to the roof."
To bear the cost of -- to pay for something, usually something you would rather not pay for
"Who will bear the cost of the new elevator?"
Wear and tear -- the normal damage that happens when something is used over time
"Faded paint is normal wear and tear."
To withhold -- to keep something back that you would normally give
"The owner withheld part of the deposit."
To be in arrears -- to be late with a payment that is already due
"The tenant is two months in arrears."
To remedy -- to correct a problem or a breach so that it no longer exists
"They have thirty days to remedy the breach."
Quiet enjoyment -- the right to use a property without being disturbed by the owner
"Daily inspections would breach our right to quiet enjoyment."
A security deposit -- money paid at the start of a lease and kept in case of unpaid rent or damage
"The security deposit is three months' rent."
To sublet -- to rent all or part of a property you are renting to another person
"We want to sublet two rooms to an accounting firm."
Prior written consent -- permission given in writing before something is done
"No changes without the owner's prior written consent."
To reimburse -- to pay somebody back for money they have spent
"The owner will reimburse us for the plumber."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A US company was about to rent an office floor on Faria Lima, and its office manager asked Rodrigo a simple question: who pays for what? The lease was in English, and almost every clause was in the passive. The Rent shall be paid by the fifth business day, and a security deposit is to be paid on signature. If the company is ever in arrears, the owner may withhold the deposit.

Rodrigo explained that the owner is liable for the structure, but all other repairs are to be carried out by the Lessee, who must bear the cost of them. Normal wear and tear is not the tenant's problem. The office may not be sublet without the owner's prior written consent.

Finally, if either side fails to perform, it shall be given thirty days to remedy the breach, and only then may the lease be terminated. Costs paid on the owner's behalf shall be reimbursed within fifteen days. "Look for by," Rodrigo said. "That is where the contract tells you who owes the duty."

1. "All other repairs are to be carried out by the Lessee." Who has to do the repairs?
A The owner of the building, because the Lessee only has to pay for them after they are done.
B The company that rents the office.
C Nobody: it is only a possibility.
2. "The lease may be terminated." What does may mean here?
A The other party has the right to end the lease, but it does not have to.
B The lease will end automatically.
C The lease must be ended at once by both parties, without any notice period at all.
3. Why does Rodrigo tell the office manager to look for by?
A Because by shows the date when each obligation has to be performed by each party under the lease.
B Because by is always the last word of a clause.
C Because it names the party who has to act.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Modal Passive in Contracts

GRAMMAR

A contract puts the duty first and the party last. The form is always modal + be + past participle.

JobFormExample
A dutyshall / must + be + participleThe Rent shall be paid monthly.
Already fixedis / are to be + participleThe deposit is to be paid on signature.
A rightmay + be + participleThe lease may be terminated.
A prohibitionshall not / may not + be + participleThe premises may not be sublet.
Who owes itby + the party, at the end...carried out by the Lessee.
Negativeshall not be held + adjectiveThe Lessee shall not be held responsible.
QuestionBy whom is it to be + participle?By whom are the repairs to be carried out?

Never: shall paid · is to be pay · may not be use · carried out from the Lessee

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You are drafting a lease and want to say that the tenant has a duty to pay on the fifth. You write:
A "The Rent shall paid by the Lessee on the fifth business day of every month during the term."
B "The Rent shall be paid by the Lessee on the fifth business day."
C "The Rent shall be pay on the fifth."
A client reads "The premises shall be kept in good condition" and asks what it means. You say:
A "It doesn't say who has to do it, so we should add by the Lessee or by the Lessor."
B "It means the owner has to keep the premises in good condition, because the owner is always the one who is responsible for that."
C "It means nothing."
A friend's landlord kept half of her deposit because the walls were a little faded after three years. You say:
A "That's fair. She should pay."
B "That's damage, so the landlord can keep the whole deposit and also ask her for more money if the paint costs more."
C "Faded paint is normal wear and tear. He shouldn't withhold the deposit for that."
The other side wants to sublet two rooms of your client's building. Your client asks if he can say no. You say:
A "Yes, but consent shall not be unreasonably withheld, so you need a good reason."
B "No, you have to say yes."
C "Yes, you can always say no for any reason you like, because it is your building and the lease cannot stop you."
