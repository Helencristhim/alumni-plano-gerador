Chapter 4: The Code

Order, Rule, Advice, Permission

Four little verbs, and the difference between them is not politeness

Grammar Discovery

Which One Leaves You a Choice?

"You must not use this on broken skin."

"You have to ask the pharmacist for the stronger one."

"You should stop if the skin becomes more irritated."

"You can buy this one without a prescription."

Put the four red verbs in order, from the one that leaves you no choice at all to the one that only opens a door.

JobFormExample
A rule, written downmust + base verbThe label must list every active ingredient.
A prohibitionmust not + base verbYou must not use it on broken skin.
An obligation from outsidehave to / need to + base verbYou have to ask the pharmacist.
No obligation at alldo not have to / do not need toYou do not have to take it with food.
Adviceshould / should not + base verbYou should stop if it gets worse.
Permission or possibilitycan / cannot + base verbYou can buy it without a prescription.
The trapmust not ≠ do not have toYou must not take it is a ban. You do not have to take it is a free choice.

In one line: must bans, have to obliges, should advises, can permits — and no modal is ever followed by to, except have to and need to.

Listening 1 · A Pharmacist

What I Say Twenty Times a Day

A pharmacist on the questions customers should ask and usually do not. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say people almost never ask, and why does it matter?
Whether the new medicine is safe with what they already take. She says half the problems she catches are interactions with something the customer did not think to mention.
2. What does she say about the generic?
That it is the same active ingredient at the same dose, that it often costs a fraction of the brand, and that a customer can simply ask for it.
3. What does she say to do if you run out of a prescription over the weekend?
Call the pharmacy rather than the clinic. In most states they can give an emergency supply for a few days while the prescriber is unavailable.
Listening 2 · A Newcomer

The First Year, Learning the System

Somebody who moved to the United States, on what nobody explained. Sound first — no text.

1. What surprised him most about the American pharmacy?
That most of what he wanted was on an open shelf, in an aisle, and that nobody was going to hand it to him across a counter.
2. What went wrong the first time he collected a prescription?
He did not know what a copay was and thought the price he was told was the full price, so he argued about it for ten minutes.
3. What sentence does he say he uses now, every time?
Excuse me, could you tell me which aisle this is in? He says four words solved a year of walking in circles.
