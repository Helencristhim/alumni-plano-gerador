Podcast
NPR Life Kit -- "How to get lower prices on prescription drugs"

The vocabulary of this lesson used by people who actually need it: copay, generic, refill, pharmacist. It is also the clearest short explanation in English of how the American system decides what you pay at the counter, which is the part nobody explains to a newcomer.

Tip: listen once, then write down every sentence in the episode that contains a modal. Say each one out loud and decide whether it is a rule, an obligation, advice or permission.

Audiobook
Elisabeth Rosenthal -- "An American Sickness" (audiobook preview)

A physician and journalist explaining, in ordinary English, how the American health system charges for what it does -- including the pharmacy counter you practised today. The free preview is the opening of the book, and it is the register you will hear in any waiting room in the country.

Tip: listen twice. The second time, note every word from this lesson that appears -- copay, prescription, generic -- and how it is used in a full sentence.

Music
The Beatles -- "With a Little Help from My Friends"

Two and a half minutes built almost entirely on modals and questions: would you stand up and walk out on me, what do I do when my love is away, I get by with a little help. Clear diction, slow tempo, and every modal in the lesson sung at least once.

Tip: read the lyrics and circle every modal verb. For each one, say whether it is asking permission, giving advice, or talking about possibility.
