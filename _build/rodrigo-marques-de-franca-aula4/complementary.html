Lesson 4 -- What the Clause Actually Says

Podcast
Planet Money (NPR) -- "Surprise, you just signed a contract! How hidden contracts took over the internet"

The law of contracts told as a story, by people who are not lawyers, for people who are not lawyers. That is exactly the register you practised in the role-play: your own field, explained to somebody paying by the minute. Transcript on the same page.

Tip: listen once, then take one idea from the episode and explain it out loud in English, in three sentences, as if to a client. Time yourself.

Talk
Alan Siegel -- "Let's simplify legal jargon!" (TED, 5 min)

Five minutes, and the shortest argument you will find for the register this lesson was built on. He rewrites a real document on screen, which makes the point better than any style guide: the simple version says the same thing and can be read once.

Tip: take the clause you brought to class and do what he does. Write the plain-English version in two sentences, then read both aloud and see which one you can say without stopping.

Watch on TED ↗
Series
Better Call Saul -- Season 1, Episode 1 "Uno" (Netflix)

A lawyer's working life at close range: documents, clients who understand nothing, and long scenes of somebody explaining a legal position out loud. American English, unhurried, and closer to your day than any courtroom drama.

Tip: find one scene where a character explains something legal to a non-lawyer. Write down the sentence that does the most work, and notice which modal verb is in it.
