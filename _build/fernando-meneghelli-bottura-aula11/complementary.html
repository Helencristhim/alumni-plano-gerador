Lesson 11 -- Under Fire: Three Hard Questions in a Row

Grammar Video
"Linking words of contrast" -- BBC Learning English, English Class

Although, even though, despite and in spite of, with the trap that costs most advanced speakers a mark: what comes after each one. Connection to Lesson 11: this is the structure that lets you agree with a hard question and keep your own position in the same sentence.

Tip: after "despite" and "in spite of" comes a noun or an -ing form, never a full sentence. Say five sentences about your own numbers out loud, and check that rule in every one.

Real Interview
HARDtalk -- Akinwumi Adesina, President of the African Development Bank

Thirty minutes of hostile questions asked back to back by a British interviewer, to somebody who answers in his second language. Connection to Lesson 11: watch what he does in the two seconds after each question, which is exactly what the first listening of this lesson describes.

Tip: watch the first ten minutes twice. The first time, follow the content. The second time, ignore the content and count only how many times he concedes something before he disagrees.

Talk
"How to stay calm when you know you will be stressed" -- Daniel Levitin, TED

A neuroscientist on what cortisol does to the brain exactly when you need it most, and on preparing the decision before the pressure arrives. Connection to Lesson 11: your three sentences (concede, contrast, stop) are the decision you make in advance.

Tip: after watching, write your own three sentences for the margin question and leave them next to your keyboard. That is the pre-mortem he describes, applied to an interview.
