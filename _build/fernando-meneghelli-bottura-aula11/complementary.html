Lesson 11 -- What You Knew When You Decided

Grammar Video
"How to use narrative tenses" -- BBC Learning English, 6 Minute Grammar

Past simple, past continuous and past perfect working together in one story. Connection to Lesson 11: a decision story has at least three moments, and without the past perfect the listener rebuilds the order themselves — usually in a way that makes you look slower than you were.

Tip: after the video, retell one decision from your own career in exactly four sentences, using at least two past perfects. Say it out loud at full speed — in real speech "had" contracts to a single sound, and that is what you have to produce.

Talk with transcript
"How to make hard choices" -- Ruth Chang, TED (15 min)

A philosopher on why the hardest decisions are hard: not because we lack information, but because the options are not comparable on a single scale. Connection to Lesson 11: that is precisely what a trade-off is, and being able to say it out loud is what separates a decision story from a result.

Tip: watch once, then write the trade-off of one real decision on a single line, with both sides of it. If you cannot name what you gave up, you have not described a decision — you have described an outcome.

Executive Interview
"Stan Druckenmiller" -- In Good Company (55 min)

An investor describing decisions he got right and decisions he got wrong, with the same tone for both. Connection to Lesson 11: listen for how often he separates the quality of the decision from the quality of the outcome — and how naming what he could not see at the time makes him sound more credible rather than less.

Tip: take thirty minutes of it and count the times he says what he did not know. Then do the same count on your own recording from this lesson. The gap between the two numbers is the work.
