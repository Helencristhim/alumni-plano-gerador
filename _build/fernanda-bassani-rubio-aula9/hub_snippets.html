Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

Three Topics in Two Minutes -- Advice and Presentations

On Thursday you present at the quarterly review: two minutes, three topics. Key words: an agenda, a slide, to present, first of all, to move on, to sum up, a recommendation, a key point. Structure: should and shouldn’t for advice.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An agenda -- the list of topics for a meeting or a presentation
"Here is the agenda for today: three topics."
A slide -- one page of a presentation on the screen
"I have four slides, one for each topic."
To present -- to show and explain something to a group
"I am going to present our results on Thursday."
First of all -- words you use to start the first topic
"First of all, let’s look at the numbers."
To move on -- to go to the next topic
"Now let’s move on to the trial."
To sum up -- to say the most important points again, at the end
"To sum up, it was a good quarter."
A recommendation -- advice about the best thing to do
"My recommendation is to send the samples by air."
A key point -- one of the most important ideas
"There are three key points in my presentation."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Fernanda is going to present at the quarterly review on Thursday. She is a little nervous, so she calls Anna. “You should have three topics,” says Anna. “And you shouldn’t put too many numbers on one slide.”

Fernanda makes a plan. Slide one is the agenda. Then: “First of all, the results.” Then: “Let’s move on to the trial.” And at the end: “To sum up, my recommendation: we should send the samples by air.”

“What should I say after that?” she asks. “Just ask: any questions?” says Anna. Three key points, two minutes.

1. How many topics should Fernanda have?
A Two.
B Three.
C Five.
2. What is on the first slide?
A The results.
B The agenda.
C The recommendation.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “You should to practice.”
B “You should practice.”
C “You shoulds practice.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Should and Shouldn’t for Advice

GRAMMAR

Use should + the simple verb to say something is a good idea, and shouldn’t for a bad idea. To ask for advice: Should I...? What should I...?

JobFormExample
Advice: a good ideashould + verbYou should have three topics.
Advice: a bad ideashouldn’t + verbYou shouldn’t put too many numbers.
Ask for adviceShould I...? / What should I...?How many slides should I have?
A recommendationI think we should + verbI think we should send the samples by air.
Same form for everybodyI / you / he / she / we / they + shouldShe should present first. (not shoulds)
The trapno to after shouldNot You should to practice. Say You should practice.

Never: You should to practice · She shoulds start · You don’t should read · What I should say?
