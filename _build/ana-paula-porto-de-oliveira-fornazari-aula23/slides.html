Keep It Going

A sunny morning, a dog park, and a stranger with a puppy. Tonight you learn to start a conversation and keep it going — with question tags.

Warm-up

From a Recipe to a Conversation

First: how does Linda make datil sauce? Start with First. Then: at a dinner, how do you start a conversation with someone new? What do you say first?

The Target

Three Moves for a Stranger

1. Start a conversation with a comment and a little question.

2. Keep it going with questions and By the way.

3. End it in a friendly way.

Diagnostic

In the Elevator

You are in an elevator with a neighbor you do not know. It is very hot outside. What do you say?

Say two sentences. Do not worry about the grammar yet.

Chapter 3: The Code

The Little Question at the End

Question tags

Grammar Discovery

What Happens at the End?

"It is really humid today, isn't it?"

"Those are border collies, aren't they?"

"He is not afraid of them, is he?"

"You live near the beach, don't you?"

Is each sentence positive or negative? And the little question at the end?

SentenceTagExample
Positivenegative tagIt is hot, isn't it?
Negativepositive tagYou are not from here, are you?
With be, can, will...use the same verbHe can swim, can't he? We will see you, won't we?
Present simple (no auxiliary)do / doesYou live here, don't you? She works here, doesn't she?
Past simple (no auxiliary)didYou moved last year, didn't you?
The subjectalways a pronounYour dogs are friendly, aren't they?
The musicvoice down: you are making conversation · voice up: it is a real question

Positive sentence, negative tag. Negative sentence, positive tag. The verb comes from the sentence.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Sunday at the Dog Park

One conversation, two recordings, one flyer

Dialogue

A Stranger With a Puppy

M
Morning! It is really humid today, isn't it?
A
It is! But there is a nice breeze by the water, isn't there?
M
True. Those are border collies, aren't they? They are beautiful.
A
Thank you! They are sisters. Your puppy is very friendly. He is not afraid of them, is he?
M
Not at all. He loves big dogs. You are not from Florida, are you?
A
No, I am from Brazil. I moved here last year. By the way, I am Ana Paula.
M
Nice to meet you, I am Mike. We come here every Sunday. You will come back next week, won't you?
A
Of course! Anyway, I have to go. The girls need their breakfast. See you next Sunday!
Comprehension

About Mike

1. What does Mike say about the weather?
It is really humid.
2. What does Mike's puppy love?
Big dogs.
3. How often does Mike come to the dog park?
Every Sunday.
Listening 1 · Weekend Radio

Another Hot Weekend

A local radio host talks about the weekend. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the weather going to be like on Saturday?
Hot and humid, with a breeze in the afternoon.
2. What happens downtown on Saturday morning?
The farmers market.
3. What does she ask the listeners to bring to the beach?
Water for their dogs, and a leash.
Listening 2 · A Voice Message

Mike Calls

Mike, from the dog park, leaves a message. Sound first — no text.

1. Where did Mike bump into Ana Paula's husband?
At the supermarket.
2. What day and time is the dog meetup?
On Sunday, at eight in the morning.
3. What does Mike want Ana Paula to bring?
The two girls (her dogs) and a leash.

What I Can Do Now

I use a negative tag after a positive sentence, and a positive tag after a negative one.
I use do, does and did in the tag when the sentence has no auxiliary.
