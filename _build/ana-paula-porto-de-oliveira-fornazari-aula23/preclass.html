Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

In the United States, people often talk to strangers: in a line, in an elevator, at the park. This is small talk, and it usually starts with a comment and a little question at the end.

"It is a beautiful day, isn't it?" "Your dog is so cute. He is a puppy, isn't he?" "You live in this building, don't you?" The little question is a question tag. You do not really need an answer: you want to start a conversation.

The rule is simple. After a positive sentence, use a negative tag. After a negative sentence, use a positive tag: "You are not from here, are you?" When there is no auxiliary, use do, does or did: "You moved last year, didn't you?"

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Question Tags

GRAMMAR

Positive sentence, negative tag. Negative sentence, positive tag. The verb comes from the sentence.

SentenceTagExample
Positive with benegative beIt is hot, isn't it?
Negative with bepositive beYou are not tired, are you?
Present simpledon't / doesn'tShe lives here, doesn't she?
Past simpledidn'tYou saw the game, didn't you?
can / willcan't / won'tYou will come, won't you?
The subjectalways a pronounThe dogs are hungry, aren't they?

Never: You like coffee, isn't it? · It is hot, is it? · She moved here, doesn't she?

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

It is very hot at the dog park. You start a conversation:
A "It is so hot today, is it?"
B "It is so hot today, don't it?"
C "It is so hot today, isn't it?"
You think your neighbor works at the hospital. You check:
A "You work at the hospital, don't you?"
B "You work at the hospital, aren't you?"
C "You work at the hospital, isn't it?"
You want to change the topic. You say:
A "Anyway, do you know a good vet?"
B "By the way, do you know a good vet?"
C "In the way, do you know a good vet?"
You want to end the conversation kindly. You say:
A "Bye. I go now."
B "By the way, goodbye."
C "Anyway, I have to go. It was nice to meet you!"
