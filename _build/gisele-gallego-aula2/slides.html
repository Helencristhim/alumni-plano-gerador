Chapter 3: The Code

This One or That One?

Two products, or the whole aisle

Grammar Discovery

Two Boxes, or the Whole Aisle?

Look at the orange words. When am I comparing two things, and when am I comparing one with all the rest?

"This one is stronger than that one on grease."

"The gentle one is more expensive than the heavy-duty one."

"The value pack is the biggest box on the shelf."

"For my hands, this is the best thing in the aisle."

AdjectiveTwo things (+ than)All of them (the ...)
short: cheap, strongcheaper thanthe cheapest
ending in -y: dirty, easydirtier thanthe dirtiest
one vowel + one consonant: bigbigger thanthe biggest
long: expensive, effectivemore expensive thanthe most expensive
good / badbetter than / worse thanthe best / the worst
the sameas gentle as the other onenot as strong as this one

Key idea: two products take than; the whole aisle takes the. And a word never takes both marks at the same time: it is cheaper or more expensive, never more cheaper.

Listening 2 · A True Story

The Worst Forty Dollars I Ever Spent

Sound first — no text on screen.

0:000:00
1. What did she think she was buying, and what was it really?
Something to wash two wool sweaters. It was really a heavy-duty degreaser, the kind you use on a garage floor.
2. Why did she choose that box, and which part did she not read?
She chose it because of the flowers on the front. She did not read the back, which said it breaks down grease and oil on contact.
3. What is her ten-second rule now?
Turn the box over, find the ingredient list, and look for two things: the words gentle on and the word fragrance-free. If they are not there, it goes back on the shelf.
Listening 3 · How to Use It

One Scoop, Cold Water

Sound first — no text on screen.

0:000:00
1. How much powder for a normal load, and why not more?
One scoop. The scoop inside the box is the right size, and more powder does not mean a cleaner shirt.
2. What must you never mix it with, and what happens if you do?
Bleach, and any heavy-duty cleaner. Together they cancel each other out at best, and they damage the fabric at worst.
3. The stain is still there after the first wash. What must you not do, and what should you do?
Do not put the piece in the dryer, because heat sets a stain permanently. Soak it for thirty minutes in cold water with half a scoop, and wash it a second time.
Real Document

The Back of the Box

MEADOW GENTLE WASH

Laundry powder · 40 loads · fragrance-free

What it does

Breaks down grease and lifts a fresh stain in one wash. Dissolves completely in cold water.

Ingredients

Plant-based cleaning agents, sodium carbonate, enzymes, oxygen whitener. No perfume. No dye.

Directions

One scoop for a normal load, two for a heavy one. Cold or warm water, up to 40 degrees. Gentle on color and on wool.

Warnings

Do not mix with bleach or with any heavy-duty cleaner. Keep out of reach of children. If the stain remains, do not dry with heat.

Size: 2.2 lb (1 kg) · not a value packShelf life: 9 months
1. You have one heavy load and one normal load. How much powder, and at what temperature?
Three scoops in total: two for the heavy load and one for the normal one, in cold or warm water, up to forty degrees.
2. Which two lines on this label tell you it is safe for the girls’ colored clothes?
Fragrance-free with no perfume and no dye in the ingredients, and gentle on color and on wool in the directions.
3. It is October and the box says nine months. Should you buy three of them?
No. With a shelf life of nine months and forty loads in each box, the third one would go bad before you used it.
