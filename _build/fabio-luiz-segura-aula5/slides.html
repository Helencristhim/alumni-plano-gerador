Chapter 3: If I Were You

The Tense That Lets You Say Anything

Advice, disagreement and a hard question — without committing to any of them

Grammar Discovery

Which Two Are Not Real?

"If the panel finishes early, I will come and find you."

"If I were in your position, I would look at the regulatory angle first."

"If you send me the deck tonight, I will read it on the flight."

"If we expanded into that market, we would need a local partner."

The blue lines describe things that may really happen tonight. The red ones use a past form for a present idea — and nobody is promising anything.

JobFormExample
Real, and it may happen tonightfirst conditional: if + present simple, will + verbIf the panel finishes early, I will come and find you.
Imagined, and probably not happeningsecond conditional: if + past simple, would + verbIf we expanded into that market, we would need a local partner.
Giving advice without giving an orderIf I were you / If I were in your positionIf I were in your position, I would look at the regulatory angle first.
The verb be in the if halfwere for everybody, including I and heIf I were you · If he were in charge
Disagreeing softlysecond conditional as a questionWould that still work if the volumes were ten times bigger?
The trapno would in the if halfNot if I would be you. Say if I were you.
The other trapthe past form is not about the pastIf we expanded is about now, not about last year.

In one line: the second conditional is how English says a hard thing softly — it moves the sentence one step away from reality, and that step is the politeness.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Ninety Minutes at One Table

One conversation, three recordings, one card on the seat

Dialogue

Table of Six, Nine at Night

P
Pieter. Rotterdam. Long day?
F
Fabio, from Sao Paulo. Long, yes, but good. What were you doing before this job?
P
Nobody ever asks that. Shipping, for eleven years. I only came into banking because somebody needed a person who understood cargo documents.
F
Then we have common ground. Half of what my division does is trade finance, which is cargo documents with a bank in the middle.
P
Ha. Then you know exactly why I lost my hair. Did you go to the panel discussion on settlement this afternoon?
F
I did. Honestly, if I were them, I would have spent less time on the technology and more on who pays for the change.
P
That is the whole argument, is it not. We are two years into the same problem. What are you doing about it on your side?
F
We are replacing the settlement platform this year. Look, I should let you get back to your table — but if you send me what you learned in year one, I will send you our requirements. That is a better follow-up than a business card.
Comprehension

About Pieter

1. What was Pieter doing before banking, and how does he react to the question?
Shipping, for eleven years. He says nobody ever asks that.
2. Why did he move into banking?
Because somebody needed a person who understood cargo documents.
3. What does he say about the problem on his own side, and what does he ask?
He says they are two years into the same problem, and he asks what Fabio is doing about it.
Listening 1 · Why the Dinner Counts

Nobody Can Walk Away

Somebody explaining why the soft part of the week is not soft at all. Sound first — no text.

1. What three things does she say make the dinner different from any meeting?
Everybody is seated so nobody can walk away, it lasts ninety minutes with the same six people, and nobody has a slide deck.
2. What does she say the person on your left actually is?
A warm introduction to somebody you have been trying to reach for two years.
3. What is her single piece of advice, and what does she say not to do?
Ask them what they were doing before this job, and then listen. Do not talk about the conference, because everybody does.
Listening 2 · How to Leave

Three Parts, Eight Seconds

Leaving a conversation politely, which almost nobody teaches. Sound first — no text.

1. What two things does he say people do instead?
They stay far too long out of embarrassment, or they disappear, which he says is worse.
2. What are the three parts?
Close the subject, say something specific about the person rather than about the event, and say what happens next.
3. What does he say the third part changes?
Without it, it sounds like an ending. With it, it sounds like a beginning, and the follow-up email becomes easy to write.
Listening 3 · A European Voice

The Evening Is Part of the Work

Why the deals that survive are the ones where the two sides had dinner. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say the dinner is practical rather than sentimental?
Because when something goes wrong eighteen months later, the question is whether the other person picks up the phone, and they pick up for somebody they have had dinner with.
2. How does she describe the evening in relation to the working day?
As part of the work, not as the reward at the end of the day.
3. What does she say happens if you skip the dinner?
You have not saved an evening. You have removed the only informal contact you were going to have all week.

The Person on Your Right

Role-Play 2 of 3 — Semi-free

Disagreeing at the Table

Scenario: The person opposite says something about your market that is wrong, in front of five other people. Disagree without hardening the table: soften it with a hypothetical, give your reason, and hand the floor back with a question.

Keyword chips:

Would that still work if...? If I were looking at it from the client side That is true in Europe. In Brazil it is different because... What has your experience been?
Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

Three Minutes, No Notes

Scenario: Three minutes at the table with a stranger. Open it, find common ground, give one piece of advice, disagree with one thing they say, and then leave the conversation in three parts so that the follow-up writes itself.

No keywords, no notes, three minutes.
