Talk
Celeste Headlee — “10 ways to have a better conversation” (TED)

Eleven minutes from somebody who interviewed people for a living, on the ten habits that keep a conversation alive. Rule number one and rule number nine are the two this lesson practised: ask open questions, and listen instead of preparing your answer.

Tip: write down the two rules you already follow and the one you never do. That last one is your target at the next dinner.

Talk
Julian Treasure — “How to speak so that people want to listen” (TED)

The other half of the same problem. In a noisy room, being understood is not only about the words: it is about pace, volume and where you put the stress. Ten minutes, and every point is something you can change tonight.

Tip: he does a vocal warm-up at the end. Do it once before your next call in English and see whether the first two minutes come out differently.

Sibos TV — “How can banks and tech companies collaborate to benefit customers?”

A conversation between a bank and a technology company, filmed at the event, on exactly the relationship this lesson is about building. Listen to how much of it is relationship language and how little of it is product.

Tip: count the hypotheticals. Every time somebody says "would", write the sentence down — that is the second conditional doing the diplomatic work.
