Lesson 13 · From Numbers to Narrative

How It Used to Be, How It Is Now

A line on a chart is not a story. Before, the turning point, and now: that is a story.

Chapter 1: First Words

Which Number Changed the Most?

Before that: one soft proposal with could, one risk with might, and a question at the end.

Now the real question. Think of one number in your area that is very different from three years ago. What was it then, and what is it now?

Today’s Goal

One Line. Three Parts.

1. The Words

a turning point, a setback, steady, sharply, to recover, to level off...

2. One Structure

used to — how things were before, and are not now

3. Your Story

one number from your area: before, the turning point, now

Fourteen percent is a number. Twelve percent for years, a turning point in one quarter, and fourteen percent today: that is a narrative.

One Question

Then and Now

Ten years ago, what was a normal working day for you? Tell me one thing you did every day then that you do not do now.

Chapter 3: The Code

True Then, Not True Now

Two small words for the past that is finished

Grammar Discovery

Is It True Today?

"We used to sell mostly through distributors. Now workshops are more than half of our volume."

"Our market share used to be steady, at roughly twelve percent."

"We did not use to track the south as a separate region."

"Did your team use to visit workshops before the program?"

What do the orange words have in common? Was each sentence true for a long time in the past? Is it true today?

FormStructureExample
Positiveused to + verbWe used to sell mostly through distributors.
Negativedid not use to + verbWe did not use to track the south.
QuestionDid ... use to + verb?Did you use to visit workshops?
One eventpast simple, not used toWe launched the program in 2024.
Todaypresent simpleNow workshops are more than half of our volume.

In one line: used to is for habits and situations that lasted a long time in the past and are not true now. With did, it is use to, without the d.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Three Years in One Line

One call, three recordings, one chart

Dialogue

The Chart Call, Before the Board

D
The board will see this chart on Thursday. Three years in one line. What is the story, Marcos?
M
Three years ago, we used to sell mostly through distributors. Our share was steady, roughly twelve percent, and nothing used to change.
D
And then? Did your team use to visit the workshops?
M
No, we did not use to. The distributors did it. Then we started our own program, and that was the turning point. Workshops grew gradually, every quarter.
D
What about this fall in the middle? The line goes down sharply.
M
That was a setback. We were changing the price in March, and sales in the south dropped for two months. It was a slowdown, not a crisis.
D
Did it recover? The board is going to ask me that first.
M
Yes. It recovered by July, and now it has leveled off at roughly fourteen percent. We used to talk about price. Now the main factor is the visits.
Comprehension

About Diane

1. When will the board see the chart, and what does Diane want from Marcos?
On Thursday. She wants the story behind three years in one line.
2. Which part of the line does Diane ask about?
The fall in the middle, where the line goes down sharply.
3. What does Diane think the board will ask her first?
If the number recovered.
Listening 1 · A Head of Strategy

Before, the Turning Point, Now

A head of strategy on how she explains a chart to the board. Sound first — no text.

1. Does she describe the line point by point? What does she do?
No. She tells it in three parts: how things used to be, the turning point, and how things are now.
2. What three examples of a turning point does she give?
A new product, a new price, a new team.
3. What can a board not accept, in her opinion?
A presenter who does not know why the month was bad.
Listening 2 · A Presentation Coach

Give the Line a Shape

A coach on the words for how a number moves. Sound first — no text.

1. Which two words do most presenters use for every chart?
Up and down: it went up, it went down.
2. Which words does he suggest for the shape of the line?
Sharply, gradually, steady, recover, and level off.
3. Is the data different in his two example sentences? What is different?
No, it is the same data. The difference is the narrative.
Listening 3 · Business Radio

Less Coffee, More Money

A short report on a coffee company that changed its customers. Sound first — no text.

1. Who did the company use to sell almost all of its coffee to?
To supermarkets.
2. What happened in the first six months after the shift, and why?
Sales dropped sharply, because the supermarkets bought less and the new customers were still small.
3. Does the company sell more coffee today? Why is the founder happy?
No, it sells less, but it earns almost twice as much on every kilo.
