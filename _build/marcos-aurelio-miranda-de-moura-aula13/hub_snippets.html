From Numbers to Narrative -- How It Used to Be, How It Is Now

A line on a chart is not a story. How it used to be, the turning point, and how it is now: that is a story. Key words: a turning point, a shift, a slowdown, a setback, a factor, steady, sharply, gradually, to recover, to level off. Structure: used to for past habits and states.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A turning point -- the moment when a situation starts to change in an important way
"The new workshop program was the turning point."
A shift -- a change from one situation or direction to another
"There was a shift from distributors to workshops."
A slowdown -- a time when something grows or moves more slowly than before
"We had a slowdown in the south for two months."
A setback -- a problem that stops progress for a short time
"The price change was a setback, not a crisis."
A factor -- one of the things that causes a result
"The main factor was the first visit, not the price."
Steady -- not changing much, the same month after month
"Our share used to be steady at roughly twelve percent."
Sharply -- very fast and by a lot, suddenly
"Sales in the south dropped sharply in April."
Gradually -- slowly, a little at a time
"Workshops grew gradually, every quarter."
To recover -- to go back to a good level after a problem
"Sales recovered by July."
To level off -- to stop going up or down and stay at the same level
"Our share leveled off at roughly fourteen percent."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Three years ago, the company used to sell mostly through distributors. The distributors used to visit the workshops, and the market share used to be steady, at roughly twelve percent. The marketing team did not use to track the south as a separate region. Things are different now: workshops are more than half of the volume.

Look at the difference. Used to is for a habit or a situation that lasted a long time in the past and is not true now. For one event, use the past simple: we started our own program, we changed the price in March. In a question, it is did ... use to, without the d: did your team use to visit workshops?

Now the part that is not grammar. When you explain a chart, do not read it point by point. Tell it in three parts. How things used to be, with a word like steady. The turning point, the shift, and what happened after: did the number grow gradually or sharply? If there was a setback or a slowdown, say the main factor and when it started to recover. And finish with now: is it still growing, or did it level off?

1. Why does the text say "we used to sell mostly through distributors"?
A Because it was true for many years, and it is not true now.
B Because it happened only one time, in one important month of last year.
C Because it is still true today, and it will be true next year.
2. Why is it "we changed the price in March", not "we used to change the price"?
A Because used to is only for people, never for prices or numbers in a report.
B Because the price change was one event, not a habit.
C Because March is in the past.
3. According to the text, what is a good data story?
A A list of every number on the chart, one by one, from the first month to the last.
B Only the number from today.
C How it used to be, the turning point, and how it is now.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used To

GRAMMAR

Used to for habits and situations that lasted a long time in the past and are not true now. Past simple for one event.

FormStructureExample
Positiveused to + verbWe used to sell mostly through distributors.
Negativedid not use to + verbWe did not use to track the south.
QuestionDid ... use to + verb?Did your team use to visit workshops?
One eventpast simpleWe changed the price in March.
Nowpresent simpleWorkshops are more than half of our volume.

Never: we use to sell (use used to) · did you used to (use did you use to) · our share was used to be (use used to be) · we used to launch the program last year (one event: we launched).

Stage 2: Put the Data Story in Order

Order

Diane wants the story of the chart in under a minute. Put it in order: how it used to be, the turning point, the growth, the setback, and now.

?"Now our share has leveled off at roughly fourteen percent."
?"We used to sell mostly through distributors."
?"The price change was a short setback."
?"Then our own field team was the turning point."
?"Workshops grew gradually, every quarter."
