Lesson 14 -- Pre-class

Reading the Room -- The Signals That Tell You What Comes Next

On a fast call, you do not need every word. You need the signal before the important part. Key words: a ballpark figure, bandwidth, traction, optics, a guardrail, a carve-out, a non-starter, to circle back. Structure: signpost phrases in spoken English (having said that, to put it differently, here’s the thing).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A ballpark figure -- a number that is close but not exact
“Can you give us a ballpark figure for the synergies?”
Bandwidth -- the time and energy a team has for more work
“We don’t have the bandwidth for a second project this quarter.”
Traction -- progress or support that is growing
“The deal is gaining traction with their board.”
Optics -- how a decision looks to other people
“The optics of a price cut right now are bad.”
A guardrail -- a limit that keeps a process safe
“We need guardrails on the integration timeline.”
A carve-out -- a part of a company that is kept out of a deal
“The training unit could be a carve-out.”
A non-starter -- an idea that has no chance of being accepted
“A minority stake is a non-starter for us.”
To circle back -- to return to a subject later in a conversation
“Let’s circle back to the price on Friday.”

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On a call with American investors, a founder rarely loses the meeting because of grammar. He loses it in the first ten seconds of a fast answer, when he is still translating the last sentence. The good news is that native speakers help you more than you think: before an important idea, they almost always send a signal.

These signals are called signposts. Having said that means the next idea goes in the opposite direction. To put it differently means the same idea is coming again, in simpler words, so it is your second chance. Here’s the thing means the important part is next. Let me be clear introduces a firm position, often a non-starter. And let’s table that closes a subject for now, but somebody will circle back to it later.

Speed is the other problem. In fast speech, going to sounds like gonna, want to sounds like wanna, and kind of sounds like kinda. You do not need to say them, but you need to hear them. When a buyer says we’re gonna need a ballpark figure, she is asking for a number, and she is asking for it soon.

The strategy is simple. Do not try to catch every word. Listen for the signpost, and predict what kind of sentence comes next. If you miss something, check it: If I understood correctly, you are saying that… Nobody in the room will think less of you. They will think you are listening.

1. According to the text, when does a founder usually lose the meeting?
A When he makes a grammar mistake in the first sentence of his own presentation.
B In the first seconds of a fast answer, while he is still translating.
C When the investors ask about the price.
2. What does to put it differently tell you?
A That the speaker is going to change the subject and talk about something completely new.
B That a problem is coming.
C That the same idea is coming again, in simpler words.
3. What should you do if you miss part of a sentence?
A Check it: If I understood correctly, you are saying that…
B Say nothing and wait, because the investors will probably send everything by email after the call.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Signposts in Spoken English

GRAMMAR

The signpost comes first. It tells you what kind of sentence comes next.

SignpostWhat comes nextExample
Having said thatthe opposite directionThe deal has traction. Having said that, the price is still open.
To put it differentlythe same idea, simplerTo put it differently, we need a smaller carve-out.
Here’s the thingthe most important pointHere’s the thing: we don’t have the bandwidth.
Let me be cleara firm positionLet me be clear: a minority stake is a non-starter.
Let’s table thatthe subject stops for nowLet’s table that and circle back on Friday.
If I understood correctlya check (you say it)If I understood correctly, you need a ballpark figure.
Could you clarify…?a question (you say it)Could you clarify what you meant by optics?

In fast speech: gonna = going to · wanna = want to · kinda = kind of. Hear them; you do not need to say them.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
