Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These are the last eight words of the program: words to talk about a journey.

Milestone -- an important moment or event in a long process
"Bangkok is a big milestone in my English."
Turning point -- the moment when something starts to change
"My first repair in English was a turning point."
Breakthrough -- an important success after a lot of work
"Following the Russian speaker was a real breakthrough."
Achievement -- something good that you did with a lot of effort
"Two minutes without notes is a real achievement."
Fluency -- the ability to speak easily, without many stops
"Fluency is not perfect grammar. It is not stopping."
Toolkit -- a set of things that you use to do a job
"My toolkit for Bangkok has sixteen expressions."
To look back on -- to think about something that happened in the past
"When I look back on July, I see how much has changed."
To build on -- to use what you have as a base to do more
"After Bangkok, I want to build on what I learned."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Lucas is a biologist from Curitiba. A year ago, he was afraid to speak English at congresses. He understood the papers, but he lost the thread in every conversation. Then he took a course for six months. Since then, he has given two talks in English. Next month, he's going to present a poster in Berlin. He has a simple plan: "If I miss a word, I'll ask for repair. My English is clearly better than last year, but it is not perfect, and that's OK." When he looks back on the course, he says the turning point was the day he stopped translating in his head.
Lucas says "Since then, he has given two talks". What does this tell you?
A He gave the talks a long time ago, before the course, when he was still afraid of speaking English
B The talks happened between the course and now
C He is going to give two talks next year
Lucas says "My English is clearly better". Why can he use a booster here?
A Because boosters are always more polite than hedges in every situation, even when you have no evidence
B Because he wants to overstate
C Because he has evidence: two talks in English since the course

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Five Tools, One Story

GRAMMAR

No new grammar today: this is your whole program in five tools. Together, they tell your story.

the pastpast simpleIn July, I lost the thread in fast conversations.
the result until nowhave + past participle (since, so far)Since July, I have learned to repair in English.
the planam / is / are going to + verbI'm going to take part in the workshop.
the possible futureif + present, will + verbIf I miss a word, I'll ask for repair.
the strong claimclearly / definitely + verb or adjectiveMy listening is clearly better than in July.

Questions with the five tools: "What did you find?" / "Have you presented here before?" / "Are you going to the dinner?" / "Will you send me the paper if I give you my card?"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
At the registration desk, a researcher asks what you work on. What do you say?
A "In a nutshell, we study why patients with leprosy have nerve pain."
B "I will tell you about my research later, maybe at the poster session on Wednesday afternoon, if you are there."
C "Pain."
In the workshop, a question has two parts and you are not sure about the first one. What do you say?
A "I think the answer is yes, but I didn't really understand the question, so I will answer the second part only."
B "Let me make sure I understood your question correctly. Are you asking about the dose?"
C "Repeat."
The conversation is over and you want to keep the contact. What do you say?
A "OK, bye."
B "Well, I have to go now, because there is another session in the big room and I want a good seat."
C "It was great meeting you. Let's stay in touch."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Two minutes. Look back on the program: what can you do in English now that you could not do in July? Then say what you are going to do in Bangkok, and what you want to build on after the congress. Use at least three tools: the past, the result until now and a plan. No script -- just speak.
