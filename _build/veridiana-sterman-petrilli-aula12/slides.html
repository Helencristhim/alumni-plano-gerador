Lesson 12

Twice a Week

How often you do things, and how to say it honestly

Warm-up

From Tuesday to the Week

First, your Tuesday in thirty seconds: the school run, the practice, the club. Then think about the whole week: what do you do every day, what do you do only once a week, and what do you almost never have time for?

The Target

How Often, Honestly

1. The word. Always, usually, often, sometimes, hardly ever, never.

2. The number. Once a week, twice a month, every other day.

3. The honest version. The week you have, not the week you plan.

Twice a week, said honestly, is still a habit.

Diagnostic

A Question at the Club

An American mother at the club asks you: “How often do you come here? And how often do you have English class?”

Answer both questions, with the days, in two sentences.

Chapter 4: The Code

Always, Usually, Hardly Ever

The small words that say how often, and where they go

Grammar Discovery

Where Does the Little Word Go?

I always start at eight.
The girls are usually tired on Tuesdays.
We hardly ever eat out on weekdays.
I have English twice a week.

Look at the blue words: are they before or after the verb? What changes when the verb is are? Now look at the red one: where does it go?

JobFormExample
The scalealways → usually → often → sometimes → hardly ever → neverI hardly ever skip English.
With a normal verbthe word goes before the verbI always start at eight.
With bethe word goes after am, is, areThey are usually tired.
The numberonce, twice, three times + a week / a month — at the endI have English twice a week.
Every and every otherevery Tuesday · every other Sunday (one yes, one no)Grandma comes every other Sunday.
The questionHow often do you...?How often do you go to the gym?
The trapnever two times for week, never I go always, never don’t neverSay twice a week and I always go.

In one line: the little word goes before the verb but after be, and the number goes at the end.

Listening 1 · A Dental Hygienist

How Often Do You Floss?

The question every patient answers, and the honest version. Sound first — no text.

1. What does almost everybody answer, and how many people really do it?
Almost everybody says every day. About one in ten actually does.
2. How does she ask the question now, and why does it work better?
She asks when did you last floss, because it asks about one day and not about a habit, so people answer honestly.
3. What is her advice, and why does it work?
Floss once a day, at the same time, right after something you never skip, like brushing at night. A small habit attached to a big one almost always survives.
Listening 2 · A Father of Two

A Week That Repeats

Two kids, two jobs, and one rule nobody breaks. Sound first — no text.

1. What happens on Mondays and Wednesdays, and what does he do on those days?
His son has soccer after school, so he leaves the office early.
2. How often is pizza night, and what do the kids do?
Every other Friday. The kids choose the movie, so they usually watch the same three movies.
3. What happens now and then, and what is the family rule?
Something breaks, like a meeting running late or a forgotten soccer bag. Then they change one thing, not everything: they can move a day, but they never cancel pizza night.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I use the scale: always, usually, often, sometimes, hardly ever, never.
I put the little word before a normal verb and after be. Never I go always.
I say once, twice, three times a week, and every other Sunday. Never two times for week.
I ask how often, and I answer with the honest number, not the planned one.
I know the words: a habit, to skip, now and then, on a regular basis, to stick to, a treat, to squeeze in, a chore, to make time for, to unwind.
