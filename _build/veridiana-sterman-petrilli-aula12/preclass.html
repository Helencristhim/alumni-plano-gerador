Vocabulary

A habit -- something you do so often that you do it without thinking
"Reading before bed is a habit I want to keep."
To skip -- to not do something you usually do
"I skip lunch on Tuesdays, which is a bad idea."
Now and then -- sometimes, but not often and not on fixed days
"Now and then I read a novel in English."
On a regular basis -- again and again, at times that repeat
"The girls see the dentist on a regular basis."
To stick to -- to keep doing what you planned, even when it is hard
"I try to stick to two gym classes a week."
A treat -- something nice you give yourself, not every day
"A long coffee with a friend is my Saturday treat."
To squeeze in -- to find a short time for something in a full day
"I squeeze in my emails between two patients."
A chore -- a small job at home that has to be done again and again
"Doing the dishes is the chore nobody wants."
To make time for -- to plan your day so that there is space for something
"I make time for English twice a week."
To unwind -- to relax after a lot of work or stress
"On Friday nights I unwind with a series."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

When somebody asks "How often do you...?", English gives you two kinds of answer. The first kind is a single word on a scale: always, usually, often, sometimes, hardly ever, never. The second kind is a number: once a week, twice a month, three times a year, every other Sunday.

The single words have a fixed place. With a normal verb, they go before it: "I always start at eight." "We hardly ever eat out on weekdays." With be, they go after it: "The girls are usually tired on Tuesdays." "I am never late for the first patient." The number expressions are more relaxed, and they normally go at the end: "I have English twice a week."

Portuguese puts the word after the verb, so a Brazilian often says I go always. In English that sounds wrong straight away. The second trap is the number: English says a week, not for week, and it prefers once and twice to one time and two times. And because never is already negative, it never comes with don't: "I never skip breakfast."

Finally, be honest with the scale. If you go to the gym twice a week and skip it now and then, the true answer is "I usually go twice a week", not "I always go". Nobody minds the honest number. It is the one that keeps the conversation going.

1. Where does always go in a sentence with a normal verb, like start?
A Before the verb: I always start at eight.
B After the verb, the same as in Portuguese: I start always at eight in the morning.
C At the very end of the sentence, after the time: I start at eight always.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A The girls usually are tired on Tuesdays after school and swimming.
B The girls are tired usually on Tuesdays.
C The girls are usually tired on Tuesdays.
3. What is the natural way to say two times in one week?
A Two times for week, which is the closest to the Portuguese and is perfectly natural.
B Twice a week.
C Two times in the week.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

An American mother at the club asks how often you come. You say:
A "I come always on Tuesdays, after school, with my two daughters and sometimes my mother."
B "I come every Tuesday, after school."
C "I come two times for week."
A patient asks how often he should come for a check-up. You say:
A "Twice a year, on a regular basis."
B "You should come always, and never skip, because the check-up is very important for everybody."
C "Two times for year."
The school asks if you read with your daughters every night. The honest answer is once a week. You say:
A "Yes, of course, we always read together every single night before they go to bed."
B "We don't never skip it."
C "Honestly, once a week, on Thursdays, and now and then we skip it."
A friend asks what you do to relax on a busy week. You say:
A "I usually unwind with a series on Friday nights."
B "I unwind usually with a series on Friday nights, and sometimes on Saturday nights too, if I can."
C "I am unwinding always on Fridays."
