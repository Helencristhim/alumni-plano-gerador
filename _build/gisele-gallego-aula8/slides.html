Chapter 3: The Code

Three Pasts, One Story

What happened, what was happening, and what had happened before

Grammar Discovery

Which Past Is Which?

"The father went out on the water and never came back."

"While the family was waiting at the dock, the radio went quiet."

"She found out that he had sold the boat months before."

"By the time we sat down, the film had already started."

The orange parts happen on the night of the story. The blue parts happened before it, and the sentence takes one step backwards to reach them.

TenseFormWhat it does
Past simplewent, came, found, satThe events, one after the other: he went out and never came back.
Past continuouswas / were + -ingThe long action already running when the event lands: while the family was waiting.
Past perfecthad + past participleOne step further back, before that moment: he had sold the boat months before.
while / when / ascontinuous + simpleLong action in the continuous, short one in the simple: while I was reading the subtitles, the twist arrived.
before / already / by the time / neverthese pull you into hadBy the time we sat down, the film had already started.

In one line: the story walks in the past simple, the continuous is the room it walks through, and the perfect is the step it took before.

That she is not going to give away the ending.

That her father had sold the boat months before, and had never told anyone.

Do not read anything, do not watch the trailer, and do not let anybody tell you what happens at the end.

The Announcement in the Dark

Times, theaters and one rule about phones. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00

Part of the film is spoken in Portuguese, and the subtitles on screen are in English.

Both are in Theater Four: one at seven fifteen, up the stairs and to the left, and one at nine forty.

Listening 3 · An Opinion

Subtitles Are Not Cheating

Why she never watches a dubbed film, and what she does twice. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Why does she say a dubbed voice teaches you less?
Because it was recorded in a studio months later by somebody who was never in the room, so you lose how people really sound when they interrupt each other or when they are tired.
2. What are the two steps of her method?
First she watches the whole film with the subtitles and enjoys the plot; then she goes back to two or three scenes and watches them again with the subtitles off, two minutes each.
3. What does she do when the dialogue is too fast?
She stops trying to catch every single word and listens for the three words that carry the meaning.
