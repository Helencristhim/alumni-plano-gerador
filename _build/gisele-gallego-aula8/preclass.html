Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

The Film I Could Not Follow -- Plot, Review and the Words for Afterwards

A review, a plot and the words that let you talk about a movie after the lights come up. Key words: a plot, a twist, the cast, to be set in, a subtitle, a score, a sequel, gripping, slow-paced, far-fetched, to give away the ending, a screening. Structure: the narrative past -- past simple, past continuous and past perfect.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A plot -- everything that happens in a film, in the order it happens
"The plot is simple enough to say in one line."
A twist -- a surprise that changes what you thought was happening
"The twist arrives in a kitchen, and nobody sees it coming."
The cast -- all the actors in a film, taken together
"The cast is mostly unknown, and that is why you believe them."
To be set in -- to happen in a particular place or a particular time
"The film is set in a fishing town on the coast."
A subtitle -- a line of text at the bottom of the screen that gives you the words
"Every line in Portuguese has an English subtitle."
A score -- the music written for a film
"The score is a guitar and a radio playing in another room."
A sequel -- a second film that continues the story of the first
"There will be no sequel, and there should not be one."
Gripping -- so tense that you cannot look away from the screen
"It is the most gripping quiet film I have seen this year."
Slow-paced -- moving slowly, with long scenes and few events
"The first hour is slow-paced, and it is supposed to be."
Far-fetched -- too unlikely to believe, even inside a story
"A letter that arrives at exactly the right moment is far-fetched."
To give away the ending -- to tell somebody how a film finishes and ruin it for them
"Tell me about it, but do not give away the ending."
A screening -- one showing of a film, at one particular time
"The nine forty screening is the subtitled one."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The film is set in a Portuguese fishing family in New Bedford, in 1986. The father went out on the water one night and never came back. While the family was waiting at the dock, the radio went quiet, and the daughter opened his desk.

That is where she found out that he had sold the boat months before, and had never told anyone. The family had bought that boat back once already, years earlier, so the argument in the kitchen was not really about money. The first hour is slow-paced on purpose, and one detail, a letter, is far-fetched.

I was reading the subtitles when the twist arrived, and I said something out loud in the cinema. By the time I got home, I had already decided to see it again. Do not ask me how it finishes: I am not going to give away the ending.

1. "She found out that he had sold the boat months before." What happened first?
A He sold the boat, and later she found out.
B She found out, and later he sold the boat.
C Both things happened at exactly the same moment.
2. "While the family was waiting at the dock, the radio went quiet." Why is one verb continuous and the other simple?
A Because after "while" the verb always takes -ing, whatever it means.
B Because the waiting was long and already running, and the radio going quiet was the short event inside it.
C Because the family is plural and the radio is singular.
3. "By the time I got home, I had already decided to see it again." What does "had already decided" tell you?
A That the decision came after arriving home.
B That the decision is still not made.
C That the decision was already taken before that moment.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Telling a Story in the Past

GRAMMAR

Three pasts, one story: the events, the background, and the step before.

TenseFormExample
Past simplewent, came, found, satThe father went out and never came back.
Past continuouswas / were + -ingWhile the family was waiting, the radio went quiet.
Past perfecthad + past participleHe had sold the boat months before.
while / when / aslong action continuous, short action simpleWhile I was reading the subtitles, the twist arrived.
before / already / by the time / neverthese words pull you into hadBy the time we sat down, the film had already started.

Never: While I was reading, the twist was arriving · When I got there, the film was already started · We had went to the earlier screening · She found out he sells the boat.
