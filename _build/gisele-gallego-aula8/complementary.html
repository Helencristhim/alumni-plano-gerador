Lesson 08 -- The Film I Could Not Follow

Film
Charade (1963) -- full film, free and legal on YouTube

Two hours, one plot, and a twist roughly every twenty minutes. Cary Grant and Audrey Hepburn in Paris, a famous score by Henry Mancini, and studio-era diction that is far clearer than anything in a cinema today.

Tip: turn the English subtitles on for the first hour, then off for the second. Afterwards, say the plot out loud in five past-tense sentences -- and do not give away the ending to anyone.

Podcast
Script Apart -- Forrest Gump, with Eric Roth

The writer of a film everybody has seen explains what was in the first draft, what was cut, and why the story is told the way it is. A whole hour of two people talking about plot, cast and structure.

Tip: listen once while you walk the dog. Write down every sentence you catch with "had" in it -- screenwriters live in the past perfect.

Listen on Spotify ↗
Talk
The clues to a great story -- Andrew Stanton, TED

The man who wrote Toy Story and Finding Nemo takes a plot apart on stage: what a story promises in the first minute, and why the audience has to do part of the work. Nineteen minutes, clear American English.

Tip: watch it once with subtitles and once without. Then answer in one sentence: what does he say every good story makes you do?
