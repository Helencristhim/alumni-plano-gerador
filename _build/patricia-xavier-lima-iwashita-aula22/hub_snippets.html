Lesson 22 -- Pre-class

Incident Report

Telling a customer what happened and how it was fixed: when the problem was detected, how long the outage lasted, and what was changed to prevent it. Key words: incident, outage, detect, notify, affected, restore, patch, post-mortem, maintenance, prevent. Structure: the past simple passive (was / were + past participle), and "by" for the person or team that did the action.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Incident -- an unplanned event that interrupts a service
"The incident was detected at 9:14 in the morning."
Outage -- a period of time when a service is not available
"The outage lasted two hours and thirty-eight minutes."
Detect -- to notice that something is wrong, often by machine
"The problem was detected by the monitoring system."
Notify -- to tell someone officially that something has happened
"The affected customers were notified fifteen minutes later."
Affected -- hit by the problem; touched by it
"The affected customers were called personally."
Restore -- to bring a service back to normal
"The service was restored at 11:52."
Patch -- a small update written to fix a problem
"A patch was written and tested the same morning."
Post-mortem -- a meeting held after an incident to learn from it
"A post-mortem was held the next morning."
Maintenance -- planned work to keep a system healthy
"Maintenance is planned for the first Sunday of every month."
Prevent -- to stop something from happening again
"Three actions were agreed to prevent the same failure."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

On March 12 an incident was detected by the monitoring system at 9:14 in the morning. New tickets were not created in the dashboard, and while the support team was checking the logs, two more reports arrived. The problem was confirmed at 9:30, and the affected customers were notified fifteen minutes later. A patch was written and tested, and it was applied at 11:40. The service was restored at 11:52, so the outage lasted two hours and thirty-eight minutes. No data was lost. A post-mortem was held the next morning, and a new alert was added to prevent the same failure. Regular maintenance is planned for the first Sunday of every month. Notice what Patrícia never writes: she never writes "we broke it" or "our team fixed it". She reports what happened to the service, and she gives the times.

1. Why does the report say "the service was restored at 11:52" instead of "we restored the service"?
A Because nobody knows who restored it.
B Because what matters to the customer is the service and the time, not the team.
C Because the action has not finished yet.
2. In "while the support team was checking the logs, two more reports arrived", what does "was checking" describe?
A The background: what was going on when the reports arrived.
B A habit that the team repeats every morning.
C A plan for the following week.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "The customers was notified fifteen minutes later."
B "The customers were notify fifteen minutes later."
C "The customers were notified fifteen minutes later."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Past Simple Passive

GRAMMAR

How to report yesterday's process when the action matters more than the person.

StructureUseExample
was / were + past participleA finished action; the action matters, the person is left out.The service was restored at 11:52.
by + person or teamOnly when the listener needs to know who did it.A patch was written by the product team.
was / were not + past participleSay what did not happen.No data was lost.
Was / Were + subject + past participle?Ask what happened.Were the customers notified?
while + was / were + -ingThe background: what was going on at the time.While the team was checking the logs, more reports arrived.
AvoidDo not drop the -ed of the participle ("was detect") → say "was detected". Do not use "from" for the person ("restored from the team") → say "restored by the team". And match the verb to the subject: one incident was detected, many customers were notified.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

The customer asks what happened this morning. You answer:
A "We had a big problem, but do not worry about it."
B "An integration failure was detected at 9:14 and confirmed at 9:30."
C "A problem was detect at 9:14 this morning."
The customer asks how long the service was down. The strongest answer is:
A "The outage lasted two hours and thirty-eight minutes; the service was restored at 11:52."
B "Not very long, and the team worked really hard on it."

A worried customer asks if anything was lost. You answer:

B "No data was lost, because every file is backed up every night."
