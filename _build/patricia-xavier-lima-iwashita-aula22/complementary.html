Lesson 22 -- Incident Report

Series
Chernobyl -- Season 1, Episode 5: "Vichnaya Pamyat"

This is the last episode of the HBO miniseries. In a courtroom, three scientists explain step by step what really happened at the power plant: what was detected, what was ignored, and what was changed afterwards. It is one of the clearest examples on screen of a technical failure explained to people who are not technical. Connection to Lesson 22: this is a post-mortem, and almost every sentence in it is built with the past passive.

Tip: watch the courtroom explanation (the last thirty minutes) with English subtitles. Write down 5 sentences with was / were + past participle, then rewrite each one about a service outage at work.

Podcast
The Downtime Project -- "Salesforce Publishes a Controversial Postmortem (and breaks their DNS)"

Two experienced engineers read and discuss the official post-mortem that Salesforce published after its outage in May 2021. They explain what caused it, how long it lasted, how it was fixed, and why the way a company writes about an incident matters as much as the fix itself. It is your own platform, in the exact situation of this lesson. Connection to Lesson 22: every word of today's vocabulary is here -- incident, outage, detect, affected, restore, post-mortem.

Tip: listen once for the story. Listen again and write down 5 past passive sentences the hosts use to say what happened, for example "customers were affected" or "the service was restored". Say each one out loud.

YouTube
"Past Passive - English Grammar Lesson (Intermediate)" -- Maltalingua English Language School

A short, clear grammar lesson on exactly today's structure: how to build was / were + past participle, and when to choose it. The teacher shows why we use the passive when the action matters more than the person who did it, which is what happens in every line of an incident report. Connection to Lesson 22: the grammar behind every answer you gave in class, explained slowly and with examples.

Tip: copy the structure table into your notebook. Then write 5 past passive sentences about a real incident at work: what was detected, who was notified, what was affected, when it was restored, and what was changed to prevent it.
