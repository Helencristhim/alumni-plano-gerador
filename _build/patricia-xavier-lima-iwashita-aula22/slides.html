Chapter 4: Language Focus

The Service Was Restored

The past simple passive — was / were + past participle

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"An incident was detected at 9:14."

"The customers were notified fifteen minutes later."

"The service was restored at 11:52."

"A patch was written by the product team."

All four happened yesterday. What is the same in every green part — and when do we add by?

StructureUseExample
was / were + past participleA finished action; the action matters, not the personThe service was restored at 11:52.
by + person or teamOnly when the listener needs to know who did itA patch was written by the product team.
was / were not + past participleSay what did not happenNo data was lost.
Was / Were + subject + past participle?Ask what happenedWere the customers notified?
while + was / were + -ingThe background: what was going on at the timeWhile the team was checking the logs, more reports arrived.

Yesterday's process is reported with was / were + past participle. One thing takes was, many things take were. Add by only when who did it is part of the answer.

Chapter 5: Telling David

The Call After the Outage

Three questions, three clear answers

Dialogue

"What Happened?"

D
I saw the alert this morning. My team could not create any tickets for almost three hours. What happened?
P
The incident was detected at 9:14, and you were notified as soon as it was confirmed. It was an integration failure after our last release.
D
And how was it fixed?
P
A patch was written and tested, and it was applied at 11:40. The service was restored twelve minutes later. No data was lost.
D
Fine. But what stops this from happening again next month?
P
A post-mortem was held yesterday. A new alert was added to prevent it, and every integration change is now tested before it is released.

Real Artifact

Incident Report #4812

Incident Report #4812 · Northwind Logistics

Sent after the ticket sync outage · Patrícia Xavier Lima Iwashita

What happened

An integration failure was detected at 9:14 and confirmed at 9:30. New tickets were not created in the dashboard for two hours and thirty-eight minutes.

How it was fixed

A patch was written and tested in a safe environment, and it was applied at 11:40. The service was restored at 11:52. No data was lost.

What was changed

A post-mortem was held the next morning. A new alert was added to prevent a repeat, and every integration change is now tested before it is released.

1. When was the failure detected, and when was the service restored?
It was detected at 9:14, and the service was restored at 11:52.
2. What was done to the patch before it was applied?
It was written and tested in a safe environment.
3. What was added after the post-mortem?
A new alert — and every integration change is now tested before it is released.
Listening 2

Karen Reads Your Draft

A manager gives two notes before you send it. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Why does Karen want times and numbers in the report?
Numbers sound like control; adjectives sound like an excuse.
2. How should the first line be written?
About what happened to the service, not about a mistake the team made.
3. Which sentence does she say the director reads?
The last one — what was changed so that it does not happen again.
