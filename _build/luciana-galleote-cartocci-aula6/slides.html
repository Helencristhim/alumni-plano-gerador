Chapter 4: The Code

From Then to Now

One tense for changes that are still true

Grammar Discovery

Four Sentences About a Changing Market

"Demand for data centers has grown over the last three years."

"Our hospital clients have doubled their servers since 2023."

"Two data centers have already reached their capacity."

"In 2023 we built two data centers. So far this year we have built four."

Three of the changes started in the past and are still true now: the blue verb has two parts, have / has + the third form. Look at the last sentence: 2023 is finished, so the verb is simple past; this year is not finished.

JobFormExample
A change that continues until nowhave / has + third formDemand has grown a lot.
From a point in timesince + a datesince 2023, since January
For a periodfor / over the last + timefor three years, over the last two years
Before now, maybe earlier than expectedalready / so farWe have already reached our capacity.
Finished timepast simple, not present perfectIn 2023 we built two data centers.
The questionHow has / Have you...?How has the market changed?

In one line: have / has + the third form for changes that are still true; a finished date takes the past simple.

These are example figures, not market data: a partner snapshot usually looks like this. Almost every line describes a change that started in the past and is still true today. That is why the present perfect is the tense of trends.
1. Turn the Demand line into a full sentence.

Listening 1 · The Market Analyst

Latin America Has Woken Up

A market analyst in London explains how the data center market in Latin America has changed. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say about Latin America ten years ago?
It was a small market: most large companies kept their data in the United States.
2. What has changed since then?
Demand has grown fast, and many global companies have built data centers in Brazil, Mexico and Chile.
3. What is the biggest problem now?
Power: finding enough electricity for the new AI rooms.
Listening 2 · Anna

What Our Hospitals Ask For Now

Anna, from the European technology group, explains how hospital requests have changed in Germany. Sound first — no text.

1. What did hospitals ask for five years ago?
More space for servers and a good backup.
2. What have they started to ask for?
Rooms for AI servers, with more power and more cooling.
3. How many of their hospital clients have asked about AI this year?
Almost half of them.
Listening 3 · The Data Center Manager

The Heat Problem

A data center manager in Texas explains why cooling has become his biggest challenge. Sound first — no text.

1. How long has he managed data centers?
For eighteen years.
2. Why is air cooling not enough for the new racks?
Because the AI racks produce too much heat.
3. What has his team done this year?
They have installed liquid cooling in two rooms.
