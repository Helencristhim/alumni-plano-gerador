The AI Boom in Data Centers -- Describing Trends

AI has changed data centers, and every meeting has a moment about the market. Key words: demand, capacity, cooling, power consumption, a rack, uptime, redundancy, latency, to double, a shortage. Structure: present perfect for trends.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Demand -- how much of a product or service people want to buy
"Demand for data center space has grown very fast."
Capacity -- the amount that a place or a system can hold or produce
"The hospital has doubled its server capacity."
Cooling -- the system that keeps machines at a low temperature
"AI servers need much more cooling than old servers."
Power consumption -- the amount of electricity that a machine or a building uses
"Power consumption is the biggest cost of a data center."
A rack -- a metal frame that holds servers and network equipment
"One AI rack can use as much power as ten old racks."
Uptime -- the time when a system is working without problems
"Hospitals ask for almost one hundred percent uptime."
Redundancy -- extra equipment that works if the main equipment fails
"With redundancy, one failure does not stop the hospital."
Latency -- the small delay before data arrives
"For video analytics, low latency is essential."
To double -- to become two times bigger
"Our data center projects have doubled since 2023."
A shortage -- a situation when there is not enough of something
"There is a shortage of engineers who understand AI cooling."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At a technology fair, an engineer talks about the market. “Demand for data centers has grown a lot over the last three years,” he says. “Our hospital clients have doubled their servers since 2023.”

“The biggest change is power. The new AI racks use much more electricity, and cooling has become a big problem. Two of our clients have already reached their capacity.”

Then he remembers the past: “In 2019 we built our first data center for a hospital. It was small. Now every project is bigger.” 2019 is finished, so that verb is in the past simple.

1. Is demand for data centers still growing, according to the engineer?
A No. It grew only in 2023.
B Yes. It started to grow in the past and it continues now.
C The text does not say.
2. Why does he say “in 2019 we built” and not “we have built”?
A Because 2019 is a finished time.
B Because the data center was small.
C Because it is a plan for the future.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “Our clients have doubled their servers since 2023.”
B “Our clients doubled their servers since 2023.”
C “Our clients are doubling their servers since 2023.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect for Trends

GRAMMAR

Use have / has + the third form for a change that started in the past and is still true. With a finished date (in 2023, last year), use the past simple.

JobFormExample
A change that continues until nowhave / has + third formDemand has grown a lot.
From a point in timesince + a datesince 2023, since January
For a periodfor / over the last + timefor three years, over the last two years
Before now, maybe earlier than expectedalready / so farWe have already reached our capacity.
Finished timepast simple, not present perfectIn 2023 we built two data centers.
The questionHow has / Have you...?How has the market changed?

Never: Demand has grown in 2023 · We work with hospitals since 2005 · Power consumption have increased · We have already reach our capacity
