Chapter 3: The Code

Three Sentences Without a Person, and One With

One structure, three tenses

Grammar Discovery

Where Did the Person Go?

"Applications are reviewed in March."

"My first project was approved on a Friday."

"The feature has been rolled out to everybody."

"Every line is read by two people who did not write it."

In all four, the thing that matters is at the front and the red verb has two parts. Only the last one names a person, in blue — and it names them because the number is the point.

JobFormExample
A rule or a routine stepam / is / are + past participleApplications are reviewed in March.
One finished eventwas / were + past participleMy project was approved on a Friday.
Done, and it still counts todayhas / have been + past participleThe feature has been rolled out to everybody.
Naming who did it+ by + person, only when it is the informationEvery line is read by two people.
Negativeis not / was not / has not been + participleNothing is signed off on a Friday afternoon.
QuestionWhen is it...? · Has it been...?When are the applications reviewed?
The two trapsthe participle is not the base form · a closed time never takes has beenNot is wrote: say is written. Not has been rolled out last month: say was rolled out last month.

In one line: put the thing that matters at the front, use be plus the past participle, and add the person only when the person is the news.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Somebody Who Runs This Every Week

One conversation, two recordings, one board

Dialogue

Your First Week on a Real Team

N
Before you write anything, you should know what happens to it afterwards. Every line is reviewed by two people who did not write it.
V
Two people for every change? Even a small one?
N
Even a small one. Then it is approved or it is flagged, and flagged does not mean wrong. Most flags are about something that was unclear.
V
So where does it get slow? On my course the bottleneck is always the same person waiting for another one.
N
You have described it exactly. Nothing is signed off on a Friday afternoon either, which sounds like superstition and is not.
V
Because if something breaks, nobody is there to fix it until Monday. And after it is signed off, is it live?
N
Not for everybody. It is rolled out to a small group first. Almost all of the work is behind the scenes, and the button people see took an afternoon.
V
That is the part nobody explains on a course. And the feedback from a review — who is it written for, me or the code?
Comprehension

About Nadia

1. What does Nadia say happens to every single line, and does the size of the change matter?
Every line is reviewed by two people who did not write it, and she says it applies even to a small change.
2. What does Nadia say a flag usually means?
That flagged does not mean wrong. She says most flags are about something that was unclear.
3. What does Nadia say about Friday afternoons, and how does she introduce it?
That nothing is signed off then. She introduces it by saying it sounds like superstition and is not, which answers the objection before it is made.
Listening 1 · The Product Manager

From an Idea to Something People Use

A product manager describing the whole path. Sound first — no text.

1. How many stages does she describe, and what does she say about moving between them?
Four stages. She says nothing moves to the next one until the previous one has been signed off, and that the rule exists because of what happened when it did not.
2. Where does she say the process actually gets slow, and what surprises new people about it?
At the review, which is a bottleneck of waiting rather than working. New people expect the building to be the slow part, and it is not.
3. What does she say about the visible part of the work?
That almost all of it is behind the scenes. She says the button somebody sees took an afternoon and everything underneath it took three weeks.
Listening 2 · The Reviewer

What Happens to Your First Submission

Somebody who reviews the work of people starting out. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say about the number of comments on a first submission?
That there are always a lot, usually around twenty, and that the number says nothing about the person. He says his own first submission had more.
2. Why does he say he writes the feedback about the work and not about the person?
Because the work can be changed and the person cannot be changed by a comment. He says the sentence has to be one the writer can act on tomorrow.
3. What is the one reaction he says worries him in somebody new?
Silence. He says a person who argues with a comment is learning, and a person who says nothing and quietly changes everything has understood nothing.

One reply, outstanding since Tuesday. The review is complete on both sides, and the whole week is waiting on a single answer.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I can describe a whole process without inventing a person to be the subject of every sentence.
I use is reviewed for a rule, was approved for one finished event, and has been rolled out for something that still counts.
I never say is wrote or are review, and I say the -ed even when I am speaking fast.
I add by plus a person only when the person is the actual information.
I know the words: to submit, to review, to approve, to flag something, a bottleneck, a stage, feedback, to roll something out, to sign something off, behind the scenes.
