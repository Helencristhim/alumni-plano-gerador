Lesson 07 -- Pre-class

How It Actually Gets Done -- When Nobody Says Who Did It

Applications are reviewed in March. The code is read by two people. Nobody is hiding anything: the process is the subject, and the person is not the point. Key words: to submit, to review, to approve, to flag something, a bottleneck, a stage, feedback, to roll out, to sign something off, behind the scenes. Structure: the passive voice in three tenses.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To submit -- to send something officially to the person or system that will decide about it
"The file is submitted once, and after that you cannot touch it."
To review -- to look at something carefully before deciding whether it is good enough
"Every line I write is reviewed by somebody who did not write it."
To approve -- to say officially that something is allowed to go ahead
"It was approved on Friday and it was live on Monday."
To flag something -- to mark something so that somebody else has to look at it before it continues
"Two lines were flagged, and neither of them was wrong. They were just unclear."
A bottleneck -- the slowest point in a process, which decides the speed of everything after it
"The bottleneck was never the writing. It was waiting for one person to read it."
A stage -- one named part of a process, which has to finish before the next one starts
"There are four stages, and nothing moves to the next one until the previous one is signed off."
Feedback -- what somebody tells you about your work so that the next version is better
"The feedback was hard to read and every single line of it was useful."
To roll out -- to release something to everybody, in steps rather than all at once
"It was rolled out to five per cent of users first, which is why nobody noticed the bug."
To sign something off -- to give the final approval that lets a thing actually go live
"Nothing is signed off on a Friday afternoon, and everybody knows why."
Behind the scenes -- the part of the work that nobody outside the team ever sees
"A button takes an afternoon. Everything behind the scenes takes three weeks."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Open any admissions page, any job description or any code review and count the sentences with a person in them. There are very few. Applications are reviewed in March. The file is submitted once. Nothing is signed off on a Friday afternoon. In every one of those, the thing that matters has moved to the front of the sentence and the verb has two parts: a form of be plus the past participle. That is the whole structure, and the participle is where it breaks. Are review and is wrote are not slightly wrong; they are not the structure at all.

The same shape works in three tenses, and choosing between them is the same decision you already know. A rule or a repeated stage takes the present: applications are reviewed in March, every year. One finished event takes the past: my project was approved on a Friday. Something that is done and still counts today takes has been: the feature has been rolled out to everybody. And the trap from earlier in this programme is still here, in a new place: has been rolled out last month is impossible, because a closed time never takes has been. Say was rolled out last month.

Finally, the person. You add by plus somebody only when the person is the actual information: every line is read by two people who did not write it. Here the number is the point, so it stays. In the other sentences nobody is hidden and nobody is protected — the reader simply does not need to know who reviews an application in March. The feedback is written about the work; the bottleneck is a place in a process, not a person; and almost everything that happens behind the scenes has no name attached to it at all.

1. According to the text, what are the two parts of a passive verb?
A A modal verb and the base form.
B A form of "be" plus the past participle.
C The base form plus "by".
2. Why is "has been rolled out last month" impossible?
A Because a closed time never takes "has been". It needs "was rolled out last month".
B Because "roll out" has no passive form.
C Because the sentence has no "by".
3. When does the text say you should add "by" plus a person?
A Always, otherwise the sentence is incomplete.
B Never, because the passive exists to hide the person.
C Only when the person is the actual information, as in "read by two people who did not write it".

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive Voice

GRAMMAR

Put the thing that matters at the front, use be plus the past participle, and add the person only when the person is the news.

JobFormExample
A rule or a routine stepam / is / are + past participleApplications are reviewed in March.
One finished eventwas / were + past participleMy project was approved on a Friday.
Done, and it still counts todayhas / have been + past participleThe feature has been rolled out to everybody.
Naming who did it+ by + person, only when it is the informationEvery line is read by two people.
Negativeis not / was not / has not been + participleNothing is signed off on a Friday afternoon.
