Podcast
BBC 6 Minute English — "Rude emails"

Six minutes on why written feedback at work lands so much harder than the same words said out loud. It is the other half of this lesson: the structure that removes the person also removes the tone, and both things have to be handled on purpose.

Tip: listen once, then find three passive sentences in the transcript. For each one, ask yourself whether naming a person would have made it better or worse.

Talk
TED — Adam Galinsky, "How to speak up for yourself" (15 min)

A social psychologist on the exact problem the reviewer describes in today’s second recording: the new person who says nothing. He explains why silence feels safe and what it actually costs, and what to say instead.

Tip: watch it, then think of one piece of feedback you accepted without arguing. Say out loud the question you should have asked.

Video
TED on YouTube — Simon Sinek, "How great leaders inspire action" (18 min)

Eighteen minutes on how teams decide what to build before anything is designed, submitted or signed off. It is the stage that comes before every stage in today’s lesson, and the one nobody puts on the board.

Tip: he repeats one structure over and over on purpose. Count the repetitions, and notice how much of the talk is built out of very short sentences.
