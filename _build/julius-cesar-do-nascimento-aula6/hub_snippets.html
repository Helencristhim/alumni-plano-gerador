The Crack in the Casting -- It Must Have Been, It Can’t Have Been

A quality investigation at a foundry. Key words: a crack, a defect, a batch, porosity, moisture, a sample, to inspect, evidence, to rule out, the root cause, a shift, to trace. Structure: modals of deduction in the past.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A crack -- a thin line on a surface where something has started to break
"The customer found a crack in one of the housings."
A defect -- a problem in a product that makes it not good enough to use
"One defect can stop a whole production line."
A batch -- a group of products made at the same time
"They sent back the whole batch: four thousand parts."
Porosity -- small holes of gas inside a piece of metal
"Every cracked part showed porosity next to the crack."
Moisture -- a small amount of water in the air or in a material
"Moisture in the sand can create gas in the metal."
A sample -- a small number of parts taken to test the whole group
"We cut twenty samples and looked inside the metal."
To inspect -- to look at something very carefully to check that it is good
"We inspected every part of the next ten batches."
Evidence -- facts or information that show that something is true
"We need evidence, not opinions."
To rule out -- to decide that something is not possible or not the reason
"We ruled out the machine after checking the records."
The root cause -- the deepest reason why a problem happened
"Wet sand was the root cause of the cracks."
A shift -- a period of work in a factory, for example from 10 p.m. to 6 a.m.
"The night shift starts at ten o’clock."
To trace -- to find where something came from by following its history
"We traced the defect back to one day in March."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last month, a customer found a crack in a brake housing and sent back the whole batch. Julius asked the quality team for evidence. A manager said, “The night shift must have made a mistake.” But the records showed that the parts were cast during the day, so the night shift can’t have made them.

The team inspected twenty samples and found porosity next to every crack. Someone said the supplier might have changed the iron, but the supplier’s certificates were the same as always, and the team ruled it out.

Finally, they traced the defect back to the sand. It had rained for five days, so moisture must have got into it. That was the root cause.

1. Why does the text say “the night shift can’t have made them”?
A Because the night shift operators were very experienced and had worked in the plant for many years without problems.
B Because the night shift is sleeping now.
C Because the records showed that the parts were made during the day.
2. Why did the team rule out the supplier?
A Because the supplier’s certificates were the same as always.
B Because the supplier was a friend of the plant manager and had always delivered excellent iron to the foundry.
C Because the supplier closed.
3. What was the root cause?
A A mistake by the new operators on the night shift, who were not trained to use the machine correctly.
B Moisture in the sand, after five days of rain.
C The crack.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Modals of Deduction in the Past

GRAMMAR

Use must have + past participle when the evidence makes you almost sure. Use might have, may have or could have when it is only possible. Use can’t have or couldn’t have when it is impossible.

CertaintyFormExample
Almost certain (yes)must have + past participleThe rain must have got into the sand.
Possiblemight have / may have / could have + past participleThe operators might have made a mistake.
Almost certain (no)can’t have / couldn’t have + past participleThe night shift can’t have been responsible.
Passivemodal + have been + past participleThe parts could have been damaged in transport.
In progressmodal + have been + verb-ingThe furnace must have been running too cold.
The trapthe negative of must have is can’t have, not mustn’t haveNot “It mustn’t have been the machine.” Say It can’t have been the machine.

Never: It mustn’t have been the machine · The rain must have get... · They might made a mistake · The parts could have damaged...

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

Your team wants to blame the night shift, but the records show the parts were made during the day. You say:
A They mustn't have made these parts, because the records of the plant show that the batch was cast during the day.
B They must have not made these parts.
C They can't have made these parts.
You are not sure if the supplier changed the material. You say:
A The supplier must have changed the material, but we are not sure and we need to check the certificates first.
B The supplier might have changed the material.
C The supplier might changed the material.
All the cracked parts show porosity, and it rained for five days. You say:
A Moisture must have got into the sand.
B Moisture can’t have got into the sand, because it rained for five days and the door was open.
C Moisture must got into the sand.
A colleague says the machine is the problem, but you have no data. You say:
A The machine must have caused it, I am completely sure of that.
B Evidence is not important here, because the machine is very old and it always gives us problems.
C Do we have any evidence?
