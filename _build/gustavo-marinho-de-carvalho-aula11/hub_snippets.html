Twenty Phrasal Verbs That Do the Work -- Talking Instead of Reading

The formal verb is correct. The phrasal verb is what people actually say on a call. Key words: to set up, to sort out, to put off, to bring up, to figure out, to look into, to turn down, to take over. Structure: separable and inseparable phrasal verbs.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To set up -- to arrange or organize something, like a meeting or a system
"Can we set up a call for Thursday morning?"
To sort out -- to solve a problem or put a situation in order
"Give me a day and I will sort it out."
To put off -- to move something to a later time
"They put the launch off until June."
To bring up -- to start talking about a subject in a conversation
"I will bring it up at the end of the meeting."
To figure out -- to understand something after thinking about it
"We still have not figured out why the numbers are different."
To look into -- to try to find out the facts about a problem
"I am looking into it, and I will call you this afternoon."
To turn down -- to say no to an offer or a request
"They turned down the first proposal, but not the second."
To take over -- to start doing a job that somebody else was doing
"Rafael is taking over the account next month."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Listen to any call between two colleagues and count the formal verbs. There are almost none. Nobody says we need to postpone the review; they say we need to put it off. Nobody says I will investigate the matter; they say I'll look into it. The formal verbs are not wrong, and in a contract they are exactly right. On a call, they make you sound as if you were reading.

A phrasal verb is a verb plus a small word — up, off, out, into, over — and the small word changes the meaning. Most of them can open in the middle. With a noun, both positions are fine: set up the call or set the call up. With it, them, him or her, there is only one position, and it is the middle: set it up, put it off, sort it out, turn it down.

A few never open. Look into, go over and run into keep the object after them, always: I am looking into it, never looking it into. And some take no object at all: something came up, Rafael is taking over next month. The trap for a speaker of Portuguese is the end of the sentence: set up it and put off it feel natural, because in Portuguese the pronoun never goes inside the verb. In English, with these verbs, it has to.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Separable and Inseparable Phrasal Verbs

GRAMMAR

With it, a separable verb opens and the it goes in the middle. An inseparable verb never opens.

TypeWith a nounWith it / them / him / her
Separable: set up, sort out, put off, bring up, figure out, turn downset up the call / set the call upset it up
Inseparable: look into, go over, run into, cover forlook into the problemlook into it
Three parts: sign off on, look forward tosign off on the budgetsign off on it

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give on a call.

You want to suggest a time to talk to your client. On the call, you say:
A Can we set up it for Thursday morning?
B Can we set up a call for Thursday morning?
C Can we put off a call for Thursday morning, if that is a good time for everybody on your side?
Your client asks about a mistake you have not checked yet. You say:
A I am looking into it right now.
B I am looking it into, and I will send you the numbers from our side this afternoon.
C I am looking at it into.
The client wants to move the launch to June, and you agree. You say:
A Fine, let's put off it until June.
B Fine, let's turn it down until June, and then we can see what happens with the numbers.
C Fine, let's put it off until June.
At the end of the meeting you want to start talking about the price, which nobody has mentioned. You say:
A Before we finish, can I take over the price, since nobody has said anything about it today?
B Before we finish, can I bring up the price?
C Before we finish, can I bring the price?

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Ninety seconds, no notes. Think of a meeting, a delivery or a trip that did not happen on the day it was planned. Tell the story: what the plan was, what came up, who sorted it out, and when it finally happened. Use at least four of the eight verbs from this lesson, and no formal verbs like postpone or investigate.
