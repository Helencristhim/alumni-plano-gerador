Chapter 3: Where the It Goes

One Small Word, Two Possible Places

Some of these verbs open in the middle. Others never do.

Grammar Discovery

Where Does It Go?

"The call is at nine. Can you set it up?"

"The launch was in May, but they put it off."

"There is a problem with the invoice. I am looking into it."

"They turned down the proposal. / They turned the proposal down."

In the red ones, it sits between the verb and the small word. In the blue one, it comes after the small word, and the verb never opens. In the orange one there is a noun instead of it, and both positions are correct.

TypeWith a nounWith it / them / him / her
Separable: set up, sort out, put off, bring up, figure out, turn down, take overset up the call / set the call upset it up — ALWAYS in the middle
Inseparable: look into, run into, go over, cover forlook into the problemlook into it — ALWAYS after
Three parts: sign off on, get away with, look forward tosign off on the budgetsign off on it — never opens
No object: come up, end up, take overSomething came up. Rafael is taking over next month.
Where to use themPhrasal verb on calls and in meetings; the formal verb (postpone, investigate) in contracts and reports.
The trapNot set up it, not put off it, not look it into.

In one line: with it, a separable verb opens and the it goes in the middle. An inseparable verb never opens.

Dialogue

Something Has Come Up

C
Gustavo, before we start — something has come up on our side, I'm afraid.
G
No problem. What is it?
C
The volumes team found a mistake in the March figures, so we need to put Thursday's review off by a week.
G
That makes sense. Do you want me to look into the March figures from our side as well?
C
That would help. If you can figure out where the difference comes from, we can sort it out in one call instead of three.
G
Great. I'll set up a call with Daniel for Monday, then. Is there anything else I should bring up with him?
C
Just one thing. Daniel is leaving at the end of the month, and Priya's team is taking over the account.
G
Thanks for the heads-up. I'll make sure we talk about the handover on Monday.
Comprehension

About Claire

1. According to Claire, what did the volumes team find?
A mistake in the March figures.
2. What does Claire want to do with Thursday's review, and for how long?
She wants to put it off by a week.
3. What news does Claire give at the end of the call?
Daniel is leaving at the end of the month, and Priya's team is taking over the account.
Listening 1 · Business Radio

Reading From a Document

A short radio piece on why native speakers rarely use the formal verb. Sound first — no text.

1. What do native speakers say instead of “postpone the meeting” and “investigate the matter”?
“Can we put it off?” and “I'll look into it.”
2. Where does she say the formal verb is exactly right, and what does it create in conversation?
In a written contract. In conversation it creates a small distance, as if you were reading from a document.
3. How many phrasal verbs does she say do most of the work, and what is the trick?
About twenty, and you probably know half already. The trick is to use the ones you know, on purpose, until they stop feeling like slang.
Listening 2 · A Voice Note

The Soft No

A voice note from somebody who has worked with British clients for ten years. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say a friendly British sentence with no decision in it often means?
It often means no.
2. How does a British manager usually turn down a proposal, according to him?
They say it is not quite what they are looking for at the moment, and they expect you to figure out the rest.
3. What does he recommend instead of waiting for the decision?
A short, neutral question: “Is that something you would like us to set up, or should we put it to one side for now?”

Subject: Thursday's review — new date
From: Claire Hensley · To: Gustavo Marinho de Carvalho

Hi Gustavo,

Thanks for being so flexible this morning. As discussed, we have put the review off until Thursday the 14th, same time. I have set up the invitation, so you should see it in your calendar.

Daniel is looking into the March figures on our side. If you figure out the difference before Monday, send it over and we will try to sort it out before the review rather than during it.

One more thing I forgot to bring up: Priya would like ten minutes at the end to introduce her team, since they are taking over from Daniel in April.

Best,
Claire

2. Why does Claire want the difference before Monday?

3. Find the phrasal verb that has no object and say what it means here.
“taking over from Daniel”: Priya's team will start doing the work Daniel was doing, from April.
