Chapter 4: The Code

The Number, and How Sure You Are of It

Quantity, proportion and approximation

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"Output is in the region of forty thousand units a week."

"Downtime was just under two per cent of scheduled hours."

"We run at roughly two thirds of capacity."

"A changeover takes forty minutes, give or take."

Every sentence has a number. The red words say how sure you are of it. Three of them sit before the number and one sits after it. The blue one is not a number at all.

JobFormExample
Close to a figureroughly / around / approximately / about + numberOutput is roughly forty thousand units.
Close, in a formal registerin the region of / in the order of + numberCapacity is in the region of fifty-five thousand.
Just below or just abovejust under / just over / a little under / well belowScrap is well below three per cent.
Close — and it goes AFTERnumber + give or takeForty minutes, give or take.
Proportion instead of a figuretwo thirds of / a third of / one in threeWe run at two thirds of capacity.
Uncountable amounta great deal of / plenty of / much (not many)There is a great deal of scrap on that line.
The trapnever more or less at the end of a business figureNot forty thousand, more or less. Say forty thousand, give or take.

In one line: the hedge goes before the number, except give or take, which goes after it.

Bottleneck identified at the cutting stage and correctly named by the operators without prompting. Changeover between batches timed at forty minutes. Line was up and running again eleven minutes after an unplanned stop during the visit. Recommend proceeding to a first order.
1. The report calls two thirds of capacity "healthy". Why would a full plant be worse news for a buyer?
Because a plant running near capacity has nothing left for the week a machine goes down, so it cannot absorb a problem without missing a delivery.
2. What does the report say about the bottleneck, and why is that detail there rather than the number?
That it was at the cutting stage and the operators named it without being asked. It shows somebody is managing the line, not only running it.
3. Two lines on this page use a different kind of hedge. Which are they, and how do they differ?
"approx. 40,000 units" and "in the region of 55,000" both sit before the number, while "just under 2%" and "well below 3%" say which side of the figure the real number falls on.
Listening 1 · The Plant Manager

Give Me the Week, Not the Year

A plant manager reporting a normal week, out loud. Sound first — no text.

1. What was the output for the week, and why does she say it is lower than it should be?
In the region of thirty-eight thousand units. She lost most of Tuesday afternoon to downtime on the second line and a changeover on Thursday ran nearly an hour long.
2. What caused the changeover to run long, and when should it have been caught?
A scrap problem in the batch before it, which they did not catch early enough.
3. What does she say about running at capacity?
That capacity is somewhere around fifty-five thousand a week, but nobody should ever run at fifty-five thousand, because you need the room.
Listening 2 · The Auditor

What I Actually Look At

A supplier auditor on what a checklist does not tell him. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say the checklist does, and what does he watch instead?
He does have a checklist, but it does not tell him anything. He watches what the floor does when nobody has been warned.
2. Why does he care whether the operators know where the slowest point is?
Because if they know, somebody has told them, and that means somebody is managing the line rather than just running it.
3. What is his last question, and which answer does he trust the least?
What went wrong most recently and what happened afterwards. He trusts least the suppliers who tell him nothing has gone wrong.

Chapter 7: Wrap-Up

Five Sentences for the Floor

Survival Card

Five Phrases for the Plant Visit

Output is in the region of forty thousand units a week.
That is roughly two thirds of capacity, and we keep the rest free.
Downtime was just under two per cent of scheduled hours.
A changeover between batches takes forty minutes, give or take.
The line was down for three hours and it is up and running again.
Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I can describe the plant in the words a visitor uses: output, capacity, downtime, scrap, bottleneck.
I put the hedge before the number, and I know that give or take goes after it.
I never end a business figure with more or less.
I can give a proportion instead of a figure: two thirds of capacity, one in three.
I can answer what went wrong most recently with a fact and an ending, instead of saying nothing has gone wrong.
