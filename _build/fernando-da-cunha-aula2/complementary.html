Video
Business Insider -- "The Rise And Fall Of The American Diner"

A short American business documentary built almost entirely on the language of this lesson: output, capacity, headcount, what a place can absorb and what breaks it. Useful because the numbers are hedged the way a native speaker hedges them, in a context that has nothing to do with your factory.

Tip: watch once for the story, then a second time with a pen. Write down every phrase used to make a number approximate.

Podcast
How I Built This -- Dyson: James Dyson

An engineer who spent years on a production problem before he had a company at all. He talks about prototypes, batches and manufacturing decisions in plain English, and he is unusually precise about numbers, which is exactly the habit this lesson is training.

Tip: listen to the first fifteen minutes. Count how many times he gives a figure and then immediately says how sure he is of it.

Talk
TED -- Alan Smith, "Why you should love statistics" (12 min)

A statistician on why people misread the numbers they are given, and on the difference between a figure and what a figure means. It is the argument behind the whole lesson: the number alone does not persuade anybody, and the way you frame it is what makes it land.

Tip: twelve minutes and very clear delivery. Try it with no subtitles first, then repeat one sentence about your own plant using a proportion instead of a figure.
