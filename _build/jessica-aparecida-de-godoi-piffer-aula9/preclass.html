Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

The One I Mean -- Two Commas, Two Meanings

Describing who does what to somebody who knows nobody. Key words: to narrow something down, a counterpart, to liaise with, to report to somebody, cross-functional, a remit, to be based in, to cover for somebody, a point of contact, to loop somebody in. Structure: defining and non-defining relative clauses.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To narrow something down -- to reduce a long list of possibilities to a much shorter one
"There were forty files. Two questions narrowed it down to three."
A counterpart -- the person who has the same job as you in another company or country
"My counterpart in Chicago has the same title and half the paperwork."
To liaise with -- to talk to another team regularly so both sides know what is happening
"She liaises with legal, which is why nothing surprises them any more."
To report to somebody -- to have somebody as your manager
"I report to Chicago, and my team reports to me here."
Cross-functional -- involving people from more than one department
"It is a cross-functional project, which is a polite way of saying nobody owns it."
A remit -- the area of work you are officially responsible for
"Training is not in my remit, and I have done it every year for four years."
To be based in -- to have your main office or home in a particular place
"The colleague who handles the filings is based in São Paulo."
To cover for somebody -- to do somebody's job while they are away
"Ana is covering for him until the fifteenth, so write to her."
A point of contact -- the one person you should speak to about something
"You need one point of contact, not a list of six people."
To loop somebody in -- to include somebody in an email or a conversation
"Loop me in when you reply, and I will keep an eye on it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Two sentences that differ by two commas. The colleague who handles the filings is based in São Paulo. Marcelo, who handles the filings, is based in São Paulo. In the first, the extra information tells you which colleague — take it away and the sentence no longer identifies anybody. In the second it only adds a fact about a person you have already named; take it away and nothing is lost but a detail. The commas are not decoration. They are the difference.

The test takes a second: cover the extra part with your hand. If the sentence still tells you who or what, the part needs commas. If it does not, it must not have them. Everything else follows. Use who for people, which for things, where for places and whose for owners: the office where she works, the client whose file we reopened.

That has one restriction worth memorising: it can only appear in the version without commas. The report that we sent on Friday is correct; the report, that we sent on Friday, is not English. And when the relative pronoun is the object of its own clause, you can simply delete it: the report we sent on Friday, the person I spoke to. Native speakers drop it far more often than they keep it.

One error deserves its own warning, because it survives translation and is hard to hear in your own speech. In Portuguese the relative pronoun is often echoed later in the clause. English does not allow that echo: the person who I spoke to her yesterday is ungrammatical. Say the person I spoke to yesterday. And the practical payoff for all of this is simple: it is what lets you say who does what in one sentence rather than four, which is most of the distance between English that works and English that sounds professional.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the sentence a careful speaker would use.

You have several colleagues, and you want to identify one of them. You say:
A The colleague who handles the filings is based in São Paulo.
B The colleague, who handles the filings, is based in São Paulo.
C The colleague which handles the filings is based in São Paulo.
There is only one Marcelo, and you want to add a fact about him. You say:
A Marcelo who handles the filings is based in São Paulo.
B Marcelo, who handles the filings, is based in São Paulo.
C Marcelo, that handles the filings, is based in São Paulo.
You are talking about a specific person you spoke to. You say:
A The person who I spoke to her yesterday is covering for him.
B The person I spoke to yesterday is covering for him.
C The person which I spoke yesterday is covering for him.
Somebody new asks who to write to. The most useful answer is:
A Marcelo, Ana, Rachel or me.
B The person who deals with anything from the auditors is Ana, who is covering for Marcelo this month.
C It depends. Ask somebody else.
