Who Writes the Rules for AI -- Phrasal Verbs of Process

How governments are regulating artificial intelligence. Key words: an algorithm, a watchdog, a breakthrough, to draw up, to carry out, to set out, to look into, to phase in, to rule out, to keep up with. Structure: phrasal verbs for processes and decisions.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An algorithm -- a set of instructions that a computer follows to solve a problem
"The algorithm decides who gets a loan."
A watchdog -- an organization that checks if companies or governments follow the rules
"The data protection watchdog opened an investigation."
A breakthrough -- an important new discovery or development
"The new model was a breakthrough in medical research."
To draw up -- to prepare and write an official document, such as a law or a plan
"The commission drew up a new law on artificial intelligence."
To carry out -- to do a task, a study or an investigation
"The agency carried out a study of fifty companies."
To set out -- to explain something clearly, usually in writing
"The law sets out four levels of risk."
To look into -- to investigate or examine a situation
"The watchdog is looking into the complaint."
To phase in -- to introduce something gradually, in stages
"The new rules will be phased in over three years."
To rule out -- to decide that something is not possible or will not happen
"The minister ruled out a total ban."
To keep up with -- to move or change at the same speed as something else
"The law cannot keep up with the technology."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

After a breakthrough in artificial intelligence, the Senate created a commission. The commission drew up a new bill, which sets out four levels of risk for systems that use an algorithm to make decisions.

The rules will not start all at once: they will be phased in over three years. The government has ruled out a total ban. Some critics say the law cannot keep up with the technology.

Meanwhile, the data protection watchdog is looking into two complaints and carrying out a study of fifty companies. Notice that the meaning of each phrasal verb is different from the two words separately.

1. What did the commission do?
A It drew up a new bill.
B It ruled out the new bill.
C It phased in a total ban.
2. What does "the rules will be phased in over three years" mean?
A The rules will start all at once, in three years.
B The rules will be introduced gradually, in stages, over three years.
C The rules will be cancelled after three years.
3. What is the watchdog doing?
A It is looking into two complaints.
B It is keeping up with the Senate.
C It is drawing up a total ban.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Phrasal Verbs for Processes and Decisions

GRAMMAR

A verb + a particle with a new meaning. More natural than formal verbs in conversation. A pronoun object always goes in the middle.

JobFormExample
A phrasal verbverb + particle, with a new meaningdraw up = prepare a document; rule out = exclude
Separable (most with an object)verb + object + particle, or verb + particle + objectThey drew up the law. / They drew the law up.
With a pronounthe pronoun always goes in the middleThe minister ruled it out. (never ruled out it)
Inseparablethe object always goes after the particleThe agency is looking into the case.
Three wordsverb + two particles, never separatedThe law cannot keep up with the technology.
Formal and spokenthe phrasal verb is more natural in conversationinvestigate → look into · exclude → rule out · conduct → carry out

Never: realize a study (say carry out a study) · The minister ruled out it · looking the complaint into · elaborate a law (say draw up a law)

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to say that an agency conducted a study. You say:
A "The agency realized a study."
B "The agency carried out a study."
C "The agency carried a study off."
The minister said a total ban is not possible. You report:
A "The minister ruled out it."
B "The minister ruled it out."
C "The minister ruled it off."
You want to say the rules start gradually. You say:
A "The rules will be phased in over three years."
B "The rules will be phased out over three years."
C "The rules will be looked into over three years."
A journalist asks if the law can follow the speed of technology. The best answer is:
A "I do not think any law can keep up with the technology."
B "I do not think any law can keep with up the technology."
C "I do not think any law can carry out the technology."
