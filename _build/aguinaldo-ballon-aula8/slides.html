Chapter 4: The Code

Verb + Particle

Phrasal verbs for processes and decisions

Grammar Discovery

Can You Guess the Meaning From the Two Words?

"The commission drew up a new law on artificial intelligence."

"The watchdog is looking into the complaint."

"The rules will be phased in over three years."

"A total ban? The minister ruled it out."

Each blue verb has two parts: a verb and a small word. The meaning of the pair is usually different from the two words separately. And look at the last sentence: where is the word in red?

JobFormExample
A phrasal verbverb + particle, with a new meaningdraw up = prepare a document; rule out = exclude
Separable (most with an object)verb + object + particle, or verb + particle + objectThey drew up the law. / They drew the law up.
With a pronounthe pronoun always goes in the middleThe minister ruled it out. (never ruled out it)
Inseparablethe object always goes after the particleThe agency is looking into the case.
Three wordsverb + two particles, never separatedThe law cannot keep up with the technology.
Formal and spokenthe phrasal verb is more natural in conversationinvestigate → look into · exclude → rule out · conduct → carry out

In one line: verb + particle with a new meaning; a pronoun object always goes in the middle.

The timeline is written with phrasal verbs, because it was prepared to be read aloud in client meetings. The same information in formal verbs would sound like a legal text, not like advice.
1. How many levels of risk does the law set out?
Four.
2. Turn the line "Years 3–4" into a full sentence.
"The obligations will be phased in over two years, starting with high-risk systems."
3. What does the firm advise clients to do now?
To carry out an internal review of their automated decisions.
Listening 1 · The Data Lawyer

What Our Clients Ask About AI

A data protection lawyer describes the most common questions from companies. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the question clients ask every week?
If they need to stop using artificial intelligence until the new law is ready.
2. Where does she say companies often use automated decisions?
In credit and in hiring.
3. Why does she say companies should not wait for the new law?
Because the watchdogs are not waiting for it.
Listening 2 · The Tech Executive

Rules That Cannot Keep Up

A technology executive explains why he is worried about new AI laws. Sound first — no text.

1. What is he worried about?
How the regulation is being drawn up.
2. What happens during the three years that lawmakers spend writing a law?
The technology changes completely.
3. What does he suggest instead of long lists of prohibited technologies?
Clear principles, with agencies updating the details every year.
