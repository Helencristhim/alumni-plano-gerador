Lesson 30 -- Phase 3 Showcase

Documentary series
Beckham -- Episode 1

Four episodes on how a boy from east London became one of the most recognisable footballers alive. Almost every person who speaks is ranking him against somebody else, which is exactly the job you did in this lesson.

Tip: watch the first episode with English subtitles and write down three sentences where somebody says how big a gap is -- "by far", "nowhere near as", "head and shoulders above". Then write one of your own about a player you would put first.

Podcast
BBC 6 Minute English -- What is in a footballer's brain?

Six minutes on what actually separates an elite player from a very good one, and the answer turns out to be in the brain rather than in the legs. It is a whole episode about why one person is far better than another, which is the question behind every ranking.

Tip: the full transcript is on the same page. Listen once without it, then read it and find every comparison. Copy two of them and rewrite each one about a player or a game you know.

YouTube
Arnel's Everyday English -- Comparatives and superlatives, advanced lesson with test

The same structures you worked with in class, on a whiteboard: much, far and considerably in front of a comparative, "by far the most ...", the as ... as pattern and its negative, and the double-comparative mistakes. There is a test at the end so you can check yourself.

Tip: watch it after the lesson, not before. Pause at every example and say out loud how big the gap is -- enormous, small, or none at all -- before she explains it.
