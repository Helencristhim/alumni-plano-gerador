Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To design -- To make the plan or drawing of a building before it is built
"Plecnik designed the Triple Bridge in 1929."
Earthquake -- A sudden shaking of the ground that can destroy buildings
"In 1895 a strong earthquake hit Ljubljana."
To rebuild -- To build something again after it was damaged or destroyed
"Much of the old town was rebuilt after the earthquake."
Riverbank -- The land along the side of a river
"The market stands on the riverbank."
Footbridge -- A narrow bridge only for people walking, not for cars
"Two footbridges were added next to the old bridge."
To complete -- To finish building or making something
"The library was completed in 1941."
To be named after -- To have the same name as a famous person, to remember them
"The main square is named after a poet."
Masterpiece -- The best work that an artist or architect ever made
"Many people say the library is his masterpiece."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

When you talk about a famous building, the building is usually more important than the people who built it. That is why English puts the building first: The Triple Bridge was designed by Plecnik. The verb is was or were + the participle: was designed, were added, was built.

If you want to say who did it, add by: The city was hit by an earthquake in 1895. If it is not important, or you do not know, leave it out: Two footbridges were added in the 1930s. For things that happen every day, use is or are: Fresh fruit is sold at the market every morning.

Be careful with irregular verbs. The participle of build is built: it was built, never it was build. And never forget was: The library built in 1941 is not correct. Say The library was completed in 1941.

1. Why does English often put the building first?
A Because the name of the architect is always unknown and nobody remembers it after the building is finished.
B Because the building is usually more important than the people who built it.
C Because it is shorter.
2. When do you use by?
A Always, in every passive sentence, even when you do not know who did it or it is not important.
B Only with the present.
C When you want to say who did it.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A The library was completed in 1941.
B The library built in 1941, after the war started in Europe and many plans changed.
C The library was build in 1941.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive Voice

GRAMMAR

When the building matters more than the builder.

TimeFormExample
present (every day)is / are + participleFruit is sold here every morning.
past (finished)was / were + participleThe bridge was designed in 1929.
who did it+ by + personIt was designed by Plecnik.
questionsWhen was it + participle?When was it built?
irregularbuild → built · hit → hitIt was built in stone.

Never: it was build · the library built in 1941 · the footbridges was added · named for a poet

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the sentence a careful speaker would use.

A friend asks about a church in Split. You want to know its age. You ask:
A When it built, and who was the architect that made the plans for it?
B When did it build?
C When was it built?
You want to say who designed the bridge. You say:
A The bridge was designed by Plecnik.
B Plecnik was designed the bridge in 1929, after he came back from Prague.
C The bridge designed by Plecnik.
You talk about the market today. You say:
A Vegetables was sold here every morning, and the flowers too, since many years ago.
B Vegetables are sold here every morning.
C Vegetables sell here every morning.
You talk about the earthquake. You say:
A The city hit an earthquake in 1895, and after that a lot of the old town was gone.
B The city was hitted by an earthquake.
C The city was hit by an earthquake in 1895.
