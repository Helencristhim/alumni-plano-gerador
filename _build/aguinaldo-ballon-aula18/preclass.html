Lesson 18 -- Pre-class

Cause, Consequence and Contrast -- Explaining a Delay

Why large infrastructure projects run late, and how to explain the chain of events. Key words: a bottleneck, a knock-on effect, to trigger, a side effect, to stem from, a shortage, demand, to offset, a root cause, to outweigh. Structure: due to, as a result, although, despite.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A bottleneck -- a point where a process slows down because there is not enough capacity
"The port became a bottleneck for the whole region."
A knock-on effect -- an indirect effect that one event has on other things
"The delay had a knock-on effect on three other projects."
To trigger -- to make something start, often something bad
"The tariff freeze triggered a wave of lawsuits."
A side effect -- an extra result that you did not want or expect
"Lower tariffs had a side effect: less investment."
To stem from -- to be the result of something
"Most delays stem from problems with environmental licenses."
A shortage -- a situation in which there is not enough of something
"A shortage of engineers slowed the construction."
Demand -- the amount of a product or service that people want to buy or use
"Demand for cargo transport grew by ten percent."
To offset -- to reduce the effect of something with an opposite effect
"Higher tariffs offset the lower traffic."
A root cause -- the original, main reason for a problem
"The root cause of the delay was a bad project design."
To outweigh -- to be more important or bigger than something else
"The benefits of the project outweigh the costs."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A highway project is seven months late. The lenders ask Aguinaldo for an explanation. He starts with the root cause: the design was incomplete. Then the trigger: the project was delayed due to a late license.

The works started in the rainy season. As a result, costs went up, and the delay had a knock-on effect on two other lots. There was also a shortage of asphalt in the region.

But there is good news. Although the project is late, traffic on the finished lots is above the forecast. Despite the delay, the revenue will partly offset the extra costs. Notice: due to and despite + a noun; although + a full sentence.

1. What was the root cause of the delay?
A The rainy season, because the works started at the wrong time of the year.
B The incomplete design.
C A shortage of asphalt in the region, which made the costs go up a lot.
2. Why does the text say "due to a late license" and not "due to the license was late"?
A Because "due to" is followed by a noun.
B Because "due to" is only for the past, and "the license was late" is in the present.
C Because "due to" means "although" in English, and needs a contrast after it.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "Despite of the delay, the revenue will partly offset the extra costs of the project."
B "Despite the delay, revenue will offset part of the costs."
C "Despite the project is late, the revenue will partly offset the extra costs of the project."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Due To, As a Result, Although, Despite

GRAMMAR

Cause, consequence and contrast. Due to and despite take a noun; because and although take a full sentence; as a result starts a new sentence.

JobFormExample
Cause + noundue to / because of + noundelayed due to a shortage of engineers
Cause + sentencebecause / since / as + subject + verbbecause the license was late
Consequence, new sentenceAs a result, / Therefore, / Consequently,As a result, investors lost confidence.
Contrast + sentencealthough / even though + subject + verbAlthough traffic fell, revenue increased.
Contrast + noun or -ingdespite / in spite of + noun or -ingDespite the crisis · despite losing traffic
The trapdespite never takes a full sentence or of; although never takes butNot Despite traffic fell or Although it fell, but.

Never: Despite of the crisis · Despite traffic fell · due to the license was late · Although it was late, but...

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You give the cause of a delay with a noun. You say:
A "The project was delayed due to the license was late and the agency had no staff."
B "It was delayed due to a late license."
C "The project was delayed although a late license from the state environmental agency."
You give a consequence in a new sentence. You say:
A "The tariff was frozen. As a result, investors lost confidence."
B "The tariff was frozen. Despite, the investors lost their confidence in the whole sector."
C "The tariff was frozen. Although, the investors lost their confidence in the whole sector."
You show a contrast with a full sentence. You say:
A "Despite traffic fell in the last year, revenue increased because of the new tariff."
B "Although traffic fell, revenue increased."
C "Although traffic fell in the last year, but revenue increased because of the new tariff."
You show a contrast with a noun. You say:
A "Despite of the crisis in the region, the port doubled its exports in only five years."
B "Despite the crisis, exports doubled."
C "Although the crisis in the region, the port doubled its exports in only five years."
