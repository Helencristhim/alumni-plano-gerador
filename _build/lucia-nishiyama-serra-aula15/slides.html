Chapter 1: Warm-Up

Same Meaning, Different Room

"I will raise the cost issue and generate an alternative before the board approves the plan."

"I will bring up the cost issue and come up with an alternative before the board signs off on the plan."

Both are correct English. Only one of them sounds like a person in the meeting. Tonight you learn the second one.

The Big Idea

The Written Verb and the Spoken Verb

What the document writesWhat the room says
raise a topicbring up a topic
generate a solutioncome up with a solution
approve the budgetsign off on the budget
encounter a problemrun into a problem
explain the process step by stepwalk somebody through the process

Neither column is wrong. The left one is what you write; the right one is what you say. Use only the left one on a call, and you sound like a translated report.

Chapter 3: How the Pieces Move

Where Does the Object Go?

Four kinds of phrasal verb, one simple test

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I will bring up the cost issue." — "I will bring it up first."

"We ran into a licensing issue." — "We ran into it in March."

"The team came up with a workaround." — "They came up with it in two days."

"The contract fell through." — "Ray weighed in the next morning."

In one of these the two words can be split. In two of them they can never be split. And one of them takes no object at all. Which is which?

TypeWhere the object goesExample
Separable
bring up, walk through		After the particle or in the middle. A pronoun (it, them, me, you) goes only in the middle."bring up the issue" / "bring the issue up" / "bring it up"
"walk me through the plan"
Inseparable
run into		Always after both words, pronoun included. Nothing goes in the middle."run into a problem" / "run into it"
Three-part
come up with, sign off on, hold off on, weigh in on		The three words stay glued. The object follows all of them."come up with a solution" / "come up with it"
"sign off on the budget"
No object
fall through, weigh in, hold off		Nothing follows the verb at all."The deal fell through." / "Ray weighed in."
The testReplace the object with it and say the sentence out loud. If "bring it up" sounds right, the verb is separable. If only "run into it" works, it is not.

Three words or two, the meaning lives in the whole group. Change the particle and you change the verb: hold off is to wait, hold on is to keep something.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I will bring up it in the first answer."
"I will bring it up in the first answer."
"Can you walk through me the timeline?"
"Can you walk me through the timeline?"
"The vendor fell through the contract in March."
"The vendor contract fell through in March."

A pronoun never sits after the particle of a separable verb, an inseparable verb never opens up, and a verb with no object never takes one. Say the sentence with it and your ear will tell you which one you have.

Mark, a senior recruiter, on a fifteen-minute prep call

Dialogue

The Prep Call

M

L
Perfect. I have the loop in my calendar, but not the people.

Three of them. Dana runs the platform team, and she will bring up cost in the first five minutes. Ray weighs in on anything that touches safety. The VP signs off on the offer at the end.

Then hold off. Tell them you would rather send the real figure than come up with one in the room. That answer lands well here.

Understood. I will bring it up in the first answer, and I will hold off on the cost number until I can send it in writing.

Listening 2

Why Your English Sounds Written

A communication coach on the gap between reading and speaking. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Why does she say strong readers often sound formal when they speak?
Because they learned English from documents, and documents use the long single verb, while people speak with two-word verbs.
2. What does she say about the long formal verb?
It is not wrong. It is written English, and it stays useful in a report or a board document.
3. What are the two rules she gives at the end?
Learn a small number of phrasal verbs really well instead of a long list, and put the pronoun in the middle of a separable verb: bring it up, never bring up it.
