Today’s Goal

Compare, Then Recommend

1. The Words

a margin, a segment, volume, a benchmark, coverage, a gap, a ranking...

2. One Structure

bigger than, more profitable than, the highest, the most efficient

3. Your Answer

two segments compared in three numbers, and one recommendation

A comparison is not a chart. It is the sentence that comes after the chart, and it always ends the same way: so this is the one I would choose.

One Question

Bigger Is Not the Same as Better

Name one part of your business that is big and one that is profitable, and say which one you would put money into.

Then hold the answer for ten seconds and give a second reason, without being asked.

Chapter 3: The Code

Bigger, and the Biggest

Two things, or all of them

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"The distributor segment is bigger than the workshop segment."

"The workshop segment is more profitable than the distributor segment."

"First sales carry the best margin we have."

"That is the most efficient use of the budget."

Two red words took an ending and two took a word in front instead. Count the syllables of each adjective. Then look at the blue words: two sentences compare two things, and two compare everything.

JobFormExample
Two things, short adjectiveadjective + er + thanbigger than · smaller than · faster than
Two things, long adjectivemore + adjective + thanmore profitable than · more efficient than
All of them, short adjectivethe + adjective + estthe highest · the lowest · the biggest
All of them, long adjectivethe most + adjectivethe most efficient · the most competitive
Irregulargood, bad, far change completelygood → better → the best · bad → worse → the worst · far → further
Spelling-y becomes -ier · short words double the last lettereasier · bigger · wider
The trapthe ending and more never come togetherNot more bigger, not more better. Say bigger and better.

In one line: short adjectives take the ending, long adjectives take more, and the word the marks that you are comparing everything.

Listening 1 · A Director

The Comparison in Three Numbers

A marketing director comparing two segments at a board. Sound first — no text.

1. What are her three numbers, in order?
Volume, margin, and coverage.
2. What does she say about the gap between the two segments?
That the workshop segment is far more profitable and that the gap has been getting wider every year for three years.
3. How does she summarise the company’s position, and what does she say she would rather be?
Second in the segment where they are comfortable and fifth in the segment where the money is. She would rather be third in the second one than second in the first one.
Listening 2 · A Podcast

The Number That Decides Nothing

A business podcast on comparing the wrong thing. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say people compare instead of what actually matters?
The thing that is easiest to measure. He uses volume as the example, because everybody has it.
2. What single question does he say you should ask before building a chart?
If this number moved twenty per cent tomorrow, would the decision change? If not, the number is decoration.
3. What is his rule about comparing two things, and how does he describe breaking it?
Never compare two things unless you are prepared to say which one you would choose. A comparison without a recommendation is homework handed to the room.

Every line on this page except the last one is a fact. The last one is a choice, and it is on the same page on purpose. A table without the last line is not an argument.
1. On which two lines is the company doing well, and on which two is it behind?
It is doing well on volume and on coverage in distributors, where it is second. It is behind on workshop coverage, under twenty per cent, and on the workshop ranking, where it is fifth.
2. The margin line says the gap is widening. Is that good news or bad news for this recommendation?
Good news for the recommendation. The more profitable segment is pulling further ahead, so the reason to invest there gets stronger every year.
3. What does the note at the bottom say is different about the last line?
Every other line is a fact and the last one is a choice. It says a table without that line is not an argument.
