Chapter 3: The Code

Who Believes It, Who Checked It

The impersonal passive and the causative

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

“It is expected that the market will recover in the second half.”

“The decline is believed to be temporary.”

“We had the forecast reviewed by our two largest clients.”

“In my first month I had the accounts audited by an outside firm.”

In which sentences do we know who did the work, and who asked for it?

FormWhat it doesExample
It is + expected / said / believed + that...Reports a general view without naming who holds itIt is expected that the market will recover.
Subject + is believed / thought + to be / to have...The same idea, starting with the thing you are talking aboutThe decline is believed to be temporary.
have + object + past participleYou arranged it, and somebody else did the workWe had the forecast reviewed by two clients.
In an interviewUse the first two to report the market. Use the third to show that a number was checked, and by whom. A board trusts the passive that names the check.

As You Would to My Board

M
I came up through the creative side. Explain last year to me as you would to my board.
F
In one sentence: revenue was up eight percent year on year, and the margin was down one point.
M
Down one point. Should my board be worried?
F
Half of that point was a one-off, the office move. The other half is a headwind from the exchange rate, and that one will still be there.
M
And next year? Everybody tells me the market is coming back.
F
It is expected that the market will recover in the second half, but I would not build the plan on that.
M
So what would you build it on?
F
On the volume our two largest clients have confirmed. We had the forecast reviewed by both of them before it went to the board.
M
That I could repeat in a corridor.
F
That is the version I would give your board. I am happy to show the detail behind it if it is useful.

2. Why was the margin down one point?

Listening 2

Who Expects It, Who Believes It

A non-executive director on the phrases he hears every quarter. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he ask when he hears a sentence like that?
Who expects it and who believes it.
2. What do the finance directors he trusts say?
That they had the forecast reviewed by the two largest clients, who confirmed the volume.
3. When does the passive make him trust a candidate?
When it shows that somebody checked, not when it hides where a number came from.
