Grammar Discovery

What Comes After It?

"Although it is still rare, it is no longer strange."

"Despite these results, many economists remain skeptical."

"The results were good. However, the companies were small."

"Productivity stayed the same, whereas burnout fell."

After although comes a full sentence, with a subject and a verb. After despite comes only a noun. However starts a new sentence and has a comma after it. Whereas puts two different groups side by side in the same sentence.

ConnectorWhat comes after itExample
although / even thougha full sentence (subject + verb)Although it was expensive, they kept it.
despite / in spite ofa noun or an -ing formDespite the cost, they kept it. Despite working less, they finished.
howeverstarts a NEW sentence, comma after itThe results were good. However, the companies were small.
whereascompares two groups or things, same sentenceCafes are doing better, whereas small shops are not.
not only ... but alsoadds a second, stronger pointIt not only saves time but also saves money.
The trapNot despite of. Not despite it was expensive. Not although the cost.
The other trapNot The results were good, however nobody changed. Use a full stop before However, or use but.

In one line: although takes a sentence, despite takes a noun, however starts a new sentence, and whereas compares two sides.

Listening 1 · Business Radio

The Length of the Thought

A short radio piece on why some people sound more educated in a second language. Sound first — no text.

1. According to her, what makes some people sound more educated, if it is not vocabulary?
The length of the thought.
2. What happens to a speaker who only has and, but and because?
They have to stop every six or seven words and start again, so even a clever idea arrives in small pieces.
3. What does she say you need, instead of difficult words?
About five small connectors, and the confidence to keep the sentence going until the second half arrives.
Listening 2 · A Voice Note

I Was Mostly Wrong

A voice note from somebody who changed his mind about a street with no cars. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the city do, and what was he sure would happen?
It closed the main street to cars two years ago. He was sure the shops would lose customers.
2. What actually happened to most of the shops?
They did not close. Despite the complaints in the first few months, most of them now have more people walking past than before.
3. Who has lost and who has won, according to him?
Small businesses that depend on deliveries say their costs have gone up, whereas the cafes and restaurants are doing better than ever.
Real Document

Before the Board Meeting

Briefing note — a four-day week pilot
For discussion at Thursday's board meeting · one page

The proposal. A six-month trial for the sales and finance teams, four days a week, same salary. The warehouse would stay on its current schedule.

The case for. Burnout is our biggest reason for people leaving. A trial would not only test whether productivity holds but also make hiring easier.

The case against. Although the office teams could cover Fridays between them, clients expect an answer five days a week. Despite the good results elsewhere, there is little evidence from companies of our size.

Recommendation. Run the trial in sales only. Finance closes the month on fixed dates, whereas sales can move its calls. Review the evidence in March.

Sample briefing note written for this lesson. The company is invented.
1. Which team would not be part of the trial, and why do you think that is?
The warehouse, which stays on its current schedule. Like a factory, it cannot simply close for one day.
2. Find the sentence with although and say the same idea with despite.
“Although the office teams could cover Fridays between them, clients expect an answer five days a week.” With despite: “Despite cover on Fridays, clients expect an answer five days a week.”
3. Why does the note recommend sales and not finance?
Finance closes the month on fixed dates, whereas sales can move its calls.

Two Sentences Into One

2. It was expensive. They kept it. (despite + noun)
"Despite the cost, they kept it."

"Cafes are doing better, whereas small shops are not."
