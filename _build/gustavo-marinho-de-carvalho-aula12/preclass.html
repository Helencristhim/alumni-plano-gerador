Lesson 12 -- Pre-class

Sounding Educated -- Two Sides in One Sentence

Two opposing ideas, one sentence, and no pause in the middle. Key words: a trial, productivity, burnout, to outweigh, a concern, to be skeptical, evidence, sustainable. Structure: contrast connectors (although, despite, however, whereas).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A trial -- a test of a new idea for a limited period of time
"The company ran a six-month trial before deciding anything."
Productivity -- how much work is done in a given amount of time
"Productivity stayed the same, even with one day less."
Burnout -- extreme tiredness caused by working too much for too long
"Two people on the team left last year because of burnout."
To outweigh -- to be bigger or more important than something else
"In this case, the benefits clearly outweigh the costs."
A concern -- a worry about something that might happen
"My main concern is the cost, not the idea."
To be skeptical -- to doubt that something is true or that it will work
"I like the idea, but I am still a little skeptical."
Evidence -- facts that show whether something is true
"There is very little evidence from factories or hospitals."
Sustainable -- able to continue for a long time without causing problems
"Ten-hour days are not a sustainable way to work."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Most of what makes an opinion sound mature is not the vocabulary. It is the second half. The four-day week is good is an opinion; although the evidence is limited, the four-day week looks promising is an argument. The difference is one small word that lets you hold both sides in the same sentence.

Each of those small words wants something different after it. Although and even though want a full sentence, with a subject and a verb: although it was expensive. Despite and in spite of want only a noun or an -ing form: despite the cost, despite working less. However starts a new sentence and takes a comma: The results were good. However, the companies were small. And whereas puts two groups side by side: cafes are doing better, whereas small shops are not.

Two traps are typical for a speaker of Portuguese. The first is despite of, which is a word-for-word translation from Portuguese and does not exist in English. The second is using however as if it were but, in the middle of a sentence with only a comma. If you want one sentence, say but. If you want However, end the first sentence before it.

1. According to the text, what turns an opinion into an argument?
A Using longer and more difficult words than the other person, so that you sound more educated.
B Holding both sides in the same sentence.
C Speaking more slowly.
2. What comes after despite?
A A noun or an -ing form.
B The word of, and then a full sentence with a subject and a verb, as in despite of it was expensive.
C Only a comma.
3. What does the text say about however?
A It means the same as whereas.
B It is used exactly like but, in the middle of one sentence, with a comma before it and nothing else.
C It starts a new sentence and takes a comma.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Contrast Connectors

GRAMMAR

Although takes a sentence, despite takes a noun, however starts a new sentence, and whereas compares two sides.

ConnectorWhat comes after itExample
although / even thougha full sentenceAlthough it was expensive, they kept it.
despite / in spite ofa noun or an -ing formDespite the cost, they kept it.
howevera new sentence, comma after itIt was expensive. However, they kept it.
whereascompares two groups or thingsSales liked it, whereas finance did not.
not only ... but alsoadds a second, stronger pointIt not only saves time but also saves money.
NegativeAlthough it did not save money, it reduced burnout.
QuestionDespite the results, are you still skeptical?

Never: despite of the cost · despite it was expensive · although the cost · It was good, however nobody changed.

Order

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

Your client asks what you think of a new idea. You like it, but you have doubts. You say:
A Although I like the idea, I am still a little skeptical.
B Despite I like the idea, I am still a little skeptical about it, to be honest with you.
C Although the idea, I am skeptical.
The project was expensive, but the company kept it. You say:
A Despite of the cost, they kept it.
B Despite the cost, they kept it.
C Whereas the cost was very high in the first year, they kept the project going anyway.

A Sales liked it, despite finance did not like it very much at the beginning of the trial.
B Sales liked it. Whereas finance.

You agree with your colleague's point, but you want to add a limit. You say:
A That is true. However, it does not work in every job.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Ninety seconds, no notes. Think of a change in your city, your country or your company that people are still arguing about. Say what changed, who is for it, who is against it, and what you think. Hold both sides in the same sentence at least three times, with although, despite, however or whereas.
