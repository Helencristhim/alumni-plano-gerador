Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

Eat Like a Local -- Advice for a Tourist at the Bar

How to tell a tourist where to eat, what to try and what to skip, like a local. Key words: a tourist trap, a dish, homemade, spicy, a street stall, to grab a bite, authentic, to skip, to be worth it, a portion. Structure: advice and suggestions with you should, if I were you I’d, why don’t you, you could, and the strong warning you’d better not.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A tourist trap -- a place that is expensive and made only for tourists
"That restaurant on the square is a tourist trap."
A dish -- food prepared in a particular way, as part of a meal
"The dish of the day is always the best choice."
Homemade -- made at home or in the kitchen, not in a factory
"Everything on the menu is homemade, even the bread."
Spicy -- with a strong, hot taste from pepper or chili
"If you like spicy food, ask for the local version."
A street stall -- a small table or stand in the street where food is sold
"We had lunch at a street stall near the port."
To grab a bite -- to eat something quickly and simply
"Let’s grab a bite before the boat leaves."
Authentic -- real and traditional, not changed for tourists
"This is authentic Galician food, not a tourist version."
To skip -- to decide not to do, eat or visit something
"If I were you, I would skip the dessert here."
To be worth it -- to be good enough to justify the money or the effort
"It was expensive, but it was worth it."
A portion -- the amount of food that one person gets
"The portions are huge, so one dish is enough for two."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Tom is a sailor from Ireland, and he has just arrived in a small port. At the bar, he asks a local fisherman where to eat. “You should go to the place behind the market,” the fisherman says. “Everything there is homemade.” He uses should for normal, friendly advice.

“And the restaurant on the beach?” Tom asks. “If I were you, I’d skip it. It is a tourist trap.” With If I were you, the fisherman puts himself in Tom’s place. Notice were, not was. Then he adds a suggestion: “Why don’t you try the octopus? Or you could grab a bite at the street stall by the boats.”

At the end, he gives a warning: “You’d better book a table. On Saturday it is always full.” You’d better is stronger than should: if Tom does not book, there will be a problem. After should, could, I’d and you’d better, the verb comes without to.

Why does the fisherman say "If I were you, I’d skip it"?
A To put himself in Tom’s place and give advice.
B Because he went to that restaurant on the beach many years ago, when he was young.
C To give Tom an order that he must follow.
Which advice is the strongest, with a problem if Tom does not follow it?
A "You could grab a bite at the street stall by the boats, if you are hungry later."
B "Why don’t you try the octopus?"
C "You’d better book a table."
Which sentence is correct?
A "You should to go to the place behind the market, because it is cheap and good."
B "You should go to the place behind the market."
C "You should going to the market."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Giving Advice Like a Local

GRAMMAR

Five ways to give advice, from soft to strong. The verb after them has no to.

JobFormExample
An idea, very softYou could + verbYou could try the street stall.
A friendly suggestionWhy don’t you + verb?Why don’t you try the fish?
Normal adviceYou should / shouldn’t + verbYou should order the dish of the day.
In your placeIf I were you, I’d + verbIf I were you, I’d skip it.
A warningYou’d better (not) + verbYou’d better book a table.
Asking for adviceWhat would you + verb + if you were me?What would you order if you were me?

Never: You should to try · If I was you · Why you don’t go? · You better don’t go · You’d better to book

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

A tourist asks a local for advice at a bar. Put the conversation in a natural order.

?"Excuse me, I’m here for two days. Where should I eat?"
?"Well, if I were you, I’d skip the restaurants on the square."
?"Really? Why? They look nice."
?"They’re a tourist trap. Why don’t you try the place behind the market?"
?"Great. What would you order if you were me?"
?"The fish of the day. And you’d better book a table."

Quiz
