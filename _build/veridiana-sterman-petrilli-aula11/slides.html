Lesson 11

A Tuesday in the Practice

Your normal week, and what is different this week

Warm-up

From the Trip to Tuesday

First, one hiccup from your ten-day trip, and how you bounced back. Then forget the trip for a moment: what does a normal Tuesday look like for you, from the morning to the night?

The Target

Two Layers of a Week

1. The routine. What happens every Tuesday, every week, every year.

2. The exception. What is different right now, this week, this month.

3. The reason. One short sentence that explains the exception.

Busy is one word. A routine plus an exception is a conversation.

Diagnostic

A Question in the Chair

A new patient from the United States sits down in your chair at twelve thirty and asks: “Is it always this busy here on a Tuesday?”

Answer him in two sentences: one about a normal Tuesday, one about this week.

Chapter 3: The Code

Always, or Right Now?

Two present tenses, and how to choose between them

Grammar Discovery

Every Week, or This Week?

I usually see patients from eight to twelve.

This week I am working through lunch.

The girls finish school at four.

Right now they are doing their homework.

Which verbs are true every week, and which are true only now, or this week? Look at the small words around them too.

JobFormExample
Routine and habitspresent simpleI usually see patients from eight to twelve.
Fixed timetablespresent simpleThe girls finish school at four.
At this momentam / is / are + -ingRight now they are doing their homework.
Only for now: this week, these daysam / is / are + -ingThis week I am working through lunch.
State verbsneed, want, know, like, mean stay simpleI need a day off.
The trapnever -ing for a routine, never -ing for need or knowNot every Tuesday I am going. Say every Tuesday I go.

In one line: always true takes the simple form, true for now takes am, is or are plus -ing.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Twelve Thirty, a New Patient

One conversation, two recordings, one schedule

Dialogue

In the Chair

V
Mr. Becker? Please come in. I'm sorry, I'm running late today.
T
Don't worry about it. Thank you for fitting me in. Is it always this hectic on a Tuesday?
V
Tuesdays are always busy. I usually see patients from eight to twelve, but this week I'm working through lunch, because my assistant is on vacation and I'm taking over her calls.
T
I know how that feels. We moved here from Chicago in July, and my wife is starting a new job this month, so I'm doing the school run every day.
V
Then you know the traffic at four! My daughters go to the same school as your son. These days my mother is staying with us, so she drops them off in the morning and I pick them up.
T
That's a good system. My son talks about your girls all the time. So, what are we doing today?
V
Just a check-up. It takes about twenty minutes. Is anything bothering you at the moment?
T
No, everything feels fine. I always start with a check-up in a new city. And Thursday is my day off, so if you need to see me again, that day works.
Comprehension

About Tom

1. Where did Tom move from, and when?
He moved from Chicago in July.
2. Why is Tom doing the school run every day this month?
Because his wife is starting a new job this month.
3. Why is Tom having a check-up if nothing is bothering him, and when is he free?
He always starts with a check-up in a new city. He is free on Thursday, because it is his day off.
Listening 1 · A Dentist in Miami

A Normal Tuesday, and This Month

Another dentist, another city, the same two layers. Sound first — no text.

1. What time does she get to the practice, and what time do they open?
She gets there by seven thirty, and they open at eight.
2. What does she normally do at lunch, and what does her husband think of it?
She stops at twelve thirty and eats at her desk. Her husband says it is a terrible habit.
3. What are two things that are different this month, and why?
They are redoing the waiting room, so they are seeing patients in two small rooms upstairs. One hygienist is on maternity leave, so she is taking over some cleanings. And she is not eating lunch at all.
Listening 2 · A School Coordinator

A Week in English

What the children do every week, and what is new this term. Sound first — no text.

1. What do the younger children do in English on Tuesdays, and on Fridays?
They do science in English on Tuesdays, and on Fridays each child presents something to the class.
2. How are the classes of the older students different?
They have fewer hours, but their classes are harder, and they write a short text every week.
3. What is the school trying this term, and what does he say about the parents' English?
They are sending a short reading home every Thursday, with three questions, for parents to read with their children. You do not need perfect English, and if your daughter is laughing at your pronunciation, the program is working.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I describe my normal week with the simple form: I see, I go, they finish.
I describe what is different now with am, is or are plus -ing.
I keep need, want and know in the simple form. Never I am needing.
I answer how is your week with a routine, an exception and a reason, not with one word.
I know the words: the school run, to drop somebody off, hectic, to run late, to fit somebody in, a check-up, a day off, to catch up on, these days, to take over.
