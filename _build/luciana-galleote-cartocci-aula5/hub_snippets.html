The Silicon Valley Call -- Proposing an Executive Meeting

From subcontractor to partner: asking for the meeting. Key words: an executive meeting, to scale, the scope, a joint offer, a subcontractor, a pipeline, a win-win, to explore, mutual, to follow up. Structure: making polite proposals.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An executive meeting -- a meeting with the directors and senior leaders of a company
"I would like to propose an executive meeting with your leadership team."
To scale -- to make a business or a service bigger, for many more clients
"We want to scale this model to your other clients."
The scope -- the limits of a project: what is included and what is not
"The scope of the first project was one client in São Paulo."
A joint offer -- a proposal that two companies make together
"We could present a joint offer to the hospital chain."
A subcontractor -- a company that another company hires to do part of a job
"Today we are your subcontractor. We would like to be your partner."
A pipeline -- the list of possible clients and deals a company is working on
"You have five more clients in your pipeline in Brazil."
A win-win -- a situation that is good for both sides
"This is a win-win: you grow in Brazil, and we grow with you."
To explore -- to discuss or study an idea to see if it can work
"Would you be open to exploring this idea?"
Mutual -- felt or done by both sides
"There is a mutual interest in Brazil."
To follow up -- to contact someone again to continue a conversation
"I will follow up with an email tomorrow."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A Brazilian company works as a subcontractor for an American group. After a successful project, the CEO calls the American partner. “I was wondering if we could do more together,” she says.

She explains the idea: the American group has more clients in its pipeline. “Would you be open to exploring a joint offer?” The partner likes the idea, but his VP decides.

So she suggests a small next step: “How about meeting in São Paulo when your VP is here?” At the end of the call she says: “I will follow up with an email today.”

1. What does the CEO want?
A To stop working with the American group.
B To do more with the American group: a joint offer for its other clients.
C To become the VP of the American group.
2. Why does she say “would you be open to exploring” and not “you must accept”?
A Because a polite proposal is easier to say yes to.
B Because she does not want the meeting.
C Because the partner is her boss.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “How about to meet in São Paulo?”
B “How about meeting in São Paulo?”
C “How about we meeting in São Paulo?”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Making Polite Proposals

GRAMMAR

Say the idea directly, in a polite form. Some structures take the simple verb; others take -ing.

JobFormExample
Propose softlyI was wondering if we could + verbI was wondering if we could meet next month.
Ask if they accept an ideaWould you be open to + verb-ing?Would you be open to exploring this?
Suggest a concrete optionHow about + verb-ing?How about meeting on the 14th?
Say what you want, politelyI would like to + verbI would like to propose a meeting.
Show the benefitIt would be great to + verbIt would be great to present our results.
The trapto here is a prepositionNot open to explore. Say open to exploring.

Never: Would you be open to explore · How about to meet · I was wondering if we can setting up · I would like propose

Quiz

You want to ask if the partner accepts an idea. You say:
A "Would you be open to exploring a joint offer?"

You want to suggest a concrete date. You say:
A "How about meeting on the tenth?"
