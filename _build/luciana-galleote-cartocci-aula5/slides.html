Chapter 3: The Code

Direct Idea, Polite Form

Three ways to propose without pushing

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Show?

"I was wondering if we could set up an executive meeting."

"Would you be open to exploring a joint offer?"

"How about meeting in São Paulo next month?"

"It would be great to present our results to your VP."

All four sentences propose something, and none of them sounds like an order. Look at the red words, and then at the verb in blue after them: sometimes it is simple, and sometimes it takes -ing.

JobFormExample
Propose softlyI was wondering if we could + verbI was wondering if we could meet next month.
Ask if they accept an ideaWould you be open to + verb-ing?Would you be open to exploring this?
Suggest a concrete optionHow about + verb-ing?How about meeting on the 14th?
Say what you want, politelyI would like to + verbI would like to propose a meeting.
Show the benefitIt would be great to + verbIt would be great to present our results.
The trapto here is a prepositionNot open to explore. Say open to exploring.

In one line: could / would like to / would be great to + verb; would you be open to / how about + verb-ing.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Call, at Real Speed

One call, three recordings, one invitation

Dialogue

Asking for the Meeting

R
Luciana, hi! Great news: our client loved the project summary you sent. The board approved the second phase yesterday.
L
That is wonderful, Ryan. Thank you. Actually, I wanted to talk about that. Today we are your subcontractor for one client. I was wondering if we could do more together.
R
Interesting. What do you have in mind?
L
You have more clients in your pipeline in Brazil. We could scale this model and present a joint offer. Would you be open to exploring that?
R
I like it, but that is a decision for our VP of Partnerships, Karen. She is visiting Brazil in November.
L
Perfect timing. How about setting up an executive meeting in São Paulo while she is here? It could be a win-win.
R
Let me check her calendar. She has two free mornings, the tenth and the twelfth.
L
The tenth works for us. I will follow up with an email today, with a short agenda.
Comprehension

About Ryan

1. What happened with Ryan’s client yesterday?
The board approved the second phase of the project.
2. Who decides about a bigger partnership, and when is she visiting Brazil?
Karen, the VP of Partnerships. She is visiting Brazil in November.
3. Which mornings is Karen free?
The tenth and the twelfth.
Listening 1 · The VP of Partnerships

Why I Say Yes to a Meeting

A VP of partnerships at a technology company in San Jose explains which meeting requests he accepts. Sound first — no text.

1. How many meeting requests does he receive every week?
About thirty.
2. What two things does a good request show?
A clear benefit for his company and a small, concrete next step.
3. What is the problem with the word "synergies"?
It says nothing: it is a word without a real idea behind it.
Listening 2 · The Partner Manager

From Subcontractor to Partner

A partner manager from an engineering company in Milan tells how her company changed its relationship with an American group. Sound first — no text.

1. What was her company for the American group five years ago?
A subcontractor for one project.
2. What did she propose, and where?
A joint offer for three hospitals, in a meeting in Milan.
3. What is the situation today?
They work together in four countries.
Listening 3 · The Sales Director

The Follow-up That Won the Deal

A sales director at a conference in Singapore explains why the email after the call is so important. Sound first — no text.

1. When does he send the follow-up email?
The same day, never the next week.
2. How many lines does his follow-up have?
Three lines.
3. What does each line say?
Thank you for the call, the idea in one sentence, and the next step with a date.
Real Document

The Email After the Call

MEETING INVITATION
Email · From: Luciana Galleote Cartocci · To: Karen, VP of Partnerships
SubjectExecutive meeting in São Paulo — November 10
Line 1Thank you for the great results with our first client.
Line 2I was wondering if we could meet while you are in Brazil.
Line 3We would like to explore a joint offer for your pipeline in Brazil.
Proposed dateNovember 10, 10:00, at our office
Duration45 minutes
AttendeesKaren, Ryan, Luciana and our technical director
A short invitation works best: the benefit in one line, a small next step, a date. The two lines in color are the proposal. They are polite, but the idea is completely clear.
1. What is the proposal in this email, in one sentence?
An executive meeting in São Paulo on November 10 to explore a joint offer.
2. Why are there only three lines before the details?
A short email is easy to read on a phone and easy to forward to the boss.
3. Rewrite line 2 with How about.
How about meeting on November 10 while you are in Brazil?
