Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

Beyond Chicken or Beef -- The Rest of the Plate

Seasonings, sides, dressings and the polite question that gets you what you actually want. Key words: seasoning, an herb, a side, a dressing, savory, tangy, mild, hearty, to go with, medium rare, a substitution, on the side. Structure: indirect questions -- Could you tell me what is in it?

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Seasoning -- the salt, pepper, herbs and spices you put on food to give it flavor
"The chicken is rubbed with a garlic and rosemary seasoning."
An herb -- a green leaf used in small amounts to give food flavor, like basil or cilantro
"It comes with an herb crust. In American English you say ERB, with no H."
A side -- a smaller dish served next to the main plate, like potatoes or vegetables
"The steak comes with two sides. Green beans or the potatoes."
A dressing -- a cold sauce you pour over a salad
"The house dressing is a mustard vinaigrette."
Savory -- salty or spicy in taste, the opposite of sweet
"I want something savory. I am not in the mood for dessert."
Tangy -- with a sharp, slightly sour taste, like lemon or vinegar
"That lemon dressing is pretty tangy."
Mild -- not strong and not spicy at all
"The beans are mild. There is no heat in them at all."
Hearty -- filling and heavy in a good way, the kind of food that keeps you full
"The white bean soup is our hearty option."
To go with -- to taste good together with something else
"That sauce goes with the steak, not with the fish."
Medium rare -- cooked so that the middle of the meat is still pink
"I will have the steak medium rare, please."
A substitution -- a change you ask for: one item served in place of another
"Can I make a substitution and get the salad instead of the fries?"
On the side -- served in a separate little dish, not poured on top
"Could I have the dressing on the side, please?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Gisele sits down at a restaurant on Connecticut Avenue and reads the menu twice. The chicken has a rosemary seasoning, the salmon has an herb crust, and the salad comes with something called a house dressing. She knows the animals. She does not know anything else on the plate.

So she asks. Could you tell me what is in the dressing? Not what is in the dressing shouted across the table, and not silence either: the opener makes it soft, and after that opener the words go back to normal order. Do you know if this comes with a side? The does is gone, and if takes its place, because the answer is only yes or no. May I ask how the salmon is cooked?

The server answers all three, and then he offers something she did not ask for: the dressing is tangy, so he suggests she takes it on the side. She orders her steak medium rare, asks for a substitution on one of the sides, and gets exactly the plate she wanted. I was wondering whether I could have the beans instead took her four seconds to say, and it changed the whole dinner.

1. Why does she say "Could you tell me what is in the dressing?" instead of "What is in the dressing?"
A Because the direct question is grammatically wrong in a restaurant.
B Because the opener makes the question softer and warns the other person that a question is coming.
C Because you can only use the word dressing in a long sentence.
2. "Do you know if this comes with a side?" Why is there an if, and where did the does go?
A The answer is only yes or no, so the clause needs if or whether, and the do and does disappear after an opener.
B If is another way of writing does, so you choose one of the two.
C If is there because the sentence is about food.
3. What is the word order after the opener in "May I ask how the salmon is cooked?"
A Question order: the verb comes before the subject, as in "is the salmon cooked".
B There is no rule, both orders are correct.
C Statement order: the subject comes first, then the verb, as in "the salmon is cooked".

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Opener Changes the Order

GRAMMAR

One opener, one small change of order, and a question nobody minds answering.

Direct questionOpenerIndirect question
What is in it?Could you tell meCould you tell me what is in it?
Where is the restroom?Do you knowDo you know where the restroom is?
Does it come with a side?Do you knowDo you know if it comes with a side?
Can I have it on the side?I was wonderingI was wondering whether I could have it on the side.
How is it cooked?May I askMay I ask how it is cooked?
The three rulesAfter the openerSubject first, then verb · no do, does or did · if or whether for yes-or-no

Never: Could you tell me what does this come with? · Do you know if is the soup spicy? · I was wondering can I have it on the side · May I ask what is an herb crust?
