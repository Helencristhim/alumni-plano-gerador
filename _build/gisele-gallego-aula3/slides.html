Chapter 3: The Code

The Question That Opens the Door

Indirect questions — softer, and with the words in a different order

Grammar Discovery

Where Did the Word Order Go?

Could you tell me what is in the dressing?

Do you know if this comes with a side?

I was wondering whether I could have it on the side.

May I ask how the fish is cooked?

The direct versions are: What is in the dressing? Does this come with a side? Can I have it on the side? How is the fish cooked? Compare the blue part in each pair. Something moved, and something disappeared.

DirectOpenerIndirect
What is in it?Could you tell me... what it is in it? No: ... what is in it.
Where is the restroom?Do you know... where the restroom is?
Does it come with a side?Do you know... if it comes with a side? (no does)
Can I have it on the side?I was wondering... whether I could have it on the side.
How is it cooked?May I ask... how it is cooked?
Why botherSofter, and it buys you timeIt also warns the other person that a question is coming.

In one line: after the opener, the sentence goes back to statement order — subject, then verb, and no do, does or did. A yes-or-no question takes if or whether.

Ordering at Maple and Vine

D
Hi there, welcome in. Can I start you off with something to drink while you look at the menu?
G
Just water for now, thank you. Could you tell me what the specials are tonight?
D
Sure. We have a roast chicken with a lemon and herb seasoning, and a strip steak. The steak comes with two sides — green beans or the potatoes.
G
Do you know if the green beans are spicy? I was wondering because my daughters do not eat anything hot.
D
They are completely mild. There is a little garlic in there, that is it. How would you like that steak cooked?
G
Medium rare, please. And could you tell me what is in the house dressing? I would like the salad, but I am not sure it goes with the steak.
D
It is a mustard vinaigrette, so it is pretty tangy. Honestly, with a steak I would take the salad and ask for the dressing on the side.
G
Perfect. Then the steak, medium rare, with the potatoes — and may I ask whether I can make a substitution and have that salad instead of the beans?
Comprehension

About Dave

1. What are the two specials, and what does the steak come with?
A roast chicken with a lemon and herb seasoning, and a strip steak. The steak comes with two sides: green beans or potatoes.
2. What does Dave say about the green beans?
They are completely mild, with no heat at all. There is only a little garlic in them.
3. What does he recommend once he hears the order is a steak, and why?
The salad with the dressing on the side, because the house dressing is a mustard vinaigrette and it is pretty tangy.

Listening 3 · An Opinion

The Part Nobody Warns You About

Somebody who moved abroad explains what actually took her two years. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. According to her, what is the problem with learning only nouns?
You can learn a hundred nouns and still open a menu and have no idea what you are about to eat, because it is never just chicken.
2. Which three adjectives does she say tell you more than the name of the dish?
Savory, tangy and mild.
3. What does she do now, and how long did it take her to start doing it?
She simply asks what is in the dish, every single time, and nobody minds. It took her two years to work that out.

What I Can Do Now

I can ask what is in a dish without sounding rude or losing my nerve.
I put the subject before the verb after Could you tell me, and I drop the do and the does.
I use if or whether when the answer is only yes or no.

I know the words: seasoning, an herb, a side, a dressing, savory, tangy, mild, hearty.
