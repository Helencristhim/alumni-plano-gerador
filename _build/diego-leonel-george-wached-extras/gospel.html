Gospel Playlist

Twelve gospel songs in English to listen to outside class. Each one was chosen for a structure you have already studied, and comes with two short gaps for you to complete by ear. Mark each song as done after you listen to it.

Gospel · Present simple
Chris Tomlin — How Great Is Our God

The chorus is one sentence repeated until you cannot get it wrong: subject, present simple verb, complement. It is the structure of your Lesson 1, sung slowly.

Tip: sing along twice. The second time, listen to how is almost always reduced to a z sound attached to the word before it.

Gospel · Relative clauses
Chris Tomlin — Good Good Father

The chorus is literally a relative clause: it is who You are. If you understand that line, you have understood all of Lesson 5.

Tip: notice that who here does not open a question. It links two ideas. That is exactly the difference Lesson 5 worked on.

Tip: this is the best one on the list to start with. Listen once just listening, then read the lyrics out loud along with it.

Gospel · Present simple and passive
Lauren Daigle — You Say

The whole song is a contrast between two present simple sentences, and the answers come in the passive: I am loved, I am strong. Lesson 1 and Lesson 2 on the same track.

Tip: split a page into two columns, You say and I say, and write each line on the right side as you listen.

Gospel · First conditional
Zach Williams — Chain Breaker

Every line of the chorus is a first conditional: if you have got pain, He is a pain taker. It is all of Lesson 8, in the form of a song.

Tip: count how many times if appears. Then write three sentences of your own in the same shape: If ___, ___.

"If you've got pain, He's a pain "

Gospel · Future with will
MercyMe — I Can Only Imagine

The song is a sequence of future questions with will: will I dance, will I sing. Exactly what you worked on in Lesson 10.

Tip: write down the questions with will that the song asks. Then answer each one out loud.

"Will I dance for You, Jesus, or in awe of You be ?"

Slow, with a lot of space between the words, so it is one of the best on this list for training your ear. The chorus alternates present simple and future with will.

Tip: because it is slow, use the 0.75x speed button at the top of this material to follow the lyrics without rushing.

Gospel · Past simple

Tip: listen to the first verse only and underline every past tense verb. Then retell the story in your own words.
