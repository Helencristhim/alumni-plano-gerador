Lesson 06 -- Pre-class

The Performance Conversation -- Six Months of Work

The appraisal cycle, the one hard sentence and what changes after it. Key words: appraisal, competency, one-to-one, rating, to acknowledge, area for improvement, to underperform, retention, turnover, to escalate, milestone, track record. Structure: present perfect continuous, with since and for.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the definition. You run every one of these twice a year already, in Portuguese.

Appraisal -- the formal meeting where a manager reviews a whole period of work
"The mid-year appraisal covers January to June."
Competency -- a skill or behaviour the company expects and measures
"Client relationships is the competency with the highest weight."
One-to-one -- a private meeting between a manager and one person on the team
"I hold a one-to-one with each consultant every month."
Rating -- the score a manager gives for each skill in the review
"On planning the rating is three out of five."
To acknowledge -- to say out loud that you have seen and valued what somebody did
"I want to acknowledge the work you have been doing in Campinas."
Area for improvement -- the one thing a person needs to get better at
"The area for improvement is reporting, and only that."
To underperform -- to deliver less than the job expects
"He has not been underperforming on results, only on reporting."
Retention -- keeping good people in the company instead of losing them
"Retention of this consultant is a priority for the region."
Turnover -- the rate at which people leave and have to be replaced
"Turnover in the interior has been high for two years."
To escalate -- to take a problem to somebody above you
"If it happens twice more, I will escalate it to the Director."
Milestone -- a point in a plan that shows real progress has happened
"Two milestones have been reached in this cycle."
Track record -- everything a person has actually delivered over time
"His track record on new accounts is the strongest in the team."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the appraisal note and answer the questions. Notice which verbs describe something still going on and which describe a finished result.

Marcos has been covering the interior of Sao Paulo since January, and he has been working with the largest group of clinics in the territory. In this appraisal three competencies are scored. On client relationships the rating is four, because he has been building a strong relationship with the two teaching hospitals in Campinas. On planning the rating is three: he has been running his week without a written plan, and Renato has been asking for one since March.

Two milestones have been reached. The backlog has come down from forty accounts to twelve, and the first order from the Ribeirao Preto group was signed in May. His track record on new accounts is the strongest in the team.

The area for improvement is reporting. Marcos has not been sending the weekly report on time, and in April and May it arrived after the regional meeting. He has not been underperforming on results, but the team cannot plan around a report that does not arrive. From now on the report goes out every Friday before six, and Renato will hold a one-to-one every two weeks. If it is late twice more, he will escalate it to the Director.

Renato has acknowledged in writing that Marcos has been carrying the heaviest workload in the territory. Retention of this consultant matters, because turnover in the interior has been high for two years.

1. The text says "he has been covering the interior since January" but "the first order was signed in May". Why the difference?
A Covering the interior is still going on today, so it takes the present perfect continuous. Signing the order happened at one finished, named moment, so it takes the past simple.
B "Cover" is a long verb and "sign" is a short one.
C The present perfect continuous is simply more formal in an HR document.
2. Why is it "Renato has been asking for one since March"?
A Because "since" can take either form in a formal report.
B Because "since" marks a point in time, and the asking has been repeating from that point up to now.
C Because March is in the past and everything about the past takes "has been".
3. "The backlog has come down from forty accounts to twelve." Why not "has been coming down"?
A Because the sentence gives the result, and results take have or has plus the past participle.
B Because "come" has no continuous form.
C Because numbers always block the continuous.
4. Which sentence would you use in the meeting to describe the problem?
A "Your report is arriving late since April."
B "Your report has been arriving late since April."
C "Your report has been arrived late since April."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Perfect Continuous

GRAMMAR

One question decides it: is the activity still running, or are you giving the result?

FormWhen you use itExample
have / has been + verb-ingAn activity that started in the past and is still running now. The focus is the activity, not the result.He has been covering the interior since January.
have / has + past participleThe result of the period, counted or finished.The backlog has come down to twelve.
past simpleOne finished moment with a name or a date.The order was signed in May.
since and forsince + a point in time: since January, since he joined · for + a length of time: for two months, for three years
QuestionHow long have you been + verb-ing?How long have you been managing this team?
Negativehas not been + verb-ing — it describes a habit, not one single mistake.He has not been sending the report on time.
Verbs that stay simpleState verbs never take -ing: know, have, need, understand, believe, own.I have known him for three years.
Three trapsNever He is working here since March. Never How long time — just How long. And never She has been finishing it yesterday — a dated moment takes the past simple.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

You open the mid-year appraisal. The strongest first sentence is:
A "The year has been fine, in general, with some ups and downs."
B "You have been building a strong relationship with the hospitals in Campinas, and both ask for you by name."
C "Before we start, I have to tell you there is a problem."
He has been sending the report late every week since April. You say:
A "Your report is arriving late since April."
B "Your report has been arriving late since April."
C "Your report arrives late since April."
You have just named the area for improvement. What do you do next?
A Stop talking, and let him answer.
B Add three softer sentences so it does not sound hard.
C Add a second area for improvement, so the first one feels smaller.
He answers that he has been hitting his numbers every month. You say:
A "You have not been underperforming on results. This is about the report, and the team cannot plan around it."
B "You are right, let us forget the report then."
C "Everybody has problems with reports, it is normal."
You want to know how long he has been in the territory. You ask:
A "How long time are you covering this territory?"
B "How long have you been covering this territory?"
C "Since when you cover this territory?"
Nothing changes and the report is late twice more. The right sentence is:
A "I will speak to the whole team about it in the cycle meeting."
B "I will escalate it to the Commercial Director, as we agreed."
C "I will wait for the next appraisal in six months."
