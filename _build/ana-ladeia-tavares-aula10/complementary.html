TED -- Julian Treasure, "How to speak so that people want to listen"

Ten minutes on delivery rather than content: the habits that make a room stop listening, and the elements of the voice you can actually change. It is the other half of what you worked on in this lesson, since the argument you close with is only ever as good as the way it is said.

Tip: watch it, then record your three-minute closing argument again and change one single thing about the delivery -- the pace, the pauses, or where you drop your voice. Compare the two recordings.

Stanford GSB -- "Think Fast, Talk Smart: Communication Techniques"

A full lecture on speaking without preparation: what to do with the anxiety, how to buy yourself time honestly, and a structure you can reach for when somebody asks you something you did not expect. This is the skill your study group will test in November, when somebody interrupts you.

Tip: it is an hour, so watch it in two sittings. Take the structure he gives for answering on the spot and use it out loud on three questions somebody else chooses for you.

Think Fast, Talk Smart -- "High-Stakes Communication: How to Manage Anxiety Speaking in Front of Others"

Fifteen minutes on the exact situation you rehearsed today: a room, a clock, and something that matters. The argument is that nerves are not the problem to be solved, and that what looks like poise is a set of things a speaker does on purpose. Transcript on the page.

Tip: listen once for the argument, then again writing down every technique he names. Choose the two you will actually use in the simulation, and say why the others are not for you.
