Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A throughline -- the single idea that runs from the first sentence of a speech to the last
"The throughline is that nobody decided to do this. Everything else hangs off it."
To signpost -- to tell the listener where you are going before you go there
"Signpost it: there are two points, and the second one is the one that matters."
To land -- for a point to reach the listener and stay there, rather than simply being said
"The closing speech is understated, which is exactly why it lands."
A closing argument -- the last speech, in which you say what everything before it added up to
"A closing argument contains no new facts. That is the whole difficulty of writing one."
To concede -- to accept openly that one point is against you, before the other side says it
"Concede the weakest point in your first minute. It buys you the room for the rest."
To labour a point -- to repeat something long after the room has already understood it
"Say it once, well. Labouring the point tells the room you do not trust it."
Delivery -- how something is said, as opposed to what is said
"The argument was right and the delivery lost it. Both things are true at once."
Filler -- the sounds and small words people use while they are thinking
"Everybody uses filler. The question is whether it is buying you time or replacing the sentence."
To hold the floor -- to keep the room's attention for as long as you are the one speaking
"She held the floor for four minutes without once raising her voice."
To wrap up -- to bring something to an end in an organized way, rather than simply stopping
"Wrap up before they expect you to. Nobody has ever complained that a closing was short."
Poised -- calm and in control in a way that other people can see, whatever is happening inside
"Nobody in that room knew she was nervous. She was poised, and poised is a skill."
To open with -- to begin a speech or a meeting with one specific thing, chosen on purpose
"Open with the thing nobody disagrees about. It is the only sentence everybody will accept."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A closing argument is the only speech in a negotiation where nothing you say is new. What is left to choose is the order and the weight — which sentence goes first, and what the room is looking at when you stop. So the advice is not to open with your strongest point but with what nobody disputes: nobody in this room decided to do this. That is the throughline, and everything else hangs off it.

Then concede your weakest point yourself, in the first minute. She should have written again in April, and I will say that before you do. A point conceded early costs almost nothing; the same point taken from you costs the room's trust. After that, the structures of the last nine weeks do the work, and each one has a job. They have not answered her, and she has never been paid says what is still true this morning. If somebody had read clause seven, none of this would have happened says where it could have stopped, without blaming anybody. Not once in two years did anybody tell her what had happened adds weight without raising your voice. All she is asking for is one line on the copyright page chooses what the room looks at. Accepting the figure alone would mean giving up the credit says what an option costs without deciding for anybody.

Then stop. Nobody regrets a closing that was short, and everybody who has laboured a point has watched the room stop listening while they were still speaking. Say it once, well, and wrap up before anybody expects you to. What people remember afterwards is rarely the argument; it is whether the person looked poised, and poise is a skill rather than a temperament.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Choosing the Structure by the Job It Does

GRAMMAR

Nothing new here. Every structure below was taught between lesson one and lesson nine. What is new is choosing between them.

The jobStructureExample
Say what is still true todaypast simple · present perfectThey have not answered her, and she has never been paid.
Show where it could have stoppedthird conditionalIf somebody had read clause seven, none of this would have happened.
Show what an old decision costs nowmixed conditionalIf somebody had read it in March, she would not be here today.
Judge a decision, and grade how sure you aremodal perfectShe should have written again in April.
Acknowledge without conceding the casewish structuresShe wishes somebody had acknowledged it two years ago.
Add weight without raising your voiceinversionNot once in two years did anybody tell her what had happened.
Choose what the room looks atcleft sentenceAll she is asking for is one line on the copyright page.
Say exactly who and what is coveredrelative clausesClause four, which nobody read, is where the case lives.
Say what an option costs, without decidinggerunds and infinitivesAccepting the figure would mean giving up the credit.

Never: if somebody would have read it · she wishes somebody would have acknowledged it · not once anybody told her · would mean to give up the credit.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose what you would actually say.

The mediator gives you ninety seconds to close. Your first sentence is:
A "I would like to remind everybody of what my client has been through."
B "Nobody in this room decided to do this."
C "There are seven points I still need to make."
You know the delay in April is the weakest part of your case. You:
A Leave it out and hope nobody raises it.
B Concede it in the first minute, before the other side reaches it.
C Wait until the very end and explain it in detail.
You want the room to look at one thing and nothing else. You say:
A "All she is asking for is one line on the copyright page."
B "She is asking for a line on the copyright page and some other things."
C "There is a copyright page, and she would like her name to be on it."
You have made your point and there is still time on the clock. You:
A Say the same thing again in different words, to use the time.
B Add a new fact you had not planned to mention.
C Wrap up and stop talking, because nobody regrets a closing that was short.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Close the case in three minutes, with no notes. Open with what nobody disputes, concede one point before anybody takes it from you, say where it could have stopped, say what is still true this morning, put the weight where you want it, and ask for one thing. Use a different structure for each of those jobs. If a sentence comes out wrong, finish it anyway -- and then listen back and count how many you started and dropped.
