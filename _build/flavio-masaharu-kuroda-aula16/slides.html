Chapter 4: The Code

The Verb Inside the Noun

Reports turn actions into things. You can turn them back.

Grammar Discovery

Report or Call?

"There was a rise of eleven per cent in spending."

"We expect strong growth in demand."

"Spending rose by eleven per cent."

"We expect demand to grow strongly."

The red lines and the blue lines say the same thing. Which pair sounds like a report, and which sounds like a call? What happens to the verb?

Verb (the call)Noun (the report)Pattern
spending rosea rise in spendinga rise / a fall / an increase in + topic
it rose by 11%a rise of 11%the AMOUNT: verb + by, noun + of
demand grewthe growth of demand / growth in demandgrow → growth (never a grow)
it grew stronglystrong growthadverb → adjective
they integrate X with Ythe integration of X with Y-ate → -ation
they reduce manual workthe reduction of manual work-ce → -ction
new vendors arrivedthe arrival of new vendorsbecause of + the noun phrase
When to use whichread and write the nounsay the verb on the call

In one line: a report hides the verb inside a noun; on the call, take the verb back out.

Twenty numbers in the report, one on the slide. The title is a noun phrase because it is written. The last line is a question because the slide is not the point: the conversation that comes after it is.
1. Say the title of the slide as a sentence you would say on the call.
For example: The thing that stops most companies is connecting the new tool to SAP or Oracle.
2. Why does the slide say who answered the survey?
Because a number means nothing without the method: 640 finance directors in the mid-market is the segment Harbrook sells to.
3. Why does the slide end with a question?
Because the goal is to start a conversation about Marta's customers, not to present the market.
Chapter 6: Your Turn

Two Recordings, Then One Number

Listening 1 · The Analyst

Written with Nouns

An analyst on how to use a market report in a sales call. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does he say reports are written with nouns?
Because nouns sound serious and put a lot of information into one line.
2. What should you do with a noun from the report when you are on a call?
Turn it back into a verb: not "an increase in the adoption of automation" but "more companies are automating".
3. What should you read before the headline, and why?
The method: who answered, how many respondents and which segment, because the same growth rate means different things in different markets.
Listening 2 · The Buyer

One Number, Not Twenty

A buyer on vendors who bring market data to the first meeting. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she usually do when a vendor shows a market report?
She stops listening, because she can read that the market is growing herself.
2. What did the vendor do last month that worked?
He gave one number about the SAP connection and then asked her if it was true for her company.
3. What are her three pieces of advice?
Bring one number, not twenty; say it in plain words; and ask a question about her company, not about the market.
