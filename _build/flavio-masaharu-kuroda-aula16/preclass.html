Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A market share -- the percentage of a market that one company has
"The three largest vendors have a market share of 52 per cent."
A segment -- one part of a market, for example companies of one size
"The mid-market segment is where we grow fastest."
Demand -- how much customers want to buy something
"Demand for automation is strongest in the mid-market."
A survey -- a set of questions asked to many people to collect data
"The survey asked 640 finance directors about their plans."
A respondent -- a person who answers a survey
"More than half of the respondents put manual work first."
A driver -- a reason that makes something grow or change
"Less manual work is the main driver of growth."
A barrier -- something that stops people from doing or buying something
"The ERP connection is the biggest barrier to a purchase."
A shift -- a clear change in direction or behavior
"There is a shift from manual work to automated matching."
Spending -- the money that people or companies pay for something
"Spending on automation rose by eleven per cent."
A growth rate -- how fast something grows, usually as a percentage per year
"The report expects a growth rate of nine per cent a year."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Market reports have a style of their own: they put the action inside a noun. A report does not say that spending rose. It says there was a rise in spending. It does not say that demand will grow strongly. It says we expect strong growth in demand. The verb rise becomes the noun a rise, and the verb grow becomes the noun growth.

The small words change too. With a verb, the amount takes by: spending rose by eleven per cent. With a noun, the amount takes of and the topic takes in: a rise of eleven per cent in spending. The adverb becomes an adjective: it grew strongly becomes strong growth.

Many nouns come from verbs with an ending: integrate becomes the integration of, reduce becomes the reduction of, arrive becomes the arrival of. These nouns let a report put a lot of information in one line: the integration of new tools with existing ERP systems remains the biggest barrier.

When you take a number from a report into a call, do the opposite. Take the verb back out of the noun. The report says there was an increase in the adoption of automation. You say: more companies are automating. Same fact, half the words, and the partner remembers it.

1. Which sentence is in the style of a report?
A "Spending rose by eleven per cent."
B "There was a rise of eleven per cent in spending."
C "Companies are spending more."
2. Why is it "rose by eleven per cent" but "a rise of eleven per cent"?
A With the verb, the amount takes by; with the noun, the amount takes of.
B Both are possible with any preposition, because by and of mean exactly the same here.
C By is for the past and of is for the present.
3. What does the text say you should do on a call?
A Use as many heavy nouns as possible, because a call should sound as serious as the report.
B Read the report aloud.
C Take the verb back out of the noun: more companies are automating.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You are writing a slide title. The best one is:
A "Strong growth in mid-market demand"
B "Demand grows strong in mid-market"
C "The grow of demand"
On a call, you want to give the same fact simply. You say:
A "There has been an increase in the adoption of automation."
B "More mid-sized companies are automating."
C "The automation has an adoption increase."
A partner asks why the big vendors are losing share. You say:
A "Their share fell by four points, mainly because smaller specialist vendors arrived."
B "Their share fell four points in 2025 because the arrive of the smaller specialist vendors."

A buyer says: "I do not need to hear that the market is growing." The best reply is:
A "But it is growing nine per cent a year!"
B "OK, let me show you twenty more numbers."
C "Fair point. Just one number, then: almost half of finance directors delay because of the SAP connection. Is that true for you?"
