Lesson 6 -- The Strategic Narrative

Documentary
Inside Bill's Brain: Decoding Bill Gates -- Part 3

The final part is the clearest example of a through-line in the whole series: one problem, three steps, one ask. Watch how rarely he adds a fact without immediately saying what follows from it — that is the "so what" test, applied by someone who has never heard the phrase.

Tip: pause after any statistic he gives and try to say the "so what" before he does. You will be right more often than you expect, and that is the skill.

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart -- episodes on structure and storytelling

The Stanford podcast returns to structure repeatedly, and always with the same conclusion: an audience remembers a shape, not a list. Their frameworks map almost exactly onto the through-line and build you used today, with different names.

Tip: pick one episode on structure and, while listening, sketch the storyboard of your next real presentation on one page. Five lines, no slides.

YouTube
Nancy Duarte -- The Common Structure of the Greatest Communicators (TEDxEast)

You watched this after lesson one for the opening. Watch it again for the middle. Her whole argument is about the build: the movement between what is and what could be, repeated until the audience arrives at the ask. It is the same shape you storyboarded today.

Tip: map her structure onto your own storyboard. Where is your "what is", where is your "what could be", and how many times do you move between them in twenty minutes?
