The Best Team, The Biggest Challenge -- Superlatives in HR Strategy

Naming the extremes: the strongest result, the biggest challenge and the real cause behind it. Key words: challenge, strength, weakness, gap, root cause, bottleneck, impact, trend, insight, metric. Structures: superlatives with the + -est, the most and the least, the irregulars the best and the worst, and one of the + superlative + plural.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the definition. These are the words you use when you explain data.

Challenge -- something difficult that you have to deal with
"Our biggest challenge this year is store retention."
Strength -- something a person or a team does very well
"The head office team is our greatest strength."
Weakness -- the part that is not strong, and needs work
"Our weakness is the first month of a new employee."
Gap -- the space between where you are and where you want to be
"The gap between the two regions is eleven points."
Root cause -- the real reason behind a problem, under the symptom
"The root cause is not salary. It is the manager."
Bottleneck -- the one point that slows everything else down
"The bottleneck is the interview stage, not the offer."
Impact -- the effect that something has on people or results
"The training had the biggest impact on the newest stores."
Trend -- the direction things are moving over time
"The trend is good: three quarters going down."
Insight -- a useful thing you understand from looking at data
"The best insight came from the open comments."
Metric -- a number you follow to see if you are progressing
"Turnover is the metric the board looks at first."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

This quarter Viviane has good news and bad news. The good news is engagement: seventy-four per cent, the highest number the company has ever had. The bad news is store retention, and it is the biggest challenge of the year. The north region has the lowest engagement and the worst turnover in the group. The first month is the most difficult moment for a new employee: sixty-two per cent of the people who leave, leave in month one. So the root cause is not the salary. The bottleneck is the manager who receives them, and the north region has the newest managers in the company. That was the best insight of the whole survey, and it is one of the biggest challenges in retail today.

1. Why does the text say "the highest" and not "higher"?
A Because it compares one thing with every other thing, not with one other thing.
B Because "high" is an irregular adjective.
C Because the sentence is in the past.
2. Why is there a "the" before every superlative?
A Because the superlative always carries "the".
B Because it is more polite.
C Because the nouns are plural.
3. The text says "one of the biggest challenges". Why is "challenges" plural?
A Because "one of the" is always followed by a plural noun.
B Because there are exactly two challenges.
C Because "challenge" has no singular form.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Superlatives -- Naming the Extreme

GRAMMAR

The comparative compares two things. The superlative compares one against all the others.

FormUseExample
the + short adjective + -estOne syllable, or two ending in -y.the biggest · the lowest · the easiest
the most / the least + long adjectiveTwo syllables or more. Never add -est.the most difficult · the least expensive
the is obligatoryWithout it, the sentence is not a superlative.It is the biggest challenge we have.
irregularThe same three as the comparative.good → the best · bad → the worst · far → the furthest
one of the + superlative + pluralWhen it is not the only one at the top.It is one of the biggest challenges in retail.
in, not of, with placesUse in for a place or a group.The lowest engagement in the company.
