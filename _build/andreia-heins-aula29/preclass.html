Lesson 29 -- Pre-class

The Full Presentation -- Congress Talk

Give a ten-minute talk at an international congress, from the first sentence to the questions from the floor. Key words: podium, slide deck, abstract, disclosure, findings, sample size, learning curve, limitation, feasible, bottom line. Structure: all the structures of the course in one talk -- signposting, narrative tenses, hedging, concession and conditions.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Podium -- a small raised stand where a speaker gives a talk
"She walked to the podium and smiled at the audience."
Slide deck -- all the slides of one presentation, in one file
"Please send your slide deck to the chair by Wednesday."
Abstract -- a short summary of a study that you send before a congress
"The congress accepted my abstract in June."
Disclosure -- a statement about any money or links with companies
"The first slide of every talk is the disclosure."
Findings -- the results of a study or an investigation
"Our main findings are on the next slide."
Sample size -- the number of people or cases in a study
"Our sample size was two hundred robotic cases."
Learning curve -- how fast people get better at something new over time
"The learning curve was steep in the first fifty cases."
Limitation -- a weak point of a study, something it cannot show
"The main limitation is that we only studied one hospital."
Feasible -- possible and practical to do
"A dedicated robotic team is feasible, even in a busy hospital."
Bottom line -- the most important point, the main message
"The bottom line is simple: train the team together."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Andreia's abstract was accepted, and on Thursday she gives her talk at a congress in Miami. At the podium, she opens with a hook: "Let me start with a question." Then she tells a story: "When we started, we were preparing the room for forty minutes, and we had never worked with a robot."

In the findings, she is careful: "It seems that the learning curve gets flatter after fifty cases." She is honest about the limitation: "Although our sample size was small, the results were clear." And she closes with a condition: "A small team can do it, provided that the nurses train together."

There is no new grammar here. Each part of the talk uses a structure she already knows, and together they make one bottom line.

1. Why does Andreia say "It seems that" when she presents the findings?
A Because she did not understand the data and wants the chair to explain it after the session.
B Because the data is good, but she wants to sound honest, not 100% certain.
C Because "it seems that" is the only correct way to start a sentence in a talk.
2. "When we started, we had never worked with a robot." What happened first?
A The team had no experience with robots before the program.
B The team worked with the robot first, and the program started three years later.
C The program and the first robotic case happened in the same week.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "Despite our sample size was small, the results were clear."
B "Although of the small sample size, the results were clear in every hospital."
C "Although our sample size was small, the results were clear."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- One Structure for Each Part of the Talk

GRAMMAR

No new grammar today: a congress talk puts the structures you already know in the right order.

PartJobStructureExample
Opena hook and a maplet me / I'd like to + verb; I'll start by + verb-ingLet me start with a question.
Storybackground and the earlier eventwas / were + verb-ing; had + past participleWe had never worked with a robot.
Findingsstrong, but honestit seems that / tend to / be likely toIt seems that the team gets faster.
Limitsaccept a weak pointalthough + clause; despite + noun or -ingAlthough the sample size was small, ...
Closea condition and the messageprovided that / as long as + presentIt works, provided that the team trains together.
Q&Acome back to a point, stay calmthat said / coming back to; could you tell me...?That said, two other centers are collecting data.

Remember: after provided that and as long as, use the present, not will. After although, use a full clause; after despite, use a noun or verb-ing.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best sentence for each moment of the talk.

The chair says: "Our next speaker is Andreia Heins." Your first words are:
A "Hello. OK. So, my name is Andreia, and I am very nervous today, sorry for my English."
B "Thank you, Ken. Let me start with a question."
C "I would like to starting with a question for everybody in the room today."
A surgeon in the audience says: "Your sample size is very small." You answer:
A "That's a fair question. Although the sample was small, the results were consistent."
B "No, it is not small."
C "Despite our sample was small, I think you are wrong about the results and about the data of the study."
You have one minute left. You close your talk with:
A "OK, that is all, I think. Any questions?"
B "The bottom line is this: a trained team makes robotic surgery feasible."
C "The bottom line is this: robotic surgery will work, provided that the team will train together every single week."
