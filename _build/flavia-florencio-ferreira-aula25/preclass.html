Lesson 25 -- Pre-class

The Bilateral Meeting

Lead a full meeting with an Italian partner: open it, move between agenda items, and close it with a summary and the next step. Key words: summarize, agenda item, chair, attendee, overview, consensus, action item, minutes, wrap-up, takeaway.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To summarize -- to say the main points in a few words
"Let me summarize the three main decisions."
Agenda item -- one topic on the list for a meeting
"The first agenda item is our figures."
To chair -- to lead a meeting and decide who speaks
"Paolo will chair the first part of the meeting."
Attendee -- a person who is at a meeting or event
"There are six attendees at the meeting today."
Overview -- a short description of the main points
"Renata gave me an overview of the meeting."
Consensus -- an agreement of everyone in the group
"We have a consensus: the visits are in April."
Action item -- a task that someone must do after a meeting
"Each action item has a name and a date."
Minutes -- the written notes of what people said and decided in a meeting
"Renata will send the minutes on Friday."
Wrap-up -- the last part of a meeting, when you close it
"We have five minutes for the wrap-up."
Takeaway -- the main idea that you remember after a meeting
"My main takeaway is that the partner wants coffee."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Flavia is in Milan for a bilateral meeting with Paolo, from a food consortium in Parma. There are three agenda items. Paolo opens the meeting, and Flavia says: "Thank you for having me. I would like to start by presenting our figures." She gives the numbers: approximately thirty-three regional offices. Then she says: "Moving on to the proposal, if your board agrees, we will start the visits in April." Paolo agrees, so they have a consensus. Before the end, Flavia says: "Before we finish, I would like to mention Canastra cheese." In the wrap-up, she says: "To summarize what we discussed, the visits are in April. The next step would be to share the minutes."

1. What does Flavia start the meeting with?
A Our figures.
B A long story about the history of Sebrae-SP and all of its regional offices.
C The products from Minas Gerais.
2. Which phrase moves the meeting to a new topic?
A "To summarize what we discussed..."
B "Moving on to the proposal..."
C "Thank you for having me, and it is a pleasure to be here in Milan today."
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "The next step would be share the minutes with all the attendees on Friday."
B "The next step would to share it."
C "The next step would be to share the minutes."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Signposts for a Meeting

GRAMMAR

How to open a meeting, move between topics, and close it.

PartPhraseExample
OpenThank you for having me. / I would like to start by + verb-ingI would like to start by presenting our figures.
MoveMoving on to + noun / On the subject of + nounMoving on to the proposal, ...
Add a last topicBefore we finish, I would like to mention + nounBefore we finish, I would like to mention our coffee.
CloseTo summarize what we discussed, ... / The next step would be to + verbThe next step would be to share the minutes.
Say goodbyeI look forward to + verb-ingI look forward to following up on this.
RuleAfter by and look forward to, use the -ing form: "start by presenting" (not "start by present"). After moving on, always use to: "moving on to the proposal". After would be, use to + verb: "would be to share".
