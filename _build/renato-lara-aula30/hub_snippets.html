The Biggest Upset

Eight words for the news (headline, upset, crowd, award, beat, eliminate, celebrate, semifinal) and how to say what happened: The contest was held at Margaret River. Two surfers were eliminated.

Stage 1.1: The News of the Day

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes. Careful: "upset" here is a surprise result, not a feeling.

Headline — the title of a news story, in big letters
"The headline says: Underdog wins!"
Upset — a result that nobody expected, when the underdog wins
"It was the biggest upset of the day."
Crowd — a lot of people together in one place
"The crowd on the beach cheered loudly."
Award — to give a score or a prize in an official way
"The judges award points for every wave."
Beat — to win against somebody
"Renato beat Finn by almost two points."
Eliminate — to take somebody out of a competition
"One bad heat can eliminate you."
Celebrate — to do something fun because something good happened
"Let's celebrate with some pizza tonight."
Semifinal — the round of a competition just before the final
"Finn and I are in the semifinal."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read Renato's message to his friend Lucas in Brazil, one hour after the quarterfinal. Then answer the questions.

Lucas, I won! The quarterfinal was surfed at two o'clock, and it was won by me, the underdog! Finn was beaten by almost two points. My last wave was given an 8.10 by the judges, the best score of the day. The crowd went crazy. Ethan and Noah were eliminated, so Finn and I are in the semifinal. After the heat, I was interviewed for the club newsletter, and a photo of the squad was posted on the club page. The headline says it was the biggest upset of the day. Tonight we're going to celebrate with pizza!
"My last wave was given an 8.10." Who gave the score?
A The judges gave it.
B Renato gave it to himself, and then he told the judges on the tower.
Who was beaten in the quarterfinal?
A Renato was beaten by Finn in the last minute of the heat.
B Finn was beaten by Renato.
"Ethan and Noah were eliminated." What does Renato mean?
A They are out of the contest.
B They are in the semifinal too, with Renato and Finn, later this month.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Past passive: was / were + past participle

Use the passive when what happened is more important than who did it. Use was for one thing and were for two or more. Add by + the person only when the person is important.

Active: who did it

The judges gave it an 8.10.
Renato beat Finn.
Dana took the photo.

Passive: what happened

It was given an 8.10.
Finn was beaten by Renato.
The photo was taken by Dana.

Positive: Two surfers were eliminated.  ·  Negative: Finn wasn't eliminated.  ·  Question: Where was the contest held?
Remember: use the past participle, not the past simple: hold → held, beat → beaten, give → given, take → taken, win → won. Never forget was / were: "The photo was taken", not "The photo taken".
Your voice: "was" is weak and short (wuz). The strong word is the participle: was GIV-en.
