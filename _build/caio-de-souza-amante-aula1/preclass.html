Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

Who Is Caio, in English? -- The Founder in the Room

A stranger at a conference asks what your company does, and the answer has to fit three sentences. Key words: founder, to specialize in, revenue, stakeholder, deal, negotiation, expansion, acquisition, consulting, to be responsible for. Structure: the third-person singular -s in the present simple.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A founder -- the person who started the company and still owns part of it
"I am the founder of Dataside, and I have run it for fifteen years."
To specialize in -- to do one thing extremely well, and only that thing
"We specialize in data and artificial intelligence."
Revenue -- the money a business brings in before any costs are taken out
"Most of our revenue comes from long consulting contracts."
A stakeholder -- anyone whose money or decisions are tied to the result
"Every stakeholder in this deal wants to see the same numbers."
A deal -- the agreement two companies sign when the negotiation is finished
"We lost a deal worth a hundred and ten million reais."
A negotiation -- the conversation where each side moves until the terms fit both
"The negotiation took nine months and four meetings in New York."
Expansion -- opening the business in a new country or a new market
"Expansion into the United States is the reason I am learning English."
An acquisition -- the purchase of one company by another
"An acquisition is not only about price. It is about trust."
Consulting -- selling expert work by the project or by the hour, not a product
"Consulting means our people work inside the client company."
To be responsible for -- to own the result, and to answer for it in front of other people
"I am responsible for every number in this presentation."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A buyer in New York asks a founder what his company does, and the founder answers well — the numbers are right, the story is clear, the strategy holds. And still, something in the room moves half a step back. Later nobody can say exactly what it was. It was one letter. Dataside specialize in data, he said. The buyer want a faster close. Three or four times in ten minutes, and each time the sentence arrived a little less finished than the thought behind it.

English marks one person in the present simple, and only one. When the subject is he, she or it, the verb carries an -s: she wants, he runs, it costs. Everybody else takes the bare verb: I run, we specialize, they want. The trap for a Portuguese speaker is not the rule — the rule is simple and most speakers can recite it. The trap is the subject that does not look like a person. Dataside has three hundred people, because a company is an it. Revenue comes from consulting, because revenue is an it too. The private equity firm wants three years of numbers. Every one of those subjects is a single thing, and every one of them takes the -s.

In the negative and in questions the -s moves house. It leaves the main verb and goes to do: he does not move on price, and does the buyer need more time? Saying she does not wants puts it in both places, which is the second most common version of the same mistake. And there is a habit that travels from Portuguese with it: depends of instead of it depends on. Neither one changes what a founder means. Both change how finished the founder sounds, and in a first meeting that is the only thing a buyer has to go on.

1. Why does the text say the buyer moved half a step back?
A Because one letter was missing from the verb three or four times, so each sentence sounded less finished than the thought behind it.
B Because the founder had the numbers wrong.
C Because the founder spoke too quickly for the room.
2. Why does “Dataside” take a verb with -s?
A Because it is a long word.
B Because a company is a single thing, so it counts as it.
C Because it is a foreign name.
3. What happens to the -s in a negative sentence, according to the text?
A It disappears completely.
B It stays on the main verb and is repeated on do.
C It moves from the main verb to do: he does not move.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Third-Person Singular -S

GRAMMAR

One letter. It decides whether the sentence sounds finished.

I / you / we / theybare verbWe specialize in data.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Ninety seconds, no notes. Somebody who has never heard of Dataside asks what your company does. Cover what it does, how big it is, where the revenue comes from, one thing that makes it different, and what you are looking for. Finish with one question back.
