Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

The GPTW Story -- Narrating a Professional Achievement

Telling the story of a real project in English: the problem before, what you did, and the result with a number. Key words: certification, milestone, rollout, stakeholder, buy-in, benchmark, sponsor, pilot, turning point, achievement. Structures: narrative sequencers (at the beginning, first, then, after that, six months later, in the end) with the past simple.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the definition. You lived all of them in the GPTW project.

Certification -- official proof that a company reached a standard
"We got the certification in November."
Milestone -- an important point inside a long project
"The pilot result was our first milestone."
Rollout -- putting a new plan into practice across the whole company
"The rollout took four months."
Stakeholder -- a person or group with a real interest in the project
"I had to convince three difficult stakeholders."
Buy-in -- the moment people agree to support your idea
"Without the buy-in of the store managers, nothing moves."
Benchmark -- a number you compare your own result with
"The market benchmark was sixty-eight per cent."
Sponsor -- the senior leader who protects and funds the project
"The CEO was the sponsor of the project."
Pilot -- a small first test before doing something everywhere
"First we ran a pilot in four stores."
Turning point -- the moment when a situation changes direction
"The turning point was the first survey result."
Achievement -- something difficult that you did successfully
"It is the achievement I am most proud of."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

At the beginning, nobody believed in the project. Viviane asked for a budget and the answer was no. So she changed the plan. First she ran a small pilot in four stores. It took them three months and it cost nothing. The result was the turning point: in those four stores people stayed. After that, the finance director became her sponsor, because now she had a number and not an opinion. Six months later, they trained every store manager and they ran the survey in the whole company. Their result was seventy-four per cent, above the market benchmark. In the end, they got the certification in November.

1. Why does the story use "at the beginning" and "in the end"?
A They mark where the story starts and where it finishes.
B They are polite ways to start a sentence.
C They are needed after every past verb.
2. The text says "it took them three months". Which sentence is correct?
A "It took to them three months."
B "It took them three months."
C "It took for them three months."
3. Why does the writer say "after that" and not only "after"?
A Because "after" alone needs something after it, like "after the pilot".
B Because "after" is not English.
C Because "after that" is more formal.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Telling a Story in Order

GRAMMAR

The words that turn a list of facts into a story a recruiter can follow.

FormUseExample
At the beginning / At firstWhere the story starts, and usually the problem.At first nobody believed in it.
First · Then · After thatThe order of your actions. Use after that, not after alone.First we ran a pilot. After that we trained the managers.
It took + person + timeHow long something took. No preposition after took.It took us eight months.
... laterMoving forward inside the story.Six months later we ran the survey again.
In the end / FinallyThe result of the whole story.In the end we got the certification.
CarefulTwo traps for Brazilian speakers.Never in the final. Never it took to us.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

A recruiter asks about an achievement. The strongest first sentence is:
A "I am responsible for culture, engagement and performance."
B "The Great Place to Work certification. It was my project from the first meeting to the last day."
C "My company is a retail group with thirty-eight stores."
You want to say how long the project took. Which is correct?
A "It took us eight months."
B "It took to us eight months."
C "It has eight months."
You are moving from the first action to the second. You say:
A "After we trained the managers."
B "After that we trained the managers."
C "In the final we trained the managers."
The interview coach says you should finish the story with:
A a list of everything you are responsible for
B an opinion about the company culture
C the result, with a number
You led the project yourself. The coach says you should say:
A "We did everything together, it was a team."
B "I led the project, and the team ran the pilot with me."
