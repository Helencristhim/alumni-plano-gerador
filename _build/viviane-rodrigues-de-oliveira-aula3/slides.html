Chapter 3: The Code

A Story Has an Order

And the order has words

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"At the beginning, nobody believed in the project."

"First we ran a pilot in four stores."

"After that, we trained every store manager."

"In the end, we got the certification."

These four sentences are one story. What do the purple words do that the verbs cannot do alone?

FormUseExample
At the beginning / At firstWhere the story starts -- and usually the problem.At first nobody believed in it.
First · Then · After thatThe order of your actions. Say after that, not after alone.First we ran a pilot. Then we trained the managers.
It took + person + timeHow long the project took. No preposition after took.It took us eight months. (never it took to us)
Six months laterMoving forward inside the story.Six months later we ran the survey again.
In the end / FinallyThe result. In the end, never in the final.In the end we got the certification.

The Structure

Problem, Action, Result

Every good project story in English has these three parts, in this order. Nothing else is needed.

1 · The problem before

"Two years ago people left the company and nobody talked about culture."

2 · What you did

"First we ran a pilot in four stores. After that we trained every store manager."

3 · The result, with a number

"In the end we got the certification, and turnover went down by eleven per cent."

Problem → action → a number. Without the number it is an opinion; with the number it is a result.

Chapter 4: The Headhunter

A Call from a Headhunter

Peter · he found your profile · he has one question

Dialogue

Tell Me About an Achievement

P
So, Viviane, tell me about a project you are really proud of.
V
The Great Place to Work certification. It was my project from the first meeting to the last day.
P
Interesting. Where did you start?
V
At the beginning nobody believed in it. So first we ran a pilot in four stores.
P
Why only four stores?
V
Because I needed one number to show the board. The pilot gave me that number.
P
And after that?
V
After that we trained every store manager. Six months later we ran the survey in the whole company, and in the end we got the certification.
P
That is a strong story. Most candidates tell me what they are responsible for. You told me what changed.
Comprehension

About Peter

1. What does Peter ask her to talk about?
A project she is really proud of.
2. What does he want to know first?
Where she started — the beginning of the project.
3. Why does he say her story is strong?
Most candidates say what they are responsible for; she told him what changed.

Listening 2

How to Tell It in Sixty Seconds

An interview coach on the story that gets you the job. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What mistake does he say almost everybody makes?
They describe the company and the sector, and the recruiter never hears what the person did.
2. What are the three parts of his shape?
The problem before, the two or three things you did in order, and the result with a number.
3. How long should the story be?
Sixty seconds is enough.
4. What does he say about the word we?
Say we for the team and I for your own decisions — that is information, not arrogance.
