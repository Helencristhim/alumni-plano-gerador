Chapter 4: The Code

Who, Which or Where?

Relative clauses

Grammar Discovery

People, Things and Places

"I am Rosa, the guide who will walk with you today."

"The Castillo is a fort which the Spanish built."

"Coquina is a stone that is made of tiny shells."

"St. George Street is the street where there are no cars."

Which colored word is for people? For things? For a place?

WordUse it forExample
whopeoplethe guide who told the story
whichthings and animalsthe fort which the Spanish built
thatpeople or things (informal)a stone that is made of shells
whereplaces (+ subject + verb)the street where there are no cars
One sentence, not twothe relative word joins two ideasThere is a fort. The Spanish built it. → There is a fort which the Spanish built.
The trapdo not repeat the subject or the object: the people who climb, never the people who they climb

who = people · which / that = things · where = places. One sentence, more information.

Grammar Practice

Who, Which or Where?

Say it first, then click to check

"Henry Flagler was the man ___who built the hotel." (a person)
"The Castillo is a fort ___which is made of coquina." (a thing)
"This is the beach ___where I walk my dogs." (a place)
"The guide ___who took us on the ghost tour was very funny." (a person)
"The lighthouse is the place ___where you can see the whole city." (a place)
"Datil sauce is something ___which every family here makes." (a thing)

A Night With a Guide

A ghost tour guide starts his tour. Sound first — no text.

1. Where does the tour start?
At the old city gates.
2. Who was Elizabeth?
A young woman who lived in the house next to the city gates.
3. What do some visitors see in the window?
A light (a candle).
Listening 2 · At the Lighthouse

Two Hundred and Nineteen Steps

A volunteer welcomes visitors to the lighthouse. Sound first — no text.

1. When was the lighthouse finished?
In 1874.
2. How many steps are there to the top?
Two hundred and nineteen.
3. Who lived in the house next to the lighthouse?
The keepers and their families.
