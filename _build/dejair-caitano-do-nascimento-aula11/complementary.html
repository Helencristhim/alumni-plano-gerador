Lesson 11 -- Telling the Story

Conference Talk
"The clues to a great story" -- Andrew Stanton, TED

The writer of Toy Story and WALL-E on why some stories hold a room and others do not. His first rule is the one you used today: give people the scene before you give them the fact.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Write down the moment when he sets a scene instead of stating a fact -- then do the same thing with your poster session story.

Listening
"What makes a good story?" -- 6 Minute English, BBC Learning English

Six minutes on why people remember stories and forget lists, at learner speed. The two presenters keep moving between the scene and the event -- the exact contrast you practiced today.

Tip: listen once with your eyes closed. Then listen again and write down every "was" or "were" plus -ing that you hear. Say each one out loud after the speaker.

Video
"Past simple and past continuous" -- 6 Minute Grammar, BBC Learning English

Six minutes on the two tenses of today's lesson, side by side, with the pronunciation of "was" and "were" in fast speech -- the part that makes the structure hard to hear in a real conversation.

Tip: pause after each example and repeat it with your own conference story. Then record yourself saying three sentences with "while" and three with "when".
