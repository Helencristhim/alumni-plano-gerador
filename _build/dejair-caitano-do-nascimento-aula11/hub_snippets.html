Telling the Story -- What Was Happening When It Happened

Setting the scene of a conference story, so people listen to the end. Key words: to notice, to realize, to overhear, to pause, context, reaction, meanwhile, dosage. Structure: past continuous for the long action, past simple for the short one that cuts it.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. Six of these eight words are about what your eyes and your ears do while something else is happening.

To notice -- to see or hear something you were not looking for
"Then I noticed one woman at the corner of my poster."
To realize -- to understand something suddenly, after not understanding it
"I realized I was talking only to one person in the room."
To overhear -- to hear something that was not said to you
"At the coffee table I overheard two people talking about my disease."
To pause -- to stop for a short moment, and then continue
"I paused, I drank some water, and I asked him directly."
Context -- everything around an event that helps you understand it
"Without the context, that number means nothing."
Reaction -- what a person says or does immediately after something happens
"The reaction of the whole room changed after that question."
Meanwhile -- at the same time as something else
"Meanwhile, everybody else was too polite to say it."
Dosage -- how much of a drug a patient takes, and how often
"They had the same problem with dosage in their trial."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Rajesh tells this story at every congress. "It was the second morning of my first international meeting. I was standing at my poster and almost nobody was stopping. Everybody was walking past me towards the big posters at the end of the row, so I was reading my own abstract again, only to look busy. Then I noticed one woman. She was not looking at the whole poster -- she was looking at one small figure in the corner, and while she was looking, she was writing something in her notebook. The thing is, that was the figure I almost removed the week before." She was from a group in Lisbon. They had the same problem with dosage, and two years later they published together.
Why does he say "I was standing" and not "I stood"?
A Because it is the long action -- the scene that was already in progress when something else happened
B Because it happened a long time ago
C Because he stood up only once
Why is "Then I noticed one woman" in the past simple and not in the -ing form?
A Because it is not important
B Because it is the short action that cuts into the scene
C Because "notice" is a difficult verb

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Scene and the Cut

GRAMMAR

A story needs two times in the same sentence. The long action is the scene, already in progress. The short action is the thing that cuts into it. You already have the short one: it is the past simple you use every day.

the scene (long)was / were + verb-ingI was standing at my poster
the cut (short)past simpleshe asked about the dosage
negativewas not / were not + verb-ingnobody was stopping / she was not looking at the whole poster
questionwas / were + subject + verb-ingWhat were you doing when she arrived?
when or whilewhen + short · while + longI was reading when he stopped · While he was writing, I waited
never with -ingknow, understand, remember, wantI knew the answer -- never I was knowing

One sentence, two times: the scene buys you two seconds, and the cut is the reason the other person waited.

Quiz

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
Tell one real story from your own working life -- a congress, a lab, a conversation that changed something. Ninety seconds. Start with the scene (what was happening around you), then the short action that cut into it, then what it changed. Use at least two "I was ... when ..." sentences and one "The thing is". No script -- just speak.
