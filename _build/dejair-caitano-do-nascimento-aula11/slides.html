Chapter 3: The Code

Two Times, One Sentence

The long thing, and the short thing that cut it

Discover the Rule

Which Action Is Longer?

I was standing at my poster when she asked about the dosage.
While the panel was discussing nerve damage, I realized I had the same data.
Everybody was walking past me, so I read my own abstract again.
We were talking about leprosy when somebody interrupted us.
Click to reveal the rule
The LONG action — the scene, the thing already in progress — takes was / were + verb-ing: I was standing, they were walking. The SHORT action that cuts into it takes the past simple you already use: she asked, I realized. Two words join them: when goes in front of the short one (when she asked) and while goes in front of the long one (while the panel was discussing). Careful: verbs about the mind and the senses — know, understand, remember, want — never take -ing. Say I knew, never I was knowing.

Chapter 5: Practice

The Man Who Was Shaking His Head

And what she did instead of continuing

Listening 2

So I Stopped

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What was the man in the front row doing while she was explaining her second slide?
He was shaking his head — every time she said a number.
2. What did she realize before she stopped?
That she was talking only to him, and she was losing the rest of the room.
3. What was the man actually disagreeing with?
Not her data — her units. She had milligrams where she needed micrograms.

What I Learned

Today You Can

Set the scene of a story with was or were plus verb-ing
Cut into that scene with a short past simple action
Put while in front of the long action and when in front of the short one
Keep know, understand and remember out of the -ing form
Buy yourself two seconds with The thing is, before the complication
