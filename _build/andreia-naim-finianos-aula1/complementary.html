Complementary Activities

Optional extras to enjoy outside class. Mark each one as done after you watch or listen to it.

Lesson 1 -- The Second Sentence

Podcast
NPR Life Kit -- "How to be a supercommunicator"

Twenty minutes on the exact skill this lesson trains: what a good talker does after the first answer. Charles Duhigg argues that the move is to ask one more question instead of telling one more fact. American English at natural speed, and the complete transcript sits on the same page.

Tip: listen once with the transcript hidden. Then read it and underline every question the host asks. Bring the one you would like to be able to ask yourself.

Listen on NPR ↗
Talk
Simon Sinek -- "How great leaders inspire action" (TED, 18 min)

A whole talk about the question you answered today: what does your company do. His point is that most companies answer with the product and lose the room, and that the answer that works starts somewhere else. Slow, deliberate delivery, and every second sentence is a relative clause.

Tip: turn on the English subtitles and stop at minute four. Write down his one-sentence version, then write your own with the same shape.

Watch on TED ↗
Film
American Factory (Netflix, 110 min)

A Chinese group reopens a closed factory in Ohio and puts Chinese managers and American workers in the same building. It is the culture clash you already described in your own words: one side that expects the relationship first, one side that expects the decision first. Two accents, side by side, both at full speed.

Tip: watch the first thirty minutes with English subtitles. Note one moment where the two sides misread each other, and be ready to say what you would have done in that room.
