Chapter 3: The Code

The Word That Stops the Full Stop

Two ideas, one sentence, no pause

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"I work for a company that builds tax software."

"I look after the clients who are outside Brazil."

"Compliance is the part that worries them most."

"The person who signs the contract is never the person who uses the system."

The purple word always comes straight after the blue one, and it decides which blue one you mean. Two of them point at a thing. Two point at a person.

JobFormExample
Point back to a thingthat (or which)a company that builds tax software
Point back to a personwho (or that)the clients who are outside Brazil
Where it goesstraight after the nounthe part that worries them most
The pronoun IS the subjectdo not repeat ita company that builds — never that it builds
One sentence, two clauseseach one keeps its own pronounthe person who signs ... the person who uses
No comma herethe clause says WHICH onethe clients who are outside Brazil (not all of them)
The trapnever what, never which for peoplenot the software what we sell

In one line: name the thing, then hook a second idea onto it with who or that. That hook is what turns seven words into twenty.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Queue, the Recordings, the Week Ahead

One conversation, two recordings, one schedule

Dialogue

Five Minutes in the Registration Queue

G
This queue is not moving. Greg, finance side, out of Chicago. So what does your company do?
A
Andreia, nice to meet you. I work for a company that builds tax and finance software, and I am the person who looks after the partners outside Brazil.
G
Tax software. That is a crowded market. What do people come to you for?
A
Usually compliance. The pain point that brings them to us is almost never the price. It is the audit that nobody could answer last year.
G
That I understand. We have three providers and none of them talk to each other. How big are the companies you work with?
A
Groups with a headcount over two thousand, which is where payroll stops being straightforward. We rolled it out in four countries last year.
G
Four countries at once. How long from signature to the day it actually works?
A
About five months, and we never go live on a Friday. Come by the stand later and I will walk you through it. And you, what brings the finance side to a software conference?
Comprehension

About Greg

1. What does Greg tell her about himself in his first line, and what does he say about the market?
He is on the finance side and he is out of Chicago. He says that tax software is a crowded market.
2. What problem does Greg have with his own systems?
He has three providers and none of them talk to each other.
3. Greg asks two questions about size and time. What exactly does he want to know?
How big the companies she works with are, and how long it takes from signature to the day the system actually works.
Listening 1 · A Sales Director

What We Actually Sell

A sales director answering a question he gets at every conference. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say people think he sells, and what does he say he actually sells?
People think he sells software. He says what he actually sells is the answer to a question the client could not answer last year.
2. What happened to the client he describes, and what did their old system do about it?
Their headcount doubled in eighteen months and they opened in two more countries. The old system kept working, which he says was the problem, because nobody noticed the numbers had stopped being right.
3. What is the one thing he says he has learned to do first, and why?
To ask what went wrong last year, before he shows anything. He says the pain point is never on the list the client sends you.
Listening 2 · The Client Side

The Month We Went Live

A finance manager describing a rollout from the other side of the table. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say she chose that provider, and what was not the reason?
She chose them because they were the only ones who asked what had gone wrong the year before. It was not the price and it was not the demo.
2. What mistake does she say they made about the date?
They went live on a Friday, at the end of the month, which meant nobody was in the office when the first payroll run failed.
3. What does she say saved the project?
The person on their side who walked her team through it screen by screen, on the phone, all weekend. She says she still has his number.

Role-Play 1 of 3 — Guided

Greg Asks Again

Scenario: The same finance director finds you again at the coffee stand and asks the same three questions: what the company does, what clients come to you for, and who he should be talking to. Answer all three, and finish with a question back to him.

Keyword chips:

a company that... compliance a pain point the person who signs
Role-Play 2 of 3 — Semi-free

Monday Dinner, No Agenda

Scenario: It is Monday night. The partners have just landed and there is no agenda tonight, on purpose. Ask two questions about the person in front of you — the flight, the city, the family, the food — before you say one word about work. Then hook your own answer onto something they said.

Keywords:

the flight that... the place where I grew up the one dish that... someone who travels every month
Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

Two Minutes, No Notes

Scenario: You are on a stage at a partner event and somebody in the audience asks the open question: tell us what your company does and why anybody should change provider. Give them the work itself, the clients you look after, the pain point they arrive with, and one thing you would never promise.

No keywords, no notes, two minutes.
