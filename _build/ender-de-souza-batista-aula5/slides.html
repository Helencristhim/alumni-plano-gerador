Chapter 3: The Code

At, On, In

Three small words for time

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Have in Common?

"The call is on Wednesday."

"It is at nine."

"I am busy in the morning."

"The project starts in November."

Look at the blue words. Which one goes with a day? With a time on the clock? With the morning and with a month?

A day or a dateonon Wednesday / on November 4

Next, this, everyno prepositionThe call is next week. (Not in next week.)
QuestionWhat time...? / What day...? / When...?What time is the call? At nine.

In one line: at for the clock, on for the day, in for the morning and the month. Next week has no preposition.

Chapter 4: The Call About the Call

The Call About the Call

One conversation, two recordings, one invite

Dialogue

When Are You Free?

P
Hello, Ender. We need a call about the new cards project. Are you available on Tuesday?
E
Hi, Priya. Let me check my calendar. On Tuesday I am busy in the morning. What about Wednesday?
P
Wednesday is good for me. What time?
E
At nine in the morning, São Paulo time. Is that okay?
P
Let me see. With the time zone, nine in São Paulo is five thirty in the afternoon in Mumbai. Perfect.
E
Great. The invite is in your email in five minutes.
P
Thank you. And the second call? Is it on Friday?
E
No, my Fridays are full. Let’s schedule it for next week, on Monday at ten.
P
Monday at ten. Okay. See you on Wednesday!
Comprehension

About Priya

1. What does Priya need?
A call about the new cards project.
2. What time is the call in Mumbai, according to Priya?
Five thirty in the afternoon.
3. What day does Priya say "See you" for?
Wednesday.

Listening 2 · Calls with Brazil

Tom and the Clock

Tom, the developer in London, talks about his calls with Brazil. Sound first — no text.

1. In the winter, how many hours ahead of São Paulo is London?
Three hours.
2. What time are Tom's calls with Brazil, London time?
At two in the afternoon.
3. When does Tom never have calls?
On Fridays in the afternoon.
