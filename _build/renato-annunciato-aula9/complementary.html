Lesson 9 -- Opening the Presentation

Series
Abstract: The Art of Design -- Season 1, Episode 1

Every episode is one person explaining their own work to people who do not do that work. They open the same way you are learning to open: what I do, what you are about to see, and the one idea that holds it together. The English is slow, the speakers are from everywhere, and almost none of them are native.

Tip: watch with English subtitles and stop after the first three minutes. Write down how the person introduced themselves and what they promised to show. That is your opening, in somebody else's field.

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart -- Create a Presence: How to Communicate in a Way Others Can Feel

A free episode from the Stanford business school on the thing you cannot put on a slide: how you occupy the room in the first minute. It is about attention and delivery, not vocabulary, which is exactly the part of the presentation that your English level does not decide.

Tip: listen once with no transcript. Then pick one thing he suggests, and record your own opening again using it. Compare the two recordings back to back — the difference is usually louder than you expect.

Listen on Stanford GSB ↗
YouTube
Nancy Duarte -- The Common Structure of the Greatest Communicators (TEDxEast)

Eighteen minutes taking famous speeches apart and finding the same shape underneath all of them: where we are now, where we could be, and the gap between the two. It is the grown-up version of the four moves you built today, and it explains why the headline has to come before the explanation.

Tip: watch it once for the argument. Then map your own opening onto her structure and ask yourself one question: in my three minutes, where exactly is the gap?
