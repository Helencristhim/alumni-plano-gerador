Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

Opening the Presentation -- The First Three Minutes

The three minutes that decide whether the room listens to the other twenty. Key words: agenda, to walk through, outline, takeaway, to signpost, handout, backup slide, the floor, Q and A, to recap, timing, headline. Structures: gerunds and infinitives, and the four moves of an opening.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the definition. These twelve words are about the room, not about your business.

Agenda -- the list of points a meeting or presentation will cover, in order
"The agenda has three points, and the numbers come first."
To walk through -- to take someone through something step by step, so they can follow it
"I will walk you through the quarter in three parts."
Outline -- a short plan of what you are going to say, given before you say it
"The outline is the second thing you say, right after your name."
Takeaway -- the one point you want the room to remember afterwards
"If there is one takeaway today, it is that the interior is the gap."
To signpost -- to say where you are going next, in one short sentence
"Signpost every section, and nobody in the room gets lost."
Handout -- a page you give people to keep, with the detail that is not on the screen
"The full account list is in the handout, not on the slide."
Backup slide -- an extra slide kept at the end, only for a question you expect
"I have a backup slide with the clinic numbers, in case she asks."
The floor -- the right to speak in a meeting
"Thank you for the floor. I will be quick."
Q and A -- the part at the end where the room asks and you answer
"I would like to keep the Q and A for the last five minutes."
To recap -- to say the main points again, shortly, at the end
"Let me recap: the growth is real, and the interior is the gap."
Timing -- how long each part takes, and whether you finish on time
"My timing was wrong: six minutes on the introduction alone."
Headline -- the one number or one message you lead with, before any detail
"The headline is that we have grown in both regions."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the preparation note and answer the questions. Notice the word that comes just before each verb in bold.

Renato is preparing the presentation he will give to the global director next month. He has decided to open with the headline instead of the background, because Claire suggested starting with the number. The agenda has three points, and he plans to signpost each one so that nobody gets lost. He will walk the room through the quarter, the interior of Sao Paulo, and the decision he needs.

He is responsible for presenting the region on his own, and he is used to doing this in Portuguese, never in English. He has avoided writing a full script, because a colleague warned him about reading from a page. Instead he practises the outline until the order is automatic, and he keeps a backup slide at the end for the one question he is afraid of. The detail that does not fit on the screen goes into the handout.

He wants to keep the Q and A for the last five minutes, and he intends to recap the takeaway just before it. He is worried about his timing, so he looks forward to rehearsing the opening one more time with his teacher.

1. Why is it "he has decided to open" but "Claire suggested starting"?
A Because some verbs take to + verb (decide, want, plan) and others take verb + ing (suggest, avoid, keep).
B Because the first sentence is about the future and the second is about the past.
C Because "open" is a short verb and "start" is a long one.
2. Why is it "responsible for presenting" and not "responsible for present"?
A Because "responsible" is an adjective and adjectives always take -ing.
B Because "for" is a preposition, and after any preposition the verb takes -ing.
C Because the action is happening right now.
3. "He looks forward to rehearsing the opening." Why is it not "to rehearse"?
A Because in "look forward to" the word "to" is a preposition, not part of the verb, so it takes -ing.
B Because "rehearse" is irregular.
C Because the sentence is a question.
4. Which sentence would you write at the end of an email to the global director?
A "I look forward to present the results to you next month."
B "I look forward to presenting the results to you next month."
C "I look forward for to present the results to you next month."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Gerunds and Infinitives

GRAMMAR

One question decides the shape: what kind of word comes immediately before the second verb?

What comes beforeShape of the second verbExample
Certain verbsto + verb — want, decide, need, plan, agree, hope, offer, refuse, learn, intendWe decided to open with the headline.
Other verbsverb + ing — enjoy, avoid, finish, keep, suggest, mind, consider, practiseShe suggested keeping the Q and A for the end.
Any prepositionverb + ing, always — for, before, after, without, by, in, at, of, aboutI am responsible for managing two regions.
The trap: to as a prepositionlook forward to · be used to · object to — here to is a preposition, so it takes -ing: I look forward to presenting.
Both, same meaningstart, begin, continue, like, love, hate — "I like presenting" and "I like to present" are both correct.
After an adjectiveto + verb — ready, happy, difficult, easy, afraidI am ready to start.
Two trapsNever for to come — a preposition never sits next to to. And never responsible for manage — after a preposition the verb always wears -ing.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

The global director gives you the floor. Your first sentence is:
A "Before I start, sorry in advance for my English."
B "Good morning, everyone, and thank you for the floor."
C "First let me give you some background on the market."
You want the room to know what is coming. You say:
A "I will walk you through three things today."
B "I will walk you for three things today."
C "I will pass three things today."
Where does the headline go?
A After the detail that explains it, so the room understands it.
B Before any detail, in one sentence.
C On the last slide, with the takeaway.
You are ending an email to the director. You write:
A "I look forward to present the results next month."
B "I look forward to presenting the results next month."
C "I look forward presenting the results next month."
Somebody asks for a number that is not on your slides, but you prepared for it. You say:
A "I have that on a backup slide. Let me show you."
