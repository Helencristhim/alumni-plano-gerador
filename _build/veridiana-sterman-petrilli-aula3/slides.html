Chapter 3: The Code

The Problem Is Still Open

Four small words that say where you are right now

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"My bag has not arrived yet."

"I have already filed a claim."

"They have just told me it is in Panama."

"It is nine in the evening and I still have not received it."

Every red group is the same shape: have or has plus one form of the verb. Not one of these sentences says when. Now look at the blue words, and at where each one sits.

JobFormExample
A problem that is still openhave / has + past participleMy bag has not arrived.
A minute agohave + just + participleThey have just told me.
Sooner than expectedhave + already + participleI have already filed a claim.
Not up to nowyet, at the end, in a negative or a questionIt has not arrived yet. Has it arrived yet?
Not up to now, and I am tired of waitingstill, before have notI still have not received it.
QuestionHave / Has + subject + participleHave you filed a claim yet?
The trapa finished time takes the simple pastNot I have filed it yesterday. Say I filed it yesterday.

In one line: no when, no simple past. If the problem is still open, English says have plus the participle.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

At the Baggage Desk

One conversation, two recordings, one document

Dialogue

Forty Minutes at the Carousel

A
Good evening. How can I help you?
V
My bag has not arrived yet. I have waited at the carousel for forty minutes, and the belt has stopped.
A
I am sorry about that. Have you already filed a claim?
V
Not yet. I have just come from baggage claim. This is my boarding pass, and this is the tag from my hand luggage.
A
Thank you. I have found it. Your bag is still in Panama City — it has missed the connection, so it is delayed, not missing.
V
That is a relief. So when does it arrive? We are only here for ten days, and everything I need is inside it.
A
Tomorrow at eleven, and we deliver it to your hotel. I have put the address in the system, and here is your reference number, so you can track it online.
V
Thank you. And what am I entitled to tonight? I have nothing with me, and the shops have already closed.
Comprehension

About the Agent

1. What is the first thing he asks her, before he looks anything up?
Whether she has already filed a claim.
2. Where does he say the bag is, and which word does he use for the situation?
Still in Panama City. It has missed the connection, so it is delayed, not missing.
3. What three things does he do or give her before she leaves the desk?
He tells her it arrives tomorrow at eleven and will be delivered to the hotel, he puts the address in the system, and he gives her a reference number so she can track it online.
Listening 1 · Baggage Services

Before You Leave the Airport

A recorded message at the baggage services desk. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the message say you must not do, and what must you bring to the desk?
Do not leave the airport before you speak to an agent. Bring your boarding pass and the tag from your hand luggage.
2. Why does the message say the reference number matters so much?
Without it nobody can track the bag, and every call starts again from zero.
3. What are you entitled to after twenty-four hours, and what do you have to keep?
You are entitled to buy what you need for the night, and you have to keep every receipt.
Listening 2 · A Travel Journalist

The First Hour Decides Everything

A journalist on what she does when a bag goes missing. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she open the file before she leaves the airport?
Because a claim opened after you go home is a much slower claim.
2. What is the one question she says almost nobody asks, and what is the usual answer?
What am I entitled to tonight? The answer is usually a small allowance for clothes and something to wash with, and the airline will not offer it unless you ask.
3. What is the last piece of advice, and since when has she followed it?
Put one change of clothes in your hand luggage. She has not travelled without doing that since two thousand and nineteen.

No keywords, no notes, sixty seconds. You are speaking for two people.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I can report a problem that is still happening, without using the simple past.
I use just, already, yet and still in the right place in the sentence.
I switch to the simple past as soon as I say when it happened.
I ask for a reference number and I ask what I am entitled to, without waiting to be offered.
I know the words: baggage claim, a carousel, delayed, missing, a reference number, to file a claim, to track, hand luggage, to be entitled to, compensation.
