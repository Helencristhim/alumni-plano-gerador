Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A risk-adjusted return -- the profit an investment makes, measured against how much risk it carries
"On a risk-adjusted return, the brownfield road beats the new port."
A leverage ratio -- how much of a project is paid for with debt compared with equity
"A leverage ratio of seventy to thirty is normal for a transmission line."
Subordinated debt -- a loan that is repaid only after the main lenders have been paid
"Subordinated debt pays more because it waits in line."
An equity stake -- the share of a company that an investor owns
"The fund took a twenty-five percent equity stake in the operator."
Blended finance -- mixing public or philanthropic money with private money so that a project becomes investable
"Blended finance made the water project possible."
A de-risking mechanism -- any tool, such as a guarantee or insurance, that reduces the risk an investor carries
"The currency guarantee was the de-risking mechanism the fund needed."
An off-take agreement -- a long contract in which a buyer agrees in advance to purchase what a project will produce
"The solar farm signed a twenty-year off-take agreement before construction started."
Credit enhancement -- support, such as a guarantee, that makes a loan look safer to lenders
"The development bank guarantee worked as credit enhancement for the bond."
Concessional lending -- loans offered on better terms than the market, usually by development banks
"Concessional lending covered the first ten years of the project."
Yield compression -- the fall in returns that happens when too many investors want the same kind of asset
"Yield compression has made brownfield toll roads expensive."
Fiduciary duty -- the legal obligation to act in the best interest of the people whose money you manage
"Her fiduciary duty is to the teachers who will retire on this fund."
Capital deployment -- the process of actually investing the money a fund has raised
"Capital deployment in Latin America was slower than the fund promised."
A currency hedge -- a contract that protects an investor from losses when exchange rates move
"Without a currency hedge, a strong dollar can erase the whole return."
An anchor investor -- the first large investor whose commitment convinces others to join
"Once the pension fund signed as anchor investor, three more funds followed."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Read any investor briefing carefully and you will notice two kinds of sentences. The first kind tells you who made something happen. The second kind lets the writer stay silent about it. Investors read both, and they always ask about the second.

The first kind uses the causative. When you arrange for an expert to do something, you use have + object + past participle: “We had the financial model audited.” When you push, negotiate or persuade to make it happen, get is more natural: “The operator got the tariff approved after nine months.” If you want to name the expert, add by: “We had the deal structured by a local bank as blended finance.”

The same structure describes something that happens to you: “The fund had its rating downgraded,” which no fund wants. And when a person does the work, the verb changes shape: have + person + base verb (“We had the bank run the numbers”) but get + person + to + verb (“We got a development bank to provide credit enhancement”).

The second kind is the passive: “The concession was extended.” It is correct, and it is useful when the agent does not matter. But in a room full of people with a fiduciary duty, the agent usually matters. The sentence that hides who decided is the sentence that gets the question. And one form does not exist at all: “We made the contract to be reviewed” is Portuguese wearing an English suit.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Causative and the Passive

GRAMMAR

Have and get tell the room who made it happen; the passive lets you stay silent about it.

JobFormExample
You arranged a servicehave + object + past participleWe had the model audited.
You pushed to make it happenget + object + past participleThey got the tariff approved.
Name the expert... + by + agentWe had it structured by a local bank.
Stay silent about the agentbe + past participleThe tariff was approved in May.
A person does it (have)have + person + base verbWe had the bank run the numbers.
A person does it (get)get + person + to + verbWe got the ministry to publish the date.

Never: we made the contract to be reviewed · the fund had reviewed its exposure by an adviser · we got the ministry publish the date · a return adjusted to the risk

Stage 2: Put the Briefing in Order

Order

A sovereign wealth fund manager has two minutes. Put your briefing in a logical order.

?"What we are offering is a twenty-five percent equity stake to one anchor investor."
?"The revenue is secured by a twenty-year off-take agreement."
?"The debt is seventy percent: a senior loan and a subordinated tranche."
?"We got a development bank to provide a partial guarantee as credit enhancement."
?"And we had the financial model audited by an independent firm in June."
?"The one open point is the cost of the currency hedge, which we would like to discuss with you."
