Complementary Activities

Optional extras to enjoy outside class. Mark each one as done after you watch or listen to it.

Talk
Mariana Mazzucato — “What is economic value, and who creates it?” (TED)

An economist on how public and private money actually create value together — the argument underneath every blended finance deal and every conversation about who should carry the risk.

Exam task (Paper 3, Part 2): watch once with no subtitles and write your own eight sentence-completion notes. Then watch again with subtitles and count how many you paraphrased instead of writing the words she actually said. That count is your Part 2 score.

Podcast
Freakonomics Radio — “Are E.S.G. Investors Actually Helping the Environment?”

How large funds decide where capital goes, and why labels and returns do not always point the same way. The vocabulary of fund managers, spoken at natural speed.

Exam task (agency): open the transcript on the same page and find five passives with no agent. Restore the agent in each. Two of them will resist, and the reason they resist is the lesson.

Listen on Freakonomics ↗
Talk
Ray Dalio — “How to build a company where the best ideas win” (TED)

The founder of one of the largest investment firms on how decisions get made when money is on the line — a close look at how investors think before they say yes.

Exam task (Paper 1, Part 4): choose ninety seconds and transform three of his sentences, keeping the meaning and changing the structure. Three to eight words each, and read both versions aloud.

The register lab · 20 minutes, any day this week

Three short tasks on material you already read for work. No new sources, no screen time you were not going to spend anyway.

  1. Hunt the missing agent. Take any term sheet, memo or press release that reaches you this week. Mark every action reported with no author. For each one, write the question you would ask on the call.
  2. The two-sentence test. Take one fact from that document and write it twice: once naming the party who acted, once hiding them. Then decide which one you would send, and be able to say why.
  3. Read it as the fund reads it. Give any article you read this week one pass for the argument and one pass for the queue: who is paid first, who waits, and what protects the person waiting. Two minutes each pass.
