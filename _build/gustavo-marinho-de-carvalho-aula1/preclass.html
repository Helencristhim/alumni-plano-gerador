Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To get in touch -- to contact someone by phone, email or message
"I got in touch with them on Monday about the new contract."
A supplier -- a company that sells goods or services to another company
"They changed supplier last year, and the service is better now."
To catch up -- to talk about what has happened since you last spoke
"Before the numbers, let us catch up for a few minutes."
Straightforward -- simple and clear, with nothing complicated in it
"The process is straightforward: you send the order and we confirm it."
A deadline -- the day or hour when a piece of work has to be finished
"The deadline is Friday, and they never move it."
To be on the same page -- to understand a situation in the same way as the other person
"Let me repeat the three points, so we are on the same page."
A follow-up -- a short message or meeting that comes after a first contact
"I will send a follow-up with the three points we agreed on."
To handle -- to be responsible for something and take care of it
"I handle the international side, so you can write to me directly."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A client you have never met opens the call and asks two questions. They sound like the same question and they are not. The first one is: what does your company do? We work with clients in several countries. I handle the international side. That answer was true last year, it is true today and it will be true in June. English marks it with the plain form of the verb, and nothing in the sentence says anything about this week.

The second question is: what are you working on at the moment? This week I am putting together the proposal for your team. At the moment we are reviewing the timeline for the first quarter. Both sentences are true now and both will stop being true, and English marks that with am, is or are in front and -ing behind. This is the shape a Brazilian speaker uses least in a work call, because in Portuguese the plain present covers the two jobs on its own.

Then there is a third group, and it is where the most expensive mistake lives. I agree with you. I think the deadline is realistic. I need the payment terms in writing. These are not actions at all: they are what goes on inside your head, and they never take -ing. And agree is already the verb, so I am agree does not exist in English, in any tense. If you say it on a call with a client, nothing breaks and everybody understands you — but it is the one sentence that makes a fluent speaker sound like a beginner, and it costs nothing to fix.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple vs Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

The shape you pick is not grammar. It is how long the sentence is going to stay true.

JobFormExample
What the business always doespresent simpleWe work with clients in several countries.
What your job is, in generalpresent simpleI handle the international side.
What is on your desk this weekam / is / are + verb-ingThis week I am putting together the proposal.
A change that is happening nowam / is / are + verb-ingCosts are going up again this year.
What goes on inside your headpresent simple, never -ingI think it is a good idea. I agree.
The signal wordsusually, every week / this week, at the momentUsually I travel in March. This year I am staying here.
The trapagree is already the verbNot I am agree. Say I agree.

Never: I am agree with you · Every week I am answering emails · At the moment we test a new partner · I am thinking that it is a good idea

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The client asks what your company does. You answer:
A We are working with clients in several countries.
B We work with clients in several countries, and I handle the international side.
C My company is working with clients since many years.
The client asks what you are working on at the moment. The strongest answer is:
A This week I am putting together your proposal, and we are reviewing the timeline for the first quarter.
B This week I put together your proposal and we review the timeline for the first quarter.
C I am working always with proposals and partners.
You want to say that you have the same understanding as the client. You say:
A I am agree with you completely.
B Let me repeat the three dates, so we are on the same page.
C We are thinking the same thing at this moment.
The client opens the call with two minutes about the weather and the weekend. The best move is:
A Bring her back to the agenda straight away, because her time is expensive.
B Answer, add something about your own week, and ask her a question back.
C Say nothing and wait, because it is not part of the business.

Reflection
