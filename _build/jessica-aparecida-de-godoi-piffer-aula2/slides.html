Chapter 4: The Two Tenses

Same Facts, Different Sentence

The choice you have been making by ear

Grammar Discovery

What Does the Second One Tell You?

"She worked at the firm for twenty years."  /  "She has worked at the firm for twenty years."

"I sent the report on Tuesday."  /  "I have sent the report."

"We found three discrepancies last quarter."  /  "We have found three discrepancies this quarter."

"He did not answer yesterday."  /  "He has not answered yet."

In every pair, the blue sentence closes the door on a finished period. The red one leaves it open onto now.

QuestionPast simplePresent perfect
Is the period finished?yes — last quarter, in 2020, yesterdayno — this quarter, today, so far
Is she still there?She worked there for twenty years. (she left)She has worked there for twenty years. (still there)
Does the moment matter?I sent it on Tuesday. (when it happened)I have sent it. (it is done, that is all)
Still waiting?He did not answer yesterday.He has not answered yet.
Signal wordsago, last, in 2020, yesterday, thensince, for, already, yet, so far, ever
In a storythe events, in orderthe result the story leaves behind
The trapNever put a finished time word with the perfect. Not I have sent it on Tuesday.

In one line: the past simple closes the period. The present perfect keeps it open onto now.

Listening 1 · A Forensic Auditor

What a Discrepancy Actually Looks Like

Twenty-two years of reviews, and the part that never changes. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say people expect a discrepancy to look like, and what does it actually look like?
People expect a large missing amount, and it is almost always something small that repeats.
2. What was the case with the parking receipts?
Eleven dollars, claimed twice a week for two years, and it led to something much larger.
3. What does he say he has never once seen in twenty-two years?
He has never seen a single large discrepancy that had no small one before it.
Listening 2 · Science Radio

Why Invented Numbers Never Look Right

A short radio piece on the pattern nobody can fake. Sound first — no text.

1. What did the physicist notice about the book of tables?
The first pages were far more worn than the last ones, which meant people looked up numbers beginning with one far more often.
2. What happens when people invent numbers, according to the piece?
They spread the first digits evenly, because that feels random, and real data never does that.
3. What does the piece say the test cannot do?
It cannot tell you who did it or why, only that the shape of the data is wrong.
Real Document

The Email, Verb by Verb

To: r.keller@headoffice.com · Cc: finance-br
Subject: Q2 review — two open items

Hi Rachel,

I have finished the second-quarter review and I have found two items that do not close.

The first one came up in the April file, and we corrected it the same week. The second one is still open. We have not identified the source yet, and I would rather tell you now than in July.

I have already asked the team to pull the original documents. I will write again on Friday.

Best,
Jessica

Seven verbs. Two of them are in the past simple, and both of those sentences name a finished moment.
1. Which two verbs are in the past simple, and what do those two sentences have in common?
"Came" and "corrected". Both sentences name a finished moment — the April file, and the same week.
2. Why is "we have not identified the source yet" not in the past simple?
Because the search is still open. "Yet" says the period has not closed.
3. What does "I have already asked the team" tell Rachel that a past simple would not?
That the action is done and its result is in place now, so she does not need to ask for it.
