Numberphile — “Number 1 and Benford’s Law”

Nine minutes on the pattern behind the second listening of this lesson: why roughly thirty percent of real numbers begin with the digit one, and why invented numbers never do. British and American speakers, unscripted, at natural speed.

Tip: watch once for the idea, then again with subtitles and write down every sentence where the speaker uses the present perfect. There are more than you expect.

Criminal — “The Caller” (episode 369)

An investigation told the way this lesson tells a career: what happened on specific days, in the past simple, and what all of it has left behind, in the present perfect. Thirty minutes at native speed, with no simplification.

Tip: listen once for the story. Then find the last two minutes, where the narrator stops telling events and starts telling consequences, and notice that the tense changes with her.

Talk
Pamela Meyer — “How to spot a liar” (TED)

Eighteen minutes from a fraud investigator on the small signals that do not match the story — the human version of a discrepancy. She talks fast, uses the exact vocabulary of this lesson, and makes an argument you can disagree with, which is the point.

Tip: pick three claims she makes and decide whether you believe them. Then say why, out loud, in English, in three sentences each.
