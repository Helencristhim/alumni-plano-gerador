The Numbers Tell a Story -- Saying Data Like an Investor

“We grew a lot” says nothing. “A thirty percent increase in revenue” says a number someone can check. Key words: a trajectory, EBITDA, a run rate, an addressable market, capital allocation, year-over-year, to break even, to plateau. Structure: noun phrases for data (an increase in, a decline in) and nominalization.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A trajectory -- the direction in which something develops over time
“Our growth trajectory has been stable for five years.”
EBITDA -- profit before interest, taxes and some accounting costs
“Our EBITDA margin improved to eighteen percent last year.”
A run rate -- what a company will earn in a year if the current month continues
“Our run rate is now ninety million reais a year.”
An addressable market -- all the possible sales in a market for a product
“Our addressable market in Latin America is still largely untapped.”
Capital allocation -- the decision about where a company invests its money
“Capital allocation went mostly to our AI team.”
Year-over-year -- compared with the same period in the previous year
“We saw a twenty percent year-over-year increase in revenue.”
To break even -- to earn exactly as much as you spend
“The new unit broke even in its second year.”
To plateau -- to stop growing and stay at the same level
“Revenue plateaued in 2023, and then it grew again.”

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

In a data room, every founder has the same numbers on the screen. What changes is how he says them. A weak sentence sounds like this: we grew a lot, and costs went down. A strong sentence sounds like this: a thirty percent year-over-year increase in revenue, and a reduction in operating costs. The second one gives the investor a number she can check.

The tool is the noun phrase. Instead of a verb (grow, fall, reduce), you use a noun (growth, a decline, a reduction) and add the details around it. The pattern is a / an + number + noun + in + what: an increase in EBITDA, a decline in margins, a twelve percent rise in the run rate. Notice the preposition: it is almost always in, not of.

This change from verb to noun is called nominalization, and it makes the number the subject of the sentence. We reduced costs, so we broke even becomes the reduction in costs allowed us to break even. We grew fast becomes our growth trajectory has been stable for five years. The sentence sounds less like a story about you and more like evidence.

Use it for the important numbers, not for every sentence. A good data slide has three or four noun phrases and one clear conclusion: revenue plateaued in 2023, but the increase in the run rate since then shows that the trajectory is back. The numbers do the talking; your job is to give them a clear shape.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Noun Phrases for Data

GRAMMAR

Turn the verb into a noun, put the number in front, and use in for what changed.

PatternFormExample
Change + whata / an + noun + in + whatan increase in EBITDA · a decline in margins
Number + changea + number + noun + ina thirty percent increase in revenue
Comparison+ year-over-yeara twenty percent year-over-year increase
Verb to noungrow → growth · reduce → reductionThe reduction in costs allowed us to break even.
Noun as subjectthe + noun phrase + verbOur growth trajectory has been stable.
Adding detailnoun + relative clause (Lesson 10)the addressable market that we have not reached yet
Trend verbsto plateau · to break evenRevenue plateaued in 2023.

Remember: a noun phrase with a number takes a singular verb when the head is singular: a thirty percent increase in sales shows… (the increase shows).
