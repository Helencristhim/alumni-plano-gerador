Chapter 3: The Code

A Question Inside a Question

Could you tell me · I was wondering whether · Do you mind if I ask

Grammar Discovery

Where Did the Question Order Go?

"Could you tell me how you chose the artists?"

"I was wondering whether you ever choose somebody you do not fully understand."

"Do you mind if I ask what the budget was?"

"When you say still changing, what do you mean exactly?"

The blue part is the polite opener. After it, the red part keeps the order of a normal statement.

RuleFormExample
Opener + question word + statement orderno do, does or did after the openerCould you tell me how you chose the artists?
A yes/no question goes inside with if or whetheropener + whether + subject + verbI was wondering whether you work with sound.
The verb comes after its subjectnot what was the budgetDo you mind if I ask what the budget was?
The punctuation follows the openera statement opener takes a periodI was wondering whether you are free on Friday.
The follow-up picks up their wordWhen you say X, what do you mean? · Such as? · In what sense?When you say risk, what do you mean exactly?
The trapexplain + to + personCould you explain to me why, not explain me why

In one line: wrap the question politely, then say the rest like a statement; follow up with their own words.

Chapter 4: Real Conversations

You Ask, They Answer

One studio visit, two recordings, one interview brief

Dialogue

The Answer Behind the Answer

L
Thank you for making time. Could you tell me how you chose the six artists for the Lisbon show?
M
Mostly through studio visits. I look for people whose work is still changing.
L
When you say still changing, what do you mean exactly? I would like to clarify that.
M
That they have not settled on a style yet. The risk is part of the show.
L
I was wondering whether you ever choose somebody whose work you do not fully understand. I do not want to put you on the spot.
M
That is a candid question. Honestly, yes. Two of the six this year.
L
Could you elaborate on what made you take that risk?
M
Their questions were better than their answers. That is usually enough for me.
Comprehension

About Marcus

1. How does Marcus usually choose artists, and what does he look for?
Mostly through studio visits. He looks for people whose work is still changing.
2. What does Marcus mean by still changing, and how does he see the risk?
That they have not settled on a style yet. He says the risk is part of the show.
3. How many artists did Marcus choose without fully understanding their work, and why?
Two of the six this year, because their questions were better than their answers.
Listening 1 · An Oral Historian

The First Answer Is Almost Never the One

Somebody who records long interviews with nurses, on what she does after the first answer. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does she say the first answer is almost never the interesting one?
Because it is the one people have given before, at dinners and in meetings. It is polished and it is safe.
2. What is the best follow-up usually made of, and what example does she give?
The other person’s own words. When a nurse says the night shift was hard, she asks: when you say hard, what did it look like at three in the morning?
3. What does she advise after a good question, and what should you not do?
Stop talking and wait, because people need a few seconds of silence before they say the real thing. Do not rescue them with another question.
Listening 2 · An Art Critic

Two Questions I Stopped Asking

Somebody who interviews artists for a living, on the questions that get nothing. Sound first — no text.

1. Which two kinds of question has he stopped asking, and what is wrong with the first?
What inspired you, and leading questions. The first is so open that it invites a prepared answer: he gets the paragraph from the press release.
2. What happens when he asks a leading question such as whether the work is about loss?
A polite artist simply agrees with him, which tells him nothing.
3. What does he do when somebody dodges, and when does he stop?
He rephrases the question once, gently, without accusing them. If they dodge it a second time, that is the answer.
Real Document

The Brief You Take Into the Room

STUDIO VISIT · INTERVIEW BRIEF
Prepared by Liliane Meira · 40 minutes, recorded
GuestMarcus Hale, curator, Lisbon
FormatStudio visit, then questions
GoalHow the six artists were chosen
RuleOne follow-up for every answer
Open: Could you tell me how the show began? — Follow-up: When you say began, do you mean the idea or the funding?
Core: I was wondering whether any of the six surprised you. — Follow-up: Could you elaborate on that?
Close: Is there a question you expected me to ask, and I did not?
1. Why does every prepared question on the brief have a follow-up under it?
Because the first answer is rarely the interesting one. The follow-up picks up the curator’s own word and asks for the second answer.
2. Which two questions are indirect, and how can you tell from the word order?
Could you tell me how the show began, and I was wondering whether any of the six surprised you. After the opener the words are in statement order: how the show began, not how did the show begin.
3. What is the closing question for?
To give the curator room to say what matters to him, including the thing she did not know to ask about.
