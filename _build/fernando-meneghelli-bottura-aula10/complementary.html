Lesson 10 -- Milestone: The Last Five Minutes

Grammar Video
"Indirect questions" -- BBC Learning English, 6 Minute Grammar

Six minutes on the form you will use for your three closing questions. Connection to Lesson 10: after the opening phrase the sentence stops behaving like a question, and Portuguese keeps the same order in both — which is exactly why this mistake survives at advanced level.

Tip: rebuild your three closing questions after each example, out loud, checking one thing only: is there an auxiliary where there should not be? Six minutes of video, six minutes of speaking.

Talk with transcript
"Curiosity is your superpower" -- Spencer Harrison and Jon Cohen, TED (12 min)

Twelve minutes of research on why the questions somebody asks tell you more about them than the answers they give. Connection to Lesson 10: the last five minutes of an interview are the only part you control, and what you choose to be curious about is the sentence the panel repeats. Full transcript beside the video.

Tip: after watching, write the three questions you would ask a company you actually want to work for — not generic ones. If any of the three would work for any employer, it is not a good question.

Executive Interview
"Marc Rowan, CEO of Apollo" -- In Good Company (45 min)

Forty-five minutes of a chief executive being asked increasingly specific questions and never once losing the thread. Useful now, after the simulation, as a benchmark rather than a lesson: this is what a completely unhurried answer sounds like, including the pauses.

Tip: listen for how long he takes before starting an answer. It is longer than you would expect, and nobody in the room minds. That silence is the single easiest thing to copy.
