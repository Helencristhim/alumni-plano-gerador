Lesson 08 -- Pre-class

From Notes to Speech

A quick note turned into speech, with the audio of the result. Key words: bullet point, headline, takeaway, figure, highlight, concern, status, update. Structure: the connectors that organise an update -- first and foremost, as far as ... is concerned, moving on to, last but not least.

Stage 1: Start with the Text

GRAMMAR

Start with the text. Read it once before you look at anything else: the language of this lesson comes out of it.

Look at three lines from a real notebook. "Audit Tuesday closed no findings." "Onboarding live Monday, three countries." "Credit team volume up a third, one analyst two portfolios." Nothing there is a sentence. All three are bullet points, and they are perfectly good notes.

Now the same three lines as an update. "First and foremost, the audit is closed with no findings. Moving on to the highlight: the onboarding process went live on Monday in the three countries. As far as the credit team is concerned, I have one concern: the volume grew by a third and one analyst is covering two portfolios."

Three things changed. The notes became full sentences. The order became the order of importance, not the order of the week. And four connectors appeared, which tell the listener where you are: the most important point, the next item, a specific subject, the final one.

The first sentence deserves special attention. It is the headline, and a headline is a conclusion, not an event. "On Tuesday we had the audit" is an event. "The audit is closed with no findings" is a headline: it tells the listener how it ended, which is the only thing they need if they hear nothing else.

One warning about the t and d sounds that appear in all these words. In update, status, detail and digital, the tongue stays behind the teeth and the sound stops. When an extra sound slips in after the t, a listener on a video call hears a word that does not exist, and the sentence takes an extra second to arrive.

1. What is the difference between a headline and an event, according to the text?
A A headline is a conclusion; an event is what happened on a day.
B A headline is longer and includes the figures.
C A headline is written, and an event is spoken.
2. What order does an update follow?
A The order of importance.
B The order in which things happened during the week.
C The order of the agenda of the last meeting.
3. What do the connectors do in an update?
A They tell the listener where you are: most important, next item, specific subject, final point.
B They make the update longer and more formal.
C They replace the figures.

Order

Put the five moves of a two-minute update in the order a manager needs to hear them.

?Give the highlight: what went well.

?Give the headline: how the week ended, in one sentence.
?Stop, and let the questions come.

Stage 3: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

The words you have just met, one by one. Listen to each expression and read it in context: you already do all of this at work. Here it gets its English name.

takeaway -- what you want the listener to remember afterwards
"The takeaway for the committee is that turnover is falling."
concern -- something that worries you and that the listener should know
"My main concern is the workload in the credit team."
first and foremost -- used to introduce the most important point
"First and foremost, the audit is closed."
as far as ... is concerned -- used to move to a specific subject or country
"As far as Chile is concerned, the process is finished."
moving on to -- used to change to the next point in an update
"Moving on to Argentina, we have one open position."
last but not least -- used to introduce the final point, which still matters
"Last but not least, the works council meets next week."
note down -- to write something quickly so you do not forget it
"I note down three bullet points during every meeting."

Stage 5: Grammar Tip -- The four connectors of an update

GRAMMAR

Four expressions organise two minutes of speech. They never change, and each one appears once.

FormWhat it doesExample
first and foremostthe most important point; used onceFirst and foremost, the audit is closed.
as far as ... is concernedmove to one specific subject or countryAs far as Chile is concerned, the process is finished.
moving on tomove to the next itemMoving on to Argentina, we have one open position.
last but not leastthe final item, still importantLast but not least, the works council meets next week.
Watch outthe direct translations. "In first place" and "at last" do not work here: say first and foremost and last but not least.

Before your next update, write three lines and choose which connector opens each one.

Stage 8: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Real moments from a weekly update. Only one option is both correct and natural.

1. You are opening your update with the most important point. What do you say?
A In first place, the audit is closed with no findings.
B First and foremost, the audit is closed with no findings.
C At first, the audit is closed with no findings.
D Firstly of all, the audit is closed with no findings.
2. Which sentence moves correctly to a specific country?
A About Chile is concerned, the process is finished.
B As far as Chile is concerned, the process is finished.
C As far Chile concerns, the process is finished.
D Concerning as far as Chile, the process is finished.
3. Your manager asks how the week was. Which answer is a headline?
A On Tuesday we had the audit and on Monday the onboarding went live.
B The audit is closed with no findings and we end the quarter with one open position.
C There were four subjects this week and all of them are in my notes.
D It was a busy week, with many meetings and some problems.
4. Which sentence presents a concern professionally?
A The credit team is impossible, nobody can work like this.
B My main concern is the workload in the credit team: the volume grew by a third.
C I have a complaint about the credit team workload.
D The credit team has a problem but it is not my responsibility.
