A die-hard fan -- a supporter who stays loyal, even when the team loses

"We were over the moon when they won the title."
Electric -- full of excitement and energy, used for an atmosphere
"The atmosphere in the stadium was electric."
Infectious -- so strong that other people start to feel it too
"Her enthusiasm was infectious."
To live and breathe something -- to be completely dedicated to something you love
"She lives and breathes football."
To geek out -- to talk about something you love in great detail
"We geeked out about old vinyl for an hour."
A guilty pleasure -- something you enjoy but feel a little embarrassed about
"Old disco is my guilty pleasure."
To be a sucker for -- to find something impossible to resist
"I am a sucker for a good horn section."
To strike a chord -- to make someone feel a strong emotional connection
"That lyric really struck a chord with me."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Fiona Reid is writing a column about passion. In São Paulo, a friend who is hooked on soul music talked about his records for an hour. In Portuguese, his enthusiasm was absolutely infectious. In English, he only said, “This music is very good.” It was such a strange moment.

Fiona explains why. Good is a normal adjective: something can be a little good or extremely good. Brilliant is an extreme adjective: it is already at the top, so we say absolutely brilliant, never very brilliant. Really works with both.

In Liverpool, a die-hard fan told her the stadium is so electric that it gives him goosebumps. Notice: so + adjective, but such a + adjective + noun.

1. Why does the friend say "very good" and not "very brilliant"?
A Because good can be stronger or weaker, but brilliant is already extreme, so it never takes very.
B Because brilliant is a word that only British people use in conversation, so Americans prefer very good.
C Because very brilliant is more polite.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A It was so a great night.
B It was such a great night.
C It was such great a night, and everybody in the bar was singing along with the band.
3. Which intensifier works with BOTH normal and extreme adjectives?
A Absolutely, because it is the strongest intensifier and it always makes any adjective sound more natural.
B Very
C Really

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Very or Absolutely?

GRAMMAR

A bigger feeling needs a bigger adjective, not just a bigger intensifier.

FormYou use it withExample
very / extremely / incrediblynormal adjectives: good, loud, tired, happyThe bar was extremely loud.
absolutely / utterly / totallyextreme adjectives: brilliant, amazing, exhausted, deafeningThe record is absolutely brilliant.
reallyboth kindsreally good, really amazing
so + adjective / such (a) + adjective + nounemphasis, often with a result (that ...)so electric that nobody sat down; such a great night

Key point: never put very before an extreme adjective: say "absolutely amazing", never "very amazing". And never put so before a noun: say "such a good game", never "so a good game".
