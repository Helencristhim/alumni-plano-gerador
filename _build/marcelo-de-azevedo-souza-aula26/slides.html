Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

Every sentence makes a feeling stronger. Why does each highlighted word fit its adjective?

A. "The music was very good."

B. "The record was absolutely brilliant."

C. "After the match I was totally exhausted."

D. "It was such a strange moment."

Grammar Rule

Which Word Makes It Stronger?

FormYou use it withExample
very / extremely / incrediblynormal adjectives that can be stronger or weaker: good, loud, tired, happyThe bar was extremely loud.
absolutely / utterly / totallyextreme adjectives that are already at the top: brilliant, amazing, exhausted, deafening, thrilledThe record was absolutely brilliant.
reallyboth kinds: the safe choice in conversationreally good, really amazing
so + adjective (that ...)
such (a) + adjective + noun		emphasis, often with a resultIt was so electric that nobody sat down. It was such a great night.

Key idea: a bigger feeling needs a bigger adjective, not just a bigger intensifier. Never "very brilliant" or "very exhausted": an extreme adjective is already at the top. And so goes before an adjective alone, such before a noun: "so good", "such a good game".

Dialogue

Passion on the Record

F
Marcelo, my next column is about passion. So tell me, honestly: what are you really hooked on?
M
Old soul, without a doubt. I have been a sucker for a good horn section since I was fifteen. Some of those records still give me goosebumps.
F
That is lovely. And football? A friend told me you are a die-hard fan.
M
I am. When my team won the title, I was over the moon. The stadium was so electric that nobody sat down for ninety minutes. Sorry, I am getting carried away.
F
Do not apologize. Your enthusiasm is infectious. But for the column I need the why. Why does soul strike a chord with you?
M
Because it is utterly honest. Those singers lived and breathed every word. It was such a different world from mine, and yet it felt like home.

Self-Assessment

What I Learned

I use very or extremely with normal adjectives: very good, extremely loud.
I use absolutely, utterly or totally with extreme adjectives: absolutely brilliant.
I use so before an adjective and such before a noun.
I can talk about soul and football with words like hooked on, goosebumps and die-hard fan.
I can explain why I love something, not only that I love it.
