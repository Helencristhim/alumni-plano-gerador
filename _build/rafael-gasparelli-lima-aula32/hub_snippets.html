Closing Deals -- The Final Negotiation

How to negotiate the last points of a deal: make an offer without a promise, ask for something back, and close. Key words: deal-breaker, concession, to meet halfway, bottom line, to walk away, upfront, term sheet, to close a deal. Structure: the second conditional to negotiate (If you lowered the price, we would sign today).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example.

Deal-breaker -- a problem so big that it stops the agreement
"The price is not a deal-breaker for us."
Concession -- something you give or accept to help the other side agree
"We made one concession on the date."
To meet halfway -- to accept a solution in the middle, between two positions
"We can meet halfway on the delivery date."
Bottom line -- the most important point, or the lowest price you accept
"Our bottom line is two million."
To walk away -- to leave a negotiation without an agreement
"If they change the terms, we will walk away."
Upfront -- paid or done at the start, before the rest
"They will pay forty percent upfront."
Term sheet -- a short document with the main points of a deal
"Let us put the main points in a term sheet."
To close a deal -- to finish a negotiation and make the agreement final
"We closed the deal on Friday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

It is the last meeting. Claire asks for a lower price. Rafael does not say yes. He says: “If we lowered the price, would you pay forty percent upfront?”

The price is not lower yet. Rafael uses the past (lowered) and would because he is imagining a situation. It is an offer, not a promise. This is the second conditional.

Then he says his limit: “Our bottom line is seven percent. If you accepted seven, we would sign today.” On the date, he is flexible: “We could meet halfway.” Claire agrees, and they close the deal.

"If we lowered the price..." Is the price lower now?
A No. Rafael is imagining a situation to test the offer.
B Yes. They lowered the price last week, after the due diligence, because of the lawsuit.
C We do not know.
Why does Rafael say "would you pay forty percent upfront?"
A Because he wants to finish the meeting quickly and go home, and he does not care about the money.
B To give a concession for free.
C To ask for something back.
Which sentence is correct?
A "If you would lower the price, we would sign the contract today in our office in São Paulo."
B "If you lowered the price, we would sign today."
C "If you lower the price, we would sign."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- If You..., We Would...

GRAMMAR

An imagined offer: if + past, would + verb. You test the other side without promising.

FunctionFormExample
An imagined offerIf + past simple, would + verbIf you lowered the price, we would sign.
Asking for something backIf we + past, would you + verb?If we accepted, would you pay upfront?
A possibilitycould + verbWe could meet halfway.
A real plan (lesson 18)If + present, will + verbIf you sign, we will start.
Neverwould after ifIf you lowered (not: if you would lower)

Never: If you would lower · If you lower the price, we would · We could to meet

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

The buyer asks for a lower price. You want something back. You say:
A "Okay, we accept ten percent less, no problem, because we want to finish today."
B "If we would lower the price, you pay?"
C "If we lowered the price, would you pay forty percent upfront?"
You want to say your limit. You say:
A "Our bottom line is seven percent."
B "Our deal-breaker is seven percent, and maybe we can accept more if you ask again next week."
C "Our concession is seven."
The delivery date is a small problem. You say:
A "That is a deal-breaker, so we will walk away from the negotiation today."
B "We could meet halfway on the date."
C "We could to meet in the middle."
You want to say what would happen if the terms changed again. You say:
A "If the terms changed again, we would walk away."
B "If the terms change again, we would walk away from the deal and look for another buyer."
C "If the terms would change, we walk."
