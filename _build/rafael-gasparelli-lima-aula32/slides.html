Chapter 4: The Negotiation Code

If You..., We Would...

An offer, not a promise

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"If you lowered the price, we would sign today."

"If we accepted your price, would you pay forty percent upfront?"

"If the terms changed again, we would walk away."

"We could meet halfway on the date."

Is the price lower now? Then why do we use lowered and accepted?

FunctionFormExample
An imagined offerIf + past simple, would + verbIf you lowered the price, we would sign.
Asking for something backIf we + past, would you + verb?If we accepted, would you pay upfront?
A possibilitycould + verbWe could meet halfway.
A real plan (lesson 18)If + present, will + verbIf you sign today, we will start Monday.
Neverwould after ifIf you lowered (not: if you would lower)

An imagined offer: if + past, would + verb. You test the other side without promising.

Chapter 5: The Final Call

The Last Ten Percent

Claire asks; Rafael negotiates

Dialogue

The Final Call

C
Rafael, my client is ready to sign, but the price is still a problem.
R
I understand. If we lowered the price by five percent, would you pay forty percent upfront?
C
Five percent is not enough. Could you go to ten?
R
Ten is not possible. Our bottom line is seven. If you accepted seven, we would sign today.
C
Seven, with forty percent upfront. And the delivery date?
R
That is not a deal-breaker. We could meet halfway: March, not February or April.
C
Okay. I think we have a deal.
R
Great. Let us put the main points in a term sheet and close the deal on Friday.
Comprehension

About Claire

1. What does Claire say is still a problem?
The price.
2. How much does she ask for?

3. What does she accept in the end?
Seven percent, with forty percent upfront.
