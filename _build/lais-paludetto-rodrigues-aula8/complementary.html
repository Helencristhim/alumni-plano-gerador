Lesson 8 -- Explaining Brazil

Grammar Video
"Using defining and non-defining relative clauses" -- BBC Learning English

The clearest explanation of the only hard part of today's grammar: when the two commas change the meaning of the whole sentence. Connection to Lesson 8: "my sister who studies English" and "my sister, who studies English" say different things about how many sisters you have.

Tip: after the video, write two sentences about your own family — one with commas and one without — and notice that they mean different things.

"Relative Clauses" -- The Grammar Gameshow, Episode 11, BBC Learning English

The same grammar as a game show, with a robot, a scoreboard and contestants who get it wrong on purpose. Faster and much sillier than a normal lesson, which makes it easier to watch twice. Connection to Lesson 8: same four words, more repetitions, none of them boring.

Tip: play along and answer out loud before the contestants do. If you beat them, you have learned it.

Talk
"The Importance of Exchange Student Programs" -- TEDxClearLakeHighSchool

Worth a second visit, this time for a different reason: listen to how she describes her own country to an audience that has never been there. Notice how often she uses a small, specific example instead of a general statement. Connection to Lesson 8: that is exactly the move you practised today.

Tip: write down the three most concrete details she gives. Then write three of your own about Sao Paulo.
