Chapter 3: The Code

One Word Joins Two Sentences

who for people, which for things, where for places

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"There is a bakery that opens at six in the morning."

"The people who queue there at that hour are not tourists."

"Brazil is a country where a four-hour flight leaves you inside it."

"Our school year ends in December, which surprises everybody."

Each of these was two sentences before. What glued them together, and what does that word stand for?

WordUse it forExample
whopeopleThe woman who runs the flower shop.
whichthings, and whole ideasA neighbourhood which nobody has heard of.
thatpeople or things, in the essential kind of sentenceThe bakery that opens at six.
whereplacesThe street where I grew up.
Two commasNo commas: the information is essential. "The bakery that opens at six" tells you WHICH bakery. With commas: it is extra. "My sister, who also studies English, is younger."
The disappearing wordWhen another person or thing does the action, you can drop it: "the assignment (that) I submitted". This is what fluent people do all the time.

One word instead of a new sentence. That is the difference between a list and a description.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"The bakery that it opens at six."
"The bakery that opens at six."
"The thing what surprises everybody is the school year."
"The thing that surprises everybody is the school year."
"My sister, which also studies English, is younger."
"My sister, who also studies English, is younger."

The relative word replaces the subject, so you never say it twice. English uses that or which, never what, in this kind of sentence. And people always get who.

Detail

Two Sentences Into One

Join them out loud. More than one version can be right.

"I have a sister. She studies at Alumni too." → "___I have a sister who studies at Alumni too."
"There is a bakery. It sells cheese bread at six." → "___There is a bakery that sells cheese bread at six."
"Sao Paulo is a city. Everything moves fast there." → "___Sao Paulo is a city where everything moves fast."
"My holiday is in January. That confuses Canadians." → "___My holiday is in January, which confuses Canadians."

So, What Is Brazil Like?

J
So you're from Brazil. What's it like? Like, beaches all year?
L
That's the stereotype, and it's true somewhere. But Sao Paulo is a city that has no beach at all.
J
Wait, seriously? I genuinely thought all of Brazil was coast.
L
It's a country where a four-hour flight still leaves you inside it. My hometown is about an hour from the sea, which is close by our standards.
J
Okay, that's already more than I knew this morning. What's the weirdest difference?
L
Our school year ends in December, which means my summer holiday is your winter. Christmas is a beach thing for us.
J
That is genuinely breaking my brain. Okay, one more: what should I eat if I ever go?
L
Something small and homemade that people buy on the way to school. I'll bring some to the culture fair and you can decide.
Comprehension

About Jesse

1. What did he believe about Brazil before this conversation?
That all of Brazil was coast, with beaches all year.
2. Which fact does he say is breaking his brain?
That the Brazilian school year ends in December, so summer holiday falls in his winter.
3. What is the last thing he asks?
What he should eat if he ever goes to Brazil.
Listening

The Culture Fair Briefing

Your teacher explains what a good table looks like. Sound first — no text.

1. What three things does he ask students to bring?
One object, one photograph and one thing people can eat — and the reason for each.
2. Who does well at the fair, according to him?
Not the students with the most information, but the ones who explain something small properly.
3. What example does he give from last year?
A student from Korea who spent five minutes on a school shoe rack, and had the best table in the room.
Listening 2

Do Not Try to Explain Brazil

Beatriz did the same fair last year. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say you should not try to do?
Explain Brazil, because it is enormous and you end up saying the three sentences everybody expects.
2. What did she talk about instead?
The bakery on her corner that sells cheese bread at six in the morning, and her school year ending in December.
3. What is her last piece of advice?
Take a lot of food, and you will have friends by the end of the afternoon.
