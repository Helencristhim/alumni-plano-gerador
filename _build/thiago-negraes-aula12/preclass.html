Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

English has two common ways to talk about the future, and the difference is not the time: it is the moment of the decision. When the plan was decided before you speak, English uses be going to: "I am going to travel for ten days in December", "we are going to set off from Los Angeles". When you decide at the moment you speak, you use will: "It is sold out? OK, I will book another hotel." Offers and promises are decisions of the moment too: "I will send you the link." For predictions, going to needs evidence you can see ("look at those clouds, it is going to snow"), and will is an opinion ("I think you will love the coast"). Portuguese uses one form for almost all of this, which is why will appears everywhere.

A "I will travel in December."
B "I am going to travel in December."

2. The hotel is full and you decide right now. What do you say?
A "OK, I will stay somewhere else."
B "OK, I am going to stay somewhere else, I decided last week."

3. Which prediction is based on evidence you can see?
A "I think you will love it."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Will vs going to

GRAMMAR

One question decides everything: did I decide this before, or am I deciding it now?

FormUseExample
am / is / are going toa plan decided beforeI am going to travel in December.
will + verba decision, offer or promise made nowI will send you the link.
Negativewon't / am not going toI won't stay in expensive hotels.
QuestionAre you going to...? / Will you...?What are you going to do in December?

After will, the verb never changes: "she will love it", not she will loves. After going to, the verb stays in the base form too: "we are going to rent a car".
