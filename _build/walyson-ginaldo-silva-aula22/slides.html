Chapter 4: The Code

We Have Been Working on It

The present perfect continuous: the story of a project that is not finished

Grammar Discovery

Started, and Still Going

"Our team has been working on the project since March."

"We have been touching base with the growers every week."

"Walyson has been keeping the director in the loop."

"How long have you been studying English?"

Look at the blue words. When did the action start? Is it finished? What is the form?

UseFormExample
Started in the past, still happeninghave / has + been + verb-ingWe have been working on it since March.
How long? (duration)for + a period · since + a start pointfor three months · since March
Negativehaven’t / hasn’t + been + -ingShe hasn’t been feeling well.
QuestionHow long + have / has + subject + been + -ing?How long have you been waiting?
Not with state verbsknow, like, own, believe: present perfect simpleI have known her for years.

In one line: an action that started in the past and is still going = have / has been + -ing, usually with for or since.

Listening 1 · A Coach

Idioms Without Fear

A business English coach on how to learn and use idioms. Sound first — no text.

1. Why do idioms confuse international professionals?
Because the meaning is not in the words, so a translation word by word does not work.
2. How does she say you should learn them?
In context: write down the whole sentence where you heard the idiom, not only the idiom.
3. What is her warning about using idioms?
Do not use too many. One or two in a meeting sound natural; ten sound strange.
Listening 2 · A Trader

My First Year in London

A Brazilian trader on the idiom he did not understand. Sound first — no text.

1. What did he think "let's call it a day" meant?
He thought his manager wanted to give the day a name, or end the project.
2. What did he do after that?
He started a notebook with every expression he heard in meetings.
3. How long has he been keeping the notebook?
For six years. He has been adding new expressions since his first year.
