Lesson 15 -- Pre-class

What Happened? What Didn't Happen? -- The Past in the Negative, and the Zurich Interview

In lesson 14 you told your career in the affirmative: I began, I got, I became, I made. Today you learn the other half — what did not happen. There are only two pieces: didn't for the negative and Did you…? for the question. And it comes with good news: after didn't and after did, the verb goes back to being bare (I didn't go, never I didn't went) — the past is already inside did, and one past per sentence is enough. The scene is the Zurich interview, a year ago. You take six new words: interview, apply, hire, offer, decide, nervous. This Pre-class is a first contact — come to class with the six in your ear.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Six words from one single scene: the interview. Listen to each one and repeat it out loud. The sound traps of the day: IN-ter-vyoo · a-PLAI · ha-i-er (with a breathed H) · di-SAID (the final E is silent).

Interview -- a meeting where a company asks you questions
"I had an interview at Zurich last year." Say IN-ter-vyoo, stress on the first syllable — never “inter-VIEW”.
Apply -- to ask for a job, in writing
"I didn't apply — they called me." Say a-PLAI, stress on the second syllable, rhyming with my.
Hire -- to give someone a job
"They hired me two weeks later." Say ha-i-er, one stretched syllable — and breathe the H, it is not silent.
Offer -- what a company puts on the table
"They sent me an offer on Friday." Say O-fer, stress on the first syllable, with a dry F.
Decide -- to choose between two things
"I didn't decide that day." Say di-SAID — stress on the second syllable and the final E is silent.
Nervous -- worried before something important
"I was nervous, but I didn't stop." Say NER-ves — and notice: it goes with was / were, never with didn't.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the story and answer the questions. The sentences in bold are today's negative — notice that after didn't the verb is always in its simple form, with no -ed and no change of shape.

The Zurich interview, a year ago, told by Paula. “I start with what did not happen: I didn't apply. Sofia called me in March, after a broker gave her my name. I did not even know the company — I didn't know the company.”

“The night before I was nervous and I didn't sleep. Not one hour. I walked into that room thinking they were going to test my English. We talked about large risks for twenty minutes, and then about my career. And here is the part I keep with me: they didn't ask about my English. Not one question.”

“The offer arrived two weeks later. I didn't decide that day — I took a week to answer. When I said yes, it was because they did not want perfect English: they wanted a real story. And that I had.”

1. The sentence “I didn't sleep” is in the past. Why is it not “didn't slept”?
A Because sleep has no past form.
B Because the past is already inside didn't — one past per sentence, never two.
C Because the sentence is a question.
2. How did Paula get to that interview?
A She did not apply — Sofia called after a broker gave her name.
B She sent her CV through the company website.
C She already worked at Zurich and changed teams.
3. What did the panel not ask about?
A They did not ask about large risks.
B They did not ask anything about her English — not one question.
C They did not ask about her career.
4. In “I didn't apply” and “they didn't ask”, what changes with the person (I, she, they)?
A The didn't changes: each person has their own.
B Nothing changes — didn't is the same for I, you, she, we, they.
C It only changes when the verb is irregular.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- one past per sentence

GRAMMAR

In lesson 14 you put the verb into the past (worked, went). Today you learn to undo that. When didn't (for the negative) or did (for the question) comes in, the past is already there — and the verb goes back to its bare form.

FormIn EnglishWhen you use it
Affirmative (lesson 14)I applied · I wenteu me candidatei · eu fui
NegativeI didn't apply · I didn't goI didn't apply · I didn't go
Yes/no questionDid you apply? · Did they hire you?Did you apply? · Did they hire you?
Wh- questionWhat did they ask? · When did you decide?What did they ask? · When did you decide?
Short answerYes, I did. / No, I didn't.yes / no

The two mistakes that feel natural to make, and the fix for each one:

1) “I didn't went to the interview.” — you put two pasts in the same sentence. didn't is already the past, so the verb goes back to bare: “I didn't go to the interview.” Same in the question: Did you apply?, never Did you applied?

2) “I not applied for the job.” — you used not on its own. In English not needs a helper in front of it, and in the past that helper is always did: “I didn't apply for the job.”

Today's relief: in the negative, the irregular verbs from lesson 14 disappear. went becomes didn't go, got becomes didn't get, left becomes didn't leave. If the past form does not come to mind, the negative sentence saves you — and it is correct, it is not a shortcut.

Quiz

A "I not applied for the job."
B "I didn't apply. They called me."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record your answer. Speak for 60 to 90 seconds, with no script. Do not redo it more than twice. This is exactly the homework audio — start building it here.

A panel will ask you one question: “Tell us about the day you joined Zurich. What happened, and what didn't happen?” Answer out loud, telling the interview from the start (Sofia's call) to the end (the week you took to decide). Use at least three negatives with didn't and at least one question back with Did or What did. The only rule of this recording is: one past per sentence. It does not have to be perfect — it has to be yours.
