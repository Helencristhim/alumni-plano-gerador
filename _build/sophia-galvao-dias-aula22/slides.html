Advice or rule?

A rule, a warning or your choice?

Read each line. Is it a rule? A warning? A good idea? Your choice?

"You should start a week before the test."
"You ought to sleep eight hours."
"You'd better not cram, or you'll forget everything."
"You mustn't check your phone during the test."
"You don't have to highlight every line."
You want to sayUse
A good idea (advice)should / ought to + verb
You ought to sleep eight hours.
A strong warning: do it now, or something bad will happenhad better (not) + verb (short: 'd better)
You'd better not cram, or you'll forget everything.
It is against the rulesmustn't + verb
You mustn't check your phone during the test.
It is not necessary: you choosedon't have to + verb
You don't have to highlight every line.

Watch out: had better is about now and the future, not the past. And no to: you'd better go, never you'd better to go. But ought always needs to: you ought to go.

"You'd better study tonight. — no to after had better."

"You ought to study more. — ought always needs to."

"You don't have to bring a pen. — it is not forbidden, just not necessary."
"You had better not to cram."
"You had better not cram. — had better not + verb, no to."
"Have you ever failed a test? Yes, I had."
"Yes, I have. — your life up to now: have, not had."
