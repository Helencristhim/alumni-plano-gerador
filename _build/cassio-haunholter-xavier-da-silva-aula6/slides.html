Lesson 6 · Where the Project Actually Is

The Progress Report

Once a month the project has to describe itself in one page, to people who were not in any of the meetings. The register of that page is not the register of a conversation, and that is the whole lesson.

Chapter 1: First Words

Nobody Says Who

You described these meetings in the consultation: the project is running, and the calls are about where it stands. The spoken version names people. The written version almost never does, and that is deliberate.

Think of the last status update you gave. Which parts named a person, and which parts did not need to?

Three Missions

The target in one line: Marko issued the drawings on Friday is a conversation; the drawings were issued on Friday is a report. English moves the important thing to the front and drops the name when the name is not the point.

Chapter 4: The Code

The Passive, On Purpose

Why reports drop the name

Grammar Discovery

Where Did the Name Go?

"The foundation drawings were issued on Friday."

"Revision C has been approved by the client."

"The equipment base has not been signed off yet."

"Three comments from the design review are still being resolved."

Every sentence starts with the thing, not with the person. Ask yourself two questions: who did it, and does the sentence tell you? Then look at what the red words are made of — a form of be, plus something else.

JobFormExample
The basic shapebe + past participleThe drawings were issued on Friday.
When the doer does not matterleave the name outApproval is expected next week.
When the doer does matter... by + the doerRevision C has been approved by the client.
Present perfect passivehave / has been + past participleThe base has not been signed off yet.
Something in progressis / are being + past participleThree comments are being resolved.
The trapthe participle, never the infinitiveNot was issue, not were sign off. Say was issued, were signed off.

In one line: be + past participle; the name comes back only after by, and only when it is the point.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

The Report Itself, and Two Voices

Real Document

One Page, Four Minutes

MONTHLY PROGRESS REPORT
Industrial Block 2 · month 4 · issued to project director and client
Exceptions this monthEquipment base not signed off · steel package 11 days behind
IssuedFoundation drawings, April 12 · GA revision C, April 18
ApprovedGA revision C — approved by the client on April 24
Variance vs baselineSteel package: 11 days · flagged in the March report
Design review2 of 7 comments still being resolved
OutstandingClient sign-off on the equipment base — review booked for May 9
Action listOwner and date against every open item — see page 2
Notice where the names are, and where they are not. Every line above is written without a person, because the subject is the work. The names all live in one place, the action list, with a date next to each one.
1. Which line would a director read first, and why?
The exceptions line, because it holds what has changed against the plan.
2. Turn the Issued line into a full sentence in the passive.
The foundation drawings were issued on April 12, and revision C of the general arrangement was issued on April 18.
3. Why does the variance line mention the March report?
Because the delay was flagged early, which makes it a scheduling problem and not a credibility problem.
Listening 1 · The Project Director

The Four Minutes I Actually Have

A project director explains how he reads a monthly report. Sound first — no text.

1. How many reports does he receive in a month?
About eleven.
2. What three things does he read?
The first paragraph, anything marked outstanding, and the variance table.
3. What does he say about bad news on page three?
He will find it in the meeting instead, and by then the team has lost the chance to frame it.
Listening 2 · The Planner

The Report That Was Technically True

A planner describes a delay that stayed invisible for two months. Sound first — no text.

1. How long did the delay stay invisible?
Two months, until month three.
2. What had the layout been waiting for?
A soil report, since week two.
3. What does she now ask for in every report?
That any item which has not moved is marked as such, with what it is waiting for.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I build the passive with be plus the past participle, never the infinitive.
I leave the name out when the name is not the point, and add by when it is.
I can report something not done with has not been plus the participle.
I lead a status update with the exceptions, not with the chronology.
I know the words: a progress report, to issue, a revision, a baseline, a variance, to sign off, a design review, outstanding, to flag.
