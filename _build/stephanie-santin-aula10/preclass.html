Lesson 10 -- Pre-class

What Somebody Else Said

Relaying what a patient, a colleague or a professor said. Key words: to report, to mention, to suggest, to recommend, to explain, follow-up, referral, complaint. Structure: reported speech, backshift, say against tell, and reported questions.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These are the verbs that let you say something without taking responsibility for having said it first.

To report -- to tell somebody officially what happened
"The nurse reported that the pain had started at night."
To mention -- to say something briefly, without going into detail
"He mentioned that the position might open in the fall."
To suggest -- to offer an idea for somebody to consider
"She suggested that we wait for the second result."
To recommend -- to advise somebody to do something, as a specialist
"The cardiologist recommended a new test."
To explain -- to make something clear to somebody who does not understand it
"I explained that the procedure was routine."
Follow-up -- a second appointment to check how the patient is
"The follow-up is in four weeks."
Referral -- a formal note sending a patient to another specialist
"She asked for a referral to a neurologist."
Complaint -- the problem the patient describes when they arrive
"The main complaint was chest pain."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

A handover is a conversation in which nothing is new. Everything was said before, by somebody else, and the nurse who arrives repeats it. That is why handovers are full of reported speech. The patient said the pain had started after dinner. The resident said he had ordered the tests. The family asked whether the surgery would happen that day. Nobody invents anything, and the words move one step back in time as they pass from mouth to mouth. When the step back is not made, the report sounds like an opinion instead of a record — and in a hospital that difference is not a detail.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

When you report what somebody said, the verb takes one step back in time, and the pronouns and time words move with it.

present simple to past simple"I work here"She said she worked here.
present perfect to past perfect"I have applied"She said she had applied.
will to would, can to could"I will send it"He said he would send it.
questionswhether or if, and no auxiliaryThey asked whether the surgery would happen.

Careful with say and tell: you tell somebody something, and you say something. "She told me that..." and "She said that..." are both correct; "She said me" and "She told that" are not.
