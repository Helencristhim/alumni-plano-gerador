Chapter 3: The Code

One Step Back in Time

What happens to a sentence when it changes mouth

Discover the Rule

What Do These Have in Common?

"I work in the emergency room." → She said she worked in the emergency room.
"I am reviewing the data." → He said he was reviewing the data.
"I have already applied." → She said she had already applied.
"I will send the report." → He said he would send the report.
Click to reveal the rule
When you report what somebody said, the verb takes one step back in time: present simple to past simple, present continuous to past continuous, present perfect to past perfect, will to would, can to could. The pronouns move too (I to she), and so do the time words: now to then, today to that day, tomorrow to the following day. Two verbs to keep apart: you tell somebody something, and you say something. She told me that... and She said that... — never she said me.

Chapter 4: The Handover

Beds Twelve to Twenty

The moment when everything one person heard becomes what another person knows

Listening 1

The Morning Handover

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What did the patient in bed fourteen tell the night nurse?
That the pain had started after dinner, and that it was different from the usual one.
2. What did the family in bed sixteen ask, and what was the answer?
They asked whether the surgery would happen today. The nurse explained that nothing had been decided yet and suggested that they speak to the surgeon after the ward round.
3. Why should bed nineteen be checked first?
Because the patient mentioned that she had stopped one of her medications two weeks ago, she did not tell anybody, and it is not in the chart.

What Did She Say?

O
You saw Mrs. Barnes this morning. What did she say?
S
She said the pain had started after dinner, and that it was different from the usual one.
O
Did she mention the medication?
S
She told me she had stopped one of them two weeks ago. It is not in the chart.
O
That changes things. What did you tell her?
S
I explained that we would repeat the blood test, and I suggested that she wait for the result.
O
Good. And the family?
S
They asked whether the surgery would happen today. I said nothing had been decided yet.
Comprehension

About Dr. Owen

1. What does Dr. Owen want to know first?
What Mrs. Barnes said that morning.
2. What does he ask about specifically?
Whether she mentioned the medication.
3. How does he react to the information about the medication?
He says that it changes things.

Listening 2

Exactly What the Professor Said

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What did the professor say about the position and the decision?
That it was still open, but that it would be decided in October, not in August.
2. What did he ask, and what was the answer?
He asked whether she had already taken the English exam, and the answer was that she had not.

He recommended that she take the exam before the interview, and he suggested that she write to him directly.

Today You Can

Move the verb one step back when you report what somebody said
Keep say and tell apart: she told me, she said that
Report a question with whether, and with no auxiliary
Relay what a patient told you to a colleague
Use report, mention, suggest, recommend and explain instead of only say
