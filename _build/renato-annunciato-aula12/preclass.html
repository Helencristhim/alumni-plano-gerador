Lesson 12 -- Pre-class

Handling Questions -- The Q and A After the Presentation

The ten minutes after the presentation that the directors remember. Key words: to clarify, to rephrase, to elaborate, off the top of my head, to get back to someone, fair point, ballpark figure, hypothetical, to bring up, concern, to dodge, bottom line. Structures: question forms (direct and indirect), and the four moves of an answer.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the definition. None of them is about your market. All of them are about surviving a question.

To clarify -- to make something easier to understand by explaining it again
"Just to clarify, do you mean the South or the Southeast?"
To rephrase -- to say a question or an idea again, in different words
"Let me rephrase the question to make sure I understood."
To elaborate -- to give more detail about something you have already said
"Could you elaborate on the interior plan?"
Off the top of my head -- from memory, without checking the exact information
"Off the top of my head, it is around five clinics."
To get back to someone -- to give someone an answer later, after you check
"I will check the number and get back to you by Friday."
Fair point -- a phrase to say that somebody's comment is reasonable
"Fair point. I went through that part too quickly."
Ballpark figure -- a number that is close to the real one, but not exact
"The ballpark figure is two million units."
Hypothetical -- about a situation that is imagined, not real
"It is a hypothetical question: what if the biggest client left?"
To bring up -- to start talking about a subject in a conversation
"Could I bring up one more point before we finish?"
Concern -- a worry about something that may become a problem
"Her main concern is that the growth depends on one clinic."
To dodge -- to avoid answering a question directly
"Directors notice when you dodge a question."
Bottom line -- the most important fact, or the final result of something
"The bottom line is that the interior is the opportunity."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the rehearsal notes and answer the questions. Notice the word order in the questions in bold.

After his presentation, Renato rehearsed the Q and A with Sofia from Global Finance. Her first question was direct: where did the November growth come from? Renato said it was a fair point, because he had gone through that part quickly, and he offered to elaborate.

Then Sofia asked: could you tell me how many of those clinics are new accounts? Renato did not invent a number. Off the top of my head, he said, around five, and he promised to get back to her the same day. Her next question was a hypothetical, and before he answered, he asked her to clarify which clinic she meant.

Her concern was simple: I am wondering whether the growth depends on one account. Renato did not dodge it. He gave a ballpark figure, and at the end he decided to bring up the topic he cared about most. Who will pay for two more consultants? The bottom line was clear: put it in writing, with the numbers.

1. Why is it "where did the growth come from?" but "could you tell me how many clinics are new?"
A Because the first is a direct question, and the second is a question inside another sentence, so the subject comes before the verb.
B Because the first question is about the past and the second one is about the future of the clinics.
C Because "could you tell me" is only used in emails.
2. "Who will pay for two more consultants?" Why is there no "does" or "did"?
A Because "will" can never be used together with "who" in a question.
B Because "who" is the subject of the question, so it goes straight to the verb.
C Because the question has no answer yet.
3. "I am wondering whether the growth depends on one account." Why "whether"?
A Because the direct question is a yes or no question, and inside another sentence it takes "if" or "whether".
B Because "whether" is a more formal word for "weather" and is used in finance reports.
C Because the sentence is in the past.
4. Which question would you write to a global director?
A "Could you tell me when will you decide about the budget for the interior?"
B "Could you tell me when you will decide?"
C "When you will decide?"

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Direct and Indirect Questions

GRAMMAR

One question decides the word order: is the question alone, or inside another sentence?

TypeWord orderExample
Direct question(question word) + do / does / did / is / will + subject + verbWhere did the growth come from?
Subject questionWho / What + verb — no do, does or didWho approved the budget?
Indirect questionCould you tell me / Do you know / I was wondering + question word + subject + verbCould you tell me where the growth came from?
Yes / no, indirectuse if or whether: I was wondering whether the interior needs more consultants.
Why indirect?It is softer and more polite. Use it with a director, a client, or when you need something.
Two trapsNever Can you tell me where is the clinic? — it is where the clinic is. And never What means outlier? — it is What does outlier mean?

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

A director asks a long question very fast, and you understand only half. You say:
A "Sorry, my English is not very good, so I did not understand your question at all."
B "Sorry, could you repeat the last part of the question?"
C "Yes, yes, I agree with you."
You want to know when the director will decide. The most polite way is:
A "When you will decide about the budget for the two new consultants?"
B "When will you decide? I need to know now."
C "Could you tell me when you expect to decide?"
Somebody asks for an exact number that you do not have. You say:
A "Off the top of my head, around forty. I will check and get back to you."
B "It is exactly forty-three, I am almost sure, but maybe it is a little different."
C "I have no idea."
A director says your slide was too optimistic, and she has a reason. You start with:
A "That is not true, and I can prove it with the numbers from last year."
B "Fair point. Let me elaborate."
C "Next question, please."
You are not sure which clinic the question is about. Before you answer, you:
A Clarify: "Just to clarify, do you mean the clinic in Curitiba?"
B Answer about the clinic that you know best, because it is the safest option for you.
C Change the subject to the interior.
Which question is correct?
A "Do you know where is the new clinic in Curitiba, the one with the big orders?"
B "Do you know where the new clinic is?"
C "Do you know where does the new clinic is?"
