Chapter 3: The Code

Where Did It Come From? Where It Came From.

Direct and indirect questions

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Where did the November growth come from?"

"Could you tell me where the growth came from?"

"Does the interior need more consultants?"

"I was wondering whether the interior needs more consultants."

Blue questions are direct. Purple questions sit inside another sentence. What happened to did and does?

TypeWord orderExample
Direct question(question word) + do / does / did / is / will + subject + verbWhere did the growth come from?
Subject questionWho / What + verb — no do, does or didWho approved the budget?
Indirect questionCould you tell me / Do you know / I was wondering + question word + subject + verbCould you tell me where the growth came from?
Yes / no, indirectuse if or whether: I was wondering whether the interior needs more consultants.
Why indirect?It is softer. Use it with a director, a client, or when you need something.
Two trapsNever Can you tell me where is the clinic? — it is where the clinic is. And never What means outlier? — it is What does outlier mean?

The Structure

Answer in Four Moves

You cannot prepare the question. You can prepare the shape of the answer.

1 · Rephrase

"So your question is whether the growth will continue next year."

2 · Answer, short

"The short answer is yes."

3 · Support

"Off the top of my head, the ballpark figure is two more percentage points."

4 · Close, or get back

"I will check the exact number and get back to you by Friday."

Rephrase → answer → support → close. Then stop talking.

The Q and A Rehearsal

S
Thank you, Renato. I have a few questions. Where exactly did the November growth come from?
R
Fair point, I went through that quickly. Most of it came from a few clinics in Sao Paulo and Curitiba. I can elaborate if you want.
S
Please. And could you tell me how many of those clinics are new accounts?
R
Off the top of my head, around five. I would rather check the exact number and get back to you today.
S
That is fine. Now a hypothetical. What would happen if the biggest clinic stopped ordering?
R
Just to clarify, do you mean the clinic in Curitiba, the outlier?
S
Yes. I am wondering whether the growth depends on one account.
R
It is a fair concern. Without that clinic, growth would be lower, but still positive. The ballpark figure is two points instead of three.
S
Good. You did not dodge the question. Last one: what is the bottom line for next year?
R
The bottom line is the interior. Could I bring up one thing before we finish? I need two more consultants there.
S
You can. Ask for it in writing, with the numbers, and I will support it.

Listening

Why the Director Asks

A global director on the two questions she always asks. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What is her first question almost always about, and why does she ask it?
The source of a number. She is not attacking the speaker; she is checking whether the speaker knows the business behind the slide.
2. What kind of question is the second one usually?
A hypothetical: what would happen if the market changed, or if the biggest client left.
3. What is the worst answer, according to her?
A long one that avoids the question.
4. What does she prefer to hear when the speaker does not know?
That he will get back to her, not a number invented on the spot.

Chapter 5: Practice

Your Answers

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "Could you tell me where is the new clinic?"
"Could you tell me where the new clinic is?" (inside another sentence: subject, then verb)
2. "Who did approve the budget for the interior?"
"Who approved the budget for the interior?" (who is the subject, so no did)
3. "What means ballpark figure?"
"What does ballpark figure mean?" (a direct question needs does)
4. "I pretend to answer your question by Friday."
"I intend to answer your question by Friday." (pretend means to act as if something is true)
5. "Could you explain me the hypothetical again?"
"Could you explain the hypothetical to me again?" (explain something to someone)
The Real Thing

The Email the Next Morning

From: Sofia Brandt, Global Finance Business Partner
To: Renato Annunciato
Subject: Three questions after your rehearsal

Hi Renato,

Thank you for the presentation yesterday. I have three questions before Boston:

  1. Could you tell me how many of the November accounts are new?
  2. I was wondering whether the interior plan needs a bigger budget.
  3. Who will manage the two new consultants?

A ballpark figure is fine for now.

Best,
Sofia

1. Which of her questions is direct, and which are indirect?
Question 3 is direct. Questions 1 and 2 are indirect: the subject comes before the verb, and there is no do, does or did.
