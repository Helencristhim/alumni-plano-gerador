Lesson 12 -- Handling Questions

Series
The Newsroom -- Season 1, Episode 1

The first episode opens with a panel discussion at a university: a television anchor is asked a simple question, tries to dodge it several times, and then answers it in a way nobody in the room expected. It is the most famous Q and A scene in any series, and it shows everything this lesson is about: what happens when somebody avoids the question, and what happens when he finally answers it.

Tip: watch the first ten minutes with English subtitles. Count how many times he tries to avoid the question before he answers it, and notice how the moderator keeps asking.

Podcast
Coaching for Leaders -- The Way to Handle Q and A, with Matt Abrahams (Episode 681)

A free half-hour interview with the Stanford lecturer who teaches spontaneous speaking. He explains a three-step method for answering any question: give the answer, give one detail that supports it, and say why it matters to the person who asked. It is very close to the four moves of today's lesson, and he speaks slowly and clearly.

Tip: listen once without notes. Then take the question you are most afraid of in your presentation and answer it out loud with his three steps.

YouTube
Matt Abrahams -- Think Fast, Talk Smart: Communication Techniques (Stanford GSB)

The famous Stanford talk on speaking when you have not prepared: questions in a meeting, a surprise comment, a Q and A that goes in a direction you did not expect. He shows how to buy a few seconds, how to rephrase a question, and how to give a short structured answer instead of a long one.

Tip: watch it with English subtitles, in two parts if you prefer. Write down the one technique you will use in Boston, and say it out loud to your teacher in the next lesson.
