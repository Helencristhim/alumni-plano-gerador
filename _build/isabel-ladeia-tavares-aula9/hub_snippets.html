Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

Asking for Help Without Apologising -- One Clear Question for Your Supervisor

The protocol is ambiguous, the notes say both, and the question you ask decides how fast you stop getting stuck. Key words: a clarification, ambiguous, a workaround, to get stuck, to double-check, straightforward, guidance, to interrupt, to run something by somebody, to figure something out, to get the hang of something, to be on the right track. Structure: indirect questions with could you tell me, do you know if and I was wondering whether.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Four of the twelve are expressions.

A clarification -- an explanation that makes something easier to understand
"I just need a quick clarification on step four."
Ambiguous -- able to be understood in more than one way
"Spin briefly is ambiguous. Is that ten seconds or a minute?"
A workaround -- a way of getting around a problem without actually fixing it
"The reader still freezes, but there is a workaround on the card."
To get stuck -- to be unable to continue because you do not know what to do next
"I got stuck on step four and lost twenty minutes."
To double-check -- to check something a second time to be completely sure it is right
"I want to double-check the dilution before I start."
Straightforward -- simple and easy to understand or to do, with no hidden difficulty
"Once he explained it, the step was completely straightforward."
Guidance -- advice or help from somebody with more experience
"I would appreciate some guidance before Thursday."
To interrupt -- to stop somebody while they are speaking or working
"Sorry to interrupt, but the reader has frozen again."
To run something by somebody -- to show somebody your idea or plan to hear their opinion before you act
"Run your plan for Thursday by me before you start."
To figure something out -- to find the answer to a problem by thinking about it yourself
"I tried to figure it out from last year’s notes, but they say both."
To get the hang of something -- to learn how to do something after some practice, so it becomes easy
"The pipettes were hard at first, but I am getting the hang of them."
To be on the right track -- to be doing something in a way that will lead to the right result
"Your plan looks good. You are on the right track."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Monday morning Isabel gets stuck on step four of a new protocol. It says spin briefly, then dilute, but last year’s notes say to dilute first. She spends fifteen minutes trying to figure it out on her own. Then, when Daniel walks past, she does not say sorry. She asks: could you tell me what spin briefly means? And then: I was wondering whether we dilute before or after the spin.

Daniel answers both in two sentences. When she asks if anyone has found a workaround for the plate reader, he sends her to Paulo. That night she sends him her plan with three more questions. In one of them she writes: do you know if the same workaround works on the old reader?

Notice the order of the words in bold. Each one begins like a question, could you tell me, do you know, I was wondering, but the part after it is in the order of a statement: subject, then verb. There is no do, does or did in the second part, and a yes or no question needs if or whether. That is where the politeness comes from. It is not in the word sorry.

1. Which sentence is correct?
A Could you tell me what does spin briefly mean in step four of the protocol?
B Could you tell me what spin briefly means?
C Could you tell me what means spin briefly in this protocol?
2. Why does Isabel say whether in her second question?
A Because the direct question is a yes or no question.
B Because whether is always more formal than every other word, so it is safer with a supervisor.
C Because she is talking about the weather in the lab that morning and how cold the samples are.
3. According to the text, where does the politeness of her questions come from?
A From the word sorry, which she repeats at the start of each question so that Daniel knows she is nervous.
B From speaking very slowly and quietly.
C From the shape of the question: a polite opening, then statement order.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- A Question Inside a Question

GRAMMAR

The polite opening is the question. What comes after it is a statement.

Direct questionIndirect questionWhat changes
Where are the buffers kept?Could you tell me where the buffers are kept?subject before verb
What does it mean?Could you tell me what it means?do / does / did disappear
Is it done on ice?Do you know if it is done on ice?yes / no question: if or whether
Do we dilute first?I was wondering whether we dilute first.the softest: no question mark
Can you check my plan?Would you mind checking my plan?would you mind + -ing

Never: Could you tell me where is it · Do you know what does it mean · I was wondering we dilute first.

Stage 2: Put the Question in Order

Order

You are stuck on a step. Put your side of the conversation with your supervisor in a logical order.

?"Great, that is straightforward. Thank you."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

You cannot find the second antibody. You checked the freezer list. You ask your supervisor:
A "Sorry, sorry, I am so sorry to bother you, I know you are busy, but where is it?"
B "It is not on the freezer list. Could you tell me where it is kept?"
C "Could you tell me where is it kept?"
You want to know if twelve hours is enough for an overnight incubation. You write:
A "I was wondering whether twelve hours is enough."
B "I was wondering is twelve hours enough for the incubation, or does it need the full sixteen."
C "Twelve hours."
The plate reader froze, and Paulo is on the phone. You say:
A "The machine is broken again and nobody ever fixes it in this lab, so I cannot finish my run today."
B "Do you know does the workaround work?"
C "Sorry to interrupt. I tried the card, but the reader is still frozen."
You want your supervisor to see your plan before Thursday. You say:
A "Would you mind if I ran my plan by you?"
B "Would you mind to look at my plan before Thursday, if you have some time this week?"
C "Look at my plan."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Think of two things you really need to ask at the lab or at university this month. In one minute, ask them as if to your supervisor: say what you have already tried, then ask each one as an indirect question. No sorry at the start.

Lesson 9 — Asking for Help Without Apologising

Series
Grey’s Anatomy — Season 1

Five first-year surgical interns who do not know where anything is, and have to ask people who are tired and busy. Watch who asks well and who gets sent away: the difference is almost always how precise the question is.

Tip: watch with English subtitles on. Every time an intern asks a direct question, pause and say it again as an indirect one, starting with could you tell me or do you know if.

Podcast
Best of Both Worlds — Heidi Grant on asking for help

A social psychologist talks with two working mothers about why asking for help feels so hard, and how to ask so that people say yes. One of her points is the same as the first listening: an apology at the start of a request makes it harder, not easier, for the other person to say yes.

Tip: listen once. Then write down two of her wrong ways to ask, and turn each one into a precise, indirect question you could use at the lab.
