Lesson 24 -- Pre-class

Not Only... But Also -- Inversion for Emphasis

Move a negative or limiting expression to the front and the sentence switches to question order: rarely have I seen, not only does it look, under no circumstances should you, only when you reach the third act does the writing live up to it. Same meaning, much more weight -- and one per paragraph, never three. Key words: acclaim, compelling, underwhelming, immersive, polished, overrated, verdict, to live up to, shortcoming, to fall short, groundbreaking, flawless.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. These let you say why something worked, instead of saying good or bad.

Acclaim -- public praise from critics and from the people who judge the work
"It arrived with enormous acclaim and almost no marketing."
Compelling -- so interesting that you cannot look away from it
"Not only is this the most compelling thing the studio has made, but it is also the bravest."
Underwhelming -- much less impressive than you expected, without being bad
"Those four missions are the most underwhelming hours in the whole game."
Immersive -- so convincing that you forget the room you are sitting in
"The world is immersive in the way marketing always promises and rarely delivers."
Polished -- finished carefully, with nothing left rough or unfinished
"The whole thing is polished to a degree that has become rare."
Overrated -- praised more than it deserves, usually by everybody at once
"The map is overrated by almost everyone, because size is not density."
Verdict -- the final decision a reviewer gives, after weighing everything
"The verdict comes with one condition attached to it."
To live up to -- to be as good as people expected or promised
"Only in the third act does the writing live up to the world it is set in."
Shortcoming -- a weak point in something that is otherwise good
"Play it properly and its shortcomings shrink to what they actually are."
To fall short -- to be less good than what was expected or promised
"The story falls short in the middle third, and only there."
Groundbreaking -- doing something nobody had done before, and changing what comes after
"The art direction is groundbreaking without ever announcing itself."
Flawless -- with no mistake anywhere in it, which is almost never true
"It is not flawless, and it is the best thing this studio has ever made."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the review and answer the questions. Count the inversions, and notice which paragraph has none.

Not only does this game look extraordinary, but it also runs beautifully on hardware that has no business handling it. Rarely has a studio this size shown such restraint with a world this large.

The world is immersive in the way marketing always promises and almost never delivers. Stand still for a minute and the city carries on without you. Little did I expect that the quietest hour of the game would be the one I remember, and yet here we are. The art direction is groundbreaking without ever announcing itself.

It is not flawless. The story falls short in the middle third, where four missions exist only to delay you, and those are the most underwhelming hours here. The map has been overrated by almost everyone reviewing it, because size is not density. Only when you reach the third act does the writing finally live up to the world it is set in.

The verdict, then, comes with one condition. Under no circumstances should you play this in twenty minute sessions: give it two hours at a time or leave it alone. Do that, and its shortcomings shrink to what they actually are.

A It is borrowed, exactly as in a question, because "look" has no auxiliary of its own.
B It is there to emphasise the verb, the way "I do like it" works.
C It is required after every negative word in English.
2. In "Only when you reach the third act does the writing live up to it", which part is inverted?

B The main clause — "does the writing live up to it".
C Both clauses, one after the other.
3. Why does the third paragraph have almost no inversion?

B Because criticism is heard anyway — it is praise and warnings that need the extra weight.
C Because the writer ran out of trigger words.

B It makes the verdict less certain than it would otherwise be.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Inversion for Emphasis

GRAMMAR

Move a negative or limiting expression to the front, and the sentence takes question order. Nothing in the meaning changes; the weight does.

Trigger at the frontWhat happensExample
never, rarely, seldom, hardly everQuestion order: auxiliary, then subject."Rarely have I seen a studio this careful."
not only ... but alsoOnly the first half inverts; the second stays normal."Not only does it look great, but it also runs well."
under no circumstances, at no pointSame flip. This is how English writes a strong warning."Under no circumstances should you skip the tutorial."
only when, only after, not untilThe flip lands on the main clause, not on the one right after "only"."Only when you reach the third act does the writing live up to it."
no sooner ... than / little"No sooner" pairs with than, never with "when". "Little" is for a realisation."No sooner do you finish it than you see the trick." / "Little did I expect that."
Where the auxiliary comes fromIf the verb has one (have, is, should, can), it moves to the front. If it has none, borrow do / does / did, exactly as in a question.
How oftenOne per paragraph. This belongs in reviews, essays and speeches — in a message to a friend it sounds like a joke.

Stage 2: Put the Review in Order

Order

Listen first, then put the five parts of a review in order.

?Name the shortcomings honestly, in plain word order.
?Close with a balanced line that admits the flaw and keeps the recommendation standing.
?State the verdict in one sentence, so the reader knows where you stand.
?Add the condition the reader has to meet for the recommendation to hold.
?Give the strongest praise, and put the one inversion there.
