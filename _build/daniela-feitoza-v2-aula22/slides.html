Chapter 3

The Polite Code

How to ask for things in an email — clear and kind

Grammar Discovery

Orders or Requests?

"I am writing to ask about the store sales data."

"Could you send me the spreadsheet by Friday?"

"Would you mind checking the numbers before the call?"

"I would appreciate it if you could confirm the time."

What do the orange words have in common? Which verb form comes after each one?

UseFormExample
Say why you writeI am writing to + verbI am writing to ask about the data.
Polite requestCould you + verb?Could you send me the file?
Softer requestWould you mind + verb-ing?Would you mind checking the numbers?
Very formal requestI would appreciate it if you could + verbI would appreciate it if you could confirm the time.

They are all requests, not orders. After would you mind, use -ing, never "to". The answer "Not at all" means yes, no problem.

Grammar Practice

Make It Polite

Say it first, then click to check

"Check the numbers." → "Would you mind ___checking the numbers?"

Register

Formal or Friendly?

Formal (a director, a new contact)

Dear Mr. Lee,
I am writing regarding...
I would appreciate it if you could...
Best regards,

Friendly (a colleague you know)

Hi Greg,
Just a quick note about...
Could you...?
Thanks, / Best,

Chapter 4

The Draft

Greg reads Daniela’s email before she clicks send

Dialogue

Before You Click Send

G
Hi Daniela. I just read your draft to Jennifer. It is good, but the subject line only says "Data". Can we make it clearer?
D
Sure. How about "Store sales data for October"? Then she knows what it is before she opens it.
G
Perfect. And your first line says "Send me the spreadsheet." It sounds a little cold for a first email.
D
You are right. I can write: "I am writing to ask about the October data. Could you send me the spreadsheet as an attachment?"
G
Much better. She is very busy, so give her a date. And please copy me, or forward me her reply.
D
OK: "I would appreciate it if you could send it by Friday." And I will copy you.
G
Great. Last thing: the sign-off. You wrote "Kisses". In the US, that is only for family and friends.
D
Oh no! That is a Brazilian habit. "Best regards, Daniela", then. Thanks, Greg. I am sending it now.
Comprehension

About Greg

1. What problem does Greg see in the subject line?
It only says "Data", so it is not clear.
2. Why does Greg want a date in the email?
Because Jennifer is very busy.
3. What does Greg say about the sign-off "Kisses"?
In the US, it is only for family and friends.
Listening 1 — The Coach

Four Email Tips

A voice message from Sarah, your English coach. Sound first — no text.

1. What should a good subject line be like?
Short and clear, so people know what the email is about before they open it.
2. What should the first sentence say?
Why you are writing: "I am writing to ask about..." or "I am writing regarding..."
3. What should you check before you click send?
Read the email one more time and check the attachment.

Hi Jennifer,

I hope you are well. I am writing to ask about the store sales data for October. Could you send me the spreadsheet as an attachment? I would appreciate it if you could send it by Friday, so we can use it in the first status call.

Thanks in advance.

Best regards,
Daniela Feitoza
IT Manager, SPF Group
