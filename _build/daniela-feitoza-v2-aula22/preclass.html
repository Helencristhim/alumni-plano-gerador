Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Subject line -- the short title of an email that says what it is about
"Write a clear subject line: Store data for October."
Attachment -- a file that you send together with an email
"Please find the report in the attachment."
Regarding -- a formal word that means “about”
"I am writing regarding the Monday meeting."
Request -- the act of asking someone, politely, to do or give you something
"I have a quick request about the sales data."
Reply -- to answer an email or a message
"Mark has not replied to Greg's email yet."
Forward -- to send an email you received to another person
"Could you forward me the invitation to the call?"
Sign-off -- the polite words before your name at the end of an email
"Best regards is a safe sign-off for a new contact."
Appreciate -- to be thankful for something someone does for you
"I really appreciate your help with the budget review."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

On Monday, Greg sent Daniela a list of three emails for the week. Her first draft to Jennifer was short and a little cold: "Send me the spreadsheet." Greg suggested a softer version, so Daniela wrote: "I am writing to ask about the October sales data. Could you send me the spreadsheet as an attachment?" She also added a date: "I would appreciate it if you could send it by Friday." For Mark, who did not reply to Greg, she wrote a friendly follow-up: "Would you mind confirming the time of the Monday meeting?" Mark answered in ten minutes: "Not at all! 10 a.m. works for me." The polite emails worked.

1. Why did Daniela change her first draft to Jennifer?
A It was a little cold, and Greg suggested a softer version.
B Jennifer asked her to write the whole email again in a more formal style.
C She forgot to add the spreadsheet as an attachment.
2. Which request is the most formal?
A "Send me the spreadsheet."
B "I would appreciate it if you could send it by Friday."
C "Could you send me the spreadsheet as an attachment, please?"
3. Mark answered "Not at all!" What does it mean?
A He did not want to confirm the time of the Monday meeting.
B Yes, no problem.
C He did not read the email from Daniela or the one from Greg.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip — Polite Requests in Emails

GRAMMAR

A request asks; an order tells. In emails, always ask.

UseFormExample
Why you writeI am writing to + verbI am writing to ask about the data.
Polite requestCould you + verb?Could you send me the file?
Softer requestWould you mind + verb-ing?Would you mind checking the numbers?
Very formalI would appreciate it if you could + verbI would appreciate it if you could confirm the time.
RememberAfter would you mind, use -ing, never "to". After could you, use the base verb, never "to". "Not at all" is a yes. Formal: Dear Mr. Lee / Best regards. Friendly: Hi Greg / Thanks.
