Chapter 3: The Order of a Question

Why Questions Come Out Wrong

The word order is the whole game

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Where are you staying?"

"How long are you staying?"

"Do you have anything to declare?"

"What is the purpose of your trip?"

Question wordAuxiliary / beSubjectRest
Whereareyoustaying?
How longareyoustaying?
—Doyouhave anything to declare?
Whatisthe purposeof your trip?

The auxiliary always jumps in FRONT of the subject. In Portuguese it does not, and that is why the question comes out flat.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

✗ WRONG

"Where you are staying?"

✓ RIGHT

"Where are you staying?"

✗ WRONG

"How long you stay in the country?"

✓ RIGHT

"How long are you staying in the country?"

A question needs an auxiliary (do, does, did, are, is) and it goes before the subject. Without it, the sentence still sounds like a statement.

Did You Catch It?

About the Officer

1. What three things does the officer ask about?
The purpose of the trip, where he is staying, and whether he has anything to declare.
2. What does she ask about the journey itself?
Whether he had a layover on the way there.
3. Where does she send him at the end?
Downstairs to baggage claim, and then straight through customs.
Listening

The Boarding Announcement

An airport announcement. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. Which rows are boarding now, and at which gate?
Rows twenty to thirty, at gate B12.
2. What two documents must passengers have ready?
The boarding pass and the passport.
3. What must passengers with a layover do before customs?
Collect their luggage at baggage claim.
Listening

The Gate Change

Something changed. Catch the numbers.

0:000:00
1. Why is the flight delayed, and by how long?
Because of the weather, by one hour.
2. What is the new departure time and the new gate?
Nine fifteen, at gate A7 in terminal one.
3. What do passengers on a round trip NOT have to do?
They do not need to check in again.
