Chapter 3

Two Past Forms

One for your life, one for one day

Grammar Discovery

Which One Has a Date?

"Have you ever been to Japan?"

"I have never seen the northern lights."

"I went there in April and stayed for five days."

"I have been to Japan. I went two years ago."

FormUseExample
Have you ever + past participle?Ask about a life experience — no dateHave you ever been to Japan?
I have / have never + past participleYour life so far, with no dateI have never seen the northern lights.
I went / I stayed (simple past)One specific moment, with a date or a timeI went in April and stayed for five days.
General first, specific secondOpen with the experience, then zoom inI have been to Japan. I went two years ago.
⚠ CarefulAfter 'have', use the past participle: 'Have you ever been?', never 'went'. A specific date always takes the simple past: 'I went in 2019', never 'I have been there in 2019'. And 'never' goes between 'have' and the verb: 'I have never done that'.

Comprehension

Did You Catch It?

1. Where did Daniel travel alone, and when?
To Patagonia, three years ago.
2. What was the highlight of his trip?
One empty valley at six in the morning.
3. How did he feel on day four, and was the trip worth it?
He felt homesick, badly. But he says it was worthwhile — ever since then, his bucket list keeps getting longer.
Listening

The Dinner Table Question

Listen first. No text. Sound only.

1. What question does the speaker like to ask at dinner?
Have you ever done something you were sure you would never do?
2. What did the quiet man do, and how old was he?
He sold his car to pay for a month in Portugal, and he was thirty-one.
3. Why does the speaker love his story?
Because on paper it was a terrible decision — he came home with no car and no money — but ever since that trip he has never been afraid of starting over.
Listening

General First, Specific Second

A different voice, a different message. Listen carefully.

1. What is the difference between "I have been to Rome" and "I went to Rome in 2019"?
"I have been" talks about your life so far, with no date. "I went in 2019" puts the story on a date, with a place and a time.
2. In what order do native speakers tell the story?
General first (the experience), specific second (the date and the details).
3. What advice does he give for the next time somebody asks about your travels?
Do not begin with a year. Begin with what you have done, and leave the details for the second sentence.
