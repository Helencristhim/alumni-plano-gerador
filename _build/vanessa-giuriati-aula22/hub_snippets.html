Lesson 22 -- Pre-class

Talking About Past Experiences

Tell the story of an experience in the right order: open with the present perfect ("Have you ever...? / I have been to...") and give the details in the simple past ("I went in April"). Key words: highlight, ordinary, worthwhile, bucket list, once in a lifetime, homesick, get the chance, ever since.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Have you ever taken a trip completely alone? I had not, until last April, when I finally got the chance. I have been to twelve countries for work, but I had never gone anywhere just for myself. So I went to Japan, and I stayed for five days. The first two days were ordinary in the best way: I walked, I got lost, I ate alone. The highlight was the third morning, in an empty temple at six o'clock. On day four I felt homesick and called my sister. She asked if I would do it again, and I said yes before she finished the question. It was worthwhile — a once in a lifetime morning. Ever since then I have kept a bucket list on my phone.

1. Which sentence talks about her life so far, with no date?
A "I went to Japan and stayed for five days."
B "I have been to twelve countries for work."
C "On day four I felt homesick."
2. What was the highlight of the trip?
A The third morning, in an empty temple.
B Getting lost on the first day.
C The phone call with her sister.
3. Why does she say the trip was worthwhile?
A Because it was cheap.
B Because she would do it again, and it changed the way she plans her life.
C Because she was never homesick.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Experience first, date second

GRAMMAR

Two past forms, two jobs: one opens the story, the other gives the details.

StructureUseExample
Have you ever + past participle?Ask about a life experience — no dateHave you ever been to Japan?
I have / have never + past participleYour life so far, with no dateI have never seen the northern lights.
I went / I stayed (simple past)One specific moment, with a date or a timeI went in April and stayed for five days.
General first, specific secondOpen with the experience, then zoom inI have been to Japan. I went two years ago.

After 'have', use the past participle: 'Have you ever been?', never 'went'. A specific date always takes the simple past: 'I went in 2019'. And 'never' goes between 'have' and the verb: 'I have never done that'.

Stage 2: Tell the Experience in Order

Order

Listen first, then put the steps of a good experience story in the right order.

?First, I open with the experience itself: I have been to Japan, and I have never regretted it.
?Next, I say how I felt, including the uncomfortable part, like the day I was homesick.
?Then I zoom in with a date in the simple past: I went last April and stayed for five days.
?After that, I give one small detail that makes it real, like the empty temple at six in the morning.
?In the end, I say what changed: ever since that trip, my bucket list keeps getting longer.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

A colleague asks about your travels and you want to open the story. Best choice:
A "I went to Japan on April the fourteenth, 2024, at 11:20."
B "I have been to Japan — I went last April."
C "I have been to Japan in April."
You want to ask about a life experience. Best choice:
A "Have you ever been to Portugal?"
B "Have you ever went to Portugal?"
C "Did you ever have been to Portugal?"
You want to say something you have not done in your whole life. Best choice:
A "I never have seen snow."
B "I have never seen snow."
C "I have not never seen snow."
You want to name the best moment of a trip. Best choice:
A "The highlight was the first morning."
B "The highlight were the first morning."
C "The highlight has been in the first morning."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think of one experience you are proud of — a trip, a course, a job change, anything. Record yourself for about one minute: open with the present perfect ("I have..."), give the details in the simple past ("I went... I stayed..."), name the highlight, and say what has changed ever since.
