Chapter 3: The Negotiation

Three Points, One Call

A clause negotiation, line by line

Dialogue

The Cap and the Penalty

H
Amanda, good morning. Thank you for the position sheet. Shall we start with the penalty clause?
A
Good morning, Henrik. Yes, and I read your comments. Two per cent a week is our number, but I have some room there.
H
Good. And on liability, we want a cap at the value of the shipment.
A
That is the one I cannot give you. If we capped our liability at one shipment, a serious quality problem would cost us five times what you could claim.
H
I understand the logic. But our commercial team will not sign an unlimited number.
A
Then let us find the middle. If you accept a cap at the annual contract value, we will bring the penalty down to one and a half per cent.
H
That is interesting. And the dispute section? We asked for arbitration in London.
A
London is acceptable, but not for free. If I gave you London and the cap on the same day, I would have nothing left to take to my director.
H
Fair enough. So: London, the annual cap, and one and a half per cent. I will take that to my side.
A
Agreed, subject to approval. If your team confirms by Friday, we will send the clean version on Monday.
Comprehension

About the Negotiation

1. What does Henrik say his commercial team will not accept?
An unlimited liability number.
2. Why is a cap at the value of one shipment refused?
Because a serious quality problem would cost five times more than the buyer could claim.
3. What is the final package, and what has to happen before it is real?
Arbitration in London, a cap at the annual contract value and a penalty of one and a half per cent. Henrik's team has to confirm by Friday, and it is subject to approval.
Chapter 4: The Code

The If That Will, and the If That Would

Real and likely, or hypothetical

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"If they sign on Friday, the shipment will leave in three weeks."

"If we capped our liability at one shipment, we would lose money."

"If you accept the annual cap, we will bring the penalty down."

"If I gave you London today, I would have nothing left for my director."

Two are about something that is probably going to happen. Two are about something that probably is not. Which is which?

FormUseExample
First conditional
if + present, will + verb		real and likely: this is on the table todayIf they sign on Friday, we will send the clean version on Monday.
Second conditional
if + past, would + verb		hypothetical: you are imagining it in order to show what is wrong with itIf we capped the liability, we would lose money.
The past is not the pastin the second conditional, capped and gave are not about yesterday. The past form is what marks the sentence as imaginary.
Never will after ifsay "if they sign", never "if they will sign". The will belongs to the other half of the sentence.
Why it matters herethe second conditional is how you say no without closing the door: you do not refuse the person, you show the consequence of the idea.

Sound Check

Say It Clearly

The weak form of would · the sentences of this lesson

Why this one is hard for a Portuguese speaker

The habit: every word arrives at full weight, so the sentence comes out as I. WOULD. PREFER, and it sounds heavier than you mean it.

In English: the helper disappears into the pronoun. "I'd prefer." "We'd need." "They'd accept." One syllable, and the stress lands on the verb.

The exception you want: the full form is for emphasis. "I would accept that, but not today." Say it strong only when you mean to.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Both Sides, Before the Call

What they want, and what you can actually give

Listening 1

The Other Side Shows Its Hand

Henrik sends his comments the night before. Sound first, no text.

1. Why is two per cent a week too high for the buyer?
Because their own insurance premium goes up, and the commercial team will not sign.
2. What cap does the buyer have in mind, and what is the alternative it replaces?
The value of the shipment, instead of the value of the whole contract.
3. What does Henrik offer in exchange for arbitration in London?
He agrees to the rest of the dispute section as it stands.
Listening 2

Where Your Line Is

Karin, from risk, tells you what you can and cannot give. Sound first, no text.

1. What penalty number is acceptable to risk, and why?
Anything above one per cent a week, because the insurer covers the difference.
2. Why does Karin worry about the liability cap?
Because a serious quality problem would cost five times what the company could claim, so she would not accept it without the finance director.
3. What does she say about London, and why is Amanda not in a weak position?
London is acceptable but should be exchanged for something on the penalty. And if the buyer walks away, there is still the other buyer.

Sample data for this lesson. Nothing here is a real company record.
1. The sheet has an opening and a floor for the penalty. What is the difference, and why write both?
The opening is where you start; the floor is the lowest you can accept. Writing both means you can move without asking for permission in the middle of the call.
2. Say, in one second-conditional sentence, why the cap they want is refused.
Example: "If we capped our liability at one shipment, a serious quality problem would cost us far more than the buyer could claim."
3. The last line but one says a second buyer is available. How does that change the way you speak in the room?
It means walking away is a real option, so the no can be calm and does not have to sound like a threat.
