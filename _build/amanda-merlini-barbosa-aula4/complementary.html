Film
Michael Clayton (2007)

An in-house chief counsel of an agrochemical company has to decide what a class action is worth, and how far she will go to close it. The vocabulary of this lesson -- liability, settlement, the cost of a recall -- is the whole plot.

Tip: watch the scenes with the in-house counsel first, with English subtitles. Every time she refuses something, write down how she does it: does she refuse the person, or show a consequence?

Podcast
Negotiate Anything -- Kwame Christian and Codie Sanchez: One Hour Straight of Negotiation Gold

A lawyer who negotiates for a living, talking for an hour about the part that is not technique: how to hold a position and still sound like somebody the other side wants to keep working with.

Tip: stop the first time you hear a conditional sentence and write it down. By the end you should have five, and you will notice how many of them are refusals.

Video
William Ury: The walk from "no" to "yes"

The co-author of Getting to Yes on what happens between a refusal and an agreement. Eighteen minutes, slow and clear, and the idea of stepping onto the balcony is one you can use in a real call.

Tip: watch with English subtitles and note the moment he explains the third side. Then say, in one sentence, who the third side is in your own negotiations.
