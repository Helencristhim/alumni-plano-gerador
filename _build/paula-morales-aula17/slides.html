Chapter 3: The Code

Two Words for the Whole Past

E não há uma terceira.

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

Quando é was e quando é were?

The drive was three hours.

I was tired at the end.

The questions were difficult.

Twelve people were in the room.

Um sujeito, was. Vários sujeitos, were. E o I… fica com qual?

Hoje (aula 2)Ontem (aula 17)
I am · he/she/it isI was · he/she/it was
you/we/they areyou/we/they were
A regra em uma linha: am e is viram was; are vira were. São duas palavras para as seis pessoas — não existe uma terceira. O único que engana é o you: mesmo para UMA pessoa, é sempre you were.
The Relief

To Be Says No By Itself

Na aula 15 você precisou de um ajudante para negar. Hoje, não.

AfirmativoNegativo
The room was crowded.The room wasn't crowded.
I was tired.I wasn't tired.
The questions were easy.The questions weren't easy.
They were all engineers.They weren't all engineers.

Repare no que sumiu: não tem didn't aqui. O verbo to be nega sozinho — was + not = wasn't, were + not = weren't. E é por isso que “I didn't tired” não existe: o certo é “I wasn't tired”.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"The questions was difficult."
"The questions were difficult."
"I didn't tired on Friday."
"I wasn't tired on Friday."

O primeiro erro é olhar para o adjetivo em vez do sujeito: quem manda em was/were é quem vem antes, e questions é plural. O segundo vem direto da aula 15: o didn't só nega verbos de ação (go, apply, ask). Tired não é ação, é estado — e estado nega com wasn't.

The Three Shapes

sujeito primeiro

Listening

David and the Word “Fine”

Sound first — no text. Leia as perguntas antes de ouvir.

0:000:00
1. What did David answer the first time his manager asked how the visit was?
He said one word: "fine". That was all — and the conversation stopped there.
2. What did the manager ask two days later, and what was he really doing?
He asked three questions: was the room crowded, were the questions difficult, were you tired at the end. He was not testing David's English — he was teaching him the difference between a report (what happened) and a description (what it was like).
3. What is David's practical recipe for the next time somebody asks "how was it"?
Five short sentences with was and were: the drive was long, the room was crowded, the manager was difficult at the start, you were tired but it was useful. After that, he says, your colleague was there with you.

Comprehension

What Did Greg Learn?

1. How long was the drive, and how was the factory?
The drive was three hours, and the factory was huge. It was a long day from the start.
2. How many people were in the meeting room, and who were they?
Twelve people were in the room, so it was crowded. Six were engineers, and the others were from the client's finance team — they weren't all engineers.
3. How was the technical manager at the start, and how was he after lunch?
He was difficult at the start, and his questions were very difficult. After lunch the room was quiet and he was patient.
4. Greg says he is surprised. Why?
Because it was her first visit alone, twelve people were in the room, and the drive was three hours. He is surprised by the size of the day, not by her English.

The numbers were right — twelve people, six hours, fourteen technical questions — and nobody in the team writes a report that clean.
