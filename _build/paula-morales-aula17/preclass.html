Lesson 17 -- Pre-class

How Was It? -- Was and Were, and the Debrief After the Visit

In lesson 16 you learned to promise the future with will. Today you go back to the past through another door: lesson 14 was what happened, lesson 15 was what did not happen, and lesson 17 is how things were. There are only two words: was (for I, he, she, it) and were (for you, we, they). And it comes with the fix for a mistake you are about to make: an adjective does not go with didn't — it is I wasn't tired, never I didn't tired. The scene is your first site visit alone, at a factory in Campinas, and the Monday call where Greg asks the simplest question in corporate life: “How was it?” You take six new words: difficult, quiet, crowded, tired, useful, surprised. This Pre-class is a first contact — come to class with the six in your ear.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Six words that answer the same question: how was it? Listen to each one and repeat it out loud. The sound traps of the day: DI-fi-kult (no “e” at the end) · KWAI-et (do not mix it up with quite) · TAI-erd (one syllable) · ser-PRAIZD (stress on the second).

Difficult -- not easy to do
"The questions were difficult." Say DI-fi-kult, three syllables — with no “e” at the end.
Quiet -- with very little noise
"After lunch the room was quiet." Say KWAI-et, two syllables — quite is another word.
Crowded -- full of people
"The meeting room was crowded." Say KRAU-did — here the -ed sounds like “id”.
Tired -- with no energy left
"I was tired, but it was a good day." Say TAI-erd, one stretched syllable — never “ti-RED”.
Useful -- that helps you do something
"It was six hours, but it was useful." Say YOOS-ful — it starts with “yoo” and the S is dry.
Surprised -- feeling something you did not expect
"Greg was surprised." Say ser-PRAIZD, stress on the second syllable — and notice: it goes with was / were, never with didn't.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the story and answer the questions. The words in bold are today's two — look at who comes before each one: what decides was or were is the subject, never the adjective.

The first site visit alone, told by Paula. “I left Osasco at five in the morning. The drive was three hours, and when I arrived the factory was already running. The factory was huge.”

“The meeting was in a small room and the room was crowded: twelve people. And they were not all engineers — six were engineers, the rest were from the client's finance team. The technical manager started hard: his questions were very difficult for me.”

“After lunch it changed. The room was quiet and he was patient with me. I left at six. I was tired, but it was useful. On Friday morning I was worried. On Friday evening, I was not any more.”

1. Why is it “the questions were difficult” and not “the questions was difficult”?
A Because difficult is a long word.
B Because what decides is the subject, and questions is plural — plural takes were.
C Because the sentence is a question.
2. How many people were in the room, and who were they?
A Twelve people — six were engineers and the others were from the client's finance team.
B Twelve people, and they were all engineers.
C Six people, all from finance.
3. What was the technical manager like, from start to finish?
A He was hard all day and did not change.
B He started hard, with difficult questions; after lunch he was patient.
C He said nothing in the meeting.
4. In “I was tired”, why do we not say “I didn't tired”?
A Because didn't only exists in questions.
B Because tired is an adjective, and adjectives go with was/were — their negative is wasn't.
C Because the sentence is in the present.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- two words for the whole past

GRAMMAR

In lesson 2 you learned I am, he is, they are. Today it is the same thing, only yesterday: am and is become was, are becomes were. Two words for six persons, and there is no third one.

FormIn EnglishWhen you use it
Affirmative — singularI was tired · the room was crowdedeu estava cansada · a sala estava lotada
Affirmative — pluralyou/we/they were · the questions were difficultyou / we / they · the questions were difficult
NegativeI wasn't tired · they weren't engineersI wasn't tired · they weren't engineers
Yes/no questionWas the room crowded? · Were they engineers?a sala estava lotada? · eles eram engenheiros?
Open questionHow was the visit? · How were the people?How was the visit? · How were the people?
Short answerYes, it was. / No, it wasn't.yes / no

The two mistakes that feel natural to make, and the fix for each one:

1) “The questions was difficult.” — you looked at the adjective. What decides is the subject, and questions is plural: “The questions were difficult.” Always ask “who?” before you choose.

2) “I didn't tired on Friday.” — this mistake is born in lesson 15, and it is a sign that you learned it well: didn't negates action verbs (go, apply, ask). Tired is not an action, it is a state — and a state makes its own negative: “I wasn't tired on Friday.”

Today's relief: to ask a question with to be you need no helper at all. In lesson 15 it was Did you apply?; here the word itself jumps to the front: Was the room crowded? Were you tired? One piece, three uses.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best option for each real situation — Greg on the call, the colleague at the coffee machine, Monday morning.

Greg asks how the visit went. You want to say that the room was crowded. You say:
A "The room were crowded."
B "The room was crowded. Twelve people were there."
C "The room is crowded yesterday."
You want to say that you were not tired on Friday morning. You say:
A "I didn't tired on Friday morning."
B "I wasn't tired on Friday morning."
C "I not was tired on Friday morning."
You want to ask a colleague how her week was. You say:
A "How was your week?"
B "How did your week was?"
C "How were your week?"
Greg asks: "Were they all engineers?" Only six were. You answer short and right:
A "No, they didn't."
B "No, they weren't. Six were engineers."
C "No, they wasn't engineers."
Somebody asks "How was it?". You want to describe the whole day, not answer with one word. You say:
A "It was long. The room was crowded, the questions were difficult, and I was tired. But it was useful."
B "It were long. The room were crowded, the questions was difficult, and I were tired."
C "Fine."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record your answer. Speak for 60 to 90 seconds, with no script. Do not redo it more than twice. This is exactly the homework audio — start building it here.

Somebody asks you one question: “How was your first visit alone?” Answer out loud, describing the day from the start (the road, at five in the morning) to the end (Friday evening). Use at least five sentences with was or were and at least one negative with wasn't or weren't. The only rule of this recording is: answering “fine” is banned. It does not have to be perfect — it has to be yours.
