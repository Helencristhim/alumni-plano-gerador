Basic English Grammar: How to Use WAS and WERE (engVid, with Rebecca, free)

A whole class about today's two words, in another voice and with other examples. Rebecca writes on the board and goes straight to what slips most: who takes was, who takes were, and the mistake of looking at the adjective instead of the subject. Hearing the same rule twice, from different people, is what makes it automatic.

Tip: watch it with English subtitles (the video's own CC). Do one thing only: every time she writes a sentence, pause and say ONE sentence of your own in the same mould, about your working week (“The meeting was long”, “The clients were friendly”). By the end of the video you should have said about five. Open channel, no sign-up.

Basic English Grammar — “Was” and “Were” (engVid, with Ronnie, free)

The same rule, now through the ear. Ronnie goes quickly through the form and spends the time where it matters for you: the pronunciation and the questions. This is where you find out that, in real speech, was and were almost disappear in the middle of a sentence — and that is why you do not hear them on the Miami calls.

Tip: listen twice. The first time, just listen. The second time, repeat EVERY sentence of hers out loud, copying the rhythm and leaving the was weak and unstressed. Then take the five sentences from your Survival Card and say them all with the same rhythm. Open and free, no sign-up.

Let's Learn English — Level 1, Lesson 24: Yesterday Was Amazing! (VOA Learning English, free)

It is not a grammar class: it is a story acted out, with subtitles on screen and American English spoken slowly on purpose. Anna wants to tell a friend how her day went, and the whole lesson is that: describing a day that has already passed. was turns up dozens of times in a real conversation, and not in a table. The page has the video, the full transcript and exercises — all open.

Tip: watch it once only, without stopping, and do one thing: write down every sentence that starts with It was or I was. Then close the video and answer the same question about yourself, out loud: how was your last working day outside the office? You do not have to understand every word; the goal is to get your mouth producing was and were without freezing. VOA is an American public service, free and with no sign-up.
