Chapter 3: The Code

Big, Medium, Small

Three words, and the size decides which one

Grammar Discovery

What Comes After Each One?

"We close the quarter in September."

"The review is on the third Monday of every month."

"The call starts at nine, and it ends at ten."

"Our headquarters is in Sao Paulo, and my analyst is at the office today."

Look at the red part after each blue word. One of the three always takes the longest period of time, and one of them always takes the shortest.

WordTakesExample
in — timemonths, years, seasons, parts of the dayin September · in 2027 · in the morning
on — timedays and dateson Monday · on September the thirtieth · on Monday morning
at — timeclock times and nightat nine · at half past eight · at night
in — placecountries, cities, rooms, buildings seen from insidein Brazil · in Sao Paulo · in the room
at — placea point: an address, a place you go toat the office · at headquarters · at home
American Englishthe weekend takes on, not atI never work on the weekend.
No word at allbefore next, last, this, everyWe meet every Monday. Not in every Monday.

In one line: in for the big box, on for the day inside it, at for the point on the clock.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Two Calendars, One Hour

One conversation, three recordings, one invite

Dialogue

Finding an Hour That Works

M
Thais, we need a monthly slot for the methodology review. Does Monday work for you?
T
Monday is difficult. I close the numbers on Monday morning, and the deadline is at noon.
M
Understood. What about Wednesday, at four in the afternoon, my time?
T
Four for you is five for me, and I pick up my children at that time. Can we do it in the morning instead?
M
The morning works. Eight my time, nine yours. We are only one time zone apart.
T
Nine works. Let us put it on the third Wednesday of every month, so it never falls in the closing week.
M
Done. I will send the invite today and I will put the agenda in the description.
T
Perfect. And if something breaks, we reschedule it to Thursday, at the same time. Does that work for you?
Comprehension

About Mark

1. What does Mark ask for at the beginning, and which day does he suggest first?
He asks for a monthly slot for the methodology review, and he suggests Monday first.
2. What time does he offer second, and what does he say about the two time zones?
He offers Wednesday at four in the afternoon, his time. Later he says eight his time is nine hers, because they are only one time zone apart.
3. What two things does he say he will do at the end?
He will send the invite today, and he will put the agenda in the description.
Listening 1 · The Team Calendar

How the Calendar Works

An operations lead explaining the rhythm of a global team. Sound first — no text.

1. When is the weekly call, and what time does it start?
On Tuesday, at eight in the morning, New York time.
2. What happens in the last week of every quarter?
Nobody books a new meeting. The week is kept free for the closing.
3. What is his rule about the agenda?
No agenda, no meeting. He says the agenda goes in the invite, never in a separate message.
Listening 2 · A Podcast

Protecting the First Hour

A remote-work researcher on calendars across time zones. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say happens to the person in the earlier time zone?
That person loses the first hour of the day, because the meetings of the other country land on top of it.
2. What is the one thing she tells people to put in the calendar?
The hour they do their own work, as a real event with a name, so that nobody books over it.
3. What does she say about saying no to a time?
Saying no to a time is not saying no to the meeting. She says to offer another hour in the same sentence.
Listening 3 · Business Radio

The Country That Shares the Clock

A short business radio report on Brazil and the working day. Sound first — no text.

1. What advantage does the report give Brazil over India for American companies?
Brazil shares almost the whole working day with the United States, and India shares almost none of it.
2. What does the report say about the size of the country itself?
Brazil has more than one time zone inside it, so a company in Sao Paulo and a customer in the north are not always on the same clock.
3. What does the report say the advantage costs?
The Brazilian side takes the early meetings and the late ones, because it is the side that can.
