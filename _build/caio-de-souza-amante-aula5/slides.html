Chapter 3: How Certain Do You Sound?

Four Sentences, Four Sizes of Promise

The word between the subject and the verb is the whole message

Grammar Discovery

Order These, Biggest Promise to Smallest

"We will deliver the transition in ninety days."

"We can deliver the transition in ninety days."

"That might slip to a hundred and twenty, and it could cost us two people."

"Governance has to be agreed first, and every contract must meet European compliance."

The green one is a commitment somebody can hold you to. The blue one says you are capable, which is not the same thing. The orange one costs you nothing and buys you room. The red one is not about you at all — it is a rule.

A real possibility, and it costs you nothingcould · may · mightThis could represent a significant opportunity.

The reliefafter a modal the verb is bare — no -s, everNot she can delivers. Say she can deliver.
The trapmust in American business sounds imposedFor obligation from outside, prefer have to.

In one line: the modal is the size of the promise. Choose it before you choose the verb.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Three People, Three Accents

The committee call, two recordings, and the ninety-day plan

Dialogue

Forty Minutes with the Committee

S
Caio, thanks for joining. Anders is on as well. We have got about forty minutes and there are three things we need to get through, so I am going to move quickly.
C
Understood. Go ahead.
S
First one. If we sign in June, how fast can you deliver a transition?
C
We can deliver it in ninety days. I want to be precise about that word: we are capable of ninety. Whether we will depends on when your side names the governance structure.
A
That is a fair distinction. My question is different. What happens to your senior people the week they find out?
C
Sorry, Anders — could you say that again, a little slower? I want to be sure I understood you correctly.
A
Of course. Retention. The week your team learns the company has been sold. What happens?
C
Three of the eleven might take a call from a competitor. That is honest. What I will do is hand over every client relationship myself, one at a time, before anybody is told.
S
That is the first honest retention answer I have had this quarter. Anders?
A
Agreed. Then the last one, and it is the one that decides this. Every contract must meet European compliance before we can consolidate. How far are you from that today?
C
Further than I would like, and I would rather you heard that from me. Can I send you the gap analysis this week, and we take that one properly at the next meeting?
Comprehension

About Susan and Anders

1. How does Susan open the call, and what does she say about time?
She says Anders is on as well, that they have about forty minutes and three things to get through, and that she will move quickly.
2. What is the difference between the question Susan asks and the one Anders asks?
Susan asks how fast the transition can be delivered. Anders asks what happens to the senior people the week they find out.
3. What does Anders do when he is asked to repeat, and what does he say is the question that decides the deal?
He repeats it shorter, not louder, and names it as retention. The one that decides it is whether every contract meets European compliance.
Listening 1 · A Board Advisor

The Difference Between Can and Will

A board advisor on the word founders use by accident. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say happens to a founder who says will when he means can?
The sentence gets written into the minutes, and six months later somebody reads it back to him as a commitment he never intended to make.
2. What does he say non-native speakers do that native speakers also do?
They reach for the strongest word available because they want to sound confident, and the strongest word is almost never the accurate one.
3. What does he recommend saying instead?
Say what you are capable of, then say separately what you will commit to, and make the two sentences audibly different.
Listening 2 · An Integration Lead

Where Acquisitions Actually Fail

An integration lead on the ninety days after the money moves. Sound first — no text.

1. How many integrations has she run, and what does she say about where deals fail?
Around thirty, and she says almost none of them fail at the price -- they fail in the ninety days afterwards.
2. What does she say is the first thing that has to be decided?
Governance -- who decides what, and who has to be told -- and she says it has to be agreed before closing, never after.
3. What does she say about founders who promise their people will stay?
That nobody believes them, and that the founder who says three of my eleven might leave is the one she trusts with the rest of the plan.

Read the right-hand column before you promise anything on the left. Two of these six are the buyer's to deliver, not yours, and saying so out loud in the meeting is not a defence -- it is the plan.
1. Which items on the plan are not the seller's to deliver?
Governance in days one to fifteen depends on the buyer, and the compliance gap and the roadmap are both sides.

Handing over the client relationships, in the first thirty days.
3. What does the note say about the right-hand column?
To read it before promising anything on the left, and that saying out loud what belongs to the buyer is the plan, not a defence.
