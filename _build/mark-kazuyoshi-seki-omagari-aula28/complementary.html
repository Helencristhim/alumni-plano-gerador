Lesson 28 -- Some People Think So, Others Do Not

Film
12 Angry Men (1957) -- the first thirty minutes

Twelve men are locked in one room and have to reach a consensus. Eleven of them start on the same side, one pushes back, and the whole film is him swaying them one at a time. Almost nobody in it ever repeats a whole sentence: they answer each other with "So do I", "Neither did he", "the one with the knife". This lesson is that room.

Tip: watch the first thirty minutes with English subtitles and write down five short answers where a whole sentence has been deleted. Next to each one, write the long version nobody bothered to say.

Podcast
BBC 6 Minute English -- Is it OK to disagree?

Six minutes of two presenters doing exactly what you practised in class: agreeing in three words, disagreeing without repeating the other person, and holding a position without turning it into an argument. It is the natural speed of the structures you just learned.

Tip: the full transcript is on the same page. Listen once without it and count every "so" and "neither" you hear. Then read the transcript and check whether you caught them all.

YouTube
English with Lucy -- Me too vs Me neither, So do I vs Neither do I

The exact trap from the lesson, taken apart slowly: why "I am tired" takes "So am I" and never "So do I", and where the casual versions ("me too", "me neither") are fine and where they are not. Watch it after the class, not before -- it will confirm the rule you already worked out.

Tip: pause the video every time she gives a new sentence and say your own agreement out loud before she does. If your auxiliary does not match hers, rewind ten seconds and find out why.
