Question: Will the second poll change anything?

"I hope that the second poll will change something."

Correct English. Ten words, and eight of them were already in the question.

Shorter. The verb is gone and nothing was lost.

"I hope so."

One word standing in for the whole clause. This is what people say.

Portuguese wearing an English coat. Nobody says this.

Repeating a phrase the other person just used does not sound careful in English. It sounds like you did not listen.

Chapter 3: The Shortcut

One Small Word Instead of Nine

What English deletes, and what it leaves behind

Grammar Discovery

Four Answers, Four Deletions

Will the second poll change anything? I hope so.

Olivia gets the early bus, and so do I.

The drivers did not like the plan, and neither did the teachers.

The old buses are slow. The new ones are worse.

Say the long version of each answer out loud. Then look at what is standing in its place, and how short that thing is.

ToolWhat it replacesExample
so / nota whole clause, after think, hope, believe, guess, be afraid"Will it pass?" — "I think so." / "I am afraid not."
do / does / dida whole verb phrase already said"She trains harder than I do."
so + auxiliary + subjectagreeing with something positive"I get the early bus." — "So do I."
neither / nor + auxiliary + subjectagreeing with something negative"I did not vote." — "Neither did I."
one / onesa countable noun already named"The blue one." / "The ones who voted no."
bare tothe verb after to, when it was just said"Do you want to speak?" — "I would love to."
ellipsis after and / but / orDelete the repeated subject or verb: "I read the report but I did not finish the report." Both halves have to share the same subject for this to work.

The Tool

Three Places It Goes Wrong

Trap 1 · "I think yes" does not exist

Portuguese answers with sim. English answers with so: "I think so", "I hope so", "I guess so", "I am afraid not". Never "I think yes", and never "I think that" on its own.

Trap 2 · the auxiliary has to match

"I play on Saturdays" takes "So do I". "I am tired" takes "So am I". "I have finished" takes "So have I". Learning "so do I" first and then using it everywhere is the single most common error here.

Trap 3 · after "to", stop — but keep the "to"

"Do you want to come?" — "I would love to." Deleting the verb is right; deleting the to as well leaves "I would love", which is not a sentence.

Deleting the right amount is the skill. Delete too little and you are long-winded; delete too much and nobody can rebuild the sentence.

Because of the hour at the other end? Somebody made that point on Friday and nobody backed him up.
O
Exactly. You lose an hour in the morning, I lose one at night. The ones who live nearby lose nothing at all.
M
That is fair, and I think we have found our common ground. I would still vote yes, but I would not push back if it failed.
O
Do you think the second one will settle it?
M
I hope so. If they print the cost at the top, people will read it. At least I hope they will.
O
So do I. And that is the first thing the two of us have agreed on all week.
Comprehension

About Olivia

1. How did she vote, and who voted the same way?
She voted no, and neither did anybody else on her bus — a group she says nobody in the hall noticed.
2. What does she say a later start would cost her?
An hour at the other end of the day. She would lose it at night, while Mark loses his in the morning.
3. Who does she say loses nothing at all?
The ones who live near the school, because they have no long journey at either end of the day.
Listening

What English Leaves Out

Your teacher on the five substitutes, and on the trap that catches everybody. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does English do instead of repeating a phrase both people already have?
It drops in a small substitute word and moves on — so, not, do, one, ones, or a bare "to".
2. Which verbs take "so" and "not" after them?
Think, hope, believe, guess and be afraid — "I hope so", "I am afraid not".
3. What has to match the sentence you are answering?
The auxiliary. "I am tired" takes "so am I", "I play on Saturdays" takes "so do I", "I have finished" takes "so have I".
4. What mistake does she say most students make?
Learning "so do I" first and then using it for everything, so it keeps coming out after "I am tired".

The Speech That Swayed the Room

Four minutes of argument, delivered in ninety seconds. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Why does the speaker refuse to repeat the first three speeches?
All three said the same thing, so saying it a fourth time would be redundant. He wants to say the part nobody has said yet.
2. What does he say is the only common ground in the hall?
That everybody wants more sleep — the yes camp does, the no camp does, and so do the teachers.
3. Who does he say would actually lose the extra hour?
The ones on the four fifteen bus, the ones with training and the ones with a job on Saturdays.
4. What does he want the second poll to include?
The price of an extra hour of buses, printed at the top of the page.

Fix the Answer
