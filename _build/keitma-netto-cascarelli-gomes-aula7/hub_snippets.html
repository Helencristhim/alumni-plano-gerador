The Auditors Are In

One question opens the meeting: how is it approved? Key words: external auditor, working paper, sample, supporting documentation, finding, internal control, to substantiate, materiality, query, to escalate. Structure: the passive voice in present and past simple.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An external auditor -- the outside firm that checks the accounts and signs an opinion on them
"The external auditors arrive in January, every year."
A working paper -- the document that shows how a number was built, kept for the auditor
"Every working paper is filed before the auditors arrive."
A sample -- a small group of items chosen to stand for the whole population
"They selected a sample of forty entries above ten thousand."
Supporting documentation -- the invoice, contract or statement that proves the number is real
"The supporting documentation is attached to each working paper."
A finding -- something the auditor decided to write down and report
"There was one finding last year, and it was closed in March."
An internal control -- a step in the process that exists to stop something going wrong
"The review before sign-off is an internal control, not a formality."
To substantiate -- to prove a number with a document, not with an explanation
"Every balance above the threshold has to be substantiated."
Materiality -- the size above which a difference has to be reported
"It is below materiality, so it goes on the list and not in the report."
A query -- a written question the auditor sends and expects an answer to
"There are eleven open queries and nine of them are mine."
To escalate -- to pass a decision up to somebody with the authority to take it
"Anything above the threshold is escalated to the manager the same day."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

An auditor asks how the reconciliation is approved, and somebody answers that Ana does it. The answer is true, it is helpful in tone, and it has just created two questions where there was one — because the auditor still does not know what happens in the month when Ana is on holiday. He is not testing Ana. He is testing whether a control exists at all, and a control that depends on one person having a good week is not a control.

English has a shape built for exactly this, and it is the one that describes a process instead of a person. The passive puts the thing that is controlled at the front of the sentence and the verb into be plus the past participle: the reconciliation is prepared by the analyst, reviewed by the manager and signed off before the fifth working day. For a step that happens every time, use the present: is prepared. For one occasion that is finished, use the past: a sample of forty entries was selected last week. For a rule rather than a habit, add a modal: anything above materiality has to be escalated.

Two details make the difference between sounding precise and sounding evasive. The first is that the agent, when you give one, comes after by and it is a role, not a name: reviewed by the manager, not reviewed by Ana. Very often you can leave the agent out completely, and that is not vagueness — it is the correct answer when who did it does not change the control. The second is the participle itself: is prepared, never is prepare and never is preparing. Get those two right and something changes in the room. The question is answered, no new question is opened, and the meeting moves on to the next line.

1. Why does “Ana does it” create two questions instead of one?
A Because the auditor does not trust Ana.
B Because the auditor still does not know what happens when Ana is on holiday.
C Because names are not allowed in an audit meeting.
2. When do you use was selected instead of is selected?
A For one occasion that is finished, instead of a step that happens every time.
B When the sentence is written rather than spoken.
C When you do not know who did it.
3. According to the text, when is leaving out the agent the correct answer?
A Whenever you would rather not say who is responsible.
B When who did it does not change the control.
C Only in written English, never when speaking.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive Voice

GRAMMAR

The thing that is controlled at the front. The role only if the role is the control.

ShapeWhen you need itExample
is / are + past participlea step that happens every timeThe reconciliation is prepared every month.
was / were + past participleone occasion, finishedThe sample was selected last week.
by + the roleonly when the role matters — and it is the role, not the nameReviewed by the manager, not by Ana.
no agent at allwhen who did it does not change the controlEvery balance above the threshold is substantiated.
has to be · must bea rule, not a habitAnything above materiality has to be escalated.
the first trapthe participle, never the infinitive or the -ingNot is prepare, not is preparing. Say is prepared.
the second trapthe agent takes by, never from or forNot signed off from the manager. Say signed off by.
the one that travels with ityou send papers to the auditors, on a day, in a monthI sent the working papers to the auditors on Friday.

Never: it is prepare by the analyst · signed off from the manager · selected in last week · I sent the papers for the auditors

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The auditor asks how the reconciliation is approved. The strongest answer is:
A Ana does it, she is very careful.
B It is prepared by the analyst, reviewed by the manager, and signed off before day five.
C It is prepare by the analyst and after the manager see it.
You are describing what happened last week, once. You say:
A A sample of forty entries is selected in last week.
B A sample of forty entries was selected last week.
C A sample of forty entries has selected last week.
You want to say that the manager approves it, and the role is the control. You say:
A It is signed off by the accounting manager.
B It is signed off from the accounting manager.
C It is signed off for the accounting manager.
The auditor asks for something another team holds. You say:
A I think it is with the tax people, I am not sure.
B I do not have that with me. It is held by the tax team, and it will be sent to you today.
C I will check and come back to you.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Thirteen minutes on what happens in organisations when nobody asks the uncomfortable question — and on the people whose job is to ask it anyway. It is the audit relationship, described from the outside.

Tip: listen for how often she describes a process rather than a person. Write down three of those sentences and check the shape: what is the verb doing?
