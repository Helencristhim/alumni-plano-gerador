Chapter 3: Who Did It, and Does It Matter

The Process, Not the Person

One shape, and the auditor stops asking

Grammar Discovery

Two Ways to Say One Thing

Ana prepares the reconciliation every month."

"The reconciliation is prepared by the analyst every month."

"Somebody selected forty entries last week."

"A sample of forty entries was selected last week."

Two pairs, and each pair says the same thing. In the blue version, what happened to the person — and what moved to the front of the sentence?

ShapeWhen you need itExample
is / are + past participlea step that happens every time — the process, not one occasionThe reconciliation is prepared every month.
was / were + past participleone occasion, finishedThe sample was selected last week.
by + the roleonly when the role matters — and it is the ROLE, not the nameIt is reviewed by the manager, not by Ana.
no by at allwhen who did it does not change the answerEvery balance above the threshold is substantiated.
has to be / must bea rule, not a habitAnything above materiality has to be escalated.
the first trapthe participle, never the infinitiveNot it is prepare and not it is preparing. Say it is prepared.
the second trapthe agent takes by, never from or forNot signed off from the manager. Say signed off by the manager.

In one line: the auditor is testing the control, not the colleague. Put the thing that is controlled at the front, and name the role only if the role is the control.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Walkthrough, Out Loud

One meeting, two voice notes and one request list

Dialogue

Take Me Through the Control

M
Thanks for the time. Let us start with the balance sheet reconciliation. How is it approved?
K
It is prepared by the analyst, reviewed by the accounting manager, and signed off before the fifth working day. The review is the internal control.
M
Good. And what happens if the reviewer finds a difference?
K
Anything above materiality is escalated to the manager the same day. Below that, it is listed and cleared in the following month.
M
Understood. We selected a sample of forty entries last week. Is the supporting documentation kept with the working paper, or somewhere else?
K
With the working paper. Every balance above the threshold is substantiated, and the invoice is attached in the same folder.
M
Last thing. There was one finding last year. What was done about it?
K
The finding was closed in March. The step that was missing is now part of the checklist. And you — how many open queries do you have on our side today?
Comprehension

About Mark

1. Which process does Mark want to start with, and what exactly does he ask about it?
The balance sheet reconciliation, and he asks how it is approved.
2. What did his team do last week, and what does he want to know about it?
They selected a sample of forty entries, and he wants to know whether the supporting documentation is kept with the working paper or somewhere else.
3. What is the last thing he asks about?
The finding from last year, and what was done about it.
Listening 1 · A Voice Note

Tell Me the Process, Not the Person

An audit partner recording a note for a client team before fieldwork. Sound first — no text.

1. What habit does he say turns a two-day request into a two-week request?
Answering a question about a process by talking about a person.
2. Why does “Ana does it” leave him with two questions instead of one?
Because he still does not know what happens when Ana is on holiday.
3. What sentence does he say he wants instead, and what does it let him do?
That the reconciliation is prepared by the analyst, reviewed by the manager and signed off before the fifth working day — so he knows what the control is and can test it.
Listening 2 · A Manager

Answer the Question, Then Stop

An accounting manager on what she learned in three closings.

1. What did she do in her first year, and why did it make things worse?
She answered every question with everything she knew — eight minutes of context — and the auditors came back with four new questions about things they had not been looking at.
2. What are the three steps of her method now?
Answer the question that was asked, say where the evidence is, and then stop talking.
3. What does she do when she does not know the answer?
She says so, she says who does know, and she says when it will reach them.
