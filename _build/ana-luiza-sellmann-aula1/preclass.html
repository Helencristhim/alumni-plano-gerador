Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

Who Is Ana Luiza? -- The First Real Conversation

Talking about who you are: your routine, your taste and your work. Key words: early riser, to wind down, to be into, to take up, laid-back, picky, to come up with, to pitch. Structure: present simple for what is always true, present continuous for what is true right now.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. Do not translate — the example sentence is the meaning.

Early riser -- a person who wakes up early and likes it
"I'm an early riser, so I'm at the gym by six."
To wind down -- to relax slowly at the end of the day
"We wind down with a series and some delivery food."
To be into -- to like something a lot and give it your time
"I'm really into tennis, even though I'm still learning."
To take up -- to start a new hobby or activity
"I want to take up painting properly this year."
Laid-back -- calm and relaxed, never in a hurry
"People in Texas felt more laid-back than in New York."
Picky -- hard to please, you only like a few things
"I'm not picky at all, so Shanghai really surprised me."
To come up with -- to think of a new idea
"I come up with ideas for brands for a living."
To pitch -- to present an idea to a client and try to sell it
"We pitch the festival campaign to the client on Friday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

My name is Ana Luiza and I live in Sao Paulo, in Brooklin, with my boyfriend and our dog. I am an early riser, so my day starts at six at the gym. After that I come home, because I work from home every day. In the evening my boyfriend gets back from work and we wind down in front of the TV.

These days a few things are different. This month I am working on a music festival, so the whole team is preparing a big pitch. At the moment I am also studying English again, because I want a remote job one day. My mother and my brother are living in Shanghai until next year, and right now they are showing the city to a friend of theirs. I miss them, but I am not a person who calls every single day.

1. Why does the text say "I live in Sao Paulo" but "my mother is living in Shanghai"?
A Because Sao Paulo is her permanent home, and Shanghai is temporary, until next year.
B Because "live" and "is living" mean exactly the same thing.
C Because you use the continuous with other people and the simple with yourself.
2. The text says "I want a remote job" and not "I am wanting". Why?
A Because the wish is not important enough for the continuous.
B Because want, like, know and love stay in the simple form, even when you mean now.
C Because the sentence already has another verb in it.
3. Which words in the text tell you that "am working" is the right choice?
A "every day" and "after that"
B "these days", "this month" and "at the moment"
C "in the evening" and "one day"

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Always True or True Right Now

GRAMMAR

One question, two answers. That is the whole decision in this lesson.

FormUseExample
Present simple
I work / she works		Always true: facts about you, routines, things that do not change this week.I live in Sao Paulo.
She works from home.
Present continuous
I am working		True right now, or temporary around now: today, this week, this month, these days.This month I am working on a festival.
My mother is living in Shanghai.
NegativeThe simple uses don't / doesn't. The continuous uses am not / isn't / aren't.I don't commute.
I am not working today.
QuestionDo / Does for the simple, Am / Is / Are for the continuous.Do you work from home?
Are you working on something new?
Signal wordsalways, usually, every day, never point to the simple. Right now, today, this week, at the moment, these days point to the continuous. The sentence usually tells you before you have to decide.
The short listThese verbs stay simple even when you mean now: know, want, like, love, need, mean, understand. Say "I want a coffee", never "I am wanting a coffee".

When you are not sure, choose the simple and keep talking. A finished sentence with the wrong tense is worth far more than a perfect tense you never say out loud.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are meeting someone new. Choose the best thing to say.

Someone at dinner asks: "So, what do you do?" You answer:
A "I work in advertising. I come up with ideas for brands."
B "I am working in advertising since six years."
C "I am publicity. I make the ideas of brands."
He asks what you are doing at work at the moment. The best answer is:
A "This month I work in a music festival."
B "This month I'm working on a music festival."
C "This month I am work on a music festival."
You want to say that you love tennis and give it your time. You say:
A "I am liking tennis very much."
B "I'm really into tennis."
C "I have tennis as a favorite."
You start a sentence, lose it halfway, and your mind goes blank. The best thing to do is:
A "Sorry, let me start that sentence again." (and you say it again, slowly)
B Stop talking and wait for the other person to change the subject.
C Say "sorry, my English is very bad" and give up on the idea.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer, and nobody is correcting this one.

You are at a dinner and someone asks: "So, tell me about yourself." Speak for about ninety seconds: who you are, where you live, what a normal day looks like, what you are into, and what you are working on this month. If a sentence breaks in the middle, say "let me start that again" out loud and keep going. Finishing the idea matters more than the tense.
