Recommend, Decide, Commit

The two verb forms that say how committed you are. Key words: to prioritize, to mitigate, trade-off, constraint, to leverage, to postpone, to commit to, quick win. Structure: gerund and infinitive.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. These are the words a leadership team argues about.

To prioritize -- to decide what comes first when you cannot do everything
"We have to prioritize the two sites that lose the most people."
To mitigate -- to make a risk smaller, without removing it
"A pilot does not remove the risk. It mitigates it."
Trade-off -- what you give up in order to get something else
"The trade-off is speed. We give it up in order to get evidence."
Constraint -- the limit you have to work inside
"The real constraint is not money. It is the time of the managers."
To leverage -- to use something you already have to get more out of it
"We can leverage the training that the other region already built."
To postpone -- to move something to a later date
"I recommend postponing the global rollout until the second quarter."
To commit to -- to promise formally that you will do something
"Nobody has committed to a global program yet."
Quick win -- a small result you can deliver fast, to buy time for the big one
"They will ask for a quick win. Choose one you will not regret."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

She opened by separating three things that people confuse. I recommend starting with the two sites that lose the most people, she said, and I suggest postponing the global rollout until the second quarter. Those were recommendations. What the committee had decided to do was different: they had agreed to fund the pilot and to review it in ninety days. And then there was the third thing, the one with her name on it: I have committed to reporting the first numbers on the fifteenth of March. She also said what she would avoid doing — promising the whole program on that date — and she named the trade-off out loud: giving up speed in order to get evidence.
Why is it "I recommend starting" and not "I recommend to start"?
A Because recommend belongs to the group of verbs followed by -ing
B Because the action is happening now
C Because it is a recommendation and not a decision
"I have committed to reporting". Why -ing after to here, but "decided to fund" without it?
A They are both mistakes
B In commit to the to is a preposition; in decide to it is part of the infinitive
C Because one is in the present perfect
What is the difference between a recommendation and a commitment in this text?
A None — they are two words for the same thing
B The recommendation binds nobody; the commitment has her name and a date on it
C The recommendation is written and the commitment is spoken

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Gerund and Infinitive

GRAMMAR

There is no rule to deduce here — there are groups to learn. What decides the form is the verb before it.

verb + -ingrecommend, suggest, consider, avoid, involve, keep, finish, practiceI recommend starting with the worst sites.
verb + todecide, plan, agree, manage, expect, need, want, offer, prepare, refuseWe decided to fund the pilot.
after a prepositionalways -ingcommitted to delivering · interested in reviewing · before presenting
purposein order to + verbWe give up speed in order to get evidence.
meaning changesstop, remember, forget, tryShe stopped to talk (paused in order to talk) ≠ she stopped talking (went quiet).

The trap that gives you away: "I recommend to start" and "I suggest to postpone". Both are wrong, and both are the first thing a native speaker notices. It is recommend starting and suggest postponing.
