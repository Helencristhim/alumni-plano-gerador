Chapter 4: The Code

Must, Have To, Should, May

Small words, very different consequences

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"I must see the compliance record before I sign off on a new limit."

"Above that figure, the request has to go to headquarters."

"You do not have to decide today."

"You must not release the product before the file is approved."

The two red forms are both obligations, but they do not come from the same place. And the two green ones look like the same idea said twice. They are not. One of them is the most expensive mistake on this slide.

JobFormExample
Obligation from me, the speakermust + base verbI must see the compliance record first.
Obligation from the rules or the situationhave to / has to + base verbThe request has to go to headquarters.
NOT an obligation at alldo not have to = it is optionalYou do not have to decide today. (You can if you want.)
The opposite of thatmust not = it is forbiddenYou must not release the product. (It is not allowed.)
Advice, not an ordershould / ought to + base verbYou should talk to the dealer before the harvest.
Permission, going up the formal scalecan < could < mayCan I ask · Could I ask · May I ask what changed?
Refusing without refusingI am not allowed to ... · we are not able to ...I am not allowed to approve that at this level.

In one line: do not have to is a door that is open. Must not is a door that is locked.

Note: this matrix is a procedure, not a regulation. It was written here and it can be changed here, which is exactly why it has to be followed while it stands. An analyst who is unsure where a decision sits does not have to guess. Ask, in writing, and the answer becomes part of the file.
1. Two lines on this matrix use the word must and one uses may. What is the difference between them?
Must marks what is obligatory or forbidden: applying the new-customer restriction, and legal signing off. May marks permission: what the analyst is allowed to approve on their own.
2. According to the note, what is the difference between this matrix and a regulation?
The matrix is a procedure. It was written inside the company and can be changed there, which is why it has to be followed while it stands. A regulation comes from outside.
3. What does the note say an analyst who is unsure should do, and why does it matter?
They do not have to guess: they ask in writing, and the answer becomes part of the file. It matters because it moves the decision out of memory and onto the record.
Listening 1 · The Compliance Officer

So That Nobody Has to Be Brave Alone

Eleven years in compliance, on what a rule is actually for. Sound first — no text.

1. What do people assume compliance is for, and what does she say is closer to the truth?
People assume it exists to catch people doing the wrong thing. She says the opposite is closer: a rule exists so that the person at the front does not have to be brave on their own.
2. What happens, according to her, when there is no written limit?
Every no becomes personal. The customer thinks you decided, and next season he remembers it.
3. What is the one thing she would say to anybody new, and what goes wrong if they ignore it?
You must know exactly what you can authorize yourself. The moment you are not sure you start apologizing, and once you start apologizing the customer knows the door is open.
Listening 2 · The Credit Manager

One Register for Everybody

A credit manager on the mistake he made for years. Sound first — no text.

1. How does he refuse a family farm he has known for twenty years, and why not in writing?
He says it plainly, explains what he can do instead, and does it on the telephone, because a letter to that man would feel like a threat.
2. With a large distributor he does the reverse. What does he do, and why is it not coldness?
Everything goes in writing, names the clause and uses formal words. It is not coldness because the person reading it has to forward it to somebody else, and a warm, vague message is useless to them.
3. What was the mistake he made for years, and what were the two results?
Having one register for everybody. He was too formal with small customers, who found him distant, and too casual with the big ones, who found him unserious.
