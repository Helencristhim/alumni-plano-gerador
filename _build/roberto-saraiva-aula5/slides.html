Chapter 3: The Code

The Shape of a Question

Get it wrong and people answer the question you did not ask

Discovery

Which Word Comes Second?

"Where are you based?"

"Do you sell to Europe as well?"

"Did you exhibit here last year?"

"Who handles distribution in Canada?"

In the first three, a red helper jumps in front of the subject. In the last one there is no helper at all — because the blue question word is the subject.

UseFormExample
With be(Wh-) + am / is / are + subjectWhere are you based?
With any other verb, present(Wh-) + do / does + subject + naked verbDo you sell to Europe?
In the past(Wh-) + did + subject + naked verbDid you exhibit here last year?
When the question word is the subjectWho / What + verb, with no helperWho handles distribution?
Short answerrepeat the helper, never the whole verbDo you sell to Europe? Yes, we do. / No, we do not.
The trapa rising voice is not a questionNot You sell to Europe? Not Where you are based?

In one line: the helper comes before the subject — unless the question word already is the subject.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Ninety Seconds at the Booth

The conversation, the announcement, and the veteran

Dialogue

Somebody Stops at Your Booth

G
Hi. I have about two minutes before my next meeting. What do you make?
R
Packaged foods, private label mostly. Before I take your two minutes — where are you based, and what do you buy?
G
Toronto. We are a distributor, so we buy and we sell on to about four hundred stores in Ontario.
R
And who do you buy this category from today?
G
Two suppliers, both in Europe. Prices went up twice last year, so I am here to look. Did you exhibit here last year?
R
No, we did not. This is our first year. Do you need a sample to take with you, or is the catalog more useful today?
G
Both, if you have them. And a price on twenty pallets, because that is what a first order looks like for us.
R
You will have it on Monday. One last question: who handles distribution decisions on your side, you or somebody above you?
G
I do, up to a point. Above a certain volume it goes to my director. Here is my badge — scan it, and follow up on Monday, not in three weeks.
Comprehension

About Grace

1. What kind of company does she work for, and how many stores does it reach?
A distributor in Toronto. It buys and sells on to about four hundred stores in Ontario.
2. Who does she buy from today, and why is she at the fair?
Two suppliers, both in Europe. Their prices went up twice last year, so she is looking for another option.
3. How much can she decide alone, and what does she ask him to do?
She decides up to a certain volume; above that it goes to her director. She asks him to scan her badge and follow up on Monday, not in three weeks.
Listening 1 · Over the Speakers

The Announcement on Day One

Nine hundred exhibitors, and one voice telling everybody where to go. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What are the two changes to the program today?
The buyer meeting room moved from hall two to hall four, and the panel on export rules starts at eleven thirty instead of eleven.
2. What does he say about the badge, and why?
Every visitor badge can be scanned at any booth, and exhibitors should scan it instead of taking business cards, because the contact details arrive already typed.
3. What is the advice for the last hour of the day?
Do not close early. The serious buyers walk the floor after four, when the crowd is gone and they can actually talk.
Listening 2 · Twenty Fairs

What Everybody Gets Wrong

Somebody who has run a booth at twenty of these. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she say most people do at a booth, and why is it wrong?
They present. They talk about their own company for two minutes, and at the end they know nothing about the person who was standing there.
2. What are her first two questions, and what is she really trying to find out?
Where are you based, and who do you buy from today. She is finding out whether this person can actually buy, and from whom she would be taking the business.
3. What does she say about the number of leads?
Sixty leads that nobody follows up on are worth less than four that somebody calls on Monday. The fair is not the work; the Monday after it is.

Follow up: Monday. Her words, not mine — not in three weeks.
1. Two lines on this card decide whether she is worth a Monday call. Which two?
Buys today from two European suppliers whose prices rose, and decision power: she decides alone up to a volume. She has a reason to change and the power to start it.
2. Which question produced the line about decision power, and why ask it at all?
Who handles distribution decisions on your side? Without it you can spend three months talking to somebody who cannot say yes.
3. What is missing from this card that you would want before Monday?
The volume limit she can approve alone, and the name of her director. Both are one question away, and neither was asked.
