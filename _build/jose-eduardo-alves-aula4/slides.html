Chapter 4: The Code

Two Words, Never One

What you always do, and what you are doing this month

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Share?

"She is living in Amsterdam this year."

"Two colleagues are handling her cases."

"She attends seminars on Monday and Wednesday."

"This week she is waiting for her supervisor."

Every red verb is two words, and every one of them is true only for now. The blue one is a single word — and it is true every week.

JobFormExample
Happening at this momentam / is / are + verb-ingShe is reading the proposal right now.
True only for this periodsame form, with this week / this year / at the momentShe is living in Amsterdam this year.
True every weekpresent simple — one wordShe attends seminars on Monday.
Negativeam / is / are + not + verb-ingThe firm is not waiting for her.
QuestionAm / Is / Are + subject + verb-ing — no do, no doesWhat are you working on?
Spelling of -ingfinal -e goes; one vowel + one consonant doubles itwrite → writing · submit → submitting · travel → traveling
Verbs that refuse itknow, want, need, mean, believe — stay simpleI know the answer. Not I am knowing.
The trapnever with usually, always, every weekNot I am usually reviewing. Say I usually review.

In one line: one word for what is always true, two words for what is true only now.

Listening 1 · A Voice Message

Four Things Are Changing This Week

A course coordinator on a Sunday evening. Sound first — no text.

1. What is happening to the Tuesday seminar, and why?
It is moving to Thursday at the same time, because Professor Lindqvist is traveling.
2. What does she say to the students who are still writing their proposal?
Not to worry about the length — to send her the two pages they have, and they will talk.
3. Why is she putting an answer in writing tomorrow?
Because two students are asking her the same question about the reading list, and she wants them to read the email before writing again.
Listening 2 · Market Podcast

Two Hundred Ships Are Waiting

What the queue outside the port is saying today. Sound first — no text.

1. What is happening outside Brazilian ports while the speaker is talking?
About two hundred ships are waiting, and most of them are carrying corn or soybeans.
2. He says a trader is not only buying a product. What else is the trader buying?
A date. If the ship sits outside Santos for eleven days instead of three, somebody is paying for those eight days.
3. Where does he start when somebody asks him about the market, and why?
With the queue, not the price — because the queue is the market talking, and at the moment it is saying that everybody is in a hurry.

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I can answer "what are you working on?" with what is on my desk now, not with my routine.
I hear the difference between "I review contracts" and "I am reviewing a contract".
I never use the -ing form with usually, always or every week.
I ask questions with Are you...? and What are you...?, never with do.
I know the words: currently, a proposal, progress, a semester, to submit, to apply, to research, a supervisor, an update, a workload.
