Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

The Person at the Table -- Saying What You Look After

A board meeting starts and somebody you have never met asks which part of the business you look after. Key words: pricing, trade marketing, market intelligence, a brand, a distributor, a workshop, an agenda item, to report to somebody, to look after, a team. Structure: present simple with I, you and we — positive, negative and question.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Pricing -- the work of deciding what a company charges for its products
"Pricing is the item nobody enjoys, and it is on every agenda."
Trade marketing -- the work of helping the shops and distributors sell the product to the final customer
"Trade marketing puts our material inside the shop, next to the product."
Market intelligence -- the work of collecting information about the market and about the competition
"Market intelligence tells me what the competition charges before I set my price."
A brand -- the name and the image a company gives to its products, so people recognise them
"In this market the brand lives in the workshop, not on television."
A distributor -- a company that buys from the factory and sells to the shops in its region
"We do not sell to the driver. We sell to a distributor."
A workshop -- a place where cars, trucks and machines are repaired
"A workshop opens a bottle of our oil twenty times a day."
An agenda item -- one subject on the list of things a meeting is going to discuss
"I have one agenda item today, and it is the price list for next year."
To report to somebody -- to have that person as your boss, and to answer to them for your work
"I report to the general manager here in Brazil."
To look after -- to be responsible for a part of the business, day after day
"I look after five areas, and pricing is the one that never sleeps."
A team -- the people who work for you and answer to you
"My team is small. There are eleven of us."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A board meeting starts and a new person asks you what you do. Here is the whole answer, in three shapes. The first one is the easy one: "I look after five areas of the business. I look after pricing, trade marketing, market intelligence, brand and communication, and business strategy." Look at the verb. It is bare. In this tense, with I, you and we, the verb never changes shape at all.

The second shape is the one people get wrong. To say no, English does not touch the verb. It puts do not in front of it: "We do not sell to the driver. We sell to a distributor, and the distributor takes the product to the shops and to the workshops." Never we not sell, and never I am look after. In Portuguese you say that you are responsible; in English the verb does the work by itself.

The third shape is the question, and it is the one that keeps a conversation alive. English puts do in front again: "Do you look after pricing as well?" A question word simply goes before it: "Who do you report to?" And the short answer is short: "Yes, I do." One last use of this tense: it says how often something happens. "I report to the general manager, and I present one agenda item every quarter, with my whole team in the room."

1. "I look after five areas." Why is there nothing in front of the verb?
A Because the sentence is short.
B Because a positive sentence with I, you or we needs only the bare verb.
C Because look after is a phrasal verb and phrasal verbs never take anything in front.
2. Why is it "we do not sell to the driver" and not "we not sell to the driver"?
A Because a negative sentence in this tense needs do not in front of the bare verb.
B Because sell is an irregular verb.
C Because the driver is one person and not a company.
3. The text warns against "I am look after pricing". What is the problem?
A Pricing needs the word the in front of it.
B The verb does the work alone. There is no am, is or are in front of it.
C The sentence has to start with a question word.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple with I, You and We

GRAMMAR

The verb never changes shape. Do carries the no and the question for it.

A "I am responsible of the marketing since some years."

C "My work is the marketing. I do many things of the company."
You want to say who your boss is. You say:
A "I am report to the general manager."
B "I report to the general manager here in Brazil."
C "My chief is the general manager of here."
You want to explain that your company sells to companies, not to people. You say:
A "We do not sell to the driver. We sell to a distributor."

The other person has finished answering. You want the conversation to continue. You say:
