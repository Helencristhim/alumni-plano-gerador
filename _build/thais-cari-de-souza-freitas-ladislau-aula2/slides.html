Chapter 4: The Code

The Letter Everybody Drops

One verb, one person, one -s

Grammar Discovery

What Do Two of These Have That the Others Do Not?

"I review the numbers before the call."

"The model approves most of them before eight."

"My analyst does not write the policy."

"Does she review every case above ten thousand?"

Only one of the red verbs carries an extra letter. Look at who is doing it. Then look at the blue words: in those two sentences the extra letter has moved somewhere else.

JobFormExample
He, she or it — positiveverb + -sThe model approves the request.
I, you or we — positivebare verb, no -sWe approve the request.
Negativedoes not + bare verbShe does not write the policy.
QuestionDoes + subject + bare verbDoes he review the case?
Short answerYes, she does. / No, he does not."Does the model decline anybody?" — "Yes, it does."
Spelling-es after ch, sh, s, x, o · -ies after consonant + ywatch → watches · study → studies
The trapthe -s goes on the verb or on does — never on bothNot Does he handles. Say Does he handle.

In one line: he, she and it take the -s — and when does turns up, does takes it instead.

One Question at a Time

Listening 1 · A Manager Describes Her Team

Who Does What

A credit manager describing three people on her team. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the first person on her team do, and at what time?
He opens the dashboard at seven and reads the overnight numbers before anybody else arrives.
2. Which cases go to a human, and which ones do not?
Everything above ten thousand goes to a human. The model handles the rest on its own.
3. What does she say she does not allow, and why?
She does not allow a limit change without a written reason, because nobody remembers the reason three months later.
Listening 2 · A Podcast

What a Model Cannot Do

A data scientist on where the model stops. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say a model is very good at?
It repeats the same decision a million times without getting tired and without changing its mind.
2. What does he say a model never does?
It never asks whether the rule behind it is still the right rule.
3. Who does he say has to answer when the rule is wrong?
The person who wrote the policy, not the model and not the engineer who built it.
