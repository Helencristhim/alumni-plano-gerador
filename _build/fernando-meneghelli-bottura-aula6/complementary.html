Lesson 6 -- Drawing the Org Chart Out Loud

Grammar Video
"Using defining and non-defining relative clauses" -- BBC Learning English

The comma rule from this lesson, demonstrated rather than explained: the entity THAT was losing money against the group, WHICH owned nine agencies. Connection to Lesson 6: this is the structure that lets a whole org chart fit into four spoken sentences.

Tip: listen for the PAUSE, not the comma. Say each non-defining example out loud with a small pause on both sides, then rebuild it with your own group. If you do not pause, the listener hears a different sentence.

Talk with transcript
"As work gets more complex, 6 rules to simplify" -- Yves Morieux, TED (12 min)

Twelve minutes on why matrix organizations produce the exact problem you described: everybody has two bosses and nobody owns the number. He is a consultant talking about structure without one word of complaint, which is also the tone this lesson is training. Full transcript beside the video.

Tip: watch once and write the two rules you would have used in your last group. Then say them out loud as one sentence each, using a relative clause. Ten minutes total, and it doubles as grammar practice.

Executive Interview
"Arvind Krishna, CEO of IBM" -- In Good Company, Norges Bank Investment Management (40 min)

A chief executive describing how an enormous group is actually organized, to an interviewer who keeps asking who decides what. Listen for how often he uses relative clauses to carry a second fact without stopping, and how he describes a reorganization without ever criticizing the structure it replaced.

Tip: pick any two minutes and write down every relative clause you hear. There will be more than you expect. That density is what makes a senior speaker sound economical rather than fast.
