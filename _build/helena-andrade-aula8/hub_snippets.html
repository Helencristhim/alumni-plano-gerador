Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read it inside a real sentence. These are the words the exam text will use.

Evacuation -- the act of moving everybody out of a ship or a space in an organised way
"The evacuation was orderly, and nobody was hurt."
Life jacket -- a personal flotation device for each person on board
"Four life jackets were in a locker that was painted shut."
Life raft -- an inflatable survival boat that opens in the water
"The officers check the life rafts by name."
Fire suppression -- the fixed system that puts out a fire in a closed space
"The fixed fire suppression system put out the fire."
Watertight compartment -- a section of the hull that can be closed so water cannot enter
"The watertight compartment was closed in four minutes."
Emergency signal -- the sound or light that tells everybody on board to react
"The company will replace every emergency signal card."
Abandon ship -- the order to leave the ship at once, given only by the master
"The order to abandon ship was never given."
Casualty -- a person killed or injured, or the accident itself in formal use
"There were no casualties in the fire."
Salvage -- the recovery of a damaged ship or her cargo
"No salvage was needed after the fire."
Distress call -- the emergency radio message a ship sends when she needs help
"Nobody sent a distress call."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Two Futures of an Order (Reading)

GRAMMAR

will have + past participle = finished before a moment in the future. will be + -ing = in progress at that moment. Find the deadline first (by, by the time, by the end of). You only need to read them.

FormWhat it doesExample
will have + past participlefinished BEFORE a moment in the futureBy the time she arrives, the crew will have completed the round.
will be + -ingin progress AT a moment in the futureThe deck team will be standing by at the bow.
by / by the time / by the end ofthe deadline. Look for it firstBy the end of the quarter, the company will have replaced...
The reading trapwill have completed = finished before. will complete on arrival = at the moment of arrival. A wrong option in the exam often changes one into the other.

You do not need to write these tenses. Find the deadline first (by...), then ask: finished before it, or in progress at it?
