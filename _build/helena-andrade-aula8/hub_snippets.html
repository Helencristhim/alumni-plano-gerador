Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read it inside a real sentence. These are the words the exam text will use.

Evacuation -- the organised removal of everybody from a ship or a space
"The evacuation was orderly and nobody was hurt."
Life jacket -- a personal flotation device kept for each person on board
"Four life jackets were in a locker that had been painted shut."
Life raft -- an inflatable survival craft that launches from a cradle
"The life raft release arrangements are verified by name."
Fire suppression -- the fixed installation that puts out a fire in a closed space
"The fire was contained by the fixed fire suppression system."
Watertight compartment -- a section of the hull that can be sealed against flooding
"The watertight compartment was sealed within four minutes."
Emergency signal -- the sound or light that tells everybody on board to react
"Every emergency signal card in the fleet will be replaced."
Abandon ship -- the order to leave the vessel immediately, given only by the master
"The order to abandon ship was never given."
Casualty -- a person killed or injured, and also the incident itself in formal use
"There were no casualties and there was no salvage."
Salvage -- the recovery of a damaged ship or her cargo, usually under contract
"No salvage was required after the fire."
Distress call -- the emergency radio transmission made when a ship needs immediate help
"No distress call was ever transmitted."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Two Futures of an Operational Order

GRAMMAR

An order describes the state a ship has to be IN when a moment arrives, not the task she starts then. That is why it is written in a tense most people never use. Find the deadline first; then decide which side of it the verb sits on.

FormWhat it doesExample
will have + past participlefuture perfect: complete BEFORE a stated point in the futureBy the time she arrives, the crew will have completed the round.
will be + -ingfuture continuous: in progress AT that point, not finishedThe deck team will be standing by fore and aft.
will have been + past participlefuture perfect passive: already done by then, by somebody the text does not namethe arrangements will have been verified
by / by the time / by the end ofthe deadline the future perfect needs. Without it the tense has nothing to be earlier thanby the end of the quarter the company will have replaced...
used to / be used to / get used toa past habit now over / being accustomed to something / becoming accustomed to itShe used to serve on tankers. She is used to working night watches.
The reading trapfuture perfect says FINISHED BEFORE; simple future says AT or AFTER. An option that turns will have completed the round into will complete the round on arrival moves the whole operation to the wrong side of the deadline, and every other word stays the same.

An operational order is written in the future perfect because it describes a state the ship has to be IN when the moment comes, not a task she starts then. Read the deadline first, then decide which side of it the verb sits on.
