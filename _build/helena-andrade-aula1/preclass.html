Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read it inside a real sentence. These are the words the exam text will use.

Vessel -- any ship or large boat that carries people or goods at sea
"The vessel had left her berth six hours earlier."
Navigation -- the work of planning and controlling the route a ship takes
"Navigation in restricted waters demands a continuous lookout."
Cargo -- the goods a ship carries from one port to another
"The cargo was declared unaffected by the grounding."
Port -- a coastal place where ships load, unload and take shelter
"The ship was cleared to leave the port at first light."
Crew -- all the people who work on board a ship
"No injuries were reported among the twenty-two crew."
Bridge -- the high room from which the officers steer and command the ship
"The bridge team had been informed of the change by radio."
Hull -- the main body of a ship, the part that sits in the water
"The hull was breached in two forward compartments."
Draft -- the distance from the waterline down to the bottom of the hull
"Her draft had been recorded as 11.4 metres on departure."
Knot -- one nautical mile per hour, the unit of speed used at sea
"She was proceeding under pilotage at eight knots."
Nautical mile -- a distance of 1,852 metres, used at sea and in the air
"The anchorage lies four nautical miles from the harbour entrance."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

At 0412 the vessel was reported aground in the outer channel. The hull was breached in two forward compartments, both of which were isolated within the hour. No injuries were sustained.

The investigation found that the passage plan had not been amended after the tidal window was revised. The bridge team had been informed by radio, but the change was never entered in the log. Recommendations are issued to the operator, and the procedure by which navigational information is passed between the pilot and the officer of the watch will be reviewed.

1. Why does the report say the hull was breached instead of the rocks breached the hull?
A Because the passive sounds more polite in English.
B Because the report is about the ship and its damage, not about what caused it.
C Because the verb breach has no active form in the past.
2. The passage plan had not been amended after the tidal window was revised. Which of the two happened first?
A The tidal window was revised, and after that the plan was still not amended.
B The plan was amended, and after that the tidal window was revised.
C The two happened at exactly the same moment.
3. The bridge team had been informed by radio. What does that sentence tell you about who informed them?
A It tells you the pilot informed them.
B It tells you nobody informed them.
C It tells you nothing. The passive leaves the agent out, so an option that names one is adding information.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive of an Incident Report

GRAMMAR

Every marine casualty report is written in the passive. Reading one is a matter of finding the verb before you look for a person, because the person is usually not there at all.

FormWhat it doesExample
be + past participlethe standard passive: what happened matters more than who did itThe hull was breached.
had been + past participlepast perfect passive: it had already happened before the moment describedThe plan had not been amended.
is / are + past participlepresent passive: a standing procedure, true every dayInformation is passed between the pilot and the officer.
by + agentonly when the agent is the point. Most report sentences drop itThe clearance was described by the operator as adequate.
The reading trapan option that turns the passive into an active names somebody the text never named. Find the verb before you look for a person.

A report is written in the passive because it is about the ship, not about a person. That is exactly where the trap lives: swap the passive for an active and the option quietly appoints a guilty party the text never appointed.
