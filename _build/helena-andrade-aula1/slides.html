Chapter 4: The Code

The Voice of the Report

Nobody did anything. Everything was done.

Grammar Discovery

Who Did It?

"The hull was breached in two forward compartments."

"The passage plan had not been amended after the tidal window was revised."

"The change was never entered in the log."

"Navigational information is passed between the pilot and the officer of the watch."

Every one of these sentences hides the person who acted. Where would you put the ship, the officer or the pilot in each one -- and why does a report leave them out?

FormWhat it doesExample
be + past participlethe standard passive: what happened matters more than who did itThe hull was breached.
had been + past participlepast perfect passive: it had already happened before the moment describedThe plan had not been amended.
is / are + past participlepresent passive: a standing procedure, true every dayInformation is passed between the pilot and the officer.
by + agentonly when the agent is the point. Most report sentences drop itThe clearance was described by the operator as adequate.
The reading trapan option that turns the passive into an active names somebody the text never named. Find the verb before you look for a person.

A report is written in the passive because it is about the ship, not about a person. That is exactly where the trap lives: swap the passive for an active and the option quietly appoints a guilty party the text never appointed.

Listening 1

The Port Authority Briefing

A duty officer reads the morning summary. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What happened at four twelve, and what was the ship doing at the time?
A bulk carrier of thirty-two thousand tonnes went aground in the outer approach channel while proceeding under pilotage at eight knots.
2. What does the officer say about the crew and about pollution?
All twenty-two crew are accounted for, no injuries have been reported, and no pollution has been observed in the channel or at the anchorage.
3. What are other ships told to do?
The outer channel is closed to deep-draft traffic. Vessels awaiting a berth should remain at the anchorage and keep a listening watch on channel sixteen.
Listening 2

Why the Plan Was Never Amended

An investigator explains the finding. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. When was the tidal window revised, and how did the bridge team learn about it?
It was revised at twenty-two hundred the night before, and the bridge team was told by radio. Two of them repeated the new time back.
2. What is the step the investigator says never happened?
Nobody entered the revision in the log and nobody amended the passage plan, so the plan on the chart table still described a tide that no longer existed.
3. Why does he say the finding is not about one careless officer?
Because it is about a procedure that allowed a change to be spoken and never written.
