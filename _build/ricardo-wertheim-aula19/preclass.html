Lesson 19 -- Pre-class

The People Who Live There

How to describe the people and places of a neighborhood. Key words: neighborhood, shopkeeper, craftsman, street vendor, a regular, newcomer, welcoming, to run. Structure: relative clauses with who, which, that and where -- the man who sells corn, the street where I grew up.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Neighborhood -- a part of a city where people live, with its own streets and character
"Bixiga is an old Italian neighborhood in Sao Paulo."
Shopkeeper -- a person who owns or works in a small shop
"The shopkeeper knows every customer by name."
Craftsman -- a person who makes beautiful things by hand, with skill
"He is a craftsman who repairs old wooden doors."
Street vendor -- a person who sells food or things in the street, not in a shop
"The street vendor who sells corn is there every night."
A regular -- a customer who goes to the same place very often
"He is a regular here. He comes every Saturday."
Newcomer -- a person who has arrived in a place recently
"Newcomers find it easy to make friends here."
Welcoming -- friendly to people who arrive, making them feel at home
"The people who live there are very welcoming."
To run -- to own or manage a business and make it work every day
"The woman who runs the bakery is the third generation."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Last month Ricardo showed Maja around Bixiga, the old Italian neighborhood where families from Italy arrived a century ago. First they visited a bakery which opened in 1897. The woman who runs it today is the third generation of the same family, and she gave Maja a piece of bread for free.

On the corner they met the street vendor who sells the best corn in the city. Ricardo is a regular, so the vendor knew his name. Then they walked to a small square where a craftsman repairs old wooden doors. Maja loved it. "In Ljubljana," she said, "I always buy honey from a shopkeeper who remembers every face. It is the same kind of place."

At the end of the day Maja said that the people who live in Bixiga are very welcoming, even with newcomers. Ricardo agreed. "A city is its buildings," he said, "and the people who use them."

1. Who runs the bakery today?
A A newcomer who arrived in the neighborhood last year and bought the business from the old family.
B The third generation of the same family.
C Ricardo's grandparents.
2. Which sentence from the text describes a PLACE?
A "the street vendor who sells the best corn in the city, which Ricardo buys every week"
B "a bakery which opened in 1897"
C "a small square where a craftsman repairs old wooden doors"
3. Why did the street vendor know Ricardo's name?
A Because Ricardo is a regular.
B Because Maja told him Ricardo's name when they arrived at the corner of the square.
C Because Ricardo is an architect.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Who, Which, That, Where

GRAMMAR

One small word joins two sentences.

WordUse it forExample
whoa personthe man who sells corn
whicha thingthe bakery which opened in 1897
thata person or a thingthe door that he repaired
wherea placethe street where I grew up
No extra wordno he, she or it after whothe man who sells (not who he sells)

Never: the man what sells · the craftsman which repairs · the vendor who he sells · the square which they play cards

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A friend asks about the man on the corner. You say:
A "He is the vendor what sells corn every night on that corner of the square."
B "He is the vendor who sells corn."
C "He is the vendor who he sells corn."
You want to describe a square:
A "It is the square which the old men play cards in the afternoon, under the trees."
B "It is the square where the old men play cards."
C "It is the square who the old men play cards."
You describe an old building:
A "It is a building which was built in 1920."
B "It is a building who was built in 1920 by an Italian architect and his son."
C "It is a building where was built in 1920."
You have talked about your neighborhood for two minutes. What do you say next?
A "And what about you? Where do you go on Sundays?"
B "So that is my neighborhood, and the other neighborhoods are not as interesting, I think, in my opinion."
C "Thank you."

Reflection
