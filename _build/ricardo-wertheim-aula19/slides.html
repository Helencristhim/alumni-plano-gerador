Chapter 3: The Code

The Man Who Sells Corn

who · which · that · where

Grammar Discovery

Two Sentences, One Sentence

"That is the man who sells corn on the corner."

"This is the bakery which opened in 1897."

"She is the craftsman that repaired the old door."

"It is the street where the festival happens every August."

Look at the blue words. Which one is for a person? Which one is for a thing? Which one is for a place? And which one works for both people and things?

WordUse it forExample
whoa personthe man who sells corn
whicha thing or an animalthe bakery which opened in 1897
thata person OR a thing (more informal)the door that she repaired
wherea placethe street where the festival happens
No extra wordnever he, she or it after who / whichthe man who sells corn (not who he sells)
The trapwhat is not a relative wordNever the man what sells. Say the man who sells.

Two short sentences become one: There is a man. He sells corn. → There is a man who sells corn.

Chapter 4: A Walk in Bixiga

Maja Comes to Sao Paulo

A bakery, a street vendor and a Sunday lunch

Dialogue

A Walk in Bixiga

M
So this is Bixiga. Why did you bring me here first?
R
Because it is the neighborhood where the Italian families arrived a century ago. It has a lot of character.
M
I can smell bread. Is that a bakery?
R
Yes. It is the bakery which opened in 1897. The woman who runs it is the third generation.
M
And the man on the corner, with the cart?
R
He is the street vendor who sells the best corn in the city. I am a regular.
M
Do people here like visitors? In Ljubljana, the old neighborhoods can be quiet.
R
They are very welcoming. On Sundays it is the kind of place where everybody eats together. And what about you? Where do you go on Sundays?
M
To the Central Market, to buy honey from the same shopkeeper. She remembers every face.
R
Then you understand Bixiga already.
Comprehension

What Did Maja Say?

1. What does Maja smell when they arrive?

2. What does Maja say about the old neighborhoods in Ljubljana?

3. Where does Maja go on Sundays, and why?

Chapter 5: Practice

A Market and a Street

Two places, and the people who make them

Listening

The People of the Market

A guide at the Central Market in Ljubljana. Sound first — no text.

1. Who is Marta, and how long has she been at the market?
She is the woman who sells honey. She has been there for forty years.
2. What does the man with the white hat do?
He is a cheesemaker who comes down from the mountains every Friday.
3. Where do the regulars go after shopping?
To the small cafe under the arches, where everybody knows their order.
Listening 2

The Street Where I Grew Up

A man on the street where he grew up, fifty years later. Sound first — no text.

1. What was there when he was a boy?
A shoemaker, a barber and a bakery, run by families who lived above their shops.
2. What is in the old bakery today?
A coffee shop which sells very expensive coffee.
3. Who still lives on the street?
The barber, who is ninety years old and still cuts hair on Saturdays.
