Vocabulary

A subject line -- the short title at the top of an email
"Write a clear subject line with your full name."
To attach -- to add a file to an email
"I have attached my CV to this email."
To forward -- to send an email that you received to another person
"Could you forward my message to the program director?"
To cc -- to send a copy of an email to another person
"Please cc my supervisor on your reply."
A reminder -- a message that helps somebody remember something
"This is a friendly reminder about Friday."
Regarding -- a formal word for about
"I am writing regarding my observership application."
To confirm -- to say that something is true or definite
"Could you confirm the date of the interview?"
A deadline -- the last day or time to do something
"The deadline for documents is October first."
Pending -- not decided or not finished yet
"My verification is still pending."
To reach out -- to contact somebody, usually for the first time
"I am reaching out to ask about your program."
At your earliest convenience -- EXPRESSION — a polite way to say as soon as you can
"Please reply at your earliest convenience."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A formal email in English connects its ideas with clear words. To show a contrast, it uses although inside one sentence: Although I sent the documents in May, the status is still pending. Or it starts a new sentence with however: The form is complete. However, one letter is missing.

To show a result, a formal email uses therefore or as a result, at the start of a new sentence and with a comma: The deadline is on Friday. Therefore, I am writing to confirm the date. In a message to a friend, but and so are enough: it is late, so I called.

Brazilian doctors often write although... but in the same sentence, because in Portuguese speech the two contrast words can appear together. In English, although never takes but. And despite takes a noun, with no of: despite the delay.

1. "___ I sent the documents in May, the status is still pending." Which word completes the sentence?
A However, because it is a contrast between two ideas in the email
B Although
C Therefore
2. Why does the email say "The deadline is on Friday. Therefore, I am writing today."?
A Because writing today is a result of the deadline.
B Because the writer is surprised by the deadline.
C Because therefore is informal.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A Although I sent the documents in May, but the status is still pending at the office.
B Despite of the delay, my file is complete.
C The form is complete. However, one letter is missing.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Connectors of Contrast and Result

GRAMMAR

Although and however show a contrast. Therefore and as a result show a consequence. Watch the punctuation.

JobFormExample
Contrast, one sentenceAlthough + clause, clauseAlthough I applied in May, I have no answer.
Contrast, new sentenceHowever, + clauseHowever, one letter is missing.
Contrast + noundespite + noun / -ingDespite the delay, the file is complete.
Result, formalTherefore, / As a result, + clauseTherefore, I am writing today.
Informalbut / soIt is urgent, so I called.
Closing a formal emailThank you for your time. Kind regards,Kind regards, Diego Wached

Never: although... but · despite of · I sent it however it is pending · Hi, I need... · Bye (to close a formal email)
