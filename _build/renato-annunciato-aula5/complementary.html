Lesson 5 -- A Day in the Field

Series
Suits -- Season 1, Episode 1 (Pilot)

The whole first episode is a senior taking a junior into real situations and correcting him afterwards, in the corridor, in one sentence at a time. That is your joint visit, in a law firm instead of a clinic. Watch how the senior almost never rescues him in front of the client, and always says the point once, straight, and then moves on.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Every time the senior gives advice, write the sentence down and mark whether he used "you should", "you have to" or "you must". You will see which one a real professional actually uses most.

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart -- Listen Up, Leaders: A Record-Setting Coach's Guide to Communication

A coach with a record nobody has beaten, talking about the part of coaching that is only language: how you say the hard thing so the person can still use it tomorrow. It is the same problem you have in the car after a visit, with the same solution: one point, said precisely, and then silence.

Tip: listen once without the transcript on the way home. Then pick the one sentence of his you would say to your own consultant, and say it out loud in English before you forget it.

YouTube
Simon Sinek -- Why Good Leaders Make You Feel Safe (TED)

Twelve minutes on why people perform in some teams and hide in others, and it lands exactly on today's lesson: a consultant who feels judged in the room will play safe, and a consultant who feels covered will try the difficult question. That is the difference between a visit that teaches something and a visit that only produces a report.

Tip: watch it once with subtitles and once without. Then answer out loud, in English: after your joint visits, does your consultant feel watched or covered?
