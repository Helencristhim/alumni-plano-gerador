Chapter 3: The Code

Must, Have To, Should

The difference between a rule and a piece of advice

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"You must agree one objective before you go in."

"We have to be at the clinic by nine, because that is the only slot."

"You should send the follow-up on the same day."

"You do not have to know every clinical answer."

Two are obligations. One is advice. One says something is not necessary. What comes straight after each one?

FormWhat it meansExample
must + verbStrong obligation, and it usually comes from you, the speaker.You must agree one objective first.
have to + verbObligation that comes from outside: the company, the rule, the situation.We have to be there by nine.
should + verbAdvice. It is the right thing to do, but nobody is forcing it.You should send it the same day.
must not + verbProhibition. It is forbidden. Do not do it.You must not answer for the consultant.
do not have to + verbNo obligation. It is optional, and that is fine.You do not have to know every answer.
Softer adviceYou could... · It might be worth... · Have you thought about... — use these with a senior consultant.
Three trapsNever must to or should to — nothing goes between the modal and the verb. Never do not must — the negative of must is must not. And must not is NOT the same as do not have to: one forbids, the other frees.

The Structure

Three Parts, Four Minutes

A debrief is not a report of everything you saw. It is one thing kept, one thing changed, and an agreement.

1 · What worked, and exactly why

"The question you asked before the pitch worked, because it made her tell you the real problem."

2 · One thing to change

"One thing for the next visit: you should close before you leave the room. Today you did not ask for anything."

3 · What you will both do

"You send the follow-up today. I will call the gatekeeper before your next visit. Agreed?"

Kept → changed → agreed. One of each. A person carries one change, never eleven.

Chapter 4: The Debrief

Dialogue

After the Visit

S
Renato, before we talk about the visit: what did you agree with him in the car this morning?
R
One objective. Today I was only watching how he handles an objection, so the rest of the sales call was his.
S
Good. Most managers say they are there to see everything, and then they see nothing. And in the room?
R
I said almost nothing. When you shadow someone you must not rescue them, or the doctor starts talking to you instead of to him.
S
That is the hardest part of a joint visit. So, the debrief. Give me one thing that worked.
R
The question he asked before the pitch. It worked because she told him the real problem was the schedule, not the price. He built good rapport in two minutes.
S
And one thing to change? One, please. Not five.
R
He did not close. He explained everything and then he left the room without asking for anything. He should ask for the next step before he stands up.
S
Then say it to him exactly like that, and agree who does what. He sends the follow-up today, and you fix the next call plan with him on Friday.
Comprehension

About Sofia

1. What does she ask about before she talks about the visit itself?
What he agreed with the consultant in the car that morning.
2. What does she say about managers who go to see everything?
They end up seeing nothing.
3. How many things to change does she want?
One, and she says so explicitly — not five.
4. What does she tell him to do at the end?
Say it to the consultant in exactly those words, and agree who does what: the follow-up today, the call plan on Friday.
Listening

Coaching, or Just Inspecting?

Fifteen years watching managers in cars. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What decides everything, according to her?
Deciding what the visit is for, before the car door opens — not knowing the product best.
2. What happens if you have not agreed one objective?
You are inspecting, not coaching — and the consultant feels it in the first two minutes.
3. Why must the manager stay quiet in the room?
The moment you rescue the conversation, the visit stops belonging to the consultant and the client learns to talk to you instead.
4. What should a debrief never be, and what should it be?
Never a list of everything you saw. One thing that worked, with the reason why, and one thing to change — because a person can carry one change, not eleven.
Listening 2

From the Other Seat

A consultant on being shadowed by his manager. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. How does the first hour feel when his manager comes with him?
Strange. He knows the doctor and the clinic, but with a second person in the room everything sounds different in his own mouth.
2. What helps him relax?
When the manager says in the car what he is looking at — then he knows the rest is his.
3. What happened when he got fourteen points of feedback?
He remembers none of them.
4. Why did the four-minute debrief actually change something?
The manager said why the good thing worked, then asked what he would change and waited — so he said it himself, and because he said it, he did it.
Chapter 5: Practice

Your Visit

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "You must to prepare the call plan before we go."
"You must prepare the call plan before we go." (nothing goes between must and the verb)
2. "You do not must arrive late."
"You must not arrive late." (must has no form with do; the negative is must not)
3. "I think you should to open with a question."
"I think you should open with a question." (should is never followed by to)
4. "You must not bring the price list, it is optional."
"You do not have to bring the price list, it is optional." (must not forbids; do not have to means it is simply not necessary)
