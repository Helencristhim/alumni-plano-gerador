Chapter 3: The Code

Must, Have To, Should

Three levels of pressure in one project

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Rules Show?

"Every change must go through the decision log." (company rule, no exception)

"We have to send the minutes within 24 hours." (the contract says so)

"You should copy the piping lead in." (good practice, not compulsory)

"You do not have to join the whole call." (it is optional)

The four verbs in blue all talk about pressure, but the pressure is not the same. Read the note in red and ask: who decided this, and what happens if I do not do it?

JobFormExample
A rule with no exception, often writtenmust + verbEvery change must go through the decision log.
An obligation that comes from outside youhave to / has to + verbWe have to send the minutes within 24 hours.
Something necessary for the work to moveneed to + verbI need to know the equipment load before Friday.
Advice, good practiceshould + verbYou should copy the piping lead in.
Not necessary, optionaldo not have to + verbYou do not have to join the whole call.
The trapmust not is a prohibition, not an absence of obligationYou must not send drawings outside the distribution list = it is forbidden. You do not have to send them = it is optional.

In one line: must = the rule; have to = somebody outside decided; need to = the work requires it; should = advice; do not have to = optional; must not = forbidden.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Flow, at Real Speed

One call, three recordings, one communication matrix

Dialogue

Setting the Ground Rules

H
Before we start the design, I would like to agree how we communicate. Otherwise we lose two weeks in email.
C
Agreed. For anything on the buildings, I am your point of contact. For the equipment, it is my structural lead.
H
Good. We have a progress call every Tuesday at three, Helsinki time. Thirty minutes, no more.
C
That works. And we have to send the minutes within twenty-four hours, with the action items and one owner each.
H
Yes. One more thing. Every change to the interface must go through the decision log. If it is not in the log, it did not happen.
C
Understood. And what is the escalation path if a question stays open?
H
Two working days with no answer, you escalate to me. Four days, we both go to the project director. You should copy me in from the start, though.
C
Perfect. I will send a short note this afternoon with all of this, to the same distribution list.
Comprehension

About Henrik

1. When is the progress call, and how long is it?
Every Tuesday at three, Helsinki time, and it lasts thirty minutes.
2. What does Henrik say about changes to the interface?
Every change must go through the decision log; if it is not in the log, it did not happen.
3. What is the escalation path Henrik describes?
Two working days with no answer, escalate to him; four days, both go to the project director.
Listening 1 · The Nordic Project Manager

Why We Write Everything Down

A project manager in Sweden explains the communication rules of her projects. Sound first — no text.

1. How long does she give the team to send the minutes?
Twenty-four hours.
2. What does she say is the only rule she never negotiates?
Every decision goes in the decision log, with a date and a name.
3. What does she say about long emails?
If an email needs more than five lines, the person should call instead.
Listening 2 · The American Lead

When Nobody Answers

A lead engineer in Houston talks about escalation and why people wait too long. Sound first — no text.

1. How long did the team wait before escalating, in his example?
Nine days.
2. Why does he say people do not escalate?
Because they think escalating means complaining about a colleague.
3. What is his rule now?
Two days with no answer, you escalate, and you copy the person you are escalating about.
Listening 3 · The Internal Briefing

The Four Lines of a Good Action Item

A short internal briefing on writing action items that actually get done. Sound first — no text.

1. What four things does a good action item need?
A verb, an owner, a date and a place where the result will appear.
2. What does the briefing say about the word team as an owner?
If the owner is the team, nobody is the owner.
3. What should you do with an action item that nobody has started after two calls?
Either give it a real owner and date, or delete it and say why.

Read the sheet as three different kinds of sentence. Some lines are rules that nobody can break, some are obligations that come from the contract, and some are only good practice. The modal verb you choose tells the other office which one it is.

The decision log: every interface change must go through the decision log.
