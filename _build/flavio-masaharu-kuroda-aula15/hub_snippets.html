Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An icebreaker -- a short friendly question or comment that starts a conversation
"A good icebreaker is a question about their week, not about the weather."
Rapport -- a relationship of trust and easy communication
"Two minutes of rapport make the next eight minutes easier."
Common ground -- things that two sides share or agree on
"We both run SAP, so we already have common ground."
To steer -- to move a conversation in the direction you want
"When the call gets lost, steer it back to the agenda."
A tangent -- a topic that goes away from the main subject
"The talk about football was a nice tangent, but we had ten minutes."
To park -- to leave a topic for later and come back to it
"Can we park the price for now and come back to it at the end?"
To sum up -- to give the main points again, briefly
"Let me sum up what I heard before we talk about next steps."
A next step -- the action that comes after a meeting, with a date and an owner
"Never end a call without a next step and a date."
To commit to -- to promise clearly that you will do something
"I can commit to a demo with your team on Thursday."
Bandwidth -- the time and energy a person or a team has for extra work
"My team has no bandwidth for a new project before October."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A good sales call has five parts, and each part has its own grammar. You open with rapport and a polite request: would you mind if we kept this to thirty minutes? Polite questions keep the normal order of a sentence after the question: could you tell me when the board meets, not when does the board meet.

Then comes discovery. Here the most useful questions are subject questions, with no do: who approves a new supplier? What happens when an invoice is wrong? You ask many of them before you say anything about the product, and you look for common ground.

When the prospect raises the price, you do not argue. You soften with could, would and might: we could look at a shorter term, which might help. If the price comes too early, you park it. If you do not understand a word, you ask: what does GL stand for? When you give proof, the story is in the past simple and the result in the present perfect: Verdana chose us in January, and the close has dropped to four days.

Finally, you sum up and agree a next step, with a fixed plan in the present continuous: I am sending you the proposal tomorrow. A call can have a few grammar mistakes. It cannot end without a date and a name.

1. Which polite question is correct?
A "Could you tell me when does the board meet?"
B "Could you tell me when the board meets?"
C "Could you tell me when meets the board?"
2. Why is "Who approves a new supplier?" a good discovery question with no do?
A Because it is in the past.
B Because the word do is never used in any English question, in any tense.
C Because who is the subject of the question.
3. According to the text, what can a call NOT end without?
A A next step with a date and a name.
B Perfect grammar.
C A discount for the customer, or at least an offer of a lower price.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The prospect asks for the price in minute two. The best answer is:
A "It is two hundred thousand. Any questions?"
B "I cannot tell you the price."
C "Good question. Can we park it for ten minutes? The price depends on what you need."
The call goes into a long tangent about football. You say:
A "Football is interesting, but please stop talking about it now, it is not important."
B "Let me steer us back to the agenda, because I want to respect your time."
C "Football is not on the agenda, sorry."
The prospect says: "Let us talk again sometime." The best answer is:
A "Happy to. Can we put a date in now? How about Thursday at ten?"
B "Sure, we will be in touch."
C "OK, call me when you can."
You want to ask who makes the final decision, politely. You say:
A "Who does make the final decision?"
B "Would you mind telling me who makes the final decision?"
C "Could you tell me who does make the decision?"
