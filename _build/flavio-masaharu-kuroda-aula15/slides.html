Chapter 4: The Whole Call

Five Parts in Eight Lines

One call, two recordings, one call plan

Dialogue

Ingrid, One More Time

F
Good to see you again, Ingrid. How was the year-end close? Last time you said it was a nightmare.
I
Still a nightmare, but finished. I have thirty minutes and a hard stop.
F
Then let me steer us straight to it. What has changed on your side since September?
I
The board has approved the budget, but my team has no bandwidth before February.
F
That is useful. So the question is not if, but when. Is the price still the main concern?
I
Honestly, yes. Your competitor is fifteen per cent cheaper.
F
Can we park the price for two minutes? Let me sum up what you need first, and then we compare the two offers line by line.
I
Fine. And if it makes sense, I can commit to a pilot in February.
Comprehension

About Ingrid

1. What has changed at Ingrid's company since September?
The board has approved the budget, but her team has no time for the project before February.
2. What is Ingrid's main concern, and why?
The price. A competitor is fifteen per cent cheaper.
3. What does Ingrid offer at the end, and on what condition?
A pilot in February, if the comparison of the two offers makes sense.
Listening 1 · The Coach

The Shape of the Call

A sales coach on what she listens for in a recorded call. Sound first — no text.

1. What is she listening for, if it is not perfect English?
The shape of the call: its five parts, in the right order.
2. What should come before any word about the product?
Questions, many more than you think.
3. What does a real next step need, in her words?
A date and a name. Not "we will be in touch".
Listening 2 · The Buyer

Why the Brazilian Won

A European buyer on the first call that decided a deal. Sound first — no text.

1. How did the Brazilian head of sales start the first call?
Not with a presentation: he asked how their month-end close worked, asked again, and wrote it down.
2. What did he do when he did not understand something?
He did not pretend. He asked the buyer to say it again and repeated it in his own words to check.
3. What did he do about the price, and at the end of the call?
He asked what the price was compared with and came back with a trade-off, and he gave a date for the next call before the buyer asked.
Real Document

The Plan Before the Call

CALL PLAN · 30 MINUTES
Flávio Kuroda, Invent Software · prospect: finance director, Europe
0-3 min · Rapporticebreaker, agenda, hard stop
3-13 min · Discovery5 questions before the product
13-20 min · Explainonly what she said she needs
20-26 min · Objectionask for it; park it if it is early
26-29 min · Sum upthree things I heard
29-30 min · Next stepa date, a name, an owner
Discovery has the most minutes on purpose: ten minutes of questions and seven of explanation. The last minute is the most important one, and it is the one people lose when a tangent eats the time.
1. Which part of the plan has the most time, and why?
Discovery, ten minutes, because you explain only after you know what the prospect needs.
2. What does the plan say to do if the objection comes too early?
Park it, and come back to it after the discovery and the explanation.
3. What usually destroys the last minute, according to the note?
A tangent that eats the time, so the call ends without a next step.
