How Much? How Many?

Stage 1.1: Amount Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Episode — one part of a series
"I watched four episodes in one night."
Screen time — the hours you spend looking at a screen
"My screen time went up again this week."
Advice — words that tell you what to do — you cannot count them
"She gave me some good advice."
Amount — how much of something there is
"The amount of homework this term is unfair."
Enough — as much as you need, and not more
"I never have enough time on Sundays."
Stuff — things, when you do not want to name them one by one
"My room is full of stuff I never use."
Hardly any — almost none of it
"I have hardly any free time this month."
Plenty of — more than enough
"Do not worry, we have plenty of time."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. Then answer the questions.

This week was a disaster. I watched eleven episodes — too many, I know — and I did hardly any homework. My screen time was twenty-nine hours. Twenty-nine! That is too much for one week. I do not have enough hours for everything, and honestly I have plenty of things I should be doing instead. My sister gave me some advice: choose a few things and drop the rest. The amount of stuff I say yes to is the real problem. Next week: a little less phone, a lot more sleep.
Why is it "too many episodes" but "too much screen time"?
A You can count episodes one by one; you cannot count time that way.
B Because eleven is a bigger number than twenty-nine.
What advice did her sister give?
A To sleep more and watch less.
B To choose a few things and drop the rest.
Why does she say "some advice" and not "some advices"?
A Because "advice" is uncountable — it has no plural.
B Because it was only one sentence.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

One test: can you count it one by one?

One episode, two episodes → countable. Time, homework, advice → uncountable.

Countable

episodes · friends · messages
How many?
a few · too many

Uncountable

time · homework · advice · money
How much?
a little · too much

Safe for both sides, when you are not sure:

a lot of · plenty of · some / any

✗ two advices · ✓ some advice / two pieces of advice
✗ hardly no time · ✓ hardly any time
too much / too many are not neutral — they mean it is a problem.

Say a lot of as one block. The "of" almost disappears.

Stage 4: In the conversation

Quiz
A friend asks how much you watch. Which answer says more?
A "A lot."
B "About three episodes a night — too many, honestly."
Your friend helped you decide something. What do you say?
A "Thanks, that was really good advice."
B "Thanks, those were really good advices."
You have almost no free time. Which sentence is correct?
A "I have hardly no free time."
B "I have hardly any free time."

Stage 5: Your week in numbers

Reflection
Record 30 seconds in English. Give me five real numbers from your week — screen time, episodes, hours of homework, hours of sleep, free hours. For each one, say if it is too much, enough, or hardly any. Use at least three words from Stage 1.1. Finish with a question for your teacher.
