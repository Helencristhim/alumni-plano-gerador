Chapter 3: Five Minutes Before

The Conversation Before the Call

A conversation between two counterparts, line by line

Dialogue

Five Minutes Before the Agenda

M
Camila, hello. We have five minutes before the others join. How are things on your side?
C
Busy, in a good way. We closed the audit this week, so the team is lighter. How about you?
M
Travelling too much, honestly. I was in Milan on Monday and I am in Paris until Friday.
C
That sounds challenging with the time zones. Do you manage to keep your catch-ups with the regions?
M
Most of them. The Asia ones are hard. And your credit team, is the workload still high?
C
It is. One analyst is covering two portfolios, and that is my main concern this quarter.
M
Understood. Speaking of which, what would help most: a replacement or a temporary contract?
C
A replacement, because the volume is not temporary. By the way, are you coming to the offsite in November?
M
I am. Two days, and I would like to visit one of the entities while I am there.
C
Then let us touch base in October, so that I can organise the visit with the local team.
Comprehension

About Mark

1. Where has Mark been this week?
He was in Milan on Monday and he is in Paris until Friday.
2. What does he ask about the credit team, and what does he want to know after her answer?
Whether the workload is still high, and then whether a replacement or a temporary contract would help most.
3. What does he want to do during the offsite in November?
To visit one of the entities while he is there.
Chapter 4: The Code

The Question After the Answer

How a conversation continues instead of stopping

Grammar Discovery

What Keeps the Conversation Alive?

"Busy, in a good way. How about you?"

"That sounds challenging with the time zones."

"Do you manage to keep your catch-ups with the regions?"

"By the way, are you coming to the offsite in November?"

Four different sentences, one job. What does each of them do to the conversation?

FormWhat it doesExample
return the questionthe shortest way to keep the turn movingBusy, in a good way. How about you?
acknowledgeshow you understood before you ask againThat sounds challenging. / I can imagine.
follow-up questionask about something inside the answer you just heardHe says he travels a lot → Do you manage to keep your catch-ups?
open a new subjectadd something light, related but newBy the way, ... / Speaking of which, ...
Watch outanswering and stopping. "Fine, thank you" closes the door politely; one short question after it keeps the door open.

Sound Check

Say It Clearly

The vowel in work (point 12 of 14) — work, first and turnover share one sound, and it is neither "u" nor "o"

Why this one is hard for a Brazilian speaker

In Portuguese: There is no vowel like this one, so it is replaced by an "u" or an open "o".

In English: The mouth is relaxed and the tongue sits in the middle. It is one single vowel, shared by work, first, turn and her.

If it slips: "Work" drifts towards "walk", and those are two different words in a sentence about your week.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Two Openings, Two Results

The same five minutes, with and without follow-up questions

Listening 1

A Call Opens in Milan

Sofia, the counterpart in Italy, opens the call. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. What has changed in Sofia's team recently?
Her team was reorganised: two business units became one, and she now covers both.
2. What does she say about the time difference?
That five hours make it difficult, so she prefers to start the calls early in her afternoon.
3. What does she suggest doing regularly?
A short catch-up every two weeks, thirty minutes, without an agenda.
Listening 2

A Call That Stops

Another opening, where the conversation does not continue. Sound first, no text.

0:000:00
1. How does the other person answer the first question?
With two words, "fine, thanks", and nothing else.
2. What happens after the second question?
The same: a short answer, no question back, and then silence until the others join.
3. What does the speaker say at the end about these five minutes?
That the five minutes before the agenda are where you learn what is really happening in the other region.

The Five-Minute Card

what to say, and what to ask next

NeverFine, thanks. And then silence.
Sample script for this lesson. The people and the trips are illustrative.
1. What do you do immediately after answering "how are you"?
Give one detail and return the question: how about you?
2. What do you say when the other person describes something difficult?
Acknowledge it first: that sounds challenging, and then ask a follow-up question.
3. According to the card, what should never happen?
Answering "fine, thanks" and letting the silence come.
