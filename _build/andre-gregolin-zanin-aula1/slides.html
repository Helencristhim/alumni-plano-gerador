Chapter 3: Four Time Frames

Four Pieces of Time, One Life

The tense is not the point. The piece of time is.

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Sentences Do?

"I grew up in the interior, and I played for a team in Franca."

"Right now I am studying for one exam, six days a week."

"I have traveled abroad six times, and I have never lived outside Brazil."

"I come from a small city, and I am an early riser."

Four sentences, one person, four different pieces of time. The red one is closed. The blue one is true only this year. The green one counts your whole life up to this second. The orange one is simply true, and always was.

JobFormExample
A closed part of your lifepast simpleI grew up in the interior.
Again and again, and then it stoppedused to + bare verbI used to train five afternoons a week.
True only in this periodam / are + verb-ingRight now I am studying six days a week.

The trapage never uses haveNot I have 18 years. Say I am 18.

In one line: the tense you pick is not grammar. It is which piece of time you are pointing at.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Same Questions, At Full Speed

One conversation, three recordings, one plan on paper

Dialogue

Two Minutes at the Gym

H
Sorry — are you using this one? I am Hannah, by the way. I moved here in March.
A
It is all yours. André. Where did you move from?
H
Chicago. My Portuguese is terrible, so I keep finding people who speak English. Are you from Sao Paulo?
A
No, I grew up in the interior. I only moved here last year, for a prep course.
H
A prep course? What is that, exactly?
A
A whole year of classes, six days a week, for one exam. Medicine. Right now that is my entire life.
H
That sounds brutal. Do you get any time for anything else at all?
A
I work out late, and I watch series in English. That is how I picked up most of my vocabulary. And you — what brought you to Brazil?
Comprehension

About Hannah

1. Where did Hannah move from, and when did she arrive?
From Chicago, and she moved here in March.
2. Why does she say she keeps finding people who speak English?
Because her Portuguese is terrible, so English is the language she can actually use.
3. What is her reaction to the prep course, and what does she want to know after it?
She says it sounds brutal, and she asks whether he gets any time for anything else at all.
Listening 1 · A Voice Note

A Year That Is Not Yours Yet

An American student recording a voice note five months into a year in Brazil. Sound first — no text.

1. How long has she been in Sao Paulo, and what can she still not do?
About five months, and she still cannot order coffee without pointing at something.
2. Where does she say a language is really picked up?
Not in a class: in the gym, at the bus stop, and standing in line at the bakery.
3. What is her advice at the end, and what reason does she give for it?
Stop waiting until your language is good, because it never gets good if you wait.
Listening 2 · A Coach

Nothing You Learned Is Gone

A basketball coach on athletes who come back after two years away. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the coach say stays, and what does he say goes?
The knowledge stays. The speed goes.
2. How does he describe what happens inside a game?
The player still knows where to pass, but takes half a second longer to decide, and in a game half a second is everything.
3. Why does he call it good news?
Because rebuilding speed is a lot faster than building it the first time, so somebody who stopped is not a beginner.
Listening 3 · Travel Radio

The Six Countries Test

A short radio piece on what really happens to your English on a trip. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the experiment show that almost nobody does?
Almost nobody fails. They find the gate, they order the sandwich, and they explain that a bag is missing.
2. If it was not the English, what does the piece say went wrong?
The measurement went wrong: they compared themselves to a native speaker in a film.
3. Which question does the piece say is the only one that matters abroad?
Whether the other person understood and answered.
