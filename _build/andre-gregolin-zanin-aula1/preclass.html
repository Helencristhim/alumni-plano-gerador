Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

Who Is André, in English? -- Your Life in Four Pieces of Time

A stranger asks who you are, and the answer has to fit a childhood, a team, a move and a year that is not over yet. Key words: to grow up, a prep course, rusty, to be into something, to get by, to pick something up, an early riser, a teammate, to work out, abroad. Structure: the four time frames of a personal story.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To grow up -- to spend your childhood in a place
"I grew up in the interior, about four hours from here."
A prep course -- a full-time year of classes that gets you ready for one entrance exam
"I am in a prep course this year, and it takes the whole day."
Rusty -- not lost, but slow, because you stopped using it for a long time
"My English is a little rusty. I stopped for two years."
To be into something -- to like something a lot and give it your free time
"I am really into tennis, and I go to the gym almost every day."
To get by -- to manage in a difficult situation with the little language you have
"In France I got by, but I had to repeat myself a lot."
To pick something up -- to learn something without ever sitting down to study it
"I picked up a lot of words from the series I watch at night."
An early riser -- a person who wakes up very early, every day, without complaining
"I am an early riser now. The alarm goes off at twenty to six."
A teammate -- a person who plays on the same team as you
"My teammates in Franca came from five different states."
To work out -- to train your body in a gym
"I work out four times a week, usually late in the evening."
Abroad -- in or to another country
"I have traveled abroad six times, and I used English in all of them."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Somebody asks who you are, and suddenly a whole life has to fit into four pieces of time. The first piece is closed. I grew up in the interior, and I played for a team in Franca. It happened, it finished, and English marks that with one shape: the past simple. Nothing in the sentence says whether it still matters. It only says it is over.

The second piece is open, and it is small. Right now I am studying six days a week. That is not who you are — it is who you are this year. English keeps a separate shape for that, with am or are in front and -ing behind, and it is the shape a Brazilian speaker uses least, because in Portuguese the same sentence can stay in the simple present. The third piece is the widest: your whole life up to today. I have traveled abroad six times. I have never lived outside Brazil. No date, no year — the counting starts at your birth and ends at this second.

The fourth piece holds what is simply true. I am eighteen. I come from a small city. I work out four times a week. Here the trap is famous, and it costs a Brazilian speaker the first ten seconds of every conversation: age never uses have in English. Not I have eighteen years. I am eighteen. And when the sentence has to say that a skill is rusty rather than gone, this is the piece of time it lives in: my English is rusty, in the present, because it is true right now.

1. “I grew up in the interior.” What does this shape tell you, and what does it not tell you?
A It tells you the fact is finished. It does not tell you whether it still matters today.
B It tells you the fact is still happening at this moment.
C It tells you the fact is going to happen again next year.
2. Why is “Right now I am studying six days a week” not in the simple present?
A Because study is an irregular verb.
B Because the sentence is about this period of your life only, not about who you always are.
C Because the sentence contains the words right now.
3. The text calls one mistake famous. Which one is it?
A Saying I have eighteen years instead of I am eighteen.
B Saying I grew up instead of I have grown up.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

Somebody asks how old you are. You answer:
A I have eighteen years.
B I am eighteen.
C I have eighteen years old.
Somebody asks where you are from. The strongest answer is:
A I am from the interior. I grew up there and moved here last year, for a prep course.
B I am of the interior of Sao Paulo since I was born.
C My city is small and is in the interior, yes.
You want to say your English is not gone, only slow. You say:
A My English is dead now.
B My English is a little rusty. I stopped for two years.
C My English was rusty in the past.
The other person has finished answering, and you want the conversation to keep going. You say:
A Very interesting. Thank you.
B Yes. I understand you.
C And you — what brought you here?

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.
