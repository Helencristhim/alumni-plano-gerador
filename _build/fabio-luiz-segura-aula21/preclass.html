Lesson 21 -- Pre-class

The Las Vegas Opener

A new city, a new conference floor, and five minutes to go from zero to a real conversation. Key words: convention center, floor plan, demo station, flagship booth, sponsored session, afterparty, to head over to, to scan the room, to lock in a meeting, to hit the ground running. Structure: subject and object questions.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A convention center -- a very large building where big conferences and trade shows take place
"The convention center is so big that I walked eight kilometers on day one."
A floor plan -- a map of a building that shows where each hall, room and booth is
"I studied the floor plan on the plane and circled three booths."
A demo station -- a small area at a booth where a company shows its product working live
"Their demo station had a long queue, so I came back after lunch."
A flagship booth -- the biggest and most important booth of a company or an event, usually near the main stage
"The flagship booths are in Hall A, right next to the main stage."
A sponsored session -- a talk at a conference that a company pays for, usually to show its ideas or products
"It was a sponsored session, so I expected a sales pitch, but it was actually useful."
An afterparty -- an informal party in the evening, after the main event of the day
"Half of the real conversations at this conference happen at the afterparty."
To head over to -- to go toward a specific place or person, with a purpose
"I'm going to head over to Hall C before the afternoon session."
To scan the room -- to look quickly around a place to find the people or groups you want to talk to
"Before I join a group, I scan the room and look for two people, not six."
To lock in a meeting -- to confirm a meeting at a fixed time, so it really happens
"My goal for day one is to lock in one real meeting before lunch."
To hit the ground running -- to start something new quickly and successfully, without a slow start
"I land at seven and my first meeting is at nine, so I have to hit the ground running."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At a big conference, the first hour decides a lot. You arrive at the convention center, you know nobody, and the hall is loud. The good news: you do not need a speech. You need one good question. And the best first questions are about the event, not about you: What have I missed so far? or Which session has been the best?

English has two kinds of questions here. In some, the question word is the one doing the action. Somebody invited you, so you ask Who invited you? Something happened, so you ask What happened? These are subject questions, and they have no do, does or did. In the present, the verb takes -s: Who runs the demos?

In the other kind, somebody else does the action, and you ask about the person or thing that receives it. You met somebody, so you ask Who did you meet? You missed something, so you ask What have I missed? These are object questions, and they need the auxiliary.

Why does it matter at the demo station? Because the mistake is easy to hear. Who did invite you? sounds strange to a native speaker. A clean question sounds confident, and confident people are the ones who lock in a meeting before lunch.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Subject and Object Questions

GRAMMAR

Who is doing the action? That decides if you need do.

UseFormExample
Subject questionquestion word + verb · no do / does / didWho invited you? · What happened?
Subject question, presentverb with -sWho runs the demos? · Which session starts first?
Object question, pastquestion word + did + subject + base verbWho did you meet at Sibos?
Object question, other tensesquestion word + have / are / will + subject + verbWhat have I missed? · Which booth are you heading over to?
Quick testask: is the question word doing the action?yes = no auxiliary · no = auxiliary

Never: Who did invite you? · What did happen? · Who you met? · Which session start first?

Quiz

A Hi, sorry to interrupt. I just landed. What have I missed so far?
B Hello. My name is Fabio Luiz Segura, and I work in the innovation area of Global Trade and Finance at Bradesco.
