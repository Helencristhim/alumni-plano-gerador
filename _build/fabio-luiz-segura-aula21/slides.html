Chapter 3: Who Asks What

Who Told You? Who Did You Meet?

Two kinds of questions, one small word

Grammar Discovery

Who Is Doing the Action?

"Who invited you to this one?"

"Who did you meet at Sibos?"

"What happened at the opening keynote?"

"What have I missed so far?"

Two questions have no did and no have. In those two, who or what is doing the action?

QuestionFormExample
Subject question (the question word does the action)who / what / which + verb · no do, does or didWho invited you? · What happened?
Subject question, presentverb with -sWho runs the demos? · Which session starts first?
Object question (somebody else does the action)question word + auxiliary + subject + verbWho did you meet? · Which booth are you heading over to?
Object question, other tenseshave / will / can + subject + verbWhat have I missed? · Who will you see tonight?
The trapnever did in a subject questionNot Who did invite you? Say Who invited you?

In one line: if the question word is doing the action, there is no do, does or did; if somebody else is doing it, add the auxiliary.

The First Hour on the Floor

Dialogue

Twenty Minutes at Two Thirty

D
Hi there! Welcome to the booth. Have you seen a live demo yet today?
F
Not yet. I literally just landed. What have I missed so far?
D
Honestly, not much. The opening keynote ran long, so everybody's just arriving at the exhibit hall now. Is this your first time at this one?
F
First time in Las Vegas, yes. I was at Sibos last month, though. Who runs the demos here, you or your team?
D
Me, today. I'm Dana. We built the translation layer you see on the screen. It takes old payment messages and turns them into ISO 20022.
F
I'm Fabio, from Bradesco, in Brazil. That's exactly what keeps my team busy. Which banks have you tested it with?
D
Two in the US and one in Mexico. Who told you about us, by the way? We're not exactly the flagship booth.
F
Nobody. I studied the floor plan on the plane and circled three demo stations. Yours was the first.
D
I love that. Look, the full demo takes twenty minutes. Can you come back after the sponsored session at two?
F
Let's lock in two thirty, then. And if it goes well, I'll see you at the afterparty tonight.
Comprehension

About Dana

1. What does Dana say about the opening keynote?
It ran long, so everybody is only now arriving at the exhibit hall.
2. What does her company's product do?
It is a translation layer: it takes old payment messages and turns them into ISO 20022.
3. Which banks has she tested it with, and what does she suggest at the end?
Two banks in the US and one in Mexico. She suggests a full twenty-minute demo after the sponsored session at two.
Listening 1 · The Veteran

The First Hour

A payments consultant explains his routine at big conferences. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he do before he goes to the floor?
He sits down with a coffee and the floor plan for ten minutes and chooses three booths. Only three.
2. Which groups does he look for, and why?
Small groups of two, not big groups of six, because two people are easy to join.
3. What is his opening line about, and what is his goal before lunch?
It is a question about the event, not about him, like which session has been the best so far. His goal is to lock in one real meeting before lunch.
Listening 2 · The Announcement

Welcome to Day One

The morning announcement at the convention center. Sound first — no text.

1. Where is the floor plan, and what does she ask you to do?
In the conference app. She asks you to download it now.
2. Where are the demo stations, and when do the live demos start?
In Hall C. The first live demos start at ten.
3. What do you need to do before noon, and what is your ticket for the afterparty?
Book a private meeting room at the help desk. Your badge is your ticket.
