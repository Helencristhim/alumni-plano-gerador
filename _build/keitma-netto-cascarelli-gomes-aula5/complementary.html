Talk
Dan Ariely — Are we in control of our own decisions? (TED)

Seventeen minutes of a researcher who is careful, out loud, about how sure he is. Every claim arrives with the evidence attached and the level of confidence named — which is the whole grammar of this lesson, used by somebody who does it for a living.

Tip: count the times he says it could be, it might be or it must be. Then notice that he never says any of them without a reason immediately after.

Talk
Tim Harford — How frustration can make us more creative (TED)

Fifteen minutes on what happens to people when the expected number does not arrive. He is an economics journalist, so he explains a variance for a living, and he does it in the plainest English you will find on the subject.

Tip: pick one minute anywhere in the talk and write down every sentence that gives a reason. Then say the same reasons in your own words, out loud.

The Indicator from Planet Money — EV sales slow, US Treasury yields grow

Nine minutes of two journalists doing exactly what you did today: taking a number that moved and offering possible drivers, one at a time, with different levels of confidence.

Tip: every time they offer a reason, pause and say out loud how sure they sound — must be, could be, might be. Then check whether they gave the evidence.
