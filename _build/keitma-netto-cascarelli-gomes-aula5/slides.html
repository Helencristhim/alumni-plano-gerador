Chapter 3: How Sure Are You

Four Levels of Almost

From almost certain to impossible, in one word each

Grammar Discovery

Same Number, Four Answers

"It must be the new contract, because nothing else changed."

"It could be freight, because the fuel index moved in the same month."

"It might be partly seasonality, but I would have to check."

"It cannot be a posting error, because every entry traced back correctly."

Four sentences about the same number. Only one word changes each time. Put them in order, from the one the speaker believes most to the one she rules out.

WordHow sure you areExample
must bealmost certain — there is no other explanation leftIt must be the new contract.
could be · may bepossible, and you have a reason to think soIt could be freight.
might bepossible, but weaker — you would have to checkIt might be partly seasonality.
cannot beruled out — you have evidence against itIt cannot be a posting error.
the shapemodal + bare verb, with no to and no -sIt must be. Not it must to be, not it musts be.
about the pastmodal + have + past participleIt must have been posted twice.
the trapthe opposite of must be is cannot be, never must not beNot it must not be currency. Say it cannot be currency.

In one line: every one of these is followed by a reason. The modal says how sure you are; the because after it is what makes anybody believe you.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Three Reasons, Ranked

One call, two voice notes and one variance table

Dialogue

Eighteen Per Cent Above Budget

M
Before anybody else joins — the south region line. Eighteen per cent above budget. Do you know why yet?
K
Not with certainty, but I have three candidates. It must be the new contract that started in May. The timing lines up and the amount is almost the same.
M
Almost the same, or the same?
K
Almost. It accounts for about eighty per cent of the increase. The rest could be a spike in freight, because the fuel index moved in the same month.
M
And is any of it just the time of year?
K
It might be partly seasonality, since we see something similar every June, although this year is bigger. I would have to check that one before I commit to it.
M
Fine. Is there any chance somebody booked it twice?
K
It cannot be that. We checked every entry above ten thousand and they all traced back correctly, so we are not overstating anything. And you — do you want the three reasons in the note, or only the first one?
Comprehension

About Mark

1. What does Mark want to know before anybody else joins the call?
Whether she knows yet why the south region line is eighteen per cent above budget.
2. Which two-word question does he use to push her on precision?
“Almost the same, or the same?”
3. What is the last possibility he raises, and what is her answer?
He asks whether somebody could have booked it twice, and she says it cannot be that, because every entry above ten thousand traced back correctly.
Listening 1 · A Voice Note

The Word That Costs You Later

A regional director on what he remembers after a review call. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say he remembers, when somebody is confidently wrong?
Not the number — he remembers the word definitely, and after that every figure from that person carries a small tax.
2. Under what two conditions does saying “I am not certain” cost nothing?
If you say what you are certain about first, and if you say how sure you are.
3. What three examples does he give, and what is his verdict on them?
“It must be the new supplier”, “it might be a timing difference” and “that cannot be currency” — three levels, and every one of them more useful to him than a confident guess.
Listening 2 · A Controller

Three Candidates, In Order

A controller walking her team through an eighteen per cent variance.

1. Which explanation does she believe most, and what two pieces of evidence does she give?
The new contract in the south region — it started in May, the timing lines up exactly, and the amount is almost the same.
2. Why does she say freight cannot explain the whole difference?
Because freight is a small part of that line, even though the fuel index moved in the same month.
3. What does she rule out completely, and on what evidence?
A posting error — because they checked every entry above ten thousand and they all traced back correctly.
