Chapter 3: The Code

An Order, or a Request

Four words at the front, and the word order behind them

Grammar Discovery

What Happens to the Second Half?

"Who is joining?" → "Could you tell me who is joining?"

"What is the agenda?" → "Could you tell me what the agenda is?"

"Does she have the file?" → "Do you know if she has the file?"

"Repeat that." → "Would you mind repeating that?"

The blue part is the frame that makes it a request. Look at what the frame does to the red part: something disappears, and something moves.

JobFormExample
Ask for information, politelyCould you tell me + question word + subject + verbCould you tell me what the agenda is?
A yes/no question insideif or whetherDo you know if she has the file?
The auxiliary disappearsno do, does or did insideNot if does she have
The word order goes backsubject before verbwhat the agenda is, not what is the agenda
Ask somebody to do somethingWould you mind + verb-ingWould you mind repeating that?
A softer, shorter requestCould you / Would it be possible to + verbCould you send it after the call?
Check something without interruptingCan I just check + that / question wordCan I just check that everyone can hear me?
The question markbelongs to the whole sentence, not the inside partDo you know where they are based?

In one line: put a frame at the front, drop the auxiliary, and let the subject go back before the verb.

F

M
That works. I would add one. Do you know if your company already works with anybody in Europe?
F
We do, and I will loop that partner in if this goes further. Shall I start, or would you rather go first?
Comprehension

About Marta

1. What is the first problem Marta raises, and what does she say exactly?
The sound. She says he is a little quiet on her end, which is a way of reporting a problem without blaming anybody.
2. How much time does Marta have, and what does she propose doing with it?
About twenty-five minutes, because she has a hard stop at half past. She proposes agreeing the agenda before anything else.
3. Marta says would you mind telling me instead of tell me. What does the frame change?
The information asked for is the same. The frame turns an instruction into a request, and it lets her ask a stranger a direct question without sounding as if she is checking up on him.
Listening 1 · The Opening Routine

Three Things Before Business

A salesperson on the two minutes he never skips. Sound first — no text.

1. What are the three things he does, and in what order?
He checks the sound, he finds out who is in the room, and he asks how much time they have. In that order.
2. Why does he say the question about time has to come at the beginning?
Because if he asks it at the end, it is already too late to change anything. Knowing the time at the start is what lets him decide what to leave out.
3. What does he say about people who apologise for their English at the start of a call?
That the apology is the only thing the other side remembers. He says nobody has ever ended a call because of an accent.
Listening 2 · What Actually Breaks

Half a Second of Delay

Somebody who runs international calls for a living. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say is the real reason people talk over each other on a call?
The delay. She says there is about half a second between the two ends, so both people think the other has finished.
2. What does she tell people to do when it happens?
Stop, say sorry, please go ahead, and let the other person finish. She says whoever stops first is the one who looks in control.
3. What does she say about meetings that run over?
That they never run over at the end, they run over at the beginning, because nobody said at the start how much time there was.
