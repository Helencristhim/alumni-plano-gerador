Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

The First Five Minutes -- Opening the Call

Everybody is on the call and nobody has said anything about business yet. Four things have to happen first, and all four are done with the politest English you own. Key words: an agenda, a hard stop, to break up, background noise, to jump in, to talk over somebody, to run over, to loop somebody in, to wrap up, to catch up. Structure: indirect questions and polite requests.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

An agenda -- the list of points a meeting is going to cover
"Shall we agree the agenda before we start?"
A hard stop -- a time at which you have to leave the meeting, with no flexibility
"I have a hard stop at half past. Shall we take the agenda in that order?"
To break up -- when a voice on a call becomes broken and hard to hear
"Sorry, you are breaking up. Could you say that again?"
Background noise -- the sound around you that the other person can also hear
"There is a lot of background noise here. Let me mute myself."
To jump in -- to start speaking in the middle of a conversation, on purpose
"Can I jump in here? I think we are answering two questions at once."
To talk over somebody -- to speak while another person is still speaking
"Sorry, I talked over you. Please go ahead."
To run over -- to last longer than the time that was booked
"We are running over. Shall we take the last point by email?"
To loop somebody in -- to add somebody to a conversation so that they know what is happening
"I will loop our partner in before the next call."
To wrap up -- to finish a meeting or a topic
"Let us wrap up with the two things we agreed."
To catch up -- to talk briefly and find out what has happened since last time
"Before the agenda, let us catch up. How did the pilot go?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Four things happen in the first five minutes of a good call, and none of them are about business. Somebody checks the sound. Somebody finds out who is in the room. Somebody asks about time. And somebody proposes an agenda. If all four happen, the meeting belongs to whoever did them.

In English, the polite version of each one is not a softer voice. It is a different shape. "What is the agenda?" becomes "Could you tell me what the agenda is?" Look at what happened inside: the verb moved behind the subject, and it stays there. "Does she have the file?" becomes "Do you know if she has the file?" The does disappeared completely, because the frame at the front is already doing that job.

The same happens with requests. "Repeat that" is an instruction. "Would you mind repeating that?" is a request, and the verb after mind takes -ing. When the line is bad and somebody is breaking up, this is the sentence you want ready, because you will need it before you need anything else.

The last one is time. Ask at the start how long you have, and ask whether the other person has a hard stop. Meetings do not run over at the end; they run over at the beginning, when nobody says how much time there is. And if somebody is missing, say that you will loop them in afterwards, so that nobody has to ask.

1. "Could you tell me what the agenda is?" Why not "what is the agenda"?
A Because the sentence would be too long.
B Because the frame at the front already makes it a question, so inside it the subject goes back before the verb.
C Because agenda is a noun that never comes after is.
2. "Do you know if she has the file?" What happened to does?
A It disappeared. The frame do you know is already carrying the question, so the auxiliary is not needed inside.
B It moved to the end of the sentence.
C It became if.
3. The text says meetings run over at the beginning, not at the end. What does it mean?
A That meetings should always start earlier than planned.
B That the last topic is always the longest one.
C That the cause is at the start: nobody said how much time there was, so nothing could be left out in time.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Indirect Questions and Polite Requests

GRAMMAR

Put a frame at the front, drop the auxiliary, and let the subject go back before the verb.

JobFormExample
Ask for information, politelyCould you tell me + question word + subject + verbCould you tell me what the agenda is?
A yes/no question insideif or whetherDo you know if she has the file?
The auxiliary disappearsno do, does or did insideNot if does she have
The word order goes backsubject before verbwhat the agenda is, not what is the agenda
Ask somebody to do somethingWould you mind + verb-ingWould you mind repeating that?
A shorter requestCould you / Would it be possible to + verbCould you send it after the call?
Check something mid-callCan I just check + that / question wordCan I just check that everybody can hear me?
The question markbelongs to the whole sentenceDo you know where they are based?

Never: Could you tell me what is the agenda? · Do you know if does she have the file? · Would you mind to repeat that? · Can I just to check the audio? · Please repeat, my English is bad

Stage 2: Put the Opening in Order

Order

You are hosting the call. Put the first ninety seconds in the order that works.

?"Can I just check that everybody can hear me?"
?"Could you tell me who else is on the call?"
?"How much time do we have? Do you have a hard stop?"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You did not understand the last sentence. You say:
A "Repeat, please. My English is not good."
B "Sorry, you are breaking up a little. Would you mind saying that again?"
C "What? I did not understand nothing."
You want to know how long the meeting will be. You say:
A "Could you tell me how much time do we have?"
B "Could you tell me how much time we have?"
C "How much time? I need to know now."
You and the other person started speaking at the same time. You say:
A "Sorry, I talked over you. Please go ahead."
B "You interrupted me."
C "Hello? Hello? Can you hear me?"
Somebody important was not invited to the call. You say:
A "He is not here. That is a problem for you."
B "We will explain to him later, maybe."
C "He could not join today, but I will loop him in before the next call."
