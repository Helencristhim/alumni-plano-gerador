A party -- a company or person who signs a contract
"Both parties shall keep the prices confidential."
A territory -- the area where a partner is allowed to sell
"Harbrook's territory is Spain and Portugal."
Exclusivity -- the right to be the only one who sells something in an area
"Exclusivity is the clause partners fight for first."
A commission -- the percentage a partner earns on each sale
"Harbrook earns a commission of twenty per cent."
Liable -- legally responsible for paying for a problem or a loss
"Invent is not liable for problems in the customer's own ERP."
A breach -- when one side does not do what the contract says
"Sharing the price list would be a breach of clause 9."
To terminate -- to end a contract officially
"Either party may terminate the agreement."
A notice period -- the time you must wait after you say you will end something
"The notice period is ninety days."
To renew -- to continue a contract for another period
"The agreement renews every year unless one party says no."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A partner agreement is written for a judge, not for a salesperson, so it has its own grammar. The most important word is shall. In a contract, shall is not the future: it is a duty. Invent shall pay the commission within thirty days means Invent has no choice. If it pays in sixty days, that is a breach.

The second pattern gives a right. Harbrook is entitled to a commission of twenty per cent means Harbrook can demand that money. After entitled to you can use a noun (a commission, exclusivity) or a verb in the base form (is entitled to receive). The third pattern gives an option: either party may terminate the agreement. Nobody has to end it, but both sides can.

The negative is just as important. Harbrook must not sell a competing product, and neither party shall share the other party's prices. Notice that neither is already negative, so you never add not after it. Finally, a contract says who pays when something goes wrong: Invent is responsible for its product, but it is not liable for the customer's own ERP.

On a call, nobody talks like this. When you explain the agreement to a partner, change shall to have to, is entitled to to get, and may to can. The meaning is the same. The partner just understands it faster.

1. In a contract, what does "Invent shall pay the commission" mean?
A Invent has a duty to pay: it is not a choice.
B Invent will probably pay one day in the future.
C Invent can decide if it wants to pay.
2. "Either party may terminate the agreement." What does this give the two companies?
A A duty: both of them must end it after one year.
B An option: each one can end it, but nobody has to.
C A punishment for a breach.
3. What is wrong with "Neither party shall not share the prices"?
A Share is the wrong verb.
B It should be shall to share.
C Neither is already negative, so not is too much: neither party shall share the prices.
