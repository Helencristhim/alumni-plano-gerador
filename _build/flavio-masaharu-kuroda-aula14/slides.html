A Duty, or a Right

One small verb decides who has to do what.

Grammar Discovery

Who Has To, Who Gets To?

"Invent shall pay the commission within thirty days."

"Harbrook must not sell a competing product in the territory."

"Harbrook is entitled to a commission of twenty per cent."

"Either party may terminate it with ninety days' notice."

Ask each one the same question: does somebody have to do this, or does somebody get something? What do the red verbs have in common, and what do the blue ones have in common?

Grammar Practice

Duty, Right, or Option?

Say it first, then click to check

A duty, contract style: "Harbrook ___shall provide first-level support to its customers."
A right: "Harbrook ___is entitled to exclusivity for the first two years."
An option: "Invent ___may end the exclusivity if the targets are not reached."
Nobody is allowed: "___Neither party shall share the other party's prices."
The same duty, said on a call: "In plain English, we ___have to pay you within a month."

The Summary for the Team

HARBROOK AGREEMENT · IN PLAIN ENGLISH
prepared by Flávio Kuroda, Invent Software
Harbrook getsSpain and Portugal, alone, for 2 years
Harbrook earns20% per licence
We have topay within 30 days of the customer
Harbrook has togive first-level support
Nobody canshare the other side's prices
To leave90 days' notice, either side
Six lines, no shall, no is entitled to. The contract is written for a judge. The summary is written for the people who will sell, support and invoice, and they need to know who has to do what by Monday.
1. Which clause does the line "Spain and Portugal, alone, for 2 years" come from, and what is missing from it?
Clause 2. It does not say that Invent may end the exclusivity if Harbrook does not reach the targets.
2. Say the line "We have to" in contract language.
Invent shall pay the commission within thirty days of receiving payment from the customer.
3. Why does the summary avoid shall and is entitled to?
Because it is for the team, not for a judge. On a call or in a summary, have to, get and can are clearer and faster.
Chapter 6: Your Turn

Two Recordings, Then the Call

Listening 1 · The Lawyer

Two Questions for Every Clause

A lawyer on how to read a partner agreement. Sound first — no text.

1. What two questions does she say every clause answers?
What must we do, and what are we entitled to?
2. What is the difference between shall and may, for her?
Shall is a duty, an obligation; if you do not do it, it is a breach. May is an option: something a party can do but does not have to do.
3. Which clause does she say people forget, and what happens?
Termination. Nobody reads the notice period, and when they want to leave they discover they have to wait ninety days.
Listening 2 · The Partner

Four Clauses, In This Order

A reseller on how he reads a vendor's agreement. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does he read the territory first?
Because a partner without a clear territory is just a reseller competing with the vendor's own sales team.
2. Why does he want exclusivity in the first two years?
Because he is going to invest in people and marketing, and he wants to know nobody else is selling the same product next door.
3. What usually makes him sign, and what usually makes him not sign?
He signs when the vendor explains the four clauses in simple English on the first call. He usually does not sign when the vendor sends the lawyer first.
