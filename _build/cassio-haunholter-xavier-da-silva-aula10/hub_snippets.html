Lesson 10 -- Pre-class

The Decision Memo -- Recommending and Defending

One page that asks somebody to decide, and the room where you defend it. Key words: a recommendation, a rationale, an assumption, a constraint, a criterion, to weigh, a caveat, an implication, to rule out, to make the case. Structure: modals of certainty and hedging language.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A recommendation -- the course of action you are formally proposing
"The recommendation is option A, with one condition."
A rationale -- the reasoning that explains why a decision was taken
"The rationale is on one page, not five."
An assumption -- something you treat as true without having proved it yet
"The assumption is that the bearing capacity is above 200 kPa."
A constraint -- a fixed limit you have to work inside
"The main constraint is the existing foundation line."
A criterion -- one of the standards you use to judge the options
"We used three criteria: cost, programme and future capacity."
To weigh -- to compare the importance of different things before deciding
"We weighed programme against future capacity, and capacity won."
A caveat -- a warning that limits what you have just said
"One caveat: this depends on the soil report."
An implication -- a consequence that follows from a decision
"The implication is a second review cycle in May."
To rule out -- to decide that something is not a possibility
"We ruled out option C on the first criterion."
To make the case -- to present the arguments in favour of something
"I will make the case for option A in eight minutes."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The recommendation is in the first line: option A, the reinforced concrete frame. The rationale follows in three sentences. Three criteria were used, and future capacity was the first of them. Option C was ruled out on programme.

Then come the two lists that most memos leave out. The assumption: a bearing capacity above 200 kPa, which the soil report has not confirmed yet. The constraint: the existing foundation line, which cannot move.

The memo does not say the frame will carry every future load. It says it is likely to, provided that the report confirms the capacity, and it adds one caveat: the slab may need to be thicker in the equipment area. The director signs it, because she can see where the certainty ends.

1. Why does the memo say “is likely to” instead of “will”?
A Because the writer is not confident about the engineering.
B Because the claim depends on a report that has not confirmed the capacity yet.
C Because will is not used in written English.
2. What is the difference between the assumption and the constraint in this memo?
A The assumption can be tested by the soil report; the constraint cannot be changed at all.
B They are two words for the same thing.
C The constraint is about cost and the assumption is about time.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “Option C is probably to not meet the programme.”
B “Option C is unlikely to meet the programme.”
C “Option C will maybe not meet the programme.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Modals of Certainty and Hedging

GRAMMAR

One line from a promise to a near refusal. Choose the point you can defend, and name the condition that holds it up.

JobFormExample
Certain, a promisewill / will notOption A will carry the load.
Probableis likely to / should / I would expectOption A is likely to carry it.
Possible, no moremay / might / couldIt may need a thicker slab.
Improbableis unlikely to / I would not expectOption C is unlikely to meet the programme.
A condition on the whole claimprovided that / as long as / subject to...provided that the soil report confirms the capacity.
The traphedging is precision, not weaknessI am sure that maybe it works says nothing. Choose one point on the line and stand on it.

Never: it will work for sure, I think · maybe the slab is thicker, maybe not · it is probably to not meet the date · I am sure that maybe

Stage 2: Put the Decision Memo in Order

Order

You are making the case for one option in three minutes. Put it in a logical order.

?"The recommendation is option A, the reinforced concrete frame."
?"We used three criteria, and future load capacity was the first."
?"Option C was ruled out on programme, not on cost."
?"The main assumption is a bearing capacity above 200 kPa."
?"That is not confirmed yet, so the recommendation holds provided the soil report agrees."
?"One caveat: the slab may need to be thicker in the equipment area."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You are confident but the soil report is not in. You say:
A "Option A will carry the future load."
B "Option A is likely to carry the future load, provided the soil report confirms it."
C "Option A will work for sure, I think."
There is a real but small possibility of a thicker slab. You say:
A "The slab may need to be thicker in the equipment area."
B "Maybe the slab is thicker, maybe not."
C "The slab will be thicker, probably."
A director asks what would make your recommendation wrong. You say:
A "Nothing, it is a solid design."
B "A bearing capacity below 200 kPa. That is the assumption it all sits on."
C "I am sure that maybe the soil is fine."
You want to say option C was dropped for schedule reasons. You say:
A "We ruled out option C on programme, not on cost."
B "We ruled option C on programme."
C "We ruled out option C for the criteria of the constraint."
