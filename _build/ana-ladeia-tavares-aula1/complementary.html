Complementary Activities

Optional extras to enjoy outside class. Mark each one as done after you watch or listen to it.

Talk
TED -- William Ury, "The walk from 'no' to 'yes'"

The man who wrote the book on negotiation explains what he calls the third side: the person in the room who belongs to neither party. It is the role you played in the second half of today's lesson, argued in eighteen minutes with stories instead of theory.

Tip: watch it once without subtitles. Then write down the three sentences he uses to describe what a mediator does, and compare them with what Marcus says in the dialogue.

Video
Bob Bordone -- "What Is a Mediator and What Do They Do?"

A Harvard Law School negotiation lecturer answering, in plain American English and under five minutes, the exact question Marcus answers in the first line of today's dialogue. Short, unscripted and spoken at normal speed.

Tip: he uses the words party, dispute and settlement without explaining them. Count how many of today's twelve words you hear, and note the one he uses that we did not cover.

Watch on YouTube ↗
Podcast
Hidden Brain -- "Relationships 2.0: Become a Better Negotiator"

Shankar Vedantam talks to Max Bazerman, of Harvard Business School, about the quiet mistakes people make at a negotiating table: assuming the other side wants exactly what you want, and asking for less than they would have given. Calm, clear American English with a full transcript on the page.

Tip: listen to the first fifteen minutes, then read only that part of the transcript. Mark every present perfect you find, and check whether a date could have been added to any of them.
