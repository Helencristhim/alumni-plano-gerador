Lesson 01 -- Pre-class

What Brought Us Here -- Opening a Mediation

Two sides sit down and somebody has to say, out loud and in order, what actually happened. Key words: a dispute, a mediator, a party, to represent somebody, a claim, a settlement, common ground, a deadlock, to walk away, an opening statement, in good faith, to reach an agreement. Structure: past simple and present perfect.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A dispute -- a serious disagreement that two sides cannot settle on their own
"The dispute is about a song used in a film without permission."
A mediator -- a neutral person who helps two sides reach their own agreement, without deciding for them
"The mediator does not say who is right. She helps them find an answer they both accept."
A party -- one of the two sides in a case: a person, a group or a company
"Both parties agreed to sit down before going to court."
To represent somebody -- to speak and act for somebody in a meeting, a case or a negotiation
"I represent the songwriter. Everything I say today, I say for her."
A claim -- what one side says it is owed, or has a right to
"Her claim is simple: the song was used without permission and she was never paid."
A settlement -- an agreement that ends a dispute before a judge decides it
"Most cases like this end in a settlement, not in a courtroom."
Common ground -- the points that both sides already agree on, before anything else is discussed
"They both want the film to stay online. That is the common ground."
A deadlock -- a point where neither side will move, so the talk stops going anywhere
"We reached a deadlock over the number, and the mediator called a break."
To walk away -- to end a negotiation and leave without an agreement
"If they offer nothing, my client is ready to walk away and go to court."
An opening statement -- the short speech each side gives at the start, before any discussion
"In her opening statement she said who she was, who she represented, and what happened."
In good faith -- honestly, with a real intention to reach an agreement
"We came here in good faith. We are not looking for a fight."
To reach an agreement -- to arrive at a point where both sides say yes to the same thing
"They reached an agreement in two hours: a credit on screen and a fee."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A songwriter and a small film company are sitting in a room with a mediator. He opens by saying what he is not: he is not a judge, and he will not decide who is right. Then he asks the first side to say what happened. In March the company used one of her songs in a short film. Nobody asked her. She found the film in July and she wrote to them twice. Every one of those sentences names a finished moment, so every verb is one word: that is the past simple, and it is how the story is told step by step.

Then the mediator asks a different question: where do things stand this morning? The answer sounds similar and is built differently. They have not answered her. She has never been paid for the use. Nothing there is a date, because the point is not when it happened; the point is what is still true right now. That is the present perfect: have or has plus the past participle.

The rest of the session runs on the same rule. Her claim is a fee and a credit on screen. She has never asked for the film to come down, which means the two sides already have common ground. If they reach a deadlock on the number, the mediator will stop them. And one warning for a Portuguese speaker: a named, finished time kills the present perfect. Not we have signed it yesterday, but we signed it yesterday.

1. "In March the company used her song." Why the past simple and not "has used"?
A Because "use" is a verb that has no participle.
B Because the sentence names a finished moment, and a named finished time takes the past simple.
C Because the company is not the person speaking.
2. Why is it "they have not answered her" and not "they did not answer her"?
A Because the sentence describes what is still true this morning, not one finished moment.
B Because the sentence is negative, and negatives take the present perfect.
C Because there is more than one person on that side.
3. The text warns against "we have signed it yesterday". What is the problem?
A "Sign" cannot be used in the present perfect at all.
B A named finished time such as "yesterday" cannot sit with the present perfect. It takes the past simple.
C "Yesterday" has to come at the beginning of the sentence.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Simple and Present Perfect

GRAMMAR

Two pasts: the one that names the moment, and the one that describes what still stands.

JobFormExample
A finished moment, and you name itpast simpleThey used the song in March.
The story, step by steppast simple, one event after anotherShe wrote, they ignored it, she called a lawyer.
The situation as it stands nowhave / has + past participleThey have not answered her.
A period that is still openpresent perfect, with this year / so farWe have met twice this month.
Experience, with no dateever / never + present perfectHave you ever mediated a case like this?
Since and forpresent perfect — never the present tenseI have worked on this case since January.
Irregular participles that trip peoplewrite / wrote / written · speak / spoke / spoken · send / sent / sentShe has written twice. Not has wrote.

Never: we have signed it yesterday · I work on this case since January · did you ever mediate a case like this? · she has wrote to them · they are in deadlock since July.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

The mediator asks who you are and who you speak for. You answer:
A "I am Ana and I am the person of the songwriter since March."
B "I am Ana Tavares and I represent the songwriter, Helena Braga."
C "My name is Ana. I make the mediation of this case today."
He asks what happened. The film went online on a date you know. You say:
A "They have used her song in March."
B "They used her song in March, and nobody asked her."
C "They are using her song since March."
He asks where things stand this morning. You say:
A "They have not answered her, and she has never been paid for the use."
B "They did not answer her yet and she is never paid."
C "They have not answered her last month."
The other side has finished and the room goes quiet. You want the conversation to move. You say:
A "That is all from me. Thank you very much."
B "I do not agree with anything they said."
C "I think we already have common ground. Can we start from what we both want?"

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

You have two minutes and a room that has never heard the case. Say who you are, who you speak for, what happened and in what order, and where things stand this morning. If a sentence comes out wrong, finish it anyway and keep going.
