Chapter 3: The Code

From Then Until Now

How a follow-up talks

Grammar Discovery

Then, or Until Now?

"I haven't heard back from you since the demo."

"Have you had a chance to look at the table?"

"Has anything changed on your side?"

"I know you've been busy with the budget."

Do we know exactly when these things happened? Are they finished, or still connected to now?

JobFormExample
No answer yethaven't + past participle (+ since, yet)I haven't heard back since the demo.
Ask about progressHave you had a chance to + verb?Have you had a chance to look at it?
Ask about changeHas anything changed...?Has anything changed on your side?

Soften the callI just wanted to + verbI just wanted to check in.

In one line: no exact time and still connected to now: have + past participle; a day or a date: past simple.

Listening 1 · The Sales Trainer

Silence Is Not a No

A sales trainer on why most deals die without a no. Sound first — no text.

1. What are the two common mistakes?
Guilt, as if the customer did something wrong, and the empty nudge with nothing new to say.
2. What should a follow-up that works always bring?
Something new: a number, a short summary, or a question about what has changed.
3. What should you do after four messages with no answer?
Send a short breakup email.
Listening 2 · The Buyer

Why I Go Quiet

An operations director on what happens on the other side of the silence. Sound first — no text.

1. Why did three projects go on hold?
Because the CEO changed the priorities for the quarter.
2. Which emails did she answer?
The short ones, with a question she could answer in one line.
3. Why does she remember one supplier?
He asked to circle back in January, and then really called on the second of January.
Real Document

The Follow-up Sequence

FOLLOW-UP PLAN · VETRA · 30 DAYS
Flávio Kuroda, Invent · for Giulia Romano, after the price comparison
Day 3 · email"Have you had a chance to look at the table?"
Day 10 · call"Has anything changed on your side?"
Day 20 · email + new number"I have updated the 2028 cost for your CFO."
Day 30 · breakup email"I haven't heard back, so I will stop writing for now."
Left column: the day and the channel. Right column: the one sentence that opens the message. Every touch brings something new, and the last one gives the customer an easy way out.
1. Why does the day 20 message have a new number?
Because a follow-up that works always brings something new, not only "just checking in".
2. Say the day 30 sentence in your own words, politely.
I haven't heard back, so I'll stop writing for now. If things change, I'm here.
3. Which message would you change for a customer who is on hold until January?
Day 20 or 30: ask to circle back in January and put the date in the calendar.
