The Pattern

Telling your team what he said

"The figure is wrong." → He said the figure was wrong.
"We will send the file." → They said they would send the file.
"We have checked it." → She said they had checked it.
"Can you resend it?" → He asked whether we could resend it.

Reporting takes the verb one step back

is → was · will → would · have → had · can → could. Questions lose the question shape: not "he asked is it wrong", but "he asked whether it was wrong".

The Pattern

Three verbs do all the work

What he said
What you tell the team
"The overhead figure is wrong."
He said the overhead figure was wrong.
"Can you send the breakdown?"
He asked whether we could send the breakdown.
"Where does the four thousand come from?"
He wanted to know where the four thousand came from.
"We will not pay it."
They said they would not pay it.

Reporting is not only grammar. "He said the figure was wrong" records his position without you signing your name under it.

Listening 1

Answering the query

1. What is the first thing she does — defend, apologise, or ask?
She asks which file he is looking at, before saying anything about the figure.
2. What does she concede, and what does she stand by?
She concedes that the description on the line was unclear; she stands by the amount, which is backed up by the invoice.
3. Where does the four thousand come from?
A warehouse invoice that arrived after his file was exported — so both numbers were right on their own dates.
Listening 2

Telling the team

1. What did the client say about the figure, in her words?
That his file and ours did not match — a discrepancy of four thousand on the warehouse line.
2. What two things did he ask for?
Whether the team could resend the breakdown, and where the four thousand came from.
3. What does she ask her own team to do, and by when?
Cross-check both versions against the invoices and come back to her before the end of the day.

Why does the concession come before the evidence?
Because conceding a small point first shows you read his message — and it makes the evidence on the big point easier to accept.

Your Turn

Four thousand, and a client who will not let go

You reconciled both files. Your figure is right: the invoice arrived after his export. He has now written twice, copying his finance director, and says he will not approve the report until the line is removed. Removing it means restating the quarter.

A

Remove the line to close the matter, and add it to the next quarter.

B

Send the invoice and the reconciliation, and ask for fifteen minutes with him and the finance director.

C

Reply that the figure is backed up and the report stands as issued.

1. Which one do you choose, and what does it cost you?
2. What happens next quarter if you choose A?
3. Is there anything you would concede, whichever one you pick?
