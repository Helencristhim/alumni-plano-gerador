Lesson 12 -- Pre-class

Deciding Out Loud -- To Do, or Doing?

The verbs behind your research decisions and your Bangkok plan. Key words: to decide, to manage, to expect, to refuse, to avoid, to consider, to recommend, to keep. Structure: verb + to + base verb against verb + -ing.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each verb and read the example out loud. Look at what comes immediately after the verb -- that is the whole lesson.

To decide -- to choose one thing after thinking about it
"I decided to present the dosage data instead of the survival curve."
To manage -- to succeed in doing something difficult
"I managed to understand most of the keynote, even with the accent."
To expect -- to think that something will probably happen
"I expect to meet two of these authors in Bangkok."
To refuse -- to say firmly that you will not do something
"The journal refused to publish the figure without the raw data."
To avoid -- to try not to do something, or to stay away from it
"I avoid speaking when I am not sure of the pronunciation."
To consider -- to think carefully about something before you decide
"We are considering running the trial for six more months."
To recommend -- to tell somebody that something is good and they should do it
"She recommended starting with the poster session, not the keynote."
To keep -- to continue doing something, again and again
"I keep practicing with different accents every week."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Mehmet plans every congress the same way. "My first one was a disaster. I tried to attend everything, and at six o'clock I remembered nothing. Now I do the opposite. The night before, I read the program and I decide to go to three sessions. Three, not ten. I avoid choosing two talks in the same hour, because then I spend the hour walking between rooms. I also keep one hour free in the afternoon, for the corridor -- that is where the real work happens. People ask me if I miss good talks. Of course I do. But I manage to have three real conversations, and in December I still remember all of them."
Why does he say "I decide to go" and not "I decide going"?
A Because the decision is about the future
B Because "decide" is one of the verbs that is always followed by to + base verb
C Because "going" is only used for movement
Why is it "I avoid choosing" and not "I avoid to choose"?
A Because "avoid" is one of the verbs that is always followed by verb + -ing
B Because he chooses more than one talk
C Because the sentence is in the present

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Two Shapes After the Verb

GRAMMAR

In Portuguese, everything after the first verb is an infinitive. English has two shapes, and the first verb decides which one comes next. There is no rule to translate -- there is a short list to get used to.

verb + to + base verbdecide, manage, expect, refuse, plan, hope, agree, want, needI decided to present the dosage data.
verb + -ingavoid, consider, recommend, keep, enjoy, finish, practice, missI avoid speaking when I am not sure.
negativethe shape does not changeI did not decide to go / I do not recommend starting there.
questionthe shape does not changeDid you manage to understand the keynote?
after a prepositionalways -ingI am thinking of submitting an abstract.
the pair that changes meaningstop + to / stop + -ingI stopped to talk to her (in order to talk) · I stopped talking (I finished)

Learn them in pairs, not as a rule: decided to go, avoid going. The verb in front carries the shape with it.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz
Two sessions you want start at eleven. A colleague asks what you did. Which answer is best?
A "I decided to go to the leprosy session, and I avoid running between rooms."
B "I decided going to the leprosy session, and I avoid to run between rooms."
C "I decide go to the leprosy session and avoid run."
You want to open a plan so the other person can give an opinion. What do you say?
A "I am thinking of submit an abstract to the next congress."
B "I am thinking of submitting an abstract to the next congress. What do you think?"
C "I think to submit an abstract to the next congress."
A younger colleague asks where to start on the first morning. Which answer is best?
A "I recommend to start with the keynote."
B "I recommend starting with the poster session. You expect to meet more people there."
C "I recommend you start, because I do not know."
