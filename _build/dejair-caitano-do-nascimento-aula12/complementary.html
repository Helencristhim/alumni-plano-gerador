Lesson 12 -- Deciding Out Loud

Conference Talk
"How to make hard choices" -- Ruth Chang, TEDSalon NY

A philosopher on why the hardest decisions are hard: not because we lack information, but because the options are different in kind. Her whole talk runs on the verbs of this lesson -- decide, consider, refuse, keep.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Write down five sentences where she uses a verb followed by "to" or by "-ing", then say each one out loud about your own research.

Listening
"Why do we procrastinate?" -- 6 Minute English, BBC Learning English

Six minutes at learner speed on why we keep avoiding the task we decided to do. The two presenters use "avoid doing", "keep doing" and "decide to do" naturally, one after the other, in a conversation you can follow.

Tip: listen once with the page closed. Then open the transcript and mark every verb that is followed by "-ing". Say three of those sentences out loud about your own writing week.

"Verb patterns" -- 6 Minute Grammar, BBC Learning English

Six minutes on exactly today's structure: which verbs take "to" plus the base verb, which take "-ing", and the small group that takes both with a change of meaning -- the "stop to talk" against "stop talking" pair.

Tip: pause after each example and rebuild it with your own research. Then record yourself saying three sentences with "to" and three with "-ing" about the Bangkok congress.
