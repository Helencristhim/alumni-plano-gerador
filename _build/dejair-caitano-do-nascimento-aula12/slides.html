Chapter 3: The Code

Two Shapes, One Idea

to present, or presenting

Grammar Discovery

What Comes After the Verb?

I decided to present the dosage data instead of the survival curve.
I avoid speaking when I am not sure of the pronunciation.
I managed to understand most of the keynote.
She recommended starting with the poster session.
Click to reveal the rule
Some verbs are followed by to + base verb: decide, manage, expect, refuse, plan, hope, offer, agree, want, need. Others are followed by verb + -ing: avoid, consider, recommend, keep, enjoy, finish, practice, miss. There is no logic to translate from Portuguese, where both are the infinitive — the first verb carries the shape with it, and you learn them in pairs. Two are worth memorizing today: I am thinking of + -ing (I am thinking of submitting an abstract) and the pair that changes meaning — I stopped to talk to her (I stopped in order to talk) against I stopped talking (I finished talking).

Chapter 5: Practice

How Other People Decide

Two voices you will hear in Bangkok

Listening 1

Three Sessions, Not Ten

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What did he do at his first congress, and what was the result?
He tried to attend everything, and at six o'clock in the evening he remembered nothing.
2. How many sessions does he decide on now, and when does he decide?
Three sessions, the night before, when he reads the program.
3. Why does he keep one hour free in the afternoon?
For the corridor — he says that is where the real work happens.
Listening 2

Do Not Read Your Own Poster

Read the questions, then listen.

1. What does she say you should do instead of reading your poster to people?
Ask them one question: what brings you to this topic?
2. What three versions of your work does she recommend preparing?
Thirty seconds, two minutes, and ten minutes.
3. What is the one thing she avoids completely, and why?
Apologizing for her English — nobody in the room is thinking about your grammar, they are thinking about your data.
