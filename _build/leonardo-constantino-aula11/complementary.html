Series
The Diplomat -- Season 1, Episode 1 "The Cinderella Thing"

An American ambassador in London handles a crisis in which every word is counted by somebody. She has to sound firm with one person and careful with the next, and she defends her position every time somebody pushes back. Connection to Lesson 11: this is register control at full speed -- the same choice you make on a call where the client does not agree with you.

Tip: pick one scene and write down three phrases she uses to soften bad news and three she uses to sound firm. Then say your own version of each, about a live file.

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart -- "The Art of Disagreeing Without Conflict"

A Stanford communication podcast on how to disagree with somebody without turning the conversation into a fight. The guest explains why you show that you have understood the other side first, and only then defend your own point. Connection to Lesson 11: twenty-three minutes of exact phrases for conceding a point before you rebut it.

Tip: listen twice. The second time, stop and repeat out loud every phrase the guests use to concede a point, and write two of them into your own words about a real client.

Video
English Speaking 360 -- "Sound More Polite and Professional in English: Hedging Language Explained"

A short, clear lesson on hedging: the words that make a strong opinion sound careful and professional, such as "it seems", "I would say" and "that may be difficult". Each direct sentence is shown next to its softer version, so you hear the difference in tone. Connection to Lesson 11: it gives you the scale, and the class teaches you where to stop on it.

Tip: take three sentences from your last client email and rewrite each one twice, once hedged and once assertive. Say both out loud and decide which room each belongs in.
