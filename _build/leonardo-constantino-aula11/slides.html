Chapter 3: The Code

Two Registers

Formal advisory · semi-formal debrief · same facts, same structures

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"We would recommend avoiding any structure that might be challenged." → "What I am saying is, we have looked at this and the risk is real."

"The client was advised that the ruling had not been granted." → "We told them straight: the ruling is not in yet."

"It is our view that the position can be defended." → "Honestly, I think we are on solid ground."

"We would suggest revisiting the timing before any filing is made." → "I would hold off on filing until we have had another look."

The facts are identical in every pair. So what moves between the left and the right — and what stays exactly where it was?

What movesFormal advisorySemi-formal debrief
Modalwould recommend, would suggest, may, mightI would, I think, we should
Agenthidden: "the client was advised"named: "we told them"
Contractionsnone: "we have looked", "it is not"natural: "we have", "I would", "it is not in yet"
Framing verb"It is our view that...""Honestly, I think..."
Length of turnUnchanged. Both registers need four sentences: your view, the one uncertain thing, the point you concede, and what you would do.
Passive voiceSame structure in both. "was advised", "can be defended", "is made" — the passive is available in either register; the formal one simply uses it more.
Reported speechSame structure in both. "was advised that the ruling had not been granted" and "we told them the ruling was not in" are the same backshift, in different clothes.
Gerund and infinitiveSame structure in both. recommend + gerund ("avoiding"), suggest + gerund ("revisiting"), and after a preposition the gerund again ("hold off on filing"). The pattern never softens because the room did.

Register is a choice, not a level. You do not become less correct when you relax — you become less formal, on purpose, for that room.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I think maybe it could possibly be a bit risky, perhaps."
"There is one real risk, and I will walk you through it now."
"You are wrong about the exposure."
"I concede the timing, but I have to push back on the exposure, and here is why."
"I guarantee the authority will never challenge this structure."
"I would not overstate it: the position is defensible, but nothing is beyond challenge."

One hedge protects you. Four hedges tell the room you are afraid of the question. And assertive is not aggressive: attack the point, never the person.

Chapter 4: The Difficult Call

Meet Dana

Chief financial officer · two contradictory opinions · twenty minutes

Dialogue

The Client Who Does Not Nod

D
I will be direct. Your note says one thing and my auditors say the opposite. Which one of you is wrong?
L
Neither, and I would not overstate the difference. Let me walk you through where the two views separate, and then you decide.
D
Go on. But I do not want a lecture. I want a number.
L
Fourteen million reais over two years. What I mean by that is the worst case, if the ruling is refused.
D
Your note said the risk was limited. Fourteen million is not limited.
L
I take the point, and I should clarify that. Limited referred to the scope, not to the amount. To be more precise, only the intercompany fees are exposed. The memo did not convey that, and it should have.
D
My auditors told me the whole structure would be challenged.
L
They warned you that it might be. That is a fair counterargument, and I concede that the timing is aggressive. Let me rebut the rest: the ruling was requested in April, and nothing has been refused.
D
So you are hedging.
L
On one half of it, yes. On the disclosure I am on solid ground, and I will leave no room for doubt: it was filed, it was complete, and I stand by that assessment. On the ruling I am tentative, because the authority has not answered.
D
And if I push back on your recommendation to restructure?
L
Then give me one reason, and I will either back up the claim with the treaty text or reframe it. What we are recommending is a smaller change, not a full restructuring.
D
That I can take to my board. Send me those two paragraphs by Friday.
Comprehension

About Dana

1. What is her problem at the start of the call?
She has two opinions on her desk that contradict each other, and she wants to know which one is wrong.
2. Which word from the memo does she challenge, and why?
The word "limited" — because fourteen million reais does not sound limited to her.
3. What did her auditors tell her about the structure?
That the whole structure would be challenged.
4. What does she ask for at the end, and what will she do with it?
Two paragraphs by Friday, so that she can take them to her board.

The Real Thing

Read This Brief

Call Brief
Northgate Pay · call with the chief financial officer
Prepared by Leonardo Constantino
ALMA · Financial Institutions
I stand byThe disclosure was filed on 3 April and it was complete. I will leave no room for doubt on this.
I concedeThe timing is aggressive, and the memo did not convey that "limited" referred to the scope.
I rebutThe auditors' counterargument that the whole structure would be challenged. Only the intercompany fees are exposed.
TentativeThe ruling. Requested in April, not yet answered. Any date I give her today is a guess.
Do notOverstate. No guarantee, no "never", no number without the two-year window attached to it.
Internal preparation note. Positions recorded here are those the firm is prepared to defend in writing.
1. Why does the brief separate "I stand by" from "Tentative"?
Because they need different registers: assertive where the evidence is complete, tentative where the authority has not answered.
2. What does the last line protect you from?
From overstating — a guarantee sounds strong in the room and costs you the client the week it fails.
