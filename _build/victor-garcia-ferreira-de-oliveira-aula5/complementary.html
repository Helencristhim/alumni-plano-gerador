BBC 6 Minute English — "Scared of speaking English?"

Six minutes on the thing that stops people who already know the words. It is the exact subject of this lesson, said by two presenters who are not trying to sell you confidence.

Tip: listen once, then read the transcript and mark every sentence that buys the speaker time. There are more than you expect, and none of them sounds like hesitation.

Talk
TED — Julian Treasure, "How to speak so that people want to listen" (10 min)

A sound expert on the mechanics of being listened to: pace, silence, pitch and where the weight of a sentence falls. He uses silence on purpose, which is the opposite of what a pause does to you when it is not planned.

Tip: watch it once and then time his longest deliberate pause. Notice that you never once wonder whether he has lost his place.

Video
TED on YouTube — Celeste Headlee, "10 ways to have a better conversation" (12 min)

A radio host on what actually keeps a conversation alive, from somebody who does it professionally with strangers. Rule number nine is about listening, and it is the one that changes how you answer a follow-up question.

Tip: she speaks fast and clearly. Watch without subtitles first, then pick the one rule you would use in your next interview and say it out loud in your own words.
