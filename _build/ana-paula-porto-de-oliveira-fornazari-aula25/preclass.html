Lesson 25 -- Pre-class

Reported Conversations -- Telling What Someone Said

How to pass on what other people said and asked. Key words: to reply, to complain, to explain, to repeat, gossip, a rumor, to announce, to pass on, to overhear, to deny. Structure: reported speech -- she said that it was, he told me that he would, they asked if I was.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To reply -- to answer somebody, in words or in writing
"I asked Mike about the new rule, and he replied that he did not know."
To complain -- to say that you are not happy about something
"My neighbor complained that the dogs were too loud."
To explain -- to make something clear by giving details or reasons
"The vet explained that the puppy needed one more vaccine."
To repeat -- to say something again
"Could you repeat that? I did not hear the last part."
Gossip -- talk about other people's private lives, often not true
"Do not believe it. It is just gossip from the dog park."
A rumor -- a story that many people repeat, but nobody knows if it is true
"There is a rumor that the park is going to close."
To announce -- to tell many people about something, officially
"The city announced that the park will close for two weeks."
To pass on -- to give a message from one person to another
"Could you pass on the message to your husband?"
To overhear -- to hear what other people say, without them knowing
"I overheard two women talking about the new rule."
To deny -- to say that something is not true
"The organizer denied that the park was closing."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Thursday morning, Mike ran to Ana Paula at the dog park. "Did you hear the rumor?" he asked. He said that the park was going to close. He had overheard two women at the gate, and one of them said that the city wanted to build a parking lot.

Ana Paula knew it was just gossip. The day before, Carol, the organizer, told her that the park was only closing for two weeks. Carol explained that they were going to fix the fence, and she said that the city would announce the dates on Monday. She also asked if Mike could help with the new fence.

Look at the verbs. When you pass on what someone said, the verb usually goes one step back: is becomes was, wants becomes wanted, and will becomes would. Use said that without a person, and told me that with a person.

1. What did Carol tell Ana Paula?
A That the park was closing forever, and that the city wanted to build a big parking lot there next year.
B That the park was only closing for two weeks.
C That Mike was going to fix the fence.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "Carol said me that the park was closing, and that the city would announce it on Monday."
B "Carol told me that the park was closing."
C "Carol told that the park was closing."
3. What happens to will in reported speech?
A It becomes would.
B It stays will, because the future does not change when you tell somebody what another person said.
C It becomes was.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

One step back for the verb. Said that, told me that, asked if.

They saidYou tellExample
is / arewas / were"It is late." → She said that it was late.
present simplepast simple"I need help." → He said that he needed help.
willwould"I will call." → She said that she would call.
say / tellsaid that · told + person + thatHe told me that the park was closed.
a yes / no questionasked + if + subject + verbHe asked if I was coming.

Never: he said me · she told that · he asked if was I · she said that she will

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

Your husband asks what the vet said. You answer:
A "She said me that the puppy is healthy and that he needs more walks every day."
B "She told me that the puppy was healthy."
C "She told that the puppy was healthy."
Mike said: "I will bring coffee." You tell Carol:
A "Mike said that he will brings coffee to the park on Saturday morning for everybody."
B "Mike said that he brings coffee."
C "Mike said that he would bring coffee."
The receptionist asked: "Do you have an appointment?" You tell a friend:
A "She asked if I had an appointment."
B "She asked if did I have an appointment, and then she asked for my insurance card too."
C "She asked do I have an appointment."
A neighbor says the park is closing forever. You know it is not true. You say:
A "It is just a rumor. Carol told me that it was only for two weeks."
B "Maybe it is true, maybe not, I do not know, people say many things at the park, and I do not want to say."
C "Carol said me two weeks."
