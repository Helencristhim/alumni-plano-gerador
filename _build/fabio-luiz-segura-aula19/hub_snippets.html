Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A padel court -- the place with walls and a net where people play padel, usually in pairs
"There's a padel court two blocks from my office, so I play before work."
A poker night -- an evening when a group of friends meets regularly to play cards for fun
"Once a week I have poker night with the same four friends. Nobody talks about work."
A weekend getaway -- a short trip away from home, usually for two or three days, to relax
"Most of my weekends are a weekend getaway. I'm in the city maybe one weekend a month."
A coastal town -- a small town next to the sea
"My father lives in a small coastal town, about three hours from Sao Paulo."
A beach house -- a house near the sea where people spend weekends and holidays
"It's not a fancy beach house, but it's where I actually switch off."
To recharge -- to rest and get your energy back after a period of hard work
"After two days of panels, I need a quiet evening to recharge."
To catch a break -- to get a short rest, or a bit of good luck, after a difficult period
"It's been a crazy week. I finally caught a break this afternoon."
To strike a chord -- to say something that another person connects with and feels strongly about
"When I mentioned my kids and the school run, it really struck a chord with her."
To steer the conversation -- to move a conversation gently toward the topic you want
"After ten minutes about padel, I steered the conversation back to payments."
To open up -- to start talking more freely about yourself and your feelings
"Once people find something in common, they open up much faster."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

In Portuguese, one small word keeps a conversation going: you add it to the end of almost any sentence, and the other person agrees. English has a whole family of these little questions, called question tags, and at a happy hour they do the same job. They are not real questions. They are invitations: It's been a long day, hasn't it?

The tag copies the auxiliary of the sentence and flips it. A positive sentence takes a negative tag: The rooftop is amazing, isn't it? A negative sentence takes a positive tag: You're not from Miami, are you? When there is no auxiliary, use do, does or did: You play padel, don't you? Let your voice go down at the end. That tells the other person you expect them to agree.

The same auxiliary helps you agree in two words. With a positive sentence, use so: I love a quiet weekend by the sea. — So do I. With a negative sentence, use neither: I haven't played poker in months. — Neither have I.

These small structures matter more than they look. A person who answers with a full, formal sentence at the bar sounds like he is still on the stage. A person who says So do I and isn't it? sounds relaxed, and relaxed people are the ones others open up to.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Question Tags, So and Neither

GRAMMAR

Copy the auxiliary, flip it, and let your voice go down.

UseFormExample
Positive sentence+ negative tag with the same auxiliaryIt's been a long day, hasn't it?
Negative sentence+ positive tagYou're not from Miami, are you?
No auxiliaryuse do / does / did in the tagYou play padel, don't you? · It went well, didn't it?
Agree with a positiveSo + auxiliary + II love the beach. — So do I.
Agree with a negativeNeither + auxiliary + II can't sleep in hotels. — Neither can I.
Intonationvoice down = you expect agreement · voice up = a real questionYou work in payments, don't you? (down)

Never: You play padel, isn't it? · You're from London, no? · I don't like it. — Me too. · I haven't been. — So have I.

Stage 2: Put the Happy Hour Conversation in Order

Order

You meet somebody at the bar after the last panel. Put the conversation in a logical order, from small talk to a small next step.

?"I've been getting back into padel after a bit of a break. Do you play anything?"
?"Let's keep it light tonight. Can I message you next week about a coffee?"
?"Long day, wasn't it?"
?"So what do you do when you finally catch a break?"
?"OK, now I'm curious. What do you actually do at the bank?"
