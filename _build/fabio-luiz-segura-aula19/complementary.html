Julian Treasure — “How to speak so that people want to listen” (TED)

A sound expert explains how register, pace, pitch and volume change the way people hear you. It is the difference between your stage voice and your bar voice, with exercises you can practice before the next conference.

Tip: try his warm-up exercises before your homework recording. Then record the same sentence in your pitch voice and in your social voice.

Podcast
Think Fast, Talk Smart (Stanford GSB) — “We Belong Together: How Communication Fuels Connection and Community”

A Stanford psychologist explains how small signals in conversation help people feel connected, and why that feeling matters so much in professional relationships. It is the science behind small talk at the bar.

Tip: write down one small signal he describes. Use it in your homework recording.

Video
Celeste Headlee — “10 ways to have a better conversation” (TED, YouTube)

A radio host who has interviewed thousands of people shares ten simple rules for real conversations: listen, ask open questions, and do not try to win. Short, practical and easy to follow.

Tip: choose the two rules that are hardest for you and write one sentence about each. Try them at your next dinner in English.
