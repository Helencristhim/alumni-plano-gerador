Chapter 3: Keep It Going

Isn't It? So Do I.

The small words that invite the other person in

Grammar Discovery

Four Ways to Invite Agreement

"It's been a long day, hasn't it?"

"You play padel, don't you?"

"I love a quiet weekend by the sea." — "So do I."

"I haven't played poker in months." — "Neither have I."

Where does the red word hasn't or don't come from? And why is it so in one answer and neither in the other?

UseFormExample
Positive sentence+ negative tag with the same auxiliaryIt's been a long day, hasn't it?
Negative sentence+ positive tagYou're not from Miami, are you?
No auxiliary in the sentenceuse do / does / didYou play padel, don't you? · She liked it, didn't she?
Agree with a positiveSo + auxiliary + II love the beach. — So do I.
Agree with a negativeNeither + auxiliary + II haven't played in months. — Neither have I.
The trapnot isn't it? for every sentence · voice goes down when you expect agreementNot You play padel, isn't it? Say don't you?

In one line: copy the auxiliary, flip positive and negative — and the other person has an easy way into the conversation.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Rooftop Happy Hour

A founder at the bar, a banker off duty, and a coach

Dialogue

No Pitch Tonight

M
Long day, wasn't it? I think I've seen every panel in the building.
F
It was. Honestly, after two days of panels, I just need to recharge. You're not from Miami, are you?
M
No, San Francisco. I'm Megan. And please don't ask me about my company. I've answered that forty times today.
F
Deal. I'm Fabio, from Sao Paulo. So what do you do when you finally catch a break?
M
Golf, mostly. And I'm trying to learn padel. Everybody in my office plays it now.
F
No way. I've been getting back into padel after a bit of a break. There's a padel court near my office, so I play before work. It's harder than it looks, isn't it?
M
So much harder! I spend half the game running into the wall. So what about weekends? Do you stay in the city?
F
Almost never. My father lives in a small coastal town, so most weekends are a weekend getaway. That's where I actually switch off.
M
That sounds perfect. OK, now I'm curious. What do you actually do at the bank?
F
Cross-border payments, mostly. But let's keep it light tonight. Can I message you next week? We could talk business over a coffee.
Comprehension

About Megan

1. Where is Megan from, and what does she ask Fabio not to do?
She is from San Francisco. She asks him not to ask about her company, because she has answered that forty times today.
2. What does Megan do in her free time, and why is she learning padel?
Golf, mostly. She is learning padel because everybody in her office plays it now.
3. What does Megan ask at the end, and why?
What he actually does at the bank. She says she is curious now, after the personal conversation.
Listening 1 · The Bar

Off Duty

A banker from Sydney talks about his weekends. Sound first — no text.

1. Where does he go most weekends, and what does he do there?
To a small beach house about two hours from Sydney. He surfs a bit, cooks, and turns off his phone.
2. What does he say about poker?
He has a poker night with old friends every Thursday. He always loses, and he says that is not the point.
3. Why does he say the conversation at the bar matters for business?
Because the people he does business with are the people he has had a real conversation with, and that usually happens at the bar, not in a meeting room.
Listening 2 · The Coach

Two Registers

A networking coach on switching between pitch mode and human mode. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the most common mistake she sees at conference happy hours?
People stay in pitch mode: long sentences, formal words and a strategy, as if they were still on the stage.
2. What should you share, and what should you avoid?
Share something small and real, like a sport, a weekend or a place you love. Avoid anything too private, and avoid talking only about yourself.
3. How should you go back to business, according to her?
Gently, and ideally when the other person asks. Then suggest a small next step, like a coffee or a call, instead of a pitch at the bar.
