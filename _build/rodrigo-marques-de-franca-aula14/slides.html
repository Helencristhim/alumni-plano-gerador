Chapter 4: The Code

By How Much, and to Where?

Grammar Discovery

Same Verb, Different Story

"Registrations fell by twelve percent."

"The amount in dispute rose to 9.6 billion reais."

"New cases went down from 243 to 214."

"There was a sharp rise of forty percent in construction disputes."

The verbs are almost the same. What do the red words tell you — the size of the change, or where the number ended up?

JobFormExample
The size of the changeverb + byFees rose by 8 percent.
Where it ended upverb + toFilings fell to six in July.
Start and finishfrom... toIt went up from 18 to 31.
The same idea as a nouna rise / fall / increase + of + size + in + thinga fall of 12 percent in new cases
How fast or how muchadverb after the verb · adjective before the nounrose sharply · a sharp rise · fell slightly · a slight fall
Stable or at the toplevel off at · peak at · remain stable atIt peaked at 31 in March.
rise or raise?rise (no object) · raise (needs an object)Costs rose. The firm raised its fees.

In one line: by is how much, to is where it landed, of comes after the noun — and the verb or adverb says how fast.

Listening 1 · One Number a Week

The Price of Your Coffee

A columnist explains one line on one chart. Sound first — no text.

1. What happened to the wholesale price of arabica over the last eighteen months?
It more than doubled. It rose slowly in the spring, surged in the summer and peaked in February.
2. What caused the surge in the summer?
A drought in Brazil cut the harvest.
3. Why has the price in the supermarket not fallen with the market?
Supermarkets buy months in advance, so today's price reflects beans bought at the peak. The shelf is about six months behind the market.
Listening 2 · The Chart Maker

Three Tricks I See Every Week

Somebody who makes charts for a living explains how they mislead. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the problem with an axis that does not start at zero?
A small change looks enormous. A rise from 98 to 102 looks like a mountain, but it is a change of four percent.
2. Where do you start a line if you want it to look like it is going up?
At the lowest point, for example at the bottom of a crisis.
3. What are the three questions he asks before he believes a chart?
Where does the axis start? Why does the line start there? What happens if you take the biggest number out?
