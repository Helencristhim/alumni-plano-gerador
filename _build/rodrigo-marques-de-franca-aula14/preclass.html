Lesson 14 -- Pre-class

Reading the Data -- Numbers, Charts and Trends

A number on its own says very little. Key words: to surge, to plummet, to level off, to peak, to fluctuate, to bottom out, a spike, a steady decline, year on year, a threefold increase, a breakdown, an outlier. Structure: describing trends -- verbs and nouns of change with by, to, of and from.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To surge -- to rise suddenly and strongly
"Construction disputes surged by forty percent in one year."
To plummet -- to fall very fast and very far
"New filings plummeted to six in July."
To level off -- to stop rising or falling and stay at about the same level
"Technology disputes have leveled off at around thirty a year."
To peak -- to reach the highest point before falling again
"Filings peaked at thirty-one in March."
To fluctuate -- to go up and down in an irregular way
"Monthly filings fluctuated between fourteen and twenty-one."
To bottom out -- to reach the lowest point before starting to rise again
"Prices bottomed out in the spring and have risen ever since."
A spike -- a sudden, short rise that quickly falls back
"The March spike is easy to explain."
A steady decline -- a slow fall that continues over a long period
"After a steady decline, the average case length has stopped falling."
Year on year -- compared with the same period in the previous year
"Registrations fell by twelve percent year on year."
A threefold increase -- becoming three times as large as before
"The amount in dispute shows a threefold increase."
A breakdown -- a list that shows how a total is divided into its parts
"The breakdown by sector shows where the growth came from."
An outlier -- a value that is far away from all the others
"Remove that outlier, and the picture looks very different."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

For the first time in ten years, the number of new cases at the center went down. Registrations fell by 12 percent year on year, from 243 to 214. That sounds like a quieter year, but it was not. The total amount in dispute rose to 9.6 billion reais, a threefold increase on the year before.

The monthly line was far from smooth. Filings fluctuated for most of the year, peaked at 31 in March and plummeted to six in July. The March spike has a simple cause: several large construction contracts ended in the same quarter. The July figure is still a mystery.

The breakdown by sector shows a rise of 40 percent in construction disputes, while technology disputes leveled off at around 30. One energy claim of 2.3 billion reais is a clear outlier: without it, the increase is closer to twofold than threefold.

1. "Registrations fell by 12 percent." What does by tell you?
A The number that registrations reached at the end of the year, which was 12 percent of the total in 2024.
B The size of the change, not the final number.
C That the change happened slowly.
2. "The total amount in dispute rose to 9.6 billion reais." What does to tell you?
A Where the number ended up.
B How much bigger the amount became, measured as a percentage of the amount in the previous year.
C That the number will continue to rise.
3. Why does the text call the energy claim an outlier?
A Because it was the first energy claim the center had ever received, and nobody had expected it.
B Because it is far bigger than the other claims and changes the total on its own.
C Because it was filed in July.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Describing Trends

GRAMMAR

The verb says how fast it changed. The small word after it says how much, or where it ended up.

JobFormExample
The size of the changeverb + byFees rose by 8 percent.
Where it ended upverb + toFilings fell to six.
Start and finishfrom... toIt went up from 18 to 31.
As a nouna rise / fall / increase + of + size + in + thinga fall of 12 percent in new cases
How fastverb + adverb · adjective + nounrose sharply · a sharp rise
Question and negativeHow much did it rise by? · It did not fall.What did it peak at?
rise or raise?rise (no object) · raise (needs an object)Costs rose. The firm raised its fees.

Never: increased of 10 percent · costs raised sharply · from 243 until 214 · a rise by 40 percent

Stage 2: Put the Description in Order

Order

A good chart description goes from the big picture to the details, and ends with what it means. Put the sentences in that order.

?"This chart shows the number of new cases filed each month in 2025."
?"Overall, filings fell by twelve percent year on year."
?"They rose steadily in the first quarter and peaked at thirty-one in March."
?"In July they plummeted to six, which looks like an outlier."
?"After that they recovered and leveled off at around seventeen a month."
?"So the center is not shrinking, but one year is not yet a trend."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A partner asks how fee income changed. It went from 50 to 54 million reais. You say:
A "Fee income increased of eight percent, and it arrived at fifty-four million reais at the end of the year."
B "Fee income rose by eight percent, to fifty-four million."
C "Fee income raised eight percent."
A client asks about one enormous claim that makes the year look much better than it was. You say:
A "That claim is an outlier. Without it, growth was much smaller."
B "That claim is a breakdown, so we should probably not include it in the total for the year at all."
C "That claim is a steady decline."
Coffee prices went up and down all year with no clear direction. A friend asks. You say:
A "They plummeted all year."
B "They leveled off."
C "They fluctuated all year, up one month and down the next."
You open a two-minute summary of a report for your team. You say:
A "Let me give you a quick breakdown of the figures."
B "Let me give you a quick outlier of the figures, and then we can talk about the details one by one."
C "I will now read every number in the report."
