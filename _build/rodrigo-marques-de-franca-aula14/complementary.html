Lesson 14 -- Reading the Data

Talk
Hans Rosling -- "The best stats you've ever seen" (TED, 20 min)

The most famous chart presentation ever filmed: a statistician turns fifty years of health and income data into a moving bubble chart and commentates it like a sports match. Every verb from this lesson is in it, said at full speed and with great timing.

Tip: pause after the first chart and describe it yourself in four sentences before he does. Then compare your verbs with his.

Video
TED-Ed -- "How to spot a misleading graph" (Lea Gaslowitz, 4 min)

A short animated lesson on the same tricks the second listening from class describes: the axis that does not start at zero, the convenient start date, and the missing context. Clear, slow English with subtitles available.

Tip: watch it twice. The second time, write down every word the narrator uses for going up or going down, and check which ones you learned in this lesson.

Podcast
Freakonomics Radio -- "How to Be Less Terrible at Predicting the Future"

Why most expert forecasts are barely better than chance, and what the few good forecasters do differently. It is about reading trends without falling in love with them -- the same caution the caseload report ends on: one year is not yet a trend.

Tip: the page has a full transcript. Listen first, then find three sentences that describe a change with by, to or from... to, and read them aloud.
