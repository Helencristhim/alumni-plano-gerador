Chapter 3: The Code

Draw It Up, Never Draw Up It

Where the object goes, and when you have no choice

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Their counsel drew up the contract in March."

"They drew it up in March."

"The supplier backed out of the deal."

"The whole thing fell through in April."

Two of these open in the middle, two never do. Where can the object stand?

TypeRuleExample
Separable
+ noun		Both positions work. Your choice.draw up the contract = draw the contract up
Separable
+ pronoun		The middle stops being optional. The pronoun must go there.draw it up · iron them out · never draw up it
InseparableIt never opens. The object always comes after the whole block.back out of the deal · never back the deal out of
No object
at all		Nothing goes anywhere. The verb stands alone.The deal fell through. The talks broke down.
Today's twelveSeparable: draw up · carry out · take over · hold up · spell out · iron out · phase out. Inseparable or alone: back out (of) · opt out (of) · fall through · break down · roll over.

The particle is not translated. It is learned with the verb, in one block.

The Habit

Same Meaning, Different Room

You already have one half of every pair. It is the written half.

You write:

execute
terminate
postpone
resolve

You say:

draw up
back out of
hold up
iron out

The page keeps the Latin verb. The call takes the two-word one. Nothing is lost, and the room stops sounding like a document.

Chapter 4: The Story of a Deal

Two Months, One Clause

The whole cycle, told out loud

Dialogue

What Happened to the Meyer Contract

D
Ana, the Meyer contract. It has been sitting there for two months. What is actually going on?
A
Their counsel drew up the first version in March. We carried out our part — the technical annex went over in April.
D
And then it just stopped. Why?
A
One clause held it up. Liability. And while we were still ironing out the wording, the term rolled over, so the old prices stayed.
D
So we lost two months over a single clause.
A
Worse. Last week the supplier backed out of the whole thing and the deal fell through. Their competitor has offered to take over the same scope. This time I would spell out the liability cap in the very first draft.
D
Agreed — we spell it out before anybody signs. Send me the competitor's name and I will move on it today.
Comprehension

About David

1. Why does David open the call?
The Meyer contract has been sitting there for two months and he does not know what is going on.
2. What does he conclude halfway through?
That they lost two months over a single clause.
3. What does he agree to do differently, and what does he ask for?
To spell the liability cap out before anybody signs, and he asks for the competitor's name so he can move on it today.
Listening

It Does Not Sound Like Authority. It Sounds Like Distance

An American lawyer on what he hears. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What did he notice about his foreign colleagues' English?
It was technically perfect, but they spoke the way a contract is written.
2. What did he always assume formal language sounded like, and what does it actually sound like?
He assumed it sounded like authority. It sounds like distance.
3. What happens to him when somebody speaks written English on a call?
He hears a document instead of a person, and he stops asking questions.
4. What is his advice, in one line?
Keep the Latin verbs for the page and use the two-word verbs on a call — same meaning, different room.

0:000:00
1. What two things did she say wrong for eight months?
"Back out the deal" and "draw up it".

Everybody understood her perfectly, so there was no reason to.

The Real Thing

Three Problems in One Status Update

Internal email
Weekly status — contracts under review

Hi all,

Where the four open files stand:

Meyer — the annex is with them; we should iron out the last two points this week.
Delaro — the agreement was executed on 12 March and will subsequently be renewed for a further period of twelve months.
Ostbau — one liability clause is holding the signature up. I will draw up it again with a cap.
Kervan — the counterparty withdrew from the negotiation and the transaction was not consummated.

Best,
A.

1. One line has a mistake of form. Which one, and what is the rule?
Ostbau: "I will draw up it again". Draw up is separable, and with a pronoun the middle is not optional — "I will draw it up again with a cap."
2. Two lines are correct but would sound strange on a call. Which ones, and how do you say them out loud?
Delaro and Kervan. "We drew it up on 12 March, and it rolls over for another year." / "They backed out, so the deal fell through."
3. Which phrasal verb describes the contract that died?
Fall through. The Kervan deal fell through, because the counterparty backed out.
