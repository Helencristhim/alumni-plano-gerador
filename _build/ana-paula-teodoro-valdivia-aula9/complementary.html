Lesson 9 -- Phrasal Verbs in Action

Documentary
FYRE: The Greatest Party That Never Happened (2019)

The entire life cycle of a deal, in ninety minutes, and every stage of it goes wrong on camera: contracts drawn up in a hurry, obligations nobody carried out, suppliers backing out days before the event, and the whole thing falling through in public. The people on screen are not lawyers, so they narrate it all in plain spoken English -- which is exactly the register you practised today.

Tip: watch with English subtitles and keep a list of every phrasal verb you hear about the deal itself. Then tell one part of the story out loud, using six of them.

Podcast
The Indicator from Planet Money (NPR) -- "Elon Musk's messy merger"

Nine minutes on a signed agreement that one side tried to walk away from, and on the clause that was supposed to stop him. It is a contract story told entirely in spoken American English: the deal is drawn up, one party wants out, the lawyers argue about what the wording forces him to do. Listen to how rarely anyone says "execute" or "terminate" out loud.

Tip: there is a full transcript on the page. Listen once with it closed, then open it and underline every two-word verb they use about the deal.

YouTube
BBC Learning English -- "6 tips for learning new phrasal verbs"

Short and practical, and it argues the same point as the second listening of today's class: an alphabetical list is the worst way to learn these verbs, because a list has no situation in it. The tips are about meeting them in context and keeping them in blocks, which is what you will be doing with the story of your own contract.

Tip: pick the tip you find most realistic for your week, and apply it to today's twelve verbs before the next lesson.
