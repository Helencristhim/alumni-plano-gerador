"Polling: The Math Behind the Madness" — NPR

A statistician explaining, slowly and without condescension, what a sample of a thousand can and cannot tell you. Connection to Lesson 9: it is the content of today's vocabulary in plain speech, and it settles the question of why country size does not matter. Transcript on the page.

Tip: listen once without reading. Then find the point where he explains what the margin does NOT cover — that passage is worth two listens on its own.

Talk
"Why You Should Love Statistics" — Alan Smith, TED

A data journalist on the gap between what people believe about their own country and what the figures say. Connection to Lesson 9: it is a demonstration of why a number needs a range attached, told with more humour than the subject deserves. English transcript on the page.

Tip: answer his questions yourself, out loud, before he gives the real figures. Being wrong is the exercise, and it is more memorable than being right.

"How to Hedge in English" — Advanced Vocabulary for Cautious Language

The full inventory of hedging language at C1–C2, with the difference between the front-of-sentence adverbs and the hedges that live inside the verb. Connection to Lesson 9: the grammar kept outside the class, as you asked.

Tip: choose five hedges you do not currently use and write one sentence with each about this week's news. Five is the right number — more and none of them will stick.
