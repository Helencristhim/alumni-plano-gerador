Lesson 19 -- Pre-class

The Conflict Question -- Disagree Without Making an Enemy

The panel does not want to know who won. It wants the words you used when you disagreed, where the common ground was, and how it ended. Key words: to clash, common ground, to hear somebody out, to agree to disagree, to meet somebody halfway, a compromise, tension, to be at odds with somebody, constructive, a sticking point, heated, to stand your ground. Structure: softening disagreement -- I see your point but, I'm not sure that, would and might, up to a point, and with respect, I have to disagree.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To clash -- to disagree strongly and openly with somebody
"My uncle and I clashed about the hospital bid for a whole week."
Common ground -- the interests or opinions that two sides share, even when they disagree
"We both wanted the hospital. That was our common ground."
To hear somebody out -- to listen to all of what somebody wants to say before you answer
"The second time, I heard him out before I said a word."
To agree to disagree -- to accept that neither side will change its opinion, and move on
"On public contracts in general, we have agreed to disagree."
To meet somebody halfway -- to accept part of what the other side wants, so that both sides give something
"He met me halfway: we bid, but as part of a consortium."
A compromise -- an agreement in which each side gives up part of what it wanted
"The consortium was a compromise, and it was a good one."
Tension -- the nervous, unfriendly feeling between people who disagree
"You could feel the tension in the Monday meeting."
To be at odds with somebody -- to disagree with somebody, usually for some time
"The architect and I were at odds for most of the project."
Constructive -- useful and intended to help, not to attack
"Her criticism was hard to hear, but it was constructive."
A sticking point -- the one issue that stops two sides from reaching an agreement
"The sticking point was not the project. It was the payment terms."
Heated -- full of anger and strong feeling
"Our first conversation about the bid got heated."
To stand your ground -- to refuse to change your position when somebody puts pressure on you
"I stood my ground on safety, and I was polite about it."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The conflict question is where many good candidates lose points, and usually not because of the conflict. They lose them because of the first sentence. "You are wrong" and "I don’t agree" are grammatically perfect, and in a meeting with a founder or a board they close the conversation. English has three tools that keep it open. The first is an opening that accepts something before it disagrees: I see your point, but I’m worried about the terms. The second is a softer verb: I’m not sure that our cash can carry it, that might be too risky, I would suggest a consortium.

The third tool is for the moments when you must not give way, on safety or on ethics: With respect, I have to disagree. It is firm, and it is still polite. Two small traps for Brazilian speakers: English says I don’t agree, never "I am not agree", and suggest takes -ing: I would suggest taking a smaller stake, never "suggest to take". And one habit to drop: "In my opinion, I think" says the same thing twice. Choose one.

The most important rule is not grammatical. In the interview, the panel is imagining you in a study group with forty executives from twelve countries. They are not asking who won your conflict. They are asking whether you can disagree with somebody on Monday and still work with that person on Tuesday.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Softening Disagreement

GRAMMAR

Accept something first, soften the claim, and keep the firm version for safety and ethics.

ToolFormExample
Soft openingI see your point, but ...I see your point, but the terms worry me.
TentativeI’m not sure (that) ...I’m not sure that our cash can carry it.
Wouldwould + suggest / prefer (+ -ing after suggest)I would suggest taking a smaller stake.
Mightmight + beThat might be too risky.
Partial agreementUp to a point, ...Up to a point, you’re right.
Firm but politeWith respect, I have to disagreeWith respect, I have to disagree on safety.

Never: I am not agree · with all the respect · in my opinion, I think · I would suggest to take · you are wrong (as a first sentence)

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

The founder of your company proposes a plan you think is a mistake. Your first sentence is:
A "You are wrong about this plan."
B "I see your point, but I'm not sure the payment terms work for us."
C "In my opinion, I think that this plan is not good."

A "That might be a good idea, let me think."
B "With respect, I have to disagree. Safety steps are not negotiable on my sites."
C "I am not agree with you."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.
