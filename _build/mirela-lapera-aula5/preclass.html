Lesson 05 -- Pre-class

A Night at the Concert -- Going To, -ing, Will

Buying concert tickets in New York and planning the evening. Key words: the box office, sold out, the front row, an aisle seat, the intermission, an orchestra, a performance, an encore, a standing ovation, to line up. Structure: future forms.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

The box office -- the place where you buy tickets for a concert, a play or a movie
"The box office opens at ten."
Sold out -- with no tickets left, because people bought all of them
"Saturday is sold out, but Friday still has seats."
The front row -- the line of seats that is closest to the stage
"We sat in the front row and saw every detail."
An aisle seat -- a seat next to the passage where people walk between rows
"I prefer an aisle seat. It’s easier to get out."
The intermission -- a short break in the middle of a concert or a play
"We had a glass of wine during the intermission."
An orchestra -- a large group of musicians who play classical music together
"The orchestra played Beethoven."
A performance -- one presentation of a concert, a play or a show
"The eight o’clock performance is the most popular."
An encore -- an extra song that musicians play at the end because the audience asks for more
"The audience shouted, and the band played an encore."
A standing ovation -- when the whole audience stands up and claps for a long time
"The pianist got a standing ovation."
To line up -- to stand in a line and wait for your turn
"We lined up for an hour to get in."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Mirela and her husband love music. On their next trip, they are going to see an orchestra in New York. They go to the box office to buy tickets, but Saturday is sold out. No problem: they are having dinner with friends on Saturday anyway.

There are no seats in the front row, but there are two aisle seats in row five. “Perfect, I’ll take them,” Mirela says. The performance is two hours long, with an intermission.

On Friday night, they line up at the door at seven-thirty. At the end, the audience gives the orchestra a standing ovation, and the conductor plays an encore. “We’re going to remember this,” her husband says.

1. Why does Mirela say “I’ll take them”?
A Because she decides at that moment, when she hears about the seats.
B Because she made the plan to buy these seats many weeks before the trip, at home in Brazil.
C Because the tickets are sold out.
2. Why does the text say “they are having dinner with friends on Saturday”?
A Because it is something they are doing at this exact moment.
B Because it is an arrangement with other people, on a specific day.
C Because they decided it at the box office, when they heard that Saturday was sold out.
3. What happens at the end of the concert?
A The audience leaves during the intermission because the performance is too long.
B The concert stops because there is a problem with the orchestra.
C The audience gives a standing ovation, and the conductor plays an encore.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Future Forms -- Going To, -ing, Will

GRAMMAR

Use be going to for a plan you made before. Use the present continuous for an arrangement with a time, a place or other people. Use will for a decision you make now, and for offers.

JobFormExample
A plan or intentionbe going to + verbWe’re going to see an orchestra.
An arrangement (time, place, other people)present continuousWe’re meeting friends at seven.
A decision nowwill / ’ll + verbPerfect, I’ll take them.
An offerI’ll + verbYou look tired. I’ll drive.
A prediction with evidencebe going toLook at the line! We’re going to be late.
The trapwill is not for a plan made beforeNot Tomorrow I will meet my sister, we booked a table. Say I’m meeting.

Never: I will meet my sister tomorrow, we booked a table · Perfect, I’m going to take them (decision now) · We going to see

Quiz

You booked a table for dinner with your sister tomorrow. You say:
A "I’m having dinner with my sister tomorrow."

The clerk offers you two good seats. You decide immediately. You say:
A "Great, I’m going to take them, I planned this a long time ago with my husband."
