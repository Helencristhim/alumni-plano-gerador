Chapter 3: The Code

Going To, -ing, Will

One future, three jobs

Grammar Discovery

Plan, Arrangement or Decision?

"We’re going to see an orchestra while we’re in New York."

"We’re meeting our friends at the hall at seven on Friday."

"Two aisle seats? Perfect, I’ll take them."

"It’s raining. I’ll call a taxi."

Blue: a plan in your head, decided before. Red: something already organized with other people, with a time and a place, like an appointment in your calendar. Green: a decision you make right now, at the moment you speak.

JobFormExample
A plan or intentionbe going to + verbWe’re going to see an orchestra.
An arrangement (time, place, other people)present continuousWe’re meeting friends at seven.
A decision nowwill / ’ll + verbPerfect, I’ll take them.
An offerI’ll + verbYou look tired. I’ll drive.
A prediction with evidencebe going toLook at the line! We’re going to be late.
The trapwill is not for a plan made beforeNot Tomorrow I will meet my sister, we booked a table. Say I’m meeting.

In one line: decided before: going to; organized with a time: -ing; decided now: will.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Concert at Real Speed

One box office, three recordings, one ticket

Dialogue

At the Box Office

T
Good morning! How can I help you?
M
Hi! My husband and I are going to be in New York next week, and we’re going to see a concert. What’s on?
T
The orchestra is playing Mozart on Friday and Saturday. Saturday is sold out, I’m afraid.
M
Friday works. We’re having dinner with friends on Saturday anyway. Are there any seats in the front row?
T
Not in the front row, but I have two aisle seats in row five.
M
Perfect, I’ll take them. How long is the performance?
T
About two hours, with a twenty-minute intermission. And this conductor usually plays an encore.
M
Wonderful. I’ll pay by card. My husband is going to love this.
Comprehension

About Tom

1. What is the orchestra playing, and on which days?
Mozart, on Friday and Saturday.
2. Why can Tom not sell tickets for Saturday?
Because Saturday is sold out.
3. What does Tom say about the conductor?
He usually plays an encore.
Listening 1 · A Concert Hall Guide

Before the Music Starts

A guide at a concert hall gives practical advice to the audience before a performance. Sound first — no text.

1. What time are the doors going to close?
At seven fifty-five, five minutes before the performance starts.
2. What can people do during the intermission?
Buy a drink at the bar on the first floor.
3. What does he ask about phones?
To turn them off, and not to take photos during the music.
Listening 2 · A Couple Who Love Music

Our Concert Tradition

An American woman talks about the concerts she goes to with her husband. Sound first — no text.

1. How often do she and her husband go to a concert?
Once a month.
2. Who chooses the concert?
They take turns: one month she chooses, the next month he chooses.
3. What are they going to see next month?
A jazz trio in a small club.
Listening 3 · A Violinist

The Last Five Minutes

A violinist from an orchestra talks about what the audience does not see. Sound first — no text.

1. How long has she been playing in the orchestra?
For twelve years.
2. What does she do in the five minutes before the concert?
She breathes slowly and looks at the first page of the music.
3. What is her favorite moment of a concert?
The silence after the last note, before the applause.
