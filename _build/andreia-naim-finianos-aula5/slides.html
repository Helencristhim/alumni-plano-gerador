Chapter 3: If It Were Up to Me

Suggesting Without Imposing

One step back in the verb, one step back in the pressure

Grammar Discovery

One Is Real. Three Are Not.

"If you have ten minutes tomorrow, I will send you the itinerary."

"If I were in your position, I would start with a pilot."

"If we had more time, I would take you to the coast."

"If you could change one thing about your system, what would it be?"

The green sentence is a real plan for tomorrow. In the other three the verb after if steps back into the past, and will becomes would. Nothing about the past is being discussed.

JobFormExample
A real, probable planIf + present, ... willIf you have ten minutes, I will send it.
Advice, without giving an orderIf + past, ... wouldIf I were you, I would start with a pilot.
Something that is not going to happenIf + past, ... wouldIf we had more time, I would take you there.
The question that opens people upIf you could ..., what would ...?If you could change one thing, what would it be?
Always were, never wasI / he / she / it wereIf I were in your position — the fixed formula for advice
Why it sounds politeone step back = less pressureWould you like ...? is softer than Do you want ...?
The trapthe half with if never takes wouldnot if I would be in your position

In one line: step the verb back and the pressure disappears. That is the whole reason this tense exists at a dinner table.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The First Twenty Minutes, at Full Speed

One dinner, two recordings, one briefing

Dialogue

Monday Night, Nothing on the Agenda

A
You must be exhausted. How was the flight — did you come through Dubai, or the other way round?
C
Through Dubai. Twenty-six hours. The jet lag will hit me tomorrow, not tonight.
A
Then we will keep tomorrow morning light. Have you been to Brazil before, or is this the first time?
C
First time. I have been to Lisbon twice, which people tell me is not the same thing at all.
A
It is not, but you already have the language. I lived in Lisbon for a year, so we have common ground already. If I were you I would order the fish tonight — it is the one thing this cuisine does better than anywhere.
C
Then I will have the fish. And what would you do with a free Sunday here, if you had one?
A
If we had more time I would take you up the coast, but you have a Friday flight. So my recommendation would be the market in the morning and nothing at all in the afternoon.
C
Nothing at all sounds ambitious. Tell me about the year in Lisbon — what took you there?
Comprehension

About Chen

1. How did he travel, and what does he say about the jet lag?
Through Dubai, twenty-six hours. He says the jet lag will hit him tomorrow, not tonight.
2. Has he been to Brazil before, and what does he mention instead?
No, it is his first time. He mentions that he has been to Lisbon twice, and that people tell him it is not the same thing at all.
3. What does he ask at the end, and what does that tell you about the evening?
He asks what took her to Lisbon for a year. It tells you the ice is broken: he is now asking about her life, not about the company.
Listening 1 · A Partner Director

Nobody Prepares the Small Talk

Somebody who has sat at a lot of these tables. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say decides the whole evening, and what does almost nobody do about it?
The first twenty minutes. Almost nobody prepares for them: people prepare the pitch, nobody prepares the small talk.
2. What does he do now before he arrives, and what kind of thing is he looking for?
He finds one thing about the person: where they went to university, a city they have both lived in, a team they support.
3. What does he say to do when the table goes quiet after somebody mentions work?
Treat the silence as information, and do not fill it with more work.
Listening 2 · An Account Director

Two Kinds of Dinner

Somebody who stopped assuming what an evening was for. Sound first — no text.

1. She describes two kinds of evening. What is the first one like?
Dinner is the meeting: nothing is signed and nothing is even discussed at the table, and then two days later everything moves, because of the dinner.
2. What is the second kind like?
Dinner is genuinely dinner: forty minutes, friendly, and then somebody says right, shall we talk about the numbers.
3. What was the mistake she made for years, and what is her advice?
Assuming which kind of evening it was. Her advice is to ask the person who invited you what the evening is for — she says nobody has ever been offended by that question.

The Page That Makes Small Talk Possible

Nothing on this page is about the product, and that is the point. Four of these five lines could open a conversation that lasts an hour. If somebody had sent you this before the last dinner, the first twenty minutes would have been a completely different evening — and the first twenty minutes are the ones people remember.
1. Which line on this briefing is the most useful for the first two minutes at the table, and why?
The flight: arriving at 06:20 after twenty-six hours via Dubai. It is the easiest thing to open with, it is true, and it needs no preparation from the guest.
2. Which two lines could give you common ground, if you had lived in Europe?
Lisbon twice, and the daughter studying in Porto. Either one connects to a year lived in Portugal.
3. Why does the paragraph say that nothing on the page is about the product?
Because the evening is not about the product. The page exists to make an hour of conversation possible, and four of the five lines can do that.

The Questions

Six Questions Nobody Answers in One Word

"If you could change one thing about your current system, what would it be?"

"If you had a free day here, what would you want to see?"

"If you were starting your career again, would you do the same thing?"

"If money were not the issue, what would your team fix first?"

"If you could take one person from your team on this trip, who would it be?"

"If I were coming to your city next month, where would you take me?"

Ask three of these out loud now, listen to the answer, and ask a second question about what you just heard. The second question is the one that matters.

When He Brings Up the Contract

Scenario: Halfway through the main course your guest brings up the pilot and the price. You do not want to negotiate at the table, and you do not want to refuse either. Answer honestly in one sentence, suggest when it would be better to go into it, and hand the evening back to something else.

Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

Three Minutes, No Notes

Scenario: You are at a long table with somebody you have never met and will need for the next two years. You have three minutes and no information about them at all. Break the ice, find common ground, make one recommendation, ask two questions that cannot be answered in one word, and leave them wanting to sit next to you again on Thursday.

No keywords, no notes, three minutes.
