Lesson 05 -- Pre-class

The Art of the Business Dinner -- Two Hours, No Agenda

How to open a table with a stranger, keep it going, and suggest without imposing anything. Key words: cuisine, jet lag, an itinerary, hospitality, an impression, small talk, to bring something up, to catch up, to find common ground, a recommendation, to break the ice, to read the room. Structure: second conditional, against the first.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Cuisine -- the style of cooking of a country or a region
"If you have never tried this cuisine, let me order for the table."
Jet lag -- being tired at the wrong hour because your body is still in another time zone
"He landed at six. Do not plan anything difficult until Tuesday, because of the jet lag."
An itinerary -- the plan of where somebody will be, and when, during a trip
"I will send the itinerary tonight so nobody has to ask me where to be."
Hospitality -- how well you look after a guest, and how welcome you make them feel
"They still talk about the hospitality, and nobody remembers the presentation."
An impression -- what somebody thinks of you afterwards, based on very little evidence
"Twenty minutes of small talk make a longer impression than the whole demo."
Small talk -- light conversation about nothing important, which is doing something important
"People prepare the pitch. Nobody prepares the small talk."
To bring something up -- to start a subject that nobody had mentioned yet
"Somebody brought up the contract before the starters arrived."
To catch up -- to talk to somebody again after a long time, and hear what has happened since
"We have not seen each other since Shanghai. Let us catch up before the others arrive."
To find common ground -- to find the one thing two people share, when everything else is different
"We found common ground in about four minutes: we had both lived in Lisbon."
A recommendation -- something you suggest to somebody because you think they will like it
"If you had one free evening, what would your recommendation be?"
To break the ice -- to say the first thing that makes strangers start talking
"The flight is the easiest way to break the ice, and it always works."
To read the room -- to notice how the people at the table are feeling, and change what you do
"If the table goes quiet after somebody mentions work, read the room."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The guest lands at twenty past six after twenty-six hours in the air, and by eight he is at a table with four people he has never met. The jet lag will hit him tomorrow, not tonight. Nobody at that table is going to sign anything, and everybody knows it. What happens instead is that somebody asks about the flight, somebody else asks whether he has been to the country before, and within four minutes they have found common ground or they have not.

This is the part that people do not prepare. They prepare the pitch and they arrive at the dinner with nothing. If I were starting again, one director said, that is the only skill I would work on, because everything else you can prepare on a plane. He now finds one thing about each guest before he arrives: a city they have both lived in, a university, a team. It takes ten minutes and it is the difference between forty minutes of small talk and an hour of conversation.

The second thing is knowing when to stop. If somebody brings up the contract between the starters and the main course, the temperature drops, and the honest move is not to refuse and not to negotiate either. If it were up to me we would go into that now, but I would rather show you properly on Tuesday does the whole job in one sentence. And if the table goes quiet after somebody mentions work, that is information: read the room, and do not fill the silence with more work. The hospitality is what they will remember, and the impression it leaves lasts longer than the demo.

1. "If I were starting again, that is the only skill I would work on." Is he starting again?
A Yes, he has just changed career.
B No. He is imagining something that is not true, and English marks that by stepping the verb back into the past.
C He is talking about something he did many years ago.
2. "If it were up to me we would go into that now, but I would rather show you properly on Tuesday." What is this sentence doing?
A Refusing to discuss the contract at all.
B Saying no to the moment without saying no to the person, and offering a better one instead.
C Agreeing to negotiate immediately.
3. Why does the text say the first twenty minutes are the part nobody prepares?
A Because people prepare the pitch and arrive at the dinner with nothing, even though ten minutes of preparation would change the whole evening.
B Because small talk cannot be prepared at all.
C Because the first twenty minutes do not matter.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Second Conditional

GRAMMAR

Step the verb back and the pressure disappears. That is the whole reason this tense exists at a table.

JobFormExample
A real, probable planIf + present, ... willIf you have ten minutes, I will send it.
Advice, without an orderIf + past, ... wouldIf I were you, I would start with a pilot.
Something not going to happenIf + past, ... wouldIf we had more time, I would take you there.
The question that opens people upIf you could ..., what would ...?If you could change one thing, what would it be?
Always were, never wasI / he / she / it wereIf I were in your position — the fixed formula for advice
Why it sounds politeone step back = less pressureWould you like ...? is softer than Do you want ...?

Never: If I would be in your position · If we had more time, I will take you · If you would have ten minutes · If you would could change one thing

Stage 2: Put the Evening in Order

Order

Your guest has just sat down after a twenty-six hour flight. Put your side of the evening in a logical order.

?"You must be exhausted. How was the flight?"
?"Have you been to Brazil before, or is this the first time?"
?"Lisbon? I lived there for a year, so we have common ground already."
?"If I were you, I would order the fish tonight."
?"If you had a free day here, what would you want to see?"
?"It has been a long day for you. Shall we pick this up tomorrow?"

Quiz

A "If I would be you, I would order the fish."

C "You order the fish."
Your guest brings up the contract between courses. You do not want to negotiate at the table. You say:
A "If it were up to me we would go into that now, but I would rather show you properly on Tuesday."
B "We do not talk about business at dinner."
C "Sorry, my English is not good enough for that."

A "If you had ten minutes tomorrow, I would send you the itinerary."
B "If you have ten minutes tomorrow, I will send you the itinerary."
C "If you would have ten minutes tomorrow, I send you the itinerary."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

You are seated next to somebody you have never met and will need for the next two years. You have three minutes and no information about them at all. Break the ice, find common ground, make one recommendation, and ask two questions that cannot be answered in one word. Do not mention your company once, and use if I were or if you could at least three times.
