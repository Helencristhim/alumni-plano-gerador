Lesson 5 -- The Art of the Business Dinner

Talk
Kio Stark -- "Why you should talk to strangers" (TED, 12 min)

Twelve minutes on the exact skill of the first twenty minutes at a table: how a conversation with somebody you have never met actually starts, and why most of us avoid it. Clear, unhurried American English, and every example is a small piece of a script you can steal.

Tip: watch with English subtitles and write down the three openings she describes. Then decide which one you would use on somebody who has just landed after twenty-six hours.

Podcast
NPR Life Kit -- "How to be a supercommunicator"

You met this one in lesson 1 and it is worth a second listen for a different reason. This time do not listen for the questions: listen for what he says about matching the other person — whether they came to the table for a decision, for a feeling, or just to be with people. It is the same distinction the second recording in class made about two kinds of dinner.

Tip: listen from minute six with the transcript hidden, then read that section. Note one thing you would do differently on Monday night.

Series
Somebody Feed Phil -- any episode (Netflix)

A man arrives in a city where he knows nobody and spends an hour making strangers talk to him over food. It is small talk as a professional skill, filmed. Watch what he does in the first minute with each person, and how often he asks instead of tells. American English, warm and fast, with a lot of the food vocabulary from this lesson.

Tip: pick the episode set in a city you have visited. Count how many questions he asks before he says anything about himself, and try the same ratio on Monday.
