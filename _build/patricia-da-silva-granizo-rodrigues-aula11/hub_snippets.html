The Pay Conversation

The ten terms of compensation and benefits in the US and the passive voice of policy. Key words: pay band, base salary, merit increase, bonus pool, total rewards, pay equity, pay transparency, employer match, cost of living, counteroffer. Structure: present and present perfect passive, modal passive and have something done.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read it inside a real sentence. Several of these have a Brazilian equivalent that works differently in the US.

Pay band -- the minimum and maximum salary set for one level of role
"Every field engineer in Austin sits inside the same pay band."
Base salary -- the fixed pay before any bonus, benefit or allowance
"Base salary is what the candidate compares first, even when it matters least."
Merit increase -- a raise given for performance, usually once a year
"Merit increases are decided in March and paid in April."
Bonus pool -- the total amount of money set aside for bonuses in a year
"The bonus pool is fixed by the board before anybody is rated."
Total rewards -- everything an employee receives for their work, not only the salary
"When we talk about total rewards, the health plan is half the story in the US."
Pay equity -- paying people fairly for the same work, whoever they are
"Pay equity is the question the engineers will ask, even if nobody asks it out loud."
Pay transparency -- rules or practices that make salary ranges visible
"Pay transparency in California means the range is public on the day the ad goes live."
Employer match -- money the company adds to an employee retirement savings plan
"A four percent employer match is the first thing an American candidate asks about."
Cost of living -- how much money you need for housing, food and transportation in a place
"The cost of living in Sacramento is higher than in Austin, and the bands show it."
Counteroffer -- an offer made to keep somebody who has accepted another job
"A counteroffer buys you six months, rarely more."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

At JEM System, salaries are reviewed once a year. Ratings are agreed in March, merit increases are calculated from the bonus pool, and everything is paid in April. This year the Texas bands have been updated, and the California bands are being built now.

In California, the range must be posted in every job ad, so we had the bands audited by an outside firm before the first ad went live. One thing was not left to the passive: I decided not to copy the Austin numbers, and I told Atlanta why.

1. Salaries are reviewed once a year. Why is the passive natural here?
A Because the passive is always more formal and polite in business English.
B Because the salaries were reviewed in the past.
C Because it describes a process, and who does the review is not the point.
2. We had the bands audited by an outside firm. Who did the audit?
A The HR team at JEM System, with some help from finance in Atlanta.
B An outside firm -- JEM System arranged it.
C Nobody yet -- it is a plan.
3. Why does the writer say I decided and not it was decided in the last sentence?
A Because a decision should have a name attached to it.
B Because decide cannot be used in the passive voice in English.
C Because the active voice is shorter, and shorter is always better in writing.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Passive of Policy

GRAMMAR

The passive moves the focus from the person to the process. That makes it the natural voice of policies, procedures and rules -- and a risky voice for decisions, because it removes the person who decided.

FormWhat it doesExample
present simple passive (is / are + participle)how a process works, when WHO does it is obvious or does not matterSalaries are reviewed every April.
modal passive (must / should / can be + participle)a rule, an obligation or a possibilityIn California the range must be posted in the ad.
present perfect passive (has / have been + participle)something done recently, where the result matters nowThe Texas bands have been updated.
have something done (causative)you arranged for somebody else to do itWe had the bands audited last year.
the trapIt was decided to freeze the bonus. The passive is perfect for a process and dangerous for a decision: it hides who decided. In a pay conversation, say who decided: The board decided, or I decided.

Form check: the passive always has a form of be plus the past participle. are reviewed, must be posted, have been updated. Losing the be or the -ed is the most common slip at B2. And have something done keeps the object in the middle: we had the bands audited, never we had audited the bands.

Stage 2: Build the Pay Explanation

Order

The order that answers the engineer before he asks the next question.

?Your range was set from the Austin market data.
?You are right to ask, and I will tell you exactly how it works.
?We had both sets of bands audited, so the gap is not an accident.
?The Sacramento range is higher because the cost of living is higher.
?What I can look at is where you sit inside your band, and I will do that this month.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

One right answer each. Read the situation before you look at the options.

You are describing the annual review process to a new US manager.
A We review the salaries every April, I think, more or less.
B Salaries are reviewed every April.
C Salaries are review every April by the team that does it.
D Salaries reviewed every April.
An engineer asks why Sacramento pays more for the same job. What do you say first?
A It was decided by somebody in Atlanta, so there is nothing I can do about it.
B Sacramento is more important for the company at the moment.
C The difference is explained by cost of living, and the bands were audited.
D Salaries are confidential, so I cannot talk about another state with you.
You decided not to match a competitor offer. How do you tell the CEO?
A It was decided not to match the offer, after a long and careful review.
B The offer was not matched.
C The offer has not been matched yet, but it could be matched later if necessary.
D I decided not to match the offer, and here is why.
An outside firm checked your pay bands at your request. Say it.
A We had the bands audited by an outside firm.
B We audited the bands by an outside firm.
C We had audited the bands with the help of an outside firm last year.
D The bands audited an outside firm.
