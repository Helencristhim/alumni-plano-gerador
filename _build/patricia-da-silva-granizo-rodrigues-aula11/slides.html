The Atlanta Call

G
Greg Hollis, Total Rewards. Before we post the Sacramento roles, I need to know one thing: where do the pay bands come from?
P
They were built from the Austin data and then adjusted. The adjustment has been reviewed by finance, but not by you yet.
G
Then let me slow you down. In California the range must be posted in the ad, so whatever we write is public on day one.
P
Understood. And in Texas?
G
No posting requirement in Texas. But engineers talk to each other, so assume every number will be compared, whatever the law says.
P
That is exactly my worry about pay equity. If two people do the same job in two states, how do you explain the gap?
G
With cost of living, and only cost of living. If location does not explain the difference, it should not be there. We had our own bands audited last year for that reason.
P
Then I would like ours to be audited too, before anything is posted. I would rather we explain the gap than have the engineers discover it.

Chapter 4: The Code

The Voice of Policy, and the Voice of Responsibility

When the passive makes you sound precise, and when it makes you disappear

Grammar Discovery

The Bands Have Been Reviewed

"Salaries are reviewed every April."

"In California the range must be posted in the ad."

"The Texas bands have been updated by finance."

"We had the bands audited last year."

In the first three, who does the action is not the subject of the sentence. In the last one, somebody else did the work for us. Why would an HR director choose these forms when she explains a pay system?

FormWhat it doesExample
present simple passive (is / are + participle)how a process works, when WHO does it is obvious or does not matterSalaries are reviewed every April.
modal passive (must / should / can be + participle)a rule, an obligation or a possibilityIn California the range must be posted in the ad.
present perfect passive (has / have been + participle)something done recently, where the result matters nowThe Texas bands have been updated.
have something done (causative)you arranged for somebody else to do itWe had the bands audited last year.
the trapIt was decided to freeze the bonus. The passive is perfect for a process and dangerous for a decision: it hides who decided. In a pay conversation, say who decided: The board decided, or I decided.

Portuguese has the same passive, and Brazilians use foi decidido to soften bad news. In English that sounds evasive very fast. Use the passive for the process, the active for the decision.

Chapter 5: The Ear

Two People Who Talk About Pay for a Living

A compensation consultant and an HR director who lost the same engineer twice

Listening 1

What People Actually Compare

A compensation consultant, briefing a new client. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What does every new client show him first, and why is that not enough?
Their base salaries. That is the number on the offer letter, but it is not what people compare once they are inside.
2. What two parts of total rewards does he say matter in the US?
The health plan, which can be worth twenty or thirty percent of the salary, and the employer match on retirement.
3. What does he say pay transparency changes?
Pay bands stop being a private document and become a public promise -- a gap you cannot explain in one sentence will be explained for you, and not kindly.
Listening 2

The Engineer We Lost Twice

An HR director, on a retention decision she regrets. Sound first -- no text.

0:000:00
1. What happened when the engineer brought her the competitor offer?
They matched the fifteen percent within a day, and eight months later he left anyway.
2. What does she say the real problem was?
Not the money. He had been asking for a different role for a year, and the request had been parked in every review.
3. What does she ask now when somebody brings her an offer?
What they would need to stay if the money were the same.
