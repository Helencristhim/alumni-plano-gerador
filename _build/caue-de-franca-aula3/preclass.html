Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

The First Two Days -- Could You Tell Me ...?

Your first morning in a new city: a cafe, a stranger on the street, a check to pay. Key words: for here or to go, to grab a bite, a refill, the check, to split the bill, a tip, the restroom, sold out, out of your comfort zone, to give it a shot, to strike up a conversation, to get by. Structure: indirect questions.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

For here or to go? -- what a cafe asks you: will you eat there or take the food with you?
"For here or to go?" "To go, please."
To grab a bite -- to eat something quickly
"Let’s grab a bite before the museum."
A refill -- a second serving of a drink in the same cup, often free
"Coffee comes with a free refill here."
The check -- the paper that shows how much you pay in a restaurant
"Could we have the check, please?"
To split the bill -- to divide the cost of a meal between two or more people
"We split the bill three ways."
A tip -- extra money you give a waiter or a driver for the service
"In the US, a tip of 18 to 20 percent is normal."
The restroom -- a public toilet, in American English
"Excuse me, where is the restroom?"
Sold out -- with nothing left to sell
"The cinnamon rolls were sold out by ten."
Out of your comfort zone -- in a situation that feels new and a little difficult
"Traveling alone takes you out of your comfort zone."
To give it a shot -- to try something, even if you are not sure it will work
"I’ve never ordered in English, but I’ll give it a shot."
To strike up a conversation -- to start talking with someone you do not know
"I struck up a conversation with the man next to me on the bus."
To get by -- to manage with what you have, even if it is not perfect
"My English isn’t perfect, but I get by."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On his second morning in Toronto, Cauê goes to a cafe alone. It takes him out of his comfort zone, but he decides to give it a shot. “Could you tell me what the special is?” he asks. The cinnamon rolls are almost sold out, so he orders the last one.

“For here or to go?” the barista asks. “For here. Do you know if the latte comes with a refill?” It does not. He also asks where the restroom is.

At lunch he meets a couple from Ireland, and they strike up a conversation. At the end, they ask for the check, split the bill and leave a twenty percent tip. His English is not perfect, but he gets by.

1. Why does the text say “where the restroom is” and not “where is the restroom”?
A Because it is the past tense, and the past changes the order of the words.
B Because after a polite opening, the question takes the order of a normal sentence.
C Because restroom is an American word, and American English uses a different order.
2. What happens with the cinnamon rolls?
A There are none left, so he orders a maple latte instead.
B They are almost sold out, so he orders the last one.
C He takes them to go, because the cafe is full.
3. Which question is correct?
A “Do you know if the latte comes with a refill?”
B “Do you know does the latte come with a refill or not?”
C “Do you know if does the latte come with a refill?”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Indirect Questions -- Could You Tell Me Where It Is?

GRAMMAR

A direct question can sound abrupt to a stranger. Start with could you tell me, do you know or I was wondering, and the rest of the question takes the order of a normal sentence, with no do, does or did.

JobFormExample
Open politelyCould you tell me / Do you know / Can you tell meCould you tell me where ...
Wh- questionsopening + wh- word + subject + verbwhere the restroom is (not where is the restroom)
Yes / no questionsopening + if / whether + subject + verbDo you know if this bus goes downtown?
No do / does / didthe second part has no auxiliaryhow much it costs (not how much does it cost)
Extra politeI was wondering if / whether ... (no question mark)I was wondering whether you have a table.
The trapquestion order after the openingNot could you tell me where is it. Say where it is.

Never: Could you tell me where is it? · Do you know what time does it open? · Can you tell me how much does it cost? · Let’s split up the bill

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You need a pharmacy and you stop a stranger. You say:
A "Excuse me, could you tell me where is the nearest pharmacy, please?"
B "Excuse me, could you tell me where the nearest pharmacy is?"
C "Excuse me, do you know where is there a pharmacy near here?"
You want to know if the museum is open today. You say:
A "Do you know if the museum is open today?"
B "Do you know is the museum open today or closed?"
C "Do you know if is the museum open today?"
Dinner is over and you want to pay. You say:
A "Could we have the check, please?"
B "Could we have the tip of the dinner, please, all together?"
C "Could we have the refill of the money now, please?"
You and a friend want to pay half each. You say:
A "Let’s split up the bill, like we did last time."
