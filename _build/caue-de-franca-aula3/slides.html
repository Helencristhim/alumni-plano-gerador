The First Two Days

Chapter 3: The Code

Could You Tell Me ...?

The polite question, and its word order

Grammar Discovery

What Do These Four Questions Have in Common?

"Could you tell me where the restroom is?"

"Do you know if this bus goes downtown?"

"I was wondering whether you have a table for one."

"Can you tell me how much a refill costs?"

The red words open the question politely. Now look at the blue words: the restroom is, this bus goes. Is that the order of a question or of a normal sentence? And where are do and does? In sentences 2 and 3, the question has no question word: what replaces it?

JobFormExample
Open politelyCould you tell me / Do you know / Can you tell meCould you tell me where ...
Wh- questionsopening + wh- word + subject + verbwhere the restroom is (not where is the restroom)
Yes / no questionsopening + if / whether + subject + verbDo you know if this bus goes downtown?
No do / does / didthe second part has no auxiliaryhow much it costs (not how much does it cost)
Extra politeI was wondering if / whether ... (no question mark)I was wondering whether you have a table.
The trapquestion order after the openingNot could you tell me where is it. Say where it is.

In one line: after could you tell me / do you know, the question becomes a normal sentence: where it is, if it goes, how much it costs.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The First Day, at Real Speed

One cafe, three recordings, one check

Dialogue

At the Counter

O
Hi there! What can I get for you this morning?
C
Hi! Could you tell me what the special is today?
O
Sure. It’s a maple latte. And the cinnamon rolls just came out of the oven, but they’re almost sold out.
C
Then I’ll give it a shot. One maple latte and one cinnamon roll, please.
O
Perfect. For here or to go?
C
For here. Do you know if the latte comes with a refill?
O
The regular coffee does, the latte doesn’t, sorry. You’re not from around here, are you?
C
No, I’m from São Paulo. It’s my second day, so I’m a bit out of my comfort zone. Oh, I was wondering where the restroom is.
Comprehension

About Olivia

1. What is the special today at Olivia’s cafe?
A maple latte.
2. What does Olivia say about the cinnamon rolls?
They just came out of the oven, but they are almost sold out.
3. Which drink comes with a refill, according to Olivia?
The regular coffee; the latte does not.
Listening 1 · A Tour Guide

Tips for Day One

A tour guide in New York gives a group of visitors advice for their first day. Sound first — no text.

1. How much should you tip in a restaurant, according to her?
About eighteen to twenty percent.
2. What is the difference between the bill and the check?
In a restaurant in the US, people usually say the check.
3. What is her advice if you are lost?
Ask a stranger politely: most people are happy to help.
Listening 2 · A Traveler’s Podcast

How I Got By

A British traveler talks about his first two days in a city where he did not speak the language well. Sound first — no text.

1. What did he do on the first day, and why?
He stayed in the hotel and ordered room service, because he was afraid of speaking.
2. What happened on the second day at the bus stop?
He asked a woman if the bus went downtown, and they struck up a conversation.
3. What did he learn from the experience?
That you do not need perfect language to get by; you need to give it a shot.
Listening 3 · A Hotel Receptionist

The Questions Guests Ask

A hotel receptionist in Toronto talks about the questions she hears every day. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the most common question she hears?
Where the nearest pharmacy or supermarket is.
2. Which question makes her laugh?
Whether it is always this cold.
3. What makes a guest sound polite, in her opinion?
Starting with could you tell me or I was wondering, and saying thank you.

In Canada and the US, the menu price is not the final price: tax comes on the check, and the tip comes after that.
1. What is the difference between the subtotal and the total?
The tax: HST of 13 percent, $1.27.
2. Ask the waiter about the tip with an indirect question.
For example: could you tell me if the tip is included?
3. A friend had the same order. You want to pay half each. What do you say?
For example: shall we split the bill?
