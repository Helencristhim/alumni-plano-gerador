Chapter 4: The Code

If..., Then Will

A real possibility and its result

Grammar Discovery

Two Parts, One Future

"If more ships come, the streets will be overcrowded."

"If the rent goes up, young families will move out."

"If you come in May, you will find a peaceful city."

"If the city doesn't act, the old town won't be alive."

Is each sentence about the past, about always, or about a possible future? Now look: which part has will -- the blue part or the orange part?

PartFormExample
the condition (a real possibility)if + present simpleIf the rent goes up, ...
the result (future)will / won't + verb..., families will move out.
the order can changeresult + if (no comma)You will find a quiet city if you come in May.
questionsWhat will happen if...?What will you do if it rains?
different from Lesson 11zero conditional = always trueIf you heat ice, it melts. (always)
the trapNot if it will rain. Say if it rains, I will stay home. Will goes in the result, never after if.

In one line: if + present for the possibility, will + verb for the result.
