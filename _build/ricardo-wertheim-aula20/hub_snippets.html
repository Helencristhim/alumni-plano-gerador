If the Tourists Keep Coming

How to talk about a city full of visitors and what will happen next. Key words: cruise ship, overcrowded, souvenir shop, rent, to move out, to limit, to protect, peaceful. Structure: the first conditional -- if the rent goes up, families will move out.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Cruise ship -- a very big boat where people sleep, eat and travel for days on holiday
"Three cruise ships stopped near the old town."
Overcrowded -- with too many people in one place
"By ten o'clock the main street is overcrowded."
Souvenir shop -- a store that sells small things that tourists take home
"The street is full of souvenir shops now."
Rent -- the money you pay every month to live in a house or apartment that is not yours
"The rent in the old town is very high."
To move out -- to leave the house or apartment where you live and go to live in another place
"Many families moved out of the old town."
To limit -- to say that only a certain number is allowed, and no more
"The city limits the number of ships each day."
To protect -- to keep someone or something safe from danger or damage
"The city wants to protect its old town."
Peaceful -- quiet and calm, with no noise or stress
"In October the old town is peaceful."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Elena runs a small guesthouse in Dubrovnik, and every summer she makes the same plans. If the cruise ships come early, the old town will be overcrowded by ten. So she gives her guests one piece of advice: If you go out at seven, you will have the streets for yourself.

Look at the two parts. After if, Elena uses the present simple: if the ships come, if you go out. In the other part, the result, she uses will + verb: will be, will have. For a negative result, use won't: If you come in October, you won't wait in line.

Be careful: never put will after if. Not if it will rain but if it rains, we will visit the museum. And this is different from Lesson 11: if you heat ice, it melts is always true. If the rent goes up, families will move out is a real possibility in the future.

1. Which verb form comes after if in Elena's sentences?
A The future with will, because the sentence is about tomorrow and next summer, not about today.
B The present simple: if the ships come.
C The past simple.
2. Where does will go?
A In both parts of the sentence, in the if part and in the result part, so it is clear it is the future.
B Only after if.
C In the result part.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A If it rains tomorrow, we will visit the museum.
B If it will rain tomorrow, we will visit the museum in the old town with Elena.
C If it rains tomorrow, we visit the museum.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The First Conditional

GRAMMAR

A real possibility and its result in the future.

PartFormExample
the possibilityif + present simpleIf the rent goes up, ...
the resultwill / won't + verb..., families will move out.
result firstno commaYou won't wait in line if you come in May.
questionsWhat will ... if ...?What will happen if the families leave?
Lesson 11zero conditional = always trueIf you heat ice, it melts.

Never: if it will rain · if the rent go up · if you come in May, you find a quiet city next year
