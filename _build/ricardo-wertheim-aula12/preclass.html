Lesson 12 -- Pre-class

The Traveler I Used to Be

How you used to travel, and how you travel now. Key words: preference, pace, spontaneous, structured, solo traveler, guided tour, blend in, immerse yourself. Structure: used to for past habits -- I used to travel for work, but now I travel for pleasure.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Preference -- the thing you like more, when you can choose
"My preference now is a small room in a normal neighborhood."
Pace -- how fast or how slowly you do something
"I travel at a slow pace now: fewer cities, more days."
Spontaneous -- done because you suddenly want to, with no plan
"That afternoon was completely spontaneous."
Structured -- organized with a clear plan, often hour by hour
"My trips used to be very structured, with every hour on paper."
Solo traveler -- a person who travels alone
"As a solo traveler, you talk to many more people."
Guided tour -- a visit with a guide who explains a place to a group
"I used to take a guided tour in every city."
To blend in -- to look and act like the local people, so nobody notices you
"I buy bread where the neighbors buy it. I try to blend in."
To immerse yourself -- to go deep into a place: its language, its food, its daily life
"Now I would rather immerse myself in one neighborhood."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

For thirty years, Paulo traveled for work. He used to fly to Milan every month, and he used to stay in the same hotel near the office. He didn't use to have free time, so in the evenings he took a guided tour or went to bed early. His trips were very structured.

In 2018 he retired, and he took his first trip as a solo traveler. Today his preference is different: one city, one neighborhood, a slow pace. When his friends ask him about the old trips, they say: "Did you really use to see six cities in one week?" And Paulo answers: "Yes. But now I would rather see one city well."

1. The text says "He used to fly to Milan every month". What does this tell you?
A He flew to Milan once, in a month he can still remember very clearly.
B He flew there many times, for years, and he does not do it now.
C He wants to fly to Milan every month in the future.
2. Why does the text say "In 2018 he retired" and not "he used to retire"?
A Because "retire" is an irregular verb and cannot go after used to in any sentence.
B Because the writer forgot the rule for this verb.
C Because it happened once, at one moment, with a date.
3. In "Did you really use to see six cities?", why is there no "d" on use?
A Because "did" already carries the past, so use stays in its plain form.
B Because questions in English are always written in the present tense, even about the past.
C Because the friends are speaking informally.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Used to

GRAMMAR

One structure for the person you were before, and one for what you prefer now.

FormRuleExample
used to + verba habit or a state in the past that is not true nowI used to stay in business hotels.
didn't use to + verbdid carries the past, so there is no d on useI didn't use to travel alone.
Did + person + use to...?the question works the same wayDid you use to take guided tours?
Used to or past simple?Many times, for years: used to. One time, often with a date: past simple — I flew to Lisbon in 2018.
I would rather + verbWhat you prefer NOW, with no "to": I would rather walk, never I would rather to walk.
TodayThere is no present form. Say I usually travel slowly, never I use to travel slowly.

This is how you tell somebody about the traveler you were: "I used to..., but now I..." Two halves, two times, one change.

Quiz

You are talking about how you travel, then and now. Choose the best thing to say.

A new friend asks about your work trips in the nineties. They happened every month for years. You say:
A "I was used to fly to Milan every month, in those years."
B "I used to fly to Milan every month."
C "I use to fly to Milan every month."
You want to ask another traveler about his habits in the past. You say:
A "Did you used to take a lot of guided tours when you were younger?"
B "Did you use to take guided tours?"
C "You used to taking guided tours?"
You prefer a free afternoon to a guided tour. You say:
A "I would rather to have a free afternoon, if that is possible."
B "I would rather have a free afternoon."
C "I would prefer have a free afternoon."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

How did you use to travel, and how do you travel today? Take about ninety seconds. Talk about one habit you used to have, one thing you did not use to do, and one moment when your style changed. End with what you would rather do on your next trip. Use "used to" at least three times and four words from this lesson. Do not stop to correct yourself.
