Chapter 4: The Code

The Person You Used to Be

Grammar Discovery

Many Times, or Once?

"I used to take a guided tour in every city."

"I didn't use to remember much from those trips."

"Did you use to travel alone?"

"In 2018 I took my first trip as a solo traveler."

Three sentences in blue talk about something that happened many times, for years, and is not true now. The one in green happened once. And look at the negative and the question: where did the d go?

FormRuleExample
Affirmative
used to + verb		a habit or a state in the past that is not true nowI used to stay in business hotels.
Negative
didn't use to + verb		the past moves to did, so there is no d on useI didn't use to travel alone.
Question
Did + person + use to + verb?		same rule: did carries the pastDid you use to take guided tours?
used to vs past simpleused to = many times, for years. Past simple = one time, often with a date.I used to fly every month. / I flew to Lisbon in 2018.
used to vs I would ratherused to = before, and not now. I would rather + verb = what you prefer today. Never I would rather to see.
Today?There is no present form. For today, use the present simple: I usually travel slowly — never I use to travel slowly.

One word does the whole job: used to tells the listener that this person does not exist any more.

Contrast

Many Times or Once?

Many times, or once? Say it first, then click to compare

1. "I flew to Milan every month for ten years."
Many times. "I used to fly to Milan every month."
2. "I flew to Lisbon in May 2018."
Once, with a date. Keep the past simple: "I flew to Lisbon in 2018."
3. "I stayed in business hotels near the office."
Many times, for years. "I used to stay in business hotels."
4. "I got lost in Alfama on the second day."
Once. Keep the past simple: "I got lost." Used to does not work here.
5. "I had a very big suitcase in those days."
A state, for many years. "I used to have a very big suitcase."
6. "A woman at a bakery explained the street to me."
Once. Keep the past simple: "She explained the street to me."
Chapter 5: Voices from the Road

Two People Who Watched It Change

A guide in Ljubljana, and a father whose daughter said one sentence

Listening

Follow the Umbrella

A guide in Ljubljana on how her travelers have changed. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What did a typical group day use to look like?
It was very structured: the castle at nine, the bridges at eleven, lunch at one. People followed her umbrella and took a photograph of everything.
2. What kind of traveler does she see more of today?
Many more solo travelers, and a lot of them are over sixty. They want two places, at their own pace.
3. When does she give the guided tour now?
On the first morning, not for the whole trip. After that, the travelers try to blend in.
Listening 2

Dad, You Used to Be Fun

A father who changed his travel style twice. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. How did he use to travel when he was young?
He didn't use to plan anything. He arrived in a city with no hotel and no map.
2. What did his daughter say, and what did he do after that?
She said: "Dad, you used to be fun." On his next trip to Slovenia he left two days completely empty.
3. What is his preference now?
Somewhere in the middle: a plan for the mornings, and nothing for the afternoons.
