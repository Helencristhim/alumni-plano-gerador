Lesson 27 -- Pre-class

The Subcontractor Who Did Not Show Up -- Firm, Not Angry

A waterproofing crew was supposed to start on Monday, nobody came, and the pour is on Thursday. A firm complaint says the fact, the impact, what you need by when, and the consequence. Key words: a no-show, a breach of contract, a penalty clause, a grace period, a recovery plan, to withhold payment, unacceptable, to back out of something, to put something in writing, to make up for lost time, to let something slide, the last straw. Structure: was supposed to and is supposed to for expectations, unless and otherwise for consequences.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A no-show -- a person or a crew who does not come when they are expected
"Monday was a no-show: nobody from the waterproofing crew came."
A breach of contract -- the failure to do what a contract says you must do
"Two weeks without a crew on site is a breach of contract."
A penalty clause -- the part of a contract that says how much you pay if you are late or fail
"The penalty clause is two thousand dollars for every day of delay."
A grace period -- extra time you give somebody before a consequence starts
"We gave them a one-day grace period, and not one hour more."
A recovery plan -- a written plan that shows how a late job will get back on schedule
"Send me a recovery plan with names, days and hours."
To withhold payment -- to decide not to pay somebody, for now, until a problem is solved
"If the work is not done, we will withhold payment for this month."
Unacceptable -- so bad that you cannot accept it
"A crew that does not come and does not call is unacceptable."
To back out of something -- to leave an agreement or a promise that you made before
"The steel supplier backed out of the deal two days before delivery."
To put something in writing -- to write down an agreement or a complaint, usually in an email
"Good call. Now put it in writing, so we both have the same version."
To make up for lost time -- to work faster or longer because you started late
"The crew will work on Saturday to make up for lost time."
To let something slide -- to accept a problem and not react to it
"I let the first delay slide. I cannot do that again."
The last straw -- the last of several problems, the one that makes you finally act
"The third missed delivery was the last straw."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

When a subcontractor lets you down, the first sentence of the call is the fact, and the best tool for it is was supposed to: Your crew was supposed to be here at seven on Monday. It says what was agreed and that it did not happen, with no insult. Use the present for what the plan says: The membrane is supposed to be finished before the pour. Brazilian speakers often forget the be ("your crew supposed to") or the final d ("suppose to").

Then comes the consequence. Unless means if ... not, so it never takes another not, and it never takes will: Unless your crew is here by seven tomorrow, we will bring in another company. Otherwise starts a new sentence: Send me a recovery plan today. Otherwise, I will have to apply the penalty clause.

The grammar does the firm part, so your voice does not have to. A good complaint is calm, specific and, at the end of the day, in writing.

1. According to the text, what does "was supposed to" say?
A What was agreed, and that it did not happen.
B What the subcontractor will probably do later this week, if he has the people.
C That the speaker is angry.
2. Why is "unless you don't send the plan" wrong?
A Because "unless" always needs "will", as in "unless you will send the plan tomorrow".
B Because "unless" already means "if ... not".
C Because it is too polite.
3. What does the text say about the voice in a firm complaint?
A It should be loud.
B It should change between loud and soft, so the subcontractor understands how serious the delay is.
C It can stay calm, because the grammar does the firm part.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Supposed To, Unless and Otherwise

GRAMMAR

Say what was supposed to happen. Then say what happens unless it does.

ToolMeaningExample
was / were supposed to + verbagreed, and it did not happenYour crew was supposed to be here at seven.
is / are supposed to + verbwhat the plan or contract saysThe membrane is supposed to be finished first.
unless + presentif ... not -- no will, no notUnless your crew is here by seven, ...
Otherwise, ...if not -- starts a new sentenceSend the plan today. Otherwise, ...
I will have to + verba firm consequence, not a threatI will have to withhold payment.

Never: your crew supposed to come · it was suppose to · unless you don't send · unless your crew will be here
