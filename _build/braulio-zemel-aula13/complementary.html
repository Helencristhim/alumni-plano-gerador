"How to speak so that people want to listen" -- Julian Treasure, TED

Ten minutes from the sound expert whose listening talk you already know, this time on the other side of the conversation. Connection to Lesson 13: it is about how you are heard at a table, which is the half of the evening that vocabulary does not cover. The page carries the full English transcript beside the video.

Tip: watch once with English subtitles and once without. Then record thirty seconds of yourself asking your door question, and listen back for pace rather than for mistakes.

Podcast
"4 habits of highly effective communicators" -- Life Kit, NPR

Twenty minutes of American English on what actually happens between two people in a conversation, and why understanding the other person is the move that changes everything. Connection to Lesson 13: the habits are the same ones you practiced today -- drawing somebody out, steering, and knowing when to stop talking. The full transcript sits under the player.

Tip: listen once without the text and write down the four habits. Then decide which of the four is the one you least do at a table, and prepare one sentence for it before the next dinner.

Grammar Video
"Question tags" -- 6 Minute Grammar, BBC Learning English

Six minutes on the structure you built today, with far more examples than one class allows. Connection to Lesson 13: it drills the only difficult part -- choosing the right auxiliary for the tag -- until it stops being a decision.

Tip: pause after each example and say the tag out loud BEFORE the presenters do. Speed is the whole point here: at a table the tag either arrives in half a second or it does not arrive.
