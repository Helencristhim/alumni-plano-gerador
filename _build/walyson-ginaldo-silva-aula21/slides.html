Chapter 3: The Little Question

It’s a Great Event, Isn’t It?

Question tags: the small question that invites the other person to talk

Grammar Discovery

Look at the End

"The coffee here is excellent, isn’t it?"

"You came from Seattle, didn’t you?"

"You haven’t been to Santos before, have you?"

"We can talk later, can’t we?"

Look at the blue words. When the sentence is positive, what is the tag? When it is negative? And which verb comes back in the tag?

SentenceTagExample
Positive sentencenegative tagIt is busy, isn’t it?
Negative sentencepositive tagYou aren’t from here, are you?
Present simpledo / doesYou work with coffee, don’t you?
Past simpledidShe flew in today, didn’t she?
Present perfect / modalsthe same auxiliaryYou’ve met Clara, haven’t you? · We can meet, can’t we?
I amaren’t II’m a little early, aren’t I?

In one line: positive sentence, negative tag; negative sentence, positive tag; the tag repeats the auxiliary. Falling voice = "agree with me"; rising voice = a real question.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Coffee Break

One conversation, two recordings, one event program

Dialogue

Megan at the Coffee Table

M
The coffee here is excellent, isn't it?
W
It really is. It's from Minas Gerais, I think. I'm Walyson, from Sucafina. And you're Megan, aren't you? I saw your name tag.
M
That's right, Megan Price. I'm a coffee buyer for a small roaster in Seattle. This is my first time in Brazil.
W
Welcome! You haven't been to Santos before, have you? What do you think of it so far?
M
I love it. I walked along the beach this morning. And you? Do you live here?
W
Yes, I do. So, what brought you to Coffee Week? Are you looking for new origins?
M
Exactly. We want a natural coffee from Brazil. Actually, I think I know your colleague Clara. We met in Miami last year.
W
You know Clara? It's a small world! She was here last week.
M
Ha! Well, the next talk starts at four, doesn't it?
W
It does. Let me excuse myself for a minute, I promised to call my director. Here's my business card. Let's continue this after the talk, shall we?
Comprehension

About Megan

1. Where does Megan work, and what is her job?
She is a coffee buyer for a small roaster in Seattle.
2. What did Megan do this morning?
She walked along the beach in Santos.
3. What kind of coffee is she looking for, and who does she know?
A natural coffee from Brazil. She knows Clara, Walyson’s colleague -- they met in Miami last year.
Listening 1 · A Coach

The Secret of Good Small Talk

A communication coach on how to keep a conversation alive. Sound first — no text.

1. What does she say is the most common mistake at events?
Asking one closed question after another, like an interview, without commenting on the answers.
2. What does a follow-up question show?
That you are really listening to the other person.
3. How does she say you should leave a conversation?
Excuse yourself with a reason, and suggest a next step, like exchanging business cards.
Listening 2 · A Colleague

A Small World at the Airport

A trader remembers a chance meeting that became a contract. Sound first — no text.

1. Where did he run into the client, and why was he there?
At the airport in Rotterdam. His flight was delayed.
2. How did he start the conversation?
With a comment and a tag: "Long delay, isn’t it?"
3. What happened two months later?
They signed a contract for a container of coffee.
