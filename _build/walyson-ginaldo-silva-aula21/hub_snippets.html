Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Conversation starter -- a question or comment you use to begin talking with someone
"The coffee is a good conversation starter at any event."
Follow-up question -- a question that asks for more details about what someone just said
"A good follow-up question shows you are really listening."
Mingle -- to move around a social event and talk to many different people
"After the talks, we all mingled in the hall."
Chit-chat -- light, friendly talk about things that are not important
"We had some chit-chat about the weather before the meeting."
Awkward -- uncomfortable, when people do not know what to say or do
"There was an awkward silence, so I asked about her trip."
Run into -- to meet someone you know by chance, without planning it
"I ran into an old client at the airport last week."
Small world -- what you say when you find a surprising connection with someone
"You know my director too? It is a small world!"
Change the subject -- to start talking about a different topic
"When the topic got too personal, I changed the subject."
Excuse yourself -- to leave a conversation or a place politely
"I excused myself to get another coffee."
Business card -- a small card with your name, job and contact details
"We exchanged business cards at the end of the chat."
Coffee break -- a short pause in a meeting or event to drink and relax
"I met her during the coffee break."
Name tag -- a small label with your name that you wear at an event
"I read her name tag before I said hello."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At a business event, the hardest part is not the first question. It is the second one. Good speakers keep the conversation going with a comment and a question tag: "The coffee here is excellent, isn’t it?" The tag is a small question at the end of a sentence. It invites the other person to agree and to say more.

The rule is simple. A positive sentence takes a negative tag: "You work with coffee, don’t you?" A negative sentence takes a positive tag: "You haven’t been to Santos before, have you?" The tag repeats the auxiliary of the sentence: is, do, did, have, can. With I am, the tag is aren’t I.

Your voice matters too. When your voice goes down on the tag, you are sure and you just want a chat. When it goes up, it is a real question. In Portuguese we often say "né?" for everything. In English, the right tag sounds more natural and more polite.

1. Which sentence is correct?
A The coffee here is excellent, is it? I tried it this morning at the terrace.
B The coffee here is excellent, no?
C The coffee here is excellent, isn’t it?
2. Why do we say "You came from Seattle, didn’t you?"
A Because came is past simple, so the tag uses did.
B Because Seattle is a city in another country, far from Brazil and its coffee farms.
C Because every question tag in English always uses the verb do.
3. What does a falling voice on the tag mean?
A You do not know the answer and you need the information urgently for your report.
B You are sure, and you are inviting the other person to agree and talk.
C You are angry with the other person.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Question Tags

GRAMMAR

Positive sentence, negative tag. Negative sentence, positive tag. The tag repeats the auxiliary.

SentenceTagExample
PositivenegativeIt is busy, isn’t it?
NegativepositiveYou aren’t from here, are you?
Present simpledon’t / doesn’tShe works in Seattle, doesn’t she?
Past simpledidn’t / didYou met Clara, didn’t you?
have / can / shouldthe same verbWe can talk later, can’t we?
I amaren’t II’m early, aren’t I?

Remember: falling voice = "agree with me, let’s talk"; rising voice = "I am not sure, please tell me".

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

You meet someone at the coffee break. Put the sentences in a natural order: start, introduce yourself, ask, follow up, excuse yourself and close.

?"Here’s my business card. Let’s talk again at the reception."
?"Excuse me, the coffee here is excellent, isn’t it?"
?"Would you excuse me? I need to call my director."
?"I’m Walyson, from Sucafina. Nice to meet you."
?"Really? And how long are you staying in Santos?"
?"So, what brought you to Coffee Week?"

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

A buyer at the coffee table says: "I just arrived from Colombia." You say:
A Yes.
B Colombia is a country in South America that produces a lot of coffee every year.
C Oh, you must be tired! How was the flight?
You want to start a chat about the event. You say:
A This event is good. Do you like it? Do you work here? Is it your first time?
B Great event, isn’t it?
C Is the event?
You need to leave a conversation. You say:
A Would you excuse me? I need to make a call.
B Bye. I am going now because I need to make a very important call to my director in the office.
C I go now.
A new contact asks about your salary. You say:
A I will not answer that question because it is personal and nobody asks that at an event.
B Ha, that’s a secret! Anyway, have you tried the cold brew?
C No.
