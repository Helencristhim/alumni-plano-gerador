Lesson 3 -- The Question Behind the Question

Grammar Video
"Modals in the past" -- BBC Learning English, English Class

Must have, might have, can't have: the three forms this lesson uses to talk about a room you have already left. Short, visual, and built entirely on examples where the speaker is working out what happened rather than reporting it.

Tip: after each example, build one of your own about the interview you lost. What the recruiter must have wanted, what he might have been testing. Say them out loud — the point is the sound, and all three contract to must've, might've, should've.

Talk with transcript
"5 ways to listen better" -- Julian Treasure, TED (8 min)

Eight minutes on listening as a trained skill rather than a talent you either have or do not. Connection to Lesson 3: his point is that we listen through filters and hear what we expect, which is exactly what happens in the four seconds after a long question in a language that is not your first. The full transcript sits beside the video.

Tip: watch once with the transcript hidden, then write the five techniques from memory before you open it. Whatever you missed is the measurement — not the talk.

Interview under pressure
"Maria Ressa, CEO of Rappler" -- BBC HARDtalk (24 min)

HARDtalk exists to ask long, layered, uncomfortable questions, which makes it the best available specimen of the thing this lesson is about. Every question runs forty to sixty words and the actual instruction is in the last eight. Watch a chief executive absorb them one after another without losing the thread.

Tip: pause after each question, before she answers, and say out loud what the instruction was. Then let her answer and check whether she answered that or something else. Ten questions is enough for one session.
