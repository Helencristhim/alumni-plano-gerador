Lesson 34 -- Pre-class

Saying Yes, with Conditions

Say yes to requests and still protect your time: answer with a condition using as long as, provided (that) and unless. Key words: take on, spread too thin, push back, make an exception, cover for, give a heads-up, go out of my way, meet halfway.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Take on -- To agree to do a piece of work or a responsibility
"I can take on the report, but not this week."
Spread too thin -- Trying to do so many things that you can't do any of them well
"I'm spread too thin right now. I can't help with that."
Push back -- To say no to a request, or ask to change it, in a polite way
"If the deadline is impossible, it's OK to push back."
Make an exception -- To agree to something that is not your usual rule
"I don't work on Saturdays, but I'll make an exception this time."
Cover for -- To do someone else's work while they are absent
"Could you cover for me on Friday? I have a doctor's appointment."
Give a heads-up -- To tell someone early that something is going to happen
"Thanks for giving me a heads-up about the meeting."
Go out of my way -- To make a special effort to do something for someone
"She went out of her way to help me find an apartment."
Meet halfway -- To give a little, so both people get part of what they want
"I can't do the whole project, but I can meet you halfway."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

On Monday, three people asked me for favors before ten o'clock. First, my manager asked me to take on a new client. I was already spread too thin, so I said, "Yes, provided that Paulo does the monthly report." She agreed. Then Ana asked me to cover for her on Friday. I said, "Sure, as long as you leave me your notes." Finally, a colleague wanted my help with a presentation that afternoon. I had to push back: "I can't today, unless it's really urgent. How about tomorrow?" He met me halfway, and we did it the next morning. Next time, I hope people give me a heads-up earlier. I like to help, and sometimes I go out of my way for people, but I only make an exception for real emergencies.

1. What condition did the writer give her manager?
A That Paulo does the monthly report.
B That she could work from home every Friday for the rest of the month.
C That she gets a higher salary.
2. What did Ana have to do?
A Take on the new client herself and call him before the end of the week.
B Leave her notes.
C Cover for the writer.
3. Why did the writer push back on the presentation?
A She thought the presentation was boring and badly written.
B She did not like the colleague.
C She couldn't do it that day, unless it was urgent.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Yes, with a condition

GRAMMAR

To say yes with a condition, use as long as, provided (that) or unless + the PRESENT, even for the future.

StructureUseExample
as long as + presentYes, only with this conditionI'll help as long as it's quick.
provided (that) + presentThe same, more formalYes, provided that we finish by five.
unless + presentYes, except in this situationI'll be there unless it rains.
condition + will / canThe resultAs long as you send it, I can check it.

Use the present after these words: 'as long as you send it', never 'as long as you will send it'. 'Unless' means 'if not': say 'unless it rains', never 'unless it doesn't rain'.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

A colleague asks for help. You can help only if it is quick. Best choice:
A "Sure, as long as it's quick."
B "Sure, as long as it will be quick and easy for the two of us."
C "Sure, unless it's quick."
Your manager asks you to take on more work. You want to be formal. Best choice:
A "Yes, provide that I have more time."
B "Yes, provided that the deadline can move."
C "Yes, provided that I will have more time to do it properly next week."
You will go to the party, except if you have to work. Best choice:
A "I'll go, unless I don't have to work."
B "I'll go, as long as I have to work late on Friday night."
C "I'll go, unless I have to work."
A friend tells you early about a change of plans. Best choice:
A "Thanks for the heads-up."
B "Thanks for the head up about the change in our plans."
C "Thanks for the up-head."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.
