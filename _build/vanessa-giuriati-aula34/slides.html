Chapter 3

Yes, If...

Three words that protect your time

Grammar Discovery

The Smart Yes

"Can you cover for me on Friday?" — "Sure, as long as you give me a heads-up."

"Could you take on the new client?" — "Yes, provided that I can drop the monthly report."

"Can I leave early today?" — "No problem, unless the client calls."

"Can I borrow your car?" — "As long as you bring it back by six, yes."

FormUseExample
as long as + presentYes, but only with this conditionI'll help as long as it's quick.
provided (that) + presentThe same, more formalYes, provided that we finish by five.
unless + presentYes, except in this situation (= if not)I'll be there unless it rains.
condition + will / canThe resultAs long as you send it, I can check it.
⚠ CarefulAfter 'as long as', 'provided' and 'unless', use the PRESENT for the future: 'as long as you send it', never 'as long as you will send it'. 'Unless' already means 'if not': say 'unless it rains', never 'unless it doesn't rain'.
