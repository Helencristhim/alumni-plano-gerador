Lesson 22 -- Pre-class

Money, Time and the Family -- Although, Even Though and Despite

The fees are the number everybody discusses, but the real cost of the program is time, family and the company. The panel wants to hear that you know the cost and that you have a plan. Key words: opportunity cost, a sacrifice, to take a toll, to justify something, a commitment, upfront, worthwhile, to cut back on something, a support system, to be stretched thin, to be on board, a leap of faith. Structure: concession -- although and even though with a sentence, despite and in spite of with a noun or -ing, and however and even so to start a new sentence.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Opportunity cost -- the value of what you give up when you choose one thing instead of another
"The fees are visible. The opportunity cost is the time."
A sacrifice -- something valuable that you give up for something more important
"Two years of weekends is a real sacrifice for a family."
To take a toll -- to have a bad effect, slowly, over a long time
"The first term took a toll on everybody at home."
To justify something -- to show that a decision or a cost is reasonable
"I can justify the fees. The time is harder to justify."
A commitment -- something that takes your time because you have promised to do it
"Twenty months is a long commitment for a man who runs a company."
Upfront -- paid or given at the beginning, before anything else happens
"Part of the fees is paid upfront, at enrollment."
Worthwhile -- worth the time, money or effort that it costs
"It was hard, but it was worthwhile."
To cut back on something -- to do or use less of something
"We agreed to cut back on weekends away for two years."
A support system -- the people who help you and make a difficult period possible
"My family and my deputies were my support system."
To be stretched thin -- to have so many demands on your time that you cannot do all of them well
"In the first term I was stretched thin between the sites and the classes."
To be on board -- to agree with a plan and support it
"Before I applied, I wanted my family to be on board."
A leap of faith -- a decision to do something important when you cannot be sure it will work
"Every big decision is a leap of faith, even with a plan."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

When the panel asks what the program will cost you, the strong answer does two things at once: it admits the cost, and it keeps the decision. English has a family of linkers for exactly this job. Although and even though are followed by a full sentence: although the fees are high, the real cost is time. Even though is stronger; it shows more surprise: even though I will keep working, the hours will come from my weekends.

Despite and in spite of are different: they are followed by a noun or an -ing form, never by a subject and a verb. Despite the flexible schedule, the first term was hard. In spite of having a support system, I was stretched thin. If you need a full sentence after despite, use despite the fact that. To start a new sentence, use however or even so, with a comma: The residencies are short. Even so, somebody has to run the company.

The most common mistake for Brazilian speakers comes from translating word for word: in Portuguese, the word for although can live in the same sentence as the word for but. In English it cannot: "Although the fees are high, but..." is wrong. Use one linker, never two. And do not forget the of in in spite of.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Concession

GRAMMAR

Although and even though take a sentence. Despite takes a noun or -ing. One linker only, never although with but.

ToolWhat followsExample
although / even though+ full clauseAlthough the fees are high, the real cost is time.
despite / in spite of+ noun or -ingDespite the flexible schedule, the first term was hard.
despite the fact that+ full clause (formal)Despite the fact that the program is flexible, ...
however / even sonew sentence + commaThe residencies are short. Even so, ...
one linker onlynever although + butAlthough it is expensive, but ... — wrong

Never: although the fees are high, but · despite the schedule is flexible · in spite the cost · even the program is flexible (without though)

Quiz

A "Only the fees. The schedule is flexible, so nothing will change."
B "Although the fees are high, the real cost is time, and my family and I have a plan for it."
C "It is very expensive, but my company will pay."
