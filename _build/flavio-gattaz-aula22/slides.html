Chapter 4: The Code

Although, Even Though, Despite

Saying yes and but in the same sentence

Grammar Discovery

A Sentence, or a Noun?

"Although the fees are high, the real cost is time."

"Even though I will keep working, the hours will come from my weekends."

"Despite the flexible schedule, the first term was hard."

"Despite having a support system, I was stretched thin."

Look at what comes after each word in color. After although and even though, is there a subject and a verb? And after despite? Which one sounds more surprised: although or even though?

ToolWhat followsExample
although / thougha full clause (subject + verb)Although the fees are high, ...
even thougha full clause; stronger than althoughEven though I will keep working, ...
despite / in spite ofa noun or -ing — never subject + verbDespite the flexible schedule / In spite of having help, ...
despite the fact thata full clause (longer, more formal)Despite the fact that the program is flexible, ...
however / even soa new sentence, with a comma after itThe residencies are short. Even so, ...
one linker onlynever although and but togetherAlthough it is expensive, but... — wrong

In one line: although and even though take a sentence; despite and in spite of take a noun or -ing; and never although with but.

Chapter 5: Real Voices

The Real Cost, Two Voices

A fact sheet, an interviewer and an alumnus

Real Document

The Program at a Glance

EXECUTIVE MBA — Time Commitment and Fees
Fact sheet sent to every applicant before the interview

Length: 20 months, starting in January.

On campus: six residencies of one week each.

Online: one live session every Thursday evening.

Study time: an estimated 15 to 20 hours per week.

Fees: payable in four installments; the first is paid upfront, at enrollment.

Although the program is designed for full-time executives, we strongly recommend that you discuss the time commitment with your family and your employer before you apply.
1. How much study time does the program expect every week?
An estimated fifteen to twenty hours per week.
2. How are the fees paid?
In four installments; the first one is paid upfront, at enrollment.
3. What does the school recommend before you apply, and which linker does the note use?
To discuss the time commitment with your family and your employer. The note uses “although”.
Listening 1 · An Interviewer

Who at Home Knows?

An admissions interviewer on a question that surprises many candidates. Sound first — no text.

1. What question surprises many candidates?
Who at home knows that they are applying, and what those people think.
2. Why does she ask it?
Because the hours come from evenings and weekends that belonged to a family, and candidates who have not had that conversation tend to leave in the first term.
3. What kind of answer worries her?
The candidate who says that nothing will change.
Listening 2 · An Alumnus

The Conversation That Saved It

An alumnus on the part of the program that was hardest. Sound first — no text.

1. What was the hard part, according to him?
Not the fees. The first term took a toll on everybody at home — he missed his daughter’s school play because of a finance exam.
2. What saved the program for him?
A conversation with his wife: they agreed on three things he would never miss, whatever the calendar said.
3. What would he do differently?
He would have that conversation before he applied, not in the middle of the first term.
