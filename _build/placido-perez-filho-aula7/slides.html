Chapter 3: The Code

We Had It Done

When someone else does the work

Grammar Discovery

Who Did the Work?

"We had the foundation checked by an engineer."

"We’re having the windows installed this week."

"I need to get the wiring done before the rain."

"Next month we’ll have the floors polished."

Did the speaker check the foundation, install the windows or polish the floors? No: somebody else did, and the speaker organized and paid for it. Look at the order: have or get, then the thing (red), then a past participle.

JobFormExample
Someone does it for youhave + object + past participleWe had the foundation checked.
More informalget + object + past participleI need to get the wiring done.
Happening nowbe having + object + past participleWe’re having the windows installed.
Future planwill have / going to have + object + p.p.We’ll have the floors polished.
Say who did itadd by + person...checked by an engineer.
The trapthe object goes before the participleWe had checked the foundation = we did it ourselves, earlier. We had the foundation checked = somebody did it for us.

In one line: have / get + the thing + past participle = you organize it, someone else does it.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

The Site, at Real Speed

One visit, three recordings, one site report

Dialogue

A Visit to the Site

R
Thank you for showing me the house on a Saturday, Plácido. It’s bigger than I expected.
P
My pleasure, Rachel. Here is the floor plan. Three bedrooms upstairs, and the living room opens to the garden.
R
It looks great. Was the work done by your own team?
P
No, we hired a contractor. But I come every Saturday, and we had the foundation checked by an independent engineer.
R
That’s reassuring. What about the electrical part?
P
The wiring is done. This week we’re having the windows installed, and next month we’ll have the floors polished.
R
So when could I move in? Is the work on schedule, or did it fall behind?
P
Two weeks behind, because of the rain. We should hand over the keys in December, and you could still choose some of the finishes.
Comprehension

About Rachel

1. What does Rachel say about the size of the house?
It is bigger than she expected.
2. What does Rachel want to know about the work?
Whether it was done by his own team, and whether it is on schedule.
3. What is Rachel’s question about moving in?
When she could move in, and if the work fell behind.
Listening 1 · The Contractor

Why the Work Fell Behind

An Australian contractor explains to a client why the project is three weeks late. Sound first — no text.

1. How many weeks behind is the project?
Three weeks.
2. What two reasons does he give?
Two weeks of heavy rain, and the windows arrived late from the factory.
3. What does he promise about quality?
He will not cut corners to catch up.
Listening 2 · The Estate Agent

How to Sell a New House

A British estate agent gives advice to a builder who wants to sell a new house. Sound first — no text.

1. When does she recommend starting to show the house?
Before it is finished, when the walls are up.
2. What should the builder have done before the first visit?
Have the site cleaned and have a simple floor plan printed.
3. What does she say about the asking price?
Do not set it too high at the start; it is hard to come down later.
Listening 3 · The Architect

The Last Ten Percent

An Italian architect talks about why the finishes matter so much. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he call the last ten percent of the work?
The finishes.
2. Why do buyers notice the finishes most?
Because they can see and touch them; nobody sees the foundation.
3. What did he have done in his own house?
He had the old floors restored instead of replaced.

A site report is a list of things other people do for you. Every line becomes a causative: we had the foundation checked, we’re having the windows installed.
1. Turn the plumbing line into a causative.
We’re having the plumbing checked this week.
2. Why is the project behind schedule?
Because of the rain.
3. Turn the last line into a future causative.
Next, we’re going to have the floors polished.
