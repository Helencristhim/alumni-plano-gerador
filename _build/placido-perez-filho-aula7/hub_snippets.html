The House on Saturday -- We Had It Done

A visit to a house under construction. Key words: a contractor, a building permit, a floor plan, the foundation, plumbing, wiring, to fall behind, the finishes, to put up, to cut corners, the asking price, to hand over. Structure: have / get something done.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A contractor -- a person or a company that you pay to do construction work
"Our contractor works with a team of twelve people."
A building permit -- an official document that allows you to build
"We waited three months for the building permit."
A floor plan -- a drawing that shows the rooms of a house from above
"The buyer asked to see the floor plan."
The foundation -- the strong base of a building, under the ground
"The foundation was finished in March."
Plumbing -- the pipes for water in a building
"We had the plumbing checked twice."
Wiring -- the electric cables in a building
"The wiring was installed last month."
To fall behind -- to be late, not to do things as fast as planned
"The work fell behind because of the rain."
The finishes -- the final materials you see: floors, tiles, paint
"The buyer can choose some of the finishes."
To put up -- to build something vertical, like a wall
"They put up the walls in two weeks."
To cut corners -- to do something badly or cheaply to save time or money
"A good contractor never cuts corners on the foundation."
The asking price -- the price that the seller wants for a property
"The asking price includes the finishes."
To hand over -- to give something, like the keys, to the new owner
"We will hand over the keys in December."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Rachel visits a house under construction. The owner shows her the floor plan. “We hired a contractor,” he says, “and we had the foundation checked by an engineer. The wiring is done.”

“This week we are having the windows installed, and next month we will have the floors polished. The plumbing is being tested.” The work fell behind a little because of the rain, but nobody cut corners.

The asking price includes the finishes, and they will hand over the keys in December.

1. “We had the foundation checked by an engineer.” Who checked it?
A An engineer.
B The owner.
C Rachel.
2. Why did the work fall behind?
A Because the contractor cut corners.
B Because there was no building permit.
C Because of the rain.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “We are having installed the windows.”
B “We are having the windows installed.”
C “We are having the windows install.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Causative -- Have Something Done

GRAMMAR

Use have (or, more informally, get) + object + past participle when you organize the work and somebody else does it. The order of the words changes the meaning.

JobFormExample
Someone does it for youhave + object + past participleWe had the foundation checked.
More informalget + object + past participleI need to get the wiring done.
Happening nowbe having + object + past participleWe’re having the windows installed.
Future planwill have / going to have + object + p.p.We’ll have the floors polished.
Say who did itadd by + person...checked by an engineer.
The trapthe object goes before the participleWe had checked the foundation = we did it ourselves, earlier. We had the foundation checked = somebody did it for us.

Never: We had checked the foundation (when someone else did it) · having install the windows · get the wiring do · the work felt behind
