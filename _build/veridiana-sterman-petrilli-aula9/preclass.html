Lesson 09 -- Pre-class

They Told Me Something Else -- Reporting What You Were Told

Three people, three answers, and the sentence that gets you one. Key words: to be under the impression, to assure, word for word, a misunderstanding, apparently, to follow up, to go back on, to clear something up, to put somebody through, straight away. Structure: reported speech with say and tell.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To be under the impression -- to believe something only because somebody told you so
"I was under the impression that the tour left at nine."
To assure -- to tell somebody firmly that something is true, so they stop worrying
"She assured me that my booking was confirmed."
Word for word -- repeating exactly what was said, with nothing changed
"So, word for word: the boat leaves at ten thirty and my booking stands."
A misunderstanding -- when two people leave a conversation believing different things
"I think there has been a misunderstanding about the time."
Apparently -- used when you repeat what you heard, and you do not know if it is true
"Apparently the trip was cancelled, but nobody has confirmed that to me."
To follow up -- to contact somebody again about something you spoke about before
"I called on Monday, and I am following up because nobody wrote back."
To go back on -- to break something you promised or agreed
"They have gone back on what the agent promised me on the phone."
To clear something up -- to end a confusion by explaining what is really true
"I hope you can clear something up for me."
To put somebody through -- to connect somebody to another person on the phone
"Could you put me through to the person who took the booking?"
Straight away -- immediately, with nothing in between
"Could you send me that in writing, straight away?"

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On a trip, the hardest conversations are almost never about a rule. They are about three people who told you three different things, and about which of the three you are supposed to believe. The good news is that the sentence which ends that conversation is not a complaint. It is a quotation, and it has a fixed shape.

Two verbs carry it. Say goes alone: "He said the tour was cancelled." Tell needs a person right after it: "She told me the boat left at nine." English never puts a person after said, which is exactly what Portuguese invites you to do, and he said me is probably the single most common mistake a Brazilian makes in this structure.

Then everything inside the sentence moves one step into the past. A present becomes a past: "It leaves at nine" becomes "she said it left at nine". Will becomes would and can becomes could: "We will call you" becomes "they said they would call me". A past becomes a past perfect: "We changed the time" becomes "she said they had changed the time". Time words move with it too, so today becomes that day and tomorrow becomes the next day.

This is why quoting works so well when you are the one with the problem. You are not saying that anybody was wrong. You are saying who spoke, when, and what came out of their mouth, and then you are asking for one answer. Add apparently for the version nobody has confirmed, close by repeating the final answer word for word, and ask for one line of it in writing. Nobody argues with a quotation. They check it.

1. Why does English never allow "he said me"?
A Because said is an irregular verb.
B Because say goes alone, and only tell takes a person after it.
C Because it is too direct, and English prefers a polite form.
2. What happens to "We will call you" when you report it?
A They said they would call me.
B They said they will call me.
C They told they would call me.
3. Why does the text say that quoting works better than complaining?
A Because it is more polite, and polite people are served first.
B Because it hides the fact that you are unhappy.
C Because you are not saying anybody was wrong, only who spoke and what they said, so the other person can check it.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Say, Tell, and One Step Back

GRAMMAR

Say goes alone. Tell needs a person. Everything inside moves one step into the past.

JobFormExample
Repeating the wordssaid + what — never a person after itHe said the tour was cancelled.
Naming who heard ittold + person + whatShe told me the boat left at nine.
Present moves backis / are → was / were · leaves → left“It leaves at nine.” → She said it left at nine.
Will and can move backwill → would · can → could“We will call you.” → They said they would call me.
Past moves back toochanged → had changedShe said they had changed the time.
Time words movetoday → that day · tomorrow → the next dayShe said the boat left the next day.
In speechthat is optional after said and told meShe told me (that) the boat left at nine.

Never: he said me · she told that the boat left · they said they will call · he said the trip is cancelled yesterday

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to open the conversation without sounding angry. You say:
A "You people have told me three different things."
B "I hope you can clear something up for me. I have been told three different things."
C "Nobody here knows anything about my booking."
The agent on the phone said "Your booking is confirmed." The next day you report it. You say:
A "She told me my booking was confirmed."
B "She said me my booking is confirmed."
C "She told that my booking is confirmed."
You heard it from the shuttle driver and nobody has confirmed it. You say:
A "The trip is cancelled, that is a fact."
B "Apparently the trip was cancelled, but nobody has confirmed that to me."
C "Maybe the trip is cancelled or maybe it is not, I do not know anything."
You are on the phone and you want to speak to the person who took your booking. You say:
A "Pass me the person of the booking."
B "Could you put me through to the person who took the booking?"
C "I want to talk with another one, not you."
