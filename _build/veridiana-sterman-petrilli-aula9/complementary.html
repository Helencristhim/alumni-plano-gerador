Complementary Activities

Optional extras to enjoy outside class. Mark each one as done after you watch or listen to it.

Series
Emily in Paris — Season 1, Episode 3 "Sexy or Sexist" (Netflix)

Half of this episode is one person repeating what another person said, and every repetition changes a little. Light, fast, and the best free practice for today’s structure that exists on television.

Tip: watch with English subtitles on. Every time somebody reports what a third person said, pause and write the sentence down.

Podcast
Life Kit (NPR) — "How to talk to customer service and actually get what you want"

A negotiation consultant on how to make the person at the desk your collaborator instead of your opponent. It is the same idea as today’s lesson, from the other side of the counter, and it answers the question of whether asking for the manager really works.

Tip: the full transcript is on the page. Find three sentences you could use in October, and say them out loud.

Listen on NPR ↗
Talk
Julian Treasure — "5 ways to listen better" (TED)

Eight minutes on why we hear a sentence and keep a different one. That gap is exactly where the three answers of this lesson come from, and he is unusually easy to follow at B1.

Tip: turn the English subtitles on. Afterwards, report the talk back in five sentences, all of them starting with he said or he told us.
