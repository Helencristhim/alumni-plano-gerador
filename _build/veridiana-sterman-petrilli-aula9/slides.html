Lesson 9

They Told Me Something Else

Three people, three answers, and the sentence that gets you one

Warm-up

Who Told You That?

First, your five sentences comparing the two hotels. Then: think of one thing somebody told you this week that turned out to be wrong. Who told you, and what did they say?

The Target

Quote, Do Not Argue

1. Who told you. A person, not the company.

2. What they said. Their words, moved one step into the past.

3. What you want now. One answer, one time, one line in writing.

Nobody argues with a quotation. They check it.

Diagnostic

The Boat at Nine

Yesterday a woman on the phone said to you: “The boat leaves at nine, and your booking is confirmed.” This morning you are at the desk, and the boat is not there.

Tell the person at the desk what she said. One sentence, exactly as it would come out.

Chapter 3: The Code

One Step Into the Past

Say, tell, and what happens to the words in between

Grammar Discovery

What Happened to Their Words?

“The boat leaves at nine.”

She told me the boat left at nine.

“The tour is cancelled.”

He said the tour was cancelled.

“We will call you back.”

They told me they would call me back.

“We changed the time last week.”

She said they had changed the time.

Look at the blue verbs: one of them always has a person right after it, and the other one never does. Now compare each quotation with the sentence under it and watch what the red word does.

JobFormExample
Repeating the wordssaid + what — never a person after itHe said the tour was cancelled.
Naming who heard ittold + person + whatShe told me the boat left at nine.
Present moves backis / are → was / were · leaves → left“It leaves at nine.” → She said it left at nine.
Will and can move backwill → would · can → could“We will call you.” → They said they would call me.
Past moves back toochanged → had changedShe said they had changed the time.
Time words movetoday → that day · tomorrow → the next dayShe said the boat left the next day.
The trapnever said me, never told that with no personNot he said me. Say he told me.

In one line: say goes alone, tell needs a person, and everything inside the sentence moves one step into the past.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Three Versions at One Desk

One conversation, two recordings, one note

Dialogue

At the Tour Desk

M
Good morning. How can I help?
V
I hope you can clear something up for me. I have been told three different things about the boat this morning.
M
Three? Go on. Who told you what?
V
The woman on the phone told me it left at nine, and she assured me the booking was confirmed. Then your website said ten thirty. And the driver told me this morning that the whole trip had been cancelled.
M
I am sorry. Nine is the old time, the website is right, and nothing has been cancelled. The driver was talking about the fishing trip, so it is a misunderstanding and not a change.
V
So, word for word: the boat leaves at ten thirty today and my booking stands. Is that right?
M
That is right. And I will put you through to the operator now, so that you hear it from them as well.
V
Thank you. Could you also send me that in writing, straight away? Then nobody has to remember who said what.
Comprehension

About Marcus

1. What does he ask as soon as he hears there are three versions?
He asks who told her what.
2. How does he explain the three answers?
He said nine was the old time, the website was right, and nothing had been cancelled. The driver was talking about the fishing trip, so it was a misunderstanding and not a change.
3. What does he offer to do before she asks him for anything?
He offers to put her through to the operator, so that she hears it from them as well.
Listening 1 · A Front-Desk Trainer

Who Told You That?

The question she teaches in week one, and why it works from your side too. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the one sentence she asks every new person to learn, and what is it not?
It is a question, not an apology: who told you that, and when?
2. What does she say most angry conversations at a desk are really about?
Two people holding two different versions of the same fact, and neither of them knowing where the other version came from.
3. What three things does quoting the person do, and how does she compare the two openings?
It shows you are not inventing it, it gives them somebody to check with, and it is not an accusation, because you are quoting and not arguing. The version that goes wrong starts with you people; the version that works starts with somebody told me.
Listening 2 · A Tour Operator

Why You Get Three Answers

Nobody is lying to you, and there are three things to ask for. Sound first — no text.

1. What happens to the morning boat in October, and who learns about it first?
It moves from nine to ten thirty when the season changes. The phone team gets the new time on the first of the month.
2. Why can the website and the driver still be wrong?
The website is edited only when somebody remembers, which can be a week later, and the drivers hear it on the morning it changes, so a driver may still be repeating last week.
3. What are the three things he says to ask for, and why the third one?
A name, an actual time rather than soon or later, and one line in writing — because writing is the only version that does not change when the shift changes.

The Note on Your Phone

Four lines, four names, four times. The last one is the only one in writing, and it is the one that ends the conversation.
1. Report the first two lines out loud. What did Dana tell you, and what did the website say?
Dana told me the boat left at nine and that my booking was confirmed. The website said the departure was at ten thirty and that I had to check in thirty minutes before.
2. Which two entries disagree with each other, and how far apart are they in time?
Dana on Monday afternoon and the website on Tuesday evening — about a day apart. The driver on Wednesday morning disagrees with both.
3. Why is the last line the one that ends the conversation?
Because it is the only one in writing, and writing does not change when the shift changes.
