Lesson 29 -- Pre-class

Reporting to Senior Partners

The day before the investment committee, Eduarda has ten minutes to brief Richard Hale, a senior partner, on the Lane deal. Key words: executive summary, briefing, recommendation, exposure, outstanding, mitigate, heads-up, green light. Structure: cleft sentences for emphasis (What worries them is... / It's the... that...).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each term and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Executive summary -- a short text at the start of a report with the most important points
"The executive summary fits on one page."
Briefing -- a short meeting where someone gives the key information about a situation
"Richard wants a briefing before the committee."
Recommendation -- advice about what someone should do
"Our recommendation is to accept the new terms."
Exposure -- the amount of money that a company could lose because of a risk
"Our exposure to the supplier case is about two million dollars."
Outstanding -- not finished or not resolved yet
"Only one point is still outstanding: the cap."
Mitigate -- to make a risk or a problem smaller
"The escrow mitigates the risk for the buyer."
Heads-up -- an early warning about something that is going to happen
"James gave Eduarda a heads-up about the meeting."
Green light -- permission to start or continue with something
"Eduarda needs the green light before Tuesday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The investment committee meets on Tuesday. On Monday, James gives Eduarda a heads-up: Richard Hale, a senior partner, wants a briefing on Lane. Eduarda writes a one-page executive summary and starts with the answer: the deal is on track.

In the meeting, she puts a spotlight on what matters. What NorthPack accepted is a twelve-month escrow, and that mitigates the risk for Victor. What is still outstanding is the cap. It's the supplier case that creates the exposure, about two million dollars.

She ends with her recommendation: accept fifteen percent and close in March. What she needs from Richard is the green light before the committee.

1. How does Eduarda start the executive summary?
A With the answer: the deal is on track.
B With the story of the deal, from the first meeting with Victor to the letter of intent.
C With the cap.
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "What is still outstanding in the term sheet from NorthPack it is the cap."
B "What is still outstanding is the cap."
C "It's the cap what is outstanding."
3. What does Eduarda need from Richard?
A A new valuation.
B A call with Sarah Kim and the NorthPack lawyers to discuss the escrow again.
C The green light before the committee.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Cleft Sentences for Emphasis

GRAMMAR

A cleft sentence divides one idea into two parts to put a spotlight on the most important information. Use one or two in a briefing, on the point that decides.

FormNeutralWith a spotlight
What + clause + isThe cap worries them.What worries them is the cap.
What + clause + wasNorthPack accepted a shorter escrow.What NorthPack accepted was a shorter escrow.
What + clause + is + toVictor wants to close in March.What Victor wants is to close in March.
It's + focus + thatThe supplier case creates the exposure.It's the supplier case that creates the exposure.
It was + person + whoSarah Kim called this morning.It was Sarah Kim who called this morning.

Never: What worries me it is the cap · It's the case what creates the exposure

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose what a confident advisor would say.

A senior partner asks: "Where are we on Lane?" You start with:
A "The bottom line is that the deal is on track."
B "Well, two months ago we had a first meeting with Victor, and then NorthPack made an offer of ninety million."
C "I don't know yet."
You want to put a spotlight on the main risk. You say:
A "It's the supplier case what creates the exposure for NorthPack and for Victor."
B "It's the supplier case that creates the exposure."
C "Risk is supplier."
The partner asks how you will handle the risk. You say:
A "We hope that it goes away."
B "We will mitigate it later, maybe, after the closing date, if the supplier still wants the money from Lane."
C "The escrow mitigates it: it covers the two million."
You finish the briefing. You say:
A "What I need from you is the green light before Tuesday."
B "So, that's everything."
C "What I need from you it is the green light, if possible, before the committee meets on Tuesday."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Think of a deal or a project from your work. Your senior partner has one minute. Record your briefing: the bottom line first, then what is still outstanding, then your recommendation and what you need. Use at least two sentences with "What ... is..." or "It's ... that...".
