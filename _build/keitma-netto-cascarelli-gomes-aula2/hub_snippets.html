Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A journal entry -- the single record that puts one transaction into the books
"The last journal entry of the month goes in before the cut-off."
To post -- to enter a transaction into the system so that it becomes official
"Nothing is real until it is posted in the system."
To reconcile -- to compare two records until they agree with each other
"We reconcile the bank accounts on the second working day."
A cut-off -- the moment after which a transaction belongs to the next period
"After the cut-off, everything you post lands in the next month."
An accrual -- a cost recorded before the invoice for it has arrived
"We book an accrual for the audit fee every December."
A variance -- the difference between the number you expected and the number you got
"Every variance above one per cent needs a written explanation."
To flag -- to mark something so that somebody else looks at it
"I flagged three items for the controller before the review."
A sign-off -- the formal approval that closes a step and lets the next one start
"Nothing goes to the auditors before the manager's sign-off."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

There is a moment, in the middle of every explanation, where a listener decides whether to keep listening. It is not the first sentence. It is the second one — the one that has to say how it is connected to the first. A person who knows the subject perfectly can lose a room there, because the facts arrive as a list of separate things and the listener has to guess the order.

English has a small set of words for exactly this, and a process is the easiest place to see them working. First, then and after that only say the order: this one, and now this one. Once says something stronger — that the second step cannot start before the first is complete. Until marks the moment a situation stops being true: nothing can be reconciled until the last entry has been posted. As soon as means immediately after, with no gap at all. And by the time looks forward from a point that has not arrived yet: by the time the fifth day arrives, the report is already with the controller.

There is one trap, and it catches almost every learner whose first language marks the future twice. All of these words already carry the future inside them, so the verb after them does not need to. English says once the cut-off has passed, not once the cut-off will pass. The same happens with until, as soon as and by the time. Notice what this does to an explanation: the future stops being repeated in every clause, and the listener stops having to hold it. The steps line up, and the second sentence finally sounds like it belongs to the first.

1. According to the text, where does a listener decide whether to keep listening?
A In the first sentence, because that is where the subject is announced.
B In the second sentence, which has to say how it is connected to the first.
C At the end, when all the facts have been given.
2. What is the difference between then and once, in the text?
A There is none; they are two ways of saying the same thing.
B Then only gives the order; once says the second step cannot start before the first is complete.
C Then is used in writing and once is used in speaking.
3. Why does the text say the verb after these words does not need the future?
A Because the words already carry the future inside them, so repeating it is unnecessary.
B Because the future does not exist in English grammar.
C Because these words are only used to talk about the past.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Sequencers and Time Linkers

GRAMMAR

Six words. They are what carries the listener from one step to the next.

WordWhat it says about the two stepsExample
first · then · after thatplain order: this one, and now this oneFirst we post the entries, then we reconcile.
oncethe second step can only start when the first is completeOnce the cut-off has passed, nothing else goes in.
untilthe moment when a situation stops being trueNothing can be reconciled until the last entry has been posted.
as soon asimmediately after, with no gapAs soon as the review is done, I send the report.
by the timewhen that moment arrives, this is already finishedBy the time the fifth day arrives, the report is with the controller.
before · after + -ingtwo actions, the same person doing bothBefore sending the report, I check the variances.
the trapafter these words English uses the present, even for a future momentNot once the cut-off will pass. Say once the cut-off has passed.
the one that travels with ityou explain something to a person; the report arrives on a day, in the morningI explained the variance to the controller on Monday.

Never: once the cut-off will pass · after to post the entries · until the report will be finished · explain the variance for the controller

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

You want to say that nothing else can go into this month's books. You say:
A Once the cut-off will pass, nothing else can be posted.
B Once the cut-off has passed, nothing else can be posted.
C Once the cut-off passing, nothing else can be posted.
Your counterpart asks when he will get the variance report. You say:
A As soon as the review is finished, I send it to you — that is day four.
B As soon as the review will finish, I will send it for you.
C When finishing the review, the report is sent for you in day four.
You need one more day and you want to ask politely. You say:
A I need more time, it is not possible today.
B Could you give me until the end of the day tomorrow?
C Please, until tomorrow I will can finish.
You have found one open item and the sign-off is about to happen. You say:
A There is a problem in one item, I think.
B Let me flag one item before we sign off.
C One item is flagged for you to look before the sign.
