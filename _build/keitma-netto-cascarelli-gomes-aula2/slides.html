Chapter 4: The Words in Between

Six Words That Hold Two Sentences Together

Not the steps. What goes between them

Grammar Discovery

What Do the Coloured Words Do?

"First we post the entries, and then we reconcile the accounts."

"Nothing can be reconciled until the last entry has been posted."

"Once the cut-off has passed, everything you post lands in the next month."

"By the time the fifth day arrives, there is nothing left to argue about."

All four sentences talk about the future. Look at the verbs in green. Which tense are they in — and which tense is missing?

WordWhat it says about the two stepsExample
first · then · after thatplain order: this one, and now this oneFirst we post the entries, then we reconcile.
oncethe second step can only start when the first is completeOnce the cut-off has passed, nothing else goes in.
untilthe moment when a situation stops being trueNothing can be reconciled until the last entry has been posted.
as soon asimmediately after, with no gapAs soon as the review is done, I send the report.
by the timewhen that moment arrives, this is already finishedBy the time the fifth day arrives, the report is with the controller.
before · after + -ingtwo actions, the same person doing bothBefore sending the report, I check the variances.
the trapafter these words English uses the present, even for a future momentNot once the cut-off will pass. Say once the cut-off has passed.

In one line: these words already carry the future, so the verb does not have to. That is why the sentence never needs will after them.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

The Same Close, Heard Not Read

Two voice notes and one calendar

Listening 1 · A Voice Note

Three Things About Next Month

A controller recording a message to her team instead of writing an e-mail. Sound first — no text.

1. Why is she recording a message instead of writing one?
Because it is long, and she says they would read it faster than they can hear it — and she wants them to hear it.
2. What are the two changes to next month's calendar?
The cut-off moves to six in the evening on the last calendar day instead of midnight, and the accrual review moves to day two instead of day five.
3. What does she ask them not to wait for, and what should they do instead?
Not to wait until the variance report is finished. If a number looks wrong on day one, they should tell her on day one.
Listening 2 · A Process Lead

The Most Expensive Ten Minutes

A global process lead explaining which step of the close really costs the most.

1. Which step do people expect to be the most expensive, and which one does he say it really is?
People expect the reconciliation. He says it is the ten minutes at the end, when somebody has to explain a number to a person who was not in the room.
2. What example does he give of a close that finished on time and still lost three days?
The person who prepared it could not say, in one sentence, why the number moved, so the close lost three days in review.
3. What does he tell his teams to do before they press send?
To write one sentence saying what changed and why. If they cannot write it, he says the close is not finished — it is just posted.
