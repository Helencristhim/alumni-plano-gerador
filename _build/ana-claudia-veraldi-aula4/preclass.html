Lesson 04 -- Pre-class

English Around the World -- The Gist First, Then the Detail

Most English today has no native speaker in the room. Key words: to catch, to make out, muffled, to trail off, the gist, to follow somebody, to chime in, to talk over somebody, filler words, to rephrase, to be on the same page, to lose the thread. Language: asking somebody to repeat, in four registers -- and confirming before you agree.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To catch -- to hear something and understand it
"Sorry, I did not catch that last part."
To make out -- to manage to hear or understand something with difficulty
"The line was bad and I could barely make out her name."
Muffled -- hard to hear, as if something is covering the sound
"Her microphone was muffled the whole call."
To trail off -- to become quieter and stop before finishing the sentence
"He started to explain and then just trailed off."
The gist -- the general meaning, without the details
"I did not catch every word, but I got the gist."
To follow somebody -- to understand what somebody is saying while they say it
"He speaks fast, but I can follow him now."
To chime in -- to join a conversation with a short comment
"Sorry to chime in, but I have one question."
To talk over somebody -- to keep speaking while the other person is still speaking
"On video calls people talk over each other constantly."
Filler words -- sounds like um and you know that fill a pause while you think
"Filler words are not a mistake -- native speakers use them too."
To rephrase -- to say the same thing again in different words
"Could you rephrase that? I am not sure I follow."
To be on the same page -- to have the same understanding as somebody else
"Let me repeat it back, just so we are on the same page."
To lose the thread -- to stop being able to follow what is being said
"I lost the thread somewhere around phase one."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Thursday's call there were four people and four accents. Lars is Norwegian and speaks slowly, but he trails off at the end of his sentences. Bram is Dutch and goes straight to the point. Priya is Indian and speaks quickly. Mike is American and his microphone was muffled.

Ana did not catch every word, and it did not matter. She got the gist of each one. When she lost the thread in the middle of Bram's message, she did not pretend: "Sorry, could you give me the short version?" When Priya used a word she did not know, she asked her to rephrase it.

Before agreeing to anything, she repeated the decision back: "Just to check, are you saying the fifteenth?" By the end everybody was on the same page — which is the only thing a meeting is for.

1. Why is "Sorry, I did not catch that" better than "What?" with a colleague you barely know?
A Because "What?" is grammatically incorrect.
B Because "What?" alone sounds impatient in English, even when you do not mean it.
C Because "What?" is only used in writing.
2. Ana says "Just to check, are you saying the fifteenth?" Why does she do this?
A To confirm a decision before agreeing to it.
B To show she was not listening.
C To change the subject.
3. According to the text, what does a meeting exist for?
A For everybody to understand every word.
B For the person with the best English to decide.
C For everybody to end up on the same page.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Asking Again, in Four Registers

GRAMMAR

All of these ask for the same thing. What changes is the distance.

SentenceDistanceWho it fits
Sorry, what was that?CloseAmericans, Australians, colleagues you know
Sorry, I did not catch that.NeutralWorks everywhere. Your default.
Could you repeat that, please?PoliteFirst meetings, clients, larger groups
Would you mind saying that again?CarefulNordic colleagues, formal settings, seniority
Could you give me the short version?NeutralYou caught the words but missed the point
Just to check, are you saying ...?NeutralBefore agreeing to anything that matters

Never: "What?" on its own with somebody you do not know well · "Repeat, please" (it is an order) · "I did not listen you" (the verb is hear, and listen does not take a direct object).

Stage 2: Put the Conversation in Order

Order

Ana did not understand a word on a call. Put the exchange in a logical order.

?"I want to go back to the sequencing of the deliverables."
?"Sorry, I did not catch that last word. Could you say it again?"
?"Sequencing. The order -- which item comes first, second, third."
?"Got it. Just to check, are you saying we agree the order today?"
?"Exactly. Otherwise we only think we are on the same page."
?"Now I follow you. Then I am happy to start."
