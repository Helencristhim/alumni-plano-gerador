Chapter 3: The Code

When the Doer Disappears

The passive voice — and why legal English lives in it

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The subsidiary was incorporated in Delaware in March."

"All filings are reviewed by outside counsel before they are sent."

"The service has been rolled out in three states."

"Nothing can be signed until the structure is approved."

In three of these sentences you cannot say who did it. In one you can. What decides whether the doer appears at all?

FormWhat it doesExample
be + past participlePuts the ACTION first. Use it when the doer is unknown, obvious, irrelevant — or when you would rather not name them."The subsidiary was incorporated in Delaware."
by + agentAdd by only when the doer is NEW information. If it is obvious, leaving it in makes the sentence heavier for no gain."reviewed by outside counsel"
not "signed by a person"
the tense sits on BEThe participle never changes. Everything — time, modality, negation — happens to be."is signed · was signed · has been signed · will be signed · can be signed"
legal and corporate registerContracts, filings and reports are written in the passive because the OBLIGATION matters more than the person. This is the English you already read every day."The Buyer shall be notified in writing."
"Payment is to be made within thirty days."
get-passive (spoken)Informal, and usually bad news. Perfect in a corridor, wrong in a contract."The filing got rejected."

Portuguese solves the same problem with se and with the third person plural, so the structure feels familiar and the FORM is where the mistakes are: the participle, and the fact that only be carries the tense.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"The subsidiary was incorporate in Delaware."
"The subsidiary was incorporated in Delaware."
"The contract was signed by we."
"The contract was signed by us."
"The filings are reviewing by the legal team."
"The filings are reviewed by the legal team."

The first is the participle: after be it is always the third form, never the infinitive. The second is the pronoun: after by English uses the object form — us, them, him. The third swaps the passive for the continuous and reverses the meaning: are reviewing means the filings are doing the reviewing.

Listening 1

A Morning and a Year

A corporate lawyer on the part nobody plans for. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say takes a morning, and what takes a year?
Incorporating the company takes a morning; everything after that takes about a year.
2. Why is the bank account so slow?
The account is subject to checks written for money laundering, and a foreign company with no American track record is what those checks were designed to slow down.
3. What is his advice?
Start the banking on day one, in parallel with everything else, and assume it will decide the launch date.
Listening 2

Four States at Once

A founder on the one thing she would do differently. Sound first — no text.

1. What would she do differently, and what would she not change?
She would refuse to launch in more than one state; she would not change the lawyers or the structure.
2. What did launching in four states actually buy them?
Four sets of payroll rules, four filing calendars and four different answers to the same question — the same small problem repeated, not growth.
3. Where did the foothold finally come from?
From a single client in a single state who agreed to be a reference.
