Lesson 2 -- Going North

Grammar Video
"How to Use the Active and Passive Voice" — 6 Minute Grammar, BBC Learning English

Six minutes on the structure behind every clause you read at work: be plus the past participle, and the moment when it is worth adding by. Connection to Lesson 2: this is the grammar of "the subsidiary was incorporated" and "all filings are reviewed".

Tip: after each example, say the same sentence about your own company. If you can say it without the agent and nothing is lost, you have understood why the passive exists.

Podcast
"We Set Up an Offshore Company in a Tax Haven" — Planet Money, NPR

The Planet Money team actually incorporates two companies on air — one in Belize and one in Delaware — and finds out how little it takes. Clear American English. Connection to Lesson 2: it is the second paragraph of today's text, lived out by people who had never done it before.

Tip: listen once without the transcript and write three sentences in the passive about what you heard. Then read the transcript and count how many passives the presenters use themselves.

Reading
"Checklist for Foreign Companies Expanding into the US" — Mintz

An American law firm's own checklist for exactly what your company is doing right now: entity choice, employment, tax and the filings nobody warns you about. Connection to Lesson 2: it is the text of today's lesson written by lawyers, for lawyers, and almost every sentence is in the passive.

Tip: read one section only, not the whole page. Underline every passive you find and rewrite three of them in the active — you will see immediately why the original is written the way it is.
