Chapter 3: The Code

Advice, or Rule?

should · ought to · had better · have to · must · do not have to

Grammar Discovery

Who Is Helping You, and Who Is Quoting a Rule?

"You should call your primary care physician first."

"You have to get a referral before you see a specialist."

"You do not have to pay the lab today."

"You must not take two of these on the same day."

Look at the verb after the orange and the blue words: call, get, pay, take. It never changes. All the meaning lives in the small word in front of it — and the two blue ones are opposites, not synonyms.

MeaningFormExample
Adviceshould / ought to + base verbYou should call the office in the morning.
Strong warninghad better (not) + base verbYou had better not skip the follow-up.
Obligationhave to / must + base verbYou have to get a referral first.
No obligationdo not have to + base verbYou do not have to pay the lab today.
Prohibitionmust not + base verbYou must not take two on the same day.
QuestionDo I have to ... ? / Should I ... ?Do I have to bring my insurance card?
NeverNo to after should / mustYou should to call · You must to get

In one line: do not have to = you may skip it. Must not = you may not do it.

The Desk, the Phone, and the Fine Print

Dialogue

First Time at This Front Desk

G
Hi. I have an eleven o’clock with Dr. Alvarez. It is my first time here.
B
Welcome. I am Brian, I run the front desk. Are you a new patient with us, or were you sent over by another office?
G
New. I moved here in May, and my shoulder has been sore for about three weeks.
B
Okay. Do you have a primary care physician yet? Because if you end up needing a specialist, she has to write you a referral first. Most plans will not cover that visit without one.
G
That is completely new to me. And what do I pay today?
B
Your co-pay today is forty dollars. If the doctor decides to run tests, the lab bills your plan separately, so you should check your coverage before you leave.
G
And do I have to come back after the tests?
B
Usually, yes. Let me put a follow-up on the calendar for two weeks from Thursday. You do not have to keep that day — call us and we will move it.
Comprehension

About Brian

1. According to Brian, what has to happen before a patient can see a specialist?
The primary care physician has to write a referral first. Most plans will not cover the specialist visit without one.
2. How much is the co-pay, and what does Brian say about the tests?
Forty dollars. If the doctor runs tests, the lab bills the plan separately, so he says to check the coverage before leaving.
3. What does Brian put on the calendar, and what does he say about that date?
A follow-up for two weeks from Thursday. He says she does not have to keep that day — she can call and they will move it.
Listening 1 · The Phone Menu

Press One, Press Three

The recording you get before a human ever picks up. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What are you told to do if this is an emergency?
Hang up now and dial nine one one.
2. Which number do you press to refill a prescription, and what do you need in front of you?
Three. You need the bottle, because they need the name of the medicine and the number on the label.
3. How long does a refill take, and what should a new patient bring?
Two business days. A new patient should arrive fifteen minutes early with an insurance card and a photo ID.
Listening 2 · A First Appointment

What She Should Have Asked

Somebody who moved here and got almost everything wrong the first time. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What did she expect at the clinic, and what happened instead?
She expected to walk in and see whoever was free, the way she did back home. Instead they asked for the name of her primary care physician, and because she did not have one, they booked her for the following week.
2. What surprised her at the front desk?
They asked her for forty dollars before she had seen anybody. That is the co-pay, and nobody had explained it to her.
3. What does she say she should have asked before going, and what did that cost her?
What her coverage actually included. Three weeks later a bill arrived for the lab work: another two hundred dollars she was not expecting.
Listening 3 · Your Plan

What Is Actually Covered

A recorded instruction from the insurance company. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Which three things does the plan cover with a co-pay?
One annual physical, most office visits with your primary care physician, and generic prescriptions.
2. What do you need before a specialist visit, and what happens without it?
A referral from your primary care physician. Without it, the visit may not be covered and you would pay the full amount yourself.
3. What are you told to do if a bill looks wrong?
Do not pay it. Call the number on the back of your card and ask for an itemized statement first.
