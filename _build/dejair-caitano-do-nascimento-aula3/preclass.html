Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

Where Have You Been? -- The Events You Already Attended

Telling what happened at a past congress, and answering the second question everybody asks at a coffee break. Key words: to present, panel, session, keynote, abstract, poster, venue, feedback. Structure: past simple -- regular and irregular verbs, negatives and questions.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. All eight are printed in the programme you will hold in Bangkok.

To present -- to give a talk about your work at an event
"I presented a poster at a congress in 2024."
Panel -- a small group who answer questions in front of an audience
"The panel took questions for twenty minutes."
Session -- one part of the programme, with one subject and a fixed time
"The morning session started at nine and ran until noon."
Keynote -- the main talk of the event, for everybody
"The keynote was about pain in low income countries."
Abstract -- a short summary of your work that you send before the event
"I sent my abstract in March and they accepted it."
Poster -- a large printed sheet with your study, that you stand next to
"My poster was number forty-two, near the door."
Venue -- the building or place where the event happens
"The venue was a university, not a hotel."
Feedback -- what other people tell you about your work
"The feedback I got at the poster changed my next study."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Last year Ayse Demir went to a pain congress in Lisbon. She sent her abstract in March and the committee accepted it. She presented a poster on the second day. For the first twenty minutes nobody came, and she felt terrible. Then a professor stopped, asked her three questions and stayed for half an hour. His feedback was hard, but he was right. She did not go to the closing keynote. She talked to four people instead, and two of them are her co-authors now.
Why do presented, asked and stayed all end in -ed?
A Because they are regular verbs, and the action is finished and in the past
B Because the subject is she
C Because they happen every year
Why is it "did not go" and not "did not went"?
A Because go is irregular and never changes
B Because did already carries the past, so the main verb goes back to its plain form
C Because the sentence is a question

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Simple

GRAMMAR

Use the past simple for finished actions at a finished time: last year, in 2024, on the second day, yesterday. The form is the same for I, you, he, she, we and they -- there is never an extra -s.

regular verbsverb + -edI presented a poster. / She asked me three questions.
irregular verbstheir own formI went to Lisbon. / He gave the keynote. / We met four people.
negativedid not + verbI did not send an abstract that year.
questiondid + subject + verbDid you present a poster?
to bewas / wereThe venue was small. The sessions were excellent.

One past per sentence. "Did you presented?" is wrong -- the past is already inside did.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
A researcher asks you at the coffee break: have you been to an event like this before? Record two minutes about the international event you went to in 2024: where it was, what the venue was like, which sessions you went to, whether you presented, who you talked to, and one thing you learned there. No script -- just speak.
