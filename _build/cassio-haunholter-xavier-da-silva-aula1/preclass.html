Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A kick-off meeting -- the first meeting of a project, where the teams agree how they will work
"We ran the kick-off meeting with the Houston office last Monday."
A counterpart -- the person in another office or company who has the same role as you
"My counterpart in Gothenburg runs the same department there."
Headquarters -- the main office of a company, where the central decisions are made
"Our headquarters are in Sweden, but we have offices on four continents."
A subsidiary -- a company that is owned and controlled by a larger company
"The Brazilian subsidiary has about two hundred engineers."
To report to -- to work under the authority of a specific manager
"Twelve engineers report to me in the projects department."
A discipline -- a technical specialism inside engineering, such as structures or piping
"Each discipline has its own lead engineer."
A workload -- the amount of work a person or a team has at a given moment
"The workload in structures is heavier than usual this quarter."
To oversee -- to watch over work and make sure it is done properly
"I oversee the industrial projects from concept to detailed design."
A portfolio -- the whole set of projects a person or a department is responsible for
"My portfolio is mostly industrial buildings and process plants."
To be based in -- to have your main office in a place
"I am based in Brazil, but half of my meetings are with Finland."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

The kick-off meeting for the new industrial block starts at two. Fifteen people are on the line, and the coordinator asks for a short round. The first engineer says: “I manage the piping team here in Helsinki, and at the moment we are checking the equipment list.”

The second one says: “I oversee the structural discipline at the Brazilian subsidiary. Eight engineers report to me. This week we are preparing the foundation drawings.”

Notice the pattern. When the sentence describes the role, the verb is simple: manage, oversee, report. When the sentence describes the task of the week, the verb takes am, is or are plus -ing.

1. Does the first engineer check equipment lists every week of the year?
A Yes, that is his permanent job.
B No. He manages the piping team; checking the equipment list is what he is doing at the moment.
C The text does not say.
2. Why does the second engineer say “eight engineers report to me” and not “are reporting”?
A Because it is the structure of his team, and it does not change from week to week.
B Because it happened once, in the past.
C Because reporting is a temporary task this week.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A “At the moment I work on the layout of block 2.”
B “At the moment I am working on the layout of block 2.”
C “At the moment I am work on the layout of block 2.”

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Present Simple vs Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

The simple form says who you are and what is always true. The -ing form says what you are in the middle of right now.

JobFormExample
Your role, always truepresent simple: I / we + verbI manage the projects department.
A routine, how oftenpresent simple + every week / usually / alwaysI report to the regional director every week.
Something temporary, happening nowam / is / are + verb-ingAt the moment I am working with the Houston office.
A project in progress, not finishedam / is / are + verb-ing + this month / right nowWe are mobilizing a structural team this month.
The two questionsWhat do you do? / What are you working on?What do you do here? — What are you working on at the moment?
The trapverbs of state stay simpleNot I am knowing that client. Say I know that client well.

Never: I am knowing that client · Every week I am joining the call · At the moment I work on block 2 · What are you doing in the company? (to ask about the job, say What do you do?)
