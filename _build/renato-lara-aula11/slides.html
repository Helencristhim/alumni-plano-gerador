Grammar Discovery

Which one has a date?

I have never been abroad.
I went to Chile in 2024.
Have you ever worn a uniform?
I wore one at my old school.

No date, and the door is still open

For experiences in your life up to now, use have / has + past participle, with ever in questions and never in negatives. The moment you add a date, the sentence closes and you need the past simple.

The Code

Ever, never, and no date

Experiences -- no date

I have been abroad.
I have never worn a uniform.
Have you ever met an Australian?
Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.

Watch the participle

be → been
wear → worn
meet → met
eat → eaten, see → seen

never is already negative: say "I have never been", never "I haven't never been".

Sound Check

The have is small, the participle is not

Have you ever been abroad?

the "have you" runs together into one sound

I have never worn a uniform.

the "have" is weak, but "never" is strong

No, I haven't. Have you?

in short answers the verb comes back, loud and clear

Did You Catch It?

About Zoe and the school

What has Renato never done, according to this conversation?
He has never been to Australia, never been abroad and never worn a uniform.
What does Zoe say about the roommate?
He is from Japan and he has been there for a month already, so he can show Renato the canteen.
What is Zoe's last piece of advice, and why?
Put sunscreen on, because Renato has never seen a sun like that one.
Listening 1

Your first orientation

1. How many subjects are on the timetable?
Six, and all of them are on the sheet in the folder.
2. What are the canteen hours?
It opens at half past twelve and closes at one fifteen.
3. What is the one thing she says to every new student?
Sunscreen, every day, even when it is cloudy.
Listening 2

Advice from your roommate

1. How long has he been there, and what has he already done?
Five weeks. He has made every mistake: missed the bus twice, gone to the wrong classroom, arrived at the canteen after it closed.
2. What does he say about eating lunch?
He has never seen anybody eat alone, so just sit at any table.
3. What does he say about the accent?
He did not understand a word in the first week, and now he understands about eighty per cent.
