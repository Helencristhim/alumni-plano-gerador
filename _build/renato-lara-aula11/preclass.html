Lesson 11 — Pre-class

While I Was Paddling Out

Eight story words (suddenly, notice, snap, shout, panic, whitewater, shallow, hold your breath) and the past continuous: was or were plus -ing for the scene, past simple for the thing that cuts it, with when and while.

Stage 1.1: Story Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Suddenly — very quickly, with no warning at all
"I was paddling out and suddenly the wind changed."
Notice — to see or hear something you were not looking for
"When I came up, I did not notice the second wave."
Snap — to break in two with a short loud sound
"I heard the board snap under my feet."
Shout — to say something very loudly so people far away hear you
"He started to shout from the beach."
Panic — a sudden strong fear that stops you thinking
"Do not panic. Stay with your board."
Whitewater — the white foam left after a wave breaks
"I was under the whitewater for twenty seconds."
Shallow — not deep -- you can stand up in it
"It is very shallow near the sandbank."
Hold your breath — to keep the air inside you and not let it out
"The only thing you can do is hold your breath and wait."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the story. Then answer the questions.

I was paddling out on the north bank and suddenly a big set came from nowhere. I was holding my breath under the first wave when the second one hit me. I was under the whitewater for about twenty seconds, and when I came up I noticed my board was gone. It snapped in two pieces. I didn't panic, because the water was shallow near the sandbank, so I walked in. While I was walking, my brother shouted something from the beach, but I wasn't listening.
What was already happening when the set came?
A The paddling — "was paddling" is the long action that was already running.
B The set — it started first and the paddling cut it.
Why is it "snapped" and not "was snapping"?
A Because both tenses mean the same thing in a story.
B Because it is one short thing that happened, not a scene that was running.
Why is it "while I was walking" and not "when I was walking"?
A "While" goes with the long scene; "when" goes with the short action.
B "While" is only for the future.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Past continuous — the scene, and the thing that cuts it

Two pieces: was / were + verb-ing. It paints what was already running. The past simple is the short thing that arrives in the middle.

The form

I / he / she was paddling
you / we / they were watching
I wasn't looking
What were you doing?

when or while

when + the short action
when I saw it, when it snapped
while + the long scene
while I was paddling out

Compare: "I paddled out at six" → one finished action. "I was paddling out when it happened" → a scene, cut in the middle.
Never "I paddling out". The -ing alone is not a verb — it always needs was or were in front of it.
Same sentence, two orders: "I was paddling out when it happened" = "While I was paddling out, it happened".

Stage 4: Telling the story

Quiz
You were in the middle of paddling out and then a set arrived. What do you say?
A I paddling out when the set came.
B I was paddling out when the set came.
Two friends on the sand, both looking at the water. Which one is correct?
A They were watching from the beach.
B They was watching from the beach.
You want to ask a new classmate about the moment of the accident.
A What you were doing when it happened?
B What were you doing when it happened?
The board broke in one second, in the middle of a long ride. How do you say it?
A While I was riding the wave, my board snapped.
B While I rode the wave, my board was snapping.

Stage 5: The scariest sixty seconds

Reflection
Tell the story of the worst minute you have had in the water, out loud, for 30 seconds. What were you doing before it started? What went wrong? Who was watching? Check one thing before you send it — every -ing needs a "was" or a "were" in front of it.
