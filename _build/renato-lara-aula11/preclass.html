Stage 1.1: Arrival Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Abroad — in another country, not your own
"This is my first time abroad."
Orientation — the first meeting that explains how everything works
"Orientation is on Monday morning."
Roommate — the person who shares your room
"My roommate is from Japan."
Canteen — the place at school where you eat
"Everybody eats in the canteen at half past twelve."
Uniform — the clothes every student has to wear
"I have never worn a uniform before."
Timetable — the paper that says which class you have and when
"My timetable has six subjects."
Accent — the way people from one place pronounce words
"The accent here is faster than on TV."
Sunscreen — the cream that protects your skin from the sun
"Nobody goes out without sunscreen here."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. Then answer the questions.

This is my first time abroad. I have never been to Australia before and I have never worn a school uniform in my life. I arrived on Tuesday and orientation was on Monday morning. I have already met my roommate. He is from Japan and he has been here for five weeks. He has made every mistake already, so he is useful. I have not understood much of the accent yet, but everybody says that changes.
What has he never done?
A He has never been to Australia and never worn a uniform.
B He has never met his roommate.
Why is his roommate useful?
A Because he is from Japan.
B Because he has been there five weeks and has made every mistake already.
Why is it "I arrived on Tuesday" and not "I have arrived on Tuesday"?
A Because the sentence has a finished date.
B Because "arrive" is a regular verb.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Present perfect — experiences up to now

have / has + past participle. Use it when there is no date and the story is still open.

Experiences -- no date

I have been abroad.
I have never worn a uniform.
Have you ever met an Australian?
Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.

Watch the participle

be → been
wear → worn
meet → met
eat → eaten, see → seen

ever lives in questions: Have you ever been abroad?
never lives in negatives, and it is already negative: I have never been. Not "haven't never".
Add a date and everything changes: I went to Chile in 2024. No "have" allowed.

Stage 4: First week

Quiz
Somebody asks if you have been to Australia before. You have not. What do you say?
A No, I never went.
B No, I have never been.
You want to ask about an experience, with no date in mind.
A Did you ever been to a reef?
B Have you ever surfed a reef?
You wore a uniform at your old school in Brazil. How do you say it?
A I wore a uniform at my old school.
B I have worn a uniform at my old school in 2023.
You met your roommate this morning. Which one is correct?
A I have already met my roommate.
B I have already meet my roommate.

Stage 5: Your experiences

Reflection
Record 30 seconds in English. Three things you have never done, two things you have already done since you arrived, and one thing you did before you came — that last one with a date. Check one thing before you send it: no sentence has "have" AND a finished date together.
