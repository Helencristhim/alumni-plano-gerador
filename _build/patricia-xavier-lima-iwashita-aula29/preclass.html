Lesson 29 -- Pre-class

If We Had Known...

An exit interview with a customer who switched to a competitor, and a win-loss report. Key words: competitor, switch, pricing, discount, budget cut, exit interview, deal-breaker, turning point, walk away, lesson learned. Structure: third conditional -- if + had + past participle, would have + past participle.

Vocabulary

Competitor -- another company that sells the same kind of product
"A competitor offered them a much lower price."
Switch -- to change from one product or company to another
"Harbor Health decided to switch in September."
Pricing -- the way a company decides and shows its prices
"Our pricing is clear, but it is not flexible."
Discount -- a lower price than the normal one
"The competitor offered a forty percent discount."
Budget cut -- a decision to spend less money than before
"The budget cut in June changed everything."
Exit interview -- a conversation with a customer who is leaving, to learn why
"Marcus agreed to a short exit interview."
Deal-breaker -- one problem so big that it stops an agreement
"For them, the price was the deal-breaker."
Turning point -- the moment when a situation starts to change
"The new CFO was the turning point."
Walk away -- to leave a deal or a relationship and not come back
"They did not walk away because of the product."
Lesson learned -- something that you understand after a mistake, for next time
"The lesson learned is to talk to finance early."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Harbor Health left in September. In the exit interview, Marcus explains why they decided to switch. First there was a budget cut in June. Then a competitor offered a big discount, and the price became the deal-breaker. Patrícia listens and thinks: if we had known about the budget, we would have offered a smaller plan. Marcus says that the price was the turning point, but if the team had called in the spring, they wouldn't have looked at other vendors. The lesson learned is clear: talk to finance early. And if the pricing changes, Marcus wants a call — Harbor Health did not walk away forever.

1. "If we had known about the budget..." Did Patrícia know about the budget?
A Yes, she knew.
B No, she did not know.
C We do not know.
2. Which form is in the result part of the sentence?
A would + base verb (would offer)
B would have + past participle (would have offered)
C had + past participle (had offered)
3. What was the turning point for Harbor Health?
A The price.
B A problem with the product.
C A new sponsor.
4. Which sentence is correct?
A "If we would have called, they would have stayed."
B "If we had called, they would have stayed."
C "If we had called, they would stayed."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Third Conditional: if + had + past participle, would have + past participle

GRAMMAR

Use it to talk about the past that did NOT happen, and its imagined result. Perfect for win-loss reviews and lessons learned.

StructureUseExample
If + had + past participleThe condition: it did not happen.If we had known about the budget...
would have + past participleThe imagined result....we would have offered a smaller plan.
wouldn't have + past participleA result that did not happen....we wouldn't have lost the account.
might / could have + past participleA possible result, less sure....they might have stayed.
Result first, no commaThe if part can come second.They would have stayed if we had called.
AvoidNever would in the if part: not "if we would have known" → "if we had known". Never the base verb after have: not "would have offer" → "would have offered". And never have instead of had: not "if I have met" → "if I had met".

Stage 2: Run an Exit Interview in Order

Order

Put the steps of a good exit interview in the right order.

?Ask what happened, and listen without defending yourself.
?Say what would have changed, with one specific if-sentence.
?Ask which problem was the deal-breaker.
?Leave the door open and write the lesson learned for your team.
?Thank the customer for their time and say that you want to learn.

Stage 3: Pronunciation

Speaking

Listen to each sentence, then record yourself saying it. In fast speech, "would have" sounds like "would-uv", and "had" is often just 'd.

If we had known about the budget cut, we would have offered a smaller plan.
If we had talked to finance, we wouldn't have lost the account.
Was the price the deal-breaker, or was there something else?
The lesson learned is simple: we talk to finance before every renewal.
If anything changes on your side, we would love a second chance.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think about a real account, deal or project in your career that did not go as you wanted. Record the story in five or six sentences: what happened, what was the turning point, what would have changed, and the lesson learned that you still use today. Use at least three third conditionals -- if + had + past participle, would have + past participle. Take your time.
