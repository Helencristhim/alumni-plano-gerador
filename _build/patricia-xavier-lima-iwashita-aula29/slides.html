Chapter 3: Language Focus

If We Had Known, We Would Have...

third conditional — if + had + past participle, would have + past participle

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"If we had known about the budget, we would have offered a smaller plan."

"If your team had called in the spring, we wouldn't have looked at other vendors."

"If I had met the new director, I would have kept the account."

"We would have seen the risk if we had talked to finance."

Did any of these things really happen? Look at the green words: which part is the condition, and which part is the result?

StructureUseExample
If + had + past participleThe condition: something that did NOT happen in the pastIf we had known about the budget...
would have + past participleThe imagined result in the past...we would have offered a smaller plan.
wouldn't have + past participleA result that did not happen...we wouldn't have looked at other vendors.
might / could have + past participleA possible result, when you are less sure...they might have stayed.
we'd known / we'd haveIn speech: the first 'd is had, the second is wouldIf we'd known, we'd have called.

Never would in the if part: not "if we would have known" → "if we had known". And never the base verb after have: not "would have offer" → "would have offered".

The Three Sentences of a Win-Loss Review

Condition, Result, Lesson

"If we had known about the budget cut, we would have offered a smaller plan."

1 — what would have changed: if + had, would have

"If we had talked to finance, we wouldn't have lost the account."

2 — the missed action: wouldn't have

"The lesson learned is simple: we talk to finance before every renewal."

3 — the lesson for next time: back to the present

Chapter 4: The Exit Interview

The Exit Interview

A customer who already left, and thirty minutes of honesty

Dialogue

What Would Have Changed?

P
Marcus, thank you for this exit interview. I really want to understand why you decided to switch.
M
Honestly, it was the budget cut. In June our finance team cut the software budget by thirty percent.
P
We did not know that. If we had known about the budget, we would have offered you a smaller plan.
M
Maybe. But the competitor offered a big discount, and our CFO saw it first.
P
Was the price the deal-breaker, or was there something else?
M
The price was the turning point. But if your team had called us in the spring, we wouldn't have looked at other vendors.
P
That is fair. The lesson learned for us is clear: talk to finance, not only to the users.
M
I appreciate that. And if your pricing changes, call me. We did not walk away forever.
Comprehension

About Marcus

1. What happened to Harbor Health's software budget in June?
Their finance team cut it by thirty percent.
2. What did the competitor offer, and who saw it first?
A big discount; the CFO saw it first.
3. According to Marcus, what would have happened if the team had called in the spring?
They wouldn't have looked at other vendors.
