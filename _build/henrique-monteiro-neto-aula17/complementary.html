Lesson 17 -- So Do I, and So Should You

Podcast
"Switchtracking" — Hidden Brain, NPR

The first episode of Hidden Brain, on the moment a conversation quietly changes subject without anybody noticing — one person answers a point the other never made. Connection to Lesson 17: it is the tangent from today's role-play, explained by the people who study it. Transcript on the page.

Tip: listen to the first segment, with Sheila Heen. Every time you hear a short answer that leaves words out, pause and say the full version in your head. You will be surprised how much is missing.

Talk
"How to Speak So That People Want to Listen" — Julian Treasure, TED

A sound expert on the habits that make people stop listening — among them, saying far more than is needed. Connection to Lesson 17: long-winded and succinct, terse and warm, all in ten minutes. English transcript on the page.

Tip: watch it for pleasure the first time. The second time, note the habits he lists and decide which one you notice most in meetings at the hospital.

Grammar Video
"Neither do I / So do I / I haven't either / Me too" — Papa Teach Me

A short, lively review of agreement with so, neither, either and too, with the auxiliary chosen to match the original sentence. Connection to Lesson 17: the first row of today's grammar table, drilled until it is automatic.

Tip: it starts at an easier level than ours, so use it as a speed drill. Pause after each example and answer before he does, without ever repeating the main verb.
