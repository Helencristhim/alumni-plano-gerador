Chapter 3: The Code

What She Said, Written Down

The sentence that tells somebody what another person said

Discovery

What Happened to the Verb?

On the call: "I can't decide that today."
In the minutes: "Megan said she could not decide that day."

On the call: "We will send the document today."
In the minutes: "Roberto said they would send the document that day."

On the call: "The container is still in Savannah."
In the minutes: "Megan said the container was still in Savannah."

On the call: "We need fifty more samples."
In the minutes: "She told us they needed fifty more samples."

On its way to the minutes, every red verb moves one step back. And look at the blue one: one verb takes a person right after it, and the other one does not.

UseFormExample
present becomes pastis → was · need → neededShe said the container was in Savannah.
will becomes wouldwill → wouldHe said they would send it that day.
can becomes couldcan → could · can't → could notShe said she could not decide.
say or tellsaid (that) + sentence · told + personShe said that... / She told us that...
time words move tootoday → that day · tomorrow → the next day...send it that day.
The trapnever said + personNot she said me. She told me, or she said to me.

In one line: REPORT IT, and every verb moves one step back. SAY a sentence, TELL a person.

Listening 1 · The Correction

Almost Perfect

Megan, after she read the minutes. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does she want to correct in the minutes?
The day: she said she would talk to her director on Tuesday, not on Monday.
2. Why is that day important to her?
Because her director is traveling on Monday, and he reads the minutes too.
3. What detail is missing from the minutes?
The fifty samples have to come with the new labels, not the old ones.
Listening 2 · Four Questions

Nobody Reads a Transcript

A manager who reads a lot of bad minutes. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What mistake does he see in almost every set of minutes?
People write down everything, like a transcript, and nobody reads it.
2. What four questions do good minutes answer?
Who was there, what was decided, what is on hold and until when, and who does what by when.
3. Why does he say to circulate the minutes the same day?
Because a week later people remember a different meeting.

Megan said she would talk to her director on Tuesday.
