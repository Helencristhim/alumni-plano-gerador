Lesson 12 -- Pre-class

The Minutes -- The Record That Arrives After the Meeting

The call with Megan is over, and somebody has to write it down. Key words: the minutes, an attendee, to circulate, to confirm, to raise, to point out, a next step, on hold. Structure: reported speech -- how you tell somebody what another person said.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

The minutes -- the short written record of what was said and decided in a meeting
"I will circulate the minutes today."
An attendee -- a person who is in the meeting
"There were three attendees on the call."
To circulate -- to send a document to everybody who needs it
"Please circulate the minutes before lunch."
To confirm -- to say that something is true, or that it is going to happen
"Paula confirmed that the document was ready."
To raise -- to bring a problem or a question into the discussion
"Roberto raised one concern about June."
To point out -- to show a fact that the others may not see
"She pointed out that the price was four percent higher."
A next step -- the next action, after a meeting or a decision
"The next step is a call with her director."
On hold -- stopped for now, and waiting for something
"The price is on hold until next Thursday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Thursday, Roberto chaired a call with Megan, a buyer in Chicago. There were three attendees. After the call, he wrote the minutes, and he did not copy anybody’s sentences. He reported them.

On the call, Megan said: The container is still in Savannah. In the minutes: "Megan said the container was still in Savannah." On the call, Paula said: The document is ready. In the minutes: "Paula confirmed that the document was ready." Notice it: is becomes was. The verb moves one step back.

Megan also said: I can’t decide today. So the minutes say: "Megan said she could not decide that day," and the price is on hold. And about the samples: "She told us they needed fifty more." She told us — with a person. She said — without one. He circulated the minutes the same day.

1. On the call: "The container is still in Savannah." Why do the minutes say was and not is?
A Because the container is not in Savannah anymore.
B Because when you report what somebody said, the verb moves one step back.
C Because was is the polite form of is, and minutes are always written in a polite and formal way.
2. "She told us they needed fifty more." Why told and not said here?
A Because there is a person right after it: us. Said does not take a person like that.
B Because told is only for numbers.
C Because told is the past of say, and said is the present form.
3. Why does Roberto not copy the sentences from the call?
A Because he did not hear them very well on the call, with the noise and the bad connection that day.
B Because the minutes report the idea of what people said, not every word.
C Because copying is not allowed.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech

GRAMMAR

How you tell somebody what another person said. The verb moves one step back, and say and tell are not the same.

UseFormExample
present becomes pastis → was · need → neededShe said the container was in Savannah.
will becomes wouldwill → wouldHe said they would send it that day.
can becomes couldcan → could · can't → could notShe said she could not decide.
say or tellsaid (that) + sentence · told + personShe said that... / She told us that...
time words move tootoday → that day · tomorrow → the next day...send it that day.
The trapnever said + personNot she said me. She told me, or she said to me.

Never: She said me · She said she can not decide · He said they will send it · Paula told that the document was ready
