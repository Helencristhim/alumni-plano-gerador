Stage 1.1: The Day After

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes. "Show off" is one expression: learn it together.

Congratulate — to tell somebody you are happy about their success
"My teachers congratulated me this morning."
Attention — when a lot of people look at you and talk about you
"All this attention is a bit strange."
Show off — to try to make people see how good you are
"Don't show off at school."
Replay — a video that shows an action again
"I watched the replay twenty times."
Rival — a person who wants to win the same thing as you
"Finn is my biggest rival now."
Exhausted — very, very tired
"I was exhausted after the long drive."
Embarrassed — feeling shy or a little stupid because people are looking at you
"I was embarrassed when the class clapped."
Thrilled — very happy and excited
"Dana is thrilled with my result."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read Renato's message to his friend Lucas in Brazil, on Monday night. Then answer the questions.

Lucas, what a day! I was exhausted this morning. The drive home on Sunday was really tiring, and we got home at nine. At school, everybody wanted to congratulate me. My teachers were excited, and some kids watched the replay on their phones. They said it was amazing! Honestly, all this attention is a bit embarrassing, and I don't want to show off. After school, Kai told me that Finn is training hard. He's my rival now, and he was disappointed on Sunday. I'm thrilled about the semifinal, but I'm also a bit nervous. Dana says the date might be announced this week. Tonight I'm going to bed early. I'm not bored, just tired!
"The drive home was really tiring." What does Renato mean?
A The car was tired, so they stopped many times on the road back to Perth.
B The drive made him feel tired.
Why is the attention embarrassing for Renato?
A Everybody is looking at him and talking about him.
B Because he lost the quarterfinal, and the kids at school are laughing at him today.
Is the date of the semifinal certain?
A Yes. It was announced on Sunday after the heat, by the judges on the tower.
B No. It might be announced this week.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

-ed and -ing adjectives

Use -ed for how a person feels. Use -ing for the thing that makes you feel that way. The heat was exciting, so I was excited.

-ed: the feeling

I was excited.
We were tired.
Kai was surprised.

-ing: the thing

The heat was exciting.
The trip was tiring.
The score was surprising.

Positive: The replay is amazing.  ·  Negative: I wasn't bored.  ·  Question: Were you surprised?
Remember: "I'm bored" is a feeling. "I'm boring" means other people feel bored with you! After interested, use in: interested in the semifinal.
Your voice: "excited" ends in an "id" sound (ex-SY-tid), but "tired" has only one syllable.

Stage 4: Monday at school

Quiz
A girl in your class says: "Your wave was amazing!" Answer her without showing off.
A Thanks! I was really surprised.
B I know. I'm the best surfer in the club now, and nobody can beat me.
Bev asks how the drive home was.
A It was really tired, and the road was very long and dark at night.
B It was really tiring.
Tom says surf contests are boring. What do you say?
A No way! They're really exciting.
B No way! They're really excited, especially in the last minute of a heat.
You slept eleven hours after the trip. Why?
A I was exhausting after the long trip home from Margaret River.
B I was exhausted.
Kai asks: "Who is Finn to you now?"
A He's my rival.
B He's my coach, and he helps me with my turns every day.

Stage 5: A day to remember

Reflection
Think about a day that was exciting and tiring at the same time. Record 30 seconds: what happened, how was the day, and how did you feel? Check one thing before you send it: -ed for your feeling (I was excited), -ing for the thing (the day was exciting).
