Grammar Discovery

The feeling, or the thing?

The heat was exciting. Renato was excited.
The drive home was tiring. Everybody was tired.
Kai: "The replay is amazing. The crowd was amazed."
Renato: "All this attention is embarrassing. I'm embarrassed."

What do the orange words have in common? Which ones are about a person, and which ones are about a thing?

-ed = how a person feels · -ing = the thing that makes you feel it

The heat was exciting, so I was excited. The thing is -ing. The person who feels it is -ed.

The Code

Two endings, two jobs

-ed: the person feels it

I was excited.
We were tired.
The class was bored.
Kai was surprised.
Are you interested in surf?

-ing: the thing causes it

The heat was exciting.
The trip was tiring.
The lesson was boring.
The score was surprising.
Surf is interesting.
Watch out: "I'm bored" is a feeling. "I'm boring" means other people feel bored with you! A person can be -ing too: "Finn is an interesting surfer."
Sound Check

Three -ed sounds

I was so excited.

"excited" ends in an "id" sound: ex-SY-tid

We were tired after the trip.

"tired" has only one syllable: TIRED

I was really embarrassed.

"embarrassed" ends in a "t" sound: em-BA-rist
