Lesson 29 -- Pre-class

Making Connections at a Conference

Get ready for a chemical industry conference in Houston: a welcome reception, a mutual contact, and thirty seconds to present your company. Learn to add extra information about people, companies, and places ("Greg, who works in New York, ...", "my company, which ..."). Key words: name badge, keynote, panel, breakout session, elevator pitch, counterpart, mutual contact, to put someone in touch, to reach out, to be based in.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Name badge -- a card with your name and company that you wear at an event
"Please wear your name badge at all times."
Keynote -- the main talk at a conference, usually given by an important speaker
"The keynote starts at nine in the main hall."
Panel -- a group of experts who discuss a topic in front of an audience
"Tom is speaking on a panel about supply chains."
Breakout session -- a smaller meeting at a conference about one specific topic
"Which breakout session are you going to after lunch?"
Elevator pitch -- a very short description of you or your company, about thirty seconds long
"I practiced my elevator pitch on the plane."
Counterpart -- a person who has the same job as you, but in another company or office
"My counterpart in Mexico runs the same team as me."
Mutual contact -- a person that two people both know
"We have a mutual contact: Greg, from New York."
To put someone in touch -- to give two people each other's contact details so they can talk
"Greg put me in touch with a buyer in Houston."
To reach out -- to contact someone, often for the first time
"I'll reach out to her by email next week."
To be based in -- to have your main office or your home in a place
"Our company is based in Sao Paulo."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Lucimara arrives in Houston for ChemConnect Americas, which is the biggest chemical industry conference in the region. At the welcome reception, she sees a name badge she knows: Tom Reyes. Greg, who she met in New York, put her in touch with Tom last month, but she didn't reach out in time.

Tom is friendly. He is based in Houston, where his company has its main office, and he runs purchasing. "My counterpart in Brazil, whose team buys from local suppliers, knows your company," he says. Lucimara gives her elevator pitch, and Tom invites her to his panel the next day, after the keynote. They agree to meet at a breakout session in the afternoon.

1. How does Lucimara know Tom's name?
A Greg, who she met in New York, put her in touch with him last month.
B She read his name in the conference app the night before and sent him a long message.
C Tom is her boss.
2. Who knows Lucimara's company?
A Tom's wife, who works for a company in Sao Paulo and buys ingredients from many suppliers.
B Nobody at the conference.
C Tom's counterpart in Brazil.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "Houston, that is a big city, is hot in the summer and very busy during the conference."
B "Tom, whose team buys ingredients, is based in Houston."
C "Tom, who his team buys ingredients, is nice."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Adding Extra Information (who, which, where, whose)

GRAMMAR

How to add extra information about a person, a company, or a place in one sentence.

WordUseExample
whoextra information about a personGreg, who works in New York, is my mutual contact.
whichextra information about a thing or a companyMy company, which is based in Sao Paulo, makes ingredients.
whereextra information about a placeHouston, where the conference is, is very hot.
whosethe person or company that something belongs toTom, whose team buys our ingredients, runs purchasing.
RuleThe extra information goes between commas, and the sentence still works without it. When you speak, make a small pause at each comma. With commas, never use that: "my company, which makes ingredients", not "my company, that makes ingredients". Never say "who his": say whose. In Lesson 15, there were no commas, because the information was necessary: "the supplier who I met in Lyon".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each real situation at the conference.

At the reception, you want to start a conversation with a stranger. You say:
A "Hi, I'm Lucimara. What brings you to the conference?"
B "Hello. I am Lucimara, and I want to sell you my products today, so please give me your email address now."
C "Your badge. Name?"
Someone asks about your company. You answer:
A "My company, that it is in Sao Paulo and it makes many different ingredients for cosmetics and cleaning products."
B "Company. Sao Paulo. Ingredients."
C "My company, which is based in Sao Paulo, makes ingredients for cosmetics."
Tom says, "My counterpart in Brazil mentioned your company." What does it mean?
A Tom's boss in Brazil, who is very important in the company, wants to stop working with your company next year.
B A person who has the same job as Tom, but in Brazil, talked about your company.
C Tom is moving to Brazil.
After the conference, you want to continue the conversation with Tom. You write:
A "Hi Tom, it was great to meet you in Houston. Could we set up a call next week?"
B "Hi Tom, I am writing this email because I need to tell you that I am back in Sao Paulo now after the trip."
C "Tom. Meeting?"

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Imagine you are at the welcome reception of a conference in Houston. A person from a big U.S. company asks about you. Give your elevator pitch (who you are, where you are based, and what your company does), mention a mutual contact, and suggest a next step. Use who, which, where, or whose at least three times. Take your time.
