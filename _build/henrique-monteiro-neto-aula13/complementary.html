Lesson 13 -- Who Said What

Podcast
"Why Facts Don't Always Change Minds" — Hidden Brain, NPR

Research on why more evidence often entrenches people further, and on what actually shifts a position. Connection to Lesson 13: the episode is a long demonstration of people reporting each other's views — sometimes fairly, sometimes not. Transcript beside the audio.

Tip: listen for fifteen minutes with one job only — note every reporting verb the host uses about the guest, and every one the guest uses about people he disagrees with.

Talk
"Ten Ways to Have a Better Conversation" — Celeste Headlee, TED

A radio interviewer on the mechanics of actually listening, from somebody who does it for a living and admits how often she fails. Connection to Lesson 13: several of her rules are about not putting words in somebody's mouth. Transcript on the page.

Tip: pick the one rule you break most often and try it deliberately in your next conversation in Portuguese. The habit transfers before the English does.

Grammar Video
"Reporting Verbs" — Advanced B2+/C1 Grammar

The full set of patterns: which verbs take that, which take -ing, which take a person plus a preposition. Connection to Lesson 13: the grammar kept outside the class, as you asked, and it comes with a free written summary.

Tip: do not try to learn thirty verbs. Choose the eight you would actually use in an argument and write one sentence with each. Eight is the right number.
