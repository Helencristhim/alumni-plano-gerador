Chapter 4: The Code

How Much? How Many?

Countable and uncountable

Grammar Discovery

Four Sentences From a Data Meeting

"How many leads did the campaign bring?"

"How much traffic comes from Google?"

"The client has a lot of data, but only a few insights."

"We have a little money for paid media this month."

Look at the blue words. You can count leads and insights: one lead, two leads. You cannot count traffic, data or money in this way. Now the red words: many and a few go with things you count; much and a little go with things you do not count.

JobFormExample
Countable (leads, clients, visits)How many...? · a fewHow many leads? Only a few clients.
Uncountable (data, traffic, money, time)How much...? · a littleHow much traffic? A little money.
Botha lot ofA lot of leads. A lot of data.
Negative and questionsmuch / manyWe don’t have much time. Do you have many clients?
Data, information, feedbackno -s, singular verbThe data is clear. Some information.
The trapno plural for uncountable wordsNot datas or informations. Say data, information.

In one line: many and a few for things you count; much and a little for things you do not count; a lot of for both.

Listening 1 · The Monthly Report

Tom Reads the Numbers

A data analyst in London explains one month of numbers to a client. Sound first — no text.

1. How much traffic did the website get in September?
About eighty thousand visits.
2. How many leads did the campaigns bring?
Only four hundred.
3. What is Tom’s insight?
The form has too many questions, so people leave.
Listening 2 · The Funnel

Priya’s Funnel

Priya, a marketing manager in India, talks about the funnel of her online store. Sound first — no text.

1. How many visitors did the store have last month?
About fifty thousand.
2. Where do a lot of people leave the funnel?
On the payment page.
3. What does Priya say the store needs?
A better payment page, not more traffic.
