Chapter 3: The Code

Wish, If Only, Would Rather

talking about what you would change, now and in the past

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I wish we had more time for due diligence."

"I wish we had asked for the audit earlier."

"If only the client would answer our emails."

"It's time we changed the way we check our partners."

What do the orange words have in common? Are they about now or about the past?

FormWhat it doesExample
wish + past simpleA situation NOW that you would like to be different. The past form means "not real".I wish we had more lawyers in Texas.
wish + past perfectA REGRET about the past. It cannot change now.I wish I had read the side letter.
wish + would + base formAnnoyance: you want ANOTHER person (or thing) to change. Not about yourself.I wish they would stop moving the deadline.
if only + the same tensesThe same as wish, but stronger and more emotional.If only we had known about the lawsuit.
would rather + subject + past simpleA polite preference about what ANOTHER person does.I'd rather you didn't send it yet.
it's (high) time + subject + past simpleSomething should already be happening, and it is late.It's time we updated the checklist.

One step back in time: past for now, past perfect for the past. And never "I wish I would have known": the regret is "I wish I had known".

Meaning

One Regret, Four Moves

The same post-mortem, from the regret to the change

"I wish I had read the side letter before the call."

A regret about the past. You take your part of the responsibility.

"If only we had one more week."

A wish about now, with more feeling. Use it once, not every sentence.

"I'd rather we didn't blame the sales team."

A polite way to stop the blame game without sounding like a boss.

"It's time we checked every partner before the term sheet."

The change. Without this sentence, the post-mortem is only a complaint.

Chapter 4: The Post-Mortem

Two Days Before Signing

Laura Brooks, from the US office, and the call nobody wanted to schedule

Dialogue

The Call Nobody Wanted

L
Thanks for joining, Pedro. I know nobody enjoys a post-mortem, but the Houston deal fell through on Friday, and I'd like to understand why before the board asks.
P
Of course. Honestly, I wish we had started due diligence earlier. We found the problem with their distributor two weeks before signing, and by then everybody was emotionally invested.
L
I keep second-guessing the timeline myself. If only I hadn't promised the board a closing date in June.
P
We all made that call together, so let's not dwell on it. The real issue is the process. It's time we checked every intermediary before the term sheet, not after.
L
Agreed. I'd rather we lost a deal in the first month than in the last week. What else should we take on board?
P
That the sales team bore the brunt of it. They had spent six months on this client. I wish someone would tell them it wasn't their fault, because it wasn't.
L
I'll call them myself this afternoon. It was a wake-up call, but it doesn't have to be a disaster.
P
Exactly. It's water under the bridge now. We learned the hard way, so let's make sure we only learn it once.

1. When did the Houston deal fall through, according to Laura?
