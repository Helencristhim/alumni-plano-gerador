Chapter 3: The Code

I Handle... I’m Handling...

What is always true, and what is true now

Grammar Discovery

Which Sentences Are About Now?

"I work in credit recovery."

"I’m starting a new job this month."

"Our firm represents banks and large companies."

"This week we’re preparing a case for a court in Florida."

Look at the blue verbs: they are always true, a routine or a fact about your job. Now look at the red verbs: they talk about now, this week, this month. Blue = the simple present. Red = am / is / are + -ing.

JobFormExample
Routine, permanent factI / you / we / they + verb; he / she / it + verb + s“Our firm represents banks.”
Now, this week, this montham / is / are + verb-ing“I’m preparing a case this week.”
Questions about a routineDo / Does + subject + verb?“Do you handle international cases?”
Questions about nowAm / Is / Are + subject + verb-ing?“Are you working on the Florida case?”
Signal wordsusually, always, every day x now, at the moment, this month“I usually work alone, but this month I’m working with the U.S. team.”
The trapsome verbs do not take -ing for a state: know, need, owe, wantNot “He is owing two million.” Say He owes two million.

In one line: always or usually = I work; now or this month = I’m working.

Listening 1 · A U.S. Attorney

A Week in a Collections Firm

An American attorney describes his normal week and what is different this month. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he usually do on Mondays?
He reads the new cases and calls the clients.
2. Who does his firm usually represent?
Creditors: banks, hospitals and small businesses.
3. What is different this month?
He is working on a case with a team in Brazil, so he is having meetings early in the morning.
Listening 2 · A London Lawyer

From Local Cases to International Ones

A British lawyer remembers the first months of her international job. Sound first — no text.

1. What kind of cases did she handle before?
Only local cases, in England.
2. What was the most difficult part at the start?
The meetings: people spoke fast and she was afraid to ask questions.
3. What is her advice for a new international lawyer?
Ask questions, even simple ones; everybody is learning.
Listening 3 · A Credit Manager

Why Debts Grow

A credit manager from a bank in India explains what happens when a client does not pay. Sound first — no text.

1. What does the bank do first when a client stops paying?
It calls the client and tries to find a solution.
2. Why does the outstanding debt grow every month?
Because of interest and fees.
3. When does the bank send the case to a law firm?
After ninety days without a payment.
