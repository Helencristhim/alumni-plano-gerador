Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Credit recovery -- the work of getting back money that people or companies did not pay
"I work in credit recovery for banks and large companies."
A creditor -- a person or a company that lends money and has the right to receive it back
"Our client is the creditor: the bank lent the money."
A debtor -- a person or a company that has to pay money back
"The debtor moved to Florida two years ago."
To owe -- to have to pay money to someone
"The company owes our client two million dollars."
An outstanding debt -- money that someone has to pay and has not paid yet
"The outstanding debt is growing every month."
A law firm -- a company of lawyers that works for clients
"I’m starting at a new law firm next week."
To represent -- to act for a client, in court or in a negotiation
"We represent the creditors in this case."
A case -- a legal situation that a court or a lawyer works on
"This case started in Brazil, but the assets are in the U.S."
To handle -- to be responsible for something and deal with it
"I handle about forty cases at the same time."
A deadline -- the last day or time to do something
"The deadline for the documents is Friday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Ana is a lawyer, and she works in credit recovery. She usually represents banks: the bank is the creditor, and the company that does not pay is the debtor. On a normal day, she reads new files, calls clients and checks every deadline.

But this month is different. She is starting a new job at a law firm with international clients. Right now, the team is working on a case with a company that owes three million dollars. The money is in Florida, so the outstanding debt is a problem for a court in the United States.

This week, Ana is reading the documents and is preparing a summary for the Miami office. She handles many cases, but this is her first one in English.

1. Why does the text say “She usually represents banks” and not “She is representing banks”?
A Because it is a routine, a normal part of her job.
B Because the sentence is about this week only, and the simple present is the form we use for one week.
C Because represent is a verb that never takes -ing.
2. Who is the debtor in the case that the team is working on now?
A The bank that lent the money.
B The Miami office, because it is going to pay for the court in the United States.
C A company that owes three million dollars.
3. What is Ana doing this week?
A She is calling all the debtors in Florida and visiting the court in Miami for her new job.
B She is reading the documents and preparing a summary.
C She is looking for a new job.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Simple Present x Present Continuous

GRAMMAR

Use the simple present for routines and permanent facts about your job. Use am / is / are + -ing for now, this week, this month, or a change that is happening. Some verbs of state (know, need, owe, want) stay in the simple present.

JobFormExample
Routine, permanent factI / you / we / they + verb; he / she / it + verb + s“Our firm represents banks.”
Now, this week, this montham / is / are + verb-ing“I’m preparing a case this week.”
Questions about a routineDo / Does + subject + verb?“Do you handle international cases?”
Questions about nowAm / Is / Are + subject + verb-ing?“Are you working on the Florida case?”
Signal wordsusually, always, every day x now, at the moment, this month“I usually work alone, but this month I’m working with the U.S. team.”
The trapsome verbs do not take -ing for a state: know, need, owe, wantNot “He is owing two million.” Say He owes two million.

Never: I am work in... · He is owing... · Do you working...? · This week I prepare...
