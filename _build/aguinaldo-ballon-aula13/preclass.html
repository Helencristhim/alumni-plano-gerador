Lesson 13 -- Pre-class

Writing That Sounds Like You -- Short and Connected

Professional emails and memos in English: short sentences that still connect. Key words: concise, wordy, to proofread, a bullet point, a subject line, register, a takeaway, a run-on sentence, to flow, a linking word. Structure: this + summary noun, moreover, in addition.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Concise -- short and clear, with no unnecessary words
"Her memo was concise: one page, three points."
Wordy -- using more words than necessary
"The first draft was too wordy, so I cut it in half."
To proofread -- to read a text carefully to find and correct mistakes
"Could you proofread my email before I send it?"
A bullet point -- a short item in a list, with a small dot before it
"Put the three risks in bullet points."
A subject line -- the short title of an email
"A good subject line tells the reader why the email matters."
Register -- the level of formality of a text, from formal to informal
"The register of a thesis is more formal than the register of an email."
A takeaway -- the main point that the reader should remember
"The takeaway is simple: the returns are safe."
A run-on sentence -- a very long sentence with too many ideas and no clear stops
"Cut every run-on sentence into two or three sentences."
To flow -- to move easily from one idea to the next
"With good links, the paragraph starts to flow."
A linking word -- a word like moreover or however that connects ideas
"Use a linking word at the start of the second sentence."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Aguinaldo writes an email to the investors of a fund. His first version has one run-on sentence of ninety words. A colleague reads it and says it is too wordy.

He cuts it into three short sentences: "The regulator froze the tariff. This decision damaged investor confidence. Moreover, it was the second freeze in five years." Then he adds a list: "In addition to the guarantee, the bank asked for collateral."

Now the email is concise and it flows. He puts the takeaway in the subject line and asks his colleague to proofread it. Notice: this + a noun points back, and moreover adds a new point.

1. What was the problem with his first version?
A The email had too many short sentences and too many bullet points for investors.
B It had one very long sentence.
C The email was written in a register that was much too informal for a fund.
2. In "This decision damaged investor confidence", what does "This decision" refer to?
A A decision that the investors will probably make next year about the fund.
B The tariff freeze.
C The decision of the colleague to proofread the email before lunch.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "In addition to the bank asked for collateral and a guarantee."
B "In addition, the bank asked for collateral."
C "In addition to the bank it asked for collateral and a guarantee."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- This + Noun, Moreover and In Addition

GRAMMAR

Write short sentences, then connect them: this or these + a summary noun points back; moreover and in addition add a new point.

JobFormExample
Point back to a whole ideaThis / These + summary nounThis decision damaged confidence.
Add a stronger point (formal)Moreover, / Furthermore, + sentenceMoreover, it has been respected.
Add a point (neutral)In addition, + sentenceIn addition, the bank asked for collateral.
Add inside one sentenceIn addition to / As well as + noun or -ingAs well as asking for a guarantee, they want collateral.
Choose a precise summary nounthis decision / this approach / this problem / these changesNot only this: say this approach.
The trapin addition to and as well as never take a full sentenceNot In addition to the bank asked. Say In addition, the bank asked.

Never: In addition to the bank asked · These decision · As well as ask for a guarantee · one sentence of ninety words joined with commas

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer a real speaker would give.

Your email has one sentence of ninety words. The best thing to do is:
A "Cut it into short sentences."
B "Add more commas, so the reader can breathe in the middle of the long sentence."
C "Translate it again from Portuguese, word by word, with a better dictionary."
You want to point back to a tariff freeze. You write:
A "This damaged confidence a lot, and the investors were not happy about it at all."

A "But, the contract has been respected for twenty years by all governments."
B "Moreover, it has been respected for twenty years."
