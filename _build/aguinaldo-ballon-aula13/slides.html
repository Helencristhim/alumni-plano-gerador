Chapter 3: The Code

Short Sentences, Strong Links

This decision, moreover and in addition

Grammar Discovery

How Do Short Sentences Stay Connected?

"The tariff was frozen for three years. This decision damaged investor confidence."

"The contract is clear. Moreover, it has been respected for twenty years."

"In addition to the guarantee, the bank asked for collateral."

"Delays, currency and politics. These risks can be managed."

Look at This decision and These risks. What do they point back to? Now look at Moreover and In addition to. Do they add a new idea, or a contrast? And what comes after In addition to: a full sentence or a noun?

JobFormExample
Point back to a whole ideaThis / These + summary nounThis decision damaged confidence.
Add a stronger point (formal)Moreover, / Furthermore, + sentenceMoreover, it has been respected.
Add a point (neutral)In addition, + sentenceIn addition, the bank asked for collateral.
Add inside one sentenceIn addition to / As well as + noun or -ingAs well as asking for a guarantee, they want collateral.
Choose a precise summary nounthis decision / this approach / this problem / these changesNot only this: say this approach.
The trapin addition to and as well as never take a full sentenceNot In addition to the bank asked. Say In addition, the bank asked.

In one line: this or these + a summary noun points back; moreover and in addition add a new point.

Chapter 4: Real Speed

Writing, at Real Speed

One conversation, three recordings, one page

Dialogue

Your Email, Edited

L
I read your email to the investors. The content is excellent, but some sentences are very long.
A
I know. In Portuguese, a long sentence sounds intelligent. In English, it sounds wordy.
L
Exactly. Your second paragraph is one run-on sentence. Try to cut it into three.
A
Okay. But how do I connect the three sentences? Should I repeat the decision every time?
L
No. Say this decision once, then use moreover for the next point. That way the text will flow.
A
That makes sense. And the subject line? I just wrote Information.
L
That tells them nothing. Put the takeaway there: Tariff decision, no impact on returns.
A
Perfect. I will rewrite it now, and then could you proofread it before lunch?
Comprehension

About Laura

1. What does Laura think about the content of the email?
It is excellent.
2. What does Laura suggest for the second paragraph?
To cut the long sentence into three sentences.
3. What does Laura want in the subject line?
The takeaway: tariff decision, no impact on returns.
Listening 1 · The Editor

What Editors Look For

The editor of a law journal explains what makes a paper easy to read. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the most common problem she sees in papers from international authors?
Sentences that are too long.
2. How many ideas should a paragraph have, according to her?
One main idea.
3. What does she say about the word this alone?
It is often not clear. A summary noun, like this decision, is clearer.
Listening 2 · The Lawyer

I Used to Write Like a Judge

A Brazilian lawyer in New York remembers how he changed his writing. Sound first — no text.

1. How long were his sentences when he arrived in New York?
Some of them were eighty words long.
2. What did his partner do with his first memo?
She sent it back with one comment: too long, where is the takeaway?
3. What is his rule now?
The takeaway goes in the first sentence, and each sentence has one idea.
