Lesson 05 -- Pre-class

Where Am I? -- Finding My Way

In lessons 1 to 4 you learned to answer: who you are, what you do, what other people do. In this lesson you learn to ask — and to find your way alone. Six places (reception, elevator, floor, meeting room, entrance, bathroom), there is / there are, and four prepositions of place (in, on, at, next to). It is also the first lesson with Travel English: half Zurich office, half airport. This Pre-class is only a first contact: come to class with there is in your ear, not on the tip of your tongue.

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions. The words in bold are the grammar of this lesson — look at when English uses is and when it uses are.

Sunday evening. David sends Paula a message, the night before her first day.

"Welcome, Paula. Let me tell you about the building. The entrance is on Avenida Paulista. There is a reception on the first floor — say your name there. The elevator is next to the stairs."

"Our team is on the second floor. There are three meeting rooms there, and there is a coffee area next to them. Is there a bathroom on our floor? Yes, there is — next to the elevator. But there isn't a parking space for visitors, so come by taxi."

Paula reads the message twice and counts: one reception, three meeting rooms, no parking space. (Notice: David had to choose every time — there is for one thing, there are for several, there isn't for none. That choice is the whole lesson.)

1. Why did David write "there are three meeting rooms" and not "there is"?
A Because meeting rooms are big places.
B Because there are three — two or more things ask for are. One thing asks for is.
C Because the sentence is in the past.
2. Where is the bathroom?
A On the first floor, next to the reception.
B On the team floor (the second one), next to the elevator.
C There is no bathroom in the building.
3. The message says "a reception on the first floor". Why on, and not in?
A Because a floor always asks for on in English — “on the first floor”.
B Because the reception is outdoors.
C Because on and in are the same — it makes no difference.
4. David wrote "there isn't a parking space". What does that mean?
A That the parking is not free.
B That there is no space for visitors — that is why he told her to take a taxi.
C That the parking is on the second floor.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- There is / There are + prepositions of place (in, on, at, next to)

GRAMMAR

To say that something exists, English makes you choose between two forms: there is for one thing, there are for two or more. That choice is the only real difficulty in this lesson — and it is not about understanding, it is about speed.

FormInglêsWhen you use it
One thingThere is a reception on the first floor.One thing exists — so it is is.
Two or moreThere are three meeting rooms.More than one — so it is are.
NegativeThere isn't a bathroom here. / There aren't any taxis.To say that something does not exist.
QuestionIs there a bathroom? / Are there any meeting rooms?To ask if something exists.
Short answer (yes)Yes, there is. / Yes, there are.Tem.
Short answer (no)No, there isn't. / No, there aren't.The short way to say no.

To ask, you only swap the two words around. “There is a bathroom” → “Is there a bathroom?” No new word comes in, the verb does not change, and there is no do or does. It is the cheapest question in English — and the one you will use most at the airport.

The most common mistake here: using have for something that exists. “Have a bathroom here?” is not English — have means to own (I have a car). For something that simply exists, it is always there is / there are. Once you see that difference, it stays with you.

The four prepositions (they say WHERE): on = on top of, and what a floor always asks for: “on the second floor” (never “in the second floor”). in = inside, with walls around you: “in the meeting room”. at = a point of reference, with no walls in mind: “at the reception”, “at the entrance”. next to = beside: “next to the elevator”.

Why this matters: “There is a bathroom” says that it exists. “Next to the elevator” says where — and the second part is what gets you there. On your first day at Zurich, and at Miami airport, the full sentence is the difference between finding the place and walking in circles.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each real situation — the first day at Zurich, Miami airport, the hotel.

First day. You need to find the bathroom. You say:
A "Have a bathroom here?"
B "Excuse me, is there a bathroom on this floor?"
C "Bathroom?" (nothing more, just pointing)
A colleague asks: "Are there any meeting rooms on this floor?" There are three. You answer:
A "Yes, there is. Three meeting rooms."
B "Yes, there are. There are three meeting rooms on this floor."
C "Yes, they have three."
You want to say that your team is on the second floor. The right sentence is:
A "My team is in the second floor."
B "My team is on the second floor."
C "My team is at the second floor."
Miami airport, on your own. You cannot find the gate. You say:
A "Excuse me, can you help me? Where is the gate?"
B "Where have the gate?"
C (you say nothing and look for it alone for an hour)
"Is there a coffee area on the first floor?" There is not — it is on the second. You answer:
A "No, there aren't."
B "No, there isn't. There is a coffee area on the second floor."
C "No, it doesn't have."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record your answer. Speak for 60 seconds, with no script. Do not redo it more than twice. This is exactly the exercise you will do in class — and the homework audio afterwards.

Describe a REAL place from your life in English: your house, your apartment, or the office where you work today. Use "There is" and "There are" at least four times, and at least two prepositions of place (in, on, at, next to). For example: "There is a kitchen next to the living room. There are two bathrooms." It does not have to be perfect — if you swap is for are, keep going and carry on speaking. If you get stuck, use the rescue kit from lesson 1.
