Lesson 5 -- Where Am I? Finding My Way

Film
The Terminal (2004) · Paramount+ — included in the subscription

A man arrives alone at a huge airport, in another country, and he hardly speaks English. He does not understand the announcements, he does not know where anything is, and people speak too fast. That man is Viktor, played by Tom Hanks — and the whole film happens inside an airport. We chose it for one very specific reason: Viktor manages. He points, he asks the wrong way, he repeats, he makes mistakes in front of everybody — and he learns English right there, in the middle of the terminal, with no class and no shame. It is today's lesson turned into a film: people who ask do not stay lost.

Tip: watch it with English subtitles — the goal here is not to understand everything. Do ONE thing only: every time Viktor (or anybody else) asks where something is, hit pause and repeat the question out loud. Then look around the airport on the screen and build a sentence of your own: “There is a restaurant.” “There are many gates.” You do not have to finish the film — if you watch only the first 30 minutes, the task is done.

Podcast
Espresso English Podcast — “There IS or there ARE an apple and two bananas on the table?” (~3 min, free)

Three minutes, American English, and the subject is exactly the grammar of this lesson. It is the same teacher as the lesson 1 episode (Shayna) — on purpose: you already know her voice and her pace, so your ear is free for the content. She takes the one case we did not cover in class, the most confusing of all: what happens when there is one thing AND several things in the same sentence? (“...an apple and two bananas...”) The answer is simple and worth hearing from her.

Tip: listen the first time without stopping, just to let your ear catch the rhythm — you understand more than you think, listening is your strength. The second time, pause after each example and say YOUR version out loud, using your own office: “There is a reception and three meeting rooms.” The episode is short on purpose: you can listen to it twice on the way to work. Open and free, with no sign-up to listen.

YouTube
IN / ON / AT — Prepositions of PLACE · JForrest English (~12 min, with a free quiz)

Jennifer is American and teaches exactly the second half of this lesson: the prepositions that say WHERE. She goes straight to the point that confuses learners most — why it is “on the second floor” and not “in the second floor”. American English, clear and unhurried, and she shows every example written on the screen as she speaks. This is the video that stops prepositions from being guesswork.

Tip: watch it once all the way through, without taking notes. The second time, pause at each of her examples and say YOUR version out loud, swapping her example for your own building: “My team is on the second floor. I am in the meeting room. I am at the reception.” At the end there is a free quiz — do it, and bring to class the question you got wrong. The channel is open, with no sign-up to watch.
