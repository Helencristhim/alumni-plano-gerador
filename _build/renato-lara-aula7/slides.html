Grammar Discovery

Decided, or just an idea?

I am going to take two boards.
Maybe I buy a board there. I don't know yet.
She is going to sign up for the surf team.
Look at that sky. It is going to rain.

Already decided → going to

Two uses, one form: a plan you already made, and a prediction you can SEE coming. And notice the shape: it is the verb to be again — am / is / are + going to + bare verb.

The Code

The same small verb, again

The form

I am going to take two boards.
He is going to meet me there.
We are going to share a room.
I am not going to buy a new one.
Are you going to sign up?

When to use it

a decision you already made
I'm going to sign up for the surf team.
a prediction you can see coming
Look at that sky. It's going to rain.

The verb after going to is bare: going to take, going to sign up — never "going to takes".

Sound Check

Nobody says going to slowly

I'm going to take two boards.

in real speech it sounds like "I'm gonna"

What are you going to do first?

four words become two sounds

It's going to be a long flight.

say it fast — but write it in full, always

Did You Catch It?

The plan

1. Why is he going to arrive two weeks early?
Because the jet lag is going to destroy him for three days, so he wants time before term one starts.
2. What is he not going to do there, and why?
He is not going to buy anything, because his budget is tiny.
3. How is he going to meet people?
He is going to sign up for the school surf team in the first week.
Listening 1

Arrival day, step by step

1. Who is going to meet him at the airport, and how will he know them?
Somebody from the programme, with a blue folder and his name on it.
2. What are they going to do before the house?
Go to the school, get his timetable and his student card.
3. What is the advice about the first night?
Don't sleep in the afternoon. Stay awake until nine, or the jet lag lasts a week.
Listening 2

Advice from somebody who did it

1. What does he say about the first two weeks?
Everybody gets homesick, and it is normal. It passes.
2. What is his advice about the phone?
Don't call home every day. Once a week is enough, or you never arrive.
3. What does he say you should sign up for?
Anything with a team, even a sport you are bad at — that is where the friends are.
