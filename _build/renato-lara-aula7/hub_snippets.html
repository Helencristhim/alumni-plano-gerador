The Plan

Eight travel words (boarding pass, luggage, jet lag, term, sign up, homesick, budget, look forward to) and "going to" for plans that are already decided.

Stage 1.1: Travel Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Boarding pass — the ticket that lets you get on the plane
"Keep your boarding pass on your phone."
Luggage — the bags you take on a trip
"My board bag counts as luggage."
Jet lag — the tired feeling after a very long flight
"The jet lag is going to be brutal."
Term — one part of the school year in Australia
"Term one starts at the end of January."
Sign up — to put your name on a list to join something
"I am going to sign up for the surf team."
Homesick — sad because you miss your home and your family
"Everybody is homesick in the first two weeks."
Budget — the money you have for something
"My budget for the whole term is small."
Look forward to — to feel happy about something that is coming
"I am looking forward to the first session."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the plan. Then answer the questions.

It is official. I am going to arrive on the twelfth of February, two weeks before term one starts. My host family are going to meet me at the airport. The flight is twenty-four hours, so the jet lag is going to be brutal — I am not going to sleep in the afternoon on the first day. I am going to take two boards and one bag, and I am not going to buy anything there, because my budget is tiny. In the first week I am going to sign up for the school surf team. That is how I am going to meet people. Everybody says you get homesick at the start, and I probably am going to feel it too, but I am really looking forward to the first session.
Why is he going to arrive two weeks early?
A Because of the jet lag, before term one starts.
B Because his budget is tiny.
What are the two things he is not going to do?
A Sign up for the team and take two boards.
B Sleep in the afternoon on the first day and buy anything there.
Why is it "going to" and not just the present?
A Because these are decisions he already made about the future.
B Because they are things he does every day.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Going to — the future you already decided

Look at the shape: it is the verb to be again. am / is / are + going to + bare verb.

The form

I am going to take
He is going to meet
We are going to share
I am not going to buy
Are you going to sign up?

When to use it

a decision already made
I'm going to sign up.
a prediction you can see
Look at that sky. It's going to rain.

Never lose the small verb. "I going to take" is broken — it is the same missing am from Lesson 1.
In speech it sounds like gonna. Say it that way if you want, but write it in full.

Stage 4: Talking about February

Quiz
Somebody asks about your plan. It is already decided. What do you say?
A I going to take two boards.
B I'm going to take two boards.
You want to ask a friend about his plans for the weekend. Which question is correct?
A What are you going to do this weekend?
B What do you going to do this weekend?
The sky is black and the wind just changed. What do you say?
A It's going to rain.
B It rains.
You decided NOT to buy a board there. How do you say it?
A I don't going to buy a board.
B I'm not going to buy a board.

Stage 5: Your real February

Reflection
February 2027. Record your real plan for 30 seconds in English. What are you going to take? What are you going to do in the first week? How are you going to meet people? What are you NOT going to do? Check one thing before you send it — every sentence has am, is or are before "going to".
