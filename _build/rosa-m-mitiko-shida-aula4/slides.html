Chapter 3: The Code

Four Small Words

ever · never · already · yet

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Have you ever attended this convention before?"

"I have never handled the operations side of a route."

"I have already met the team from Santiago."

"I have not met the regional director yet."

Two of the four colored words only appear in questions and negatives. And one of them sits at the end of the sentence, not in the middle. Which are they?

WordWhat it doesWhere it goes
everAsks about your whole life up to now. Only in questions.Between the subject and the verb.
"Have you ever worked in Chile?"
neverZero times, ever. It is already negative, so you never add not.Between have and the verb.
"I have never worked in Chile."
alreadyIt happened, and sooner than the other person expected.Between have and the verb.
"I have already sent it."
yetIt has not happened up to now, and it is still expected. Questions and negatives only.At the end of the sentence.
"I have not met him yet."
The trapnever already carries the negative, and ever does not work in a normal statement.NOT "I have not never worked there."
NOT "I have ever worked there."

Four words, and they answer the four questions a stranger will actually ask you in that corridor.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I never have been to this convention."
"I have never been to this convention."
"I have not never negotiated a contract like that."
"I have never negotiated a contract like that."
"I have ever worked with a client in Colombia."
"I have worked with a client in Colombia before."

Never goes after have, not before it, and it is already negative — English does not allow two negatives in one sentence the way Portuguese does. And ever belongs to questions: in a normal statement, the word you want is before. Never after have, yet at the end.

Comprehension

About Andres

1. How did he know she works in commercial before she said so?
Her name badge is blue, and blue means commercial.
2. How many times has he attended this convention?
Three times.
3. What has happened to the client base in Chile, and why?
It has grown a lot, since the new route opened.
4. What does he offer to do before the next session?

Listening 2

Eleven Years of Coffee Breaks

A colleague gives you his one piece of advice. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the one thing he says he has learned in eleven years?
Do not try to meet everybody.
2. What exactly does he tell you to do in the twenty minutes?
Find one person you have never spoken to, ask them something you actually want to know, and stay there.
3. What does he say you should do before you walk away?
Say the conversation was interesting, and ask for their email.

Three Questions in a Row
