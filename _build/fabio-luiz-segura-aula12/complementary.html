Talk
Damon Brown — “How to choose your news” (TED-Ed, under 5 min)

A short animated lesson on how opinion, fact and almost-fact get into a news story, and how a careful reader tells them apart. It is built around exactly the question this lesson ends on: not is this true, but what is the writer doing with it.

Tip: watch it twice. The first time, just listen. The second time, write down every point the narrator makes about what a source leaves out, and turn each one into a sentence with point out, touch on or pick up on.

Podcast
Insights Podcast — “The Global Payments Report 2026: Six trends defining payments” (26 min)

A whole episode spent unpacking one sector report — the methodology behind the survey of 63,000 respondents across 42 markets, and then six trends drawn from it, including wallet fragmentation, domestic schemes against the card networks, and stablecoins as a cross-border layer. The show belongs to The Payments Association, a UK industry body.

Tip: this is the spoken version of what you did on paper today. Listen once and write the six trends in six lines. Then listen again and note where the speakers break a number down, and where they only touch on something and move on.

Video
Joshua Yen — “How to Skim Read from an Oxford Student”

An Oxford undergraduate goes through his own tips for skimming faster and then demonstrates them on a real text, on screen. He is reading academic material rather than vendor reports, but the moves are the same ones you used today: go fast until something snags, then stop and read properly.

Tip: pause the video the moment he starts his live demonstration, skim the text yourself, and compare where he stopped with where you stopped. Then say out loud, in three sentences, what he laid out and what you picked up on.
