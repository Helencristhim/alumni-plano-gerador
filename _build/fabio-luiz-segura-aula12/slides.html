Chapter 4: Where the Object Goes

Two Words, and Something in the Middle

Some of these verbs open up. Others never do

Grammar Discovery

Where Does the Object Go?

"The report breaks the numbers down into three corridors."

"The report breaks them down into three corridors."

"I came across the article on Monday morning."

"She picked up on it before anybody else did."

Two of these verbs let something sit between the words. Two of them never do. Which is which, and what happens when the object is just it or them?

TypeWhere the object goesExample
Separable: break down · point out · lay out · sum upafter the verb or between the two wordsIt points out the risk. · It points the risk out.
Separable + a pronoun (it, them, me)always between the two wordsIt points it out. Never points out it.
Inseparable: come across · skim through · touch onalways after the whole verb, pronoun includedI came across the article. · I came across it.
Three-part: zoom in on · pick up on · keep up withalways after the third wordShe zoomed in on the number. · She zoomed in on it.
No object at allsome of them simply end the sentenceTo sum up, the timeline is the real story.
The stressthe second word carries it, and the pronoun hides in the middlepoint it OUT · break them DOWN · sum it UP

In one line: a long object can go either side of a separable verb, but it and them never sit at the end.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

Three People Who Read for a Living

The method, the trap, and the habit — in three accents

Listening 1 · The Ten Minutes

Almost None of It, on Purpose

He reads the industry for a living and reads almost nothing. Sound first — no text.

1. What is he waiting for while he skims?
For one thing to snag: something that contradicts what he thought last month, or a number that is bigger than it should be.
2. What does he do when something snags?
He stops skimming and zooms in on that one paragraph, and reads it properly, twice.
3. How does he describe what happens to the rest of the pages?
They have been skimmed through and forgotten on purpose, and he says he has never once regretted it.
Listening 2 · What a Release Is Doing

Not Lying. Selecting.

Something he wishes somebody had told him in his first year. Sound first — no text.

1. Why does he say a press release is not information?
Because it is an argument, written by the company it is about and paid for by the same company. It points out every number that helps and stays silent about the one that does not.
2. What does he say growth in percentage terms, with no absolute figure, tells you?
That the absolute figure is small.
3. What is the exercise he recommends after reading?
Make a short list of what a normal person would have mentioned and did not appear. That list is the real story.
Listening 3 · Fifteen Minutes on Monday

A Habit, Not an Event

Why the quiet afternoon never arrives, and what she does instead. Sound first — no text.

1. What do the intelligent people she has watched do wrong?
They save everything for a quiet afternoon that never arrives, and then panic-read four hundred pages the night before a conference.
2. She says what you need is not the information. What is it?
The pattern — and a pattern is only visible if you look at the same thing at regular intervals.
3. What does she say about the things she comes across in those fifteen minutes?
Four or five times a year one of them changes what her team does that quarter, and none of them were ever sent to her by anybody.
Real Document

Ninety Seconds with a Front Page

SECTOR REPORT · FRONT PAGE
Distribution list: Fabio Luiz Segura · illustrative, written for this lesson
Corridor Economics 2027: Why Instant Settlement Has Not Reached the Middle Market
By the research desk of a payments infrastructure vendor · 34 pages · February
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
•Instant settlement volume across the eleven corridors we track grew 41% year on year.
•Adoption remains concentrated in large corporates; the middle market is four years behind.
•The barrier is not technology. Every bank surveyed already has the rails.
•Compliance and reconciliation costs are named as the blocker by 68% of respondents (see page 29).
•Our middleware removes the reconciliation step entirely (see pages 9 to 26).
Illustrative front page, written for this lesson. Every number on it is invented; the shape of the page is not.
1. Say what this page lays out, and where the argument actually is.
"It lays out the whole argument in five bullet points on the front page, and the thirty-four pages behind it exist to defend them."
2. One number is missing. Point out which one, and say what its absence tells you.
"It points out that volume grew 41%, but it never gives the absolute volume — and if the percentage is there without the figure, the figure is small."
3. It touches on one thing in a single line and spends eighteen pages on another. Say what you picked up on.
"It touches on compliance in one bullet and gives its own middleware eighteen pages, so I picked up on the fact that this is a sales document with a research byline."

The Author Is on the Call

Scenario: The vendor who published that report is on a call with you and is proud of it. Role-Play 3 of 3 — Free

The Most Useful Thing You Read This Year

Scenario: Three minutes to a colleague from another bank who has just asked you what is worth reading in this market. Tell them about one real thing you have read this year: where you came across it, what it laid out, what you broke down and told your team, and what it never touched on. Say it the way you would actually say it, not the way you would write it.
