Film
The Two Popes

Two men who disagree about almost everything spend two hours telling each other what the other one should do. It is a whole film made of the grammar you learned today, and the argument is spiritual rather than political — which is the part of it you are most likely to care about.

Tip: English subtitles on. Write down five sentences with should, could or might. They are everywhere, and hearing them inside a real argument is what makes them stick.

Video
BBC Learning English -- "May, might and could" (6 Minute Grammar)

Six minutes on the two lighter words from today’s lesson, the ones that only say something is possible. They also bring in may, which is the more formal cousin of might and is worth recognizing when it shows up in writing.

Tip: pause every time they give an example and say it back before they explain it. If your version and theirs are the same, the rule is already yours.

Talk
Ruth Chang -- "How to make hard choices" (TED)

She is a philosopher, and her argument is the one behind the third recording of today’s lesson: a hard choice is hard precisely because neither option is better, and that is good news rather than bad. Fifteen minutes, slow and very clear.

Tip: English subtitles on. Afterwards, pick one decision you keep putting off and say out loud, in six sentences, what you should do, what you could do instead, and what might go wrong either way.
