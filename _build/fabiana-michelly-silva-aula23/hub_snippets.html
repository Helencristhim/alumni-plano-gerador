Lesson 23 -- Pre-class

Data Storytelling -- The Story Behind the Numbers

Presenting a KPI story to senior leadership. Key words: spike, dip, plateau, baseline, outlier, year over year, slide, bottom out, level off, trajectory, skew, drill down. Structure: narrative tenses -- past simple for the events, past continuous for the background, past perfect for what finished before, and past perfect continuous (had been falling) for a trend that lasted up to a moment in the past.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Spike -- a sudden, short rise in a number
"Freight costs spiked in August, then came back down."
Dip -- a small, short fall in a number
"There was a dip in March, but nothing serious."
Plateau -- a period when a number stays flat after rising
"After eight weeks, the rate reached a plateau at ninety-seven percent."
Baseline -- the normal level you use to compare new results
"The new rate is above our old baseline of ninety-six percent."
Outlier -- a result that is very different from all the others
"June was an outlier: the fire, not the trend."
Year over year -- compared with the same period one year earlier
"Year over year, on-time delivery is up three points."
Slide -- to fall slowly and steadily over time
"The rate had been sliding for two quarters before anyone noticed."
Bottom out -- to reach the lowest point and stop falling
"In June, the rate bottomed out at eighty-four percent."
Level off -- to stop rising or falling and stay at the same level
"The rate climbed for eight weeks and then leveled off."
Trajectory -- the general direction a number is moving in over time
"Tell them the trajectory first, and let the chart confirm it."
Skew -- to distort a result so that it gives the wrong impression
"One freight bill skews the whole quarter."
Drill down -- to look at the detailed data behind a total
"If we drill down, the spike was a one-time cost."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Ninety days after the Monterrey pilot started, Fabiana has ten minutes with the board. She does not read the chart. She tells its story. "Before Penang, our on-time rate had been sliding for two quarters, and nobody had noticed. In June, while we were still relying on one plant, the fire hit and the rate bottomed out at eighty-four percent. By the time the pilot started, clients had been waiting eleven days for keyboards."

Four past tenses, one timeline. The past simple moves the story forward: the fire hit, the rate bottomed out. The past continuous is the background: while we were relying on one plant. The past perfect is something finished before another past moment: by the time the review started, Monterrey had shipped forty thousand keyboards. And the past perfect continuous, had been + -ing, is a process that lasted up to that moment: the rate had been sliding for two quarters.

The words do the rest. June was an outlier. After the pilot, the rate climbed and leveled off at ninety-seven percent, above the old baseline, a gain of three points year over year. Freight had a spike in August, but if you drill down, it was a one-time cost, and one number should not skew the picture. Her advice to herself: tell them the trajectory first, and let the chart confirm it.

C Because it happened only once.
2. In "while we were still relying on one plant, the fire hit", which part is the background?
A "while we were still relying on one plant"
B "the fire hit"

3. What does Fabiana do instead of reading the chart?
A She shows more slides.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Narrative Tenses

GRAMMAR

Past simple moves the story forward; the other three tell the listener what was already happening.

TenseJob in the storyExample
Past simplethe main events, in orderThe rate bottomed out in June.
Past continuousthe background: in progress when something happenedCosts spiked while we were moving stock.
Past perfectfinished BEFORE another past momentBy the time the review started, Monterrey had shipped 40,000.
Past perfect continuoushad been + -ing: a process that lasted UP TO another past momentIt had been falling for six months when Penang hit.
Negativehad not been + -ing · had not + past participleWe had not been tracking it weekly.
QuestionHad it been + -ing...? · How long had...?How long had clients been waiting?

Never: The rate has been falling when Penang hit · By then, Monterrey already shipped (use had shipped) · We had been knowing · Clients had been wait
