Chapter 3: Tell It in Order

Which Came First?

Four past tenses, one timeline

Grammar Discovery

Put Them on a Timeline

"On-time delivery had been falling for six months when Penang hit."

"Freight costs spiked while we were moving stock between sites."

"By the time the review started, Monterrey had shipped forty thousand keyboards."

"In June, the rate bottomed out at eighty-four percent."

Which action came first? Which one was in progress? Which one lasted up to another moment in the past?

TenseJob in the storyExample
Past simplethe main events, in orderThe rate bottomed out in June.
Past continuousthe background: in progress when something happenedCosts spiked while we were moving stock.
Past perfectfinished BEFORE another past momentBy the time the review started, Monterrey had shipped 40,000.
Past perfect continuoushad been + -ing: a process that lasted UP TO another past momentIt had been falling for six months when Penang hit.
The trapno continuous with state verbs; no present perfect in a past storyNot had been knowing, not has been falling ... when Penang hit.

In one line: past simple moves the story forward; the other three tell the board what was already happening.

Listening 1 · The Board Review

The Story Behind the Line

Fabiana presents the ninety-day pilot review. Sound first — no text.

1. What had been happening before Penang, and why had nobody noticed?
The on-time delivery rate had been sliding for two quarters, from ninety-six to ninety-one percent. Nobody had noticed because the slide was slow.
2. What happened to the rate after the pilot started?
It climbed for eight weeks and then leveled off at ninety-seven percent, above the old baseline: a gain of three points year over year.
3. Which number skews the picture, and why is it not a concern?
Freight costs spiked in August while they were moving stock between the two sites. If you drill down, it was a one-time cost.
Listening 2 · Marcus Calls Back

The Chart Was Good. The Story Was Better.

A voicemail after the board review. Sound first — no text.

1. Which line did the most, according to Marcus?
When she said the rate had been sliding for two quarters before anyone noticed. The chairman sat up.
2. What does the CFO want, and by when?
He wants to know whether the freight spike could happen again, so she has to send him the drill-down by Friday.
3. What is Marcus's note, and what did the board decide?
At one point she was reading the numbers off the slide; next time she should tell the trajectory first and let the chart confirm it. The board approved the second supplier for all hardware.
