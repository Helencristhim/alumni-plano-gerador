Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example out loud. All eight live in the same place: the line at passport control.

To arrive -- to get to a place at the end of a journey
"We arrived in Miami at six in the morning."
Passport -- the document that proves who you are in another country
"The officer looked at my passport for a long time."
Immigration officer -- the person who decides if you can enter the country
"The immigration officer asked me three questions."
Customs -- the place where they check what you are bringing into the country
"After customs, I finally found my family."
To declare -- to tell the officer what you are carrying with you
"I had nothing to declare, so I said no."
Nervous -- worried about something that is going to happen
"I was nervous in the line, but I said nothing."
On my own -- without help from anybody else
"Next time I want to do it on my own."
Used to -- something that was true before, but is not true now
"My father used to answer for me at immigration."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Last January I travelled to Miami with my family. We arrived at six in the morning and the airport was already full. I was nervous in the line, but I said nothing. When it was our turn, the officer asked three questions and my father answered all of them. I did not say a single word. Then we went through customs, and we had nothing to declare. I used to think that was normal. Now I want to do it on my own.
Why does the text use "arrived", "asked" and "answered" with -ed?
A Because they are happening right now
B Because they are finished actions in the past
C Because the subject is he or she
"I did not say a single word." Why is it "say" and not "said"?
A Because the past is already in "did"
B Because the sentence is about the present
C Because "say" is an irregular verb

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Simple

GRAMMAR

Use the past simple for actions that are finished — they happened and they are over.

regular verbsverb + -edWe arrived at six.
irregular verbsa new word, learned one by onego → went, say → said, buy → bought
negativedid not + base verbI did not understand.
questiondid + subject + base verbDid you buy anything?

Careful 1: after did the verb goes back to normal. "I did not understood" is wrong — it is "I did not understand".

Careful 2: the past does not change with he, she or it. I went, she went, they went — the same word.

Reflection
