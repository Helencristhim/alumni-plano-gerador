Lesson 03 -- Pre-class

Making Your Case -- Structuring a Legal Opinion in Spoken English

A spoken opinion cannot build up to the answer: it has to start with it. Key words: finding, assessment, exposure, precedent, compliance, jurisdiction, remedy, dispute, arbitration, due diligence, caveat, material. Structures: the connectors that carry an argument -- in short for the position, first and second for the reasons, therefore for the consequence, however for the caveat -- and the four-slot shape that holds them together.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the definition. These are the words of an opinion, not of a contract.

Finding -- something you discovered while reviewing the document
"I have three findings, and only one of them is serious."
Assessment -- your professional judgement of the situation
"My assessment is that we should not sign this version."
Exposure -- how much the company can lose if things go wrong
"Our exposure under this clause is the whole contract value."
Precedent -- an earlier decision that is used as a rule for later cases
"There is no precedent for that wording in our group."
Compliance -- following the rules that apply to the company
"The compliance annex is missing from this draft."
Jurisdiction -- the country whose courts can decide the case
"That clause sends every dispute to a jurisdiction we do not know."
Remedy -- what the injured side can ask for when a rule is broken
"The only remedy in this draft is a written apology."
Dispute -- a formal disagreement between the two sides
"Every dispute goes abroad under this clause."
Arbitration -- settling a disagreement with a private judge instead of a court
"Arbitration in Singapore is not a neutral choice for us."
Due diligence -- the checking a company must do before it commits
"Without the annex we cannot prove due diligence."
Caveat -- a warning that limits what you have just said
"One caveat: this changes if they accept our wording."
Material -- important enough to change the decision
"It is a defect, but it is not material."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

A manager asks Ana one question: can the group sign on Friday? She does not start with the history of the document. In short, her assessment is that they should not sign this version. Then come the reasons, and there are two. First, the arbitration clause sends every dispute to a jurisdiction where the group has no local lawyer, so the exposure there is real and the only remedy would be slow. Second, the compliance annex is missing, and therefore the group cannot prove due diligence if a regulator asks. Then, and only then, the caveat. However, neither finding is material if the other side accepts the standard wording, and there is precedent for that in two other countries. So, to come back to the question: not this version, but possibly Friday.

1. Why does the text put "In short, her assessment is..." at the beginning?
A Because a spoken opinion has to give the answer first, before the reasons.
B Because "in short" is a polite way to open any conversation.
C Because the manager has already read the analysis.
2. What is the job of "First" and "Second" in the text?
A They show which reason is more serious.
B They order the reasons, so the listener knows how many are coming.
C They introduce a contrast between two opposite ideas.
3. "The annex is missing, and therefore the group cannot prove due diligence." What does "therefore" introduce?
A The cause of the problem.
B The consequence of the fact just mentioned.
C An exception to the rule.
4. Why does the caveat come after the reasons and not before them?
A Because a caveat placed early makes the whole opinion sound uncertain.
B Because the caveat is less important than the vocabulary.
C Because "however" can only appear at the end of a text.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Connectors of Argument and Sequence

GRAMMAR

Four slots, always in the same order: position, two reasons, one caveat, position again.

ConnectorJobExample
In short
The short answer is		Opens with your position. The answer comes first, never last.In short, we should not sign this version.
First ... Second ...Orders your reasons and tells the listener how many are coming. Two is enough.First, the arbitration clause... Second, the annex...
therefore
as a result
which means		Links a fact to its consequence. It comes before the consequence — because comes before the cause.The annex is missing, and therefore we cannot prove due diligence.
However
That said
One caveat		Introduces the caveat, after the reasons and never in the middle of them.However, none of this is material if they accept our wording.
So, to come back to your questionCloses by repeating the position in the same words you used to open.So, to come back to your question: not this version.
Three trapsNever stack connectors: "But however", "Also moreover". It is on the other hand, not "in the other hand". And a spoken opinion never opens with "It depends" — the dependency belongs in the caveat, with a name.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each situation.

The manager has four minutes and asks: "Can we sign on Friday?" You start with:
A "The short answer is no, not this version. Two reasons, and one caveat."
B "The agreement was signed in 2019 and amended twice, so let me explain the history."
C "It depends a little on the jurisdiction, there are arguments in both directions."
You found six problems, but only two would change the decision. On the call you present:
A All six, in the order you found them.
B The two material ones, and mention that the rest are minor.
C None of them, until the manager asks a direct question.
You want to link a missing annex to what it prevents. Which is correct?
A "Because the annex is missing, therefore we cannot prove due diligence."
B "The annex is missing, and therefore we cannot prove due diligence."
C "Therefore the annex is missing, we cannot prove due diligence."
There is real uncertainty about one point. The best place for it is:
A Spread across every sentence, with "maybe" and "I think".
B In one named caveat after the reasons, with a size and a condition.
C Left out completely, so the opinion sounds stronger.
The manager interrupts your second reason to ask if the problem is real. You say:
A "It is material, and here is why. Let me finish this one and I will come back to your question."
B "Sorry, sorry, my English is not good, let me start again from the beginning."
C "But however, in the other hand, it depends."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

A manager abroad has four minutes and one question: can we sign this contract? Answer in the four slots, in order -- your position, two reasons (with First and Second, and one therefore), one named caveat, and then your position again. Take your time to think before you start, and try to keep the whole answer under thirty seconds.
