Chapter 3: The Code

First, Therefore, However

The words that carry an argument

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"In short, my assessment is that we should not sign this version."

"First, the arbitration clause sends every dispute abroad."

"Second, the compliance annex is missing, and therefore we cannot prove due diligence."

"However, none of this is material if they accept our wording."

Four sentences, one opinion, in order. What job does each orange and purple word do — and where does the answer live?

ConnectorJobExample
In short
The short answer is		Opens with your position. The answer comes first, never last.In short, we should not sign this version.
First ... Second ...Orders your reasons. Two is enough. Seven means you have not decided.First, the arbitration clause... Second, the annex...
therefore
as a result
which means		Links a fact to its consequence. It comes before the consequence — because comes before the cause.The annex is missing, and therefore we cannot prove due diligence.
However
That said
One caveat		Introduces the caveat — and only after the reasons, never in the middle of them.However, none of this is material if they accept our wording.
So, to come back to your questionCloses by repeating the position in the same words you opened with.So, to come back to your question: not this version.
Three trapsNever stack connectors: "But however", "Also moreover". It is on the other hand, not "in the other hand". And a spoken opinion never opens with "It depends" — the dependency belongs in the caveat, with a name.

The shape that never fails: position · two reasons · one caveat · position again.

The Habit

Four Slots, Always the Same

When you know the next slot, you never have to invent the next sentence.

1. Position

"The short answer is no, not this version."

2. Two reasons

"First... Second, and therefore..."

3. One caveat

"However, this changes if..."

4. Position again

"So, to come back to your question: not this version."

Thirty seconds. The room hears the answer twice and the doubt once.

Chapter 4: The Opinion Call

He Needs It Now

One question, thirty seconds, four slots

Dialogue

Can We Sign on Friday?

D
Ana, I need a straight answer. Can the group sign this master agreement on Friday?
A
The short answer is no, not this version. My assessment rests on two findings, and there is one caveat.
D
Go on. I have four minutes before the next call.
A
First, the arbitration clause sends every dispute to a jurisdiction where we have no local counsel, so our exposure there is real. Second, the compliance annex is missing, and therefore we cannot prove due diligence if a regulator asks.
D
And the caveat? Because Friday is not easy to move.
A
However, neither finding is material if they accept our standard wording, and there is precedent for that in two other countries. In that case the remedy is a one-page amendment. So, to come back to your question: not this version, but possibly Friday.
D
That is the first opinion this month I can take to the board without translating it first. Send me those two reasons in writing.
Comprehension

About David

1. What does David ask for in his first sentence?
A straight answer: whether the group can sign the master agreement on Friday.
2. How much time does he have, and why does that matter?
Four minutes, before another call. The whole opinion has to fit inside that.
3. What does he say about the other opinions he has received this month?
That this is the first one he can take to the board without translating it first.
Listening

Give Me the Ending First

A general counsel on the shape of a spoken opinion. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What does he say a good opinion starts with?
The answer itself: yes we can sign, or no we cannot sign yet. Never the history of the document.
2. How many reasons does he want, and what does he think seven reasons mean?
Two. Seven means the lawyer has not decided which ones matter — and deciding is what he is paying for.
3. Is he afraid of the caveat?
No. He is afraid of a lawyer who hides the caveat until the deal is signed.
4. What comparison does he use at the end?
A detective novel — nobody in a board meeting wants the answer saved for the last page.
Listening 2

It Did Not Sound Careful

What hedging sounds like from the other side of the table. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What kind of answers did she use to give?
On the one hand, on the other hand. It depends on the circumstances. Arguments in both directions.
2. What did the director ask her after four minutes?
So what would you do — and she had no answer, because she had just proved no answer was possible.
3. What did he actually think of her, and why is that the surprise?
He thought she was hiding, not being careful. To a business person, hedging sounds like unfinished work or self-protection.
4. Where does she put the uncertainty now?
In the caveat, with a name and a size — not spread everywhere like fog.

The Real Thing

Find the Problem in This Opinion

Legal memorandum
Re: Master services agreement — can we sign?

1. Background. The agreement was first signed in 2019 and amended twice, in 2021 and 2023. The second amendment introduced the current arbitration clause.

2. Analysis. There are six points to consider: the arbitration seat, the missing compliance annex, the notice address, a typo in the recitals, the currency of the cap, and the signature block.

3. Comment. It depends to some extent on the jurisdiction, and there are arguments in both directions.

4. Conclusion. On balance, therefore, we would probably not recommend signing at this stage.

1. Where is the answer, and why is that a problem on a call?
In the very last line, section 4. On a call the manager has already interrupted twice by then — and in an email he may never reach it.
2. Six points are listed. What does that tell the reader?
That nobody decided which ones matter. A typo in the recitals is not material; the missing compliance annex is. Ranking them is the lawyer's job, not the reader's.
3. Rewrite the opening line out loud.
"In short: we should not sign this version. Two reasons — the arbitration seat and the missing compliance annex — and one caveat."

Self-Assessment

What I Can Do Now

I open a spoken opinion with the answer, not with the history of the document.
I give two reasons, ordered with First and Second, and I choose which two.

I put my uncertainty in one named caveat instead of spreading it over every sentence.
I know the words of an opinion: finding, assessment, exposure, jurisdiction, remedy, caveat, material.
