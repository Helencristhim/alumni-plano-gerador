Chapter 3: Can You Count It?

Much or Many, Less or Fewer

Quantifiers with countable and uncountable nouns

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"How much overtime did we pay?"

"How many incidents were there?"

"There were fewer incidents than in August."

"There is very little space left in the warehouse."

Countable (incidents, pallets, people)Uncountable (overtime, money, space)Both

Can you put a number in front of it? Then it takes many, few and fewer. If you cannot, it takes much, little and less.

Chapter 4: The Meeting

Twenty Minutes, Four Areas

Presenting the month and answering the questions

Dialogue

Twenty Minutes, Four Areas

G
Right, Felipe. Where do you want to start?
F
With the good news. Revenue in the stores was up in September.
G
And the margin?
F
Slightly lower, because our costs grew too. Not much, less than one percent.
G
How many incidents in the stores?
F
Only a few. Eleven, and that is fewer than in August.
G
Good. And how much overtime did you pay?
F
Too much. Almost four hundred hours, most of it in the last week.
G
Why so many hours?
F
Two big deliveries arrived on the same day, and there was very little space in the warehouse.
G
Can you bring a plan for October?
F
Yes. Fewer deliveries on Fridays, and a lower stock level before the holidays.
