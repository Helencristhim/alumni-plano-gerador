Numbers in a Results Meeting -- Presenting Figures

The words of a results meeting: revenue, margin, year-on-year, spending, overtime, stock level, to drop, slightly. Structure: much, many, a few, a little, less and fewer, with countable and uncountable nouns. Mission: present your month and answer the questions after.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Revenue -- the money a company receives from selling its products
"Revenue grew in September, but our costs grew faster."
Margin -- the difference between the selling price and the cost, often as a percentage
"A higher margin means more money stays in the company."
Year-on-year -- compared with the same period of the year before
"Shrinkage is down three percent year-on-year."
Spending -- the amount of money that is used on something
"We cut spending on outside cleaning by a fifth."
Overtime -- extra hours that people work after their normal working day
"We paid too much overtime in the last week of the month."
Stock level -- how much product you have in the warehouse at one moment
"Stock levels are higher than we planned for October."
To drop -- to go down, or to become less
"The number of incidents dropped to eleven this month."
Slightly -- a little, not very much
"Maintenance costs were slightly above the budget."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Let me start with the good news. There were only a few incidents in the stores this month, eleven, and that is fewer than in August. We also spent less money on outside cleaning. The bad news is the overtime. We paid a lot of overtime in the last week, almost four hundred hours, because there was very little space in the warehouse.

Notice the pattern. Incidents, people and pallets can be counted, so we say how many, a few and fewer. Overtime, money and space cannot be counted, so we say how much, a little and less. A lot of works with both, and nobody ever says three overtimes.

1. Why does the text say fewer incidents and not less incidents?
A Because incidents can be counted.
B Because the number of incidents went down this month.
C Because fewer is the more formal word and less is the more informal word.
2. Why is it a lot of overtime and not a lot of overtimes?
A Because a lot of is singular.
B Because overtime is a verb.
C Because overtime is uncountable, so it has no plural.
3. What is the problem in the warehouse?
A There were too many incidents and too many people working overtime there.
B There was very little space, so they paid a lot of overtime.
C Cleaning was expensive.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Quantifiers with Countable and Uncountable Nouns

GRAMMAR

Before every number there is one question: can you count it? The answer decides the word that comes before the noun.

FormUse it forExample
how many? / how much?many for things you can count, much for things you cannot.How many incidents? How much overtime?
a few / a littleA small amount, and it is positive.A few incidents · a little space
few / littleNot enough. It sounds negative.There is very little space left.
fewer / lessSmaller number, smaller amount.fewer incidents · less money
a lot ofWorks with both, in every kind of sentence.a lot of pallets · a lot of overtime
too many / too muchMore than you want.too many deliveries · too much overtime

Uncountable nouns never take a plural -s: overtime, spending, money, space, information. And much sounds natural in questions and negatives; in a positive sentence, use a lot of.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are presenting your month in a results meeting. Choose the best thing to say.

Your director wants to know the number of people on overtime. He asks:
A How much people did work overtime in the last week of the month?
B How many people did worked overtime?
C How many people worked overtime?
There were eleven incidents this month and fifteen last month. You say:
A There were fewer incidents than last month.
B There were less incidents than last month, which is very good news for us.
C There were few incidents than last month.
The warehouse is almost full. You say:
A There is very few space in the warehouse for the deliveries next week.
B There is very little space in the warehouse.
C There is a few space in the warehouse.
You paid four hundred hours of overtime. You say:
A We paid a lot of overtime last week.
B We paid a lot of overtimes.
C We paid many overtime.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think about your real last month in one of your four areas. Record about ninety seconds. Start with the good news, then the bad news, then the number you are most worried about. Use real numbers, or numbers close to the real ones. Use at least one how many, one how much, one fewer and one less. Then imagine your director asks why, and answer. Use at least four words from this lesson. Do not stop to correct yourself.
