Stage 1.1: Body Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Shoulder — the part of your body between your neck and your arm
"My shoulder has been sore since Sunday."
Ankle — the joint between your leg and your foot
"He landed badly and twisted his ankle."
Bruise — the dark mark on your skin after you hit something
"I have a bruise on my hip from the reef."
Sore — when a part of your body hurts, usually after using it a lot
"My arms are sore after a long session."
Stretch — to make your muscles longer, slowly, before or after sport
"You should stretch before you paddle out."
Warm up — to move gently for a few minutes before you start properly
"Nobody warms up, and everybody gets hurt."
Physio — the person who helps you recover from an injury
"The physio told me to rest for a week."
Recover — to get better after an injury or an illness
"A shoulder takes about six weeks to recover."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the conversation. Then answer the questions.

My shoulder has been sore since Saturday. There is a big bruise, but I can lift the arm, so it might just be the bruise. Zoe says I should not paddle today. She says I could go to the beach and just watch, or I could stretch and skip one day. If it is still sore on Wednesday, I should see the physio at the club. She also says that I should warm up before every session, because I never do, and that is probably why this happened.
Why does he think it might just be the bruise?
A Because he can lift the arm.
B Because the physio said so.
What are the two options Zoe suggests?
A See a doctor or take medicine.
B Go and watch, or stretch and skip one day.
Why is it "it might just be the bruise" and not "it should be"?
A Because he is guessing, not advising.
B Because "might" is more polite.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Should, could, might

Same shape as every modal you know. What changes is how strong it is.

The three words

should → this is what I advise: You should rest.
could → here is an option: You could try ice.
might → a guess: It might be just a bruise.
shouldn't → do not do it: You shouldn't paddle.

How they behave

No to: you should rest, never "should to rest".
No -s: he should rest, never "he shoulds".
Questions flip: Should I rest?
And you can stack the vocabulary: you should warm up and stretch.

should = real advice. could = a softer option. might = a guess, not advice at all.
No "to", no "-s": he should rest, never "he shoulds" and never "should to rest".
Negative: you shouldn't paddle. Never "you not should".
And the one that matters most: in a real emergency you do not need a modal. You say I need help.

Stage 4: When something hurts

Quiz
You are giving real advice to a friend.
A You should to see a physio.
B You should see a physio.
You are guessing, because you are not sure what it is.
A It might just be a bruise.
B It should just be a bruise.
You want to say that paddling is a bad idea this week.
A You not should paddle this week.
B You shouldn't paddle this week.
You are in the water and something is wrong. What do you say first?
A I need help.
B I think that possibly I should ask somebody for assistance.

Stage 5: Explain an injury

Reflection
