Eight words for when something hurts (cut, bleed, bruise, ankle, swollen, twist, first aid, bandage) and how to say HOW somebody did something: quickly, calmly, carefully, badly, and the rebels well, fast, hard.

Stage 1.1: When Something Hurts

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes. "First aid" is two words, but one idea.

Cut — an opening in your skin made by something sharp
"He has a deep cut on his foot."
Bleed — to lose blood from a cut
"The cut didn't bleed much."
Bruise — a purple or blue mark on your skin after you hit something
"Ollie has a big bruise on his leg."
Ankle — the part of your body between your foot and your leg
"He hurt his ankle on the rocks."
Swollen — bigger than normal, because it is hurt
"His ankle is really swollen."
Twist — to turn a part of your body the wrong way, so it hurts
"Be careful, or you can twist your ankle."
First aid — simple help for a person who is hurt, before a doctor comes
"Mick knows first aid."
Bandage — a long piece of cloth that you put around a cut
"He put a bandage on the cut."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read Renato's message to his mum in Brazil. Then answer the questions.

Hi Mum, don't worry, I'm fine. On Saturday we went to a beach an hour south. The waves were small and the water was glassy. But Ollie paddled to the north end, near the rocks, and the current pulled him really quickly. A wave pushed him onto the rocks and he landed badly. He got a deep cut on his foot and he twisted his ankle. Harper and I paddled hard to get him. I talked to him calmly, so he didn't panic. On the sand, Jess's dad did first aid. He cleaned the cut carefully and put a bandage on it, so it didn't bleed much. Now Ollie's ankle is swollen and he has a big bruise, but it isn't broken. I got a small cut on my hand, that's all. Kai says I did really well.
"He cleaned the cut carefully." What does "carefully" tell you?
A How he cleaned the cut: slowly, with a lot of attention.
B What kind of person Mick is.
Why does he write "I did really well" and not "I did really good"?
A "Good" describes a person or a thing. For an action, the word is "well".
B Because "good" is only for food.
Which sentence is correct?
A Harper and I paddled hardly to get him.
B Harper and I paddled hard to get him.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

How? Adjective + -ly

An adverb of manner tells you how somebody does something. It usually goes after the verb, or after the verb and its object.

Add -ly

quick → quickly
calm → calmly
bad → badly
careful → carefully
easy → easily

The rebels

good → well
fast → fast
hard → hard
→ "hardly" means almost not

Positive: She surfs well.  ·  Negative: He didn't paddle fast.  ·  Question: How did he land? — Badly.
The person or the action? Harper is a good surfer (the person). She surfs well (the action).
Word order: He cleaned the cut carefully — never "he cleaned carefully the cut".

Stage 4: After the trip

Quiz
Your host mum asks how Harper surfs. She is the best surfer in the club.
A She surfs really good.
B She surfs really well.
A teammate asks about Ollie's ankle. It is bigger than normal, but not broken.
A It's swollen, but it isn't broken.
B It's a bandage, but it isn't broken.
You want to say how you talked to Ollie in the water.
A I talked calmly to him, so he didn't panic.
B I talked to him calmly, so he didn't panic.
Kai asks how the current pulled Ollie.
A It pulled him very fast.
B It pulled him very fastly.
A grommet asks what to do in a strong current. Give the best advice.
A Don't panic. Paddle slowly and stay calm.
B Swim hard to the rocks and hold on.

Stage 5: Tell the club

Reflection
It's Tuesday at training, and Kai says: "Renato was there. Tell them what happened." Record 30 seconds: what happened on Saturday, in order, and how everybody acted — Ollie, Harper, Mick and you. Check one thing before you send it: the word that says HOW goes after the verb, and good becomes well.
