Grammar Discovery

Three ways to advise

You should see a physio.
You could try ice first.
It might be just a bruise.
You shouldn't paddle for a week.

Same shape, different weight

All four are modals and all four behave the same way: no to, no -s, and the verb after them stays bare. What changes is the strength. Should is real advice. Could is a suggestion, softer. Might is not advice at all — it is a guess about what is probably true.

The Code

Advice, suggestion, guess

The three words

should → this is what I advise: You should rest.
could → here is an option: You could try ice.
might → a guess: It might be just a bruise.
shouldn't → do not do it: You shouldn't paddle.

How they behave

No to: you should rest, never "should to rest".
No -s: he should rest, never "he shoulds".
Questions flip: Should I rest?
And you can stack the vocabulary: you should warm up and stretch.

In an actual emergency, none of this matters and you say one thing: I need help. Learn that one first.

Listening 1

At the physio

1. What is the good news and how long is the recovery?
Nothing is torn, and he should be back in the water in about ten days.
2. Why does she say this did not start on Saturday?
Because the shoulder has been overloaded for months: he paddles every morning and never warms up.
3. What are the three instructions?
No paddling for a week, ice twice a day, and five minutes of stretching before every session when he comes back.
Listening 2

The mistake a friend made

1. What did he get wrong two years ago?
He went back too early, after four days, and lost the whole winter instead of one week.
2. What does he say might happen while you are out of the water?
You might get a bit down, and that is normal.
3. What is his advice about that?
Go to the beach anyway, watch and talk to people. The ones who disappear come back slowest.
