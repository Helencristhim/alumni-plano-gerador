On a Call -- Clarifying

A call with the Houston office about the pumping station, and how to ask polite questions when you did not understand. Key words: clarify, catch, spell out, double-check, go over, follow up, action item, on the same page. Structure: indirect questions (could you tell me, do you know, I'd like to know if).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Clarify -- to make something easier to understand
"Could you clarify the second point?"
Catch -- to hear and understand what somebody says
"Sorry, I didn't catch the last number."
Spell out -- to explain something very clearly, step by step
"Can you spell out what the client wants?"
Double-check -- to check something again to be sure it is correct
"I need to double-check the flow rate."
Go over -- to look at or discuss something carefully, from start to end
"Let's go over the scope before Friday."
Follow up -- to contact somebody again about something you discussed
"I will follow up with an email tomorrow."
Action item -- a task that somebody must do after a meeting
"We have three action items from today's call."
On the same page -- understanding something in the same way as other people
"Let's repeat the deadline so we are on the same page."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On Thursday, Rachel from the Houston office calls Thiago. She wants to go over the pumping station before Friday, but the connection is bad. Thiago does not catch the last part, so he asks: Could you tell me what you want to go over?

The client changed the capacity last week. Thiago asks: Do you know what the new flow rate is? Rachel is not sure, so she needs to double-check. Then he asks: Could you tell me why the client changed it? A new neighborhood is going to connect to the network.

At the end, Rachel promises to spell out the action items in an email, so they are on the same page, and Thiago says he will follow up tomorrow. Notice the questions: after could you tell me and do you know, the verb goes to the end, and there is no does or did.

1. Why does Thiago ask Rachel to repeat?
A Because Rachel is speaking very fast about a project that he has never worked on before.
B Because he doesn't want to go over the project.
C Because the connection is bad and he didn't catch the last part.
2. Which question is correct?
A Do you know what is the new flow rate?
B Do you know what the new flow rate is?
C Do you know what does the new flow rate is?
3. Why did the client change the capacity?
A Because a new neighborhood is going to connect to the network.
B Because the old pumping station is too old and the city wants to replace all the pipes this year.
C Because the budget is smaller now.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Indirect Questions

GRAMMAR

A polite question has two parts: a polite start, and then a normal sentence. The polite start is the question.

Direct questionPolite questionWhat changes
What is the deadline?Could you tell me what the deadline is?the verb goes to the end
When does the client need it?Do you know when the client needs it?does disappears; the verb takes -s
Did the client approve it?Do you know if the client approved it?no question word: add if; did disappears
Is the budget the same?I'd like to know if the budget is the same.not a question: full stop at the end
Explain me the problem.Could you explain the problem to me?explain + what, never explain me

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are on a call with the office in the United States. Choose the best thing to say.

You did not hear the last number:
A Sorry, I didn't hear nothing of what you said about the number, repeat it now please.
B Sorry, I didn't catch that. Could you say it again?
C What? Say again.
You want to know the deadline, politely:
A Could you tell me what the deadline is?
B Could you tell me what is the deadline for the new report on the pumping station?
C Could you tell me what does the deadline?
You are not sure the client approved the scope:
A Do you know did the client approve the scope?
B Do you know if the client did approved the scope last week in the meeting?
C Do you know if the client approved the scope?
The call is ending and you want everybody to agree on the next steps:
A Let's go over and spell out every single thing that we said today, from the start.
B Let's repeat the action items so we are on the same page.
C Let's catch the action items.
You want to check the numbers again before you send them:
A Let me double-check the numbers and follow up tomorrow.
B I will clarify the numbers yesterday.
C Let me follow the numbers and up them tomorrow with the team in the office.

Stage 5: Think About It

Reflection

No right or wrong. Think in English, tap the microphone and speak freely.
