Chapter 3: Could You Tell Me

The Polite Question

Same question, different order

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"What is the new flow rate? → Could you tell me what the new flow rate is?"

"When does the client need it? → Do you know when the client needs it?"

"Did the client approve it? → Do you know if the client approved it?"

"Is the deadline the same? → I'd like to know if the deadline is the same."

Polite startWhat happensExample
Could you tell me / Do you know + what, when, where, whynormal sentence order: subject + verbCould you tell me what the flow rate is?
no do, does or didthe verb keeps its -s or its past formDo you know when the client needs it?
yes/no questionadd ifDo you know if the client approved it?
I'd like to know / I wondernot a question: end with a full stopI'd like to know if the deadline is the same.

One idea decides it: the polite start is the question; the rest is a normal sentence.

Chapter 4: The Houston Call

Sorry, I Didn't Catch That

A call about the pumping station

Dialogue

The Houston Call

R
Hi Thiago, thanks for joining. I want to go over the pumping station before Friday.
T
Sure. Sorry, the connection is bad. I didn't catch the last part. Could you tell me what you want to go over?
R
The capacity. The client changed the requirement last week.
T
I see. Do you know what the new flow rate is?
R
I think it is two hundred liters per second, but I need to double-check.
T
Okay. Could you tell me why the client changed it?
R
A new neighborhood is going to connect to the network next year.
T
That makes sense. I'd like to know if the deadline is still the same.
R
Yes, Friday. I will spell out the action items in an email, so we are on the same page.
T
Perfect. I will follow up tomorrow with the new specification.

Listening

The Call Coach

A coach for engineers in international teams. Sound first, no text.

1. According to the coach, what is the real problem on most international calls?
Silence: people do not catch something and they do not ask.
2. What does she say is enough if you did not understand?
Sorry, I didn't catch that.
3. What does she recommend at the end of every call?
Repeating the action items -- it saves a week of emails.
Listening

A Message from Mark

A voicemail from the Houston office. Catch the requests.

1. Why is Mark calling?
To follow up on the pumping station.
2. When does Mark want the new specification, and why?
Monday morning, because the client meeting is on Tuesday.
3. What does he ask Thiago to double-check?
The new flow rate, with his team, before he sends anything.
