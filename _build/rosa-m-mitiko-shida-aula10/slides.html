Chapter 3: The Code

Two Sentences, One Person

who · which · that · where · whose

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The colleague who covers for me is called Ana."

"This is the route that connects São Paulo to Bogotá."

"Ana, who joined in March, is my backup."

"The client whose contract expires in March called me."

Three of these tell you which person or thing. One only adds extra information. Which one is it, and what on the screen tells you?

WordWhat it introducesExample
who · thatA person. In everyday speech that is also used for people, and both are correct."the colleague who covers for me"
which · thatA thing. That is more common in speech; which is more formal in writing."the route that connects the two cities"
where · whosewhere for a place; whose for something that belongs to the person or thing."the office where the team is based"
"the client whose contract expires in March"
Defining: no commasThe clause says which one. Remove it and the sentence stops making sense."The colleague who covers for me is called Ana."
Non-defining: two commasExtra information about somebody already identified. Remove it and the sentence still works. Never use that here."Ana, who joined in March, is my backup."

The commas are not decoration: they carry the meaning. The clients who pay late get a reminder means only some of them. The clients, who pay late, get a reminder means all of them pay late.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"The colleague who she covers for me is called Ana."
"The colleague who covers for me is called Ana."
"Ana, that joined in March, is my backup."
"Ana, who joined in March, is my backup."
"My counterpart in Santiago, which handles mining, is called Marcos."
"My counterpart in Santiago, who handles mining, is called Marcos."

The relative word replaces the subject — it does not sit next to it, so there is no second she or it. That never appears after a comma, and which is only for things. Who for people, which or that for things, and no pronoun twice.

Dialogue

The Handover Call

M
Rosa, I am taking over two accounts from a colleague who is leaving at the end of the month, and both of them are yours in the north.
R
Which two? And more importantly, do you know who the point of contact is at each one?
M
I have the names on the contract. One of them is a travel manager whose team books everything, and the other is a director whose name I have never seen in an email.
R
Then you have half the map. The name on the contract is the person whose signature we need. It is almost never the person you call at eleven at night.
M
So who do I call at eleven at night?
R
The analyst who actually runs the account. In that company it is a woman who has been there eleven years and who answers in five minutes, which is more than I can say for the director.
M
Is that written anywhere?
R
It is written in a list that I keep myself, because nothing in the onboarding pack has it. Let us go through it together before the handover.
Comprehension

About Marcos

1. Why is he taking over two accounts?
Because a colleague who is leaving at the end of the month was handling them.
2. What information does he already have, and what is missing?
He has the names on the contract, but not the person he should actually call.
3. How does he describe the two names he has?
A travel manager whose team books everything, and a director whose name he has never seen in an email.
4. What does he ask at the end, and what does he get?
Whether the real contacts are written anywhere — and Rosa offers to go through her own list with him before the handover.
Listening

Three Names Before Friday

A voice message from the regional office. Sound first — no text.

1. Which three names does she need?
The person who handles pricing, the colleague who covers for Rosa when she travels, and the person at the client she really calls.
2. Which of the three does she say everybody forgets?
The person at the client whom you actually call when something goes wrong at night.
3. What will the contact sheet be used for?
It will be the thing every new colleague reads in their first week.
Listening 2

The List Nobody Publishes

Advice given to every new account manager. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say the org chart actually shows?
Who reports to whom — and that is a much smaller piece of information than people expect.
2. Which three kinds of people are on the map you really need?
People who answer quickly, people who know things that are not written anywhere, and the two or three whose approval unblocks everything.
3. What is the fastest way to build that list, according to him?
Asking the question nobody likes asking: who is the person I should call about this?
