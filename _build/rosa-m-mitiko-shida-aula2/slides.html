Chapter 3: The Code

The Past That Is Still True

have · has · and the third form of the verb

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I have worked in this industry for more than twenty years."

"The company has just announced the regional expansion."

"I have already sent the proposal to the client."

"I have not met the new regional team yet."

All four colored verbs are built the same way. What are the two pieces, and where exactly does each action finish?

FormWhat it saysExample
have / has + third form
worked · sent · met · taken		The past, connected to now. You do not say when it happened, because that is not the point."I have worked here for twenty years."
"She has taken over the Chile accounts."
for · sincefor + a period of time. since + the point it started. Both mean it is still true today."for twenty years" · "for two months"
"since 2003" · "since March"
just · already · yetjust = a moment ago. already = sooner than expected. yet = still missing, and it goes at the end."They have just announced it."
"I have already sent it."
"I have not decided yet."
Negative and questionThe auxiliary is already there. You only move it, or add not to it."I have not met them yet."
"Have you signed the contract?"
The trapPortuguese keeps the present with desde and há. English never does. A period that started in the past and is still open takes have plus the third form.NOT "I work here since 2003."
NOT "I am working here for twenty years."

One question decides it: is the period closed, or is it still open today? If it is still open, use have plus the third form.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"I work in this industry since 2003."
"I have worked in this industry since 2003."
"I am working here for twenty years."
"I have worked here for twenty years."
"She has took over the Chile accounts."
"She has taken over the Chile accounts."

After have or has, English uses the third form of the verb, not the past: take, took, taken · write, wrote, written · send, sent, sent. And a period that is still open never takes the present or the continuous. If the period is still open today, use have plus the third form.

Dialogue

A Call from Chicago

T
Rosa, Tom Keller, from headquarters. I have read your name on the regional list all week. How long have you been with the company?
R
Nearly twelve years here, and I have worked in travel and aviation since well before that. I started in tourism.
T
Most good people in this business did. Have you already met the team in Bogotá?
R
Not yet. I have spoken to Diego twice, but I have not met anyone face to face. I think we all meet at the convention.
T
You do. And you should know they have asked every account manager to speak for five minutes there.
R
I heard. Can I ask you something? What has actually changed at headquarters since the announcement?
T
Honestly? We have launched the regional office and we have moved four teams into one reporting line. We have not fixed the reporting system yet, and everybody knows it.
R
That is the most useful thing anybody has told me this month. Thank you, Tom.
Comprehension

About Tom

1. Where does Tom work, and why has he noticed her name?
At headquarters — he has read her name on the regional list all week.
2. What does he say headquarters has already done?
They have launched the regional office and moved four teams into one reporting line.
3. What has headquarters not fixed yet?
The reporting system — and he says everybody knows it.
4. What does he warn her about the convention?
That they have asked every account manager to speak for five minutes.
Listening

The Call About the Convention

A voice message from the convention team. Sound first — no text.

1. When and where is the convention?
On the tenth and eleventh of November, in Bogotá.
2. What have they asked every account manager to do?
To speak for five minutes about one client they have worked with for a long time.
3. How many people have accepted, and what is still missing?
Six have accepted so far, and she has not heard from Rosa yet.
Listening 2

What Has Changed Since March

The opening of a quarterly update. Sound first — no text.

1. How long has the regional office in Bogotá been running?
For two months.
2. What was the promise made in March, and was it kept?
That nobody would lose an account — and nobody has.
3. What have they not solved yet?
The reporting system — three teams still work in four different formats.
