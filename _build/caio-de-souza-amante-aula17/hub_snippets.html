Hedging at the Highest Level -- Precise When You Are Not Sure Yet

In a negotiation, a number said too firmly becomes a promise, and a number said too softly sounds weak. Hedging gives the number its limits. Key words: indicative, preliminary, subject to, provisional, roughly, in the range of, to leave room for, tentative. Structure: hedging and stance markers (based on, the data suggests, it would appear that).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Indicative -- giving an idea of something, but not final
“This is an indicative price, not a final offer.”
Preliminary -- coming before the final version
“These are preliminary numbers from the first audit.”
Subject to -- depending on something that must happen first
“The offer is subject to due diligence.”
Provisional -- true for now, but it may change later
“We have a provisional date for the signing in May.”
Roughly -- not exactly; about
“Roughly sixty percent of our revenue comes from banks.”
In the range of -- between two numbers, approximately
“We expect a valuation in the range of ninety to one hundred million.”
To leave room for -- to keep space for something to change later
“Let’s leave room for further negotiation on the earnout.”
Tentative -- not certain or fully agreed yet
“We have a tentative agreement on the price.”

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

In a negotiation, the way you say a number matters as much as the number. Say it too firmly, our revenue will be one hundred million next year, and it becomes a promise the buyer will remember. Say it too softly, maybe, I don’t know, perhaps one hundred, and you sound like you do not know your own company.

The solution is hedging: giving a number its limits, on purpose. The strongest hedges point to a source: based on our current pipeline, according to the independent valuation, the data suggests that… Others give a range instead of a point: roughly sixty percent, in the range of ninety to one hundred million, a ballpark figure.

A third group shows that something is not final yet: an indicative price, preliminary numbers, a provisional date, a tentative agreement, an offer subject to due diligence. And a fourth group creates distance between you and the claim: it would appear that the buyer wants more time; there is reason to believe that the market will grow.

Hedging is not weakness. A CEO who says based on our current pipeline, we expect revenue in the range of ninety to one hundred million, subject to two contracts we are still negotiating sounds more precise, not less. He gives the number, the source and the risk, and he leaves room for negotiation without promising too much.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Hedging and Stance Markers

GRAMMAR

Give the number, then give it its limits: a source, a range, or a condition.

JobFormExample
Point to a sourcebased on / according to + nounBased on our pipeline, we expect growth.
Point to evidencethe data suggests that + clauseThe data suggests that churn is falling.
Give a rangeroughly / in the range ofIn the range of ninety to one hundred million.
Say it is not finalindicative · preliminary · provisional · tentativeThis is an indicative price.
Add a conditionsubject to + nounThe offer is subject to due diligence.
Create distanceit would appear that + clauseIt would appear that they need more time.
Keep spaceleave room for + nounLet’s leave room for negotiation.

Remember: after the data suggests, the valuation indicates and it would appear, the verb is in the third person: suggests, indicates.
