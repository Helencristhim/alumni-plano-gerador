Lesson 25 -- Pre-class

Conditionals in Real Life -- Hypotheticals, Plans and What-Ifs

How to talk about what you would do if, and how to tell a real plan from a daydream. Key words: a sabbatical, to uproot, a pipe dream, far-fetched, to weigh the pros and cons, a trade-off, to take the plunge, a safety net, a long shot, wishful thinking, to daydream, realistically. Structure: the second conditional (if + past, would / could / might) for imagined situations, and the first conditional (if + present, will) for real plans.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example.

A sabbatical -- a long period away from your job to rest, study or travel
"If I took a sabbatical, I would cycle across Patagonia."
To uproot -- to leave the place where you have lived for a long time
"I would not uproot my family for a job."
A pipe dream -- a plan or hope that will probably never happen
"A house in Tuscany is a pipe dream for now."
Far-fetched -- very difficult to believe, not likely at all
"Retiring at fifty sounds far-fetched."
To weigh the pros and cons -- to think about the good and bad points before deciding
"I need to weigh the pros and cons."
A trade-off -- when you accept losing one thing to get another
"More freedom, less money: that is the trade-off."
To take the plunge -- to finally decide to do something risky
"After ten years, he took the plunge."
A safety net -- something that protects you if things go wrong
"My savings would be my safety net."
A long shot -- something with a small chance of success, but worth trying
"The Lisbon job is a long shot."
Wishful thinking -- believing something because you want it to be true
"That is wishful thinking."
To daydream -- to think about pleasant things that are not real
"On long rides, I daydream about other lives."
Realistically -- when you think about what is really possible
"Realistically, I could take three months."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Fiona asks Marcelo a question for her column: “If your company gave you a year off, what would you do?” She is not talking about the past. The past after if shows that the situation is imagined.

Marcelo answers: “If I had a year off, I would cycle from Patagonia to Peru. If I were thirty, I might not take it.” With might or could, he is less sure.

Then he says something different: “Our company offers sabbaticals. If they approve it, I will go in 2028.” Now it is a real plan, so he uses if + present and will. The daydream has become a plan.

"If your company gave you a year off..." Did the company give him a year off?
A No. The past shows that the situation is imagined.
B Yes, last year, and he used it to travel around South America on his bike with friends.
C We do not know.
Why does Marcelo say "If they approve it, I will go"?
A Because it is a real plan that can happen.
B Because he is sure that the company will never approve it, so it is only a dream that he has on long rides.
C Because will is more polite.
Which sentence is correct?
A "If I would have a year off, I would cycle from Patagonia to Peru with two friends from the club."
B "If I have a year off, I would cycle."
C "If I had a year off, I would cycle to Peru."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Would or Will?

GRAMMAR

will = I am planning it. would = I am imagining it. The past after IF is distance from reality.

UseFormExample
A real planif + present, will + verbIf I get the job, I will move.
An imagined situationif + past simple, would + verbIf I had a year off, I would travel.
Less surecould / might + verbI might study engineering.
With beif I wereIf I were you, I would take it.
The questionWhat would you do if...?What would you do if you won?

Never: If I would have · If I have a year off, I would · If I were twenty, I will

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that a real speaker would give.

A friend asks what you would do with a million dollars. You say:
A "If I had a million dollars, I would buy a house near the sea."
B "If I will have a million dollars, I buy a house near the sea and a new bike for every day of the week."
C "If I have a million, I would buy a house."
The client is deciding this week. If he signs, you start in April. You say:
A "If he signed, we would start in April, but I am not sure if it will happen this year or the next."
B "If he signs, we will start in April."
C "If he would sign, we start."
A colleague wants to quit and travel, but he has no savings. You say:
A "If I am you, I take the plunge tomorrow and go."
B "If I were you, I would build a safety net first."
C "If I were you, I will build a safety net first, before I quit my job and travel around the world."
Someone says the traffic in São Paulo will be perfect next year. You say:
A "That is a safety net, because the government has a lot of new plans for the city."
B "That is wishful thinking."
C "That is a sabbatical."
