Chapter 3: The Code

Would or Will?

The difference between a plan and a daydream

Grammar Discovery

Plan or Daydream?

"If I get the Lisbon job, I will move in March."

"If I had a year off, I would cycle across Patagonia."

"If the board approves the budget, we will open a new warehouse."

"If I were twenty again, I might study engineering."

Blue or red: which sentences are real plans, and which ones are imagined? Look at the verbs after IF.

UseFormExample
A real plan (it can happen)if + present, will + verbIf I get the job, I will move.
An imagined situationif + past simple, would + verbIf I had a year off, I would travel.
Less surecould / might instead of wouldI might study engineering.
With beif I / he / she were (also was in speech)If I were you, I would take it.
The questionWhat would you do if...?What would you do if you won the lottery?

In one line: will = I am planning it. would = I am imagining it. The past after IF is distance from reality, not past time.

Chapter 4: A Year Off

The Question Fiona Asked

One conversation, two recordings, one document

Dialogue

A Year Off

F
So, Marcelo. If your company gave you a year off tomorrow, what would you do?
M
Honestly? I would cycle from Patagonia to Peru. I daydream about it on every long ride.
F
Would you uproot the family for that?
M
No. That would be a pipe dream. Realistically, they would stay, and I would go for three months, not twelve.
F
And the job? Some people say they would never take the year, because they would worry too much.
M
That is the trade-off. If I were thirty, I might not take it. At my age, the risk is smaller than the regret.
F
So is it a plan, or wishful thinking?
M
A plan, I think. Our company offers sabbaticals after fifteen years. If they approve it, I will go in 2028.
