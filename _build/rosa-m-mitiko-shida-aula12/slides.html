Chapter 3: The Code

Questions and Requests, Passed On

asked if · wanted to know when · asked me to · told me not to

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"She asked me if the fare included two bags."

"He wanted to know when the contract would be ready."

"She asked me to loop in the finance team."

"He told me not to send another reminder."

Two of these pass on a question and two pass on a request. Look at the words after if and when: is it question order? And where did do and did go?

FormWhat it saysExample
yes / no question → if · whetherThere is no question word, so if or whether opens the reported part."Do you have the figures?" → "She asked if I had the figures."
wh- question → statement orderKeep the question word, then subject before verb, like a normal sentence. The tense still steps back."When will it be ready?" → "He asked when it would be ready."
no do · does · didThe auxiliary of the question disappears. And there is no question mark at the end."Where did you send it?" → "She asked where I had sent it."
request → ask + person + toPolite requests become asked me to + base verb. The person comes first."Could you call me back?" → "He asked me to call him back."
instruction → tell + person + (not) toFirmer instructions use told. For a negative, not goes before to."Don't book anything yet." → "She told me not to book anything yet."

Wanted to know is a softer way to report a question, and it is what most people say at work. He asked me that I call him does not exist: a request always takes to.

Comprehension

About Julio

1. Why does Julio have messages for Rosa?
Because he covered for her on Tuesday and Wednesday while she was away.
2. Which two questions did Diego ask, according to Julio?
Whether the new fare included two bags, and when the contract would be ready.
3. What did Diego tell Julio not to do?
Not to send another reminder about the invoice.
4. What does Julio admit when Rosa asks about the last reminder?
That he was the person who chased Diego up.
The Real Thing

The Handover Email

Email · to the Brazil commercial team
Client messages from Tuesday and Wednesday

Mining account. Diego asked whether the new fare included two bags and wanted to know when the contract would be ready. He asked me to call him back before noon on Thursday, and I will.

Please note. He told us not to send another reminder about the March invoice. It was paid on Friday.

Bank account. The travel manager asked if we could loop in her finance team. I have acknowledged it and asked her to clarify which team she means.

1. Why does Rosa put the reminder in a separate paragraph?
Because an instruction not to do something is the message a colleague is most likely to miss, and getting it wrong damages the relationship.
2. Why does she write wanted to know when the contract would be ready and not when will the contract be ready?
Because a reported question uses statement order, with no question mark, and the tense steps back.
Listening

Before Noon on Thursday

A voicemail from a client. Sound first — no text.

1. What does his finance director want to know?
Whether the new fare includes two bags or only one, because the team travels with equipment.
2. What does Diego ask Rosa to do, and by when?
To call him back before noon on Thursday, because after that he is flying to Santiago.
3. Why does he ask her not to send another reminder?
Because the March invoice was paid on Friday and he has the confirmation.
Listening 2

Nobody Calls Twice

Advice from a senior account manager. Sound first — no text.

1. What is the test of passing on a question well, according to her?
That a colleague could answer it without calling the client again.
2. What three things should you write when somebody asks you to do something?
Who asked, what they asked for, and by when.
3. What does she say about replying when you do not have the answer yet?
Reply on the same day anyway: a short message that acknowledges it and gives a date is professional, and silence is not.
