Lesson 12 -- Pre-class

She Said She Would Call -- Passing On Questions and Requests

Reported questions and requests, for the voicemails and emails that are waiting when you come back. Key expressions: voicemail, to get back to, inquiry, to chase up, to forward, on behalf of, to acknowledge, to clarify, thread, to loop in, out-of-office, reminder. Structure: she asked whether, he wanted to know when, and she asked me to.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each expression and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it. These twelve stay here in your material -- they do not disappear after the class.

Voicemail -- a recorded phone message left when nobody answers
"He left a voicemail asking whether we could change the dates."
To get back to -- to reply to somebody later, usually with an answer
"She asked me to get back to her before Friday."
Inquiry -- a formal question that asks for information
"The inquiry asked whether the fare included two bags."
To chase up -- to contact somebody again because something has not been done yet
"He told me to chase up the invoice on Monday."
To forward -- to send a message you received on to another person
"Could you forward me the whole thread from the client?"
On behalf of -- as the representative of another person
"I am calling on behalf of Rosa, who is travelling this week."
To acknowledge -- to say officially that you have received something
"Please acknowledge the request so the client knows it arrived."
To clarify -- to make something clear by explaining it again
"I asked him to clarify what he meant by next week."
Thread -- a series of emails or messages that reply to each other
"The answer is somewhere in a thread of thirty emails."
To loop in -- to include somebody in a conversation so that they are informed
"She asked me to loop in the finance team before Thursday."
Out-of-office -- an automatic email reply that says you are away
"My out-of-office tells clients who to call while I am away."
Reminder -- a message that helps somebody remember something
"He asked me not to send another reminder about the invoice."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Rosa came back from three days in Lima on Wednesday night. Julio had covered for her, and on Thursday morning he gave her three messages from clients. None of them was a statement. They were all questions and requests.

Diego, from the mining account, had left a voicemail. Julio told her that Diego asked whether the new fare included two bags, and that he wanted to know when the contract would be ready. Diego also asked her to call him back before noon. And he told Julio not to send another reminder about the invoice, which had already been paid.

The second message came from a bank. The travel manager asked if Rosa could loop in her finance team, and she asked Julio to forward the whole thread. Rosa did not guess what she meant. She acknowledged the message the same morning and asked the travel manager to clarify which finance team she meant.

Notice how Julio passed the messages on. He did not say Diego called about the contract. He kept each question as a question, so Rosa could answer it without calling anybody twice.

1. Why does the text say "Diego asked whether the new fare included two bags" and not "does the fare include"?
A Because the fare changed after Diego called, so the present tense would no longer be true.
B Because a reported question uses statement order, with no does, and the tense steps back.
C Because Diego was angry.
2. What did Diego tell Julio not to do?
A To send another reminder about the invoice.
B To forward the contract to his legal department before it was ready.
C To call him after noon.
3. What did Rosa do when the bank's message was unclear?
A She guessed which finance team the travel manager meant and forwarded the whole thread to them.
B She waited for a second message.
C She acknowledged it and asked the travel manager to clarify.
4. What made Julio's handover useful, according to the text?
A He wrote a short summary of every call so that Rosa could read everything in one minute.
B He kept each question as a question.
C He answered the clients himself.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Reported Speech -- Questions and Requests

GRAMMAR

Last lesson you passed on what people said. This one is for what they asked, and what they asked you to do. The question loses its question form; the request keeps the person and takes to.

FormWhat it saysExample
yes / no question → if · whetherThere is no question word, so if or whether opens the reported part."Do you have the figures?" → "She asked if I had the figures."
wh- question → statement orderKeep the question word, then subject before verb. The tense still steps back."When will it be ready?" → "He asked when it would be ready."
no do · does · didThe question auxiliary disappears, and so does the question mark."Where did you send it?" → "She asked where I had sent it."
request → ask + person + toA polite request becomes asked me to + base verb."Could you call me back?" → "He asked me to call him back."
instruction → tell + person + (not) toFirmer instructions use told. For a negative, not goes before to."Don't book anything yet." → "She told me not to book anything yet."

Wanted to know is a softer way to report a question, and it is what most people say at work. And the professional rule matters as much as the grammar: keep the question as a question, so that a colleague can answer it without calling the client again.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are back from a trip and passing on client messages. Choose the best thing to say.

1. The client asked: "When will the contract be ready?" You report:
A He asked when will the contract be ready, and I promised I would check it today.
B He asked when the contract would be ready.
C He asked when did the contract be ready.
2. The client asked: "Does the fare include two bags?" You report:
A She asked whether the fare included two bags.
B She asked does the fare include two bags, because the team travels with equipment.
C She asked that the fare included two bags.
3. The client said: "Could you loop in my finance team?" You report:
A She asked that I loop in her finance team.
B She said me to loop in her finance team before the end of the week.
C She asked me to loop in her finance team.
4. The client said: "Please don't send another reminder." You report:
A He told me to not sending another reminder about the March invoice.
B He told me not to send another reminder.
C He told me don't send another reminder.
5. A client's question is unclear and you do not have the answer yet. The most professional reply is:
A Thank you, I have received your inquiry. Could you clarify the dates? I will get back to you on Thursday.
B Say nothing until you have the full answer, even if it takes a week or two.
C Send your best guess.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
