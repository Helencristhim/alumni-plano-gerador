Lesson 12 -- She Said She Would Call

Radio Story & Reading
"Text before you call, don't leave voicemails: Unwritten rules around telephone etiquette" -- NPR

A short public radio story on the new rules for calls, texts and voicemails, and why they differ so much between generations. Connection to Lesson 12: half of the messages waiting for you are voicemails, and the story explains why some clients leave them and others never will. The full text is on the page.

Tip: read it once, then pick two pieces of advice from the people interviewed and pass them on out loud in reported speech: she said that..., he asked people not to.... Then compare them with how your own clients prefer to be contacted.

Talk
"How to ask for help -- and get a yes" -- Heidi Grant, TED

About twelve minutes on why requests fail and how to make them clear enough that people want to help. Connection to Lesson 12: every request you pass on is somebody else's ask, and this talk shows what a good one contains -- who, what and by when.

Tip: watch it with English subtitles. Then report two of her points in reported speech, for example she told us to be specific, and write one real request you need to make this week using her advice.

Grammar Video
"Reported Speech: Everything You Need To Know!" -- Espresso English

An American teacher goes through reported statements, questions, requests and commands, with many examples. Connection to Lesson 12: the second half covers exactly today's structures -- asked if, asked where, asked me to and told me not to.

Tip: jump to the part on questions and commands. Pause after each example and report a real question or request that a client sent you this week, then check the word order.
