Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Fluent -- able to speak easily and continuously, without stopping to search for words
"She is fluent in meetings, even when the questions are hard."
Articulate -- able to express ideas clearly and well
"The new controller is very articulate on calls."
To hesitate -- to stop for a moment before you speak, because you are not sure
"I hesitated before the number, and then I said it clearly."
A filler word -- a sound or word like uh or so that fills a silence
"I recorded myself and counted eleven filler words in one minute."
To self-correct -- to notice your own mistake and fix it while you speak
"I said went, then I self-corrected to have been."
A milestone -- an important point in a process that shows progress
"Lesson ten is the first milestone of the course."
To sound natural -- to speak in a way that is normal for native and expert speakers
"I'll sounds more natural than I will in most sentences."
Confident -- sure about yourself and your abilities
"I feel more confident when I know my first sentence."
A habit -- something you do regularly, almost without thinking
"Recording myself every morning is now a habit."
To keep up -- to continue doing something at the same level
"I want to keep up the daily practice in the next block."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Before her first global call, Mariana, a finance manager in Porto Alegre, hesitated before every sentence and filled every silence with filler words. Ten weeks later she ran the whole monthly call. Here is what she said, and what each part shows.

“I joined the company in 2018, and I have led the tax team since 2022.” A finished fact with a date, then an open one with have. “The headline is that we closed on time. We have not received two invoices yet, and I'll send the final file by Thursday.” Headline first, a status, a promise with a date.

When the board asked something she did not know, she did not invent it: “I do not have that off the top of my head. If the rate changes, the cost changes too. I will get back to you by noon.” And the next day she reported: “The CFO asked me whether the cost went up.” She did not become perfect. She became articulate, and people noticed.

Which part of Mariana’s first sentence is an open situation that continues today?
A I joined the company in 2018.
B Both parts are finished.
C I have led the tax team since 2022.
What did she do when the board asked something she did not know?
A She said she did not know it off the top of her head and promised a time.
B She invented a number.
C She changed the subject.
What changed most in ten weeks, according to the text?
A She became perfect.
B She became articulate and clear.
C She lost her accent.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- The Block in One Table

GRAMMAR

No new grammar today. One line for each point of lessons 1 to 9, with the sentence you would say at work.

JobFormExample
Career: finished and openpast simple · have + third formI joined in 2018. I have led the team since 2022.
A system and a reasonpresent simple · because ofThe tax is charged at the destination because of the reform.
Headline and statusThe headline is… · not… yetThe headline is that we closed. We have not received it yet.
The small wordsexplain to · depend on · by / untilIt depends on the bank. I'll send it by Thursday.
Two kinds of ifif + present · if + past, wouldIf it changes, … · If we moved, we would…
Future and reportI'll · going to · asked whetherI'll check. He asked whether it went up.

Never: I have joined in 2018 · because the reform · explain me · if it will change · he asked me what is the cost
