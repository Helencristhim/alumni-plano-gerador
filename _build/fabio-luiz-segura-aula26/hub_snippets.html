The Post-Sibos Momentum

The weeks after a big conference: short follow-ups, three open threads, and deals that keep moving. Key words: dormant lead, re-engagement, deal pipeline, touchpoint, warm introduction, stakeholder map, escalation path, to fall through the cracks, to keep a relationship warm, to nudge. Structure: verbs that change meaning with -ing or to (remember, forget, stop, try).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A dormant lead -- a business contact who showed interest once, but has not answered or acted for a long time
"Two of my Sibos contacts are dormant leads now; nobody has answered in a month."
Re-engagement -- the action of bringing a contact back into an active conversation
"Our re-engagement plan starts with a short, useful message."
A deal pipeline -- the list of all the possible deals you are working on, from first contact to signature
"After Sibos, our deal pipeline has six new vendors and two partner banks."
A touchpoint -- any moment of contact with a person or a client, such as a call, an email or a meeting
"Every touchpoint after the conference should give the other person something useful."
A warm introduction -- when somebody you trust introduces you personally to a new contact
"She offered me a warm introduction to the head of payments at her bank."
A stakeholder map -- a simple picture of all the people involved in a decision, and how much each one matters
"Before the follow-up, I drew a stakeholder map of their bank."
An escalation path -- the agreed order of people to go to when a problem cannot be solved at one level
"If the vendor does not answer, the escalation path is their head of sales."
To fall through the cracks -- to be forgotten or not done, because nobody is clearly responsible for it
"I want to make sure this does not fall through the cracks after the conference."
To keep a relationship warm -- to stay in regular, friendly contact so a professional relationship does not go cold
"A short message every few weeks helps to keep a relationship warm."
To nudge -- to gently remind or encourage somebody to do something
"If she does not answer by Friday, I will nudge her with a short message."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

A conference creates a lot of new contacts, but many of them become dormant leads in the following weeks. The reason is simple: nobody takes the next step, and the conversation falls through the cracks. The first seventy-two hours are the best moment for a follow-up.

A good follow-up starts with a memory. Here, the verb remember + -ing looks back at something that happened: I remember talking to you about real-time settlement. But remember + to looks forward, to a task: Please remember to send the results by Friday. The same is true for forget: I forgot to reply means the reply never happened.

Stop also changes meaning. We stopped testing the old format means the testing ended. She stopped to answer my message means she paused in order to answer it. And try: I tried to reach her three times is an effort; Try calling her is an experiment, a different way to get a result.

Short messages, one useful thing, and one clear question: that is how you keep a relationship warm. And when somebody goes silent, a polite nudge or a warm introduction can bring the conversation back.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Verbs That Change Meaning

GRAMMAR

With remember, forget, stop and try, -ing and to say different things.

UseFormExample
A memory of the pastremember / forget + verb-ingI remember meeting her at Sibos.
A task (not) doneremember / forget + to + verbRemember to send the deck. · I forgot to reply.
End an activitystop + verb-ingWe stopped testing the old format.
Pause in order tostop + to + verbShe stopped to answer my message.
An efforttry + to + verbI tried to reach him three times.
An experimenttry + verb-ingTry calling her directly.

Never: I remember to meet you last month · Please remember sending it · I forgot replying · We stopped to test the old format (= it ended)

Stage 2: Build the Follow-Up

Order

You are writing a short follow-up to a fintech founder you met at Sibos. Put the message in a logical order.

?"Would it make sense to set up a short call with my innovation team next week?"
?"Thank you for the pilot results. I tried to read them on the plane, and they look very promising."
?"Best regards, Fabio"
?"I remember talking to you about real-time settlement for trade finance."
?"Hi Priya, I hope the trip home from Sibos was good."
?"I want to make sure this does not fall through the cracks."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the answer that works best in the weeks after a conference.

You want to start a follow-up to a contact you met at Sibos. You write:
A I remember talking to you about real-time settlement at Sibos.
B Dear Sir, it was a pleasure. Let us keep in touch. Best regards.
C I remember to talk to you at Sibos about real-time settlement, trade finance and many other topics.
You never answered a vendor's email. You write:
A Sorry, I forgot replying to your email.
B Sorry, I was very busy after the conference, and I think I maybe forgot about your email and the other emails too.
C Sorry, I forgot to reply to your email.
Email did not work with a contact. You suggest to a colleague:
A Try to call.
B Try calling her directly. She doesn't read emails after conferences.
C Try to calling her directly, because I think she is very busy and maybe she does not read her emails.
A contact has not answered for three weeks. You:
A Send a short, polite nudge with something useful.
B Forget the contact.
C Send a very long email with all the details of your bank, your products and the whole history of your project.

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

Think of a real contact from a recent conference who has not answered yet. In about ninety seconds, record a voicemail for that person: remind them where you met (remember + -ing), say why you are following up, mention something you tried to do, and propose one clear next step with a date.
