Lesson 25 -- Pre-class

The Hearing -- Sequencing and Argument Markers

How to keep a tribunal, a client or a dinner table with you during a long argument: say how many points you have, and put a sign at every step. Key words: a hearing, the claimant, the respondent, the tribunal, an opening statement, to cross-examine, to take the floor, to lay out, to signpost, to lose the thread, to drive the point home, to wrap up. Structure: markers to open (to begin with, first of all), to add (furthermore, what is more, in addition to), to show a result (consequently, it follows that) and to close (finally, to sum up).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

A hearing -- a formal meeting where each side presents its case to a judge or a tribunal
"The hearing will last three days, in São Paulo."
The claimant -- the party that starts a case and asks for something, usually money
"The claimant is asking for four million dollars."
The respondent -- the party that answers a claim in a case
"The respondent says the machines worked perfectly."
The tribunal -- the arbitrator, or the group of arbitrators, who decide a case
"The tribunal has three members, and one of them is from Geneva."
An opening statement -- the first speech at a hearing, where a side says what it is going to prove
"Her opening statement took only twenty minutes."
To cross-examine -- to ask a witness from the other side questions, to test their story
"Their lawyer will cross-examine our plant manager after lunch."
To take the floor -- to start speaking in a meeting or a formal event, when it is your turn
"After the chair's welcome, the claimant's counsel took the floor."
To lay out -- to explain something clearly and in order
"Let me lay out the timeline before we discuss the damages."
To signpost -- to tell your listeners where you are in a talk and what comes next
"Good speakers signpost every change of topic."
To lose the thread -- to stop following what someone is saying, or to forget what you were saying
"He talked for an hour, and I lost the thread after ten minutes."
To drive the point home -- to make your listeners fully understand and remember a point
"She showed one email on the screen to drive the point home."
To wrap up -- to finish something, for example a talk or a meeting
"We have five minutes left, so let's wrap up."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

At half past nine on Monday morning, in a hearing room in São Paulo, the presiding arbitrator, Judith Kerr, invited the claimant to begin. Rodrigo took the floor. He did not read his written submission. Instead, he said: "I have three points. To begin with, the contract set a minimum speed. Secondly, the machines never reached it."

Judith asked for the third point, because she did not want to lose the thread. "Thirdly," Rodrigo said, "the seller knew about the problem in March and said nothing. Consequently, my client lost four months of production." Furthermore, he told the tribunal that the March emails were in the file, and that the plant manager would give evidence that afternoon. Counsel for the respondent was ready to cross-examine her.

When Judith asked him to wrap up, he needed only one more sentence. "Finally, a machine that cannot reach the speed in the contract is not the machine in the contract. It follows that the seller must pay." His opening statement took twenty-six minutes.

1. "Consequently, Atlântica lost four months of production." What does consequently tell the tribunal?
A That this is a new fact, added to the last one, and that the lawyer will explain it in more detail later in the hearing.
B That this is the result of the facts before it.
C That this is the last point.
2. Why did Judith Kerr say the opening could be brief?
A Because the tribunal had already read both written submissions.
B Because the respondent's counsel had asked the tribunal for more time in the afternoon for the cross-examination.
C Because Rodrigo had only one point.
3. Rodrigo wants to say that his third point is his last one. Which marker is correct?
A At last,
B Finally,
C Resuming, and this is the third and last point that I want the tribunal to consider,

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Sequencing and Argument Markers

GRAMMAR

The marker goes first, then a comma, then a full sentence. Each one tells the listener what kind of step comes next.

JobMarkersExample
OpenTo begin with, / First of all, / First,To begin with, the contract set a speed.
Next pointSecondly, / Next, / Turning to + noun,Turning to the damages, ...
AddFurthermore, / What is more, / In addition to + noun or -ingIn addition to the delay, the quality was poor.
ResultConsequently, / As a result, / It follows that + clauseIt follows that the seller must pay.
Last pointFinally, / Lastly,Finally, I will turn to the damages.
CloseTo sum up, / In short,To sum up, the seller must pay.

Never: At last (= after a long wait) for your last point · Resuming (= starting again) for to sum up · In addition of · Firstly of all

Stage 2: Put the Opening in Order

Order

Announce the map, open, add, give the result, and close. Put the opening statement in that order.

?"Thank you, Madam President. I have three points."
?"To begin with, the contract set a minimum speed."
?"Secondly, the machines never reached it."
?"Furthermore, the seller knew about the problem in March."
?"Consequently, my client lost four months of production."
?"To sum up, the seller must pay."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose what a real speaker would say.

You are starting a ten-minute presentation to a client's board. What is the best first sentence?
A "Resuming, I will talk about many things today, so please be patient with me while I go through all the details."
B "I have three points, and I will take them in order."
C "At last, here is my presentation."
You want to add a second, stronger fact to your argument. You say:
A "In addition of that, they never paid."
B "What is more, they never paid."
C "Consequently, and also because of many other reasons that we will see later, they never paid."
The arbitrator says: "Please wrap up in two minutes." What do you say?
A "Certainly. Finally, I will turn to the damages."
B "Certainly. To begin with, I would like to go back to the very first email between the parties."
C "Certainly. At last, the damages."
You have shown that the machines did not reach the speed in the contract. Now you want the conclusion. You say:
A "Firstly of all, the seller must pay."
B "Furthermore, the seller must pay, and I will also explain the other reasons for that in my second point."
C "It follows that the seller must pay."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Think, then record your answer. There is no wrong answer here.

A client asks you: "Should we go to arbitration, or try to settle?" Record a sixty-second answer. Start by saying how many points you have, use at least four different markers (one to open, one to add, one for a result and one to close), and end with one sentence the client will remember.
