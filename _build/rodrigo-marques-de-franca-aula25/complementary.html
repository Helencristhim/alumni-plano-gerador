Lesson 25 -- The Hearing

Talk
Nancy Duarte -- "The secret structure of great talks" (TED, 18 min)

A presentation designer shows that the best speeches, from Martin Luther King to Steve Jobs, follow the same hidden map, and that the audience can feel it. The same idea as the speaking coach in this lesson, with real examples.

Tip: every time she moves to a new part of her talk, write down the words she uses to signpost it.

Film
My Cousin Vinny (1992)

A New York lawyer with no trial experience defends two students in a small Alabama court. It is a comedy, but the opening statements and the cross-examination of the witnesses are famous among American lawyers as real lessons in structure.

Tip: watch the cross-examination scenes and count how many short, ordered questions Vinny asks before he drives the point home.

Podcast
Clifford Chance, The Arbitration Lifecycle -- "Act III: Factual Evidence"

International arbitration lawyers talk through witness statements, document production and how witnesses are prepared and cross-examined at the hearing. Professional, clear and close to your practice.

Tip: note every sequencing marker the speakers use (first, then, finally, it follows that) and the order of the stages they describe.
