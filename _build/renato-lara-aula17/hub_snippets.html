What Did They Ask?

Eight paperwork words (form, deadline, requirement, interview, permission, contact, confirm, apply) and reported questions: asked if or whether for yes/no, and the question word with no inversion for wh-.

Stage 1.1: Paperwork Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes.

Form — the paper or the screen where you write your details
"I had to fill in a form before the first session."
Deadline — the last day when you can still do something
"The deadline for the junior squad is Friday."
Requirement — something that you must have or must do
"A medical certificate is a requirement here."
Interview — a meeting where somebody asks you questions
"The interview took about ten minutes."
Permission — when somebody says that you are allowed to do it
"I need permission from my host family."
Contact — the person you call if there is a problem
"They asked me for an emergency contact."
Confirm — to say that something is definitely true or definitely happening
"They will confirm the place by email."
Apply — to ask officially to join something
"I applied for the squad on Monday."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the report of the interview. Then answer the questions.

The interview took ten minutes. She asked where I was from and how long I had been surfing. Then she asked if I had my own board and whether I needed a wetsuit for winter. The medical part is a requirement, so she asked if I could swim four hundred metres. She also asked who my emergency contact was and if I knew the number by heart. The last question was whether my host family would sign the form. The deadline is Friday and they confirm places by email.
What did she ask about equipment?
A If he had his own board and whether he needed a wetsuit.
B Where he bought his board.
Why did she ask about swimming four hundred metres?
A Because it is a requirement.
B Because there is a race on Friday.
Why is it "she asked where I was from" and not "where was I from"?
A Because a reported question goes back to normal word order.
B Because "where" is a long word.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Reported questions — if, whether, and no inversion

Once you report it, the question becomes a statement. Statements put the subject first.

Yes/no questions → asked if

"Do you have a wetsuit?"
→ He asked if I had a wetsuit.
"Are you competing?"
→ She asked if I was competing.
"Can you swim 400 metres?"
→ They asked if I could swim 400 metres.

Wh- questions → keep the word

"Where are you from?"
→ She asked where I was from.
"How long have you surfed?"
→ He asked how long I had surfed.
"What do you want?"
→ She asked what I wanted.

Yes/no question → asked if or whether: "Do you have a board?" → He asked if I had a board.
Wh- question → keep the question word: "Where are you from?" → She asked where I was from.
Never invert: "she asked where was I from" is broken. Subject first, always.
And the verb still steps back: are → was, have → had, can → could, will → would.

Stage 4: At the front desk

Quiz
The original was "Where are you from?" How do you report it?
A She asked where was I from.
B She asked where I was from.
The original was "Do you have a wetsuit?"
A He asked if I had a wetsuit.
B He asked do I have a wetsuit.
The original was "Can you swim 400 metres?"
A She asked that I could swim four hundred metres.
B She asked whether I could swim four hundred metres.
