The Surf Club

Eight words for joining a group that already knows each other (join, member, teammate, captain, hang out, get along, shy, break the ice) and how to say what you like: love, enjoy, don't mind, can't stand + verb-ing.

Stage 1.1: Club Words

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes. "Hang out" and "get along" are one word each: the second part never falls off.

Join — to become part of a club or a group
"I want to join the surf club."
Member — a person who belongs to a club or a group
"We have a new member today."
Teammate — a person who is on the same team as you
"My teammates love talking in the water."
Captain — the leader of a sports team
"Kai is the captain of the club."
Hang out — to spend free time with friends, not doing anything special
"After training we hang out at the kiosk."
Get along — to have a friendly relationship with somebody
"You are going to get along with Harper."
Shy — nervous and quiet with people you do not know
"I'm a bit shy with new groups."
Break the ice — to say or do something that makes new people feel relaxed
"A joke about the cold water can break the ice."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read the message. Then answer the questions.

Today I went to the school surf club for the first time. I was nervous, because I'm a bit shy with new groups and they all know each other. The captain, Kai, asked me what I like surfing. I said I love surfing beach breaks and I don't mind small waves. He told me that nobody in the club enjoys getting up at half past five, but they train on Saturday at six anyway. Honestly, I can't stand getting up early, but I want to join. It is easy: one form and you are a member. My new teammates talk all the time. After training we hang out at the kiosk. I think I am going to get along with them. Millie told me how to break the ice: complain about the cold water.
Why does he write "I love surfing" and not "I love surf"?
A "Surfing" is the activity. After "love" the verb takes -ing, and "surf" alone is the wave.
B Because "surf" is too short to be a verb in English.
"I don't mind small waves." How does he feel about small waves?
A He hates them and he never surfs them.
B They are okay for him. They are not his favorite, but they are not a problem.
Which sentence is correct?
A Nobody enjoys getting up at half past five.
B Nobody enjoys to get up at half past five.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Love, enjoy, don't mind, can't stand + verb-ing

When the thing you like is an activity, the verb after these words ends in -ing.

The scale

I love surfing.
I enjoy training.
I like watching the WSL.
I don't mind waiting.
I can't stand getting up early.

Never

I enjoy to surf
I love surf in the morning
I don't mind early
→ I don't mind training early

Positive: She loves talking.  ·  Negative: He doesn't like losing.  ·  Question: Do you enjoy competing?
Don't mind is in the middle: not good, not bad, just okay. Can't stand is stronger than don't like.
Keep it going: answer, then send it back — "I love it. What about you?"

Stage 4: At the club

Quiz
The captain asks what you like doing. You love surfing beach breaks.
A I love surf beach breaks.
B I love surfing beach breaks.
Training is on Saturday at six in the morning. It is not great for you, but it is okay.
A I don't mind training early.
B I can't stand training early, so I'm happy.
You want to break the ice with a quiet teammate. What do you ask?
A Do you enjoy to compete?
B Do you enjoy competing?
Somebody says: "I love surfing reef." You want the conversation to keep going.
A Me too, but I don't mind beach breaks. Where do you like surfing here?
B Okay.
Your host mum asks about the club. You really, really do not like crowded line-ups.
A I don't mind crowded line-ups.
B I can't stand surfing in a crowded line-up.

Stage 5: Tell the club about you

Reflection
Training is over and everybody is at the kiosk. Kai says: "Right, new guy. Tell us about yourself." Record 30 seconds: two things you love doing, one thing you don't mind, one thing you can't stand — and finish with a question back to the group. Check one thing before you send it: after love, enjoy, don't mind and can't stand, the verb ends in -ing.
