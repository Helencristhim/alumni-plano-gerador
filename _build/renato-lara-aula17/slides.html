Grammar Discovery

The question stops being a question

She asked: "Where are you from?"
She asked where I was from.
He asked: "Do you have a wetsuit?"
He asked if I had a wetsuit.

No inversion, no question mark

Two things happen. The word order goes back to normal — subject, then verb, exactly like a statement. And a yes/no question needs a new word to hold it together: if (or whether). Wh- questions keep their question word: where, what, how long.

The Code

Yes/no takes if. Wh- keeps the word.

Yes/no questions → asked if

"Do you have a wetsuit?"
→ He asked if I had a wetsuit.
"Are you competing?"
→ She asked if I was competing.
"Can you swim 400 metres?"
→ They asked if I could swim 400 metres.

Wh- questions → keep the word

"Where are you from?"
→ She asked where I was from.
"How long have you surfed?"
→ He asked how long I had surfed.
"What do you want?"
→ She asked what I wanted.

The trap is the inversion: never "she asked where was I from". Once it is reported, it is a statement, and statements go subject first.

Did You Catch It?

What she asked

What were the first two things she asked?
She asked where he was from and how long he had been surfing.
What did she ask about equipment?
She asked if he had his own board and if he needed a wetsuit for winter.
What is the requirement she checked, and what does Zoe conclude at the end?
She asked whether he could swim four hundred metres. Zoe concludes that he is already in, because nobody gets asked about the form unless they are.
Listening 1

The interview itself

1. What are the two questions about equipment?
Whether he has his own board or needs one lent to him, and whether he has a wetsuit for winter.
2. What is the medical requirement?
Swimming four hundred metres without stopping.
3. What are the two dates at the end?
The deadline is Friday, and they confirm places by email on Monday.
Listening 2

What they were really checking

1. Why did they ask where he was from?
Not because of the country, but to hear him speak for thirty seconds.
2. Why did they ask if he had his own board?
Because it tells them how serious he is.
3. Which question actually matters, and why?
The emergency contact. If you do not know the number by heart, they write that down.
