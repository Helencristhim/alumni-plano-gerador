Getting Around the City

Get ready for a busy morning in Manhattan: ask for the cross street, understand the subway signs, and say what is normal for you with be used to and get used to ("I'm used to driving", "I'm getting used to the subway"). Key words: borough, cross street, off-peak, service change, out of service, jaywalking, bike-share, tourist trap, to get off, to get lost.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Borough -- one of the five big parts of New York City
"Manhattan and Brooklyn are two of the five boroughs."
Cross street -- the street that crosses the main street, to give an exact place
"The address is on Fifth Avenue. The cross street is Forty-Second."
Off-peak -- at a time of day when fewer people travel
"The subway is much quieter at off-peak hours."
Service change -- a planned change to a train route or schedule
"There is a service change this weekend, so the train skips my stop."
Out of service -- not working or not available to use
"The elevator at this station is out of service."
Jaywalking -- crossing the street in the wrong place or against the light
"Jaywalking is very common in New York."
Bike-share -- a system where you rent a bike from a station for a short ride
"I took a bike-share bike along the river."
Tourist trap -- a crowded, expensive place made only for tourists
"That restaurant in Times Square is a tourist trap."
To get off -- to leave a bus, a train, or a subway car
"Get off at Grand Central and walk two blocks."
To get lost -- to not know where you are or which way to go
"I always get lost in the West Village."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Lucimara is back in New York for a meeting with a supplier on Fifth Avenue. In São Paulo, she is used to driving everywhere, so on her first trip, Manhattan was a challenge. She wasn't used to walking so much, and she got lost on her first day.

Today, it is different. She is getting used to the grid: the avenues go north and south, and the streets go east and west. She always asks for the cross street, and she travels off-peak when she can. There is only one thing she is not used to yet: everybody is jaywalking, and she still waits for the light.

1. Why was Manhattan a challenge on her first trip?
A Because her hotel was very far from Fifth Avenue, and the subway was closed that week.
B Because she wasn't used to walking so much.
C Because she doesn't like New York.
2. What is Lucimara not used to yet?
A Jaywalking. She still waits for the light.
B The subway map, because it is very different from the metro map in São Paulo.
C The cross streets.
3. Which sentence is correct?
A "I'm used to drive everywhere in São Paulo, so walking is strange for me."
B "I'm getting use to the grid."
C "I'm used to driving everywhere."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Be Used To / Get Used To

GRAMMAR

How to talk about what is normal for you, what is becoming normal, and what is still strange.

FormUseExample
be used to + -ing / nounit is normal for you, not strangeIn São Paulo, I am used to driving.
get used to + -ing / nounit is becoming normal (a process)I am getting used to the subway.
not be used to + -ing / nounit is still strange for youI am not used to jaywalking.
used to + base verb (Lesson 20)a past habit that is not true nowI used to take a cab everywhere.
RuleAfter be used to and get used to, "to" is a preposition, so use -ing or a noun: "I'm used to walking", never "I'm used to walk". Use the future to calm someone down: "Don't worry, you'll get used to it."

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

A New Yorker gives you an address: "It's on Fifth Avenue." You say:
A "Great, thanks. What's the cross street?"
B "Thank you so much. I think I can find it, but could you tell me how many blocks it is from here?"
C "Fifth Avenue is big."
Your colleague is nervous on her first day in Manhattan. You say:
A "Don't worry. You will used to it in a week or two, like me."
B "Don't worry. You'll get used to it."
C "You get use to it."
You hear an announcement: the elevator is out of service. You have a heavy suitcase. You ask the agent:
A "Elevator no. Where?"
B "The elevator is out of service, so I am going to carry my suitcase on the stairs to the platform."
C "Excuse me, is there another entrance with an elevator?"

A "No, I used to it now, because I go there very often for work."
