Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the passage, then answer the questions about how it is written.

Persuasion is not always a matter of saying things forcefully. Some of the most effective moves in argument work by saying less than the speaker means, and trusting the listener to supply the rest.

The rhetorical question is the most familiar of these moves. If a prompt is a brushstroke, is a camera a painter? The speaker does not expect an answer, or rather expects one particular answer, and by asking instead of stating, invites the listener to arrive at the conclusion independently. Conclusions we reach ourselves feel more convincing than those we are handed. The technique becomes weaker, however, with repetition. A string of questions without answers sounds evasive, and in an interview or an essay a rhetorical question is usually most effective when the speaker answers it immediately.

Litotes works in the opposite direction. Instead of asking, it denies. The objection is not unreasonable means that the objection is reasonable, but it says so with a certain restraint, as if the speaker were conceding a point rather than celebrating it. The technology is not without its critics means that it has plenty of them. Because litotes sounds modest, it is especially useful when a speaker wants to acknowledge an opposing view without appearing to surrender to it.

Understatement is the broader family to which litotes belongs. Describing a painting destroyed in front of a room of bidders as hardly a quiet gesture deliberately minimizes the event, and the gap between the words and the reality produces irony. English speakers, particularly in academic and professional settings, use understatement constantly, and a listener who takes it literally may miss the speaker's meaning entirely.

For a proficient speaker, the challenge is therefore double: to use these devices sparingly, and to read them accurately when others use them. Examination questions about a writer's attitude very often depend on exactly that second skill.

A Because listeners find conclusions they reach themselves more convincing.

2. What does not without its critics mean?
A It has no critics at all.
B It has plenty of critics.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Rhetorical Questions and Understatement

GRAMMAR

A rhetorical question implies a claim and invites the listener to complete it. Litotes affirms by denying the opposite. Understatement minimizes deliberately for emphasis or irony.

FormWhat it doesExample
rhetorical questiona claim disguised as a questionIs a camera a painter?
question + immediate answerraises and settles a pointSo who is the artist? The person who chooses.
litotes: not + negative adjectiveaffirms with restraintnot unreasonable, not unimpressive
litotes: not withoutsignals a significant quantitynot without its critics
understatement: hardly / a littleminimizes deliberately for ironyhardly a quiet gesture
The C2 trapstrings of unanswered questions and double negatives that confuse. Use one question and answer it; check that every litotes resolves clearly to a positive meaning.

When reading, translate every litotes into a positive statement before answering a question about the writer's attitude.
