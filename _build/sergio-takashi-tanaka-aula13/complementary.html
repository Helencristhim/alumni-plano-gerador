"The walk from 'no' to 'yes'" -- William Ury (TED)

The co-author of "Getting to Yes" explains how a difficult negotiation moves from a flat no to an agreement both sides can sign. Connection to Lesson 13: this is the moment you practiced today -- naming the deal-breaker, pushing back on one point only, and still settling on an offer.

Tip: watch with English subtitles. Every time he describes two positions, stop and compare them out loud with one comparative: "their offer is far shorter than ours".

"Negotiate Anything" -- Kwame Christian

A free show about real negotiations, in clear American English, with short episodes on offers, counteroffers and hard conversations. Connection to Lesson 13: the guests spend most of the time weighing one option against another out loud -- exactly the language you used to compare the two term sheets.

Tip: pick any episode and listen once for the main idea. Write down two sentences where a speaker compares two options, and reuse one at work this week.

Video
"Comparatives and superlatives -- 6 Minute Grammar" -- BBC Learning English

A short, free grammar programme on exactly the shapes you practiced today: -er than, more ... than, the -est, the most, and the irregular forms. Connection to Lesson 13: it drills the two mistakes that cost you most -- "more cheap" and "more expensive that".

Tip: watch once, then pause after each example and repeat it out loud. Finish by saying three sentences about your own work: one with a comparative, one with a superlative, and one with as ... as.
