Chapter 3: The Code

Comparatives & Superlatives

tighter than · the longest · not as flexible as

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"Meridian's spread is tighter than Nordbank's."

"Nordbank offers the longest tenor on the table."

"Their review clause is not as flexible as ours."

"The tenor is far shorter, and that is the trade-off."

What do the orange words tell us — when something happened, or how one offer stands against another?

ShapeWhen you use itExample
short adjective + -er + thanTwo things, side by side.Their spread is tighter than ours.
more + long adjective + thanSame job, but the adjective has three or more syllables.Their clause is more flexible than ours.
the + -est / the most + adjectiveThe top of a group of three or more.Nordbank has the longest tenor.
(not) as + adjective + asSay two things are equal — or that one falls short.It is not as flexible as ours.
far / much / slightly + comparativeSay HOW BIG the gap is: far shorter, much cheaper, slightly longer. Irregular forms: good → better → the best; bad → worse → the worst. Never use both shapes at once: "more cheaper" does not exist.

The comparison lives inside the adjective. Choose the shape first, and the number will speak for itself.

Common Mistake

Right vs Wrong

"This offer is more cheap than the other one."
"This offer is cheaper than the other one."
"Meridian is more expensive that Nordbank."
"Meridian is more expensive than Nordbank."
"Their spread is not as tight than ours."
"Their spread is not as tight as ours."

Short adjectives take -er, never "more". A comparison closes with than, never "that". And the equal shape opens and closes with as ... as.

Chapter 4: The Two Offers

The Artifact

Two Term Sheets, One Decision

Term Sheet A — Meridian Bank
Prepared for: Sergio Tanaka, Risk Management
Facility: Revolving credit line

Amount: 400 million reais

Tenor: 3 years

Spread: 145 basis points

Ceiling on drawdowns: 300 million reais

Review clause: annual, both sides may reopen

Term Sheet B — Nordbank
Prepared for: Sergio Tanaka, Risk Management
Facility: Revolving credit line

Amount: 400 million reais

Tenor: 5 years

Spread: 165 basis points

Ceiling on drawdowns: 180 million reais

Review clause: every two years, lender only

1. Which bank offers the tighter spread, and by how much?
Meridian, by twenty basis points: 145 against 165.
2. Which bank offers the longer tenor?
Nordbank, with five years against Meridian's three.
3. Whose ceiling on drawdowns is far lower?
Nordbank's: 180 million against Meridian's 300 million, on the same 400 million facility.
Dialogue

Choosing the Offer

M
Two term sheets on the table, Sergio. Meridian and Nordbank. Which one do we take to the committee?
S
Meridian's spread is tighter, so it looks cheaper at first. But the tenor is two years shorter, and that is the trade-off.
M
Good. And what is the deal-breaker for you? There is always one.
S
The ceiling on drawdowns. Nordbank sets it far lower than our internal benchmark, and that is the one point I need to push back on.
M
Then push back on the ceiling and leave the spread alone. Our leverage is better this year than it was last year, so use it.
S
Agreed. I will ask Nordbank to raise the ceiling to the benchmark, and if they do, that is the offer we settle on.
Comprehension

About Marina

1. What does Marina ask Sergio to decide?
Which of the two term sheets they take to the committee.
2. What does Marina say there is always one of?
A deal-breaker.
3. What does Marina tell Sergio to push back on, and what to leave alone?
Push back on the ceiling; leave the spread alone.
