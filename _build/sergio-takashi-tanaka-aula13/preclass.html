Lesson 13 -- Pre-class

Negotiating Terms — Weighing Two Offers

Two term sheets on the table and one decision to defend, point by point. Key words: term sheet, tenor, spread, trade-off, leverage, deal-breaker, push back, settle on, benchmark, ceiling. Structure: comparatives and superlatives (tighter than, the longest, not as flexible as, far lower than).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Term sheet -- a short document listing the main conditions of a deal
"Both banks sent a term sheet before the formal contract."
Tenor -- the length of time until a loan or contract ends
"The tenor is five years, so we refinance in 2031."
Spread -- the extra cost added on top of a base interest rate
"Their spread is twenty basis points tighter than ours."
Trade-off -- something you give up in order to get something else
"A tighter spread with a shorter tenor is the trade-off."
Leverage -- the advantage that lets you ask for better terms
"We have more leverage this year, so we can ask for more."
Deal-breaker -- one condition that stops the whole deal
"The ceiling is a deal-breaker; we cannot sign with it."
To push back -- to say no politely and ask for something better
"I will push back on the ceiling, but not on the spread."
To settle on -- to finally agree on one option
"We settled on Nordbank after they raised the ceiling."
Benchmark -- a standard number used to compare offers
"Their ceiling sits far below our internal benchmark."
Ceiling -- the highest level something is allowed to reach
"The ceiling on drawdowns is two hundred million."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Two term sheets arrived on the same morning, and only one of them goes to the committee. Meridian's spread is tighter than Nordbank's, twenty basis points tighter, so on price Meridian looks like the cheaper option. Nordbank, however, offers the longest tenor of the two: five years against three. That is the trade-off. Their review clause is not as flexible as ours, and the ceiling on drawdowns sits far lower than our internal benchmark — for me, that is the deal-breaker. Our leverage is better this year than it was last year, so I will push back on the ceiling and leave the spread alone. If they raise it, that is the offer we settle on. If they do not, Meridian is the best deal on the table.

1. Why does the writer use "the longest tenor" and not "a longer tenor"?
A He is naming the top of the whole group of offers, not one side of a pair.
B He is saying the tenor became longer over time.
C He is saying the two tenors are equal.
2. What does "not as flexible as ours" tell us about their review clause?
A It is the most flexible clause of the three.
B It is less flexible than ours — the two are not equal.
C It is exactly as flexible as ours.
3. Why does the writer write "far lower than" instead of just "lower than"?
A To show that the gap is big, not small.
B To show that he is not sure about the number.
C To show that the two numbers are the same.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Comparatives & Superlatives

GRAMMAR

The comparison lives inside the adjective. Choose the shape first, and the number speaks for itself.

ShapeWhen you use itExample
short adjective + -er + thanTwo things, side by side. One syllable, or two ending in -y (easy → easier).Their spread is tighter than ours.
more + long adjective + thanSame job, three or more syllables. Never both shapes at once: "more cheaper" does not exist.Their clause is more flexible than ours.
the + -est / the most + adjectiveThe top of a group of three or more. The article the is not optional.Nordbank has the longest tenor.
(not) as + adjective + asTwo things are equal, or one falls short. It closes with as, never with "than".It is not as flexible as ours.
far / much / slightly + comparativeShow the size of the gap: far lower, much cheaper, slightly longer. Irregular: good → better → the best; bad → worse → the worst. A comparison always closes with than, never "that".

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best option for each moment at the negotiation table.

You want to say that one spread costs less than the other. Which is best?
A "Their spread is more cheap than ours."
B "Their spread is tighter than ours."
C "Their spread is the tighter that ours."
Three banks have sent offers and one tenor is the top of the group. Which is best?
A "Nordbank has a longer tenor of the three."
B "Nordbank has the longest tenor of the three."
C "Nordbank has the most long tenor of the three."
You want to say their clause does not reach the level of yours. Which is best?
A "Their clause is not as flexible than ours."
B "Their clause is not so flexible that ours."
C "Their clause is not as flexible as ours."
The ceiling is the one point you cannot accept, and you want to ask for more. Which is best?
A "The ceiling is our deal-breaker, so we would like to push back on that one point."
B "The ceiling is bad. Change everything, please."
C "We accept the ceiling and we settle on it today."
