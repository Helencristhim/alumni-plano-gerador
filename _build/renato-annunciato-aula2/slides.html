Chapter 3: The Code

Bigger, Heavier, the Hardest

Comparing two things, and finding the extreme

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"The Southeast is bigger than the South, but the coverage is worse."

"Sao Paulo is the largest territory we have."

"This consultant carries the heaviest workload in the team."

"The backlog is not as long as it was last year."

Two sentences compare two things. Two point at one extreme. What changes at the end of the adjective?

TypeComparative (two things)Superlative (the extreme)
Short adjective (1 syllable)big → bigger thanthe biggest
Ending in -yheavy → heavier thanthe heaviest
Long adjective (3+ syllables)complex → more complex thanthe most complex
Irregulargood → better · bad → worse · far → furtherthe best · the worst · the furthest
Saying they are equalas ... as — "The South is as important as the Southeast." Negative: not as ... as.
Making it strongermuch / far / a lot + comparative · by far + superlative — "Sao Paulo is by far the largest."
Three trapsNever more big — short adjectives take -er. Never bigger that — the word is than. And never more better — one comparison per adjective.

The Structure

Three Layers, One Ask

This is how you ask for a person, a budget or a change of plan without sounding like you are complaining.

1 · The structure

"We have two territories. Four consultants cover the Southeast and three cover the South."

2 · The comparison that shows the problem

"Coverage in the South is better than in the Southeast, because the workload there is much heavier."

3 · The ask

"One more position in the interior would close the largest gap we have. That is what I need."

Structure → comparison → the ask. Never a complaint without a number.

Dialogue

Drawing the New Line

E
Renato, I have the review here. Before I redraw anything, tell me in your own words where the problem is.
R
The problem is not the size of the territory. It is the workload. My people in the Southeast carry almost twice as many accounts as the ones in the South.
E
And that shows up in the numbers?
R
It does. Coverage in the South is better than in the Southeast, ninety-one against eighty-four. The benchmark is one visit a month for every key account, and we are missing it in the interior.
E
Where exactly is the worst part? I need one place, not a state.
R
The interior of Sao Paulo. Reach is lowest there and the backlog is the longest we have. One more position would close the biggest gap in the country.
E
Good. Headquarters has approved one position, so we can split the territory. How do you want the two consultants there to be ranked?
R
Coverage first, growth second, and the backlog they inherited third. And I want to speak to each of them alone before anything is announced.
E
That last part is the one most managers skip. Send me the criteria in writing and I will allocate the new position this week.
Comprehension

About Emma

1. What does she ask for before she redraws anything?
Where the problem is, in his own words — not the written review.
2. How precise does she want him to be about the location?
One place, not a state.
3. What has headquarters already approved?
One additional position, which makes the split possible.
4. Which part of his answer does she say most managers skip?
Speaking to each consultant alone before anything is announced.
Listening

How a Territory Is Really Drawn

Sales operations, on the part nobody sees. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What happens if you split a country by area?
One consultant gets a beautiful map and no clients, and another one drowns in a city block.
2. Where do they start from instead?
From the clients who actually buy: they count them, weight them, and only then draw the line.
3. What makes a territory the best one, if not size?
The workload matching the person.
4. What is the part that is not a spreadsheet problem?
Somebody always loses a client they have known for years, and a manager who treats that as a spreadsheet makes the redraw fail.
Listening 2

The Split That Cost Two Quarters

Right decision, wrong sequence. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. How did she announce the new maps, and why was that a problem?
On a Monday morning group call, with the numbers on a slide. It was efficient, but two of her best people spent the quarter wondering what it meant about them.
2. What would she do differently, and what does it cost?
Talk to each person alone before the group call. It costs nothing.
3. What should come first, the reason or the map?
The reason first, the map second. The reason is almost always coverage.
4. Was the split itself a mistake?
No. They reached more clients that year with the same headcount. What cost her two quarters of trust was the way she told the story.

Your Structure

Spot the Error

Fix the Sentence

Say the correction out loud first, then click

1. "The Southeast is more big than the South."
"The Southeast is bigger than the South." (short adjectives take -er, never more)
2. "This is the most large territory we have."
"This is the largest territory we have."
3. "My workload is heavier that last year."
"My workload is heavier than last year." (the comparison word is than, not that)
4. "Coverage in the South is the more good of the two."
"Coverage in the South is the better of the two." (good is irregular: better, the best)
