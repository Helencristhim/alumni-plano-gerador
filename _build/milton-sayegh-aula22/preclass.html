Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Candidate -- a person who applies for a job and is being considered for it
"We interviewed five candidates for the job."
Shortlist -- a small list of the best people, chosen from a longer list
"Rachel is on our shortlist of three."
Recruit -- to find new people and bring them into a company
"We need to recruit two people for the US office."
Track record -- the results a person has achieved in the past
"She has a strong track record in luxury sales."
Delegate -- to give part of your work or responsibility to another person
"A good leader knows how to delegate."
Accountable -- responsible for something and ready to explain the results
"The sales manager is accountable for US revenue."
Turnover -- how often people leave a company and must be replaced
"Turnover in sales jobs is high in New York."
Mentor -- an experienced person who guides and advises someone with less experience
"I want to be a mentor, not just a boss."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Milton needs an export sales manager for his US office. The agency sent sixty applications, and Milton has three candidates on his shortlist. He doesn't want to ask only about the past. He wants to know what people would do with his clients.

In the first interview, he asked Rachel: "Suppose a client asked for a thirty percent discount. What would you do?" Then: "Imagine you had two orders on the same day." And finally: "What would you have done if your manager had said no?" Rachel answered every question with calm, clear examples.

1. "Suppose a client asked for a discount." Did the client really ask?
A No. It is an imagined situation.
B Yes, the client asked yesterday, and Milton is telling Rachel what really happened in the meeting.
C Yes, but only once.
2. Why does Milton say had in "Imagine you had two orders"?
A Because the orders arrived last week, so the sentence needs a past form.
B Because after imagine, an imagined situation uses the past form.
C Because had is more polite.
3. "What would you have done if your manager had said no?" What is this question about?
A A real plan for next month, when Rachel starts working with Milton in the US office.
B A rule about the company.
C An imagined past: the manager did not say no.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Hypothetical Questions

GRAMMAR

Imagined situation: past form after suppose, imagine or what if. Would in the question.

FormRuleExample
Suppose + past simplean imagined situation now or in the futureSuppose a client asked for a discount. What would you do?
Imagine + past simpleputs the person inside the sceneImagine you had two orders on the same day.
What if + past simple?a short, direct hypothetical questionWhat if the shipment arrived late?
What would you have done if + past perfect?an imagined past that did not happenWhat would you have done if the client had canceled?
The trapno would after suppose, imagine, what if or ifSuppose a client asked (not would ask).

These questions test what a person would really do -- not only what is on the application.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

You are interviewing a candidate. Choose the best thing to say.

You want to test how a candidate deals with discounts. You ask:
A "Suppose a client asked for a big discount. What would you do?"
B "Suppose a client would ask for a big discount at the end of the year. What would you do then?"
C "Suppose a client ask a discount."
You want to ask about a past situation that did not happen. You ask:
A "What would you do if the client cancels the order, and the shipment is already on its way to the boutique?"
B "What you would have done?"
C "What would you have done if the client had canceled?"
You tell a candidate the next step. You say:
A "You are in our shortlist and we call you maybe next week, or the week after, if everything is OK."
B "You are on our shortlist. We will contact you on Friday."
C "You are shortlist."
You describe the job. You say:
A "The manager will be accountable of the revenue in all the boutiques we sell to in the United States."
B "The manager will be accountable for US revenue."
C "The manager is accounting the revenue."
