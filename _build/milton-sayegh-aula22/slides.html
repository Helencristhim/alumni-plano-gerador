Chapter 3: The Question Code

What Would You Do?

suppose · imagine · what if · what would you have done

Grammar Discovery

What Do the Orange Words Do?

"Suppose a client asked for a big discount. What would you do?"

"Imagine you had two orders on the same day. Which would you choose?"

"What if the shipment arrived late?"

"What would you have done if your manager had said no?"

Look at the orange words. Are these real or imagined situations? Which tense comes after suppose, imagine and what if?

FormUseExample
Suppose / Supposing + past simpleSets up an imagined situation; the question uses would.Suppose a client asked for a discount, what would you do?
Imagine + past simplePuts the candidate inside the scene.Imagine you had two orders on the same day.
What if + past simple?A short, direct hypothetical question.What if the shipment arrived late?
What would you have done if + past perfect?An imagined past: the situation did not happen.What would you have done if the client had canceled?
The trapNo would after suppose, imagine, what if or if.Suppose a client asked (not would ask).

Past form after suppose / imagine / what if. Would in the question. That is how you test a candidate.

Chapter 4: The Interview

The Interview

Video call, Thursday afternoon. Milton is the interviewer today.

Dialogue

Milton Asks, Rachel Answers

M
Thanks for coming in, Rachel. I read your application, and your track record in luxury sales is impressive.
R
Thank you. I spent six years at a jewelry house in Chicago, and I recruited and trained most of their sales team.
M
Good. Let me ask you something practical. Suppose a big client asked for a thirty percent discount. What would you do?
R
I wouldn't say no right away. I'd ask what the discount is for, and I'd offer something else, like faster delivery.
M
And imagine you had two important orders on the same day, but only one shipment. Which client would you choose?
R
The one with the stronger relationship. But I'd call the other client first, before the problem became a surprise.
M
That's exactly what I want. What would you have done if your old manager had said no to that call?
R
Honestly, I would have made the call anyway. In sales, I'm accountable for the client, not for my comfort.
M
One last question. Supposing I offered you the job, when could you start?
R
In four weeks. And if I'm on your shortlist, I'd love to meet the team in Brazil first.
Comprehension

About Rachel

1. Where did Rachel work before, and for how long?
At a jewelry house in Chicago, for six years.
2. What would Rachel do if a big client asked for a thirty percent discount?
She would not say no right away. She would ask what the discount is for and offer something else, like faster delivery.
3. When could Rachel start, and what would she like to do first?
In four weeks. She would like to meet the team in Brazil first.

Chapter 5: Two Voices

Two Voices on Hiring

Listening

A Message from the Recruiter

Megan, from the recruitment agency, updates Milton on the search. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. How many candidates are on the shortlist?
Five, chosen from sixty applications.
2. What kind of questions does Megan recommend asking?
Questions about what candidates would do, not only what they did.
3. Why does Megan suggest talking about salary early?
Turnover in this job is high, and losing a good candidate at the end because of money would be a waste.
Listening 2

My Worst Hire

A CEO tells what a bad hire taught him. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. What kind of question did the CEO never ask his worst hire?
A hypothetical question, like what he would do if a client got angry.
2. What happened in the first six months?
The company lost three good clients.
3. What two things does the CEO do differently today?
He asks every candidate a hypothetical question about a mistake, and he delegates real responsibility.
