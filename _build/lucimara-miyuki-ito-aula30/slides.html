Chapter 4: Doing or To Do?

I Enjoy Traveling, I Decided to Move

enjoy doing · avoid doing · decide to do · plan to do

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"I enjoy traveling to new markets."

"I avoid making promises I can't keep."

"I decided to move into regulatory affairs."

"We're planning to expand to the U.S. market."

Look at the orange words. After which verbs do we use -ing, and after which verbs do we use to + verb?

FormVerbsExample
verb + -ingenjoy, avoid, consider, keep, finish, missI enjoy traveling to new markets.
verb + to + verbdecide, want, plan, hope, need, agree, learnI decided to move into regulatory affairs.
preposition + -inginterested in, good at, before, afterI'm interested in expanding to the U.S.
remember / stop: both, new meaningremember to do (later) / remember doing (a memory)Remember to bring the samples. / I remember meeting Greg.

Many to verbs look at the future (decide, plan, hope). Many -ing verbs talk about an activity (enjoy, avoid, keep). Never "I enjoy to travel" and never "I decided moving".
