Lesson 30 -- Pre-class

Telling Your Story -- Career, Turning Points, and What Drives You

Get ready for a breakfast meeting in Houston, where Tom Reyes and his boss want to hear your career story. Learn which verbs take -ing and which take to + verb ("I enjoy traveling to new markets", "I decided to move into regulatory affairs"). Key words: turning point, milestone, accomplishment, field, to oversee, drive, rewarding, demanding, to take on, to move up.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Turning point -- a moment when your life or career changes direction
"Moving to regulatory affairs was the turning point in my career."
Milestone -- an important event or result in a long process
"Our first export to Europe was a big milestone for the company."
Accomplishment -- something good that you achieved with a lot of work
"Getting the product approved was my biggest accomplishment last year."
Field -- an area of work or study
"I have worked in the chemical field for over twenty years."
To oversee -- to manage a group of people or a process and check the work
"Today I oversee production and regulatory affairs."
Drive -- the energy and motivation that make you keep working hard
"My drive is simple: I love solving problems."
Rewarding -- making you feel happy and proud of what you do
"Training young chemists is the most rewarding part of my job."
Demanding -- needing a lot of time, energy, and effort
"The first year as director was very demanding."
To take on -- to accept a new job, role, or responsibility
"In 2015, I took on the regulatory team."
To move up -- to get a higher position in a company
"She moved up from analyst to director in twelve years."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Verb + -ing or Verb + to?

GRAMMAR

After some verbs we use -ing. After other verbs we use to + verb.

FormVerbsExample
verb + -ingenjoy, avoid, consider, keep, finish, missI enjoy traveling to new markets. / I avoid making promises I can't keep.
verb + to + verbdecide, want, plan, hope, need, agree, learnI decided to move into regulatory affairs. / We're planning to expand.
preposition + -inginterested in, good at, before, afterI'm interested in expanding to the U.S.
remember / stopboth forms, different meaningRemember to bring the samples (later). / I remember meeting Greg (a memory).
RuleMany to verbs look at the future (decide, plan, hope). Many -ing verbs talk about an activity (enjoy, avoid, keep). Negative: "I decided not to travel." Never "I enjoy to travel" and never "I decided moving".

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection
