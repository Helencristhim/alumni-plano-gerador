The Countdown

Eight words to get ready for the semifinal (countdown, schedule, strategy, checklist, ding, repair, fitness, confirm) and how to say what is done and not done: I've just..., I've already..., I haven't ... yet.

Stage 1.1: Getting Ready

Vocabulary

Tap Listen, then say the word out loud. Eight words, two minutes. "Ding" is a surf word: a small hole in your board.

Countdown — counting the days or the time before an important event
"The countdown to the semifinal has started."
Schedule — a plan with the days and times of your activities
"Dana has sent me a new training schedule."
Strategy — a plan to win or to get what you want
"We need a strategy for the first five minutes."
Checklist — a list of things to do, with a check for each one
"Three things on my checklist are done."
Ding — a small hole or crack in a surfboard
"There's a ding on the nose of my board."
Repair — to fix something that is broken or damaged
"I've already repaired the ding."
Fitness — how strong and healthy your body is
"Fitness training is on Monday and Wednesday."
Confirm — to say that something is true or certain
"Jess hasn't confirmed her seat in the van yet."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

Grammar

Read Renato's message to his friend Lucas in Brazil, on Wednesday night. Then answer the questions.

Lucas, big news! The organizers have just announced the date of the semifinal: Saturday the 22nd, at Margaret River. The countdown has already started. Dana has made a new schedule for me, with fitness training two days a week. I have already repaired the ding on my board, and I have just bought a new leash. But I haven't watched the full replay with Dana yet. We're going to watch it tomorrow and talk about a strategy. I haven't packed my bag yet, but it's on my checklist. Kai has already booked the van, but Jess hasn't confirmed her seat yet. Have you seen the countdown on the club page yet? Ten days to go!

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip

Grammar

Present perfect with just, already and yet

have / has + past participle. Use just for a very short time ago, already for something done (maybe sooner than expected), and yet for "until now" in negatives and questions.

just

I've just bought a leash.
Dana has just called.

already

I've already repaired it.
Kai has already booked the van.

yet

I haven't packed yet.
Has Jess confirmed yet?

Positive: I've already packed.  ·  Negative: I haven't packed yet.  ·  Question: Have you packed yet? Short answer: Not yet.
Remember: just and already go between have / has and the participle. yet goes at the end of the sentence.
Your voice: "I've" is one short sound. In "already", the strong part is in the middle: al-RED-y.
