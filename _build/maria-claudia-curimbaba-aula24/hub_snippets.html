Curating Your Words -- The Language of Art Criticism

Describe a work of art and give an opinion with the reason inside the sentence. Relative clauses: "who" for people, "which" for things, "whose" for possession, "where" for places, and "that" when the clause is essential. New vocabulary: brushwork, texture, figurative, muted, bold, evocative, restrained, layered, understated, retrospective.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Brushwork -- the visible way the paint was applied with the brush
"The brushwork is loose, almost careless, and completely deliberate."
Texture -- the surface quality of a work: rough, smooth, thick, flat
"Up close, the texture is almost sculptural."
Figurative -- showing recognizable figures or objects, not abstract shapes
"She returned to figurative work after ten years of pure color."
Muted -- soft and low in tone; the opposite of bright and loud
"A muted palette of grey, sand and faded blue."
Bold -- strong and confident; a choice that takes a visible risk
"Hanging the smallest work first was a bold decision."
Evocative -- bringing a strong feeling or memory to mind
"The smallest canvas is the most evocative in the room."
Restrained -- deliberately controlled; nothing is done in excess
"The hang is restrained: eleven works in a room that could hold thirty."
Layered -- built up in levels, so there is more the longer you look
"A layered surface: three paintings underneath the one you see."
Understated -- quiet and simple in a way that works better than display
"An understated show, with no wall text competing for attention."
Retrospective -- an exhibition covering an artist's whole career
"Her retrospective opens in Houston in November."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

The artist whose retrospective opened last week in Houston has never shown in Brazil. The early canvases are figurative and loud, but in the room where the nineties begin the colors turn muted and the brushwork loosens. The centre of the show is a small painting which she made in 1998, the year she returned to figurative work. Stand close and the texture gives the game away: the surface is layered, and three earlier compositions sit underneath the one you can see. It is the most evocative object in the building. There is one bold decision, and it belongs to the curator who designed the hang: the largest painting, the one that everybody photographs, has been hung last instead of first. The result is an understated, restrained show about an artist who spent thirty years getting quieter.

1. Which pronoun does the text use before "retrospective", and why?
A "whose" -- the retrospective belongs to the artist.
B "which" -- the artist is a thing.
C "where" -- the retrospective is a place.
2. In "the room where the nineties begin", what does "where" tell us?
A That the room belongs to the nineties.
B That the noun before it is a place.
C That the clause is about a person.
3. Why is "the curator who designed the hang" correct, and not "which"?
A Because "which" is never used in reviews.
B Because a curator is a person, and people take "who".
C Because the clause is not essential to the sentence.

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Relative Clauses

GRAMMAR

Criticism is a judgement with a reason attached, and the relative clause is what lets the reason travel inside the sentence. WHO comes after a person. WHICH comes after a thing. WHOSE comes after whoever owns the next noun. WHERE comes after a place. And THAT can replace who or which when the clause is essential to the meaning (no comma).

PronounComes afterExample
whoa person"the artist who painted this"
whicha thing"the canvas which hangs alone"
whosewhoever owns the next noun"the artist whose retrospective opens tonight"
wherea place"the room where the small works hang"
thatreplaces who / which when the clause is essential"the painting that everyone photographs"

Key point: two traps. The pronoun is not repeated inside the clause: "the room where I saw it there" → "the room where I saw it". And whose is followed directly by the noun, with no article: "the artist whose the retrospective" → "the artist whose retrospective".

Stage 2: Reading a Work in Order

Order

Listen first, then put the steps of forming an opinion about a work in the correct order.

?You attach the reason to the judgement with a relative clause, so the evidence travels inside the sentence.
?You look for longer than feels comfortable and name one thing you can actually see.
?You give your verdict in one sentence, and say whether you would live with the work or only visit it.
?You say what the work does to you, because that half of the opinion is yours alone.
?You place the work in the artist's story: what changed, when, and on which side of that change this one falls.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best sentence for each situation.

You want to introduce the person who designed the exhibition. Which is best?
A "The curator which chose the hang was trained in Houston."
B "The curator who chose the hang was trained in Houston."
C "The curator whose chose the hang was trained in Houston."
You want to say the exhibition belongs to that artist. Which is best?
A "The artist whose retrospective opens tonight is Brazilian."
B "The artist whose the retrospective opens tonight is Brazilian."
C "The artist which retrospective opens tonight is Brazilian."
