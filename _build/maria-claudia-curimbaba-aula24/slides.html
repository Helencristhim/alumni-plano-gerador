Chapter 3 -- The Code

Putting the Reason Inside the Sentence

who / which / whose / where

Discovery

Listen and Notice

Look at the orange words. What kind of noun comes just before each one?

"The artist who painted this worked in muted colors for thirty years."

"The canvas which hangs alone is the most evocative in the show."

"The artist whose retrospective opens tonight has never shown in Brazil."

"The room where the small works hang is the quietest in the museum."

PronounComes afterExample
whoa person"the artist who painted this"
whicha thing or an animal"the canvas which hangs alone"
whosea person or thing that OWNS the next noun"the artist whose retrospective opens tonight"
wherea place"the room where the small works hang"
thatreplaces who / which when the clause is essential (no comma)"the painting that everyone photographs"

Key idea: the relative clause carries the REASON, so your opinion and your evidence fit in one sentence. Two traps: the pronoun is not repeated ("the room where I saw it there"), and whose is followed directly by a noun ("the artist whose the retrospective" → "the artist whose retrospective").

Listening 1

The Curator's Tour

Sound first -- no text on screen.

0:000:00
1. Why does the curator hang only eleven works in that room?
Because the paintings are quiet, and a crowded wall would make the visitor walk past them; the restraint is deliberate.
2. What does she ask visitors to do in front of "Evening, Interior"?
To stand close enough to see the texture, because there are three earlier paintings underneath the one they are looking at.
3. What happened in 1998, according to her?
The artist returned to figurative work after a decade of pure color, and the palette became muted at the same time.
4. What does she say about the painting everyone photographs?
That it is the boldest one in the show, and not the best -- the smallest canvas is the most evocative.
Listening 2

How to Have an Opinion

Sound first -- no text on screen.

0:000:00
1. What does he say people are actually afraid of in a gallery?
Not the art: being caught saying something naive in front of someone who knows more.
2. What is his two-part formula for an opinion?
Name one thing you can actually see -- the brushwork, the texture, the palette -- and then say what it does to you.
3. Why does he say a relative clause is useful in front of a painting?
Because it lets the evidence travel inside the sentence, so the judgement never arrives naked.
4. What does he think of the phrase "I do not know much about art"?
That it is the only sentence that really disqualifies you, and that nobody has ever asked him for credentials in a gallery.
