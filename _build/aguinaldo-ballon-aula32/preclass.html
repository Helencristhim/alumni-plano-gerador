Lesson 32 -- Pre-class

The Language of Technology and Disruption -- Big Ideas, Few Words

How to talk about technology that changes a whole market, in short and dense phrases. Key words: to disrupt, an incumbent, cutting-edge, to scale up, obsolete, a game changer, an early adopter, a learning curve, to automate, mainstream. Structure: compound adjectives before a noun -- an AI-driven port, a ten-year contract, a data-heavy system, a long-term investment.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

To disrupt -- to change an industry or a market completely, often with a new technology
"Automated terminals could disrupt the whole port business."
An incumbent -- a company that already holds a market before new competitors arrive
"The incumbent had run the terminal for almost forty years."
Cutting-edge -- the most modern and advanced, using the newest ideas
"The new terminal uses cutting-edge cranes from Finland."
To scale up -- to make a project or a business bigger, so that it serves many more people
"The pilot worked, so now they want to scale it up to the whole port."
Obsolete -- no longer used, because something newer and better exists
"Five years later, the old cranes were obsolete."
A game changer -- something that completely changes the way a situation develops
"Driverless trucks were a game changer for the new terminal."
An early adopter -- a person or a company that uses a new technology before most others
"Rotterdam was an early adopter of automated cranes."
A learning curve -- the time and effort you need to learn a new skill or a new system
"The new software has a steep learning curve."
To automate -- to make a process work by machines or software, without people doing it
"They want to automate the gate, so trucks enter without stopping."
Mainstream -- accepted, used or known by most people, not only by specialists
"In ten years, automated terminals will be mainstream."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

On a technology panel, you have one minute, so every word counts. Good speakers do not say "a port that is driven by artificial intelligence". They say "an AI-driven port". They do not say "a contract that lasts ten years". They say "a ten-year contract". Two words join with a hyphen and go before the noun.

Notice the number. In "a ten-year contract", "year" is singular, because the two words work together as one adjective, and adjectives in English have no plural. The same is true for "a two-hour delay" and "a thirty-year concession".

Other common patterns are "a data-heavy system", "a labor-intensive business", "a long-term investment" and "a fast-growing market". After the verb be, use the long form: the contract is ten years long, the port is driven by AI.

1. Which is the short way to say "a contract that lasts ten years"?
A "a ten-years contract", with the plural, because there are ten of them
B "a ten-year contract"
C "a contract ten-year"
2. Which sentence is correct?
A "They opened an AI-driven terminal."
B "They opened a terminal AI-driven on the other side of the bay."
C "They opened a driven-AI terminal."
3. After the verb be, what do you use?
A The short form with a hyphen, because it is always shorter and more natural in English
B No adjective at all
C The long form: "The contract is ten years long."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Long Idea, Short Phrase

GRAMMAR

Join two words, put them before the noun, and keep the number singular.

Long wayShort wayPattern
a port that is driven by AIan AI-driven portnoun + past participle
a contract that lasts ten yearsa ten-year contractnumber + singular noun
a system that uses a lot of dataa data-heavy systemnoun + adjective
an investment for the long terma long-term investmentadjective + noun
a market that grows fasta fast-growing marketadjective + -ing

Never: a ten-years contract · a terminal AI-driven · a heavy-data system · a fastly-growing market
