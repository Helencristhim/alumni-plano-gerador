Lesson 23 -- Pre-class

What We Should Have Done

The internal post-mortem: saying what should have been done and what could have been done faster, without blaming a person. Key words: retrospective, blameless, hindsight, assumption, overlook, gap, threshold, contingency, rollback, accountability. Structure: past modals -- should have / could have + past participle, and the passive "should not have been overlooked" for when no name belongs in the sentence.

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example. Tap Listen to hear it.

Retrospective -- a meeting where a team looks back at how the work went
"The retrospective is held two days after every incident."
Blameless -- looking at the cause of a problem, never at the person
"A blameless review gets to the truth much faster."
Hindsight -- clear understanding of a situation, but only after it happened
"In hindsight, the threshold was the problem all along."
Assumption -- something you believe is true without checking it
"Our assumption was that the alert would fire on its own."
Overlook -- to fail to notice something important
"The warning in the log was overlooked for forty minutes."
Gap -- a missing piece in a process
"There is a gap between the alert and the engineer on call."
Threshold -- the level at which an alert is sent
"The threshold was set too high, so nobody was warned."
Contingency -- a backup plan kept ready for a problem that may happen
"We could have used the contingency plan on the first day."
Rollback -- putting a system back to the version that worked
"A rollback would have restored the service in ten minutes."
Accountability -- accepting that a result is yours to answer for
"Accountability is not the same thing as blame."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text and answer the questions.

Two days after the outage, the team sits down for the retrospective. Nobody is there to say sorry; the customer already has the report. The question on the wall is different: what should we have done? In hindsight, the answer is easy. The threshold was set too high, so no alert was sent, and the warning in the log was overlooked for forty minutes. The team should have checked that threshold before the release. The rollback was ready at ten past ten, so they could have rolled back then and saved an hour. Their assumption was that the patch would be faster, and they should not have assumed it without testing. There was also a contingency plan, and it might have helped, but nobody opened it. The meeting is blameless: the name of the engineer on call is never written down, because the gap is in the process, not in the person. What is written down is one action item, one owner, one date. That is accountability.

1. "We should have checked that threshold before the release." Did the team check it?
A Yes, and it was the right decision.
B No. It was the right thing to do, and it was not done.
C They are still checking it now.
2. What is the difference between "we should have rolled back" and "we could have rolled back"?
A There is no difference; they are two ways to say the same thing.
B "Should have" says it was the right thing to do; "could have" says it was possible.
C "Could have" is about the future and "should have" is about the past.
3. Why does the text say "the warning was overlooked" and not "the engineer on call overlooked the warning"?
A Because nobody knows who was on call that morning.
B Because the passive is more formal, and formal is always better.
C Because the review is blameless: it records the gap in the process, not the person.
4. Which sentence is correct?
A "We should had rolled back at ten past ten."
B "We should have rolled back at ten past ten."
C "We should have roll back at ten past ten."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Past Modals: should have / could have + past participle

GRAMMAR

How to talk about the thing that did not happen -- and keep everybody's name out of the sentence.

StructureUseExample
should have + past participleThe right thing that was not done.We should have checked the threshold.
should not have + past participleThe wrong thing that was done.We should not have assumed it.
could have + past participleA possibility that existed and was not used. Always add the number.We could have rolled back in ten minutes.
should have been + past participleThe passive: the mistake with nobody's name in it.The warning should not have been overlooked.
might have + past participleYou are not sure about a past cause.The contingency plan might have helped.
AvoidAfter the modal it is always have, never had: not "should had checked" → "should have checked". Never leave the verb bare: not "should have check" → "should have checked". And never add "to": not "could have to use" → "could have used". In speech, should have sounds like one word: should've, SHUD-uv.

Stage 4: Situational Quiz

Quiz

Choose the best answer for each real work situation.

The engineering lead asks what the team missed before the release. You answer:
A "We should have checked the threshold before the release."
B "We should had check the threshold before the release."
C "Nobody told us to check the threshold, so it is not on us."
A director asks how much time was lost. The strongest answer is:
A "We could have been a lot faster that morning."
B "We could have rolled back at ten past ten and saved sixty-two minutes."
C "We could have to roll back earlier, more or less."
Somebody in the retrospective starts naming the engineer who was on call. You say:
A "You are right, he should have seen it much earlier."
B "The warning should not have been overlooked -- that is a gap in the process, not a person."
C "Let us not talk about this part at all."
Your manager asks why the team waited for the patch instead of rolling back. You answer:
A "We should not have assumed that the patch would be faster than the rollback."
B "We should not assumed that the patch would be faster than the rollback."
C "The patch was the only option we had at that moment."

Stage 5: Free Production

Reflection

Record yourself answering the question below. There is no right or wrong answer.

Think of one thing that really went wrong on one of your accounts -- a late renewal, a failed data load, a call that went badly. Run your own post-mortem in four or five sentences, using should have and could have + past participle: what should have been done, what could have been done faster and how many minutes it would have saved, what assumption was never tested, and the one action item that closes the gap. Two rules: not a single name, and every "could have" gets a number. Take your time.

Film
Sully (2016) -- the NTSB hearing scenes

A pilot lands a plane on the Hudson River and saves everyone on board. Then the investigation starts, and the room fills with people saying what he could have done instead: he could have turned back, he should have reached the airport. The film is, from beginning to end, a post-mortem -- and the turning point is the moment the hearing stops asking who made the mistake and starts asking what the process left out. Connection to Lesson 23: this is your grammar under pressure, spoken by people who are deciding a career with it.

Tip: watch the hearing scenes with English subtitles. Write down 5 sentences with should have or could have + past participle, then mark which ones point at a person and which ones point at the process. Rewrite the first group in the passive.
