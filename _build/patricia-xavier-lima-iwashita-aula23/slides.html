Chapter 3: Language Focus

We Should Have Checked...

past modals — should have / could have + past participle

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

"We should have checked the threshold before the release."

"We could have rolled back in ten minutes."

"We should not have assumed that the alert would fire."

"The warning should not have been overlooked for forty minutes."

Did these four things happen? And what comes straight after should and could, every single time?

StructureUseExample
should have + past participleThe right thing that was not doneWe should have checked the threshold.
should not have + past participleThe wrong thing that was doneWe should not have assumed it.
could have + past participleA possibility that existed and was not usedWe could have rolled back in ten minutes.
should have been + past participleThe passive: keep the person out of itThe warning should not have been overlooked.
might have + past participleYou are not sure about a past causeThe log might have been slow that morning.

After should and could the verb is always have + past participle — never had, never the bare verb. In speech, should have becomes should've: one word, SHUD-uv.

The Three Sentences of a Post-mortem

Three Sentences and the Meeting Works

"We should have checked the threshold before the release."

1 — the right thing that was not done

"We could have rolled back in ten minutes."

2 — the option that existed, with the number

"The warning should not have been overlooked."

3 — the mistake, with nobody's name in it

Chapter 4: The Retrospective

Four O'Clock, No Customer in the Room

Arjun asks — and the answer is never a name

Dialogue

Where Did the Forty Minutes Go?

A
Before we close the retrospective, one question. Why did forty minutes pass before anyone opened the log?
P
The threshold was set too high, so no alert was sent. In hindsight, we should have checked it before the release.
A
Agreed. And the rollback? It was ready at ten past ten.
P
We could have rolled back then and saved an hour. Our assumption was that the patch would be faster.
A
That is the assumption I want written down. Not the name of the person who was on call — the gap in the process.
P
So the action item is: lower the threshold, and page the engineer on call directly. That closes the gap.
A
Good. This is a blameless review, so we write what should have happened, not who should have done it.
P
I will send the summary before six. Accountability, not blame.
Comprehension

About Arjun

1. What does Arjun want to understand before he closes the retrospective?
Why forty minutes passed before anyone opened the log.
2. What does he ask to have written down, and what does he refuse to write?
The assumption and the gap in the process — not the name of the person who was on call.
3. How does Arjun describe the review at the end?
As a blameless review: they write what should have happened, not who should have done it.

Listening 2

Mark's Warning

A director on where these two words get dangerous. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. According to Mark, what happens when you point "should have" at a person?
The room closes: nobody speaks after that, and the real cause stays hidden.
2. What two things does he suggest instead of "you should have"?
Use "we", or use the passive — "the warning should not have been overlooked".
3. What does he say you must always add to "could have"?
The number — "we could have rolled back in ten minutes", not "we could have been faster".

Role-Play — Semi-free

Three Sentences and One Refusal

Scenario: I am Karen and I arrived late. Give me the whole retrospective in three sentences: what should have been done, what could have been done faster with the number, and the action item. Then I will ask you who was on call — bring me back to the process.

Keywords:

should have been reviewed sixty-two minutes closes the gap blameless
Free Practice

Sixty Seconds, One Real Post-mortem

Sixty seconds: choose one thing that really went wrong on one of your accounts — a late renewal, a failed data load, a call that went badly — and run your own post-mortem: what should have been done, what could have been done faster, and the one action item that closes the gap.

No keywords. One rule: not a single name, and every "could have" gets a number.
