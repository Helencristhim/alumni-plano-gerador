Chapter 3: The Pattern

Two Words That Open Any Counter

I'd like · Would you like

Grammar Discovery

Listen and Notice

I'd like a window seat.
I'd like to check in, please.
Would you like a receipt?
Yes, please. / No, thank you.

Two of them ask. One offers. What comes after LIKE in each one?

UseFormExample
Ask for a thingI'd like + nounI'd like a window seat.
Ask to do somethingI'd like to + base verbI'd like to check in. — the TO never falls out.
OfferWould you like + ... ?Would you like a receipt?
Answer an offerYes, please. / No, thank you.Never only "yes" or only "no" — it sounds hard.
CarefulI'd like = I would like. "I like coffee" = always, in general. "I'd like a coffee" = now, it is a request. They are not the same thing.

Chapter 4: The First Hours

Four in the Afternoon, Manhattan

one bag, twenty nights, and a front desk

Dialogue

At the Front Desk

D
Good afternoon, sir. Welcome to the hotel. How can I help you?
R
Good afternoon. I'd like to check in, please. I have a reservation.
D
Of course. Can I see your passport, please?
R
Here it is. The reservation is for twenty nights.
D
Thank you, Mister Luiz. Room fourteen oh six. Here is your key.
R
Thank you. I'd like a quiet room, please. I work early in the morning.
D
No problem. Fourteen oh six is at the back, and it is very quiet. Would you like help with your luggage?
R
No, thank you. I only have one bag.
D
Perfect. And would you like breakfast in the room tomorrow?
R
Yes, please. At seven. And I'd like the bill by email, not on paper.
Comprehension

About the Front Desk

1. What does the receptionist ask to see?
His passport.
2. Why is room fourteen oh six a good room for him?
It is at the back, so it is very quiet.
3. What two things does she offer him?
Help with his luggage, and breakfast in the room tomorrow.
Listening

The Gate Changed

An announcement in the airport, before the flight. Sound first — no text.

0:000:00
1. Which gate is it now?
