Lesson 28 -- Pre-class

Change and Progress -- How GI Programs Have Evolved

How to tell the story of change: where you started, what has changed until now, and the result so far. Key words: to evolve, to expand, to double, steady, decade, recognition, momentum, sustainable, to boost, benchmark. Structure: the present perfect for change (The program has grown, Exports have doubled, since / for / so far) and the past simple for a finished year (In 2016, we started).

Stage 1.1: Vocabulary Cards

Vocabulary

Listen to each word and read the example.

To evolve -- to change slowly over a long time
"The program has evolved a lot since 2016."
To expand -- to become bigger or to reach more places
"We have expanded beyond coffee and cheese."
To double -- to become two times bigger
"The number of regions has doubled."
Steady -- regular, without big jumps or falls
"The growth has been steady, year after year."
Decade -- a period of ten years
"Over the last decade, a lot has changed."
Recognition -- when people know and respect something
"The biggest change is recognition."
Momentum -- the energy that keeps something moving forward
"We need to keep the momentum."
Sustainable -- that can continue for a long time without damage
"We want a sustainable program."
To boost -- to help something grow or become stronger
"The fair boosted our sales."
Benchmark -- a good example that others use to compare
"Our regions have become a benchmark."

Stage 1.3: Grammar in Context

GRAMMAR

Read the text, then answer the questions.

Flavia is preparing a report for Giorgio. She writes: “In 2016, the program started with three regions.” She uses the past simple because 2016 is a finished year.

Then she describes the change: “Since then, the program has evolved a lot. The number of regions has doubled, and the growth has been steady.” She uses has + past participle because the change connects the past with today.

At the end, she gives the result until now: “So far, we have trained more than two thousand families. Sebrae-SP has worked with GI producers for ten years.”

Why does Flavia say "In 2016, the program started" and not "has started"?
A Because 2016 is a finished year, so we use the past simple.
B Because the program is not working today, and it stopped many years ago, after the first pilot.
C Because start is an irregular verb.
"The number of regions has doubled." Is the number big now?
A No. It was big in the past, but it is small again today because some regions left.
B Yes. The change continues until today.
C We do not know.
Which sentence gives the result until today?
A "In 2016, we started with three regions and a very small team in one office."
B "We started in 2016."
C "So far, we have trained two thousand families."

Stage 1.4: Grammar Tip -- Then, Now and So Far

GRAMMAR

Have / has + past participle connects the past with today. A finished year takes the past simple.

FunctionFormExample
A change until nowhave / has + past participleThe program has grown.
How longfor + a period / since + a startfor ten years / since 2016
The result until todaySo far, + have / has + past participleSo far, we have trained 2,000 families.
A finished yearpast simpleIn 2016, we started.
QuestionsHave / Has + subject + past participle?Has the program grown?

Never: We have started in 2016 · since ten years · have grew · the families has joined
