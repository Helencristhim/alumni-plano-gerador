Series
Down to Earth with Zac Efron -- Season 1

Two men go to eight countries and spend every episode comparing: this water is cleaner, that system is older, this way of building a house is cheaper and lasts longer. The English is slow, the sentences are short, and there are subtitles for everything — it is one of the easiest series on Netflix to watch in English, and every episode is a comparison from beginning to end.

Tip: pick one episode and write down five comparisons you hear. Then say each one back with the two places you know instead.

Video
BBC Learning English -- "Comparatives and superlatives" (6 Minute Grammar)

Six minutes on exactly today’s grammar, including the part nobody explains well: which adjectives count as short and which count as long. Two speakers, normal speed, and a short test at the end that you can answer out loud before they do.

Tip: listen once without pausing. The second time, pause before every answer and say yours first — that is where the learning is.

Talk
Barry Schwartz -- "The paradox of choice" (TED)

Why having more options makes people less happy, not more. It is the other half of today’s lesson: comparing is useful, and comparing forever is a trap. He is funny, he speaks clearly, and after this one you will understand why the second recording in class insisted on finishing the sentence instead of finding the best.

Tip: English subtitles on. Stop after the first ten minutes if it gets long — his argument is complete by then, and finishing is worth less than understanding.
