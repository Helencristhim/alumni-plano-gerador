Chapter 4: Saying What Somebody Else Said

Three Shapes, One Verb That Judges

He said, he asked, he agreed — and they are not the same

Grammar Discovery

What Happens to the Verb?

She said: “The invoice will arrive on Friday.” → She said the invoice would arrive on Friday."

He asked: “Has the forecast been updated?” → He asked whether the forecast had been updated."

They said: “We are reviewing it before the close.” → They said they were reviewing it before the close."

He said: “I will look at it.” → He agreed to deliver it. — is that the same thing?"

In the first three, what happened to the orange verb when it became blue? And in the fourth — what did the red verb add that the speaker never said?

OriginalReportedExample
willwouldShe said the invoice would arrive on Friday.
is / arewas / wereThey said they were reviewing it.
has / havehadHe asked whether it had been updated.
a yes/no questionwhether or if, and no inversionHe asked whether the forecast had been updated. Not had the forecast been.
an instructionasked me to + bare verbShe asked me to send the file by Thursday.
time words move tootoday → that day · tomorrow → the next dayHe said he would send it the next day.
the trap that costs mostthe reporting verb is your judgement, and everybody reads it as onesaid ≠ agreed ≠ promised ≠ mentioned. If unsure, use said.

In one line: the verb steps back one tense, the question loses its inversion, and the reporting verb carries an opinion you may not have intended.

Chapter 5: Real Speed

The Email, Heard First

Two voice notes and one real recap

Listening 1 · A Voice Note

The Verb Is Your Judgement

A finance director on the two things people get wrong in a recap. Sound first — no text.

1. What does he say a recap is not, and what should it contain instead?
It is not a transcript. It should say what was decided, who is doing what, and by when.
2. What is the test he gives for a recap that is too long?
If the email is longer than the meeting felt, you have written the wrong thing.
3. What does he say about the reporting verb, and what should you do when you are unsure?
The verb is your judgement and everybody reads it as one — he said he would look at it is not he agreed to deliver it. If you are not sure which verb fits, use said.
Listening 2 · A Manager

The Same Shape, Every Time

An accounting manager describing the recap she writes in eight minutes.

1. What are the five parts of her recap, in order?
What the meeting was about, the decisions, the actions with a name and a date, anything still open with the name of who it is waiting for, and the one thing she wants them to remember.
2. What does she say about an action without a name?
That it is a wish, not an action.
3. What three shapes does she use to report what somebody said?
“She said the invoice would arrive on Friday”, “he asked whether the forecast had been updated”, and “they agreed to review it before the close”.
Real Document

The Recap, Eight Minutes

Recap — global close alignment, 4 March
from: Keitma Gomes · to: the six country leads · cc: accounting manager

Hi all,

Short recap of this morning's call on the close calendar.

Decided: the cut-off moves to six in the evening on the last calendar day, from April. The accrual review moves to day two.

Actions: Mark said he would update the calendar in the system by Friday. I will follow up with the BPO before Thursday. Ana asked me to send her the new template today.

Pending: the tax sign-off is still open and is waiting for the tax team. Mark asked whether the forecast had been updated, and I said I would confirm on Monday.

Takeaway: nothing else changes this month. If I have written anything here that is different from what you remember, tell me today rather than in six weeks.

Best regards,
Keitma

1. What are the two decisions, and when do they start?
The cut-off moves to six in the evening on the last calendar day, from April, and the accrual review moves to day two.
2. Which item is pending, and who is it waiting for?
The tax sign-off, and it is waiting for the tax team.
3. What does the takeaway line ask people to do?
To say today, rather than in six weeks, if anything written there is different from what they remember.
