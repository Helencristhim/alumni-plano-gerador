Talk
Celeste Headlee — 10 ways to have a better conversation (TED)

Eleven minutes on listening well enough to be able to report accurately afterwards — which is exactly what a recap depends on. Several of her ten moves are about not putting your own words into somebody else's mouth.

Tip: after watching, take the last call you had and write two sentences reporting what somebody else said. Then check the verb you chose: is it what they meant, or slightly more?

Talk
Julian Treasure — 5 ways to listen better (TED)

Seven minutes, and the shortest useful thing you will find on the skill a recap actually rests on. He is a sound engineer, so his advice is mechanical rather than motivational.

Tip: pick one of his five and use it on your next call in English. Then write the recap and see whether it was easier to remember what was decided.

Podcast
The Indicator from Planet Money — Is bankruptcy really all that bad?

Nine minutes in which the journalists spend most of their time reporting what other people said — economists, lawyers, company statements. It is reported speech at full native speed, in your own subject area.

Tip: write down five sentences where they report somebody else. Mark the reporting verb in each one, and ask whether a different verb would have changed the meaning.
